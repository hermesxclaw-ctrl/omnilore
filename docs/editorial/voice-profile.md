VOICE PROFILE
=============

Author: Omnilore editorial desk
Goal: Make each dossier lucid enough to trust and vivid enough to remember, without confusing atmosphere for evidence.
Confidence: Provisional; derived from the seven strongest source-aware dossiers and the user’s explicit critique.

Source Set
- `entity/achilles.html` — precise separation of attested character, acts, and later interpretation
- `entity/anubis.html` — procedural clarity grounded in named textual traditions
- `entity/athena.html` — concrete examples instead of adjective piles
- `entity/banshee.html` — corrects a popular misconception directly and memorably
- `entity/baphomet.html` — distinguishes historical object, occult symbol, and modern reception
- `entity/hades.html` — resists flattening an underworld ruler into a Christian devil analogue
- `entity/lilith.html` — strongest visual identity and clearest chronology of changing traditions

Rhythm
- Open with one concrete tension, scene, object, action, or misconception.
- Alternate short orienting sentences with longer evidence-bearing sentences.
- Let a paragraph turn once; do not stack four dramatic pivots in a row.

Compression
- Lead with the answer. Expand only where a source, variant, or disputed identity changes the reader’s understanding.
- A vivid detail earns its place by carrying information.

Capitalization
- Conventional sentence case. Preserve names and tradition-specific terms accurately.

Parentheticals
- Use for dates, original-language terms, and narrow qualifications.
- Do not hide major uncertainty or source limits inside parentheses.

Question Use
- Rare. Ask only when the dossier can answer immediately with evidence.
- Never use a question as a generic curiosity hook.

Claim Style
- Evidence before atmosphere.
- Name the source tradition and approximate date near the claim it supports.
- Separate attested material, later interpretation, adaptation, and Omnilore inference.
- Prefer “the text presents,” “later folklore recasts,” or “scholars disagree” over timeless declarations.

Preferred Moves
- Correct a familiar misconception in plain language.
- Put the reader beside a specific threshold: a tomb rite, manuscript line, household warning, cult image, or narrated choice.
- Show how the figure changes between sources instead of inventing one seamless biography.
- Use exact names, actions, objects, places, dates, and consequences.
- End sections on a fact that changes the meaning of what came before.

Banned Moves
- Do not begin with “Imagine” or stock phrases such as “In the vast,” “In the shadowy corners,” and fake invitations to “step into” a world.
- Delete empty praise such as “fascinating,” “rich and complex,” “captivating,” “enigmatic,” and “tapestry” unless the sentence proves the description.
- No “more than just,” “stands as a testament,” “throughout history,” or other prefab significance claims.
- No invented sensory details presented as history.
- No merged myths, unsourced motives, false quotations, or confident claims built from a single weak source.
- No Wikipedia imitation by synonym swapping; reorganize around Omnilore’s own question and evidence.

CTA Rules
- Entity dossiers do not end with sales language or generic invitations.
- Navigation labels state the action: browse, compare variants, open sources, follow a connection.

Channel Notes
- Entity page: factual spine first, then controlled scene-setting and interpretation.
- Search excerpt: one distinctive fact in plain language; no throat-clearing.
- Wing copy: atmospheric but concrete, one image at a time.
