Content advisory

Omnilore documents mythology, folklore, religion, art, literature, horror, urban legends, internet folklore, and fictional worlds. Entries or linked sources may involve artistic or historical nudity; religious or occult imagery; demons; monsters; fictional violence or gore; death; body horror; trauma; or language and perspectives that are historically dated, prejudicial, or offensive.

Inclusion is not endorsement, proof, or a claim that a tradition is fictional. Historical, devotional, literary, contemporary fictional, and internet-born material are labeled as carefully as the evidence allows. Viewer discretion is advised. Omnilore is not designed for children.

Terms of use

Use Omnilore for personal learning, research, criticism, teaching, and cultural reference. Do not misrepresent an Omnilore page as an official statement from a culture, creator, rights holder, religious body, or source institution.

  • Do not scrape, mirror, resell, or mass-republish the archive without permission.
  • Do not use the archive to harass, threaten, impersonate, or target a person or community.
  • Do not treat a confidence label as a guarantee. Read the sourcing and uncertainty notes.
  • External links, images, and third-party services remain subject to their own terms.

Omnilore may change, correct, remove, or temporarily restrict an entry while research, rights, safety, or accessibility issues are reviewed. Nothing here creates a warranty that every record is complete, current, or error-free.

Copyright, images & takedowns

Omnilore’s original layout, code, editorial writing, and generated interface are project material unless stated otherwise. Traditional stories and cultural knowledge are not claimed by Omnilore. Third-party images and quotations belong to their respective creators or institutions and should retain their displayed attribution and license information.

To report an issue

Before public launch, the project owner must provide a monitored rights contact. A request should identify the exact URL, the claimant or authorized representative, the work or material at issue, the basis of the request, and a way to respond. Requests may ask for removal, correction, attribution repair, or license clarification. Do not include unnecessary personal information.

Pending a real process, this page should not be read as a promise of a specific statutory response timeline. Urgent safety concerns should be escalated through the hosting provider as well.

Research and cultural standards

Omnilore separates attestation from invention. Pages use confidence labels, source standing, cultural context, and research-gap notes when available. A page may use evocative interface language, but its factual claims should remain traceable to the record and its cited source material.

  • Names, spellings, translations, and self-descriptions are treated as meaningful, not decorative.
  • Living traditions and communities are not flattened into a monster list or treated as aesthetic props.
  • Contested, devotional, fictional, and modern internet material are labeled rather than quietly blended.
  • AI-assisted research may help organize leads, but it is not itself evidence. Human review and source checking remain required before publication.
  • Thin records stay thin. The archive does not pad uncertainty with confident-sounding filler.

Accessibility

Omnilore aims to support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic headings, readable contrast, alternative text for meaningful images, responsive layouts, reduced-motion preferences, and touch targets suitable for phones. Interactive effects are enhancements, not required to understand an entry.

Known limitations may include third-party image availability, external font loading, complex SVG sigils, long names, and content whose historical language is difficult to make accessible without preserving its meaning. Report a barrier with the page URL and a description of what prevented access.
