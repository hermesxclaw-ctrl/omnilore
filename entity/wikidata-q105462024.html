Who It Is

In the sprawling, ever-expanding multiverse of DC Comics, few names carry the weight of 'Who's Who'—not as a single hero or villain, but as the definitive chronicle of the extraordinary, the obscure, and the forgotten. This is the story of the tome that became a legend in its own right, a doorway into a universe where every character, no matter how minor, has a tale worth telling.

As a reference work, the series is objective and encyclopedic in tone. It presents facts about each character without narrative embellishment, though the entries often include a brief history and current status.

Comprehensive Character Indexing

The series cataloged and described virtually every DC character, including heroes, villains, and minor characters, with details on their powers, abilities, and histories.

Limit: The series is limited to the characters that existed at the time of publication; it does not include characters created after the final update.

Canonical Reference

The series was often treated as an authoritative source for character information, though it was not always perfectly aligned with the comics.

Limit: As a printed work, it could not be updated in real-time, leading to inaccuracies.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The original series (1985-1987) featured a distinctive cover design with a large 'Who's Who' logo and a collage of DC characters. Each issue contained alphabetical entries, with each character receiving a half-page or full-page entry, including a full-color illustration and a text summary of their history, powers, and affiliations.

Symbols — The series used a standardized format: each entry had a header with the character's name, a small illustration, and a text block. The covers often featured a central character or a montage. The 'Who's Who' logo itself became an iconic representation of DC's vast universe.

Domains — The 'Who's Who' series exists in the real world as a published comic book series and later as collected editions. Within the DC Universe, it is sometimes referenced as an in-universe document, though it is primarily a real-world publication.

Accounts I
The Publication of Who's WhoDC Comics, various interviews and historical accounts

1985-1987

Following the success of Marvel's 'Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe,' DC launched its own comprehensive character encyclopedia. The series was published biweekly and featured contributions from numerous writers and artists. It became a beloved reference for fans and a valuable resource for creators. The series established a standardized reference for DC characters and influenced later encyclopedias and digital databases.

Accounts II
The Legacy of Who's WhoDC Comics, fan retrospectives

1990s-present

After the original series ended, DC continued to publish updates and new editions. The 'DC Comics Encyclopedia' and online databases like the DC Database have largely replaced the print series, but 'Who's Who' remains a nostalgic touchstone for fans. The series' legacy lives on in digital fan resources and in the continued demand for comprehensive character guides.
