The Approach

In the sprawling, ever-expanding multiverse of DC Comics, few names carry the weight of 'Who's Who'—not as a single hero or villain, but as the definitive chronicle of the extraordinary, the obscure, and the forgotten. This is the story of the tome that became a legend in its own right, a doorway into a universe where every character, no matter how minor, has a tale worth telling.

Identity

Who's Who: The Definitive Directory of the DC Universe

Pronunciation HOOZ HOO

Known As

Who's Who in the DC UniverseWho's Who

Etymology

The title 'Who's Who' is a common phrase for a biographical directory, echoing reference works like 'Who's Who in America.' The subtitle 'The Definitive Directory of the DC Universe' emphasizes its authoritative and comprehensive nature.

Cultural Ownership

DC Comics, a subsidiary of Warner Bros. Discovery. The series is a corporate-owned publication.

Disputed Identifications

There is no dispute about the identity of this series, but it is sometimes confused with other DC directories or with the Marvel equivalent, 'Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe.'

Description

Appearance

The original series (1985-1987) featured a distinctive cover design with a large 'Who's Who' logo and a collage of DC characters. Each issue contained alphabetical entries, with each character receiving a half-page or full-page entry, including a full-color illustration and a text summary of their history, powers, and affiliations.

Symbols & Iconography

The series used a standardized format: each entry had a header with the character's name, a small illustration, and a text block. The covers often featured a central character or a montage. The 'Who's Who' logo itself became an iconic representation of DC's vast universe.

Behaviour & Personality

As a reference work, the series is objective and encyclopedic in tone. It presents facts about each character without narrative embellishment, though the entries often include a brief history and current status.

Domains & Habitats

The 'Who's Who' series exists in the real world as a published comic book series and later as collected editions. Within the DC Universe, it is sometimes referenced as an in-universe document, though it is primarily a real-world publication.

Version Differences

The original 1985-1987 series covered the post-Crisis on Infinite Earths DC Universe. Subsequent updates (1990-1994) reflected continuity changes, character deaths, and new characters. The later 'DC Comics Encyclopedia' serves as a successor, though it is not a direct continuation of the 'Who's Who' series.

Stories & Myths

The Publication of Who's Who

Source DC Comics, various interviews and historical accountsCulture American comic book fandomPeriod 1985-1987

Summary

Following the success of Marvel's 'Official Handbook of the Marvel Universe,' DC launched its own comprehensive character encyclopedia. The series was published biweekly and featured contributions from numerous writers and artists. It became a beloved reference for fans and a valuable resource for creators.

Participants

DC Comics editorial staff

Writers and artists including Len Wein, Marv Wolfman, George Pérez, and many others

Consequences

The series established a standardized reference for DC characters and influenced later encyclopedias and digital databases.

Variants

The series spawned multiple updates and spin-offs, including 'Who's Who Update' and 'Who's Who in the Legion of Super-Heroes.'

The Legacy of Who's Who

Source DC Comics, fan retrospectivesCulture Comic book fandomPeriod 1990s-present

Summary

After the original series ended, DC continued to publish updates and new editions. The 'DC Comics Encyclopedia' and online databases like the DC Database have largely replaced the print series, but 'Who's Who' remains a nostalgic touchstone for fans.

Participants

DC Comics

Fans and collectors

Consequences

The series' legacy lives on in digital fan resources and in the continued demand for comprehensive character guides.

Variants

Later editions include 'The DC Comics Encyclopedia' and 'DC: The Definitive Visual History.'
