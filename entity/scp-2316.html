Who It Is

SCP-2316 is an anomalous phenomenon centered around a lake whose true name and location are a cognitohazard — recognizing the lake for what it is causes individuals to become part of it. The Foundation designates the phenomenon with the intentionally misleading header 'Field Trip' and describes it in publicly accessible documents as a benign location where Foundation personnel once went on an outing. The true documentation is buried under layers of misdirection. The lake contains a collection of human corpses floating on its surface, which anomalous properties cause to appear as living individuals — specifically, as people the observer knew in childhood. These apparitions speak to the observer, calling them by name and inviting them into the water. Anyone who enters the lake or stays too long in proximity falls under its influence and joins the floating bodies. The entity behind SCP-2316 appears to be a distributed consciousness formed from all the individuals it has absorbed, speaking with one voice that refers to itself as 'we.' The phenomenon has a memetic component — knowledge about the lake's true nature increases susceptibility to its influence. The Foundation discovered this the hard way, losing multiple Mobile Task Force units before understanding the mechanics of the anomaly. Containment involves maintaining a perimeter and ensuring all documentation refers to the location only by its safe designation. Personnel assigned to the site must be carefully screened for childhood connections to the area. SCP-2316 taps into deep-rooted fears about returning 'home' to a place that has been corrupted, and the horror of recognizing faces that should not be there. Its subtlety — the way it uses childhood memories against its victims — makes it one of the more psychologically disturbing recent additions to the Foundation catalog.
