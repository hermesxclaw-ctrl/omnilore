The Approach

He is the most famous name in English literature, yet the man himself remains a mystery. William Shakespeare, the 'Bard of Avon,' wrote plays that have been performed for over four centuries, but we know surprisingly little about his life. From his humble beginnings in Stratford-upon-Avon to his rise as London's leading playwright, his story is one of talent, ambition, and enduring legacy.

Content advisory. Contains historical references to death, disease, and social hierarchy of Elizabethan England.
Identity

William Shakespeare

Pronunciation /ˈʃeɪkspɪər/

Known As

The BardBard of AvonShake-sceneSwan of AvonThe Immortal Bard

Etymology

The surname Shakespeare is of English origin, meaning 'one who wields a spear' or 'a spearman'. The first name William is of Germanic origin, meaning 'resolute protector'.

Cultural Ownership

English, British, global

Disputed Identifications

There is a fringe theory that Shakespeare's works were written by someone else (e.g., Francis Bacon, Edward de Vere, Christopher Marlowe), but this is not accepted by mainstream scholarship.

Description

Appearance

No authenticated portraits exist from his lifetime. The Droeshout portrait on the First Folio and the statue in Holy Trinity Church are posthumous and may not be accurate. He is often depicted with a receding hairline, a beard, and wearing an Elizabethan ruff.

Symbols & Iconography

The quill pen, the Globe Theatre, the mask of tragedy and comedy, and the 'Bard' image are common symbols. His likeness is often used to represent literature and drama.

Behaviour & Personality

Based on his works and contemporary accounts, he was likely witty, observant, and ambitious. He was a businessman as well as an artist, investing in property and theatre shares. He was known as a playwright, poet, and actor.

Domains & Habitats

Stratford-upon-Avon (birthplace, family home, retirement), London (theatrical career, Globe Theatre, Blackfriars Theatre).

Version Differences

There is no 'version' of Shakespeare himself, but interpretations of his life and works vary widely across cultures and eras.

Powers & Abilities

Playwriting

Source Version All periods of his career

Description

Shakespeare wrote approximately 39 plays, including tragedies, comedies, histories, and romances. His works are renowned for their complex characters, poetic language, and deep exploration of human nature.

Limitations

His plays were written for the stage of his time, with limited scenery and female roles played by boys.

Contradictions

Some scholars have questioned whether he wrote all the plays attributed to him.

Poetry

Source Version Primarily in the 1590s

Description

He wrote 154 sonnets and several long narrative poems, such as 'Venus and Adonis' and 'The Rape of Lucrece'.

Limitations

His poetry, especially the sonnets, is complex and often ambiguous.

Contradictions

None.

Acting

Source Version From the 1580s to around 1613

Description

He was an actor and a 'sharer' (part-owner) of the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later the King's Men. He performed in his own plays and those of others.

Limitations

He may have retired from acting in the early 1600s.

Contradictions

The exact roles he played are unknown.

Stories & Myths

The Lost Years

Source Various apocryphal accountsCulture EnglishPeriod 1585-1592

Summary

After the baptism of his twins in 1585, there are few records of Shakespeare until he appears in London in 1592. Stories suggest he fled to London to escape prosecution for deer poaching, or worked as a schoolmaster, or held horses at the theatre.

Participants

William Shakespeare

Thomas Lucy (in the poaching story)

Consequences

These years remain a mystery, but they were formative for his career.

Variants

Several versions exist, but none are substantiated.

The Greene Attack

Source Robert Greene's 'Groats-Worth of Wit'Culture EnglishPeriod 1592

Summary

Robert Greene, a university-educated playwright, attacked Shakespeare in print, calling him an 'upstart Crow' and a 'Johannes factotum', accusing him of overreaching his rank.

Participants

Robert Greene

William Shakespeare

Consequences

This is the first surviving mention of Shakespeare as a playwright, and it shows he was already a target of envy.

Variants

Scholars differ on the exact meaning, but most agree it was an attack on Shakespeare's social climbing.

The First Folio

Source John Heminges and Henry CondellCulture EnglishPeriod 1623

Summary

Seven years after Shakespeare's death, his friends published a collection of 36 of his plays, which had previously only existed in unreliable quarto editions.

Participants

John Heminges

Henry Condell

William Shakespeare (posthumously)

Consequences

The First Folio preserved many of his plays that might otherwise have been lost, and is the primary source for many of them.

Variants

None.

Religious tensions

Source Historical records of the Arden family

Description

Shakespeare's family had Catholic connections, and his father may have been a recusant. This could have posed legal and social risks in Protestant England.

Scholarly Context

Scholars debate the extent of Shakespeare's Catholic sympathies, but the possibility is significant.

Social climbing

Source Groats-Worth of Wit

Description

Greene's attack suggests resentment of Shakespeare's rise from a provincial background to success in London.

Scholarly Context

This reflects the class tensions of Elizabethan society.

Death and mortality

Source Burial records, historical accounts

Description

The death of his son Hamnet and the plague outbreaks in London would have shaped his worldview, as reflected in his plays.

Scholarly Context

The high mortality rate was a common experience in the era.

Contradictions & Disputes

Authorship of the plays

Position A

William Shakespeare of Stratford wrote the works.

Position A — evidence

Contemporary records, title pages, and the First Folio attribute the works to Shakespeare.

Position B

Someone else (e.g., Francis Bacon, Edward de Vere) wrote them.

Position B — evidence

Proponents point to supposed inconsistencies in his education and social background.

Scholarly Consensus

Overwhelmingly supports Shakespeare as the author.

Religious beliefs

Position A

Shakespeare was a secret Catholic.

Position A — evidence

His mother's family was Catholic, and his father may have been a recusant.

Position B

He was a conforming Protestant.

Position B — evidence

He was baptized and buried in the Church of England, and his children were baptized there.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus; the evidence is inconclusive.

Marriage to Anne Hathaway

Position A

Position A — evidence

Position B

Position B — evidence

He left Anne in Stratford while he worked in London.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus; lack of evidence.
