Who It Is

He is the most famous name in English literature, yet the man himself remains a mystery. William Shakespeare, the 'Bard of Avon,' wrote plays that have been performed for over four centuries, but we know surprisingly little about his life. From his humble beginnings in Stratford-upon-Avon to his rise as London's leading playwright, his story is one of talent, ambition, and enduring legacy.

Based on his works and contemporary accounts, he was likely witty, observant, and ambitious. He was a businessman as well as an artist, investing in property and theatre shares. He was known as a playwright, poet, and actor.

Playwriting

Shakespeare wrote approximately 39 plays, including tragedies, comedies, histories, and romances. His works are renowned for their complex characters, poetic language, and deep exploration of human nature.

Limit: His plays were written for the stage of his time, with limited scenery and female roles played by boys.

Poetry

He wrote 154 sonnets and several long narrative poems, such as 'Venus and Adonis' and 'The Rape of Lucrece'.

Limit: His poetry, especially the sonnets, is complex and often ambiguous.

Acting

He was an actor and a 'sharer' (part-owner) of the Lord Chamberlain's Men, later the King's Men. He performed in his own plays and those of others.

Limit: He may have retired from acting in the early 1600s.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No authenticated portraits exist from his lifetime. The Droeshout portrait on the First Folio and the statue in Holy Trinity Church are posthumous and may not be accurate. He is often depicted with a receding hairline, a beard, and wearing an Elizabethan ruff.

Symbols — The quill pen, the Globe Theatre, the mask of tragedy and comedy, and the 'Bard' image are common symbols. His likeness is often used to represent literature and drama.

Domains — Stratford-upon-Avon (birthplace, family home, retirement), London (theatrical career, Globe Theatre, Blackfriars Theatre).

Accounts I
The Lost YearsVarious apocryphal accounts

1585-1592

After the baptism of his twins in 1585, there are few records of Shakespeare until he appears in London in 1592. Stories suggest he fled to London to escape prosecution for deer poaching, or worked as a schoolmaster, or held horses at the theatre. These years remain a mystery, but they were formative for his career.

Accounts II
The Greene AttackRobert Greene's 'Groats-Worth of Wit'

1592

Robert Greene, a university-educated playwright, attacked Shakespeare in print, calling him an 'upstart Crow' and a 'Johannes factotum', accusing him of overreaching his rank. This is the first surviving mention of Shakespeare as a playwright, and it shows he was already a target of envy.

The First FolioJohn Heminges and Henry Condell

1623

Seven years after Shakespeare's death, his friends published a collection of 36 of his plays, which had previously only existed in unreliable quarto editions. The First Folio preserved many of his plays that might otherwise have been lost, and is the primary source for many of them.

Shakespeare was born on 23 April 1564. — His exact birth date is unknown; only his baptism on 26 April is recorded. 23 April is a traditional guess.

Shakespeare wrote all his plays alone. — Some of his later plays were collaborations with other playwrights, such as John Fletcher.

Shakespeare was uneducated. — He likely attended grammar school, where he received a rigorous classical education in Latin and rhetoric.

Shakespeare's plays were all published during his lifetime. — Many were published in quarto editions, but the First Folio was posthumous and included plays not previously printed.
