Who It Is

The Lorelei is a legendary maiden who sits atop the Loreley rock — a steep 132-meter slate cliff on the Rhine River where the current is treacherous and the echo particularly resonant. In the most famous version by Heinrich Heine (set to music by Friedrich Silcher), the beautiful golden-haired maiden combs her hair atop the rock while singing a bewitching song. Sailors, transfixed by her beauty and melody, fail to notice the dangerous rocks and currents below — and their boats are dashed to pieces. The myth was heavily shaped by the German Romantic movement — Clemens Brentano invented the figure in his 1801 ballad, and Heine immortalized her in 1824. However, the idea of a fairy or spirit at the dangerous Loreley bend may have deeper folk roots. The name 'Lorelei' itself derives from Old German 'lureln' (to murmur) and 'ley' (rock), originally describing the echo from the cliff. The Nazi regime, despite Heine's Jewish heritage, attempted to appropriate the Lorelei as an archetypal Germanic figure, labeling the poem 'author unknown.' Today the Lorelei is one of Germany's most famous tourist legends, a UNESCO World Heritage site, and a symbol of the fatal allure of beauty and art.
