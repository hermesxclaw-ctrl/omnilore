Who It Is

Xolotl is the monstrous, dog-headed twin of Quetzalcoatl — a god of deformities, twins, the ball-game, fire, lightning, and the evening aspect of the planet Venus. While his twin Quetzalcoatl represents the bright morning star (the triumphant, creative aspect), Xolotl represents the evening star (the dark, descending, sacrificial aspect). His name derives from xolotl ('dog'), and he is often depicted with the head of a hairless dog (the Xoloitzcuintli breed), inverted feet, and twisted limbs — physical malformations that mark him as an 'otherworldly' being. In the creation myth, Xolotl alone refused to sacrifice himself to create the sun and attempted to hide by transforming into various life-forms (maize, maguey, the axolotl salamander), but was found and sacrificed anyway — a myth that explains both the existence of the evening star and the 'hidden' nature of the axolotl. As patron of the ball-game, Xolotl presides over the cosmic contest whose losers were sacrificed — a willing embrace of the death that he earlier tried to evade.
