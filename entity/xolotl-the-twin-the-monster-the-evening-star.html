Xolotl (the Twin / the Monster / the Evening Star)

Who It Is
Form

Xolotl is often depicted as a dog-like figure, sometimes with human characteristics, embodying the dual nature of his role in Aztec mythology. His appearance can vary, reflecting his association with the underworld, the evening star, and his role as a twin. In some representations, Xolotl is shown with a skeletal body, symbolizing his connection to death and the underworld. Other depictions might portray him with a more canine appearance, highlighting his role as a guide and protector. The variability in Xolotl's appearance underscores the complex and multifaceted nature of his character, reflecting the rich symbolism and metaphors present in Aztec mythology.

Powers

Guidance to the Underworld Xolotl is believed to have the power to guide souls to the underworld, ensuring their safe passage and protecting them from the dangers that lie within. This ability reflects his role as a psychopomp, a figure that escorts souls to the afterlife, and underscores his importance in Aztec funerary rites and beliefs about the afterlife.

Scholarly Debates
Xolotl's Role in Human Sacrifice
Some scholars argue that Xolotl was directly involved in rituals of human sacrifice, given his association with the underworld and death. ⇌ Others propose that Xolotl's role was more nuanced, focusing on his guidance of souls rather than any direct involvement in sacrificial practices, highlighting the need for a more detailed understanding of Aztec religious practices and the specific roles of deities within those contexts.
What We Don't Know

Despite the extensive research on Xolotl, there remain gaps in our understanding, particularly regarding the specifics of his worship and the nuances of his role in Aztec mythology. Further study of primary sources and archaeological findings is necessary to fill these gaps and provide a more comprehensive picture of Xolotl's significance.

A way to hold it in your mind

The figure of Xolotl, with his complex and multifaceted nature, offers a fascinating lens through which to explore the richness and depth of Aztec mythology. By examining his roles as the Evening Star, the guide to the underworld, and the patron of twins, we can gain insights into the Aztec worldview and the cultural practices that defined their civilization.
