Who It Is

In the shadow of the Alborz mountains, where the wind carries the scent of sulfur and the cries of the damned, the divs hold court. These are not the petty demons of later folklore, but the ancient giants of Persian myth—creatures of immense strength, cunning, and malice, who once waged war against the heroes of the Shahnameh. To understand the div is to peer into the heart of Zoroastrian darkness, where the lie (druj) takes form and the world's greatest champions find their match.

Divs are malevolent, destructive, and deceitful. They embody the druj (lie) and work to corrupt creation. They are often portrayed as aggressive, warlike, and cannibalistic, but also as cunning and capable of trickery. In the Shahnameh, they are shown as arrogant and cruel, taking pleasure in harming humans. Some divs are also depicted as guardians of treasures or captives.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'div' derives from Avestan 'daēva', which originally meant 'celestial' or 'divine' in Indo-Iranian, but in Zoroastrianism was demonized to denote a class of evil beings. The shift reflects the theological revolution attributed to Zoroaster, who reclassified the old gods as demons.

Pronunciation — /diːv/ (English approximation: 'deev')

Aliases — Daeva, Daēva, Dēw, Dīv, Daevic, Div of Mazandaran

Ownership — Persian (Iranian) culture, with roots in Zoroastrianism and pre-Zoroastrian Indo-Iranian religion. The concept evolved through the Middle Persian period and into Islamic-era Persia

Powers
Superhuman Strength

Divs possess immense physical strength, capable of crushing rocks, uprooting trees, and overwhelming even the mightiest heroes.

Limit: They can be overcome by clever heroes who exploit their arrogance or weaknesses.

Shape-shifting

Some divs can change their form, appearing as animals, humans, or other beings to deceive their victims.

Limit: They may be unable to change form in certain circumstances or may be recognized by their eyes or odor.

Illusion and Deception

Divs can create illusions, deceive the senses, and spread lies, corrupting the minds of mortals.

Limit: The power of truth (asha) and the recitation of holy verses can dispel their illusions.

Magic and Sorcery

In the Shahnameh, divs are associated with sorcery and dark magic, able to cast spells, curse, and control supernatural forces.

Limit: Their magic can be countered by the power of God (Yazdan) and by heroes with divine support.

Immortality or Longevity

Divs are often considered immortal or extremely long-lived, surviving for millennia.

Limit: They can be killed in combat, as demonstrated by Rostam's victory over the White Div.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Divs are typically described as giant, monstrous beings, often with animal features such as horns, tusks, claws, and wild hair. In the Shahnameh, the White Div is specifically described as a huge, white-fleshed demon with a face like a leopard, and eyes that glow. They may have multiple heads (as in some depictions) or be shape-shifters. In Zoroastrian texts, they are formless or have shadowy, terrifying forms, often associated with darkness and

Symbols — Divs are associated with darkness, night, desert, mountains, and waste places. They are often depicted with weapons like clubs or rocks. In art, they are shown as hairy, muscular giants, sometimes with animal heads. The White Div is often depicted as a massive, pale-skinned monster.

Domains — Divs inhabit dark, desolate places: mountains, caves, deserts, and the underworld. In the Shahnameh, the White Div resides in the dark cavern of Mazandaran, a land of sorcerers and demons. In Zoroastrian cosmology, the daēvas dwell in the northern region of darkness, the realm of Angra Mainyu.

Accounts I
The Daēvas in the AvestaAvesta (Yasna, Vendidad)

c. 1000-600 BCE

The Avesta repeatedly condemns the daēvas as false gods who lead humanity astray. Zoroaster's revelation establishes Ahura Mazda as the supreme god, and the daēvas are cast as demons, servants of Angra Mainyu. The Vendidad provides rituals to ward off their influence, and the Yashts describe how heroes and gods battle them. The daēvas become the archetypal demons of Zoroastrianism, shaping the religion's dualistic worldview.

The Creation of the DaēvasBundahishn

9th-12th century CE

According to the Bundahishn, Angra Mainyu created the daēvas to oppose the good creation of Ahura Mazda. Each daēva is assigned to counter a specific Amesha Spenta or to promote a particular sin. For example, Akoman (Evil Mind) opposes Vohu Manah (Good Mind), and Aēshma (Wrath) opposes Sraosha (Obedience). This establishes a cosmic order where every good force has an evil counterpart, and the world is a battleground between them.

Accounts II
Rostam and the White DivShahnameh

c. 1010 CE

In the Shahnameh, the king Kay Kavus is lured to Mazandaran by a div in disguise, and he and his army are captured by the White Div. Rostam, the great hero, undertakes a perilous quest to rescue them. After a seven-stage journey, he defeats the White Div in a fierce battle, cutting off its head and using its blood to cure the blinded king and his warriors. Rostam's victory cements his reputation as the greatest hero of Iran and frees the king. The White Div's death is a blow to the demonic forces.

Div-e Akvan (Akvan the Div)Shahnameh

c. 1010 CE

In this episode, the div Akvan kidnaps a horse and taunts Rostam. Rostam eventually captures him, but Akvan uses his shape-shifting power to escape. Later, Akvan appears while Rostam is sleeping and throws him into the sea, but Rostam survives and eventually kills the div. The story highlights the cunning and malice of divs, as well as Rostam's resilience and cleverness.

Divs are the same as Christian demons. — While both are evil beings, divs are specific to Iranian mythology and have their own characteristics and origins.

The White Div is a dragon. — In the Shahnameh, the White Div is described as a demonic giant, not a dragon. Dragons (azhi) are a separate category.

All divs are stupid and easily tricked. — While some folklore portrays them as foolish, many divs in the Shahnameh are cunning and dangerous, such as Div-e Akvan.
