Who It Is

The Div (also Daeva, Dev) is a class of demonic, monstrous beings in Persian mythology, Zoroastrian tradition, and Iranian folklore. In Zoroastrian cosmology, the Divs (daevas) are the demonic forces created by Ahriman (Angra Mainyu), the destructive spirit, to oppose the creations of Ahura Mazda (the Wise Lord). They represent the seven deadly sins and various forms of evil, corruption, and darkness. In the Shahnameh (the Persian Book of Kings), the Divs are described as colossal, ugly, horned, and taloned giants with superhuman strength, covered in thick fur or scales, with faces like monsters — red eyes, boar-like tusks, and elephant-like trunks in some descriptions. They inhabit the dark, mountainous region of Mazandaran (a real province in northern Iran, mythologized as a land of demons and sorcerers). The greatest Persian hero, Rostam, famously battles the White Div (Div-e Sepid), the chieftain of all Divs, in the seven stages of his legendary quest (Haft Khan-e Rostam). The White Div is described as a massive, white-haired, cloud-darkening giant who could crush boulders in his fists. Rostam defeats the White Div in a cave on Mount Damavand, gouging out his eyes and decapitating him, freeing King Kay Kavus from the Div's captivity. Other notable Divs include the Akvan Div (a malevolent Div who threw Rostam into the sea) and the Arzhang Div (who guarded a Chinese princess). In later Iranian folklore, the Div became a more generic monstrous entity — a giant, often stupid, who could be outwitted by clever heroes. The Persian Div tradition influenced the development of the Islamic and Turkish 'dev' and eventually entered Christian European demonology through the Crusades. The word 'div' survives in modern Persian as an everyday term for 'monster,' 'giant,' or 'demon,' particularly in fairy tales told to children.
