Who It Is

The legendary, possibly allegorical, founder of the Rosicrucian Order (Order of the Rose Cross). According to three manifestos published in early 17th-century Germany — the Fama Fraternitatis (1614), Confessio Fraternitatis (1615), and The Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosenkreutz (1616) — Rosenkreuz was a German mystic born in 1378 who traveled through the Middle East, learning esoteric wisdom from Arab and Persian sages, before founding a secret brotherhood in Europe. He died at the age of 106, and his perfectly preserved tomb, illuminated by an 'eternal lamp,' was discovered 120 years later by his followers — a vault inscribed with magical symbols, containing his uncorrupted body and the Order's sacred books. The historical consensus is that Rosenkreuz was a literary creation by the manifestos' likely author, the Lutheran theologian Johann Valentin Andreae, though Rosicrucian orders take him as a literal founder.
