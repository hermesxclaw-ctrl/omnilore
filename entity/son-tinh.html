Who It Is

Son Tinh (Sơn Tinh, 'Mountain Spirit') is the Vietnamese god of the mountains and the central figure in one of Vietnam's most culturally significant myths—the eternal rivalry between the Mountain God (Son Tinh) and the Water God (Thuy Tinh) for the hand of Princess My Nuong. According to legend, during the reign of the 18th Hung King, the beautiful princess My Nuong came of age, and two powerful suitors presented themselves: Son Tinh, the god of the mountains, and Thuy Tinh, the god of the waters. Both demonstrated extraordinary powers—Son Tinh could command the forests and raise mountains with a gesture, while Thuy Tinh could summon rain, raise the seas, and call forth typhoons. Unable to choose between them, the king set a challenge: whichever suitor arrived first the next morning with the required wedding gifts—a hundred jars of sticky rice, a hundred sticky rice cakes, a nine-tusked elephant, a nine-spurred rooster, and a nine-red-maned horse—would win My Nuong's hand. Son Tinh arrived first at dawn with all the gifts and married the princess, carrying her away to Tan Vien Mountain. Thuy Tinh arrived moments too late and, in a fury of jealousy and humiliation, unleashed his full power: he raised the seas, summoned storms, and sent devastating floods against the mountain. Son Tinh countered by raising the mountains ever higher, and the battle between them has continued ever since—every year, Thuy Tinh attacks with monsoon rains and floods, and every year, Son Tinh resists. This myth is a profound allegorical encoding of Vietnam's annual struggle with monsoon flooding, explaining why the Red River Delta experiences devastating seasonal floods despite (and because of) the protective mountains. Son Tinh's worship is centered at the Tan Vien Son Temple on Ba Vi Mountain near Hanoi, where he is venerated as one of the Four Immortals (Tứ Bất Tử) of Vietnamese folk religion. The Son Tinh-Thuy Tinh myth is taught to every Vietnamese schoolchild and remains a powerful cultural metaphor for Vietnamese resilience.
