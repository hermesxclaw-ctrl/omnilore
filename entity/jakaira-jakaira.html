Who It Is

In the green depths of Guarani belief, where the forest breathes and the mist rolls off the rivers, there dwells a spirit of the mist itself—Jakaira, the owner of the fog, a being whose breath veils the world in mystery. The Guarani people of South America speak of him as one of the four divine beings who sustain the earth, a lord of the atmospheric waters that nourish the land and shroud the unseen. To encounter Jakaira is to step into a realm where the boundaries between the visible and the hidden blur, where the fog carries whispers of the sacred and the unknown.

Jakaira is a benevolent and sustaining deity, associated with the life-giving aspects of water in its atmospheric form. He is a guardian of the mist, which both conceals and reveals, and is seen as a protector of the natural world. He is not known for capricious or destructive behavior; rather, he maintains the balance of atmospheric waters.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Jakaira' is derived from the Guarani language. It is often interpreted as 'owner of the mist' or 'lord of the fog', from 'jaka' (mist) and 'ira' (owner or lord). Some scholars suggest a connection to the verb 'jaka' meaning 'to cover' or 'to veil', reinforcing his association with mist and

Pronunciation — jah-KAI-rah (approximate; the 'j' is pronounced as in French 'je' or English 'measure', and the stress falls on the second syllable).

Aliases — Jakaíra, Ñande Ryke'i Jakaira, Jakaira Guasu

Ownership — Guarani people, primarily in Paraguay, Brazil, Argentina, and Bolivia. The Guarani are an indigenous people of South America with a rich oral tradition.

Powers
Control over mist and fog

Jakaira has the ability to generate, shape, and dispel mist and fog. He uses this power to veil the earth, protect his domain, and communicate with mortals through the hidden world.

Limit: No limitations are recorded, but his power is typically exercised in harmony with the other divine beings.

Sustenance of the world

As one of the four beings who sustain the earth, Jakaira contributes to the cosmic order by ensuring the atmospheric moisture that supports life. His mist is essential for the growth of plants and the well-being of all creatures.

Limit: His role is complementary to the other three beings; he cannot act alone.

Communication through mist

Jakaira is said to communicate with shamans and the faithful through visions in the mist, revealing hidden knowledge and guidance.

Limit: This ability is mostly associated with shamanic practices and is not universally attested.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Jakaira is not described in anthropomorphic terms in the available sources. He is primarily associated with mist, fog, and the atmospheric moisture that envelops the earth. Some narratives suggest he appears as a luminous being within the mist, but specific physical descriptions are lacking. He is more a presence than a figure with a defined form.

Symbols — Jakaira's primary symbol is mist or fog. In Guarani art and ritual, mist is often associated with the breath of the divine, the veil between worlds, and the nourishing moisture that supports life. He may be represented in ceremonial contexts by the use of smoke or steam, though no codified iconography exists.

Domains — Jakaira's domain is the atmosphere, particularly the mist, fog, and clouds that bring rain and moisture to the earth. He dwells in the sky but is intimately connected to the earth, as his mist descends to nourish the land. He is also associated with the east, where the sun rises and the morning mist often gathers.

Accounts I
The Creation of the WorldVarious Guarani myths, particularly among the Mbyá and Chiripá.

Pre-Columbian to present (oral tradition)

In the beginning, the supreme god Tupã created the world with the help of four divine beings, one of whom was Jakaira. Each being was assigned a domain: Jakaira received the mist and atmospheric waters. Together, they shaped the earth, established the cardinal points, and set the cosmic order. Jakaira's mist was spread over the land to bring fertility and to veil the sacred from the profane. The world was established with a delicate balance of elements, and Jakaira's mist became a symbol of the divine presence on earth.

Accounts II
Jakaira and the First HumansEthnographic accounts of Mbyá Guarani.

Oral tradition

After the creation, Jakaira helped to shape the first humans from clay and mist. He breathed the fog into them, giving them the breath of life and connecting them to the atmospheric realm. This act established the bond between humans and the mist, which is why shamans can see visions in fog. Humans gained a spiritual connection to the mist, and mist became a medium for divine communication.
