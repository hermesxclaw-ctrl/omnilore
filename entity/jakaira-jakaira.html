Who They Are

Jakaira is a major Guarani deity, the god of mist, fog, good winds, spring, and the life-giving moisture that brings the earth back to life after the dry season. He is the brother of Tupã (the thunder god) and Guaracy (the sun god), and together these three brothers represent the primary forces of the natural world. Jakaira's domain — the gentle, life-giving mist — stands in contrast to Tupã's powerful thunder and Guaracy's burning sun.

Cultural Significance

Jakaira's association with yerba mate makes him a particularly significant deity in modern South American culture. Yerba mate is consumed daily by millions of people in Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay, and southern Brazil, and the ritual of drinking mate (the 'mateada') is a deeply ingrained social custom. The Guarani tradition that Jakaira created the yerba mate plant as a gift to humanity is a foundational myth of this cultural practice. Jakaira also represents the more gentle, nurturing aspects of nature in Guarani religion — a balance to the more fearsome divine forces.
