Who It Is

In the neon-lit underworld of South Town, a crime lord with a martial artist's soul rules with an iron fist and a flawless counter. Geese Howard, the man who murdered Jeff Bogard and set the stage for the Fatal Fury saga, is a figure of calculated ambition and tragic pride. His story is one of power, betrayal, and a final fall that he chose himself.

Geese is arrogant, ruthless, and calculating. He is a crime boss who values power and control above all. He is also a martial arts genius, having mastered the martial art of Aiki Jujutsu. Despite his villainy, he shows a twisted sense of honor and pride. He refuses to be pitied or to accept help, as seen in his death scene where he rejects Terry's outstretched hand. He is also a father to Rock Howard, though he initially abandoned him.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Geese' is derived from a boss character of the same name in SNK's earlier game The Super Spy (1990). 'Howard' is a common English surname, chosen to reflect his American background.

Pronunciation — Gees How-ard (English); Gī-su Ha-wā-do (Japanese)

Aliases — Nightmare Geese, ギース・ハワード (Gīsu Hawādo)

Ownership — SNK (formerly SNK Playmore). The character is a creation of SNK and appears primarily in their franchises.

Aiki Jujutsu

Geese's primary fighting style, focusing on countering and throwing opponents. He can parry attacks and respond with devastating grabs.

Limit: Requires precise timing; some attacks are punishable if whiffed.

Rashomon

A powerful counter move where Geese catches an incoming attack and delivers a brutal throw.

Limit: Only works against certain attack types.

Shippuken

A projectile attack where Geese throws a sphere of energy.

Limit: Can be jumped over or blocked.

Raging Storm

A super move that creates a large energy storm around Geese, damaging opponents.

Limit: Has a long startup and can be avoided.

Deadly Rave

A series of rapid strikes that can be followed by a final blow.

Limit: Requires a long combo to execute fully.

Nightmare Geese's spectral powers

As Nightmare Geese, he gains a ghostly appearance and can use purple flames, possibly enhancing his attacks.

Limit: Not fully explained; may be a manifestation of his will.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Geese is a tall, muscular man of German-American descent. In his classic appearance, he has short, slicked-back blond hair and a stern face. He typically wears a white uwagi (jacket) and red hakama (pleated trousers), sometimes with the uwagi removed and worn around his waist. In The King of Fighters XIV, he appears topless but retains the red hakama. His 'Nightmare Geese' form has silver hair, rotten skin, and purple flames. In The King of Fight

Symbols — Geese is associated with the 'Howard Connection,' his criminal organization. His symbol is often a stylized eagle or a 'G' monogram. He is also associated with the Rashomon and Shippuken attacks, which are iconic to his fighting style.

Domains — Geese operates primarily in South Town, a fictional city in the United States. He rules the city's underworld from his headquarters, the Geese Tower. He also has connections in Japan and other locations, as seen in The King of Fighters series.

Accounts I
The Murder of Jeff BogardFatal Fury: King of Fighters backstory

Before the events of Fatal Fury

Geese and Jeff Bogard were both students of Tung Fu Rue. Geese became jealous when Tung chose Jeff as his successor. In a fit of rage, Geese murdered Jeff in front of his adopted sons, Terry and Andy. Terry and Andy vow revenge and train to defeat Geese. This sets the plot of the Fatal Fury series.

The King of Fighters TournamentFatal Fury: King of Fighters

1991

Geese hosts a fighting tournament to lure the Bogard brothers. Terry and Andy enter and fight their way through, eventually confronting Geese. Terry defeats Geese, who falls from the top of Geese Tower. Geese is presumed dead, but survives and returns in later games.

The Death of GeeseReal Bout Fatal Fury

1995

In the final battle, Terry defeats Geese. As Geese hangs from a ledge, Terry offers his hand, but Geese refuses and lets go, falling to his death. Geese dies, but later returns as Nightmare Geese. His son Rock Howard appears in Garou: Mark of the Wolves.

Accounts II
Nightmare GeeseReal Bout Fatal Fury Special, Maximum Impact 2, The King of Fighters series

1997 onwards

After his death, Geese appears as a spectral entity known as Nightmare Geese. He is a manifestation of his hatred and pride, often appearing as a hidden boss or a playable character. Nightmare Geese serves as a recurring antagonist, allowing Geese to appear in games set after his death.

The King of Fighters '96The King of Fighters '96

1996

Geese, along with Mr. Big and Wolfgang Krauser, forms the 'Boss Team' and enters the tournament. They are defeated by the heroes, but Geese survives. Geese continues to appear in later The King of Fighters games.

Tekken 7Tekken 7 DLC

2017

Geese appears as a guest character in Tekken 7, crossing over into the Tekken universe. He is involved in the King of Iron Fist Tournament, seeking to test his strength. Geese's appearance in Tekken 7 was well-received, leading to his inclusion in later promotions.
