The Approach

In the neon-lit underworld of South Town, a crime lord with a martial artist's soul rules with an iron fist and a flawless counter. Geese Howard, the man who murdered Jeff Bogard and set the stage for the Fatal Fury saga, is a figure of calculated ambition and tragic pride. His story is one of power, betrayal, and a final fall that he chose himself.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of violence, murder, and criminal activity. Some storylines involve revenge and death. No explicit sexual content.
Identity

Geese Howard

Pronunciation Gees How-ard (English); Gī-su Ha-wā-do (Japanese)

Known As

Nightmare Geeseギース・ハワード (Gīsu Hawādo)

Etymology

The name 'Geese' is derived from a boss character of the same name in SNK's earlier game The Super Spy (1990). 'Howard' is a common English surname, chosen to reflect his American background.

Cultural Ownership

SNK (formerly SNK Playmore). The character is a creation of SNK and appears primarily in their franchises.

Disputed Identifications

There is no significant dispute about his identity, but some fans confuse him with other SNK bosses like Wolfgang Krauser or Mr. Big. His 'Nightmare Geese' persona is a distinct version, not a true resurrection.

Description

Appearance

Geese is a tall, muscular man of German-American descent. In his classic appearance, he has short, slicked-back blond hair and a stern face. He typically wears a white uwagi (jacket) and red hakama (pleated trousers), sometimes with the uwagi removed and worn around his waist. In The King of Fighters XIV, he appears topless but retains the red hakama. His 'Nightmare Geese' form has silver hair, rotten skin, and purple flames. In The King of Fighters XV, he wears a black business suit, emphasizing his crime lord status.

Symbols & Iconography

Geese is associated with the 'Howard Connection,' his criminal organization. His symbol is often a stylized eagle or a 'G' monogram. He is also associated with the Rashomon and Shippuken attacks, which are iconic to his fighting style.

Behaviour & Personality

Geese is arrogant, ruthless, and calculating. He is a crime boss who values power and control above all. He is also a martial arts genius, having mastered the martial art of Aiki Jujutsu. Despite his villainy, he shows a twisted sense of honor and pride. He refuses to be pitied or to accept help, as seen in his death scene where he rejects Terry's outstretched hand. He is also a father to Rock Howard, though he initially abandoned him.

Domains & Habitats

Geese operates primarily in South Town, a fictional city in the United States. He rules the city's underworld from his headquarters, the Geese Tower. He also has connections in Japan and other locations, as seen in The King of Fighters series.

Version Differences

Different games portray Geese at different ages and stages. In Art of Fighting 2, he is a young man with long blond hair and a purple suit. In the Fatal Fury series, he is a middle-aged man in his 30s. In The King of Fighters XIV, he is older but still formidable. Nightmare Geese is a spectral version that appears after his death. In Tekken 7, he is a guest character with a moveset adapted to that game's mechanics.

Stories & Myths

The Murder of Jeff Bogard

Source Fatal Fury: King of Fighters backstoryCulture Video gamePeriod Before the events of Fatal Fury

Summary

Geese and Jeff Bogard were both students of Tung Fu Rue. Geese became jealous when Tung chose Jeff as his successor. In a fit of rage, Geese murdered Jeff in front of his adopted sons, Terry and Andy.

Participants

Geese Howard

Jeff Bogard

Terry Bogard

Andy Bogard

Consequences

Terry and Andy vow revenge and train to defeat Geese. This sets the plot of the Fatal Fury series.

Variants

The exact circumstances are not detailed in the games, but it is consistently depicted as a murder.

The King of Fighters Tournament

Source Fatal Fury: King of FightersCulture Video gamePeriod 1991

Summary

Participants

Geese Howard

Terry Bogard

Various fighters

The Death of Geese

Source Real Bout Fatal FuryCulture Video gamePeriod 1995

In the final battle, Terry defeats Geese. As Geese hangs from a ledge, Terry offers his hand, but Geese refuses and lets go, falling to his death.
