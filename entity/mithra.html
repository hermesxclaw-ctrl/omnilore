Who It Is

Mithra (Miθra in Avestan, Mehr in Middle Persian) is one of the most significant and complex deities of the ancient Iranian religious world—a god of covenants, truth, the sun, judgment, and warfare whose worship spanned from India (as the Vedic Mitra) through Iran to the Roman Empire (as Mithras). In the Zoroastrian hierarchy, Mithra is a yazata—a 'worship-worthy spirit' created by Ahura Mazda—and one of the most powerful among them. The great Mihr Yasht (Hymn to Mithra), one of the longest and most beautiful texts in the Avesta, describes him as a vigilant, all-seeing deity with ten thousand eyes and ten thousand ears who traverses the world after sunset, observing all human actions and bringing covenant-breakers and liars to justice. He is the guardian of mithra—the sacred contract, promise, or covenant (the word mithra literally means 'that which binds' or 'treaty'). As such, he is simultaneously a solar deity (bringing all things to light), a warrior deity (arming himself for battle against those who violate oaths), and a judge (punishing the deceitful). Mithra rides a gleaming chariot drawn by four white horses, and his primary weapons are a mace and a spear. In pre-Zoroastrian Iranian religion, Mithra likely held a position comparable to the Vedic Mitra as one of the supreme gods alongside Ahura Mazda and Apam Napat. In Zoroastrianism, he was subordinated to Ahura Mazda but retained immense importance. His cult gave rise to the Roman mystery religion of Mithraism, which spread throughout the empire between the 1st and 4th centuries CE, particularly among soldiers. While scholarly debate continues about the relationship between Persian Mithra and Roman Mithras, the tauroctony (bull-slaying) scene central to Mithraic iconography has deep roots in Iranian cosmological sacrifice narratives. Mithra is also the deity associated with the seventh month (Mehr) and the sixteenth day of each month in the Zoroastrian calendar, and the festival of Mehregan, celebrating the harvest and the bonds of love and friendship, is dedicated to him.
