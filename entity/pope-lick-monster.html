Who It Is

The Pope Lick Monster is one of the most dangerous cryptid legends in America -- not because the creature itself is real, but because the location associated with it has claimed real lives. The legend centers on a large railroad trestle spanning Pope Lick Creek near Louisville, Kentucky, where a creature described as part-man, part-goat or part-sheep is said to reside. The monster is reported to use hypnotic powers or mimicry to lure victims onto the trestle, where they are either killed by the creature or -- more commonly -- by an oncoming train. The trestle is a working railroad bridge with no pedestrian walkways; the legend has tragically led to multiple deaths of curious teenagers and thrill-seekers attempting to cross it. The creature itself is described with varying morphology: some accounts depict it as a classic satyr-like figure with a goat's lower body and a man's upper body, others as a grotesque hybrid with a sheep's head, and still others as a shape-shifter. Its name derives from Pope Lick Creek, though 'lick' in this context refers to a salt lick (a natural mineral deposit), not the verb. The legend appears to date to at least the 1940s and may be connected to stories of a circus train derailment that released a 'freak show' creature into the wild, or to the broader Goatman tradition of the eastern United States. Local authorities have repeatedly warned against trespassing on the trestle, which remains active. The Pope Lick Monster has been featured in numerous documentaries, paranormal shows, and a 1988 independent film.
