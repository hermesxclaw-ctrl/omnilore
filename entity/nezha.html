Who It Is

Nezha (Nézhā) is one of the most beloved and dynamic figures in Chinese mythology—a childlike warrior deity of immense power whose rebellious spirit and fiery temper are matched only by his fierce loyalty to the celestial order. His origins trace back to Buddhist scripture, where Nalakuvara (or Nalakubara) appears as a yaksha guardian, but his character was thoroughly sinicized through Taoist mythology and the great Ming dynasty novels 'Investiture of the Gods' (Fengshen Yanyi) and 'Journey to the West.' The most famous version of his origin tells that Nezha was born to Li Jing, a military commander, after his mother carried him in her womb for three years and six months. He emerged not as an infant but as a ball of flesh, which his father split open with his sword to reveal a fully formed child. The immortal Taiyi Zhenren took him as a disciple and bestowed upon him powerful weapons: the Wind Fire Wheels (allowing him to fly), the Universe Ring (a golden ring worn around his body), the Red Armillary Sash (a silk ribbon that binds enemies), and the Fire-tipped Spear. As a child, Nezha's recklessness led to a catastrophic conflict with the Dragon King Ao Guang of the Eastern Sea, culminating in Nezha slaying the Dragon King's son. To spare his family from the Dragon King's wrath, Nezha committed filial suicide by returning his flesh and bones to his parents. Taiyi Zhenren later resurrected him using lotus roots to construct a new, indestructible body. Nezha remains a popular protective deity, especially invoked by children and in exorcism rituals. His character has experienced a massive resurgence in modern Chinese popular culture through animated films, video games, and television series.
