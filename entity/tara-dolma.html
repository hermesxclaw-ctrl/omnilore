Who It Is

Tara (Tibetan: Dölma, meaning 'She Who Liberates') is the most beloved female deity in Tibetan Buddhism, a fully enlightened Buddha who manifests in the form of a female bodhisattva of compassion and protection. According to the most famous origin story, Tara was born from a tear of Avalokiteshvara, the Bodhisattva of Compassion. When she attained enlightenment, certain monks suggested she should be reborn as a male to reach full Buddhahood. She replied: 'There is no man and no woman — I will remain in female form until all beings reach liberation.' She is the 'Swift Liberator' — the deity who answers prayers faster than any other. Tara manifests in 21 primary forms, the most important being White Tara (Sitatara), who symbolizes purity, long life, and healing, and Green Tara (Khadiravani Tara / Shyam Tara), who symbolizes enlightened activity, protection, and swift assistance. The practice of Tara is widespread across all schools of Tibetan Buddhism. Her mantra, 'Om Tare Tuttare Ture Svaha,' is one of the most commonly recited in the Tibetan Buddhist world. Tara is particularly invoked for protection from the eight great fears (lions, elephants, fire, snakes, thieves, drowning, imprisonment, and demons). In Tibetan iconography, Green Tara sits with one leg extended, ready to rise quickly to help beings, while White Tara sits in meditation posture. Tara is often called 'the Mother of the Buddhas' for her role in nurturing the enlightened qualities in all beings.
