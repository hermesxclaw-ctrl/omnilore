Who It Is

In the vast, shadowed pantheon of Japan, where every mountain, stream, and storm has its keeper, there is a name that appears nowhere in the ancient chronicles — a name that exists only as a whisper in a database, a ghost of a goddess: Adakayanushitakikihime, the High Lady of the Grudges' Grass. She is the deity who may never have been, a princess of cogon grass and vengeance, a figure whose very obscurity makes her the perfect canvas for myth. Is she a forgotten local kami, her shrines crumbled to dust? Or is she a name waiting for a story — a story that you, the reader, might be the first to tell? The Archive of All Things Unreal preserves her record, not because we know who she is, but because we honor the possibility that she might be real.

Unknown. The 'ada' (grudge/enmity) element suggests a deity who may be vengeful or who presides over the resolution of wrongs. In Shinto thought, powerful deities (kami) can be both beneficent and wrathful (araburu kami). A deity of grudges might be invoked for vengeance, for the pacification of wrongs, or for the purification of resentment.

Powers
Presiding over grudges (ada)

If the 'ada' element is interpreted as a domain of power, this deity would control the force of enmity and resentment. This could include the ability to exact vengeance, to pacify vengeful spirits (onryō), or to transform grudges into something constructive.

Limit: Completely unknown; any specific power set is conjecture

Authority over cogon grass (kaya)

As a 'nushi' (master) of cogon grass, the deity might have power over this specific plant, its growth, its use in thatching, and its ritual applications. Cogon grass is used in Shinto purification (harae) and in the construction of shrines (e.g., Ise Grand Shrine's roof).

Limit: Unknown

Exalted status (takaki)

The 'takaki' (high/elevated) element suggests a deity of high rank, possibly one who dwells in the heavens (Takamagahara) or on a sacred mountain. This could confer authority over other deities or a role as an intercessor.

Limit: Unknown

Symbols — Cogon grass (茅, kaya), high ground (高, takaki), possibly a thatched roof or a field of reeds. The color white (for purity) and red (for anger) might be associated, though this is speculative.

Domains — Hypothetically: vengeance, grudges, wrongs, cogon grass, high places, thatched dwellings, purification of resentment, karmic balance.
