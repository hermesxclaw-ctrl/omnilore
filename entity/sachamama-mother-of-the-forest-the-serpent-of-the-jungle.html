Who It Is

Sachamama ('Sacha' = forest, 'Mama' = mother) is the great forest-spirit of the Amazon — the mythic serpent-boas that embodies the raw, untamed power of the primary rainforest. She is the counterpart to Yacumama (the river-mother) and together they form the serpentine dualism of Amazonian hydrology: the rivers (Yacumama) and the forest (Sachamama) are two aspects of one living, breathing serpentine cosmos. Sachamama is described as an enormous boa constrictor that lives in the most remote parts of the jungle, where the canopy is so thick that sunlight never reaches the floor. She is so large that her body appears like a fallen log or a ridge of earth covered with moss and leaves; her head is hidden underground or in the treetops. She guards the wild animals (the masters of the game — the 'owners' of prey species) and the sacred medicinal plants (ayahuasca vines, chacruna, tobacco). The deep stillness of the jungle at noon, when the animals are silent, is said to be the hush of Sachamama's presence.
