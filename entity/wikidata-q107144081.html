The Approach

In the twilight of the Roman Republic, a philosopher's quiet battle over a crumbling wall in Athens became a testament to the enduring power of an ancient school's legacy. Patro, the head of the Epicurean garden, found himself pitted against the ambition of a Roman noble, and his plea for preservation echoes through the letters of Cicero.

The name Patro (Πάτρων) is a Greek name, possibly derived from πατήρ (father) or πατρός (of a father), but its specific meaning in this context is not documented.

Behaviour & Personality

Patro appears as a devoted guardian of Epicurus's legacy, willing to solicit powerful Roman friends to protect the school's property. He was evidently respected by Cicero and Atticus, who interceded on his behalf.

Domains & Habitats

He lived in Rome for a time, then moved to Athens, where he became head of the Epicurean school.

Stories & Myths

The Affair of the Wall

Source Cicero's letters to Atticus and MemmiusCulture Roman and GreekPeriod c. 70 BC

Summary

Gaius Memmius, while in Athens, obtained permission from the Areopagus to demolish an old wall on the property left by Epicurus for his school. Patro, as head of the school, viewed this as desecration and appealed to Atticus and Cicero for help. Cicero, arriving in Athens after Memmius had left, wrote to Memmius urging him to abandon the project.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

The exact dates of Patro's birth and death are unknown. His philosophical teachings are not recorded. The details of his life before becoming head of the school are sparse. The outcome of the wall affair is inferred from Cicero's letter; we do not have Memmius's response. The location of Epicurus's property is not precisely known. Further research might explore archaeological evidence of the Epicurean garden.

Research Scope

This dossier focuses on the historical Epicurean philosopher Patro, head of the school in Athens around 70 BC. It draws primarily on Cicero's letters and the Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography and Mythology. Due to the scarcity of sources, much of Patro's life remains obscure; this research acknowledges the limitations.
