Who It Is

A Pisadeira ('The Stepper') is a terrifying figure in Brazilian folklore, directly linked to the universal phenomenon of sleep paralysis. She is described as a hideous old woman -- thin, with long, dirty fingernails, tangled gray hair, red eyes, and tattered clothing -- who enters homes at night and sits or steps on the chests of sleeping victims, pinning them down and preventing them from moving, breathing, or crying out. The victim is conscious and aware but utterly paralyzed, able only to feel the crushing weight and terror of her presence. The attack typically ends when the victim -- through immense effort -- manages to move a toe or finger, breaking the paralysis. The Pisadeira is said to enter through cracks in the walls, keyholes, or under doors, and she specifically targets those who go to sleep on a full stomach (the legend is often used to warn against eating heavily before bed). The experience aligns precisely with medical accounts of sleep paralysis (isolated sleep paralysis, ISP), in which the body's natural REM atonia persists into wakefulness and is often accompanied by hypnagogic hallucinations -- frequently of a menacing presence sitting on the chest. Cross-culturally, similar entities exist: the 'Old Hag' in Newfoundland folklore, the 'Kanashibari' in Japan, and the incubus/succubus in European tradition. The Pisadeira is thus both a folkloric entity and a culturally specific interpretation of a real, terrifying neurological phenomenon. Brazilian urban legends maintain she appears most often between midnight and 3:00 AM, and protection involves sleeping with a broom behind the door or praying before bed.
