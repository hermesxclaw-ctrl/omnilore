c. 1500 BCE - 1500 CE (longue durée)

In the Rigveda, asuras are powerful lordly beings (e.g., Varuna is called asura). In later Vedic and Puranic literature, asuras become a distinct class opposed to devas/suras, often depicted as powerful, ambitious beings who perform austerities (tapas) to gain boons from Brahma or Shiva, then contend with devas for sovereignty of the three worlds. Stories include the Samudra Manthana (Churning of the Ocean), defeats by Vishnu's avatars, and individual asura kings like Mahabali, Ravana (often classed as rakshasa/asura), and Bhandasura.
