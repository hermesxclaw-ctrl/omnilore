Who It Is

The personification of death and the parched sterility of the summer drought, Mot (lit. 'Death') is an insatiable primordial being whose gaping maw consumes all living things. In the Baal Cycle he defeats the storm-god in a spectacular underground confrontation, forcing Baal to descend as a shade among the dead. Yet Mot's power, however absolute, is not final: Anat's intervention and the seasonal cycle ensure that death is temporary, that Mot himself can be defeated (or at least delayed) each year. The ritualized agricultural imagery of Mot's destruction — threshing, winnowing, grinding — identifies death as a natural process integrated into the cycle of growth.
