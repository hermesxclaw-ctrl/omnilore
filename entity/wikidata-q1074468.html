Who It Is

In the cold breath of the north wind, a name whispered among the snows: Chione, the snow nymph, whose very being is a flurry of myth and mystery. From the icy peaks of Thrace to the starry vault, her story is as multifaceted as a snowflake, touching gods, mortals, and the very elements themselves.

As a mythological figure, her personality is not deeply characterized. She is primarily defined by her interactions with gods (Poseidon, Apollo, Hermes, Boreas) and her tragic fate in some versions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a nymph associated with snow, Chione is typically imagined as a beautiful young woman, pale-skinned, with hair like snow or ice. However, no detailed physical description survives in ancient sources.

Symbols — Snow, winter, cold; in art, she may be depicted with snowflakes or in a snowy landscape, though no definitive ancient iconography is known.

Domains — Associated with snow and cold regions, particularly Thrace and Mount Parnassus, where her father Daedalion ruled.

Accounts I
Chione, daughter of BoreasHyginus, Fabulae; possibly other sources

Classical antiquity

Chione, daughter of Boreas (the North Wind) and Oreithyia, was the mother of Eumolpus by Poseidon. To hide her pregnancy, she threw the child into the sea, but Poseidon saved him and raised him in Ethiopia. Eumolpus became a legendary Thracian king and founder of the Eleusinian Mysteries.

Chione, daughter of CallirrhoeOvid, Metamorphoses 11.301

Augustan era

Chione, daughter of Callirrhoe, was a nymph who was raped by Hermes and Apollo. In anger, Zeus struck her with a thunderbolt, and Hermes turned her into a snow cloud. Her transformation into a snow cloud explains the origin of snow.

Accounts II
Chione, daughter of ArcturusHyginus, Fabulae; possibly others

Classical antiquity

Chione, daughter of Arcturus, was abducted by Boreas and bore him three sons. The details are sparse. She became the mother of three sons by Boreas.

Chione, daughter of DaedalionOvid, Metamorphoses 11.301; Hyginus, Fabulae 200

Augustan era

Chione, daughter of Daedalion, was a mortal princess of Phocis. She was loved by both Apollo and Hermes. Apollo disguised himself as an old woman and slept with her, and Hermes put her to sleep and raped her. She bore twins: Philammon (by Apollo) and Autolycus (by Hermes). In her pride, she claimed she was more beautiful than Artemis, and the goddess shot her with an arrow. Her death caused her father Daedalion to throw himself from a cliff, but Apollo turned him into a hawk.
