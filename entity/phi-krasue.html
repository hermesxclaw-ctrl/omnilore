Phi Krasue

The Floating Ghost, The Lady of the Swinging Intestines, The Village Vampire

She is the beauty and the beast joined at the neck — a woman's face above, a trail of entrails below. She did not choose to be this way.

Who It Is

Phi Krasue is one of the most feared spirits in Thai folklore — a floating female ghost that appears as a beautiful woman's head with her internal organs (stomach, intestines, heart) dangling beneath it. She drifts at night, hovering just above the ground, her entrails trailing behind her like grotesque tentacles. By day, she appears as a normal woman, but one who is under a curse — often the result of a past life of witchcraft, evil deeds, or simply genetic inheritance (the condition is sometimes believed to run in families). Phi Krasue feeds on blood, raw meat, animal entrails, and human feces. She is especially dangerous to pregnant women, whose unborn children she devours. The only protection is to surround oneself with thorny bushes or sharp objects, since her entrails can be torn on thorns. The belief is widespread not only in Thailand but also in neighboring Cambodia (where she is called Krasue or Ap), Laos, and Malaysia (where she is known as Penanggalan). Despite the gruesome nature of the legend, Phi Krasue is sometimes treated with a measure of sympathy — she is a cursed soul who did not choose her monstrous existence. She remains a staple of Thai horror films and television.
