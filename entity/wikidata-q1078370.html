Who It Is

Two Zettons haunt Japanese pop culture under the same katakana — ゼットン — one a faceless insectoid that ended the original Ultraman, the other a faceless beatmaker who built Fukuoka's underground hip-hop from a private studio called Lb12. Both are final bosses in their own domains, both are deliberately stylized in all-caps as ZETTON, and the internet constantly collapses them into one.

Producer: Attested behavior is that of an independent producer-executive: studio builder (2004), label co-founder (2007 with rapper Rowshi), remixer and beatmaker for both underground (Rowshi, Brown Sugar) and major J-Pop/R&B acts. Known for instrumental remix series (The Beat Maker, Mo Better EP) reworking US hip-hop/R&B acapellas (M.O.P., Talib Kweli, Common, Nas, The Pharcyde) for Japanese market, and for low-profile release strategy (R.O.B dropped without announcement in 2016). Personality not publicly documented via interviews; reputation in publisher listings is as a craftsman

Powers
Sampling and Remix Production (The Beat Maker method)

Construction of beats by chopping and re-sequencing US hip-hop/R&B acapellas and instrumentals, layering drums, bass, and melodic loops. Attested on The Beat Maker series remixing M.O.P., Talib Kweli, Anthony Hamilton, The Game, Common (2006) and Mo Better EPs remixing The Beatnuts, The Pharcyde, Kool G Rap, Nas (2007-2008).

Limit: Dependent on clearance of source acapellas; unofficial remixes sold as mixtape/EP culture, not always licensed for wide distribution. Limited to instrumental reinterpretation, not original vocal performance.

Executive Production and Artist Development

Full album/single production for Rowshi and Brown Sugar (hit records), plus track production for major artists: Nitro Microphone Underground, HI-D, JUJU, Kana Nishino, Miliyah Kato, SUPER JUNIOR-K.R.Y., EXILE ATSUSHI, GENERATIONS from EXILE TRIBE, Sandaime J SOUL BROTHERS. Role includes beat provision, arrangement, studio direction at Lb12.

Limit: Credits are track-specific, not whole-album takeovers; exact track titles require liner-note verification per release.

Studio Engineering (Studio Lb12)

Operation of private studio built 2004, used for recording, mixing, and mastering of Inside Muzic releases and external commissions. Provides controlled acoustic environment and analog/digital hybrid workflow typical of 2000s Japanese hip-hop.

Limit: Facility is private, not commercial rental studio; output limited to Inside Muzic network.

1 Trillion Degree Fireball (Ichi Chō Do Kasen)

Zetton's signature attack: a large fireball emitted from the circular facial orifice, stated in official data to reach one trillion degrees Celsius. In narrative, it is sufficient to down Ultraman and destroy military weaponry. Visual is a glowing orange-red sphere with trailing flame.

Limit: Requires charging pose (arms raised); can be interrupted before release in some later depictions. Barrier must be lowered to fire in original version.

Barrier / Reflective Shield

Hexagonal energy barrier that absorbs and reflects Ultraman's Specium Ray and other beam attacks. In Episode 39, Ultraman's Specium Ray is reflected back, contributing to his defeat.

Limit: Barrier is directional and temporary; physical attacks (Science Patrol's experimental weapon in 1967) bypass it. In some games, barrier has cooldown.

Teleportation and Hypersonic Movement

Instantaneous disappearance and reappearance, used to dodge attacks. Original Zetton teleports short distances on battlefield; Hyper Zetton teleports across dimensions.

Limit: Original use is short-range and not spammed; later versions have extended range but require energy.

Physical Strength and Durability

60m, 30,000t body with exoskeleton resistant to conventional weapons, tanks, and Ultraman's physical blows. Can swat Ultraman and withstand explosions.

Limit: Vulnerable to specially developed weapons (Science Patrol's new experimental rifle/ray in 1967) and to Ultraman's later successors.

Accounts I
1996: Emergence in Fukuoka UndergroundInside Muzic biography; Nichion profile

1996-2003

Zetton begins career in 1996 as 'Zetton' in Fukuoka's underground hip-hop scene, a regional hub distinct from Tokyo/Osaka. No major releases from this period are catalogued in public discographies, indicating a formative period of beatmaking, DJing, and local collaborations that built reputation before studio ownership. This underground origin is consistently cited as his artistic identity. Establishes credibility and network that later enables Studio Lb12 and Inside Muzic; positions him as regional scene builder rather than Tokyo-import.

2004: Construction of Studio Lb12Inside Muzic biography timeline

2004

Zetton builds his own studio, Studio Lb12 (Lab 12), giving him autonomous recording, mixing, and production capacity. The move from renting studios to owning a facility marks transition from beatmaker to executive producer and enables the high volume of 2006-2008 remix EPs. Enables independent production for Rowshi, Brown Sugar, and later major-label commissions without major-label studio dependency.

2007: Establishment of Takaki Shoten / Inside Muzic with RowshiInside Muzic corporate history

2007

With rapper Rowshi, Zetton establishes Takaki Shoten/Inside-Muzic as label and management entity, with Zetton as CEO. The imprint becomes the vehicle for his productions and for Rowshi/Brown Sugar's hit records, formalizing the Fukuoka-to-national pipeline. Creates institutional home for discography; explains CEO credit and ability to produce for major artists while remaining independent.

Accounts II
2006-2008: The Beat Maker and Mo Better EP Remix CampaignDiscogs, Juno Records UK listings, CD/LP liner notes

2006-2008

Zetton releases a concentrated series of remix projects: The Beat Maker 001 (2006), R&B remix 002 (2006), 3Maeme Remix 003 (2006), 004 (2006), da beat breaker 005 (2007) on CD, and Mo Better EP 001 (2007), 002 Common Remixes (2007), 003 with Bach Logic (2008), 004 with Smith (2008) on 12-inch vinyl sold via Juno Records UK. Source acapellas include M.O.P., Talib Kweli, Anthony Hamilton, The Game, Common, The Beatnuts, The Pharcyde, Kool G Rap, Nas. The vinyl EPs target international DJ market. Builds international DJ credibility and demonstrates technical sampling skill; vinyl distribution via UK indicates niche global reach.

2016: Beatz Hustler and R.O.B — Instrumental AlbumsInside Muzic release notes; retailer listings (HMV Japan, Tower Records)

2016

After a relative quiet period, Zetton releases two instrumental albums in same year: Beatz Hustler (announced) and R.O.B (7th instrumental album, released without announcement). Both are beat-focused, no vocalists, showcasing mature production style after a decade of executive work. Marks return to artist-producer identity beyond behind-the-scenes role; R.O.B's stealth drop becomes a noted anecdote in his catalogue.

1967: Farewell, Ultraman — Zetton Defeats UltramanUltraman Episode 39 'Farewell, Ultraman' (TBS/Tsuburaya, 1967-04-09)

Shōwa tokusatsu, 1967

Alien Zetton deploys Space Dinosaur Zetton to Earth as final weapon against the Science Special Search Party and Ultraman. Zetton withstands all military attacks, reflects Ultraman's Specium Ray with its barrier, and beats Ultraman into defeat (Color Timer extinguished). The Science Patrol, using a new experimental weapon developed by Dr. Iwamoto, finally destroys Zetton, but Ultraman is left near death until rescued by Zoffy. The episode ends the original series. Establishes Zetton as the only kaiju to definitively defeat Ultraman in the original series, cementing its status as 'strongest monster' in franchise lore and ensuring perpetual returns.

1971: Zetton II vs Ultraman JackReturn of Ultraman Episode 51 'The Ultimate Weapon of the Ultra Race' (1971)

1971

A second individual, Zetton II, is deployed by Alien Bat to attack Earth. Ultraman Jack (Hideki Go) battles it, initially struggling against its fireball and barrier, but ultimately destroys it with a modified Specium Ray after learning from the original battle. This establishes Zettons as a species/weapon type, not a unique individual. Retcons Zetton from singular ultimate monster to mass-produced weapon, enabling reuse without diminishing original's feat.

Zetton the producer is named after a mythical Japanese beast from folklore — Producer name matches the tokusatsu kaiju Zetton (1966), a modern science-fiction invention by Tsuburaya Productions, not folklore. The kaiju itself has no folkloric origin; it is not a yōkai or kami. The producer's name is a pop-culture borrowing, not a folklore reference.

Zetton produced entire albums for Kana Nishino, JUJU, EXILE, etc. — Publisher credits show track-level production for those major artists, not full album production. He contributed beats/tracks within larger major-label albums alongside other producers.

Zetton's Mo Better EPs are official remixes commissioned by US artists — They are independent remix EPs using US acapellas, sold via import vinyl (Juno Records UK) in mixtape culture, not commissioned official remixes. No evidence of commission from The Beatnuts, Nas, etc.

Kaiju Zetton breathes fire like a dragon — Zetton emits a fireball from a circular facial organ, not a dragon-like mouth; it also uses a barrier and teleportation, not just flame. The '1 trillion degrees' is a stated setting, not depicted as continuous breath.
