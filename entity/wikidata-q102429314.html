The Approach

Before there were ogres, before there were titans, there were the giants—beings whose bones were said to litter the earth, whose footsteps carved valleys, and whose existence haunted the edges of every culture's map. From the Cyclops of Homer to the Nephilim of Genesis, from the jentilak of the Pyrenees to the Quinametzin of Mesoamerica, the giant is not a single monster but a universal mirror held up to humanity, reflecting our fears of the monstrous, our awe of the natural world, and our fascination with the limits of the human body.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses mythological and folkloric giants, including themes of violence, cannibalism, and death. Some biblical and legendary accounts contain descriptions of warfare and destruction. No explicit graphic content is included.

Etymology

The English word 'giant' is first attested in 1297 in Robert of Gloucester's chronicle, derived from the Ancient Greek 'gigas' (plural 'gigantes'), which is of uncertain origin but associated with the earth-born giants of Greek mythology.

Cultural Ownership

While the term 'giant' has Greek roots, the concept of giant beings is universal, appearing in the mythologies and folklores of cultures worldwide, including Greek, Norse, Celtic, Basque, Armenian, Aztec, and many others.

Disputed Identifications

The identification of specific figures as 'giants' is often disputed. For example, the biblical Nephilim are sometimes translated as 'giants' but are not always depicted as such. Similarly, mythological figures like the Cyclops are sometimes classified as giants, but they have distinct characteristics. There is also debate over whether certain legendary figures, like Paul Bunyan, are true giants or merely exaggerated humans.

Description

Appearance

Giants are typically described as humanoid beings of prodigious size and strength. Their exact appearance varies widely: some are simply larger versions of humans, while others have monstrous features such as multiple heads, one eye, or animalistic traits. In many traditions, they are depicted as ugly and terrifying, but some are handsome and noble, like Hayk.

Symbols & Iconography

Giants are often associated with mountains, large stones, and megalithic structures. They are frequently depicted in art as towering over landscapes or battling heroes. In Christian iconography, they may symbolize pride or rebellion.

Behaviour & Personality

Giants are often portrayed as violent, dim-witted, and gluttonous, sometimes cannibalistic. However, exceptions exist: some giants are wise, friendly, or protective. Basque giants are generally righteous, while some literary giants like the BFG are kind and intelligent.

Domains & Habitats

Giants are said to inhabit remote places: mountains, forests, caves, islands, and sometimes the underworld. They are often connected to the creation of geological features, such as the Giant's Causeway.

Version Differences

There is immense variation across cultures. Greek Gigantes are earth-born and warred with the gods; Norse jötnar are often adversaries of the gods; biblical giants are often descendants of angels; and modern fairy-tale giants are often ogres. Some traditions see giants as ancient beings who predate humans, while others view them as contemporary creatures.

Contradictions & Disputes

Goliath's Height

Position A

Masoretic Text: six cubits and a span (approx. 9-10 feet)

Position A — evidence

Hebrew Bible

Position B

Septuagint and Dead Sea Scrolls: four cubits and a span (approx. 7-8 feet)

Position B — evidence

Greek translation and earlier Hebrew manuscripts

Scholarly Consensus

The shorter height is likely original; the longer may be a later exaggeration.

Nephilim Identity

Position A

Nephilim are giants, as translated in some Bibles

Position A — evidence

Greek Septuagint translates as 'gigantes'

Position B

Nephilim are fallen ones or heroes, not necessarily giants

Position B — evidence

The Hebrew root 'naphal' means 'to fall'

Scholarly Consensus

The exact identity is uncertain; they are often associated with giants in later tradition.

Existence of Giants in History

Position A

Giants are purely mythological

Position A — evidence

Lack of physical evidence for human giants beyond gigantism

Position B

Some ancient cultures believed giants existed based on fossil finds

Position B — evidence

Historical accounts of giant bones, such as those by Josephus

Scholarly Consensus

Fossilized megafauna bones were often misinterpreted as human giants.
