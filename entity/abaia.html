Who It Is

Imagine a lake that is alive, a vast freshwater expanse whose depths are guarded by a colossal eel with a mother's fury. In the islands of Melanesia, the Abaia is no mere monster; it is a living force of nature, a guardian that punishes human greed with drowning waves and unending rain. Its story is a warning whispered across generations: take too much, and the waters themselves will rise against you.

The Abaia is fiercely protective of all creatures in its lake, considering them its children. It is vengeful against those who harm them, using its tail to create waves or summoning rainstorms to flood the land. It is not described as malevolent without provocation, but rather as a guardian that punishes those who disrupt the natural balance.

Powers
Tail Thrashing

The Abaia can create a large wave by thrashing its powerful tail, overwhelming those who try to catch fish from its lake.

Limit: Only effective when the offender is near the lake.

Rain Summoning

The Abaia can cause a great rainstorm, leading to flooding that drowns those who have harmed its creatures.

Limit: The rainstorm occurs after the harm is done, possibly at night.

Guardianship

The Abaia protects all creatures in its lake, considering them its children. This is not a supernatural power per se, but a behavioral trait with magical consequences.

Limit: It only acts when its children are threatened.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Abaia is described as a 'huge' and 'magical' eel, dwelling at the bottom of freshwater lakes. No specific size, color, or distinctive features are given in the source material. It is likely imagined as a giant eel, perhaps similar to a moray or conger eel but adapted to freshwater.

Symbols — No known iconography exists. The Abaia may be symbolized by the eel itself, representing the untamed power of water and the protective spirit of the lake.

Domains — Freshwater lakes in the Fiji, Solomon, and Vanuatu Islands. It is said to dwell at the bottom of these lakes, implying a deep, dark habitat.

Accounts I
The Man Who Discovered the LakeThe source material (likely from Jobes or Rose)

Undetermined (traditional oral legend)

A man discovers a lake full of fish and catches many, unaware that a magic eel lives at the bottom. He returns with his village, and they all catch fish successfully. One woman even grabs the great eel, Abaia, but it escapes. Enraged, Abaia causes a great rain that night, and the lake rises, drowning everyone except an old woman who had not eaten the fish and saved herself in a tree. The village is destroyed by flood; only the old woman survives, likely as a warning to others.

The Warning of the LakeInferred from the source material

Undetermined

The Abaia's role as a guardian is emphasized: it considers all lake creatures its children and will furiously protect them. Anyone who harms them will face the Abaia's wrath, either through a wave or a flood. Offenders are drowned or overwhelmed.

Accounts II
The Old Woman's EscapeThe source material (part of the main story)

Undetermined

An old woman who did not eat the fish survives the flood by climbing a tree. Her survival may imply that she was innocent or had not partaken in the transgression. Her survival serves as a testament to the Abaia's selective punishment.

The Seizing of AbaiaThe source material (part of the main story)

Undetermined

A woman in the village manages to lay hold of the great eel, but it escapes. This act of seizing the eel itself may have provoked its wrath more than the catching of fish. Abaia becomes angry and causes the flood.

The Unknowing DiscoveryThe source material (part of the main story)

Undetermined

The man who first discovers the lake is unaware of the Abaia's presence. His ignorance does not spare him from punishment, suggesting that the Abaia's protection is absolute. His discovery leads to the village's downfall.
