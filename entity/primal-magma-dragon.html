Who It Is

A terrifying dragon whose scales are plates of obsidian and cooling magma, with rivers of molten rock flowing between them. Its breath is a torrent of superheated volcanic gas and liquid stone. Magma dragons dwell in active volcanoes and are among the most destructive of the primal dragons.

Form

Attested: Magma dragons are described as having jet‑black scales through which glowing rivulets of lava run, creating a stark contrast between dark armor and incandescent seams. The membranes of their wings are shot through with veins of heat that shine visibly, giving the impression of a living furnace in flight. Their overall body plan follows the classic Western dragon morphology—quadrupedal, winged, with powerful jaws, claws, and a long tail—but visually distinguished by volcanic coloration and the suggestion that molten rock flows beneath or between their scales. In Pathfinder 2nd Edition art and text, adult magma dragons are depicted as huge, muscular dragons whose bodies can partially merge with or burrow through magma, reinforcing their elemental nature. Reconstructed: While specific sensory details like smell are not explicitly stated, it is reasonable to infer a strong odor of sulfur and scorched stone, and an ambient heat aura, from their fire immunity and magma‑related abilities.

Powers

Fire immunity and cold vulnerability Attested: Across age categories, magma dragons are immune to fire damage and vulnerable to cold. This reflects their deep attunement to magma and elemental fire, making them impervious to ordinary and magical flames while susceptible to cold‑based attacks that can harden or disrupt molten rock. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium Cold damage is more effective against them; game rules specify vulnerability, typically increasing cold damage taken. None noted; both 1E and SRD sources agree on fire immunity and cold vulnerability.

Breath weapon (fire cone) Attested: All magma dragons possess a breath weapon that manifests as a cone of fire, dealing fire damage proportional to age and size. Young adult examples show a 40‑ft cone dealing 10d6 fire damage, with a saving throw to reduce damage. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD (Magma Dragon, Young Adult) Usable at intervals defined by game rules (typically once every 1d4 rounds); subject to saving throws; fire immunity and resistance can mitigate damage. None; fire cone is consistent across sources.

Magma Breath (clinging lava) Attested: Ancient or older magma dragons can replace their standard fire breath with a cone of lava. Damage is initially identical to the fire breath, but the magma clings to targets, dealing half damage each round for 1d3 rounds before cooling and crumbling to dust. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium Limited to three uses per day for ancient or older dragons; only available at higher age categories. None; all SRD‑derived sources describe the same effect.

Magma Tomb Attested: Great wyrm magma dragons can spit a glob of lava at a target within 120 feet, dealing normal breath weapon damage. The magma cools instantly, ceasing damage but entrapping the victim in hardened lava for 3d6 minutes, with specified hardness and hit points. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium Usable once per day; requires the target to fail a saving throw to be entrapped; hardened lava can be broken or destroyed by allies. None; descriptions are mechanically aligned.

Superheated bite Attested: At young age and older, a magma dragon’s bite deals additional fire damage equal to its age category, reflecting the intense heat of its jaws and the molten energy coursing through its body. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium Only applies to bite attacks; requires proximity; fire‑immune targets negate the additional damage. None.

Sorcerous spell‑like abilities (fire magic) Attested: Magma dragons gain spell‑like abilities as they age, focused on fire and battlefield control: burning hands (very young), scorching ray (juvenile), wall of fire (adult), fire shield (old, constant warm shield), delayed blast fireball (ancient, 3/day), and wall of lava (great wyrm, 1/day). These abilities emphasize their mastery of destructive fire magic and environmental manipulation. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium Usage limits vary (at will vs. per day); subject to standard spell rules such as concentration and saving throws. None; tables across SRD sources match.

Damage reduction and spell resistance Attested: As magma dragons age, they gain increasing damage reduction (DR 5/magic at young adult, DR 10/magic at mature adult, DR 15/magic at very old, DR 20/magic at wyrm) and spell resistance. These defenses reflect their hardened scales and resistance to hostile magic. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium DR applies only against non‑magical attacks; spell resistance can be overcome by caster level checks. None.

Frightful presence Attested: Adult magma dragons and older possess frightful presence, an aura that can cause creatures of lower HD to become shaken or frightened when the dragon attacks, charges, or flies overhead. Pathfinder 1E Bestiary 2; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium; Archives of Nethys (2E frightful presence aura) Creatures of sufficient HD or with strong will saves may resist; typically does not affect other dragons or very powerful beings. None; both editions include a fear aura, though mechanics differ.

Magma burrow Attested: In Pathfinder 2E, adult magma dragons have a burrow speed that functions only through magma or molten lava, allowing them to move through such material as if it were earth. Pathfinder 2E Draconic Codex; Archives of Nethys Burrow speed applies only in magma or molten lava; not usable in solid rock or ordinary soil. This ability is specific to 2E; 1E does not explicitly grant magma burrowing, but the difference is system design rather than a lore contradiction.

Pyroclastic Breath (2E) Attested: Adult magma dragons in 2E possess Pyroclastic Breath, a blast of magma dealing fire damage, with recharge tied to critical hits via Draconic Momentum. This is a system‑specific expression of their magma breath concept. Pathfinder 2E Draconic Codex; Archives of Nethys Requires two actions to use; recharges via critical hits or after a set number of rounds; subject to saving throws. Mechanically distinct from 1E magma breath but conceptually aligned; no explicit lore conflict.

Change Shape (humanoid form) Attested: Adult magma dragons can take on the appearance of a Medium humanoid via a polymorph ability, retaining their speed and attack bonuses but altering the damage type of their strikes. This allows them to interact with other beings in disguise or infiltrate societies. Pathfinder 2E Draconic Codex; Archives of Nethys Change Shape affects appearance but not core statistics; does not grant humanoid social skills automatically; subject to concentration. 1E magma dragons are not explicitly given a polymorph ability in core bestiary entries; this is an added 2E feature, not a contradiction.

Accounts

Attested: The Emberen Recess is a volcanic region within the Plane of Fire ruled by a collection of ancient magma dragons. These dragons govern their younger kin and are themselves led by the ancient dragon Caliniaya, described as dangerously insane but occasionally lucid. The Emberen Recess functions as a rare magma dragon polity, contrasting with their usual solitary existence. Salamander slaves guard the borders and mine gemstones for the dragons’ hoards, while massive volcanic fey called dalos are sometimes coerced into serving as lair guardians. The narrative portrays magma dragons as volatile rulers whose dominance is enforced through elemental power and coercive relationships with other planar beings.

Attested: Outside the Emberen Recess, magma dragons are typically solitary, establishing lairs in mountains, underground magma chambers, or volcanic regions. They are temperamental, prone to violent outbursts, and often regarded by other dragons as dangerously unbalanced. Their interactions with other creatures are unpredictable—sometimes attacking without warning, sometimes engaging in conversation—but they rarely feel compelled to justify their actions. In many published and implied scenarios, magma dragons serve as regional tyrants whose presence shapes local geography and politics, controlling access to volcanic resources and threatening nearby settlements.
