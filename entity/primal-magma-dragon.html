Who It Is

Between its jet‑black scales, rivers of molten lava glow like fault lines in a living volcano—a Primal Magma Dragon is less a creature than an eruption given wings, a volatile mind riding the edge between fire and stone.

Attested: Magma dragons are temperamental and prone to violent outbursts. Other dragons regard them as dangerously insane, an assessment that is repeatedly affirmed in descriptive lore. Their moods are unpredictable; observers often cannot tell whether a magma dragon will attack or attempt conversation until it acts. Despite this volatility, magma dragons possess justifications for their actions, though they rarely feel compelled to explain themselves. Powers
Fire immunity and cold vulnerability

Attested: Across age categories, magma dragons are immune to fire damage and vulnerable to cold. This reflects their deep attunement to magma and elemental fire, making them impervious to ordinary and magical flames while susceptible to cold‑based attacks that can harden or disrupt molten rock.

Limit: Cold damage is more effective against them; game rules specify vulnerability, typically increasing cold damage taken.

Breath weapon (fire cone)

Attested: All magma dragons possess a breath weapon that manifests as a cone of fire, dealing fire damage proportional to age and size. Young adult examples show a 40‑ft cone dealing 10d6 fire damage, with a saving throw to reduce damage.

Limit: Usable at intervals defined by game rules (typically once every 1d4 rounds); subject to saving throws; fire immunity and resistance can mitigate damage.

Magma Breath (clinging lava)

Attested: Ancient or older magma dragons can replace their standard fire breath with a cone of lava. Damage is initially identical to the fire breath, but the magma clings to targets, dealing half damage each round for 1d3 rounds before cooling and crumbling to dust.

Limit: Limited to three uses per day for ancient or older dragons; only available at higher age categories.

Magma Tomb

Attested: Great wyrm magma dragons can spit a glob of lava at a target within 120 feet, dealing normal breath weapon damage. The magma cools instantly, ceasing damage but entrapping the victim in hardened lava for 3d6 minutes, with specified hardness and hit points.

Limit: Usable once per day; requires the target to fail a saving throw to be entrapped; hardened lava can be broken or destroyed by allies.

Superheated bite

Attested: At young age and older, a magma dragon’s bite deals additional fire damage equal to its age category, reflecting the intense heat of its jaws and the molten energy coursing through its body.

Limit: Only applies to bite attacks; requires proximity; fire‑immune targets negate the additional damage.

Sorcerous spell‑like abilities (fire magic)

Attested: Magma dragons gain spell‑like abilities as they age, focused on fire and battlefield control: burning hands (very young), scorching ray (juvenile), wall of fire (adult), fire shield (old, constant warm shield), delayed blast fireball (ancient, 3/day), and wall of lava (great wyrm, 1/day). These abilities emphasize their mastery of destructive fire magic and environmental manipulation.

Limit: Usage limits vary (at will vs. per day); subject to standard spell rules such as concentration and saving throws.

Damage reduction and spell resistance

Attested: As magma dragons age, they gain increasing damage reduction (DR 5/magic at young adult, DR 10/magic at mature adult, DR 15/magic at very old, DR 20/magic at wyrm) and spell resistance. These defenses reflect their hardened scales and resistance to hostile magic.

Limit: DR applies only against non‑magical attacks; spell resistance can be overcome by caster level checks.

Frightful presence

Attested: Adult magma dragons and older possess frightful presence, an aura that can cause creatures of lower HD to become shaken or frightened when the dragon attacks, charges, or flies overhead.

Limit: Creatures of sufficient HD or with strong will saves may resist; typically does not affect other dragons or very powerful beings.

Magma burrow

Attested: In Pathfinder 2E, adult magma dragons have a burrow speed that functions only through magma or molten lava, allowing them to move through such material as if it were earth.

Limit: Burrow speed applies only in magma or molten lava; not usable in solid rock or ordinary soil.

Pyroclastic Breath (2E)

Attested: Adult magma dragons in 2E possess Pyroclastic Breath, a blast of magma dealing fire damage, with recharge tied to critical hits via Draconic Momentum. This is a system‑specific expression of their magma breath concept.

Limit: Requires two actions to use; recharges via critical hits or after a set number of rounds; subject to saving throws.

Change Shape (humanoid form)

Attested: Adult magma dragons can take on the appearance of a Medium humanoid via a polymorph ability, retaining their speed and attack bonuses but altering the damage type of their strikes. This allows them to interact with other beings in disguise or infiltrate societies.

Limit: Change Shape affects appearance but not core statistics; does not grant humanoid social skills automatically; subject to concentration.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested: Magma dragons are described as having jet‑black scales through which glowing rivulets of lava run, creating a stark contrast between dark armor and incandescent seams. The membranes of their wings are shot through with veins of heat that shine visibly, giving the impression of a living furnace in flight. Modern fantasy role‑playing (2010s–2020s publications)

Attested: The Emberen Recess is a volcanic region within the Plane of Fire ruled by a collection of ancient magma dragons. These dragons govern their younger kin and are themselves led by the ancient dragon Caliniaya, described as dangerously insane but occasionally lucid. The Emberen Recess functions as a rare magma dragon polity, contrasting with their usual solitary existence. Salamander slaves guard the borders and mine gemstones for the dragons’ hoards, while massive volcanic fey called dalos are sometimes coerced into serving as lair guardians. The narrative portrays magma dragons as volatile rulers whose dominance is enforced through elemental power and coercive relationships with other planar beings. Attested: The Emberen Recess establishes a structured hierarchy among magma dragons, providing a canonical example of collective governance rather than pure solitude. It also introduces themes of slavery and coercion in their interactions with salamanders and dalos, shaping the ethical landscape of planar politics in the Plane of Fire. Magma dragons as solitary volcanic tyrantsBestiary 2 ecology text; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Compendium

Modern fantasy role‑playing

Attested: Outside the Emberen Recess, magma dragons are typically solitary, establishing lairs in mountains, underground magma chambers, or volcanic regions. They are temperamental, prone to violent outbursts, and often regarded by other dragons as dangerously unbalanced. Their interactions with other creatures are unpredictable—sometimes attacking without warning, sometimes engaging in conversation—but they rarely feel compelled to justify their actions. In many published and implied scenarios, magma dragons serve as regional tyrants whose presence shapes local geography and politics, controlling access to volcanic resources and threatening nearby settlements. Reconstructed: In adventure design, such solitary magma dragons often become major antagonists or dangerous neutrals whose defeat or negotiation can change the fate of a region—stopping eruptions, redirecting lava flows, or securing passage through volcanic terrain.
