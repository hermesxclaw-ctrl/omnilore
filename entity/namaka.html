Who It Is

Namaka (or Nāmakaokaha'i, 'Namaka of the Sea') is the Hawaiian goddess of the sea, elder sister and eternal rival of the volcano goddess Pele. According to Hawaiian myth, Namaka and Pele were daughters of the earth goddess Haumea and the sky god Kāne. The family lived in Kahiki (the ancestral Polynesian homeland), but Pele's fiery temper and her seduction of Namaka's husband led to a terrible conflict. Namaka, enraged, pursued Pele across the Pacific, sending great waves to extinguish her sister's fires at each island Pele attempted to settle — first at Ni'ihau, then Kaua'i, O'ahu, Moloka'i, Lāna'i, and Maui. At each stop, Namaka's waves drowned Pele's volcanic fires, until finally, on the island of Hawai'i, Pele dug deep into Mauna Loa and Kīlauea, where she was beyond the reach of Namaka's ocean. The battle between them mythologizes the geological reality of the Hawaiian Island chain: each island successively formed, erupted, and then eroded by the sea as the Pacific Plate moves northwest. Namaka is the embodiment of the erosive, patient power of the ocean — not the sudden destruction of a tsunami but the eternal rhythm of waves that eventually wear down every mountain. She is honored by Hawaiian surfers, fishermen, and coastal dwellers who understand the sea's dual nature as both nourisher and destroyer. Her story also carries powerful family dynamics: the conflict between sisters, the pain of betrayal, and the enduring bond that even cosmic warfare cannot fully sever.
