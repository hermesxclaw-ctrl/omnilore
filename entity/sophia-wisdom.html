Who It Is

Sophia (σοφία, 'Wisdom') is the most tragic and consequential aeon in the Valentinian Pleroma — the youngest of the 30 aeons whose transgressive desire to know the Father directly (an attempt proper only to Nous) precipitates the entire cosmogonic and soteriological drama of Gnosticism. In her passion, Sophia 'reaches beyond her measure,' producing a defective, formless offspring (the Enthymesis or 'thought-formation') that is cast out of the Plerona. This abortive substance — the 'Sophia Achamoth' or Lower Wisdom — becomes the raw material from which the Demiurge fashions the material world in a flawed imitation of the Pleromatic perfection. Sophia herself, having repented and been purified, is restored to her syzygy (her consort Theletos, 'Will'). But the damage is done: the material cosmos is the 'afterbirth' of her transgression, and all creation is shot through with the longing for reunion with the Pleroma that characterizes her repentance. Sophia thus embodies both the Gnostic critique of the material world (as the product of error) and the Gnostic hope of redemption (as the pattern of return).
