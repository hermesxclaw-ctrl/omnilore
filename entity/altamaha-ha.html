The Altamaha runs deep and dark through the heart of Georgia. Something moves in its waters that the Creek people knew about long before the white man came. Call it Altamaha-ha. Call it a monster. But it has been seen by too many to ignore.

Who It Is

The Altamaha-ha (or 'Altie') is a cryptid reported from the Altamaha River in southeastern Georgia -- one of the largest undammed rivers on the East Coast of the United States. The creature is described as a 20 to 30 foot aquatic animal with a horizontal tail like a dolphin or manatee, a long neck, a head resembling a snake or alligator, and a gray-brown, smooth-skinned body. The Creek people had traditions of a great water serpent inhabiting the river long before European arrival. The first European-documented sighting dates to 1830, when lumbermen near Darien, Georgia reported a large creature in the river. Sightings have continued sporadically into the 21st century, with notable reports in 1935 (a Boy Scout troop), 1969 (two police officers), 1980 (a fisherman who photographed what appeared to be a long-necked creature), and 2002 (a man who captured video footage of a large dark shape moving against the current). The creature has been variously described as a surviving zeuglodon (prehistoric whale), a giant manatee or sea cow, an alligator gar, or a misidentified pod of dolphins or manatees that occasionally enter the river from the Atlantic. The Georgia legislature has recognized Altie as a cultural icon. The river's depth (up to 50 feet in places) and its maze of tributaries and swamps provide ample habitat for a large aquatic animal.
