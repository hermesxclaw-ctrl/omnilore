The Approach

In the misted valley of Kangleipak, where the Loktak waters reflect Thangjing Hill and every clan remembers its heroes, a name surfaces on a database with almost nothing else attached: Nugire Yainu, heroic-legend. No epic chapter, no temple mural, no film credit explains who he — or she — was, what battle was fought, or why the memory persisted. The silence itself is the story: a reminder that Manipur's heroic archive is vast, largely oral, partly burned in 1729, and still only half-translated for the outside world.

Puya Meithaba - Destruction of Manuscripts (1729)

Source Parratt & Parratt (1997); Kabui (1991) pp. 210-215

Description

Under King Pamheiba (Garib Niwaz) during Hinduization, many puyas were burned, causing irretrievable loss of pre-Hindu Meitei texts. This colonial-like internal cultural suppression affects ability to verify Nugire Yainu's original form and may have led to sanitized Hinduized recensions if narrative survived.

Scholarly Context

Viewed as religious and cultural trauma; modern Sanamahi revival movement seeks to reconstruct lost texts, with debates over authenticity of reconstructed puyas
