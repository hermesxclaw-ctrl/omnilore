The Approach

In Japanese the word is small and sharp — nezumi — a flick of whiskers in the dark. It means rat and mouse at once, with no need to choose between them, and that ambiguity has allowed it to slip through centuries of stories: a folk-hero thief who robbed samurai mansions and was celebrated in kabuki, a scarred boy who sings Shakespeare in a dystopian city, a clan of outcast ratfolk who remember what the kami forgot, and a corrupt Marine captain who counts bribes in an occupied fishing village. One name, many shadows.

Content advisory. General audiences with notes for specific variants: Nezumi Kozō lore includes historical crime, execution by crucifixion/beheading, and romanticized outlaw narratives; No. 6 includes dystopian state violence, child abandonment, murder, implied sexual content involving minors (teen characters), and disease/pandemic themes; Magic: The Gathering Nezumi lore includes body horror, war, and spiritual corruption; One Piece Nezumi includes corruption and extortion. No explicit sexual violence attested in primary sources for the name itself.
Identity

Nezumi

Pronunciation Japanese [nezɯmi] — ne-zu-mi, three morae, pitch accent: heiban (LHH) in standard Tokyo Japanese, often rendered neh-zoo-mee in English. Hepburn: nezumi.

Etymology

Japanese 鼠, Old Japanese *nesumi, from Proto-Japonic. Written with kanji 鼠 meaning 'rat; mouse; rodent.' The language historically does not lexically distinguish rat (Rattus) from mouse (Mus); both are nezumi, with modifiers ō-nezumi (large rat), hatsu-nezumi etc. used when needed. Sino-Japanese reading so/sho is rare; kun reading nezumi is standard. The word appears in earliest Japanese texts (Kojiki, Nihon Shoki, Man'yōshū) and is associated with the first branch of the Earthly Branches (ne, the Rat) in the zodiac.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese language and folklore. The zodiac rat (nezumi) is part of shared Sinosphere culture (Chinese shǔ, Korean jwi, Vietnamese tý) but the word nezumi itself is Japanese. Historical figure Nezumi Kozō belongs to Japanese Edo-period popular history. Fictional uses are modern Japanese and American franchise creations that borrow the Japanese word; the MTG/L5R ratfolk are American-designed interpretations of Japanese folklore motifs.

Description

Appearance

Varies completely by referent and must not be merged. (1) Linguistic nezumi: small rodent, 5-30 cm, gray/brown/white fur, long tail, incisors, whiskers; zodiac iconography shows white rat with large ears and coiled tail. (2) Nezumi Kozō: historical woodblock prints (nishiki-e) show a slender young man in thief's black装束 (shozoku), face cloth, carrying a crowbar; kabuki idealizes him as handsome, pale, quick-footed. No verified contemporary portrait survives; all images are post-arrest popular prints. (3) No. 6 Nezumi: described by Asano as 16-20 year old male, ash-gray eyes, dark hair, lean and scarred, theatrical bearing; anime design (Bones, 2011, character design Satoshi Ishino) gives him long steel-gray hair, black coat, scarred torso from dog attack as child; stage adaptations vary. (4) MTG Nezumi: anthropomorphic ratfolk, 4-5 feet tall, digitigrade legs, snout, patchy fur (black/gray/brown), worn leathers, often missing ears/tails due to spiritual exile; Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty (2022) redesign adds cybernetic augmentations. L5R Nezumi (Ratlings): similar but more bestial, with tribal warpaint, chipped teeth, Rememberer shamans carry memory sticks. (5) One Piece Captain Nezumi: short, rat-like human male, Marine Captain (branch 16), thin mustache mimicking whiskers, buck teeth, Marine cap with rat ears motif, corrupt uniform, wields no special weapon. (6) Software/Majisuka uses have no distinctive visual lore.

Symbols & Iconography

Zodiac rat: first of 12 junishi, symbol of fertility, wealth (Daikokuten's messenger), cunning; netsuke and ema frequently depict nezumi with rice bales. Nezumi Kozō: symbol of robin-hood-like giri-ninjo, his calling card 'Nezumi Kozō' graffiti, and the jitte (police truncheon) that caught him. No.6 Nezumi: symbol is the theater — mask, books (especially Shakespeare's Macbeth/Hamlet), and the ruined West Block; his name itself is a self-chosen alias meaning rat, marking his status as vermin outside No.6's walls. MTG Nezumi: symbols are the Taken One (O-Kagachi) and the kanji for shame/exile; their villages are marked by jumbled scrap and bone totems; Ink-Eyes' oversized caul and stolen teeth are iconic. One Piece Nezumi: symbol is Marine corruption — bribe money and Arlong's mermaid map.

Behaviour & Personality

(1) Folkloric nezumi: trickster, thief of rice, sometimes deity messenger, sometimes plague carrier; behavior in otogizōshi and kaidan is nocturnal, hoarding, gnawing. (2) Nezumi Kozō: historical accounts describe a non-violent, solitary burglar who entered daimyo estates via roof tiles, stole only when occupants slept, distributed little to poor despite legend; confessed to 100+ intrusions. Popular legend recasts him as generous chivalrous thief (gizoku). (3) No.6 Nezumi: fiercely independent, sardonic, theatrical, traumatized survivor of West Block's forest people purge; values freedom and memory, distrusts institutions, uses Shakespeare and song as coping; deeply loyal to Shion after their childhood meeting, but pushes him away to protect him. (4) MTG Nezumi: Kamigawa Nezumi are pragmatic, clan-loyal, spiritually severed from the kami after siding with humans; they are scavengers, mercenaries, and necromancers (Ink-Eyes). L5R Nezumi are matriarchal, memory-obsessed, paranoid about the coming Tomorrow, with Rememberers who literally carry tribal history. (5) One Piece Nezumi: cowardly, greedy, obsequious to stronger pirates, abusive to subordinates and villagers; exemplifies systemic Marine corruption in East Blue.

Domains & Habitats

Linguistic nezumi: ubiquitous in Japan — houses, fields, shrines (Daikoku shrines). Nezumi Kozō: Edo (present Tokyo), specifically daimyo upper residences (kamiyashiki) in Edo Castle surrounds. No.6 Nezumi: West Block (abandoned industrial zone outside the utopian city-state No.6), later travels with Shion; habitat is ruins, library, theater stage. MTG Nezumi: Takenuma Swamp and Jukai Forest fringes on Kamigawa plane; exiled from the spirit world (kakuriyo). L5R Nezumi: Shadowlands-adjacent warrens, the Warrens of the Rememberers, and later the Shinomen Forest after Exodus. One Piece Nezumi: East Blue, Marine Branch 16 jurisdiction, Cocoyasi Village and Arlong Park. Software Nezumi: private Ragnarok Online servers (digital).

Version Differences

Critical: Japanese does not distinguish rat/mouse, but English translations force a choice — zodiac 'Rat' vs 'Mouse' is translator decision, not original distinction. MTG vs L5R Nezumi diverged after Wizards of the Coast ended L5R licensing in 2005; Kamigawa (2004) Nezumi are spiritually deaf humans' allies turned pariahs, while L5R Nezumi have a full alternate cosmology (the Realm of Dreams, the Name, Tomorrow). No.6 Nezumi differs between novel (more explicit political philosophy, longer timeline to age 20, more violent), manga (Hinoki Kino, 2011-2014, softened), anime (Bones, 11 episodes, compressed ending, more homoerotic subtext foregrounded), and stage plays (2013, 2018). One Piece Nezumi is minor in manga (2 chapters) but expanded slightly in anime filler. Nezumi Kozō's historical crime record (30+ confessed) vs kabuki/rakugo legend (100+ mansions, money to poor) are contradictory layers.

Powers & Abilities

Linguistic/Folkloric: Gnawing, Hoarding, Disease Vector, Divine Messenger

Source VersionJapanese folklore / zodiac tradition / Edo kaidan collections

In folklore, nezumi can gnaw through anything, multiply rapidly, foretell famine, and serve Daikokuten/Ebisu. No supernatural powers beyond folk belief; in kaidan (e.g., Tono Monogatari) they shapeshift into humans.

Limitations

Folk belief only; modern biology distinguishes pest behavior from myth. No attested magical ability.

Contradictions

Messenger of wealth vs harbinger of plague — regional variation.

Nezumi Kozō: Infiltration and Non-Violent Burglary

Source VersionEdo bugyōsho confession 1831; kawaraban news sheets

Historical skill set: roof-tile entry (yane yaburi), silent movement, lock picking, knowledge of daimyo residence layouts. No violence attested; carried only a small knife. Popular legend adds superhuman agility and invisibility.

Limitations

Caught in 1831 due to informant; executed. No evidence of martial arts or supernatural ability.

Contradictions

Historical non-violent thief vs fictionalized Robin Hood who fights samurai.

No.6 Nezumi: Survival, Performance, Combat, and Literary Intelligence

Source VersionAsano No.6 novel Vol.1-9; anime Episodes 1-11

Self-taught survivalist: hunting, trapping, first aid, hand-to-hand combat (trained in West Block), knife fighting, evasion. Exceptional actor/singer — performs Shakespeare, plays guitar, uses voice to manipulate. High intelligence, photographic memory for texts, multilingual (reads English literature). No superpowers; grounded human abilities.

Limitations

Human vulnerability: scarred, susceptible to disease and injury; emotional trauma impairs judgment; depends on Shion for emotional anchor. No supernatural ability.

Contradictions

Novel emphasizes his pragmatism and willingness to kill; anime emphasizes vulnerability and aestheticizes him.

MTG Nezumi: Ninjutsu, Fear, and Necromancy (Kamigawa)

Source VersionChampions of Kamigawa block (2004-2005); Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty (2022) adds cyber-ninja augmentations

Card mechanics translate lore: Ninjutsu (return unblocked attacker to summon Nezumi), discard/hand disruption (Nezumi Shortfang / Stabwhisker the Odious), fear/intimidation, and Ink-Eyes' rat-based reanimation (steals creatures from graveyard). Lore: spiritually deaf, can see but not be heard by kami, expert scavengers and assassins.

Limitations

Exiled from kami blessings; cannot channel spirit magic like kitsune or orochi; vulnerable to kami wrath. Ninjutsu requires unblocked attacker and mana cost.

Contradictions

Early Kamigawa Nezumi were pure outcasts; Neon Dynasty retcons some clans as integrated into cyberpunk city.

L5R Nezumi: Memory, Resistance to Shadowlands Taint, and Pack Tactics

Source VersionLegend of the Five Rings CCG (1995-2015) and RPG 4th Edition: Enemies of the Empire

L5R Ratlings possess racial memory via Rememberers, resistance/immunity to Shadowlands Taint (unlike humans), heightened smell/hearing, pack fighting, and shamanic Name magic. Some chieftains (e.g., Tchuk) exhibit prophetic dreams of Tomorrow.

Limitations

Short lifespan (~15 years), low birth rate after Exodus, culturally unable to use human magic (shugenja), dependent on Rememberers for history. After Race to Volturnum, many left Rokugan.

Contradictions

Early L5R portrayed Nezumi as comic relief; later lore made them tragic survivors with deep cosmology — tonal contradiction.

One Piece Captain Nezumi: Marine Authority and Corruption

Source VersionOne Piece Chapter 69, 94; Anime Episode 31

No combat power; rank of Captain (Taisa) gives legal authority to command Marines, collect taxes, and ignore piracy. Uses bribery network with Arlong. Standard Marine pistol/sword, no Haki or Devil Fruit.

Limitations

Physically weak, cowardly, easily defeated by Nami's financial leverage and later dismissed by Marine HQ after exposure.

Contradictions

Marine rank suggests competence, but narrative shows incompetence/corruption — intentional satire.

Stories & Myths

The Word Itself: Nezumi in the Kojiki and Zodiac

Source Kojiki (712), Nihon Shoki (720), Jikkan Junishi (Earthly Branches)Culture Japanese (Yamato court)Period Nara period (8th century) with oral antecedents

Summary

The nezumi appears as both animal and calendrical sign. In the zodiac origin tale (later otogizōshi variant), the rat tricks the ox to become first of the junishi by riding its back and leaping ahead at the finish. In Kojiki, mice/nezumi help or hinder deities (e.g., the mouse that helps Ōkuninushi escape fire by showing an underground hole). The dual role — clever survivor and pest — establishes the archetype that later fictional Nezumi inherit.

Participants

Ōkuninushi (deity)

Ox (zodiac rival)

Daikokuten (later association)

Consequences

Establishes nezumi as culturally liminal: lucky yet pestilential, small yet first. This duality is consciously invoked by authors naming characters Nezumi.

Variants

Chinese zodiac tale has cat tricked by rat; Japanese versions omit cat or add different morals. Modern children's books sanitize the rat's deception.

Nezumi Kozō Jirokichi: The Rat-Boy Thief of Edo

Source Edo machi-bugyō trial confessions and kawaraban; later kabuki Nezumi Komon Haru no Shingata (Kawatake Mokuami, 1857)Culture Japanese (Edo period, late Tokugawa)Period 1823-1831 crimes; 1831 execution; Meiji-era dramatization

Summary

Nakamura Jirokichi, a low-ranking craftsman's son and former apprentice, burgled over 30 (confessed; legend says 100+) upper residences of daimyo and hatamoto in Edo. He entered through roof tiles at night, stole gold and goods while residents slept, and evaded capture for eight years. Arrested in 1831 after being turned in by his lover's family, he was paraded and executed by crucifixion and beheading at Suzugamori. Within months, woodblock prints and songs turned him into a folk hero; later kabuki and rakugo claimed he gave to the poor (no evidence). His grave at Ekō-in became a folk shrine where people chip stones for luck.

Participants

Nakamura Jirokichi / Nezumi Kozō

Edo machi-bugyō (magistrate)

Daimyo victims

Lover O-Maki (in legend)

Consequences

Became archetypal Japanese Robin Hood (gizoku); name 'Nezumi Kozō' became generic for agile thief. Grave still visited; idiom 'nezumi kozō' remains in Japanese.

Variants

Historical record: non-violent, selfish thief. Popular legend: generous, acrobatic, anti-samurai hero. Modern scholarship (Matsui) distinguishes the two.

No. 6: The Rat Who Left the Utopia

Source Asano, Atsuko. No. 6 novel (Kodansha, 2003-2011); manga (Hinoki Kino, 2011-2014); anime (Bones, 2011)Culture Japanese (contemporary dystopian SF)Period 2003-2011 (Heisei era publication)

Summary

In the utopian city-state No.6 (post-war 2013), 12-year-old elite Shion shelters a wounded fugitive boy who calls himself Nezumi (real name unknown, later implied to be from the forest people). Nezumi disappears. Four years later, Shion is exiled to West Block after a parasitic wasp incident, and reunites with Nezumi, now 16, a hardened survivor, actor, and resistance figure. Together they uncover No.6's secret: the city maintains its perfection via human hunting, corrective facilities, and parasitic wasps controlled by the forest. Nezumi's arc is revenge for his people massacred by No.6, complicated by love for Shion and Safu. The novel ends with the wall's fall, Nezumi leaving to wander after Shion chooses to stay and rebuild; anime/manga endings vary in explicitness of their relationship.

The Nezumi of Kamigawa: The Outcasts Who Remember

Source Magic: The Gathering Champions of Kamigawa block story (2004-2005) and The Kami War; Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty (2022)Culture American fantasy using Japanese mythology (Wizards of the Coast)Period 2004-2005 original; 2022 revisit

Summary

On the plane of Kamigawa, the Nezumi are ratfolk who sided with humans during the Kami War 1000 years ago. When the kami turned against mortals, the Nezumi were severed from the spirit world — they can no longer hear or be blessed by kami, unlike other races. They dwell in Takenuma Swamp as scavengers, mercenaries, and thieves, despised by orochi and kitsune. Figures like Ink-Eyes, Servant of Oni, a blind Nezumi who steals eyes and reanimates the dead, and Marrow-Gnawer, embody their desperation. In Neon Dynasty (1200 years later), some Nezumi clans have adapted to the neon city as hackers and cyber-ninjas, while others cling to old swamp ways, still spiritually deaf in a world where kami and technology merge.

The Nezumi (Ratlings) Who Remember Tomorrow

Source Legend of the Five Rings CCG and RPG — Way of the Ratling (1997), Enemies of the Empire (2011)Culture American tabletop (Alderac Entertainment Group)Period 1995-2015 (AEG era)

Summary

In Rokugan, the Nezumi (called Ratlings by humans) are an ancient race who built a civilization before humanity. Their Rememberers memorize the entire tribal history, because Nezumi have no written language that survives Taint. They alone remember that the Shadowlands Taint can be resisted and that 'Tomorrow' — a prophesied apocalypse — is coming. When humans arrived, Nezumi retreated. During the Clan War and later the Destroyer War, Nezumi allies (Mat'cha, Tchuk, etc.) help Rokugan, but after the Race to Volturnum they lead an exodus from Rokugan, sensing humanity's doom. Their story is one of memory vs forgetting.

Relationships
Nezumi→Shion (No. 6)ally

Core dyad of No.6. Nezumi saves 12-year-old Shion from storm, Shion shelters him; four years later Nezumi rescues exiled Shion in West Block. Relationship is romantic/queer-coded, explicitly confirmed as love in novel Vol.9 and by author statements, but left subtextual in anime. Shion's idealism tempers Nezumi's revenge; Nezumi's cynicism protects Shion. Ends with separation — Nezumi leaves No.6 to wander, Shion stays.

Nezumi→Safu (No. 6)ally

Childhood friend of Shion, later sacrificial figure. Nezumi is initially jealous/hostile toward Safu, seeing her as embodiment of No.6's elite. Later respects her sacrifice when she becomes host for Elyurias. Safu's death catalyzes Nezumi's decision to destroy No.6's system.

Nezumi→Elyurias / Forest People (No. 6)family

Nezumi is last survivor (with others) of the forest people massacred to build No.6. Elyurias is the forest's hive-mind/wasp queen, worshipped by his people. Nezumi's hatred of No.6 stems from this genocide; his songs are forest laments.

Nezumi→Arlong and Cocoyasi Village (One Piece)hostile

Captain Nezumi is Arlong's corrupt Marine protector; takes bribes to ignore Arlong Park's extortion. Hostile to Nami, Nojiko, Genzo. Relationship is transactional and cowardly — Nezumi fears Arlong but exploits villagers.

Nezumi→Takenuma Swamp Nezumi Clans / Ink-Eyes (MTG)family

Nezumi are a clan-based society; Ink-Eyes, Servant of Oni is a legendary Nezumi oiran-like necromancer who serves the ogre-demon Hidetsugu. Marrow-Gnawer is a chieftain who breeds endless rats. Internal hierarchy: chieftains, shortfangs (thieves), graverobbers. All share spiritual deafness.

Nezumi→Rokugani Clans and Rememberers (L5R)ally

L5R Nezumi have complex alliances: historically allied with Crab Clan against Shadowlands, later with all clans during Tomorrow. Rememberers are shaman-historians who carry the Name. Humans often dismiss them as vermin, but Crab and some Dragon respect them.

Nezumi→Daikokuten / Ōkuninushi (Folklore)worshipper

Folkloric nezumi are messengers/attendants of Daikokuten (god of wealth), often depicted with his rice bales. Zodiac nezumi is also associated with fortune. This divine link contrasts with Nezumi characters who are outcasts — an ironic inversion.

Nezumi→Nakamura Jirokichi's Victims and Edo Magistratevictim

Historical Nezumi Kozō's victims were daimyo and hatamoto households; magistrate Toyama Kagemoto (Kinshiro) is legendary as his captor, though historical capture was by other officers. Victim-perpetrator relationship romanticized later.

Warning: Execution of Nezumi Kozō and Edo Penal Cruelty — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Execution of Nezumi Kozō and Edo Penal Cruelty

Source Edo machi-bugyō records; Botsman, Punishment and Power in the Making of Modern Japan (2005)

Description

Nakamura Jirokichi was sentenced to crucifixion (haritsuke) followed by beheading and public display at Suzugamori execution ground, 1831. Edo punishment was deliberately theatrical to deter crime. His head was displayed for three days. Contemporary prints sensationalized his death.

Scholarly Context

Scholars note Nezumi Kozō's harsh sentence reflected Tokugawa anxiety about status intrusion — a commoner entering daimyo homes violated hierarchy more than theft itself.

No. 6: State-Sponsored Genocide and Human Experimentation

Source Asano No.6 novel Vol.6-8; anime Episode 9-10

Description

Nezumi's backstory reveals No.6 was built by massacring the forest people and later conducts human hunting (the Hunt), corrective facilities, and parasitic wasp experiments that kill children. The city euthanizes 'defective' citizens. Nezumi's own village was burned, his family killed, and he was left with scars from wild dogs as a child.

Scholarly Context

Critics read No.6 as allegory for eugenics, Japanese wartime Unit 731, and contemporary surveillance states; Asano has cited post-war Japanese history as influence.

No. 6: Sexualization of Minors and Power Imbalance

Source No.6 novel Vol.9; anime Episode 7, 11; manga Vol.7

Description

Nezumi and Shion are 12 at meeting, 16 at reunion; later volumes include sexual intimacy between 16-18 year olds, and the anime includes suggestive scenes (bath, bed sharing). Some adaptations aestheticize teen trauma. No explicit sexual violence, but the narrative includes coercive institutional control over bodies (wasp implantation, facility abuse).

Scholarly Context

Academic reception (e.g., Fujimoto Yukari on BL) notes No.6 navigates shōnen-ai tropes while depicting minors; English publishers added 16+ ratings. Responsible reading distinguishes consensual teen relationship from adult gaze.

One Piece Nezumi: Systemic Corruption and Extortion

Source One Piece Chapter 90-94

Description

Captain Nezumi embodies institutional abuse: he knowingly allows Arlong's eight-year extortion and murder in Cocoyasi, steals Nami's 100 million berries (her life's savings to free her village), and uses Marine violence against civilians. The arc depicts children (Nami, Nojiko) under prolonged terror.

Scholarly Context

Oda uses Nezumi to illustrate how legal authority can be more villainous than piracy — a recurring One Piece theme about justice vs law.

Contradictions & Disputes

Rat vs Mouse translation

Position A

Nezumi should be translated as 'Rat' (zodiac, MTG tribe, One Piece epithet).

Position A — evidence

Zodiac junishi is officially 'Rat' in English; MTG creature type is Rat; One Piece Captain Nezumi is called 'Captain Rat' in Viz translation.

Position B

Nezumi should be translated as 'Mouse' (cute characters, diminutive nuance).

Position B — evidence

Japanese uses same word for both; Majisuka Gakuen Nezumi is a small, cute girl — 'mouse' fits better; some No.6 translations use 'Mouse.'

Scholarly Consensus

Both are wrong alone; accurate translation requires context or keeping 'Nezumi' untranslated. Best practice: gloss as 'rat/mouse' on first use.

Was Nezumi Kozō a Robin Hood?

Position A

He was a chivalrous thief who gave to the poor (popular legend, kabuki).

Position A — evidence

Kawaraban, rakugo, and Mokuami's play claim he distributed money; grave folk belief says chipping his gravestone brings luck because he was virtuous.

Position B

He was a self-serving burglar who kept proceeds and was not generous (trial records).

Position B — evidence

Confession recorded 30+ thefts of gold, clothing, with no mention of charity; contemporaries noted he spent on gambling and women; no victim testimony of charity.

Scholarly Consensus

Historians (Matsui, Botsman) agree Robin Hood element is posthumous romanticization, common for Edo thieves (cf. Ishikawa Goemon).

Are MTG Nezumi and L5R Nezumi the same?

Position A

They are the same race, shared design (Wizards owned L5R early).

Position A — evidence

Early L5R and MTG both by Wizards/Alderac; visual similarity (anthropomorphic rats) and name Nezumi/Ratling; some designers worked on both.

Position B

They are separate continuities with different lore and should not be conflated.

Position B — evidence

Wizards sold L5R to AEG in 1995; Kamigawa (2004) Nezumi have Kami War exile lore, L5R Nezumi have Rememberer/Tomorrow lore; FFG reboot explicitly separates them. No in-universe crossover.

Scholarly Consensus

Separate IPs; treat as parallel interpretations of Japanese rat folklore, not same canon. OmniLore separates them.

Nature of No.6 Nezumi's relationship with Shion

Position A

Canonically romantic/lovers (authorial intent, novel text).

Position A — evidence

Novel Vol.9 explicitly describes mutual love confession and kiss; Asano afterword and drama CDs confirm; anime Episode 11 kiss scene (censored in some broadcasts).

Position B

Close friendship/bromance, romance is fan interpretation.

Position B — evidence

Anime TV broadcast obscured kiss with light; English marketing at time used 'friendship' to avoid controversy; some readers cite lack of explicit sex as ambiguity.

Scholarly Consensus

Japanese critical consensus and author statements treat as canon queer romance; Western localization historically downplayed due to market concerns.

Is Nezumi a single character or a name?

Position A

Nezumi is one character (e.g., No.6's Nezumi is the primary referent for Q107723967).

Position A — evidence

Wikidata Q107723967 is typed as fictional character, suggesting one entity; No.6 fandom treats Nezumi as unique.

Position B

Nezumi is a homonym/disambiguation for many unrelated entities sharing a Japanese word.

Position B — evidence

Source disambiguation page lists 7+ distinct uses (word, thief, software, multiple characters); no shared continuity; Japanese Wikipedia has separate pages for 鼠, 鼠小僧, No.6, etc.

Scholarly Consensus

Disambiguation is required; this dossier treats Nezumi as a name cluster with separate pages ideally, but provides unified overview with clear separation per charter anti-conflation rule.

Research Scope

This dossier treats 'Nezumi' as a Japanese homonym with multiple independent referents that share a name but not a continuity. Primary focus is given to (1) the linguistic term nezumi (鼠) and (2) the four most documented fictional/historical uses: Nezumi Kozō (historical figure Nakamura Jirokichi, 1797-1831), Nezumi the ratfolk race in Magic: The Gathering / Legend of the Five Rings (Wizards of the Coast / Alderac Entertainment Group), Nezumi from Atsuko Asano's No. 6 (2003-2011), and Captain Nezumi from Eiichiro Oda's One Piece (1997-present). Software (Ragnarok Online emulator fork) and Majisuka Gakuen character are noted as namesakes. Research cutoff 2026-08-09. Japanese-language primary sources consulted in translation; Edo-period trial records accessed via secondary scholarship.
