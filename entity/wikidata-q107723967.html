Who It Is

In Japanese the word is small and sharp — nezumi — a flick of whiskers in the dark. It means rat and mouse at once, with no need to choose between them, and that ambiguity has allowed it to slip through centuries of stories: a folk-hero thief who robbed samurai mansions and was celebrated in kabuki, a scarred boy who sings Shakespeare in a dystopian city, a clan of outcast ratfolk who remember what the kami forgot, and a corrupt Marine captain who counts bribes in an occupied fishing village. One name, many shadows.

(1) Folkloric nezumi: trickster, thief of rice, sometimes deity messenger, sometimes plague carrier; behavior in otogizōshi and kaidan is nocturnal, hoarding, gnawing. (2) Nezumi Kozō: historical accounts describe a non-violent, solitary burglar who entered daimyo estates via roof tiles, stole only when occupants slept, distributed little to poor despite legend; confessed to 100+ intrusions. Popular legend recasts him as generous chivalrous thief (gizoku). (3) No.6 Nezumi: fiercely independent, sardonic, theatrical, traumatized survivor of West Block's forest people purge; values freed

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese 鼠, Old Japanese *nesumi, from Proto-Japonic. Written with kanji 鼠 meaning 'rat; mouse; rodent.' The language historically does not lexically distinguish rat (Rattus) from mouse (Mus); both are nezumi, with modifiers ō-nezumi (large rat), hatsu-nezumi etc. used when needed. Sino-Japanese rea

Pronunciation — Japanese [nezɯmi] — ne-zu-mi, three morae, pitch accent: heiban (LHH) in standard Tokyo Japanese, often rendered neh-zoo-mee in English. Hepburn: nezumi.

Aliases — Nezumi (鼠), Nezumi Kozō (鼠小僧), Nezumi (No. 6), Nezumi (One Piece), Nezumi (Magic: The Gathering / Legend of the Five Rings), Nezumi (Majisuka Gakuen)

Powers
Linguistic/Folkloric: Gnawing, Hoarding, Disease Vector, Divine Messenger

In folklore, nezumi can gnaw through anything, multiply rapidly, foretell famine, and serve Daikokuten/Ebisu. No supernatural powers beyond folk belief; in kaidan (e.g., Tono Monogatari) they shapeshift into humans.

Limit: Folk belief only; modern biology distinguishes pest behavior from myth. No attested magical ability.

Nezumi Kozō: Infiltration and Non-Violent Burglary

Historical skill set: roof-tile entry (yane yaburi), silent movement, lock picking, knowledge of daimyo residence layouts. No violence attested; carried only a small knife. Popular legend adds superhuman agility and invisibility.

Limit: Caught in 1831 due to informant; executed. No evidence of martial arts or supernatural ability.

No.6 Nezumi: Survival, Performance, Combat, and Literary Intelligence

Self-taught survivalist: hunting, trapping, first aid, hand-to-hand combat (trained in West Block), knife fighting, evasion. Exceptional actor/singer — performs Shakespeare, plays guitar, uses voice to manipulate. High intelligence, photographic memory for texts, multilingual (reads English literature). No superpowers; grounded human abilities.

Limit: Human vulnerability: scarred, susceptible to disease and injury; emotional trauma impairs judgment; depends on Shion for emotional anchor. No supernatural ability.

MTG Nezumi: Ninjutsu, Fear, and Necromancy (Kamigawa)

Card mechanics translate lore: Ninjutsu (return unblocked attacker to summon Nezumi), discard/hand disruption (Nezumi Shortfang / Stabwhisker the Odious), fear/intimidation, and Ink-Eyes' rat-based reanimation (steals creatures from graveyard). Lore: spiritually deaf, can see but not be heard by kami, expert scavengers and assassins.

Limit: Exiled from kami blessings; cannot channel spirit magic like kitsune or orochi; vulnerable to kami wrath. Ninjutsu requires unblocked attacker and mana cost.

L5R Nezumi: Memory, Resistance to Shadowlands Taint, and Pack Tactics

L5R Ratlings possess racial memory via Rememberers, resistance/immunity to Shadowlands Taint (unlike humans), heightened smell/hearing, pack fighting, and shamanic Name magic. Some chieftains (e.g., Tchuk) exhibit prophetic dreams of Tomorrow.

Limit: Short lifespan (~15 years), low birth rate after Exodus, culturally unable to use human magic (shugenja), dependent on Rememberers for history. After Race to Volturnum, many left Rokugan.

One Piece Captain Nezumi: Marine Authority and Corruption

No combat power; rank of Captain (Taisa) gives legal authority to command Marines, collect taxes, and ignore piracy. Uses bribery network with Arlong. Standard Marine pistol/sword, no Haki or Devil Fruit.

Limit: Physically weak, cowardly, easily defeated by Nami's financial leverage and later dismissed by Marine HQ after exposure.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Varies completely by referent and must not be merged. (1) Linguistic nezumi: small rodent, 5-30 cm, gray/brown/white fur, long tail, incisors, whiskers; zodiac iconography shows white rat with large ears and coiled tail. (2) Nezumi Kozō: historical woodblock prints (nishiki-e) show a slender young man in thief's black装束 (shozoku), face cloth, carrying a crowbar; kabuki idealizes him as handsome, pale, quick-footed. No verified contemporary portra

Symbols — Zodiac rat: first of 12 junishi, symbol of fertility, wealth (Daikokuten's messenger), cunning; netsuke and ema frequently depict nezumi with rice bales. Nezumi Kozō: symbol of robin-hood-like giri-ninjo, his calling card 'Nezumi Kozō' graffiti, and the jitte (police truncheon) that caught him. No.6 Nezumi: symbol is the theater — mask, books (especially Shakespeare's Macbeth/Hamlet), and the ruined West Block; his name itself

Domains — Linguistic nezumi: ubiquitous in Japan — houses, fields, shrines (Daikoku shrines). Nezumi Kozō: Edo (present Tokyo), specifically daimyo upper residences (kamiyashiki) in Edo Castle surrounds. No.6 Nezumi: West Block (abandoned industrial zone outside the utopian city-state No.6), later travels with Shion; habitat is ruins, library, theater stage. MTG Nezumi: Takenuma Swamp and Jukai Forest fringes on Kamigawa plane; exiled from the spirit world

Accounts I
The Word Itself: Nezumi in the Kojiki and ZodiacKojiki (712), Nihon Shoki (720), Jikkan Junishi (Earthly Branches)

Nara period (8th century) with oral antecedents

The nezumi appears as both animal and calendrical sign. In the zodiac origin tale (later otogizōshi variant), the rat tricks the ox to become first of the junishi by riding its back and leaping ahead at the finish. In Kojiki, mice/nezumi help or hinder deities (e.g., the mouse that helps Ōkuninushi escape fire by showing an underground hole). The dual role — clever survivor and pest — establishes the archetype that later fictional Nezumi inherit. Establishes nezumi as culturally liminal: lucky yet pestilential, small yet first. This duality is consciously invoked by authors naming characters Nezumi.

Nezumi Kozō Jirokichi: The Rat-Boy Thief of EdoEdo machi-bugyō trial confessions and kawaraban; later kabuki Nezumi Komon Haru no Shingata (Kawatake Mokuami, 1857)

1823-1831 crimes; 1831 execution; Meiji-era dramatization

Nakamura Jirokichi, a low-ranking craftsman's son and former apprentice, burgled over 30 (confessed; legend says 100+) upper residences of daimyo and hatamoto in Edo. He entered through roof tiles at night, stole gold and goods while residents slept, and evaded capture for eight years. Arrested in 1831 after being turned in by his lover's family, he was paraded and executed by crucifixion and beheading at Suzugamori. Within months, woodblock prints and songs turned him into a folk hero; later kabuki and rakugo claimed he gave to the poor (no evidence). His grave at Ekō-in became a folk shrine where people chip stones for luck. Became archetypal Japanese Robin Hood (gizoku); name 'Nezumi Kozō' became generic for agile thief. Grave still visited; idiom 'nezumi kozō' remains in Japanese.

No. 6: The Rat Who Left the UtopiaAsano, Atsuko. No. 6 novel (Kodansha, 2003-2011); manga (Hinoki Kino, 2011-2014); anime (Bones, 2011)

2003-2011 (Heisei era publication)

In the utopian city-state No.6 (post-war 2013), 12-year-old elite Shion shelters a wounded fugitive boy who calls himself Nezumi (real name unknown, later implied to be from the forest people). Nezumi disappears. Four years later, Shion is exiled to West Block after a parasitic wasp incident, and reunites with Nezumi, now 16, a hardened survivor, actor, and resistance figure. Together they uncover No.6's secret: the city maintains its perfection via human hunting, corrective facilities, and parasitic wasps controlled by the forest. Nezumi's arc is revenge for his people massacred by No.6, complicated by love for Shion and Safu. The novel ends with the wall's fall, Nezumi leaving to wander after Shion chooses to stay and rebuild; anime/manga endings vary in explicitness of their relationship. No.6 became a landmark BL-adjacent dystopia; Nezumi is iconic as a queer-coded, anti-utopian survivor. Debates over whether his name is dehumanization or reclamation.

Accounts II
The Nezumi of Kamigawa: The Outcasts Who RememberMagic: The Gathering Champions of Kamigawa block story (2004-2005) and The Kami War; Kamigawa: Neon Dynasty (2022)

2004-2005 original; 2022 revisit

On the plane of Kamigawa, the Nezumi are ratfolk who sided with humans during the Kami War 1000 years ago. When the kami turned against mortals, the Nezumi were severed from the spirit world — they can no longer hear or be blessed by kami, unlike other races. They dwell in Takenuma Swamp as scavengers, mercenaries, and thieves, despised by orochi and kitsune. Figures like Ink-Eyes, Servant of Oni, a blind Nezumi who steals eyes and reanimates the dead, and Marrow-Gnawer, embody their desperation. In Neon Dynasty (1200 years later), some Nezumi clans have adapted to the neon city as hackers and cyber-ninjas, while others cling to old swamp ways, still spiritually deaf in a world where kami and technology merge. Established Nezumi as a black-aligned tribe in MTG, with mechanics around discard and ninjutsu. Lore expanded representation of Japanese folklore in Western game design, critiqued for orientalism in early block.

Captain Nezumi: The Rat in the Marine UniformOda, Eiichiro. One Piece Vol.8-11 (Chapters 69-94)

1999-2000 publication (Heisei)

In East Blue, Marine Captain Nezumi (Branch 16) takes bribes from fish-man pirate Arlong to ignore his conquest of Cocoyasi Village and extortion of 100 million berries. When Nami steals 100 million to buy her village's freedom, Nezumi, tipped by Arlong, confiscates the money under pretext of 'pirate funds' to keep her enslaved. Nami's sister Nojiko and villagers protest; Nezumi has them beaten. Later, after Luffy defeats Arlong, Nezumi returns to claim credit and seize Arlong's treasure, but is confronted by Nami and exposed. Higher Marine HQ (later implied) dismisses him; he is last seen fleeing. His name is literal — a rat in uniform. Mini-arc that cements One Piece's theme of systemic corruption vs individual justice; Nezumi is often cited in fan discussions of Marine hypocrisy before later Marineford arcs.

The Nezumi (Ratlings) Who Remember TomorrowLegend of the Five Rings CCG and RPG — Way of the Ratling (1997), Enemies of the Empire (2011)

1995-2015 (AEG era)

In Rokugan, the Nezumi (called Ratlings by humans) are an ancient race who built a civilization before humanity. Their Rememberers memorize the entire tribal history, because Nezumi have no written language that survives Taint. They alone remember that the Shadowlands Taint can be resisted and that 'Tomorrow' — a prophesied apocalypse — is coming. When humans arrived, Nezumi retreated. During the Clan War and later the Destroyer War, Nezumi allies (Mat'cha, Tchuk, etc.) help Rokugan, but after the Race to Volturnum they lead an exodus from Rokugan, sensing humanity's doom. Their story is one of memory vs forgetting. L5R Nezumi became fan-favorite non-human race, praised for matriarchal structure and unique memory mechanics; their exodus was controversial among players.

Nezumi means only 'rat' or only 'mouse' in Japanese. — Japanese 鼠 covers both rats and mice without distinction; English forces a choice that Japanese does not make. Zodiac 'Rat' is a translation convention, not a biological claim.

Nezumi Kozō was a heroic Robin Hood who stole from samurai to give to the poor. — Trial confessions and magistrate records show he kept proceeds for himself (gambling, lovers); no evidence of charity. The Robin Hood image was created by kawaraban and kabuki after his execution to sell prints and critique samurai excess.

The Nezumi in Magic: The Gathering are based on a specific Japanese yōkai. — There is no single yōkai called 'nezumi' as a ratfolk race; Japanese folklore has nezumi yōkai (e.g., Tesso the iron rat, Ōnezumi), but MTG Nezumi are an original synthesis of folklore motifs (rat spirits, Tesso, zodiac) for a Western game, not a direct folklore transcription.

No.6's Nezumi has superpowers or is a mutant. — Nezumi is a baseline human with exceptional training and intelligence; the supernatural elements (Elyurias, parasitic wasps) affect others, not him. His survival skills are grounded, not superhuman.

Captain Nezumi in One Piece is a major villain or has Haki/Devil Fruit. — He is a minor, weak, corrupt Marine captain with no combat abilities, appearing in only ~3 chapters. He is narrative device to show systemic corruption, not a power-scaling threat.

Nezumi (Ragnarok emulator) was created by the Ragnarok Online company. — It was an unofficial open-source fork of the Athena emulator by Mark Karpeles, released under GNU GPL, not affiliated with Gravity Co. Now defunct.
