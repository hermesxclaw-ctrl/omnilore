Accounts

In this genealogy, Argeus is one of the sons of Pelops and Hippodameia, making him a prince of the Peloponnese and a member of one of the most dysfunctional families in Greek mythology. His brothers include Atreus and Thyestes — whose feud over the golden lamb led to cannibalism, curses, and the Trojan War. Argeus himself plays no role in any surviving narrative.

In this account, Argeus is a son of Zeus and Niobe (daughter of Phoroneus), making him a brother of Pelasgus. This genealogy connects him to the Pelasgians, the pre-Hellenic inhabitants of Greece. Again, no stories or deeds are attached to his name.

In the final book of the Odyssey, Odysseus — disguised and testing his aged father Laertes — claims to have hosted a man named Argeus (or Argeas) among the Taphians. Some scholia identify this Argeus as Odysseus's maternal grandfather, though the identification is uncertain. The name appears only in this single passing reference.
