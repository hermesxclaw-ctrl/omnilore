Accounts

In the most prominent mythological tradition, Argeus is one of the sons born to Zeus and Niobe, the daughter of Phoroneus, a primordial king of the Peloponnese. This Niobe is not the famous Theban queen who boasted of her children, but a separate, earlier figure. Argeus's brother in this tradition is Pelasgus, the eponymous ancestor of the Pelasgians, a name used for the pre-Greek inhabitants of the region. Together, the brothers represent the mythological foundation of the two main ethnic groups of the ancient Peloponnese: the Pelasgians and the Argives. This genealogy places Argeus at the very dawn of human civilization in Greece, making him a figure of immense, if shadowy, antiquity.

A separate tradition names Argeus as one of the sons of Pelops and Hippodameia, making him a brother of Atreus and Thyestes, and thus a member of the cursed House of Atreus. In this version, he is a mortal prince who may have ruled Argos before his more famous brothers, inheriting the city through his mother's connection to the region. This Argeus is a far more 'historical' figure, embedded in the complex and tragic saga of the Pelopid dynasty.

Scholarly Debates
Identity: Son of Zeus vs. Son of Pelops
Argeus is a son of Zeus and Niobe, making him a primordial divine figure and eponymous ancestor of the Argives. ⇌ Argeus is a son of Pelops and Hippodameia, making him a mortal prince of the historical/heroic age and a member of the cursed House of Atreus.
Evidence for A: Pseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.1.1
Evidence for B: Hyginus, Fabulae 84; various scholia
Conflation with Argus
Argeus is a distinct figure from Argus, the many-eyed giant and guardian of Io. ⇌ Argeus and Argus are the same figure, or their names are interchangeable variants in the eponymous-founder tradition.
Evidence for A: The distinct roles in mythology: Argus Panoptes is a monster; Argeus is a king or god.
Evidence for B: The similarity of names and the shared connection to the city of Argos lead to confusion in later mythographers.
