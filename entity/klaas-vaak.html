Who It Is

In the quiet of a Dutch evening, when the last light fades and children's eyelids grow heavy, a gentle figure slips through the half-open door. He carries a sack of dreams, and with a soft touch, he sprinkles sand into waiting eyes. This is Klaas Vaak, the Sandman of the Low Countries, a being who has watched over Dutch childhoods for centuries, weaving the boundary between waking and sleep.

Klaas Vaak is gentle, kind, and patient. He visits children at bedtime, sprinkling sand into their eyes to help them fall asleep. He is not a trickster or a malevolent figure; he is a benevolent guardian of sleep. He is often portrayed as a comforting presence, ensuring that children have peaceful dreams.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Klaas' is a Dutch short form of 'Nicolaas' (Nicholas), and 'Vaak' derives from the Dutch word for 'sleep' (from the verb 'vaken', meaning 'to be sleepy' or 'to doze'). Thus, Klaas Vaak roughly translates to 'Sleepy Nicholas' or 'Nicholas Sleepy'. The name is a personification of sleep itse

Powers
Sand Sprinkling

Klaas Vaak sprinkles magical sand into the eyes of children (and sometimes adults) to induce sleep. The sand is said to bring pleasant dreams.

Limit: Only works on those who are ready to sleep; resistant individuals may not be affected. It is not harmful.

Dream Weaving

He is able to influence the content of dreams, often bringing pleasant, soothing visions to sleepers.

Limit: Not explicitly detailed in most sources; it is a gentle influence, not absolute control.

Invisibility and Stealth

He can move unseen and unheard, entering bedrooms without waking the occupants.

Limit: He is only visible to those who are still awake, and he disappears when they fall asleep.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Klaas Vaak is typically depicted as a small, elderly man with a long white beard, often wearing simple, old-fashioned clothing, sometimes with a nightcap. He carries a sack or bag filled with magical sand, which he sprinkles into the eyes of sleeping children. In some depictions, he may have a gentle, kindly face, and he is often shown in a night-time setting, with stars and the moon.

Symbols — His primary symbol is the sack of sand, which represents sleep. He is also associated with the night, the moon, and stars. In illustrations, he is often shown with a sleepy, peaceful demeanor. The sand itself is symbolic of the transition from wakefulness to sleep, and the dreams that follow.

Domains — He is associated with the domestic sphere, specifically the bedroom at night. He is said to visit homes when children are going to sleep. He is a liminal figure, dwelling in the twilight between waking and sleeping, and is connected to the realm of dreams.
