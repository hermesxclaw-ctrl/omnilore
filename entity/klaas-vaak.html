Who It Is

Klaas Vaak is the Dutch equivalent of the Sandman — a benevolent night spirit who helps children fall asleep by sprinkling magical sand (slaapzand) into their eyes. He is depicted as a kindly, elderly man with a long white beard, dressed in simple workman's clothes, carrying a large sack of sleep-sand. He is often accompanied by a small helper or by his cat. The name 'Klaas Vaak' translates roughly to 'Nick Sleepy' or 'Nicholas Sleep,' with 'Vaak' being an old Dutch word for 'sleep' or 'drowsiness.' By night, Klaas Vaak climbs through bedroom windows and sprinkles the sleep-sand into children's eyes, making them irresistibly drowsy and ready for bed. Children who wake up with 'sleep dust' (slaapzand or 'sleepies') in the corners of their eyes know that Klaas Vaak has visited. The figure was popularized in the 19th century through children's literature and was made famous by author Hans Christian Andersen's 1841 fairy tale 'Ole Lukøje,' which describes a Danish Sandman figure (Ole Lukøje) who uses a magical umbrella — opening it with pictures over good children to give them wonderful dreams, and opening it blank over naughty children to leave them dreamless. The Dutch Klaas Vaak specifically has deep roots in local folklore and enjoys continued popularity in the Netherlands through the works of author Leonard Roggeveen, who wrote children's books about Klaas Vaak in the early 20th century. Unlike the sometimes-scary Sandman of Germanic tradition (who could also punish), Klaas Vaak is purely benevolent, representing the gentle transition from waking to sleep in Dutch children's culture. The figure shares traits with the Scandinavian Ole Lukøje, the German Sandmännchen, and the English Sandman.
