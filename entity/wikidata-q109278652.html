Who It Is

On Krypton he was the general who believed order required absolute obedience. In the Phantom Zone he was a ghost who refused to be forgotten. On Earth he is the one Kryptonian who makes Superman look merciful — because when Dru-Zod says 'Kneel before Zod,' he is not asking for respect. He is demanding the surrender of a planet he believes was always his to rule.

Attested core traits across all Kryptonian versions: megalomania, militaristic authoritarianism, absolute belief in Kryptonian superiority and his own right to rule, contempt for democracy and for Jor-El's Science Council, charismatic command presence, tactical brilliance, and refusal to submit. Described in Action Comics Annual #10 as a charismatic revolutionary who became the tyrant he claimed to oppose. Powers
Kryptonian Physiology (Yellow Sun Empowerment)

As a Kryptonian under a yellow sun (Earth's sun), Zod possesses the same powers as Superman: solar energy absorption grants superhuman strength, speed, durability, flight, heat vision, x-ray vision, telescopic/microscopic vision, super-hearing, super-breath (including freeze breath), and near-invulnerability. Power level depicted as equal to or occasionally exceeding Superman due to superior military training and ruthlessness.

Limit: Powers depend on yellow solar radiation; depleted under red sun (Rao) or without solar charge. Requires solar battery — prolonged deprivation weakens him. Not inherently magical; vulnerable to magic.

Military Strategy and Combat Training

Master strategist, tactician, and hand-to-hand combatant trained as head of Krypton's military. Expertise in Kryptonian martial arts (Klorour, Torquasm-Rao/Torquasm-Vo in some continuities), weapons, and large-scale warfare. Often shown outfighting Superman initially due to skill, not raw power.

Limit: Superior skill does not overcome overwhelming numbers or Superman's experience with diverse foes; arrogance leads to tactical overreach.

Phantom Zone Knowledge and Survival

Decades in the Phantom Zone grant intimate knowledge of its physics, ability to survive as phantom, to possess or influence others at Zone breaches, and to lead other Zone criminals. In Last Son, he and Ursa conceive a child (Lor-Zod) immune to Zone effects, exploiting a loophole.

Limit: While in Zone, non-corporeal and powerless to affect physical world except through rare breaches, telepathic projection, or with projector technology.

Leadership and Charisma

Ability to command loyalty from Kryptonian criminals (Ursa, Non, Faora, Jax-Ur, Quex-Ul, Zaora) and armies. On New Krypton, commands entire Military Guild; on Pokolistan (Russian Zod), commands nation-state.

Limit: Loyalty is fear-based; subordinates (especially Non) are sometimes uncontrollable; betrayals common (Ral-Dar, Lane conspiracy).

Vulnerabilities: Kryptonite, Red Solar Radiation, Magic, Psionics

Shares Superman's weaknesses: green kryptonite causes severe pain, power loss, and death with prolonged exposure; gold kryptonite can permanently remove powers (used by Superman to depower Pocket Universe Zod); red solar radiation immediately depowers; magic bypasses invulnerability; telepathic attacks can affect him.

Limit: Availability of kryptonite is rare; Zod often prepares countermeasures (armor, lead shielding).

Accounts I
The Phantom Zone Criminal - First Appearance and 40-Year SentenceAdventure Comics #283 (April 1961) 'The Phantom Zone!'

1961

Dru-Zod, leader of Krypton's military, attempts to seize power after Krypton's space program is abolished following Jax-Ur's destruction of moon Wegthor. He creates an army of imperfect robotic duplicates of himself (described as Bizarro-like) to enforce his rule. The Science Council sentences him to 40 years in the Phantom Zone. Decades later, Superboy (Clark Kent) releases him upon learning his sentence has expired, but Zod immediately attempts to conquer Earth with his superpowers, forcing Superboy to return him to the Zone. This establishes Zod's core loop for the next 25 years: escape, battle Superman/Supergirl, return to Zone. Creates the Phantom Zone rogues gallery and Zod's status as recurring Superman foe; establishes 40-year sentence and Zone as prison dimension; later retconned that Zod's duplicates foreshadow Bizarro.

The Pocket Universe Execution - Superman Becomes an ExecutionerSuperman #22 (October 1988) 'The Pocket Universe' by John Byrne; continued in Adventures of Superman #444 and Action Comics #591

1988

After Crisis on Infinite Earths removed Kryptonian survivors to keep Superman the 'last son,' writer John Byrne creates a 'pocket universe' created by the Time Trapper that mimics pre-Crisis Krypton. Its Zod (with Quex-Ul and Zaora) escapes the Phantom Zone after Superboy's death and systematically exterminates all life on that Earth's humanity — razing cities and terraforming. The survivors summon the main-universe Superman, who lures the three to his Earth, strips their powers with gold kryptonite (harmless to him as an outsider), and then, faced with unrepentant killers who vow to regain powers and kill again, and with no prison capable of holding them, executes them with green kryptonite. Superman is left traumatized and later exiles himself to space. Most controversial Zod story; defines post-Crisis Superman's moral code and guilt; leads to Superman's self-exile and the 'Superman in Exile' arc; often cited in scholarly discussions of superhero ethics; this Zod is dead and does not return.

The Russian Zod - Avruiskin, Pokolistan, and the Red Sun PlotAction Comics #785 (January 2002) introduction; arc through Action Comics #786-789, Superman #189-190 (2002-2003) by Joe Kelly, Pascual Ferry, Duncan Rouleau

2002-2003

A new General Zod appears claiming to be Kryptonian, conquering the fictional former Soviet nation Pokolistan. Revealed as Avruiskin, a human born to parents exposed to kryptonite radiation in a Soviet experiment, giving him Kryptonian-like powers inverted: empowered by red sunlight, weakened by yellow. Raised in a KGB lab as 'Zed,' hearing the voice of the dead Pocket Universe Zod, he builds filtering armor to survive under yellow sun. His long plan is to turn Earth's sun red (with help from Soviet tech and later Lex Luthor's machinations), depowering Superman and making himself supreme. Lex Luthor restores the yellow sun with a solar restoration, Superman regains powers and defeats Zod, who dies when he punches the now-invulnerable Superman at super-speed and shatters his own depowered body. Explains editorial attempt to have a Zod while keeping Superman last Kryptonian; introduces inversion of Kryptonian power source; Zod's death is definitive for this version; later retconned away when true Kryptonian Zod returns in 2006.

Sentenced to death, they are saved by Jor-El who argues for Phantom Zone imprisonment and becomes their jailer — earning Zod's eternal hatred for House El. In the Zone, Ursa bears a son, Lor-Zod, who is corporeal and can escape. He reaches Earth and is adopted by Clark and Lois as Chris Kent. Zod, Ursa, Non and 25 other Zone criminals escape during a breach, conquer Metropolis, trap Superman in the Zone, and attempt to transform Earth into New Krypton. Superman escapes with Mon-El's help, allies temporarily with Lex Luthor, Metallo, Parasite and Bizarro to kill several Kryptonians, and forces Zod's army back into the Zone. Zod and Ursa take Chris with them as they are pulled back. The Phantom Zone's General on Screen - From Krypton to Earth in FilmSuperman: The Movie (1978) and Superman II (1980) - Richard Donner/Richard Lester; Man of Steel (2013) - Zack Snyder

1978-2013

Film Zod condenses comics lore: In Superman (1978), General Zod, Ursa and Non are tried by Jor-El and the Kryptonian Council for insurrection and sentenced to the Phantom Zone (a rotating glass-like shard). In Superman II (1980), a missile explosion frees them; they arrive on Earth, gain powers, and demand humanity kneel, battling Superman in Metropolis and the Fortress of Solitude. Zod's calm, aristocratic cruelty and line 'Kneel before Zod!' become definitive. In Man of Steel (2013), Zod is reimagined as a genetically engineered warrior (born to be a general) who leads a coup against Krypton's Council as the planet dies, kills Jor-El, is sentenced to the Zone, escapes after Krypton's destruction, and seeks to terraform Earth into New Krypton using the World Engine and the Codex (genetic future of Krypton) hidden in Kal-El. Superman kills Zod by breaking his neck to stop him from murdering a family with heat vision — a controversial mirroring of the 1988 comics execution. Zod's catchphrase 'Kneel before Zod!' comes from the original 1961 comics. — The phrase never appears in Silver Age comics. It was written for Terence Stamp in Superman II (1980) and later imported into comics, first used in comics in the late 1980s/For Tomorrow era and popularized post-2006.

General Zod is Superman's uncle or relative. — Zod has no blood relation to House of El. He is a military contemporary of Jor-El who hates House El for imprisoning him. Lor-Zod is Zod's son, not related to Superman except as adoptive brother to Chris Kent when Clark and Lois adopt him.

Zod has the same weakness to kryptonite as Superman in all versions. — Russian Zod (Avruiskin) has inverted weakness: yellow sun weakens him, red sun empowers him, requiring armor. Main Kryptonian Zod shares Superman's green kryptonite and red sun vulnerabilities.

Zod was always depicted with black hair, beard, and black armor. — Original Zod (1961-1979) was bald and clean-shaven. The hair/goatee/armor look is entirely derived from Terence Stamp's 1978/1980 film costume, retroactively adopted by comics.

Faora and Ursa are the same character. — Faora Hu-Ul (1977) is a Kryptonian serial killer, distinct from Ursa (film-created 1980, comics-canonized 2007 as Zod's wife). Man of Steel uses Faora-Ul in Ursa's role, increasing confusion. DC now has both separately.
