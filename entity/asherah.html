Asherah

also known asAšerdu(š)Ašertu(š)Athirat
Typegoddess
Who It Is
Form

Asherah is often depicted as a maternal figure, surrounded by trees, animals, and other symbols of fertility and abundance. In some depictions, she is shown as a powerful, goddess-like figure, wielding a sceptre or other symbols of authority.

Powers

Fertility Asherah's power to bring forth fertility and abundance is a central aspect of her character and role in ancient Near Eastern cultures.

Protection Asherah is often depicted as a protective figure, who watches over her people and the land.

Authority Asherah's power to command respect and obedience is a key aspect of her character and role in ancient Near Eastern cultures.

Accounts

Asherah is depicted as the consort of El, the chief god of the Ugaritic pantheon. Together, they have a number of children, including Baal and Anat.

Asherah is depicted as the goddess of the sea, who is worshipped by the people of Israel. However, her role is later subsumed by the worship of Yahweh.

Scholarly Debates
Asherah's role in ancient Israelite culture
Asherah was a major goddess in ancient Israelite culture, worshipped by the people of Israel. ⇌ Asherah's role was later subsumed by the worship of Yahweh, and she was relegated to a minor or forgotten role.
What We Don't Know

There are several gaps in our understanding of Asherah, including her role in ancient Israelite culture and the significance of her worship in the ancient Near East.

A way to hold it in your mind

Asherah's story is a fascinating and complex one, full of contradictions and paradoxes. Her role in ancient Near Eastern cultures and traditions is still not fully understood, and her worship continues to be the subject of debate and discussion.
