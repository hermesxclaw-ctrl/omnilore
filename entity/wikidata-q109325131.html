Who It Is

In a war of masked aces and teenage Newtypes, the most dangerous man in Mobile Suit Zeta Gundam never fires a beam rifle — he signs a funding contract. Commodore Blex Forer, a graying, bearded Federation officer who decides his own government has become the enemy, builds the Anti-Earth Union Group from a senatorial office and an Anaheim Electronics ledger, and his murder halfway through the Gryps Conflict proves that in the Universal Century, politics kills as surely as any mobile suit.

Attested: Principled, moderate, and politically astute. Dialogue in episodes 1, 7, and 37 portrays him as a senatorial idealist who believes spacenoids must be freed from Earth-centric tyranny through political and economic pressure, not merely military force. He is paternal and calm, mediating between hot-headed pilots (Kamille, Quattro) and cautious captains (Henken, Bright). He shows willingness to compromise with corporate power (Anaheim Electronics) while openly clashing with Titans leaders Jamitov Hymem and Bask Om over the gassing of Colony 30 Bunch and other atrocities. Political Leadership and Founding Authority

As founder and supreme leader of the Anti-Earth Union Group (AEUG), Blex holds ultimate political authority over AEUG operations. He authorizes operations such as the theft of the Gundam Mk-II prototypes, the Jaburo drop operation, and the alliance with Karaba. His orders are obeyed by Quattro Bajeena, Henken Bekkener, and Bright Noa without question until his death.

Limit: Authority is political, not absolute military command; tactical command aboard Argama is delegated to Henken and Bright. After his death, AEUG leadership fragments, showing his authority was personal and not institutionalized.

Senatorial Influence and Corporate Negotiation

Uses his position as a Federation commodore and assembly member to lobby and secure funding and materiel from Anaheim Electronics (AE), whose business was hurt by Federation trade embargoes on colonies. This funding provides the Argama, Rick Dias, and later Zeta Gundam development.

Limit: Dependent on Anaheim's self-interest; Wong Lee and other Anaheim figures later exert leverage over AEUG, showing Blex's deal creates corporate entanglement.

Oratory and Ideological Framing

Articulates AEUG's ideology: opposition to Titans' Earth-supremacist oppression and advocacy for human migration to space and Spacenoid autonomy. His final recorded will and political testament is carried to Dakar by Quattro/Char, forming the basis of Char's famous speech to the Federation Assembly.

Limit: He does not live to deliver the Dakar speech himself; his ideology is mediated through Char, whose interpretation may differ from Blex's more moderate federalism.

Strategic Coalition Building

Forges the AEUG-Karaba-Anaheim alliance network, linking space-based AEUG with Earth-based resistance (Karaba led by Hayato Kobayashi and Amuro Ray) and neutral Side 6. Enables two-front pressure on Titans.

Limit: Coalition is fragile; Karaba and AEUG have divergent priorities, and Anaheim's profit motives sometimes conflict with AEUG ideals.

No Mobile Suit Combat or Newtype Ability

Explicit negative ability: Blex is a non-pilot, non-Newtype commander. He never sorties, has no personal mobile suit, and exhibits no psychic or enhanced spatial awareness. His power is entirely institutional and rhetorical.

Limit: Physically vulnerable; his lack of combat ability and his trust in crew security directly leads to his assassination aboard ship.

Accounts I
The Founder — From Commodore to DissidentMobile Suit Zeta Gundam TV anime episodes 1-2; Tomino novel Vol.1; Gundam Officials and Entertainment Bible 2 backstory entries

UC 0083-0087 (in-universe); production 1985

After Operation Stardust (UC 0083) and the subsequent formation of the elite Titans task force, Federation Commodore Blex Forer becomes one of the first senior officers to see the Earth Federation government sliding into authoritarianism under Jamitov Hymem and Bask Om. Using his seat in the Federation Assembly and his naval commodore status, Blex approaches executives at Anaheim Electronics — whose lunar and colonial business has been damaged by Federation embargoes — and persuades them to secretly fund an anti-government movement. With Anaheim money and disaffected Federation officers (including former Zeon ace Char Aznable as 'Quattro Bajeena' and veteran captain Henken Bekkener), he formally establishes the Anti-Earth Union Group (AEUG) in UC 0086-0087, operating from the newly built Argama. The story is told in exposition and flashback dialogue rather than depicted as a full episode arc. Operation Gundam Jack — Stealing the Mk-IIMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 1 'The Black Gundam' and Episode 2 'Departure'

UC 0087 March (in-universe); aired March 1985

Under Blex's overall command but executed in the field by Quattro Bajeena, Apolly Bay, and Roberto, AEUG agents infiltrate Green Noa 1 (later Gryps) colony to capture the Titans' new RX-178 Gundam Mk-II prototypes. The operation succeeds largely because Kamille Bidan, a civilian teenager who has just assaulted Titans pilot Jerid Messa, helps Quattro steal two of the three units. Blex, aboard Argama, authorizes the operation and immediately grants sanctuary to Kamille and later to defector Emma Sheen, demonstrating his policy of welcoming Titans defectors and civilian talent. He appears via video and on Argama's bridge, calmly directing the political fallout. AEUG gains its first Gundam-type mobile suits; Kamille and Emma join AEUG, becoming central pilots; Titans' prestige is damaged, accelerating Bask Om's brutal reprisals. Validates Blex's strategy of elite infiltration over open battle.

Jaburo, Kilimanjaro, and the Two-Front WarMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episodes 11-13 (Jaburo descent) and Episodes 30-33 (Kilimanjaro)

UC 0087 mid-year; aired May-August 1985

Blex coordinates AEUG's Earth operations in tandem with Karaba, the Earth-based resistance. He orders the Argama to support Karaba's attack on Titans headquarters at Jaburo (revealing Titans have already abandoned it and set it to self-destruct) and later the assault on Titans' Kilimanjaro base. Though he remains in space, his strategic decisions — to commit AEUG forces to Earth despite risk, and to rescue Kamille after his encounter with Four Murasame — show his willingness to sustain casualties for political legitimacy. Dialogue in these episodes has Blex clashing verbally (via communications) with Jamitov and Bask over the Titans' colony gassing and civilian oppression, reinforcing his role as AEUG's moral voice. Jaburo operation exposes Titans' willingness to destroy their own base to kill AEUG; Kilimanjaro operation leads to Four Murasame's death and deepens Kamille's trauma, but weakens Titans' Earth infrastructure. Demonstrates Blex's coalition strategy and its human cost.

Accounts II
The Dakar Will — Preparing the Political OffensiveMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episodes 36-37, culminating in Episode 38 'Reccoa's Shadow' and Episode 39 'Lake of Dakar'

UC 0087 November; aired November 1985

As AEUG prepares to expose Titans atrocities to the Federation Assembly at Dakar, Blex records his political testament and explicitly tasks Quattro Bajeena (Char Aznable) with delivering AEUG's case to the world. He tells Quattro that the time for hiding behind the alias is over and that Spacenoids need a true leader to speak for them. Blex's plan is to use the Assembly session — broadcast globally — to present evidence of the 30 Bunch colony gassing and other Titans crimes, shifting the conflict from purely military to political legitimacy. He remains aboard Argama to maintain fleet command while Quattro descends to Earth. Sets up the most famous political scene in UC Gundam — Char's Dakar speech — which is explicitly presented as fulfilling Blex's dying wish. Establishes Blex as the ideological author of AEUG's post-war vision (humanity's migration to space).

Assassination on the ArgamaMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 37 'Dakar Days' (Japanese: Dakāru no Hi)

UC 0087 November; aired 23 November 1985

While the Argama is in Earth orbit preparing for the Dakar operation, an assassin — a Titans sympathizer who has infiltrated the ship's crew (identified in scripts and reference books as a Titans agent; in some summaries as a crewman swayed by Titans propaganda) — approaches Blex in his quarters/corridor and shoots him at close range. Mortally wounded, Blex lives long enough to be found by Quattro and Kamille. With his last strength he urges Quattro to go to Dakar and speak in his place, saying AEUG's future now depends on him. He dies aboard Argama, and his body is later mourned by the crew. His death is not a battlefield casualty but a political murder, underscoring that Titans' reach extends inside AEUG's flagship. Immediate leadership crisis in AEUG; Quattro/Char is forced from his self-imposed role as 'just a pilot' into political leadership; Bright Noa and Henken must stabilize fleet morale. Legacy — The Dakar Speech and the End of the Gryps ConflictMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episodes 38-50 (especially Episodes 39 and 50); Gundam Officials legacy entry

UC 0087 November - February UC 0088; aired November 1985 - February 1986

Though dead, Blex's political project dominates the final third of Zeta Gundam. Char's 15-minute speech before the Federation Assembly at Dakar — broadcast across Earth and Space — is repeatedly framed by characters as 'Blex's will' and 'Blex's dream.' It denounces Titans' crimes and calls for humanity's migration to space, swaying public opinion and isolating Jamitov. After the final three-way battle at Gryps 2 (AEUG vs Titans vs Axis), AEUG is victorious militarily but leaderless; Bright Noa becomes the senior surviving commander. In epilogue materials and sequel Mobile Suit Gundam ZZ (1986), AEUG's continued existence is presented as carrying on Blex's founding principles, even as the organization fractures without his moderating influence. Blex's ideology outlives him and becomes canonical UC history; his assassination is cited in-universe as proof of Titans' illegitimacy and in meta-text as Tomino's statement that idealistic reformers are vulnerable to political violence. Sets up thematic transition to Gundam ZZ, where AEUG lacks Blex's restraint.

Blex Forer was a Newtype or had psychic powers like Kamille or Char. — No canonical source attributes Newtype abilities to Blex. He is explicitly a non-Newtype 'Oldtype' politician and officer. His strength is institutional and rhetorical, not psychic. Tomino's statement that Kamille has the strongest Newtype powers in Zeta underscores that senior officers like Blex are not Newtypes.

Blex himself delivered the famous Dakar speech that denounced the Titans. — Blex was assassinated before the Dakar Assembly session. He tasked Quattro Bajeena (Char Aznable) with delivering the speech in his place, and Char's 15-minute address is presented as carrying out Blex's will. Blex's own political testament was recorded/written, not spoken at Dakar.

Blex survives in the A New Translation film trilogy's altered ending. — Blex dies in all continuities — TV, novel, and A New Translation films. The films do alter other fates (e.g., Kamille's ending is less tragic), but Blex's assassination in Film II: Lovers is retained and still serves as the catalyst for Dakar. No official version has him survive the Gryps Conflict.

Blex Forer and Henken Bekkener are the same person or interchangeable AEUG leaders. — They are distinct characters with different roles: Blex is the supreme political founder/leader; Henken is a ship captain and tactical commander who co-operates the Argama. They appear together in multiple scenes and have separate voice actors (Takaya Hashi for Blex, Jūrōta Kosugi for Henken) and separate deaths (Blex assassinated episode 37, Henken killed later at Gryps 2).
