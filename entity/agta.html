Who It Is

Agta (not to be confused with the Aeta indigenous people) are dark-skinned, hairy forest spirits in Bicolano and Visayan mythology that dwell in caves and deep forests. They are described as large, ape-like humanoids with black or very dark skin, sharp teeth, long claws, and backward-facing feet that make it impossible to track their movements. Unlike diwatas who are neutral or benevolent, agta are generally malevolent — they kidnay children who wander too far into the forest, steal livestock, and lead hunters to their deaths in ravines. They are nocturnal and emerge only on moonless nights. Some stories describe them as the souls of evil people who were reincarnated as forest monsters. In Bicol regions, agta are associated with specific caves and are blamed for disappearances in the wilderness. To ward off agta, Filipinos carry salt, avoid whistling at night (which attracts them), and draw crosses on doors. The agta belief is closely related to the capre (a giant cigar-smoking creature) and the bulong (whispering spirit). In modern times, the agta is sometimes conflated with Bigfoot in cryptozoological discussions.
