Who It Is

Jūratė is the beautiful queen of the Baltic Sea, a Lithuanian sea goddess who rules a magnificent underwater castle made entirely of amber. She is one of the most romantic figures in Baltic mythology. According to legend, Jūratė fell in love with Kastytis, a mortal fisherman who dared to fish in her sacred waters. In some versions, she first became angry and tried to punish him, but was won over by his steadfastness and beauty. They lived together in her amber castle beneath the waves — but their love broke the natural order of the sea. The thunder god Perkūnas, angered that a goddess would consort with a mortal, struck the amber castle with lightning and shattered it, killing Kastytis and chaining Jūratė to the ruins of her palace for eternity. The bits of amber that wash up on Baltic shores after storms are said to be fragments of Jūratė's shattered castle — or her frozen tears, still mourning her lost love. Jūratė appears in the 18th-century collection of Lithuanian legends by Liudvikas Adomas Jucevičius and was later adapted by the poet Maironis into a national epic. She remains a powerful cultural symbol of Lithuania's connection to the sea and the national longing for freedom.
