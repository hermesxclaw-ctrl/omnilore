Who It Is

Beneath the amber-strewn waves of the Baltic, there once reigned a queen whose palace shimmered with coral and gold—and whose love for a mortal fisherman would shatter her undersea kingdom and scatter its treasures across the shores of Lithuania forever.

Jūratė is portrayed as a powerful, independent sea queen who is initially wary of humans but falls deeply in love with the mortal fisherman Kastytis. She is compassionate and romantic, but also subject to the authority of the thunder god Perkūnas, who punishes her for her transgression.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Jūratė is derived from the Lithuanian word 'jūra' meaning 'sea'. The suffix '-atė' is a feminine ending, thus Jūratė can be interpreted as 'she of the sea' or 'sea goddess'.

Pronunciation — YOO-rah-teh (IPA: /juːˈrɑːteɪ/ or /ˈjuːrəteɪ/ in English approximation)

Aliases — Jurate, Jūratė (goddess)

Powers
Control over the sea

As a sea goddess/queen, Jūratė has authority over the Baltic Sea, its waves, and its creatures. She can calm or stir the waters.

Limit: Her power is subordinate to Perkūnas, the thunder god, who can override her will.

Immortality

As a divine being, she does not age and lives for millennia.

Limit: She can be punished by higher gods, but not killed permanently.

Creation of amber

Her palace is made of amber, and her tears are said to turn into amber, which washes ashore.

Limit: Amber creation is a byproduct of her existence, not a deliberate power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Jūratė is typically described as a beautiful young woman with long golden hair, often depicted wearing a crown and robes made of sea foam and silk. She resides in an amber palace at the bottom of the Baltic Sea.

Symbols — Amber is the primary symbol associated with Jūratė; the legend explains that pieces of amber found on the shore are fragments of her destroyed palace. She is also associated with the sea, coral, and pearls.

Domains — She dwells in an underwater palace made of amber in the Baltic Sea, near the Lithuanian coast. Her domain is the sea, and she has power over its creatures and treasures.

Accounts I
Jūratė and KastytisLithuanian folklore

Pre-Christian era (oral tradition), recorded in 19th century

Jūratė, the queen of the Baltic Sea, lived in an amber palace at the bottom of the sea. She heard the beautiful singing of Kastytis, a young fisherman, and rose to the surface to see him. They fell in love and spent time together. However, the thunder god Perkūnas, angered by Jūratė's union with a mortal, struck her palace with lightning, shattering it into pieces. The amber fragments washed ashore, and Jūratė was chained to the ruins of her palace as punishment. The destruction of the palace explains the presence of amber on Baltic shores. Jūratė's fate is tragic, and she is often depicted as forever mourning her lost love.
