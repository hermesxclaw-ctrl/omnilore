Who It Is

In the thunderous crossroads of Afro-Brazilian faith, three forces converge: Ogum, the iron-handed opener of ways; Iansã, the tempestuous queen of winds and storms; and Xangô, the fiery orisha of justice whose axe splits the sky. Together they form a triad of power, passion, and cosmic order, yet each carries a distinct story and a unique domain that shapes the lives of millions.

Ogum is stubborn, hardworking, and direct; he is the patron of blacksmiths, soldiers, and all who work with metal. Iansã is passionate, independent, and tempestuous; she is a warrior queen who controls storms and the winds. Xangô is just, powerful, and charismatic, but also quick-tempered and prone to excess; he is the enforcer of cosmic and social order.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Ogum derives from Yoruba Ògún, associated with iron and war. Iansã is a Brazilian adaptation of Yoruba Yánsàn, a name linked to the River Niger (Odò Òyá). Xangô is a Brazilian spelling of Yoruba Sàngó, the fourth king of Oyó.

Pronunciation — oh-GOOM, ee-ahn-SAHN, shan-GOH (Brazilian Portuguese); Yoruba: oh-GOON, yan-SAN, shan-GOH

Aliases — Ogun, Ogoun, Ogúm, Iansan, Oyá, Yansã

Ownership — Yoruba people of West Africa (primarily Nigeria, Benin, Togo); Afro-Brazilian religions (Candomblé, Umbanda); also practiced in Cuban Santería, Haitian Vodou, and other African dia

Powers
Iron mastery

Ogum can forge and control all iron and steel, granting him power over weapons and tools.

Limit: Depends on the availability of iron; must be honored with offerings.

Warfare and protection

Ogum is a fierce warrior who clears paths and protects his devotees in conflict.

Limit: His protection is conditional on proper devotion.

Control of winds and storms

Iansã can summon hurricanes, tornadoes, and lightning, and she is the mistress of the winds.

Limit: Her powers are tied to her role as a warrior; she cannot act against the will of Olodumare.

Power over the dead

Iansã is the only female orisha who can enter the realm of the dead and guide souls; she is associated with the ancestors.

Limit: She must use her eruexin to control the spirits.

Thunder and lightning

Xangô throws lightning bolts and creates thunder, often using his double-headed axe.

Limit: He is subject to the will of Olodumare and the Ifá oracle.

Justice and judgment

Xangô is the orisha of justice, punishing wrongdoers and protecting the innocent.

Limit: His justice is harsh and swift, but he can be deceived by clever arguments.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ogum is typically depicted as a strong warrior wearing iron or red and green attire, carrying a machete or sword and sometimes a shield. Iansã is depicted as a fierce woman with flowing hair, often wearing a skirt with nine panels (representing the nine paths of the dead) and holding an eruexin (fly-whisk) or a sword. Xangô is depicted as a regal figure with a double-headed axe (oxê), often wearing red and white, with a crown or a warrior's helme

Symbols — Ogum: iron tools, machete, anvil, dog, palm wine. Iansã: lightning, wind, rainbow, horsehair fly-whisk, copper, the number nine. Xangô: double-headed axe, mortar, lightning, the colors red and white, the number six.

Domains — Ogum: roads, forests, frontiers, forges, and all places where iron is found. Iansã: wind, storms, lightning, the marketplace, and the cemetery (as a psychopomp). Xangô: thunder, lightning, fire, justice, and the royal court.

Accounts I
Ogum and the Path to the WorldYoruba mythology

Mythic

In the beginning, the world was a swampy morass. Ogum descended from the heavens on a spider's web and used his iron machete to clear the wilderness, creating paths for humanity and the other orishas to follow. Ogum became the opener of roads and the patron of all who work with iron.

Iansã and the Fire of XangôYoruba mythology

Mythic

Iansã, as Oyá, was married to Xangô. She stole the secret of fire (or lightning) from the orisha of thunder and gave it to humanity, or she helped Xangô acquire his power. In some versions, she is the one who wields the lightning before Xangô. Iansã gained power over storms and winds, and became known as a warrior queen.

Accounts II
Xangô and the Judgment of the WorldYoruba mythology

Mythic

Xangô, as the fourth king of Oyó, was a powerful and just ruler, but his pride led to his downfall. He was exiled and, in his anger, he called down lightning and destroyed his own city. In some versions, he ascended to heaven and became the orisha of thunder. Xangô became the orisha of justice and thunder, but his story warns against hubris.

The Love Triangle of Xangô, Iansã, and OxumAfro-Brazilian mythology

Contemporary

Xangô is married to both Iansã and Oxum. Iansã is the senior wife, fierce and proud, while Oxum is the younger, sweet and beautiful. The two wives often quarrel, and Xangô must balance his love and his authority. This story illustrates the dynamics of polygamy and the importance of each orisha's domain.
