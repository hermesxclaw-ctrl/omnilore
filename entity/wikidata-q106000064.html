Who It Is

In the late 1970s, a horse named Maruzensky thundered across Japanese racetracks with a stride so devastating that rivals' jockeys openly admitted relief at not facing him again. Undefeated in all seven starts, he won the Asahi Hai Sansai Stakes by an astonishing thirteen lengths, setting a course record that stood for fourteen years. But this 'Supercar' of the turf was denied the ultimate glory of the Japanese Classics—not by defeat, but by a rule that barred foreign-bred horses, even those conceived abroad. His story is one of brilliance, controversy, and a legacy that would reshape the breed.

On the track, Maruzensky was known for his explosive speed and ability to open up large leads. He displayed a tendency to 'switch off' when in front, as seen in the Nihon Tampa Sho where he almost lost focus. Off the track, he was reportedly a calm and sensible horse, though the source does not provide extensive detail.

Explosive acceleration

Maruzensky was renowned for his ability to accelerate rapidly and build large leads, often leaving rivals far behind. His 13-length victory in the Asahi Hai Sansai Stakes is a testament to this.

Limit: His tendency to relax when in front could allow rivals to close, as seen in the Nihon Tampa Sho.

Accounts I
The Purchase of ShillProvided source material

1973

Owner Zenkichi Hashimoto, a cattle rancher new to horse racing, bought the mare Shill at Keeneland Sales for $300,000 after feeling insulted by his interpreter's assumption that he couldn't afford her. The mare was pregnant with a Nijinsky foal, which became Maruzensky. Acquired the dam of a future champion.

The Asahi Hai Sansai Stakes TriumphProvided source material

1976

In his third start, Maruzensky won the Asahi Hai Sansai Stakes by an astonishing 13 lengths, setting a course record that stood for 14 years. Jockey Seiichi Nakanowatari commented that he couldn't hear the footsteps of other horses past the third corner. Earned the nickname 'Supercar' and was named champion two-year-old.

Accounts II
The Derby ControversyProvided source material

1977

Maruzensky was barred from entering the Japanese Classics due to a rule that excluded horses brought over from abroad, even as fetuses. This caused significant controversy, with jockey Nakanowatari pleading to let the horse run, and owner Hashimoto considering legal action. Maruzensky never competed in the Classics, leaving his true potential against the best of his generation untested.

The Nihon Tampa ShoProvided source material

1977

In front of nearly 80,000 spectators, Maruzensky won the Nihon Tampa Sho by 7 lengths despite a moment of near-collapse when he lost focus and was almost passed by Inter Spencer. He regained the lead and won convincingly. Further solidified his reputation as a champion.

Retirement and Stud CareerProvided source material

1978-1997

After developing tendonitis, Maruzensky was retired and stood at stud in Hokkaido. He became a successful sire, producing multiple Grade I winners, including Derby winner Sakura Chiyono O, and was also a notable broodmare sire. Left a lasting legacy on the Japanese thoroughbred breed.
