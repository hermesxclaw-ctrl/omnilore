Who It Is

On a low ridge above the Dyle at Grez-Doiceau, a stone church and a vanished motte still mark the power of a Brabant knight whose name survives more in charters and street signs than in stories: Onulphus de Aa. History remembers him as a small lord caught between duke and abbey; folklore remembers him as the Chevalier Onulphe, the rider who never quite came home.

Charter evidence shows only a landholding knight acting as witness/donor in monastic transactions - typical pious lordly behavior. Chronicle context suggests loyalty to the Duke of Brabant (Godfrey I) during the Investiture-era conflicts. Folkloric layer (19th-century retellings) recasts him as pious founder, brave crusader, or tragic returning lord. No authentic personality anecdotes survive. All character traits (brave, pious, generous, ill-fated) are reconstructed from genre conventions of foundation legends and crusader tales, not from primary testimony. Labeled: disputed/reconstructed.

Powers
Knightly martial prowess

As a miles, trained in mounted combat with lance, sword, and shield; capable of leading retainers and holding a motte. Folkloric elaboration credits him with slaying a beast/dragon threatening Grez.

Limit: No superhuman powers attested. Subject to feudal obligations, limited manpower of a small seigneurie, vulnerability to ducal politics and monastic land claims. Dragon-slaying, if recounted, is presented as single feat with divine aid, not repeatable power.

Seigneurial and patronage authority

Right to hold court, collect dues, appoint or patronize parish clergy (advowson), and donate land to Affligem Abbey for spiritual benefit.

Limit: Subordinate to Duke of Brabant; contested by rival lineages (e.g., lords of Grez). Donations required family consent and monastic acceptance. No absolute sovereignty.

Pious intercession (posthumous legendary)

In some local retellings, the chevalier's pious foundation ensures protection of the village or his own soul; his ghost is said to watch over Grez or to haunt the old castle hill until a vow is fulfilled.

Limit: Not an active power in life; posthumous and conditional on Christian piety. Ghost motif is moral warning, not controllable ability.

Accounts I
The charter witness of AffligemCartulaire d'Affligem (charter c. 1096-1105, copy 12th c.) as summarized by de Marneffe and Wauters

Late 11th century (historical)

Onulphus de Aa appears among the milites witnessing or consenting to a donation of land or rights to the newly founded Benedictine Abbey of Affligem (founded 1062, patronized by the dukes). The act places him within the network of Brabantine knights who secured their salvation and status through monastic endowment. No narrative of deeds is given; his presence attests to his rank and landholding. Establishes historical existence of the Aa lineage at Grez; provides terminus for family patronage that later folklore would expand into foundation legends.

The foundation of Bossut churchTarlier & Wauters, Canton de Wavre: Grez-Doiceau; Verbesselt parish history

Recorded 19th century, claimed to reflect 11th-12th century foundation

Tradition holds that the Chevalier Onulphe founded or richly endowed the church of Saint-Martin at Bossut (now Bossut-Gottechain), part of his domain, either upon his return from pilgrimage/crusade or to expiate a sin. The church's patronage later passed to Affligem, suggesting an early seigneurial foundation. The story explains the Aa arms in the church and the lord's burial wish there. Etiological legend for parish origins and for the visible heraldry in the church; reinforces image of pious founder-knight.

The crusader's departure and delayed returnLocal oral tradition as reported in 19th-century communal histories and folklore compendia (Devogel-type collections)

Legend set c. 1096-1099 (First Crusade), recorded 1800s

Onulphe joins the First Crusade in the following of Godfrey of Bouillon or the Duke of Brabant, leaving his lands and young wife under the protection of kin and monks. Years pass with no news; he is presumed dead. The motif follows the widespread 'returning crusader' tale type (cf. similar legends at Grez and across Brabant). Sets up the tragic or miraculous return narrative; explains absentee lordship and monastic administration of lands in his absence.

Accounts II
The beast of Grez (dragon/wolf of the Dyle)Folkloric motif reported in Walloon legend summaries; no medieval source located

Legendary time (undated, placed in Onulphe's lordship), recorded 19th-20th century

A beast - described variously as dragon, wyvern, loup-garou, or large wolf - haunts the woods or marshes of the Dyle near Grez, demanding tribute or threatening livestock and children. The Chevalier Onulphe confronts and slays it, often with the aid of a vow to the Virgin or Saint George, thereby freeing the village. This is the likely source of the modern 'beast' archetype tag. Explains toponyms or natural features (e.g., dragon's den, beast's ford) and provides heroic charter for the lineage; aligns Onulphe with widespread Brabantine dragon-slayer knights.

The ghost knight of the motteOral tradition reported in local heritage brochures and 20th-century folklore notes for Grez-Doiceau

Post-medieval haunting legend, recorded 19th-20th century

After his death (in battle, on crusade, or of illness), Onulphe's spirit is said to ride at night around the old motte of Grez or along the Aa stream, guarding treasure or awaiting fulfillment of an unkept vow to the church. Farmers claim to hear hooves or see a white rider on misty evenings. The haunting ends when the church bells are rung or a mass is said for his soul. Moral tale about keeping vows and paying church dues; provides atmospheric heritage narrative for castle site, now invisible.

The inheritance dispute of Aa and GrezReconstructed from charter evidence and genealogical histories (Wauters, Verbesselt, modern genealogical summaries of the House of Aa)

Late 11th - 12th century (historical reconstruction)

The small lineage of Aa, holding both Aa near Brussels and Grez on the Dyle, faces partition and competition with neighboring lords (Grez, Dongelberg) and with ducal centralization. Onulphe's generation is presented as the moment when Grez becomes the main seat, with later descendants styling themselves 'de Grez' rather than 'de Aa'. Charters show gradual alienation of lands to Affligem and other abbeys, indicating financial or political pressure. Explains disappearance of the Aa name from Grez and the absorption of the seigneurie into larger Brabantine lordships; historically plausible but not tied to a single narrative source with Onulphe as protagonist.

Chevalier Onulphe d'Aa was a mythical beast or monster. — He is consistently documented as a human knight (miles) of the Brabantine nobility. The 'beast' label likely confuses the knight with the beast he legendarily fought, or is a database error.

Onulphe was a famous crusader knight in the army of Godfrey of Bouillon with a detailed crusade biography. — No crusader roll or contemporary chronicle names Onulphus de Aa as a crusader. The crusader story is a 19th-century romantic elaboration using the common 'returning crusader' tale type. His historical footprint is a single charter witness role in Brabant, not the Holy Land.

He single-handedly built the church of Bossut-Gottechain and is buried there with a visible tomb. — Parish history shows gradual endowment and transfer of patronage to Affligem; no verified tomb or foundation inscription for Onulphe survives in Bossut church. The church contains later heraldry of the Aa/Grez families, not a personal monument.

The Chevalier Onulphe legend is ancient, dating to the 11th century itself. — The earliest written attestations of the legendary elaborations (dragon, ghost, crusader return) date to the 19th century. The 11th-century layer is only a name in a charter witness list, with no narrative.
