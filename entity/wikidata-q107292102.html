The Approach

In the shadow of Mount Kyllene, where the gods once walked among mortals, a young man named Scephrus sought a private word with Apollo—and paid for it with his life. His story, preserved in a single ancient source, is a tragedy of suspicion, fratricide, and divine vengeance that echoes through the rituals of Tegea.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of murder, fratricide, and divine retribution.

Stories & Myths

The Death of Scephrus and Leimon

Source Pausanias, Description of Greece 8.53.2-4Culture GreekPeriod Mythic (pre-historic), recorded in the 2nd century CE

Summary

Scephrus, son of Tegeates and Maera, was the brother of Leimon. When Apollo and Artemis visited Tegea, Scephrus approached Apollo and had a private conversation with him. Leimon, suspecting a plot against himself, murdered Scephrus. To avenge Scephrus's death, Artemis shot and killed Leimon. In memory of Scephrus, the Tegean festival of Apollo Agyieus included honors for him.
