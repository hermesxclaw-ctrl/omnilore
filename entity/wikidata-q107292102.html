Who It Is

In the shadow of Mount Kyllene, where the gods once walked among mortals, a young man named Scephrus sought a private word with Apollo—and paid for it with his life. His story, preserved in a single ancient source, is a tragedy of suspicion, fratricide, and divine vengeance that echoes through the rituals of Tegea.

Scephrus is depicted as a youth who sought a private conversation with Apollo, suggesting trust and perhaps ambition. He is not otherwise characterized.

Accounts I
The Death of Scephrus and LeimonPausanias, Description of Greece 8.53.2-4

Mythic (pre-historic), recorded in the 2nd century CE

Scephrus, son of Tegeates and Maera, was the brother of Leimon. When Apollo and Artemis visited Tegea, Scephrus approached Apollo and had a private conversation with him. Leimon, suspecting a plot against himself, murdered Scephrus. To avenge Scephrus's death, Artemis shot and killed Leimon. In memory of Scephrus, the Tegean festival of Apollo Agyieus included honors for him. Leimon was killed by Artemis; Scephrus was honored in the local festival of Apollo Agyieus.
