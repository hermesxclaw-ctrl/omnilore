Who It Is

Xipe Totec ('Our Lord the Flayed One') is one of the most shocking and structurally essential gods of the Aztec pantheon — the deity of agricultural renewal whose iconography of flayed human skin directly dramatizes the seasonal death-and-rebirth of the maize. Xipe's yellow-red body (the color of maize under the husk) is always shown wearing the skin of a flayed sacrificial victim, the hands of which dangle loose from his wrists. This is not a chaotic horror: the flayed skin represents the dried husk of the corn that must be shed for new germination; the rotting skin (which the impersonators — xipeme — wore for 20 days during Tlacaxipehualiztli) represents the decomposition of the old season that fertilizes the new. Xipe's rattle-staff (chicahuaztli) shakes to summon the rains. In Aztec cosmology, Xipe corresponds to the red aspect of Tezcatlipoca — the 'Red Tezcatlipoca' who rules the eastern direction and the spring equinox, the season when the earth's 'skin' is renewed.
