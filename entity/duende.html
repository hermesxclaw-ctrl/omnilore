Who It Is

The Duende is a fascinating and enigmatic figure, full of mystery and intrigue. What do you think is the key to understanding this household spirit?

The Duende is known for its mischievous and cunning nature, often playing tricks on humans and causing chaos in the household. However, it is also revered as a source of inspiration and creativity, and is often called upon by artists and writers seeking to tap into its spark of imagination.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Duende' roughly translates to 'elf' or 'goblin' in Spanish, and is derived from the Latin word 'dunus', meaning 'hill' or 'mountain'.

Pronunciation — doo-ehn-day

Aliases — The Goblin of the House, Spirit of Flamenco, Imp of Inspiration

Ownership — Spanish folklore, with variations from Iberian, Ibero American, and Latin American cultures.

Powers
Inspiration

The Duende is said to possess the power of inspiration, able to spark creativity and imagination in those who seek it out. It is often called upon by artists, writers, and musicians seeking to tap into its spark of creativity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Duende is often depicted as a small, mischievous humanoid figure, with a fondness for dark colors and a penchant for hiding in the shadows. In some accounts, it is described as a tall, imposing figure with a commanding presence. Its appearance can vary depending on the region and cultural context.

Symbols — The Duende is often associated with symbols of creativity, inspiration, and mischief, such as the flamenco guitar, the bullfighter's cape, and the mischievous grin. It is also linked to the concept of the 'duende' in Spanish literature, which refers to the spark of creativity that ignites the imagination.

Domains — The Duende is said to inhabit the domestic sphere, often residing in the walls, floors, or ceilings of homes. It is also associated with the natural world, particularly the mountains and hills of the Iberian Peninsula.
