Who It Is

Your city looks the same as yesterday — same streetlights, same rain-slick alleys, same late-night convenience store. But in the World of Darkness, the streetlight flickers because something that has not breathed since the Crusades is watching from the roof, the alley is territory marked by a werewolf pack in a war against a spirit of pollution, and the clerk behind the counter is quietly rewriting reality because she believes she can. It is our world, turned one degree toward midnight, where every shadow hides a secret society that thinks it owns the night.

The setting is defined by 'personal horror' — player characters are monsters struggling to retain humanity or purpose while complicit in systemic horror. Powers
Vampiric Disciplines (Vampire: The Masquerade / Requiem)

Supernatural powers fueled by blood (Vitae). Classic Disciplines include Celerity (supernatural speed), Potence (strength), Fortitude (resilience), Dominate (mind control), Presence (emotional manipulation), Auspex (heightened senses/precognition), Obfuscate (invisibility), Protean (shapeshifting), Thaumaturgy/Blood Sorcery (blood magic). Each clan has affinity Disciplines.

Limit: Require expenditure of blood pool, risk of Hunger/Frenzy (Beast), limited by Generation/Blood Potency, vulnerable to fire, sunlight, stake through heart, True Faith. Thaumaturgy requires rituals and learning. Dominate requires eye contact and generation advantage in older editions.

Rage, Gifts and Rites (Werewolf: The Apocalypse / Forsaken)

Garou can shapeshift between five forms (Homid, Glabro, Crinos, Hispo, Lupus), regenerate, step sideways into the Umbra/Shadow, and invoke spirit Gifts (e.g., Claws of the Wyrm, Luna's Armor). Rage fuels extra actions and Frenzy.

Limit: Vulnerable to silver (aggravated, inhibits regeneration), susceptible to Frenzy (loss of control), Harano (despair), and spiritual corruption of the Wyrm. Gifts require Gnosis and spirit negotiation. Delirium causes humans to forget/ rationalize Crinos form.

True Magick - Spheres / Arcana (Mage: The Ascension / Awakening)

Mages alter consensual reality through paradigms. Ascension uses nine Spheres (Correspondence, Entropy, Forces, Life, Matter, Mind, Prime, Spirit, Time) rated 1-5; Awakening uses ten Arcana (Death, Fate, Forces, Life, Matter, Mind, Prime, Space, Spirit, Time). Effects range from coincidence to vulgar.

Limit: Paradox (reality backlash) for vulgar magick without witnesses; coincidental magick safer. Requires Arete/Gnosis rolls, Quintessence/Mana. Hubris leads to Quiet (madness) or Paradox flaws. Technocracy enforces consensus. Awakening adds Reach, Paradox, and Withstanding.

Arcanoi, Shadow and Corpus (Wraith: The Oblivion / Geist: The Sin-Eaters)

Wraiths (restless dead) use Arcanoi (e.g., Argos for travel through Tempest, Castigate to battle Shadow, Embody to manifest) powered by Pathos. Each wraith is haunted by a Shadow - a dark self with Thorns. Geist's Sin-Eaters have Bargains with geists granting Manifestations and Keys.

Limit: Anchored by Fetters and Passions; vulnerable to Oblivion, Spectres, soulforging, Harrowings. Shadow can seize control via Catharsis. Cannot easily affect living without Embody. Geist requires Synergy with geist.

Glamour, Cantrips and the Dreaming (Changeling: The Dreaming / The Lost)

Dreaming changelings (fae souls in human hosts) wield Glamour to cast Cantrips combining Arts (e.g., Chicanery, Sovereign) and Realms. Lost changelings (abducted by True Fae) use Contracts with fae entities and traverse the Hedge.

Limit: Vulnerable to Banality (disbelief, mundane routine) which can cause Undoing; Glamour must be harvested from creativity/dreams. Iron (cold iron) deals aggravated damage. Lost suffer Clarity damage and fetch complications.

Faith, Lores and Torment (Demon: The Fallen / Descent)

Fallen angels possess human hosts, using Lores (e.g., Lore of Flame, of the Forge, of Humanity) fueled by Faith. Torment measures corruption. Descent's Unchained demons use Embeds, Exploits, and Demon Form via Cover and pacts with the God-Machine.

Limit: Fallen are hunted by Earthbound, need Faith from thralls, risk Torment and apocalyptic form. Descent demons risk compromise of Cover and detection by Angels/God-Machine Infrastructure.

Storyteller/Storytelling System Dice Mechanics (Meta-ability)

System-level power: trait + skill dice pools (d10s) determine success. Original: Attribute + Ability vs variable difficulty (6 default). Chronicles: Attribute + Skill vs fixed 8, with exceptional success on 5+ successes, Conditions and Beats for narrative control.

Limit: Botches (1s cancel successes) in early editions; Hunger Dice and Willpower economy in V5. System does not simulate tactical combat depth; favors narrative adjudication.

Accounts I
The Book of Nod and Caine's CurseVampire: The Masquerade corebooks; The Book of Nod (1993) by Sam Chupp and Andrew Greenberg; Erciyes Fragments

1991-1998 (in-universe: mythic prehistory)

The foundational vampire myth: Caine, first murderer, is cursed by God and Lilith, becoming the first vampire. He Embraces three childer (Second Generation) who in turn Embrace the Antediluvians, progenitors of the 13 clans. The Book of Nod presents this as fragmentary scripture studied by Noddists, prophesying Gehenna when Antediluvians will awaken and devour their descendants. Justifies clan divisions, sect ideology (Camarilla's denial of Gehenna vs Sabbat's crusade), and the metaplot countdown to Time of Judgment. Tremere's diablerie of Saulot creates clan legitimacy crisis.

The Impergium, War of Rage and the ApocalypseWerewolf: The Apocalypse Core (1992); Werewolf: The Apocalypse Revised; Book of the Wyrm

1992-2000 (in-universe: 10,000 BCE to Apocalypse)

Gaia creates Garou to be her warriors against the Wyrm (corruption). Early Garou cull humans in the Impergium to control population, breeding resentment. The War of Rage sees Garou exterminate many other Changing Breeds (werebears, werecats). In modern nights, the Wyrm's manifestation as pollution, Pentex corporation, and the Weaver's stagnation drive the Garou toward inevitable Apocalypse. Explains Garou isolation, guilt, and doomed warrior identity. Leads to Time of Judgment scenario Apocalypse where players choose to fight, reconcile with Weaver/Wyrm, or die.

The Ascension War and the Avatar StormMage: The Ascension 1st-2nd Edition; The Ascension War supplement; Mage Revised and Avatar Storm metaplot (1998-2000)

1993-2000 (in-universe: Renaissance to 1999)

Nine Mystic Traditions (Order of Hermes, Verbena, etc.) battle the Technocratic Union (Iteration X, New World Order, etc.) for control of consensual reality. Mages Awaken Avatars and seek Ascension. In 1999, the Technocracy's assault on the Traditions and the Week of Nightmares causes the Avatar Storm, severing the Horizon Realms and trapping masters beyond the Gauntlet. Avatar Storm isolates Earth, kills or strands archmages, forces Revised edition's gritty, street-level focus. Sets up Ascension (Time of Judgment) where Ascension may be personal, collective, or impossible.

Accounts II
The Sixth Great Maelstrom and Ends of EmpireWraith: The Oblivion 2nd Edition; Wraith: The Great War (1996); Ends of Empire (1999) by Bruce Baugh et al.

1994-1999 (in-universe: 1999)

The afterlife's empire Stygia rules the Shadowlands. Five prior Maelstroms (great storms of Oblivion) shaped wraith history. In 1999, the Sixth Great Maelstrom erupts when the Labyrinth's Malfeans stir and the atomic horror of the 20th century weakens the Shroud. Stygia is shattered, the Hierarchy falls, and most wraiths are destroyed or Transcend. In-universe justification for cancelling Wraith line due to poor sales; metaplot finale that was unusually definitive. Directly leads to Orpheus (2003) where mortal mediums investigate the post-Maelstrom Underworld.

The Shattering, the Sundering and the Long WinterChangeling: The Dreaming 1st/2nd Edition; The Shadow Court; War in Concordia

1995-1999 (in-universe: mythic to 1969)

Fae are souls of dreams. After the Shattering (14th century, when disbelief and iron severed Arcadia), most fae fled. The Sundering further divided them. In 1969, the Apollo moon landing's banality and wonder triggers the Resurgence, returning sidhe. Changeling society splits between Seelie and Unseelie Courts, struggling against encroaching Banality and the coming Winter. Frames Changeling as elegy for imagination. Metaplot remained underdeveloped compared to Vampire/Werewolf; Time of Judgment offered multiple endings from endless Winter to re-enchantment.

The Week of Nightmares and the Ravnos AntediluvianWeek of Nightmares supplement (1999); Time of Judgment: Gehenna; Ravnos clanbook

1999 (in-universe: July 1999, Bangladesh/India)

The Ravnos Antediluvian awakens in Bangladesh, causing supernatural chaos: Ravnos vampires frenzy worldwide, werewolves and mages converge, Technocracy deploys orbital mirrors and spirit nukes. Three Kuei-jin bodhisattvas sacrifice themselves to destroy the Antediluvian, while the event triggers the Avatar Storm and Sixth Maelstrom, linking all lines. Only cross-line metaplot event to affect all games simultaneously. Demonstrated White Wolf's commitment to shared universe but criticized for power-creep and for using South Asia as exotic cataclysm backdrop.

Time of Judgment - Gehenna, Apocalypse, AscensionTime of Judgment line (2004): Gehenna (Vampire), Apocalypse (Werewolf), Ascension (Mage), World of Darkness: Time of Judgment

2004 publication (in-universe: 2004, the End Times)

White Wolf ended the original continuity with four books offering multiple playable apocalypses rather than one canon. Gehenna offers scenarios from Wormwood (God judges vampires) to Crucible of God; Apocalypse offers Last Battle, Weaverskin, etc.; Ascension offers Judgment, Anarchy, and personal Ascension. Storytellers choose their ending. Allowed groups to conclude decade-long chronicles. Critically, it freed White Wolf to reboot as Chronicles of Darkness without invalidating any group's ending. Commercially marked end of oWoD until 2011 20th Anniversary revival.

What counts as canon - metaplot vs toolkitOriginal World of Darkness has a single advancing metaplot (Caine, Gehenna, Apocalypse) that Storytellers should follow as official history.Chronicles of Darkness has no metaplot; all setting material is optional toolkit, and even Classic's metaplot was always presented as optional despite marketing.
Tremere and Order of Hermes originsClan Tremere were originally Hermetic mages who became vampires via diablerie, tying Ars Magica directly to World of Darkness history.After 1994 rights sale, Tremere are vampires who diablerized Saulot, with Hermetic ties minimized; Ars Magica is separate universe.
Nature of the supernatural - personal horror vs power fantasyWorld of Darkness is personal horror where monstrosity costs Humanity and power is a curse, as intended by Rein-Hagen.Actual play often becomes superheroes-with-fangs power fantasy, and later supplements power-creep undermines horror.
V5 and W5 revisions - continuity vs reinventionVampire 5th Edition (2018) and Werewolf 5th Edition (2023) correctly update setting for modern nights (Second Inquisition, Beckoning, climate crisis) and fix problematic content.Revisions betray original lore, sanitize or politicize content, and impose new canon that invalidates long chronicles.

World of Darkness is just Vampire: The Masquerade. — Vampire is the flagship but the franchise includes eight original lines (Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Wraith, Changeling, Hunter, Mummy, Demon) plus historical and Chronicles lines. Each has distinct cosmology and themes; they share a universe but are not interchangeable.

Chronicles of Darkness (New World of Darkness) is just a new edition of the same story. — Chronicles is a full reboot with separate continuity, cosmology, and design philosophy (no metaplot, five clans/tribes, toolkit). A Ventrue in Masquerade and a Ventrue in Requiem have different origins, weaknesses, and societies.

The games are manuals for real occult practice or Satanism. — All magick, rituals, and infernal lore are fictional game mechanics. White Wolf included disclaimers that the World of Darkness is fiction. Academic studies found no causal link to occult practice beyond subcultural aesthetics.

You need to know decades of metaplot to play. — Core rulebooks are designed to be playable without prior lore; metaplot was always optional and Chronicles explicitly discards it. Many groups ignore Gehenna/Apocalypse entirely.
