Lake Erie is shallow, but she has her secrets. Ask any old fisherman from Sandusky to Buffalo -- they've all got a Bessie story. Something big and dark moves beneath those waves.

Who It Is

Bessie, the Lake Erie Monster, is the Great Lakes' most famous aquatic cryptid. Lake Erie, the shallowest and warmest of the Great Lakes, has generated monster sightings since the late 18th century. The earliest recorded report came from 1793, when the captain of a sloop near Sandusky, Ohio reported a large serpentine creature. The creature is generally described as 30 to 40 feet long, dark gray or brown, with a serpentine body, multiple humps, and a flat, snake-like head. Bessie's name -- a diminutive of 'Lake Erie Serpent' (LES) -- was popularized in the 1980s after a series of sightings near Huron, Ohio prompted a local newspaper to hold a naming contest. In 1990, a fisherman named Harold Bricker photographed what appeared to be a dark serpentine form in the lake, and multiple fishermen have reported their nets being torn by unknown large creatures. The most dramatic series of sightings occurred in 1993 near Port Clinton, Ohio, when multiple witnesses reported a creature with a snake-like head emerging from the water. The Ohio legislature has passed resolutions acknowledging the legend. Skeptics attribute sightings to lake sturgeon (which can reach 7+ feet and have a prehistoric appearance), floating logs, wave patterns, or large schools of fish seen from a distance in poor light.
