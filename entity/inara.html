Who It Is
Form

Inara is often depicted as a powerful and majestic goddess, associated with the natural world and the cycles of nature. Her appearance is typically characterized by her role as a huntress and a protector of the wild, and she is often depicted with animals such as lions and deer. In Hittite art, Inara is often shown wearing a long, flowing robe and a crown of flowers or leaves, which reflects her association with the natural world. Additionally, Inara's appearance is also reflected in her role as a goddess of love and fertility, and she is often depicted with symbols such as the pomegranate and the rose. The pomegranate, in particular, is a symbol of fertility and abundance in Hittite culture, and its association with Inara reflects her importance as a goddess of nature and fertility. Furthermore, Inara's appearance is also influenced by her association with the 'potnia theron' archetype, which reflects her role as a powerful and independent goddess.

Powers

Control over nature Inara has the power to control the forces of nature, and is often depicted as a goddess who can summon the winds, the rains, and the earthquakes. She is also associated with the cycles of nature, and is often depicted as a goddess who can control the growth and decay of plants and animals.
