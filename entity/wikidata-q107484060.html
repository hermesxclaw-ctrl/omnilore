The Approach

Misery is a name that echoes through horror, music, and fantasy alike—from Stephen King's terrifying muse to a shapeshifting demon in a cult video game. But behind the screams and the riffs lies a surprising web of characters, songs, and places, each with its own story of suffering and survival.

Content advisory. Contains references to violence, psychological torment, and dark themes in various fictional works.
Identity

Misery

Pronunciation /ˈmɪzəri/

Etymology

From Old French 'mesaise', meaning 'suffering' or 'distress', ultimately from Latin 'miser' (wretched).

Cultural Ownership

Global; the name is used across various cultures and media, though the most famous usage is American (Stephen King).

Disputed Identifications

The name is shared by many unrelated entities; there is no single canonical 'Misery'.

Description

Appearance

Varies by entity. In Stephen King's novel, Misery Chastain is a fictional character within a novel series, described as a beautiful woman with red hair. In Cave Story, Misery is a witch-like character with a distinctive hat and cloak. In Ruby Gloom, Misery is a goth girl with pale skin and dark hair.

Symbols & Iconography

The name 'Misery' often evokes suffering, obsession, and darkness. In the novel, the typewriter and the hobbling scene are iconic. In Cave Story, Misery's magic and her connection to the Doctor are symbolic.

Behaviour & Personality

In the novel, Misery Chastain is a character in Paul Sheldon's books, but the real 'Misery' is Annie Wilkes, who is obsessive and violent. In Cave Story, Misery is initially a villain but later shows complexity. In Ruby Gloom, Misery is a cheerful goth girl.

Domains & Habitats

Varies: from the fictional world of Paul Sheldon's novels, to the isolated Colorado farmhouse in the film, to the floating island in Cave Story, to the town of Gloomsville in Ruby Gloom.

Version Differences

The name is used for entirely different characters and concepts across media, with no connection between them.

Misery (novel)

Source Stephen KingCulture AmericanPeriod 1987

Summary

Author Paul Sheldon is rescued from a car crash by his 'number one fan' Annie Wilkes, who holds him captive and forces him to write a new novel reviving her favorite character, Misery Chastain, whom he had killed off. Annie's obsession turns violent as she inflicts physical and psychological torture.
