Who It Is

Misery is a name that echoes through horror, music, and fantasy alike—from Stephen King's terrifying muse to a shapeshifting demon in a cult video game. But behind the screams and the riffs lies a surprising web of characters, songs, and places, each with its own story of suffering and survival.

In the novel, Misery Chastain is a character in Paul Sheldon's books, but the real 'Misery' is Annie Wilkes, who is obsessive and violent. In Cave Story, Misery is initially a villain but later shows complexity. In Ruby Gloom, Misery is a cheerful goth girl.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Old French 'mesaise', meaning 'suffering' or 'distress', ultimately from Latin 'miser' (wretched).

Pronunciation — /ˈmɪzəri/

Aliases — Misery

Ownership — Global; the name is used across various cultures and media, though the most famous usage is American (Stephen King).

Powers
Literary creation

In the novel, Misery Chastain is a character created by Paul Sheldon, but she has no powers herself. The real power lies with Annie Wilkes, who is physically strong and manipulative.

Limit: Fictional character within a story; no supernatural powers.

Magic

In Cave Story, Misery is a powerful magician who can cast spells and teleport. She serves the Doctor and later helps the protagonist.

Limit: Her powers are not fully explained; she is defeated by the protagonist in some endings.

Musical expression

Various songs titled 'Misery' convey emotional suffering through music.

Limit: No supernatural powers; artistic expression.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Varies by entity. In Stephen King's novel, Misery Chastain is a fictional character within a novel series, described as a beautiful woman with red hair. In Cave Story, Misery is a witch-like character with a distinctive hat and cloak. In Ruby Gloom, Misery is a goth girl with pale skin and dark hair.

Symbols — The name 'Misery' often evokes suffering, obsession, and darkness. In the novel, the typewriter and the hobbling scene are iconic. In Cave Story, Misery's magic and her connection to the Doctor are symbolic.

Domains — Varies: from the fictional world of Paul Sheldon's novels, to the isolated Colorado farmhouse in the film, to the floating island in Cave Story, to the town of Gloomsville in Ruby Gloom.

Accounts I
Misery (novel)Stephen King

1987

Author Paul Sheldon is rescued from a car crash by his 'number one fan' Annie Wilkes, who holds him captive and forces him to write a new novel reviving her favorite character, Misery Chastain, whom he had killed off. Annie's obsession turns violent as she inflicts physical and psychological torture. Paul eventually escapes after a brutal struggle, but the experience leaves him traumatized.

Accounts II
Misery (short story)Anton Chekhov

1886

A sleigh driver named Iona Potapov tries to tell passengers and fellow workers about the death of his son, but no one listens. He eventually pours his heart out to his horse. The story highlights isolation and the human need for empathy.

Misery in Cave StoryPixel (Daisuke Amaya)

2004

Misery is a witch who serves the Doctor on the floating island. She assists him in his plans but later aids the protagonist, Curly Brace, and others. Her backstory involves being enslaved by the Doctor. Depending on the player's actions, Misery can survive or die. She plays a key role in the game's multiple endings.
