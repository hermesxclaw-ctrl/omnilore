Who It Is

The 'Hammer of Witches,' the most infamous and influential treatise on witchcraft ever published. Written by German Dominican inquisitor Heinrich Kramer (Henricus Institoris) and first published in Speyer in 1486, the Malleus codified the demonological theory that witches — overwhelmingly women — made pacts with Satan, flew through the air to Sabbaths, and caused harm through maleficium. It provided legal procedures for witch trials, including the admission of spectral evidence and the use of torture. Though initially condemned by some Church authorities, the Malleus went through 28 editions by 1600 and became the de facto handbook for witch-hunters across Europe, fueling three centuries of witch persecution that resulted in an estimated 40,000-60,000 executions.
