Who It Is

In the shadowed courts of the underworld, where souls are weighed and judged, there stands a creature unlike any other—a chimera with the head of a tiger, the body of a dragon, and ears that can hear the truth of every being across the universe. This is Diting, the loyal steed of the bodhisattva Kṣitigarbha, a being whose single horn pierces the veil of deception and whose very presence guards the gates of hell.

Diting is portrayed as loyal, protective, and wise. It acts as a guardian and companion to Kṣitigarbha, and in the Journey to the West, it shows prudence by not revealing the truth to avoid conflict. In modern media, it is depicted as a responsible and somewhat bureaucratic figure, as the CEO of the Underworld.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Diting' is composed of two Chinese characters: 谛 (dì) meaning 'truth' or 'to examine closely' and 听 (tīng) meaning 'to listen'. Thus, the name can be interpreted as 'one who listens to the truth' or 'truth-hearer'. In Vietnamese, it is rendered as Đế Thính.

Pronunciation — Dìtīng (dee-ting), with the third tone on 'di' and the first tone on 'ting'.

Aliases — 谛听, Dìtīng, Đế Thính, Ti听, Earth Listening, Earth Listener

Powers
Truth discernment

Diting can distinguish good from bad, true from false, among all creatures. This ability is highlighted in Journey to the West, where it identifies the true Sun Wukong.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Diting is described as a composite creature with the head of a tiger, the body of a dragon, the tail of a lion, a single horn, dog-like ears, and feet like a qilin. Its body is often depicted with scales and a flowing mane. In some modern depictions, such as in the manhua 'Fabulous Beasts', Diting is shown as a wolf-like creature with black fur, except for white ears and a white spinal ridge in quadrupedal form.

Symbols — Diting is often depicted in Buddhist art as a small, lion-like beast with a single horn, sometimes shown alongside Kṣitigarbha. It symbolizes the ability to hear and discern the truth, and its horn is sometimes interpreted as a tool to receive information from across the universe.

Domains — Diting resides in the underworld (Diyu) with Kṣitigarbha, where it guards the souls of the dead. It can also travel between realms, as seen in its ability to discern beings across the universe.

Accounts I
The Legend of the White DogPopular Buddhist legend

Tang dynasty (7th-9th century)

Before Kṣitigarbha left heaven, he learned that his mother from a past life would be reborn as a dog. He was reincarnated as Kim Gyo-gak, a prince of Silla, who later traced the dog and adopted it. The dog became his companion and followed him to Mount Jiuhua, where he became a monk. The dog acted as his guard and, upon Kṣitigarbha's enlightenment, became Diting, the guardian of hell. The dog became Diting, a divine creature, and Kṣitigarbha fulfilled his vow to save all beings from hell.

Accounts II
The True and False Monkey KingJourney to the West, Chapter 58

Ming dynasty (16th century)

When the real and fake Sun Wukong argued, they sought help from Kṣitigarbha, who referred them to Diting. Diting identified the true monkey but, fearing the fake monkey would cause havoc in his abode, advised sending them to Buddha. The fake monkey was ultimately exposed and defeated by Buddha.

Diting in Fabulous BeastsManhua and animated series 'Fabulous Beasts' (有兽焉)

21st century

Diting is depicted as the CEO of the Underworld. He was born as a white wolf, but his fur darkened to black over time due to the underworld's influence. He has an adopted skeleton cat daughter named Kulou. This modern adaptation reimagines Diting's origin and role, blending traditional elements with new narrative.
