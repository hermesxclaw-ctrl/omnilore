Cosmological Creation (RELATED TRADITION ONLY - Kayra Han, NOT Kayra Su)

In Altai creation myths (Radloff 1866, Verbitsky 1893), Kayra Han / Kudai creates the world after hovering over primordial waters, commands the retrieval of earth from the depths (often via a duck/goose or via his co-creator/person Erlik), and creates humanity. This is the central power attested for the homonymous deity. Explicitly NOT attested for Kayra Su; included only as contextual disambiguation with source_version flag.

Limit: In these myths Kayra is not omnipotent: he requires a helper to bring earth, is deceived by Erlik who hides earth in his mouth, and subsequently curses/expels Erlik. He becomes distant after creation and delegates judgment. Powers are cosmological, not heroic-combat powers.

The Creation Over the Primordial Waters (RELATED TRADITION: Kayra Han)Altai creation myth as recorded by Wilhelm Radloff (1866) Proben Vol. I and V.I. Verbitsky (1893); retold in Eliade, Shamanism (1964) pp. 177-180 and Roux, La religion des Turcs et des Mongols (1984)

Oral tradition pre-19th century, first published 1866

In the beginning there is only water and Kayra Han (also called Kudai). Kayra hovers over the waters. He creates Erlik (or finds him floating) as companion. He sends Erlik (or a duck/goose) to dive to the bottom of the primordial ocean to bring up earth to create land. Erlik hides some earth in his mouth to later create his own land, but the earth expands in his mouth, forcing him to spit it out, forming barren mountains and swamps. Kayra creates the earth, plants the World Tree Bay Terek with nine branches, and creates humanity. He then expels Erlik to the underworld for his deceit. This is NOT a Kayra Su story; it is the canonical Kayra Han narrative provided for disambiguation. Establishes dualistic cosmology: upper world (Kayra) vs lower world (Erlik); explains landscape features; establishes Kayra as distant creator

Kayra Han and the Creation of Humanity (RELATED TRADITION)Verbitsky (1893), Anokhin collection (1924), summarized by Ögel (1971) Vol I pp. 449ff and Roux (1984)

Oral tradition, recorded late 19th-early 20th century

After creating the earth, Kayra creates the first humans from clay or earth, breathes life into them, and places them under the protection of the World Tree. He instructs them not to listen to Erlik. Erlik seduces or deceives them (in some versions, causes them to eat forbidden fruit or to sin), introducing death and suffering. Kayra judges and assigns Erlik to the underworld, where he becomes lord of the dead. Again, this is Kayra Han lore, not Kayra Su. Etiology of death, moral dualism, shamanic cosmology of soul judgment

The World Tree Bay Terek and the Nine Peoples (RELATED TRADITION)Radloff (1866), Potanin (1883) Ocherki Severo-Zapadnoi Mongolii, Eliade (1964)

Oral tradition, recorded 19th century

Kayra plants the great poplar or pine Bay Terek (Bay Kaying) at the center of the world, whose branches reach the sky and roots reach the underworld. From its branches come the ancestors of peoples or the souls of children (in some tellings, Umay or Ymai guards infants). Kayra dwells above it. Shamans climb it in ritual. This cosmology is central to Altai shamanism and is associated with Kayra Han as high god. Cosmological axis mundi; ritual practice of shamanic ascent

The Expulsion of Erlik and the Establishment of the Underworld (RELATED TRADITION)Verbitsky (1893), Anokhin (1924), Roux (1984) pp. 112-118

Oral tradition, recorded 19th-20th century

After Erlik's betrayal (hiding earth, corrupting humans, or demanding worship), Kayra banishes him from the upper world, casting him down to the lower world, which becomes the realm of darkness, cold, and the dead. Kayra seals the heavens and establishes the order where prayers and sacrifices go upward to him, while fear-offerings go downward to Erlik to appease him. The moral geography of the cosmos is fixed. Dualistic afterlife; shaman's role as mediator between worlds; Erlik as judge of the dead

Kayra Han in the Teleut Variant - The White Creator Who Withdraws (RELATED TRADITION)Potanin (1883), Ögel (1971) Vol I, Sagalaev & Oktyabrskaya (1990) Traditsionnoe mirovozzrenie tyurkov Yuzhnoi Sibiri

Oral tradition, systematized in 20th century scholarship

In Teleut systematization, Kayra is the highest, most abstract creator, above Ülgen. After creation, Kayra withdraws to the 17th heaven, leaving active governance to Ülgen (or Tengri) and to lesser spirits. He is invoked as 'Kayra Kan' in prayers but rarely appears in narrative action after the creation. This withdrawal motif explains why later folklore mentions Ülgen more often than Kayra. Scholarly debate about whether Kayra and Ülgen are originally same figure split by systematization; explains scarcity of Kayra in later epics

Kayra Su is an ancient Turkic water goddess (because Su means water). — No attested Turkic deity named Kayra Su exists in Radloff, Verbitsky, Ögel, or Roux. Kayra Han is a sky creator, not a water deity; water is the primordial element he creates from, not his domain. 'Kayra Su' as a theonym appears to be a modern misreading of a personal name (Kayra + Su) as a mythological compound.

Kayra (Su) is the same as Kara Han or Kara Su. — Kara Han (Black Khan) is a distinct figure in some Turkic genealogies (sometimes a title for the first human or a rival khan), not to be confused with Kayra Han (Merciful Khan). The similarity is phonetic; Ögel explicitly warns against conflating Kayra and Kara.
