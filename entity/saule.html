Who It Is

Before the thunder god Perkūnas wielded his hammer, before the Moon's infidelity was written in the sky, there was Saulė—the Baltic Sun herself, a golden mother who rides a chariot of fire across the heavens by day and sails a silver boat through the world-sea by night. She is the warmth that wakes orphans, the bride betrayed by a wandering Moon, and the blazing wheel whose turning marks the longest day and the darkest night. To the Latvians and Lithuanians, the world itself is simply 'under the Sun.'

Saulė is a nurturing mother figure, especially to orphans; she delays her rise to comfort them. She is also a betrayed wife, who divorced Mėnuo after his infidelity. She is depicted as dancing on a silver hill, driving her chariot with fiery sparks, and sailing her boat at night. She is powerful and responsible for all life on Earth.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Proto-Baltic *Sauliā > *Saulē, meaning 'sun'. The Lithuanian 'Saulė' and Latvian 'Saule' are also the standard words for the sun.

Pronunciation — Lithuanian: [ˈsau̯lʲeː]; Latvian: [ˈsaule]

Aliases — Saule (Latvian), Saulytė (diminutive, Lithuanian), Saules māte (Latvian, 'Mother Sun')

Ownership — Baltic peoples, primarily Lithuanians and Latvians; also recognized in Prussian mythology as a solar deity.

Powers
Solar radiance and life-giving

Saulė is the goddess of the sun, responsible for all life on Earth. Her light and warmth sustain the world.

Limit: She must rest at night, traveling through the sea.

Celestial travel

She rides a chariot or carriage across the sky by day, and sails a boat on the sea by night.

Limit: Her vehicle changes form at night.

Divine authority

She is one of the most powerful deities, and her word is law. She can punish the Moon by disfiguring his face.

Limit: She is subject to the supreme god Dievas.

Patronage of orphans

She is the patroness of the unfortunate, especially orphans, and comforts them.

Limit: None noted.

Shape-shifting (vehicle)

Her chariot transforms into a boat for her night travels.

Limit: Only her vehicle transforms, not herself.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Saulė is anthropomorphized as a beautiful woman. In Latvian dainas, she is dressed in garments woven with gold thread, wearing gold shoes, a sparkling crown, and a silk or silver costume. She is also described as wearing clothes of red, gold, silver, and white. In the morning, she is 'red' or 'in a red tree.' In Lithuanian tradition, the sun is a golden wheel or circle.

Symbols — Gold is her primary symbol: golden shoes, crown, boat, chariot, horses, and various golden objects (rings, ribbons, tassels). The rose is associated with the setting and rising sun. The silver hill and silver gated castle are also symbolic. The 'golden wheel' is a common motif.

Domains — She dwells in a silver gated castle at the end of the sea, in the east, or on an island in the sea. At night she sleeps in a silver cradle in the sea or at the bottom of a lake. She travels across the sky by day and through the sea by night.

The Creation of the SunSlavic translation of the Chronicle of John Malalas (1261)

13th century

A smith named Teliavelis made the Sun and threw it into the sky. The Sun became a celestial body.

The Kidnapping of SaulėAccount of Jerome of Prague

15th century

Saulė was held in a tower by a powerful king. The zodiac (Signa Zodiaci) rescued her using a giant sledgehammer. Jerome claimed to have seen the hammer venerated by locals. Saulė was freed; the hammer became a venerated object.

Accounts II
The Marriage of Saulė and MėnuoLithuanian and Latvian folklore

Unknown (pre-Christian)

Saulė and Mėnuo (the Moon) were married. Mėnuo fell in love with Aušrinė (the morning star). Perkūnas punished him for infidelity. Mėnuo was punished in various ways: cut into two pieces (repeated monthly), divorced from Saulė, or disfigured. The Sun and Moon now alternate in the sky.

The Quest for the Second SunLithuanian myth

Unknown

A man named Joseph becomes fascinated with Aušrinė and goes on a quest to find the 'second sun', who is actually a maiden living on an island in the sea, with hair like the Sun. Unknown; the myth is fragmentary.

Saulė is the same as the Greek Helios or Roman Sol. — While both are solar deities, Saulė is a distinct Baltic goddess with her own myths, attributes, and cultural context. Direct equation is a modern scholarly hypothesis, not an ancient identification.

The Moon's infidelity is a universal Baltic myth. — There are multiple versions, and the Moon's punishment varies. Some versions do not involve infidelity at all.

Saulė is a minor deity. — She is one of the most powerful deities, responsible for all life on Earth, and is central to Baltic cosmology.
