Who It Is

In the deep forests of the Alps, where mist clings to the pines and streams tumble over mossy stones, there is said to dwell a being both feared and revered: the Wildfrau, the wild woman of the mountains. She is no mere fairy tale, but a living presence in the folklore of Austria, Bavaria, Switzerland, and South Tyrol—a guardian of the wild, a weaver of fate, and a mirror of humanity's own ambivalence toward the untamed. Her story, told in whispers around hearths for centuries, reveals a world where the boundary between human and nature, civilized and wild, is thinner than we imagine.

The Wildfrau is ambivalent: she can be helpful or harmful, benevolent or malevolent. She is a guardian of the forest and its creatures, and she punishes those who harm them without need. She is also a protector of children and may rescue lost travelers, but she can also abduct children or lead men astray. She is shy and elusive, avoiding human contact, but if captured or married, she becomes a good wife and mother, bringing prosperity to the household. Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Wildfrau' (plural 'Wildfrauen') is German for 'wild woman.' 'Wild' in this context carries connotations of untamed, savage, and belonging to the wilderness, as opposed to the cultivated world of humans. Powers
Shape-shifting

The Wildfrau can change her form, appearing as a beautiful woman, an old crone, or an animal such as a deer, bear, or cat. She can also become invisible or appear as a mist.

Limit: She may lose her shape-shifting ability if captured or if her veil or girdle is stolen.

Spinning and weaving

The Wildfrau is an expert spinner and weaver. She can spin flax into fine thread overnight and weave garments of extraordinary quality. She may also spin straw into gold in some tales.

Limit: She may require specific materials, such as flax, and may stop working if her true nature is discovered.

Prophecy and knowledge

The Wildfrau has knowledge of the future, of herbs and healing, and of hidden treasures. She may reveal secrets to those who help her.

Limit: She may only share knowledge under certain conditions, such as a promise of secrecy.

Gift-giving

The Wildfrau can bestow prosperity, fertility, and blessings on households that treat her well. She may leave gifts of bread, cheese, or gold in exchange for offerings.

Limit: Her gifts may turn to curse if she is offended or if her taboos are broken.

Cursing and punishment

The Wildfrau can curse those who harm her, her forest, or her children. She may cause illness, misfortune, or death. She can also lead people astray in the forest.

Limit: Her curses may be lifted if the offender makes amends.

Abduction

The Wildfrau may abduct children or adults, taking them to her realm. The abducted may be returned if certain conditions are met, or they may become her servants.

Limit: She may be forced to return the abducted if the human can pass a test or retrieve a magical object.

Guardianship of treasure

In mining regions, the Wildfrau is a guardian of mineral wealth, such as silver and gold. She may reveal treasure to deserving miners or punish those who steal from the earth.

Limit: She may only reward those who follow mining customs and offer proper respect.

In some tales, she is dressed in moss or leaves, blending with the forest. She is sometimes depicted as taller than human women and possesses an otherworldly grace. Accounts I
The Wildfrau of the Spinning RoomCollected in Tyrol by Zingerle (1859), variant recorded in 'Sagen aus Tirol'.

19th century (recorded), traditional oral tradition

A farmer captures a Wildfrau in the forest and brings her home to spin flax. She spins beautifully and quickly, but the farmer's wife is jealous and insults her, calling her a 'wild thing.' The Wildfrau immediately disappears, leaving the flax unfinished and the household cursed with misfortune. The farmer's household loses its prosperity, and the Wildfrau is never seen again.

The Wildfrau and the MidwifeRecorded in Bavaria, in various collections such as 'Sagen und Gebräuche aus dem Bayerischen Wald' (1857) by Schöppner.

19th century (recorded)

A midwife is called to help a Wildfrau give birth in a cave. She is blindfolded and led into the mountain, where she assists with the birth. As a reward, she is offered a choice: gold or a piece of moss. She chooses the gold, but as she leaves, the Wildfrau tells her that she would have been blessed with fertility and prosperity if she had chosen the moss. The midwife returns home to find that the gold has turned to leaves. The midwife learns that the Wildfrau's gifts are not always what they seem, and that true blessings come from nature, not material wealth.

Accounts II
The Wildfrau's VeilRecorded in South Tyrol, in Schöpf's 'Die Sagen von den Saligen' (1895).

19th century (recorded)

A young man sees a Wildfrau bathing in a mountain lake. He steals her veil, which forces her to follow him and become his wife. They live happily and have children, but one day he accidentally reveals that he has her veil, hidden in a chest. She retrieves it and immediately disappears, leaving him and their children. The man loses his wife and his happiness because he broke the taboo of keeping her veil hidden. The children remain human, but they are marked by their mother's absence.

The Wildfrau and the WoodcutterRecorded in Switzerland, in 'Sagen aus der Schweiz' (1850) by Rochholz.

19th century (recorded)

A woodcutter is about to cut down a tree that is the home of a Wildfrau. She appears and begs him to spare the tree, offering him a reward. He agrees, and she gives him a small piece of wood that turns into gold. She warns him not to tell anyone. He becomes rich, but his wife forces him to reveal the source. The next time he goes to the forest, the Wildfrau is gone, and he loses his wealth. The woodcutter's greed and his wife's curiosity cause him to lose the Wildfrau's favor and his fortune.

The Wildfrau's DanceRecorded in the Austrian Alps, in Vernaleken's 'Alpensagen' (1858).

19th century (recorded)

A young man wandering in the forest at night comes upon a group of Wildfrauen dancing in a moonlit clearing. They invite him to join, and he dances with them until dawn. When he leaves, he finds that he has been gone for a year. He is never quite the same, and he has a longing for the forest that never leaves him. The man experiences a time distortion and is forever changed, caught between the human and spirit worlds.
