Who It Is

When the ice giant of Undvik began to throw longships like driftwood and eat Skelligers whole, only one son of Kaer Trolde thought the answer was to sail straight at it — with a handful of friends, a lot of mead, and no real plan to get home. That is Hjalmar an Craite: Crach's red-bearded hammer of a son, beloved by the bards for his courage, quietly mourned by the mothers of the boys who followed him.

Attested in dialogue and journal: Hjalmar is hot-headed, impulsive, fiercely brave, and hungry for glory. He is charismatic and inspires intense loyalty – men like Folan and Vigi the Loon follow him even into suicidal danger. He is honorable by Skellige standards: he values strength, directness, and keeping oaths, and despises scheming (contrasted with Birna Bran's intrigue). He is also reckless and dismissive of caution; Crach and Cerys both call him headstrong. Powers
Master Skellige Melee Combat

Exceptional proficiency with Skellige arms: longsword, battle-axe, and shield. In cutscenes he cleaves through Sirens and Ice Trolls and holds his own against the Ice Giant Myrhyff (also called Myrhyff or simply Ice Giant) alongside Geralt. Game stats (if allied in combat) show high health and heavy attacks.

Limit: Purely physical; no signs, magic, or alchemy. Can be overwhelmed by numbers or by the giant alone – his initial expedition fails catastrophically with most crew killed. Requires Geralt's help to finish the giant.

Leadership and Charisma (Hirdman Loyalty)

Ability to inspire and retain a loyal warband (hird). Followers Folan, Vigi, Octo, and others follow him to Undvik despite knowing the danger, and remain loyal even after disaster. At Kaer Trolde he can rally young warriors for the kingship moot.

Limit: Charisma is double-edged – his followers' loyalty leads them to death; his leadership is criticized as reckless. Does not extend to political cunning; easily manipulated by more subtle players like Birna Bran if Geralt does not intervene.

Endurance and Survival

Survives weeks on Undvik, a frozen, monster-infested island with limited supplies, after his longship is wrecked. Endures cold, hunger, and constant attacks from Sirens, Ice Trolls, and the Ice Giant. Shows notable pain tolerance and recovery.

Limit: Survival is marginal – he is exhausted, wounded, and would have died without Geralt's arrival. No supernatural resistance to cold or hunger.

Kingship Claim and Ordeal Proof

As son of Jarl Crach an Craite and descendant of King Bran through clan lineage, he has a legitimate claim to the Skellige throne under the moot system. His attempt to slay the Ice Giant is a traditional Skellige 'deed of worth' to prove fitness to rule. If successful and politically supported, he can be crowned King of Skellige.

Limit: Claim is not automatic – Skellige kings are elected by jarls at the moot, not by primogeniture. His deed must be witnessed and accepted; political support from jarls (especially Ermion, Lugos, Donar) is required. Fails if Cerys's deeds are deemed more worthy or if Birna's conspiracy is exposed in a way that favors Cerys.

Naval and Raiding Competence

Implied competence in longship sailing, raiding, and island navigation – standard for a Skellige jarl's son. Led the seaborne expedition to Undvik.

Limit: Expedition ends in shipwreck due to Sirens and giant – demonstrates limits of seamanship against monsters. Not depicted as a navigator on Geralt's level of world travel.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Hjalmar is depicted as a quintessential young Skellige warrior-chief: tall (approx. 6'2"/188cm in scale to Geralt), broad-shouldered and heavily muscled, with long, thick red hair worn loose or in small braids, a full red beard, and pale blue eyes. His skin is weathered and freckled, with scars on forearms and torso consistent with raiding and arena fighting. In The Witcher 3 he wears heavy Skellige clan armor: chainmail with leather and fur trim

The boar signifies ferocity, stubbornness, and fecundity in Skellige culture. Hjalmar's personal symbols are the Ice Giant's story (giant-slaying as kingship proof) and the Undvik expedition itself – his followers wear tokens from Undvik. The Lord of Undvik – The Giant-Slaying ExpeditionThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Main Quest 'The Lord of Undvik' (Skellige arc, Level 17)

1272 (in-universe, during Geralt's search for Ciri); development period 2011-2015

After King Bran's death leaves the throne vacant, Hjalmar seeks to prove his worth by undertaking the most dangerous deed imaginable: slaying the Ice Giant of Undvik, who has driven Clan an Undvik from their island and sunk any ship that approaches. He sails with a hand-picked crew of young warriors including Folan, Vigi the Loon (Vigi Loose?), Octo, and others. On Undvik, the expedition is shattered – Sirens wreck their longship, Ice Trolls harry them, and the giant picks them off one by one. Survivors scatter: Folan is trapped by Ice Trolls planning to cook him, Vigi is crazed and worships the giant, Octo is dead. Geralt, sent by Crach to find his son, tracks Hjalmar's path across the island, rescuing Folan and confronting Vigi. He finds Hjalmar alive but exhausted in a ruined village, preparing for a final suicidal attack on the giant's lair (a beached, frozen ship graveyard in a mountain cave). Together, Geralt and Hjalmar (and optionally Folan) confront Myrhyff the Ice Giant, who wields an anchor as a club and can freeze the ground. With Geralt's help, Hjalmar lands killing blow

The King is Dead – Long Live the King / Possession – The Funeral and the MootThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Quests 'The King is Dead – Long Live the King' and 'Possession'

1272, immediately after King Bran's death

King Bran of Skellige dies (of natural causes, though poisoning rumors swirl). All major clans gather at Kaer Trolde for the wake, a seven-day feast where jarls drink, boast, and position their candidates. Crach an Craite presents both his children as candidates: Hjalmar, who argues that Skellige needs a strong sword-arm to lead raids against Nilfgaard and the Wild Hunt, and Cerys, who argues for cunning and unity. During the wake, Geralt witnesses Hjalmar's brash challenge – he publicly vows to kill the Ice Giant – and Cerys's quieter vow to lift the curse on Jarl Udalryk. The wake is interrupted by Birna Bran's manipulations and later by the hym (a spirit) possessing Udalryk's son. Hjalmar leaves the feast early to prepare his Undvik expedition, dismissing Cerys's approach as unwarriorlike. Sets up the succession contest as a contrast between martial glory (Hjalmar) and wisdom/rite (Cerys). Establishes Hjalmar's impulsiveness and Cerys's thoughtfulness. Leads directly to both siblings' proving quests. Birna Bran begins conspiring to install her son Svanrige.

King's Gambit – The Plot of Birna Bran and the ElectionThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Main Quest 'King's Gambit'

1272, after both proving quests are complete (if both siblings survive)

With Hjalmar returned from Undvik (giant dead) and Cerys returned from Spikeroog (having freed Jarl Udalryk from the hym curse and uncovered the treachery surrounding the massacre at Kaer Muire), the jarls convene for the moot to elect the new ruler. Birna Bran, widow of King Bran, has orchestrated a massacre: she hired men (led by Arnvald) to slaughter her own clan's warriors at Kaer Muire and frame the an Craites, hoping to eliminate both Hjalmar and Cerys and install her son Svanrige. During the feast, the plot is exposed – Geralt, having investigated with Cerys or Hjalmar's help, presents evidence (a letter, testimony of the surviving assassin). Violence erupts; Birna's co-conspirators are killed. The jarls then vote. If Geralt supported Cerys by helping her and exposing Birna in a way that highlights Cerys's cleverness, Cerys is elected Queen – the first queen in Skellige history – and Hjalmar graciously kneels and pledges his sword to her. Coronation – The Aftermath at Kaer TroldeThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Quest 'Coronation'

1272, immediately after King's Gambit

A short ceremonial quest where the newly elected ruler is crowned at Kaer Trolde. If Hjalmar is king, he is crowned with the Skellige crown, gives a speech vowing to lead Skelligers to glory and to raid the continent, and thanks Geralt. He promises ships and warriors for Geralt's later battle at Kaer Morhen if asked. If Cerys is queen, Hjalmar stands at her side, publicly supporting her, and pledges to be her sword. In both cases, Crach expresses pride (or grief if Hjalmar is dead). The ceremony is attended by all jarls and marks the end of the Skellige main arc before Geralt returns to Novigrad or prepares for the Battle of Kaer Morhen. Locks Skellige world state. Unlocks later war support at Kaer Morhen: Hjalmar or Cerys will send Skellige warriors (led by Hjalmar personally if he is king or loyal brother) to fight the Wild Hunt. Provides closure to Hjalmar's arc.

The Battle of Kaer Morhen – Skellige Aid (Conditional Epilogue)The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Main Quest 'The Battle of Kaer Morhen'

1272, late game

If Geralt successfully completed the Skellige succession and secured Hjalmar or Cerys's support, Skellige warriors arrive at Kaer Morhen to help defend Ciri against the Wild Hunt. Hjalmar personally leads the Skellige contingent if he is alive and allied (whether king or prince), fighting alongside Folan and other Skelligers on the walls. He is seen battling Wild Hunt hounds and warriors, shouting challenges. His presence is optional and depends on earlier choices; his survival through Undvik and the moot is prerequisite. Provides payoff for Skellige arc; Hjalmar's martial prowess is showcased in a continent-scale battle. If he survives Kaer Morhen, he returns to Skellige. If he dies earlier, Skellige sends no aid or sends a smaller force under Cerys.

Childhood and Clan Lineage – The Son of the BoarThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Journal entries, ambient dialogue at Kaer Trolde, and Gwent flavour text; supplemented by book lore on Crach an Craite

c. 1245-1272 (Hjalmar's lifetime); backstory told in 1272

Hjalmar is born to Crach an Craite, Jarl of Kaer Trolde and chieftain of Clan an Craite, and his late wife (unnamed in game, mother of Hjalmar and Cerys). Raised in the warrior culture of Ard Skellig, he is trained from childhood in axe, sword, and sailing, and competes constantly with his younger sister Cerys, who is cleverer but less physically imposing. Crach, who lost his wife young and is fiercely protective, indulges Hjalmar's martial ambitions while worrying about his recklessness. Hjalmar grows into the archetypal Skellige hero: winner of fistfights, leader of youthful raids on the Continent, and favorite of the skalds. His rivalry with Cerys is affectionate but competitive – both love their father and want to make him proud. This backstory is delivered via ambient NPC gossip ('Young Hjalmar once lifted a barrel of mead over his head'), Crach's dialogue, and Cerys's comments. Explains Hjalmar's motivation for the Undvik deed – not just ambition but desire to live up to his father's legend (Crach himself once sought glory). Hjalmar an Craite appears in Andrzej Sapkowski's Witcher books. — He does not. Only his father Crach an Craite appears in the books (notably at the Thanedd banquet in Time of Contempt). Hjalmar and Cerys were created by CD Projekt RED for The Witcher 3 to give Skellige a succession story. Sapkowski has not written about them and has stated he considers game content separate from his canon.

Hjalmar killed the Ice Giant single-handedly. — He attempted to but failed without help. His initial expedition was decimated, and he was exhausted and would have died in a solo attack. In the canonical successful path, Geralt deals most of the damage and Hjalmar lands the final blows together with Geralt (and optionally Folan). The journal explicitly says 'with Geralt's help, Hjalmar slew the giant.'

Hjalmar is the 'canon' King of Skellige. — There is no single canon. The Witcher 3 has three possible rulers after King's Gambit: Cerys, Hjalmar, or Svanrige (if the player fails to expose Birna). CDPR has not declared an official canon for Skellige's ruler in Witcher 4 marketing; both Hjalmar and Cerys outcomes are treated as valid imports. Player choice determines the ruler.

Hjalmar is a berserker or has supernatural powers. — He is a highly skilled but entirely mundane human warrior. He has no magic, curses, or berserker transformation. His endurance on Undvik is human resilience, not supernatural. The 'berserker' association comes from his bear-pelt armor and Skellige cultural tropes, not from any ability.

Hjalmar and Cerys hate each other and are enemies. — They are rivals but affectionate siblings. Dialogue shows mutual respect and concern – Cerys worries Hjalmar will get himself killed, Hjalmar is proud of Cerys's cleverness even when he disagrees. If one becomes ruler, the other loyally serves. No fratricidal hostility is attested.
