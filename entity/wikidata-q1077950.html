The Approach

Born during a starship duplication that killed her other self before she drew breath, Naomi Wildman is the only child to grow up entirely aboard the USS Voyager — a half-Ktarian, half-human girl who learned to navigate Borg corridors and holodeck forests while the adults around her tried to navigate getting home.

Content advisory. Mild. Recurring themes of parental separation and presumed parental death, existential death of a duplicate ("Deadlock"), on-screen death and resurrection of a close mentor ("Mortal Coil"), Borg assimilation of children, and wartime isolation. No sexual violence or graphic gore depicted for this character.
Identity

Naomi Wildman

Pronunciation /naɪˈoʊmi ˈwaɪldmən/ (ny-OH-mee WILD-man); Ktarian father's name pronounced /ˈɡrɛskrɛndtrɛɡk/ per script pronunciation guides, though never spoken on screen.

Known As

Naomi Greskrendtregk WildmanEnsign Wildman (future alternate timeline in "Shattered")Flotter's friend (holoprogram nickname)

Etymology

Given name Naomi from Hebrew Na'omi (נָעֳמִי) meaning 'pleasantness, sweetness.' Surname Wildman is English occupational/descriptive. In-universe, she carries her human mother's surname; Ktarian naming conventions for hybrids are not established on screen. The full formal name Naomi Greskrendtregk Wildman appears in production notes and licensed fiction to acknowledge Ktarian patriline.

Cultural Ownership

Fictional character created for Star Trek: Voyager by executive producers Jeri Taylor, Michael Piller and Brannon Braga for Paramount Television. Owned and managed by Paramount Global (formerly ViacomCBS) via CBS Studios. Ktarian species created by Hilary Bader and Olaf Pooley (TNG: "The Game"). Character's storylines developed by Voyager writing staff including Kenneth Biller, Bryan Fuller, and Joe Menosky.

Disputed Identifications

No major disputed identification. Not to be confused with: Samantha Wildman (her mother, human xenobiologist), Ensign Samantha Wildman as played by Nancy Hower; or with other Star Trek children (Molly O'Brien, Jeremy Aster, Borg children Mezoti/Azum/Icheb). Fan confusion occasionally misattributes her as full Ktarian or full human; canon establishes half-Ktarian, half-human hybrid. Some early press misspelled father as Greskrendtregk vs Greskrendtregk — variant transliteration, same person.

Description

Appearance

Half-Ktarian hybrid child. On screen from infancy to age ~6 (played at ages 4-10 by Scarlett Pomers). Human-like with distinctive Ktarian traits: two small cranial horns (exostoses) on the forehead, visible from "Mortal Coil" onward, created with prosthetics. Light skin, dark blonde/brown hair, typically worn short with bangs. Ktarian genetics also implied subtle cranial ridge structure. Wears standard Voyager civilian children's clothing (pastel jumpsuits, later Delta Flyer jacket in play) rather than Starfleet uniform, except as adult Ensign in "Shattered" where she wears 2370s Starfleet operations gold. No Starfleet combadge as child; sometimes wears a toy tricorder or Flotter pin. Growth accelerated compared to human norm — appears ~4 years old at chronological age ~1.5 years.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated symbols: Flotter and Trevis holoprogram characters (based on in-universe children's holo-series The Adventures of Flotter), her handmade Flotter doll, Captain Proton paraphernalia via Neelix's storytelling, and Borg alcove iconography after befriending Seven of Nine and the Borg children. In licensed art, often depicted with Neelix or Seven, holding a PADD or Flotter book. No religious or mythic iconography; her horns are biological, not symbolic.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested personality across 15+ episodes: precocious, inquisitive, highly verbal, and socially confident despite being the only child on a warship for two years. Seeks adult companionship and mentorship; forms intense attachments to surrogate parental figures (Neelix as godfather/mentor, Seven of Nine as big-sister figure). Loves stories, games, and holodeck play but shows anxiety about parental loss ("Once Upon a Time") and about being different (Ktarian heritage). Demonstrates Starfleet-adjacent precocity: knows ship systems, assists in astrometrics, understands Borg terminology. Described in scripts as 'captain's assistant' — she appoints herself Captain Janeway's assistant and takes the role seriously. Shows resilience and humor; also willful and prone to eavesdropping and bending rules to help crew.

Domains & Habitats

Habitat is exclusively USS Voyager, Intrepid-class starship stranded in Delta Quadrant (2372-2378). Domains: Deck 5 residential quarters shared with mother Samantha Wildman; mess hall/Neelix's kitchen (primary social space); holodeck 2 (Flotter program); astrometrics lab (with Seven of Nine); cargo bay 2/Borg maturation chamber area after arrival of Borg children; bridge (as 'captain's assistant' visitor). Never lives planetside during series; post-return habitat in licensed relaunch fiction is Earth and later Voyager-Beta Quadrant exploration.

Version Differences

Infant version ("Deadlock" to Season 3, played by Brooke Stephens and uncredited infants) — non-speaking, no horns visible. Child version (Scarlett Pomers, S4-S7) — speaking, horns, accelerated growth; definitive version. Alternate adult version ("Shattered", Vanessa Branch) — age ~22, Ensign, timeline where Voyager never returned; non-canonical future erased when timeline restored. Mirror/holodeck versions: Flotter's forest is fictional within fiction. Licensed fiction version (novels by Kirsten Beyer, 2009-2014) — ages to ~10-12, more developed Ktarian abilities, Starfleet dependent. No Kelvin timeline or animated version exists.

Powers & Abilities

Ktarian Hybrid Physiology - Accelerated Development

Source VersionCanon television: "Deadlock" (birth), "Mortal Coil" dialogue (Neelix: 'Ktarians mature faster'), "Once Upon a Time" (age 3-4 at ~2 years), "Infinite Regress" reference

As half-Ktarian, Naomi exhibits significantly faster gestation and maturation than full humans. Samantha Wildman was pregnant for only a few months before birth (Ktarian females have shorter gestation), and Naomi reaches apparent age 4-5 within ~2 chronological years.

Limitations

Not superhuman speed; still requires learning and education. Growth rate slows after early childhood per dialogue. No canonical adult lifespan stated.

Contradictions

Exact rate inconsistent: appears ~2 years old at birth +1 year in "Mortal Coil" but dialogue treats her as 3-4. Production aging due to casting Pomers (age 8 at casting) vs in-universe age.

Cranial Horns - Sensory/Structural Trait

Source VersionCanon television, visible prosthetic from S4 onward

Two small frontal horns, Ktarian trait. No offensive function shown; purely anatomical marker of heritage.

Limitations

No evidence of enhanced senses, weaponization, or vulnerability; never used as plot device beyond identity.

Contradictions

None; consistently depicted.

High Cognitive Aptitude and Shipboard Knowledge

Source VersionCanon: "Once Upon a Time", "Bliss", "Collective", "Child's Play"

Exceptional memory and technical precocity for age: operates PADDs, understands astrometrics, knows Borg designations, assists Seven of Nine, and serves as 'Captain's assistant' with bridge access privileges granted by Janeway.

Limitations

Child-level judgment; requires supervision; knowledge is learned not innate. No telepathy or super-intelligence.

Contradictions

None, but fan exaggeration sometimes claims genius-level; canon shows bright child, not super-genius.

Social-Emotional Influence

Source VersionCanon: "Mortal Coil", "Once Upon a Time", "The Haunting of Deck Twelve"

Not a power, but notable ability to influence crew morale: Neelix's morale officer duties center on her, Seven of Nine develops humanity through mentoring her, and Janeway uses her as emotional anchor for crew-as-family theme.

Limitations

Interpersonal only; no command authority.

Contradictions

None.

Stories & Myths

Deadlock - Birth During Duplication

Source Star Trek: Voyager S2E21 "Deadlock" (teleplay by Brannon Braga & Joe Menosky)Culture American science fiction television (Paramount/UPN)Period 1996, in-universe stardate 49548.7 (2372)

Summary

A spatial scission duplicates Voyager. On one copy, Samantha Wildman goes into premature labor due to proton burst damage. The Doctor, with Kes's help, delivers Naomi Wildman while Vidiians attack and harvest crew. That Voyager is heavily damaged; Harry Kim dies and is replaced by his duplicate from the other Voyager. Naomi's duplicate on the less-damaged Voyager dies shortly after birth due to the same anomaly. When the two Janeways meet, they merge crews and the surviving Naomi is brought to the intact ship, where she is raised. Her birth thus involves the death of her alternate self and becomes a symbol of Voyager's survival through impossible odds.

Consequences

Establishes Naomi as first child born on Voyager; introduces Ktarian hybrid premise; Samantha Wildman becomes recurring character; sets up Naomi's unique status as only child aboard for two seasons.

Variants

Novelization by Christie Golden (1997) adds internal monologue; no contradictory variant. Some viewers misremember two Naomis surviving — canon confirms only one survives.

Mortal Coil - Neelix's Death and Naomi's Faith

Source Star Trek: Voyager S4E12 "Mortal Coil" (teleplay by Bryan Fuller)Culture American science fiction televisionPeriod 1997, stardate 51449.2 (2374)

Summary

Naomi, now a toddler played by Scarlett Pomers, is Neelix's closest friend and self-appointed sous-chef. When Neelix dies in a shuttle accident sampling protomatter, then is resurrected 18 hours later by Seven of Nine's Borg nanoprobes, he suffers an existential crisis — his afterlife vision was void, not the Talaxian Great Forest. Naomi, unaware of the metaphysics, waits for him with a drawing and asks why he is sad. Her innocent faith and need for his stories force Neelix to choose to live despite despair. Naomi's presence provides the episode's emotional resolution without resolving Neelix's theological crisis.

Once Upon a Time - The Flotter Program and Presumed Loss

Source Star Trek: Voyager S5E05 "Once Upon a Time" (teleplay by Michael Taylor)Culture American science fiction televisionPeriod 1998, stardate 52081.2 (2375)

Summary

Voyager's shuttle Delta Flyer crashes with Samantha Wildman aboard, presumed lost. To shield 3-year-old Naomi from grief, Neelix keeps her occupied in the holodeck with The Adventures of Flotter, a children's program about a forest sprite. Naomi, precocious, detects inconsistencies and eventually learns the truth when Neelix, guided by Seven of Nine's blunt honesty vs Neelix's protective storytelling, tells her her mother may be dead. She insists on honesty and confronts fear directly. Samantha is ultimately rescued, and Naomi learns that stories help but cannot replace truth. Episode is Naomi's only true spotlight episode.

Defines Naomi's relationship with Seven (truth vs comfort) and Neelix; establishes Flotter as her totem; shows Voyager's approach to children in crisis. Pomers' performance widely praised.

Variants

Flotter was created for this episode; later appears in licensed children's book 'The Adventures of Flotter' (1999). No alternate version.

Bliss and Collective - The Borg Children Peer Group

Source Star Trek: Voyager S5E14 "Bliss" and S6E16 "Collective" (and arc S6E16-S6E22)Culture American science fiction televisionPeriod 1999-2000, stardates 52542.3 to 53556.4 (2375-2376)

Summary

In "Bliss," Naomi, working in astrometrics with Seven, is among the few not seduced by a telepathic bio-organism's false wormhole home; her skepticism helps expose the illusion. In "Collective," Voyager rescues a damaged Borg cube with five adolescent drones. Four Borg children — Mezoti, Azan, Rebi, and later Icheb — are de-assimilated and housed in Cargo Bay 2. Naomi, previously the only child, suddenly has peers. She initially feels displaced (no longer the only child) but becomes big-sister figure, especially to Mezoti, teaching them games (Kadiss-Kot) and human social norms while Seven serves as mother-figure. Arc spans "Child's Play," "Ashes to Ashes," and "Imperfection."

Transforms Naomi from isolated child to social leader; provides long-running B-plot for final seasons; explores de-assimilation and found family. Mezoti and twins eventually leave to live with Wysanti; Icheb remains.

Variants

In novels, Borg children arc extended; Mezoti's departure handled differently. Deleted scene in "Collective" had Naomi teaching Borg children to play with Flotter toys.

Shattered - Encounter with Adult Self

Source Star Trek: Voyager S7E11 "Shattered" (teleplay by Mike Sussman & Michael Taylor)Culture American science fiction televisionPeriod 2001, stardate 53049.2 (2377) with fractured timeline spanning 2371-2394

Summary

A temporal fracture splits Voyager into different time periods per deck. Chakotay, inoculated, moves through eras. On a deck in ~2394 (17 years after "Caretaker"), he meets adult Naomi Wildman, now Ensign Wildman (played by Vanessa Branch, age 28), serving as astrometrics officer. She does not recognize him initially, believes Voyager never returned to Alpha Quadrant, and has grown up entirely in Delta Quadrant. She helps Chakotay restore the timeline, at cost of erasing her own future. When timeline is corrected, she reverts to child Naomi with no memory of the encounter, but Chakotay remembers.

Only glimpse of Naomi's potential future; confirms Starfleet career path and Ktarian longevity; emphasizes tragedy of Voyager's lost years. Non-canonical future erased, so not binding.

Variants

Adult Naomi appears only here; licensed novels later show different adult path (civilian scientist, not Starfleet). Contradiction noted in register.

The Haunting of Deck Twelve and Homestead - Storyteller Role

Source Star Trek: Voyager S6E25 "The Haunting of Deck Twelve" and S7E23 "Homestead"Culture American science fiction televisionPeriod 2000-2001

Summary

In "The Haunting of Deck Twelve," powerless Voyager drifts in nebula; to calm Borg children, Neelix tells them (and Naomi) a ghost story about Voyager itself — a meta-narrative where Naomi is audience surrogate, interjecting and questioning. In "Homestead," Neelix decides to leave Voyager to live with Talaxian colony; Naomi, now ~6, is central to his farewell, giving him a handmade compass and tearfully negotiating that he will still be her godfather. She represents the family he leaves behind and the reason he can leave — Voyager's children are grown enough to let go.

Relationships
Naomi Wildman→Samantha Wildmanfamily

Human mother, xenobiologist (Ensign, later Lieutenant JG). Pregnant at series start; gives birth in "Deadlock." Deeply bonded; Samantha balances science duties with single motherhood while father remains in Alpha Quadrant. Naomi's primary attachment figure; separation anxiety in "Once Upon a Time" centers on Samantha's presumed death. Samantha teaches Naomi Ktarian language fragments and human traditions.

Naomi Wildman→Greskrendtregkfamily

Ktarian father, never seen on screen. Name given in script and encyclopedia as Greskrendtregk (also transliterated Greskrendtregk). Assigned to Deep Space 9 at time of Voyager's departure; thus absent for entire Delta Quadrant journey. Naomi knows him only via subspace letters (with years-long delay) and mother's stories. Ktarian heritage explains horns and accelerated growth. No dialogue or appearance; existence confirmed via Samantha's logs.

Naomi Wildman→Neelixally

Talaxian morale officer, godfather and primary mentor. Self-appointed guardian from birth; teaches cooking, storytelling, and Delta Quadrant survival. Central to Naomi's emotional development; she calls him 'Neelix' not uncle, but relationship is explicitly godfather-like. He creates Flotter program for her, tells her stories to process death ("Mortal Coil"), and she gives him compass at his departure ("Homestead").

Naomi Wildman→Seven of Nine (Annika Hansen)ally

Former Borg drone, astrometrics officer, secondary mentor and big-sister figure. Initially blunt and logical, Seven learns parenting by mentoring Naomi and later Borg children. Teaches Naomi astrometrics, Borg efficiency, and honesty (contrasting Neelix's comforting stories). Naomi in turn humanizes Seven; their scenes in "Once Upon a Time," "Bliss," and Borg children arc are key to Seven's arc. Naomi is one of few crew Seven shows open affection to.

Naomi Wildman→Kathryn Janewayally

Captain, grants Naomi unique status as 'Captain's Assistant' — an honorary title Naomi takes literally, with bridge access and duties delivering PADDs. Janeway acts as grandmotherly authority, indulges Naomi's precocity, and uses her as symbol of Voyager as generational ship. In "Shattered," adult Naomi still serves under Janeway. Janeway's logs in "Deadlock" record Naomi's birth as crew milestone.

Naomi Wildman→Mezoti, Azan, Rebi, and Icheb (Borg children)ally

Peer group rescued from Borg cube. Mezoti (Ktarian/Borg hybrid, similar age) becomes Naomi's best friend and rival; Azan and Rebi (Wysanti twins) are younger playmates; Icheb (Brunali, older teen) is protective big brother. Naomi transitions from only child to leader of children's cohort, teaching games (Kadiss-Kot), sharing quarters, mediating conflicts. Group housed together in Cargo Bay 2 under Seven and Doctor's care.

Naomi Wildman→The Doctor (EMH) and Flotter (hologram)creator

The Doctor delivered Naomi and monitors her Ktarian development medically; also programs educational holoprograms. Flotter, the holographic forest sprite from The Adventures of Flotter (played by Wallace Langham), is Naomi's imaginary friend and coping mechanism; she interacts with him as if real during "Once Upon a Time." Flotter represents Voyager's use of holodeck as childcare and Naomi's rich inner life.

Borg Assimilation of Children

Source Voyager S6E16 "Collective" arc

Description

Naomi's peer group (Mezoti, Azan, Rebi, Icheb) are former Borg drones assimilated as children, with visible implants and trauma. Naomi witnesses their de-assimilation and PTSD. No sexual content; focus on bodily autonomy and recovery.

Contradictions & Disputes

Rate of Ktarian Maturation and Apparent Age

Position A

Naomi ages ~2-3x human rate — appears 4 at chronological age 2.

Position A — evidence

Dialogue in "Mortal Coil" (Neelix: 'Ktarians mature quickly') and "Once Upon a Time" (Doctor notes accelerated growth); Encyclopedia states Ktarian hybrids mature faster.

Position B

Production casting makes her appear 8-9 at chronological age 2 due to Pomers' actual age (8 when cast).

Position B — evidence

Visual evidence: Pomers born 1988, playing 2-year-old in 1997; no in-universe explanation for jump from infant to 8-year-old appearance in one year.

Scholarly Consensus

Production necessity acknowledged by writers (Biller interview in Companion). Canon explanation is Ktarian genetics; fandom accepts hand-wave. No official retcon.

Father's Name Spelling

Position A

Greskrendtregk (as per script and Encyclopedia 3rd ed.)

Position A — evidence

Voyager production script for "Deadlock" spells Greskrendtregk; Okuda Encyclopedia uses same.

Position B

Greskrendtregk / Greskrendtregk (variant in StarTrek.com and early call sheets)

Position B — evidence

StarTrek.com databank (2001 version) and some licensed novels use Greskrendtregk with additional 't'.

Scholarly Consensus

Same person; transliteration variance for alien name. Most recent official source (StarTrek.com 2023) uses Greskrendtregk.

Adult Naomi's Future - Starfleet vs Civilian

Position A

Adult Naomi in "Shattered" is Starfleet Ensign, implying she joins Starfleet if Voyager remains in Delta Quadrant.

Position A — evidence

"Shattered" shows Ensign Wildman in operations gold, age ~22, serving in astrometrics — alternate timeline where Voyager never returns.

Position B

Relaunch novels depict adult/teen Naomi as civilian scientist dependent, not Starfleet officer, after Voyager returns in 2378.

Position B — evidence

Kirsten Beyer's Full Circle (2009) and subsequent novels: Naomi (age 10-12) lives on Earth, attends school, no Starfleet academy path yet.

Scholarly Consensus

Both are alternate futures; "Shattered" future was erased. Novels are licensed, not canon. No contradiction within canon; divergence is expected.
