Who It Is

Born during a starship duplication that killed her other self before she drew breath, Naomi Wildman is the only child to grow up entirely aboard the USS Voyager — a half-Ktarian, half-human girl who learned to navigate Borg corridors and holodeck forests while the adults around her tried to navigate getting home.

Attested personality across 15+ episodes: precocious, inquisitive, highly verbal, and socially confident despite being the only child on a warship for two years. Seeks adult companionship and mentorship; forms intense attachments to surrogate parental figures (Neelix as godfather/mentor, Seven of Nine as big-sister figure). Loves stories, games, and holodeck play but shows anxiety about parental loss ("Once Upon a Time") and about being different (Ktarian heritage). Demonstrates Starfleet-adjacent precocity: knows ship systems, assists in astrometrics, understands Borg terminology

Ktarian Hybrid Physiology - Accelerated Development

As half-Ktarian, Naomi exhibits significantly faster gestation and maturation than full humans. Samantha Wildman was pregnant for only a few months before birth (Ktarian females have shorter gestation), and Naomi reaches apparent age 4-5 within ~2 chronological years.

Limit: Not superhuman speed; still requires learning and education. Growth rate slows after early childhood per dialogue. No canonical adult lifespan stated.

Cranial Horns - Sensory/Structural Trait

Two small frontal horns, Ktarian trait. No offensive function shown; purely anatomical marker of heritage.

Limit: No evidence of enhanced senses, weaponization, or vulnerability; never used as plot device beyond identity.

High Cognitive Aptitude and Shipboard Knowledge

Exceptional memory and technical precocity for age: operates PADDs, understands astrometrics, knows Borg designations, assists Seven of Nine, and serves as 'Captain's assistant' with bridge access privileges granted by Janeway.

Limit: Child-level judgment; requires supervision; knowledge is learned not innate. No telepathy or super-intelligence.

Social-Emotional Influence

Not a power, but notable ability to influence crew morale: Neelix's morale officer duties center on her, Seven of Nine develops humanity through mentoring her, and Janeway uses her as emotional anchor for crew-as-family theme.

Limit: Interpersonal only; no command authority.

Accounts I
Deadlock - Birth During DuplicationStar Trek: Voyager S2E21 "Deadlock" (teleplay by Brannon Braga & Joe Menosky)

1996, in-universe stardate 49548.7 (2372)

A spatial scission duplicates Voyager. On one copy, Samantha Wildman goes into premature labor due to proton burst damage. The Doctor, with Kes's help, delivers Naomi Wildman while Vidiians attack and harvest crew. That Voyager is heavily damaged; Harry Kim dies and is replaced by his duplicate from the other Voyager. Naomi's duplicate on the less-damaged Voyager dies shortly after birth due to the same anomaly. When the two Janeways meet, they merge crews and the surviving Naomi is brought to the intact ship, where she is raised. Her birth thus involves the death of her alternate self and becomes a symbol of Voyager's survival through impossible odds. Establishes Naomi as first child born on Voyager; introduces Ktarian hybrid premise; Samantha Wildman becomes recurring character; sets up Naomi's unique status as only child aboard for two seasons.

Mortal Coil - Neelix's Death and Naomi's FaithStar Trek: Voyager S4E12 "Mortal Coil" (teleplay by Bryan Fuller)

1997, stardate 51449.2 (2374)

Naomi, now a toddler played by Scarlett Pomers, is Neelix's closest friend and self-appointed sous-chef. When Neelix dies in a shuttle accident sampling protomatter, then is resurrected 18 hours later by Seven of Nine's Borg nanoprobes, he suffers an existential crisis — his afterlife vision was void, not the Talaxian Great Forest. Naomi, unaware of the metaphysics, waits for him with a drawing and asks why he is sad. Her innocent faith and need for his stories force Neelix to choose to live despite despair. Naomi's presence provides the episode's emotional resolution without resolving Neelix's theological crisis. Cements Naomi-Neelix godfather bond; first major character development for Naomi as speaking role; introduces her reliance on stories to process death. Frequently cited in scholarship on Star Trek and grief.

Once Upon a Time - The Flotter Program and Presumed LossStar Trek: Voyager S5E05 "Once Upon a Time" (teleplay by Michael Taylor)

1998, stardate 52081.2 (2375)

Voyager's shuttle Delta Flyer crashes with Samantha Wildman aboard, presumed lost. To shield 3-year-old Naomi from grief, Neelix keeps her occupied in the holodeck with The Adventures of Flotter, a children's program about a forest sprite. Naomi, precocious, detects inconsistencies and eventually learns the truth when Neelix, guided by Seven of Nine's blunt honesty vs Neelix's protective storytelling, tells her her mother may be dead. She insists on honesty and confronts fear directly. Samantha is ultimately rescued, and Naomi learns that stories help but cannot replace truth. Episode is Naomi's only true spotlight episode. Defines Naomi's relationship with Seven (truth vs comfort) and Neelix; establishes Flotter as her totem; shows Voyager's approach to children in crisis. Pomers' performance widely praised.

Accounts II
Bliss and Collective - The Borg Children Peer GroupStar Trek: Voyager S5E14 "Bliss" and S6E16 "Collective" (and arc S6E16-S6E22)

1999-2000, stardates 52542.3 to 53556.4 (2375-2376)

In "Bliss," Naomi, working in astrometrics with Seven, is among the few not seduced by a telepathic bio-organism's false wormhole home; her skepticism helps expose the illusion. In "Collective," Voyager rescues a damaged Borg cube with five adolescent drones. Four Borg children — Mezoti, Azan, Rebi, and later Icheb — are de-assimilated and housed in Cargo Bay 2. Naomi, previously the only child, suddenly has peers. She initially feels displaced (no longer the only child) but becomes big-sister figure, especially to Mezoti, teaching them games (Kadiss-Kot) and human social norms while Seven serves as mother-figure. Arc spans "Child's Play," "Ashes to Ashes," and "Imperfection." Transforms Naomi from isolated child to social leader; provides long-running B-plot for final seasons; explores de-assimilation and found family. Mezoti and twins eventually leave to live with Wysanti; Icheb remains.

Shattered - Encounter with Adult SelfStar Trek: Voyager S7E11 "Shattered" (teleplay by Mike Sussman & Michael Taylor)

2001, stardate 53049.2 (2377) with fractured timeline spanning 2371-2394

A temporal fracture splits Voyager into different time periods per deck. Chakotay, inoculated, moves through eras. On a deck in ~2394 (17 years after "Caretaker"), he meets adult Naomi Wildman, now Ensign Wildman (played by Vanessa Branch, age 28), serving as astrometrics officer. She does not recognize him initially, believes Voyager never returned to Alpha Quadrant, and has grown up entirely in Delta Quadrant. She helps Chakotay restore the timeline, at cost of erasing her own future. When timeline is corrected, she reverts to child Naomi with no memory of the encounter, but Chakotay remembers. Only glimpse of Naomi's potential future; confirms Starfleet career path and Ktarian longevity; emphasizes tragedy of Voyager's lost years. Non-canonical future erased, so not binding.

The Haunting of Deck Twelve and Homestead - Storyteller RoleStar Trek: Voyager S6E25 "The Haunting of Deck Twelve" and S7E23 "Homestead"

2000-2001

In "The Haunting of Deck Twelve," powerless Voyager drifts in nebula; to calm Borg children, Neelix tells them (and Naomi) a ghost story about Voyager itself — a meta-narrative where Naomi is audience surrogate, interjecting and questioning. In "Homestead," Neelix decides to leave Voyager to live with Talaxian colony; Naomi, now ~6, is central to his farewell, giving him a handmade compass and tearfully negotiating that he will still be her godfather. She represents the family he leaves behind and the reason he can leave — Voyager's children are grown enough to let go. Bookends Neelix arc; Naomi's goodbye is emotional climax of Neelix departure; final significant Naomi appearance before series finale "Endgame" (where she appears briefly in montage).

Naomi Wildman is fully human or fully Ktarian. — Canon explicitly establishes half-Ktarian, half-human hybrid. Mother Samantha Wildman is human; father Greskrendtregk is Ktarian. Hybrid status explains horns and accelerated growth, confirmed in "Deadlock" and Encyclopedia.

Naomi was played by a single actress throughout. — Three actresses: Brooke Stephens (infant, S2-S3), Scarlett Pomers (child, S4-S7, 12 episodes), Vanessa Branch (adult alternate in "Shattered"). Pomers is definitive but not sole.

Naomi is Captain Janeway's daughter or Neelix's daughter. — No biological relation to Janeway or Neelix. Janeway is captain/mentor, Neelix is godfather/family friend. Mother is Samantha Wildman; father is absent Ktarian. Confusion arises from close surrogate-parent scenes.

The Flotter holoprogram is a real Star Trek species or planet. — Flotter is fictional within fiction — a children's holo-series (The Adventures of Flotter) created for Voyager's children, seen only in "Once Upon a Time." Not a real Delta Quadrant species.
