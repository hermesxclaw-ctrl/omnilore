Who It Is

In the rolling hills of southern France, a name whispers through centuries: Aude, the betrothed of Roland, whose tragic fate in the Chanson de Roland echoes the region's own turbulent history. But Aude is more than a legend—it is a land of Cathar castles, Roman roads, and winds that shape both the landscape and the people. From the ancient skull of Tautavel to the modern wind turbines, Aude is a living testament to the enduring interplay between myth and reality.

Aude is portrayed as devoted, loyal, and emotionally vulnerable. In the Chanson de Roland, she is proactive in seeking confirmation of Roland's fate and dies of grief, demonstrating the depth of her love.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description is provided in the Chanson de Roland. She is depicted as a young noblewoman, likely of high social standing.

Symbols — In medieval art and later illustrations, Aude is often depicted as a sorrowful maiden, sometimes shown fainting or dying upon hearing of Roland's death. She symbolizes loyalty and tragic love.

Domains — She resides at the court of Charlemagne in Aix-la-Chapelle (Aachen), as depicted in the Chanson de Roland.

Accounts I
The Death of Aude (Chanson de Roland)Chanson de Roland, Oxford manuscript

c. 1100-1125

After the Battle of Roncevaux, Charlemagne returns to Aix-la-Chapelle. Aude, Roland's betrothed, approaches Charlemagne and asks if Roland is alive. Charlemagne, unable to tell her the truth, says Roland is dead. Aude then collapses and dies at Charlemagne's feet. Charlemagne mourns her and orders her burial with honor. Aude's death underscores the tragic cost of the war and the personal loss suffered by Roland's loved ones. It also highlights Charlemagne's grief and the human toll of his campaigns.

Accounts II
Historical Oda, Wife of BertrandVita Sancti Huberti

8th-9th century

Oda, also called Aude, was the wife of Bertrand, Duke of Aquitaine, and the mother of Eudo, who was a brother of Saint Hubertus. This historical figure may have influenced the naming of the literary character. Her historical existence provides a possible origin for the name, but the literary character is separate.
