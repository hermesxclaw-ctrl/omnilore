Who It Is

The name Bogard hits like a fist in an arcade — most people hear it and see a red cap, a ponytail, and a shout of 'Are you okay?' echoing across Southtown. But that same surname has been printed on Baptist tracts, stamped on a Missouri post office, and inked by a Flemish printer in Leuven four centuries before any Power Wave ever left a glove.

Fictional Bogards - Terry: Attested as easygoing, charismatic, orphan-raised street fighter with strong sense of justice, surrogate father to Rock Howard (Geese Howard's son) after Fatal Fury: Mark of the Wolves. Loner who travels America, loves fast food and basketball, dislikes octopus. Canonically refuses to hold grudges; spares Geese in multiple endings. Andy: stoic, disciplined, more traditionally Japanese-influenced despite being American, devoted to fiancée Mai Shiranui but emotionally reserved. Jeff: idealized martial arts master, benevolent father figure, practitioner of Hakky

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Surname Bogard is a variant of Bogart / Bogaert / Boomgaard. Most authorities derive it from Middle Dutch bogaert / boomgaert 'orchard, fruit garden' (modern Dutch boomgaard), originally an occupational or topographic name for an orchard keeper or someone living near an orchard. Powers
Hakkyokuseiken (Eight Extremes Holy Fist) - Power Wave

Terry and Jeff's signature chi projectile. Terry slams fist to ground, sending yellow energy wave along ground. Andy cannot use Power Wave (uses projectile Zanei Ken instead). Jeff was master of the style.

Limit: Ground-traveling only in original version; can be jumped over; later variants include Power Geyser (upward eruption) and Buster Wolf. Requires contact with ground; ineffective airborne. Andy explicitly unable to perform it, showing style divergence.

Burn Knuckle / Burning Knuckle

Terry's rushing punch with extended arm engulfed in flame/energy, closing distance rapidly. Core special move across all appearances.

Limit: Punishable on block; linear trajectory; Terry vulnerable during windup. In Smash, has armor on startup but not invincible.

Power Geyser / Power Dunk / Buster Wolf

Power Geyser: Terry punches ground causing massive vertical geyser of energy (Desperation Move). Power Dunk: rising knee into diving punch. Buster Wolf (Garou: Mark of the Wolves, 1999): Terry dashes, punches, shouts 'Are you okay? Buster Wolf!' - became his Smash Final Smash.

Limit: Super moves require meter (Power gauge). Buster Wolf requires close range for second hit. In Smash, Buster Wolf is directional input special, not Final Smash (which is Triple Geyser).

Kōga-ryū Ninjutsu and Shiranui-ryū influence (Andy Bogard)

Andy uses ninjutsu-derived techniques: Shoryu Dan (rising dragon bullet), Zanei Ken (vacuum hand slash), Kuhadan (air projectile), and shuriken. More speed-oriented, less raw power than Terry.

Limit: Shorter range projectiles than Terry; requires precise spacing; physically less durable than Terry in lore (Terry wins most canonical brother vs brother bouts).

Printing and publishing (Jan Bogard) - technical ability

Operation of movable-type printing press, Latin and Dutch typesetting, woodcut device printing, university thesis and theological folio production for University of Leuven and Douai.

Limit: Pre-industrial press, limited to ~1,000-1,500 sheets per day, dependent on paper supply and ecclesiastical censorship (Catholic Counter-Reformation context). No surviving press equipment; only books.

Oratory, debate and denominational organization (Ben M. Bogard)

Public debate (118+ formal debates attested), radio broadcasting (early 1930s, KTHS), authorship of 30+ books/pamphlets, founding of American Baptist Association (1924).

Limit: Influence largely confined to Landmark Missionary Baptist movement; rejected by Southern Baptist Convention mainstream; radio reach limited to Arkansas/Texas. Debating style described as aggressive and polarizing.

Dark-sky advocacy and nature writing (Paul Bogard)

Authorial ability to synthesize science, memoir, and advocacy; led to International Dark-Sky Association advocacy, public speaking, university teaching (James Madison University).

Limit: Not a scientific researcher; relies on interviewing astronomers and ecologists. Advocacy impact measurable but light pollution continues to increase.

The Murder of Jeff Bogard and the Brothers' Vow - Fatal Fury: King of FightersSNK Fatal Fury: King of Fighters arcade prologue, manual, and Fatal Fury: Legend of the Hungry Wolf anime (1992)

1991 (in-universe: c.1981, ten years before first King of Fighters tournament)

Jeff Bogard, master of Hakkyokuseiken and adoptive/biological father of Terry and Andy, is murdered by crime lord Geese Howard after Jeff refuses to submit and after Jeff's rival Tung Fu Rue is also challenged. Eight-year-old Terry witnesses the murder from a doorway; Andy is elsewhere. The brothers are taken in by Tung Fu Rue, who trains them. Ten years later they enter Geese's King of Fighters tournament in South Town to avenge Jeff. Terry defeats Geese, who falls from Geese Tower but survives in later canon. Establishes core Fatal Fury saga; Terry becomes 'Legendary Hungry Wolf'; Geese becomes recurring antagonist; sets up Terry's later guardianship of Geese's son Rock Howard. Foundation for all SNK crossover lore.

Terry's Lone Wolf Pilgrimage - Fatal Fury 2 and Fatal Fury SpecialFatal Fury 2 (1992) story mode, character endings, and Neo Geo Freak mook interviews

1992-1993 (in-universe: one year after Geese's fall)

After defeating Geese, Terry becomes aimless and leaves South Town to travel America, entering street fights to test himself. He is challenged by new antagonist Wolfgang Krauser, Geese's half-brother and German noble who seeks to prove himself the strongest. Krauser defeats Terry in their first encounter, wounding his pride. Terry trains under Tung Fu Rue again, learns Power Geyser, and returns to defeat Krauser in Germany. Andy, meanwhile, has trained in Japan and reunites with Terry. Introduces Power Geyser, establishes Terry's wanderer archetype, expands world beyond South Town, introduces Krauser as fan-favorite boss. Terry's loss and comeback becomes defining character arc.

The King of Fighters Crossover - Dream Match ContinuityThe King of Fighters '94 (1994) through KOF XV (2022) official story prologues

1994-present (annual releases; in-universe separate timeline)

SNK creates a dream-match tournament inviting teams of three. Terry teams with Andy and Joe Higashi as Team Fatal Fury (also called Team Garou). The KOF story is explicitly non-canon to Fatal Fury's South Town saga but reuses characters. Terry remains a mainstay, appearing in every KOF entry, often as team leader. His KOF story includes rivalry with Ryo Sakazaki, friendship with Blue Mary, and mentoring Rock Howard in later entries (KOF: Maximum Impact, KOF XV DLC). Made Terry SNK's mascot, more recognizable than Kyo to Western audiences; kept Bogard name alive through 30 years of annual games; led to Smash invitation. Created continuity confusion between Fatal Fury canon and KOF canon.

The Founding of Bogard, Missouri and Bogard TownshipCarroll County Historical Society; History of Carroll County, Missouri (1881); U.S. Post Office records; GNIS

1880-1884 (town platted); township organized 1870s

Bogard Township was organized in Carroll County in the early 1870s, named for an early settler family (sources differ whether for John Bogard or Rev. Bogard). The city of Bogard was platted in 1884 along the Chicago, Burlington and Quincy Railroad extension, with a post office established 1884. It grew as an agricultural shipping point for corn, wheat and livestock. Population peaked at 519 in 1920, then declined steadily with railroad abandonment and farm consolidation to 167 in 2020. Bogard Creek, 20 miles south, was named for the same family or for a trapper named Bogard who camped there in the 1830s (per Missouri place-name scholar Robert L. Ramsay). Created persistent toponym that preserves surname in geography; Bogard School District and Bogard Baptist Church remain. Example of railroad-era prairie town boom-bust cycle.

Ben M. Bogard's Landmark Baptist Crusade and the American Baptist AssociationBogard, Ben M. Pillars of Orthodoxy (1900); American Baptist Association minutes 1924; Dan Gilbert biography; secondary scholarship by James Tull

1890-1951 (peak 1905-1930)

Ben Marcus Bogard (1868-1951), born in Elizabethtown, Kentucky, became a Missionary Baptist preacher in Arkansas. Rejecting the Southern Baptist Convention's centralization, he championed Landmarkism - the belief in an unbroken succession of Baptist churches since John the Baptist. After the 1905 split, he led the formation of the American Baptist Association in Texarkana, Texas/Arkansas in 1924, serving as its most prominent polemicist. He claimed 118 public debates against Methodists, Campbellites, and others, edited The Baptist Commonwealth, and was an early radio preacher on KTHS Hot Springs. His writings defended strict congregational autonomy and opposed evolution, Catholicism, and later, desegregation. ABA grew to ~1,700 churches by 1950; Bogard's books remained in print in Landmark circles into the 21st century. His segregationist editorials in the 1940s-50s are now cited as example of religious justification for Jim Crow. His name remains on Bogard Press (Texarkana).

Bogard is the same as Boggart - the Lancashire household spirit is called a Bogard — Boggart (also bogart, bogle) is a distinct English dialect word for a mischievous household spirit, from Middle English *boggart, of unknown origin possibly related to 'bug' (as in bugbear). Bogard the surname is from Dutch/Flemish boomgaard 'orchard'. They are homophones in some accents but etymologically unrelated. The folklore spirit is never spelled Bogard in scholarly folklore collections (Briggs, Simpson).

Terry Bogard is originally a King of Fighters character — Terry debuted in Fatal Fury: King of Fighters (1991), three years before The King of Fighters '94 (1994). KOF borrowed him as its most famous guest. Fatal Fury is his origin franchise; KOF is the crossover.

Bogard, Missouri was named after Terry Bogard or vice versa — Bogard, Missouri was platted in 1884, 107 years before Terry Bogard's creation in 1991. No connection. SNK has never referenced the Missouri town; the surname was chosen for its American sound.

All people named Bogard are related to the Fatal Fury family — Bogard is a real surname with hundreds of bearers unrelated to the fictional family. Ben M. Bogard, Jan Bogard, Paul Bogard, and the Missouri Bogards have no familial or narrative connection to Terry/Andy/Jeff, who are fictional Americans created by a Japanese studio.

Jeff Bogard was a playable fighter who died in gameplay — Jeff Bogard has never been playable in any official SNK game; he appears only in backstory, portraits, and non-canonical cameos. His death occurs before Fatal Fury 1's events and is never shown interactively, only in prologues and anime flashbacks.

Seth Bogart and Terry Bogard share the same spelling and are the same person — Artist Seth Bogart was born Seth Davis Bogard (with D) and performs as Bogart (with T). The spelling difference is intentional stage name; he is not related to SNK's Bogards and his work is queer punk/pop, not gaming.
