The Approach

In the mid-1990s, a horse with a shadow roll and a timid heart became the most dominant force in Japanese racing, capturing the nation's imagination with a Triple Crown triumph and a bitter rivalry that ended in tragedy.

Content advisory. Contains themes of animal injury, illness, and euthanasia.
Identity

Narita Brian

Pronunciation NAH-ree-tah BRY-an (Japanese: ナリタブライアン)

Known As

Narita BuraianShadow Roll MonsterNarita Brian (Japanese: ナリタブライアン)

Etymology

The name 'Narita' comes from the owner's prefix 'Narita', likely referencing the city of Narita in Chiba Prefecture. 'Brian' is derived from his sire Brian's Time.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese horse racing culture; he is a national icon in Japan.

Disputed Identifications

None known. He is clearly a real racehorse, not a fictional character.

Description

Appearance

Narita Brian was a bay horse with a white star and stripe. He was known for wearing a shadow roll (a sheepskin-covered roll on the noseband) during races, which earned him the nickname 'Shadow Roll Monster'. His build was described as powerful and athletic, with a strong hindquarter.

Symbols & Iconography

The shadow roll became his iconic symbol, often depicted in fan art and in Umamusume. His racing colors were those of the owner's silks.

Behaviour & Personality

Despite his physical prowess, he was known to have a timid temperament. For example, he was reportedly startled by a puddle during training and threw his rider. He was also described as having a strong will during races, often coming from behind to win.

Domains & Habitats

He was trained and raced in Japan, primarily at Central Horse Racing facilities. He stood at stud at CB Stud after retirement.

Version Differences

The real horse is a historical figure. In Umamusume: Pretty Derby, he is anthropomorphized as a horse girl with a personality that emphasizes physical strength and a somewhat lazy attitude toward student council duties. This is a fictional interpretation.

Health decline and euthanasia

Source Race records and news reports

Description

After developing a hip joint disease and later tendonitis, Narita Brian's performance declined. In 1998, he developed colic and a gastric rupture, leading to his euthanasia.

Scholarly Context

This is a common fate for racehorses, highlighting the physical toll of racing.
