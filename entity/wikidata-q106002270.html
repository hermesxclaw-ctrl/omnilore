The Approach

In the world of horse racing, where victory is the only measure of worth, one small bay mare from Hokkaido ran 113 races without a single win—and in doing so, became a national symbol of perseverance, a living talisman for the unlucky, and an unlikely star whose story continues to inspire long after her final race.

Content advisory. No dark material; contains themes of animal welfare concerns regarding merchandise made from mane/tail hairs, and the emotional weight of a losing streak and eventual death by colic.
Identity

Haru Urara

Pronunciation HAH-roo oo-RAH-rah

Known As

ハルウララThe Shining Star of Losers Everywhere負け組の星The Star of Losers, Haru Urara

Etymology

The name 'Haru Urara' (ハルウララ) means 'Glorious Spring' or 'Gentle Spring' in Japanese, combining 'haru' (spring) and 'urara' (gentle, serene).

Cultural Ownership

Japanese horse racing culture, particularly associated with Kochi Prefecture and the Kochi Racetrack.

Disputed Identifications

None; she is a real horse, not a fictional character. However, her cultural persona as 'the horse that never won' is a constructed narrative that sometimes overshadows her actual life and achievements.

Description

Appearance

Bay mare, typical Thoroughbred build, not particularly large; her appearance was often noted as unremarkable in racing circles.

Symbols & Iconography

Her name and image were used on numerous products: stuffed toys, key rings, phone straps, stickers, stamps, train tickets, rice, shochu, hats, T-shirts, bras. Her betting tickets became omamori (charms) for traffic safety. In the anime Umamusume: Pretty Derby, she is depicted as a cheerful rose pink-haired girl.

Behaviour & Personality

Described as a determined and persevering runner, never giving up despite consistent losses. Post-retirement, she was known to be gentle and calm, interacting with visitors at Matha Farm.

Domains & Habitats

Raced primarily at Kochi Racetrack (a small local track). Retired to Matha Farm in Onjuku, Chiba Prefecture, where she lived for her final years.

Version Differences

There are no major version differences, but her public image evolved from a losing racehorse to a symbol of perseverance, to a beloved cultural icon, and finally to a media personality in anime.

Stories & Myths

The Losing Streak

Source Various news reports and racing recordsCulture JapanesePeriod 1998-2004

Summary

Haru Urara debuted on November 17, 1998, at Kochi Racetrack, finishing last. Over the next five years, she raced almost monthly without ever winning. In June 2003, after her 80th loss, national media picked up her story, and she became a household name as a symbol of perseverance. Her popularity peaked in March 2004 when 13,000 fans attended her race, betting over ¥121 million on a win that never came. She retired in August 2004 with 0 wins and 113 losses.

Haru Urara became a national icon, inspiring merchandise, songs, books, and a film. Her story resonated with many as a metaphor for not giving up in the face of defeat.

The First Win at the Soft Girls' Gathering

Source Matha Farm, news reportsCulture JapanesePeriod 2019

Summary

On May 18, 2019, Haru Urara, then 23, participated in a time trial race for older horses at Matha Farm. She ran 16.54 seconds and was placed first, marking her first-ever victory.

The Haru Urara Boom

Source Japanese media coverageCulture JapanesePeriod 2003-2004

Summary

In 2003, Haru Urara's losing streak captured national attention. Her betting tickets became omamori for traffic safety, due to the pun on 'ataranai' (to not win/not be hit). Merchandise ranged from stuffed toys to bras. The Prime Minister even commented on her. The boom peaked with a race where fans waited hours to bet on her, but she lost again.

Consequences

The boom made Haru Urara a cultural phenomenon, but also raised animal welfare concerns over the sale of hair charms, which were replaced with wooden plaques.

Death by Colic

Source Matha Farm announcementCulture JapanesePeriod 2025

Summary

On September 8, 2025, Haru Urara became ill, and despite veterinary care, she died shortly after dawn on September 9, surrounded by staff. The cause was colic.

Animal welfare concerns

Source News reports

Description

In 2003, the Kochi Prefecture Horse Racing Association produced omamori from Haru Urara's mane and tail hairs, but production was halted due to concerns from animal welfare groups.

Scholarly Context

This reflects broader debates about the ethics of using animals for commercial gain.
