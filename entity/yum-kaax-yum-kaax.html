Who They Are

Yum Kaax is the Maya maize god, one of the most important deities in a civilization where maize was the foundation of life itself. He is a young, beautiful god whose life cycle mirrors the growth of corn — he dies at harvest, travels through the underworld, and is reborn at the planting season. In the Popol Vuh, the Maize God's resurrection is a central episode of the creation myth.

Cultural Significance

The Maya Maize God is arguably the most fundamental religious symbol of Maya civilization, reflecting the complete dependence of the culture on corn agriculture. The Toniná and Copán sites have produced spectacular Maize God sculptures. The resurrection of the Maize God in the Popol Vuh is one of the most profound expressions of Maya theology — the belief that life and death are a continuous cycle, not an end. The Maya king would often dress as the Maize God during important ceremonies, identifying himself with the god who dies and is reborn, just as the king's own power was understood to derive from this cosmic cycle.
