Who It Is

In the high, cold plains of what is now Colombia, the Muisca people spoke of a figure who came as a wind, a whisper of change that would shape their world. His name—Bochica—survives in fragments: a destroyed building, a father to a great cat, a teacher of laws. Who was this elusive being, and how did colonial chroniclers transform him into a hero?

Described as a civilising hero: he taught the Muisca moral codes, agriculture, and religious practices. He is also associated with wind, as the 1563 record states he 'is a wind.'

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The etymology of 'Bochica' is uncertain; it may derive from Muysccubun (the Muisca language) elements, but no definitive scholarly consensus exists. The name 'Nemqueteba' possibly relates to 'nemque' (meaning 'to shine' or 'light') but this is speculative.

Pronunciation — bo-CHEE-ka

Aliases — Nemqueteba, Nemquetaha, Sadigua, Xue

Ownership — Muisca (Chibcha) culture of the Altiplano Cundiboyacense, present-day Colombia (pre-Columbian and colonial period).

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description survives in primary sources. Colonial accounts refer to him as a figure, but without specific attributes. Later artistic depictions often show an old man with a staff, but this is not attested.

Symbols — Unknown. No pre-Columbian iconography has been securely identified as Bochica. The Temple of the Sun at Sugamuxi may have been associated with him, but no specific symbols are recorded.

Domains — Associated with the Altiplano Cundiboyacense, particularly Pasca, Gámeza, and the Toya cave. Sugamuxi became a sacred city due to his arrival.

Accounts I
Arrival and establishment of sacred sitesChronicles of Simón and Piedrahita

Pre-Columbian (as narrated post-conquest)

Bochica appeared in Pasca and later in Gámeza, where he was hospitably received. He retreated to the Toya cave, where caciques from Tópaga, Tota, Pesca, Firavitoba, and others came to consult him. After Chiminigagua (the supreme being) sent him to Sugamuxi, the city became sacred, and the Temple of the Sun was erected there, with festivities organised around his arrival. Establishment of Sugamuxi as a religious centre; Temple of the Sun; annual festivals.

Accounts II
Bochica as civilising heroPiedrahita (1688)

Post-conquest interpretation

According to Piedrahita, Bochica descended to earth to found the Muisca religion, teaching moral and social norms. Foundation of Muisca religious and social order.
