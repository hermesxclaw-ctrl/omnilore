Bochica is the great culture hero of Muisca mythology, a bearded white man who appeared among the Muisca after the supreme god Chiminigagua had withdrawn from the world. He taught the Muisca the arts of civilization — weaving, pottery, agriculture, social organization, and the worship of the true gods — and established the moral and legal code that governed Muisca society.

Cultural Significance

Bochica is the Muisca equivalent of figures like Quetzalcoatl in Mesoamerica or Viracocha in the Inca world — a civilizing hero who brings knowledge and order. The myth of Bochica served to legitimize the Muisca social and political structure, including the authority of the Zipa (supreme ruler) and the priesthood. The Spanish chroniclers noted that the Muisca regarded Bochica's laws as the foundation of their civilization, and the parallel between Bochica's teachings and the Ten Commandments was often commented on by missionaries. The Piedra de Bochica (Bochica's Rock) near the Muisca town of Cogua is still considered a sacred site by some modern Muisca descendants.
