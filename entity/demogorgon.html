Who It Is

Demogorgon is the self-styled 'Prince of Demons' — the most powerful and feared of all the demon lords who war for supremacy in the infinite chaos of the Abyss. Physically, Demogorgon is a creature of pure nightmare: he towers 18 feet tall with a powerful reptilian-humanoid body, but above his massive shoulders rise two long, serpentine necks, each ending in a monstrous baboon-like head. These two heads — named Aameul and Hathhori — are independent consciousnesses sharing a single body, and they despise each other almost as much as they despise all other life. They constantly argue, scheme against each other, and occasionally turn to bite one another mid-battle. Each head has a different gaze attack: one can hypnotize and beguile, the other can rot flesh and wither limbs.

Demogorgon rules the 88th layer of the Abyss, a realm called the Gaping Maw, which is half-sunken in an endless briny sea and populated by horrific aquatic-demonic hybrids. From this fortress-palace of Abysm, he commands legions of demons, particularly hezrou and other amphibious fiends. His primary goals are twofold: to destroy his eternal rivals Orcus (Demon Prince of Undeath) and Graz'zt (the Dark Prince), and to ascend to true godhood by consuming the divine essence of a greater deity.

Demogorgon has the distinction of being the highest Challenge Rating demon lord in several editions of D&D, and facing him is considered a campaign-defining event. His abilities include dual actions (each head acts independently in combat), a rotting aura that kills vegetation and sickens living creatures, spell-like abilities that include feeblemind, insanity, and mass suggestion, and the ability to tear reality itself with his claws. His name is borrowed from a real-world mystery: 'Demogorgon' was a name that appeared in late antiquity as a supposed primordial deity or demon, possibly a scribal error for the Greek 'demiurge' (creator), popularized by Boccaccio and later by Milton's Paradise Lost. D&D transformed this obscure reference into one of the most iconic villains in gaming history — and his name reached mainstream recognition when the creators of Stranger Things borrowed 'Demogorgon' for their series' central monster, a flower-faced predator from the Upside Down (though that creature bears no resemblance to the D&D original).
