Who It Is

On a night when the sky tore open with a twin-tailed comet, a barbarian chieftain lifted a dwarf-forged warhammer and smashed a path through an age of blood and Chaos — and twelve tribes who had only ever known each other as rivals knelt and called him Emperor. That man was Sigmar Heldenhammer, and whether he was a brilliant warlord, a living god, or the most successful piece of Imperial propaganda ever written depends on which century of Warhammer history you ask.

Attested personality is layered. In earliest tribal period (McNeill, WFRP): impulsive, fiercely loyal to kin, quick to anger, pragmatic, suspicious of magic and priests, but capable of remarkable diplomacy — uniting twelve mutually hostile tribes through charisma, oaths, and calculated generosity to Dwarfs. Valued strength, oaths, and personal combat; disdained intrigue. As Emperor (c. 0-50 IC): statesmanlike, law-giver, builder of roads and cities (Reikdorf), institutor of Counts Elector system, but increasingly weary and haunted by Chaos. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Modern fantasy construction from Germanic elements: Old High German sigu / sigi 'victory' + mari 'famous, renowned' (cf. historical Sigmar, Siegmar). Epithet Heldenhammer is Germanic compound Helden (heroes') + Hammer, literally 'Hammer of Heroes' but rendered in-universe as hammer that fells heroes

Pronunciation — SIG-mar HEL-den-ham-mer /ˈsɪɡ.mɑːr ˈhɛl.dənˌhæm.ər/; in German-language editions: [ˈzɪɡmaʁ ˈhɛldn̩ˌhamɐ]

Aliases — Sigmar Unberogen, Sigmar of the Unberogen, Heldenhammer (Hammer of the Goblins, in Khazalid: Dwarf name), The God-King, God-King Sigmar, The Founder

Ownership — Original fictional character owned by Games Workshop Group PLC (Nottingham, UK), created for Warhammer Fantasy Battle (first edition 1983). Developed collaboratively by Games Works

Powers
Peerless Martial Prowess and Superhuman Physique

Even before receiving Ghal Maraz, Sigmar is described as the strongest and most skilled warrior of the Unberogen, capable of slaying a Norseman champion in single combat as a youth, wrestling a cave bear, and leading charges that break greenskin lines. Post-apotheosis, strength is divine-scale.

Limit: Mortal Sigmar is explicitly not invulnerable — wounded multiple times (e.g., Battle of Astofen), ages, tires, requires rest and healing. His strength is exceptional human, not supernatural, until apotheosis.

Wielder of Ghal Maraz (Skull Splitter)

Wields the Dwarf-forged rune warhammer Ghal Maraz, crafted by Rune-smith Alaric the Mad and gifted by High King Kurgan Ironbeard. The hammer emits runic power, shatters armor and Chaos mutations, disrupts magic, and is especially lethal to greenskins and daemons. In lore it can only be wielded by one worthy; others find it impossibly heavy or are rejected. Later recovered by Magnus the Pious and then Karl Franz.

Limit: Hammer does not grant immortality or protect from aging; Sigmar must still close to melee; Chaos champions like Morkar can briefly withstand it. In Age of Sigmar, Ghal Maraz is lost and reclaimed, Sigmar cannot always reclaim it himself.

Leadership, Oath-binding and Empire-Founding Authority

Supernatural charisma and political ability to bind twelve tribes by oath into a single nation, institute the system of Counts Elector, commission the Runefangs, and establish laws, roads, and the office of Emperor. His word is treated as divine law after deification.

Limit: Authority depends on personal presence and Dwarf alliance; several tribes (e.g., Asoborns) resist initially; after his disappearance the Empire fractures into civil war, showing limits of institutionalization.

Divine Ascension and Godhood (Post-Mortal)

After abdicating in 50 IC and walking into the Worlds Edge Mountains, Sigmar ascends to godhood, becoming the patron god of the Empire. As god he grants miracles to priests (healing, smiting, protection from Chaos), empowers Warrior Priests, and answers prayers. In Age of Sigmar he is a full Realm-God who can create storms, forge souls, and wage inter-realm war.

Limit: As Imperial god, power is limited by faith and by the Winds of Magic; cannot manifest physically in the Old World except through avatars/comets; during the Time of Woes and later Storm of Chaos, prayers go unanswered. In Age of Sigmar, his power wanes when Azyr is sealed and he suffers repeated defeats by Chaos.

Creation of the Stormcast Eternals (Age of Sigmar)

As God-King of Azyr, Sigmar captures worthy mortal souls at moment of death and reforges them on the Anvil of Apotheosis into immortal Stormcast Eternals, lightning-infused superhuman warriors. He also commands celestial tempests and the Dracothian draconic alliance.

Limit: Reforging degrades soul with each death — Stormcast lose memory and humanity; process requires Sigmar's power and Grungni's smithcraft and is not infinite; Sigmar cannot reforge those claimed by Chaos or Nagash.

Resistance to Chaos Corruption

Sigmar shows unusual resistance to Chaos influence, able to gaze upon Chaos artifacts and champions without immediate mutation or madness, attributed to willpower and later divine protection. He rejects offers from Chaos Gods via Morkar.

Limit: Resistance is not immunity — prolonged exposure in the Chaos Wastes still affects him, and his Empire remains vulnerable to Chaos cults; he cannot cleanse already-corrupted lands alone.

Accounts I
The Twin-Tailed Comet and the Birth of SigmarGraham McNeill - Heldenhammer (2008), Ch. 1-2; Warhammer Armies: The Empire 7th ed. p.4

Mythic prehistory, dated -30 IC (Imperial Calendar) in later chronology; literary creation 1980s, novelized 2008

On the night King Bjorn Unberogen leads his tribe against Norsii raiders, his wife gives birth to a son under a twin-tailed comet — an omen the tribal priest interprets as divine favor from Ulric. The child is named Sigmar. The comet later becomes the central holy symbol of his cult. Bjorn raises Sigmar as a warrior-prince, teaching him axe and oath-law. The story establishes Sigmar's semi-divine birth omen and his upbringing in a brutal, honor-bound Iron Age culture where survival depends on tribal loyalty. Establishes Sigmar's legitimacy and divine portent; comet becomes Empire's holy sign; sets up conflict between Ulrican and later Sigmarite interpretation of the omen.

The Rescue of Kurgan Ironbeard and the Gift of Ghal MarazWarhammer Armies: The Empire 7th ed. p.6; McNeill - Heldenhammer Ch. 12-16; White Dwarf 122 (1990) Dwarf history

c. -15 IC (Sigmar aged ~15-16); codified in 1990s lore, expanded 2008

Young Sigmar, leading a warband to rescue captives from greenskins, discovers a besieged Dwarf caravan. He saves High King Kurgan Ironbeard from orcs, though Kurgan is badly wounded. In gratitude, Kurgan swears an eternal oath of friendship between Dwarfs and Men and, after Sigmar later saves Kurgan's hold from a greenskin siege, commissions Rune-smith Alaric the Mad to forge a rune-hammer. That hammer is Ghal Maraz, the Skull Splitter, presented to Sigmar at the Stone of Gazul. With it Sigmar's status transforms from tribal prince to legendary champion. Creates the eternal Dwarf-Man alliance that underpins Empire survival; provides Sigmar's signature weapon; oath is later invoked by Dwarfs to aid Magnus the Pious and Karl Franz. Forging of Ghal Maraz becomes most sacred relic in Imperial cult.

The Battle of Black Fire PassWarhammer Armies: The Empire 7th ed. pp. 7-8; McNeill - Empire (2009) Ch. 18-22; Warhammer Fantasy Battle 6th ed. rulebook timeline

c. -1 IC (year 0 in some chronologies); central founding battle, lore from 1986 onward

A massive greenskin horde pours through Black Fire Pass intending to overrun the human tribes. Sigmar, now King of the Unberogen after Bjorn's death, unites the twelve tribes — many of whom hate each other — by personal combat, diplomacy, and invocation of the Dwarf oath. At Black Fire Pass, the combined human-Dwarf army makes a stand. Sigmar, wielding Ghal Maraz, duels the Orc warlord and breaks the horde. The victory is so decisive that the tribal kings swear fealty to Sigmar on the battlefield and crown him Emperor, founding the Empire. The Runefangs are later forged as symbols of that oath. Founding of the Empire of Man, dated 0 IC; establishment of the Elector Count system; beginning of Imperial Calendar; elevation of Sigmar from king to Emperor. Celebrated annually as Sigmarzeit in Imperial cult.

Accounts II
The Duel with Morkar the Uniter and the Defense Against ChaosWarhammer Armies: Warriors of Chaos 7th ed. p.18; McNeill - God King (2011) Ch. 15-19; Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay - Tome of Corruption p.14

c. 1-20 IC, after founding; Morkar lore originates 1990s

Morkar, the first great Everchosen of Chaos Undivided, unites the northern tribes and marches south to destroy the nascent Empire, wielding dark power and bearing the favor of all four Chaos Gods. Sigmar meets him in single combat before the walls of Reikdorf. After a brutal duel where Morkar wounds Sigmar and mocks his mortality, Sigmar shatters Morkar's Chaos armor and crushes his skull with Ghal Maraz, ending the invasion. The victory cements Sigmar's reputation as Chaos-bane and proves the Empire can survive the northern darkness. First defeat of an Everchosen; establishes template for later Everchosen cycles (Asavar Kul, Archaon); Morkar's armor becomes Chaos relic; Sigmar's hatred of Chaos becomes doctrinal core of his cult.

The Abdication and the Walk into the Worlds Edge MountainsWarhammer Armies: The Empire 7th ed. p.10; McNeill - God King Ch. 23-24; Sigmar's Heirs p.15

c. 50 IC; lore present since 1980s, novelized 2011

After fifty years of rule, having built roads, codified laws, established the Cult of Ulric's coexistence, and seen his wife and friends age and die, the aging but still vigorous Sigmar abdicates the throne to his chosen successor. He gives his crown and Runefangs to the Electors, takes only Ghal Maraz, and walks east into the Worlds Edge Mountains, saying he goes to answer a debt or seek a final battle. He is never seen again in mortal form. Search parties find no body. Within a generation, miracles are reported at his tomb and the Cult of Sigmar declares he ascended to godhood and now watches over the Empire. Creates succession crisis and eventual civil war (Age of Wars of the Electors); enables deification and formation of Cult of Sigmar as state religion centuries later under Magnus the Pious; leaves Ghal Maraz lost until recovered by later heroes; provides mystery that fuels pilgrimages.

Sigmar's Return as God-King and the Forging of the Stormcast EternalsWarhammer: Age of Sigmar Core Book (2015) pp. 12-19; Battletome: Stormcast Eternals (2017) pp. 6-14; Warhammer: Age of Sigmar - The Realmgate Wars novel series (2015-)

Mythic Age of Myth to Age of Sigmar (in-universe millennia after Fantasy); publication 2015-present

After the destruction of the World-That-Was (Warhammer Fantasy world) during the End Times, Sigmar survived by clinging to the core of the world and was found by the celestial drake Dracothion, who brought him to the Mortal Realms — eight elemental planes. In the Age of Myth Sigmar explored the Realms with gods Gorkamorka, Nagash, Alarielle and others, building great civilizations. After Chaos under Archaon invaded and shattered that alliance, Sigmar retreated to Azyr and sealed its gates, abandoning the other Realms in shame. Millennia later, to reclaim them, he initiates the Reforging: with Grungni and the Six Smiths, he harnesses the storm to transform worthy dead heroes into Stormcast Eternals, his immortal thunder-warriors, and launches the Realmgate Wars to retake the Realms. Reframes Sigmar from tribal founder to multiversal god; launches entire Age of Sigmar game line; introduces concept of Reforging flaw and Sigmar's moral ambiguity; provides narrative engine for ongoing tabletop campaigns.

Sigmar and the Emperor of Mankind from Warhammer 40,000 are the same person. — They are separate characters in separate settings. The Emperor of Mankind is a 40k character created in 1987 with distinct history (born 8000 BC, created Primarchs). Sigmar is a Fantasy character. Games Workshop designers (Rick Priestley, Gav Thorpe) have explicitly denied they are the same, though early Rogue Trader lore hinted at a link as an in-joke. Fan theory persists due to both wielding hammers and being god-emperors.

Sigmar was always worshipped as a god and founded his own church. — In all canonical sources Sigmar explicitly forbade worship while alive and remained a follower of Ulric and tribal gods. The Cult of Sigmar was founded decades after his disappearance by Johann Helstrum and only became state religion in 1360 IC (and again after Magnus the Pious in 2304 IC). For centuries Sigmar was revered as a great ancestor, not a god.

Sigmar single-handedly created the Empire by conquering the tribes. — Sigmar united the tribes primarily through diplomacy, marriage alliances, oaths, and mutual defense against greenskins/Chaos, not conquest. Several tribes joined willingly after Black Fire Pass; others required negotiation. The Dwarf alliance was crucial — without Dwarf arms and Ghal Maraz, unification would have failed. Later Imperial propaganda emphasizes conquest.

Ghal Maraz is Sigmar's hammer and can only be lifted by Sigmar (like Thor's Mjolnir). — Ghal Maraz can be wielded by others deemed worthy — notably Magnus the Pious and Karl Franz both wield it in battle, though lore notes it is heavier and less responsive for them. It is not genetically locked; worthiness is judged by Dwarf runes and Sigmarite faith, not bloodline.
