Who It Is

At the Detroit Police Department's bullpen, Detective Gavin Reed doesn't hide his contempt — leather jacket, sneer, and a muttered 'fucking androids' every time Connor walks past. In a precinct built on procedure, Reed is friction incarnate: brilliant enough to make detective young, abrasive enough that almost no one wants him as a partner, and openly, aggressively anti-android in a city where androids are about to revolt.

Attested: abrasive, insubordinate, openly prejudiced against androids, uses slurs ('tin can', 'plastic prick', 'fucking android'). Quick to anger, sarcastic, dismissive of protocol when it benefits him, but competent as an investigator — Captain Fowler keeps him on despite complaints because of clearance rate. Shows contempt for Connor specifically as an android sent to assist, and professional jealousy. Powers
Detective Investigation

Trained DPD detective; competent in criminal investigation, evidence collection, and interrogation. Fowler cites his case record as reason for retention despite disciplinary issues.

Limit: No forensic android capabilities; relies on human procedure. Bias against androids impairs objectivity in deviant cases, leading him to dismiss Connor's android-assisted analysis.

Firearms Proficiency

Standard DPD sidearm proficiency. Seen drawing and threatening with pistol in 'Last Chance, Connor' confrontation.

Limit: Human accuracy and reaction time; can be disarmed and killed by Connor (android with superior reflexes) if player initiates combat.

Physical Intimidation and Assault

Uses physical intimidation — shoving, grabbing, punching — particularly against Connor, who is programmed not to retaliate unless player overrides.

Limit: Ineffective against androids who can overpower him; triggers disciplinary risk. Cannot sustain fight against RK800/RK900 tier androids without player-determined outcome.

Interrogation (Human Suspects)

Experienced in interrogating human suspects; favors aggressive pressure tactics.

Limit: Technique fails on deviant androids who do not respond to human intimidation the same way; Connor outperforms him in android interrogation.

Accounts I
Waiting for Hank... — Precinct IntroductionDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'Waiting for Hank...'

2038 (in-universe); 2018 (publication)

Connor, the RK800 prototype sent by CyberLife to assist DPD, arrives at Central Station. Gavin Reed is introduced at his desk, openly hostile, mocking Connor as a 'plastic detective' and complaining to Hank and Fowler that an android is taking human jobs. He establishes the precinct's anti-android sentiment and his personal antagonism, which persists as ambient harassment throughout Connor's investigation. Establishes Reed as Connor's primary human foil within DPD; sets relationship stat to hostile baseline; foreshadows later confrontations. No branching outcome — introduction is fixed.

The Interrogation — Observation Room ContemptDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'The Interrogation'

2038 / 2018

Connor interrogates a deviant android (Carlos Ortiz's android) suspected of murder. Gavin watches from the observation room with Hank and Chris, heckling Connor's methods, urging brute force, and expressing disgust at deviant behavior. If Connor fails or uses excessive pressure, Gavin's commentary becomes more scornful; if Connor succeeds via empathy, Gavin dismisses it as android trickery. Reinforces thematic contrast between Gavin's coercive policing and Connor's analytical/empathic options. No direct effect on Reed's fate, but contributes to Connor's software instability and precinct reputation.

Last Chance, Connor — Evidence Room ConfrontationDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'Last Chance, Connor'

2038 / 2018

With Hank off-duty and Connor under pressure to find Jericho's location, Gavin confronts Connor in the DPD evidence/archive room. He accuses Connor of being a defective machine, shoves him, and draws his weapon if Connor remains. The player can choose to endure, argue, or — if Connor has become deviant — fight back. In the violent branch, Connor can overpower and kill Gavin by shooting him or breaking his neck; in the submissive branch, Gavin humiliates Connor and leaves him shaken. Major branching point: Gavin can die here (permanent death flag), which removes him from 'Battle for Detroit.' If he lives, his hostility escalates. If Connor kills him, it raises Connor's deviance and affects Hank's and Fowler's later attitudes and public opinion stats.

Accounts II
Battle for Detroit — Precinct Siege / Final StandoffDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'Battle for Detroit' (Connor strand)

2038 / 2018

During the android revolution, DPD becomes a flashpoint. If Gavin survived earlier, he appears defending the precinct or hunting Connor. In the machine-Connor path (Connor remains loyal to CyberLife), he may fight alongside Gavin against deviants; in the deviant-Connor path, Gavin is an enemy combatant who can be killed by Connor, Markus's forces, or survive to be seen evacuating. His final lines remain defiant, never renouncing anti-android views. Determines Gavin's ultimate fate in the game's flowchart (survives, dies, or absent). No redemption arc is coded; survival does not alter ending slides except via public opinion and Connor's personal story.

Public Enemy and Partners — Ambient Precinct Harassment ArcDetroit: Become Human, Chapters 'Partners' and 'Public Enemy' (ambient)

2038 / 2018

Across early investigation chapters, Gavin appears in ambient bullpen scenes harassing Connor, filing complaints, and gossiping with Tina Chen and Chris Miller. He is the vocal embodiment of DPD's institutional bias, contrasting with Hank's gradual thaw. These vignettes have no single climax but cumulatively build the precinct's social ecosystem. World-building; reinforces theme of systemic prejudice. No mechanical consequence beyond flavor and relationship stats.

The Deviant's Dilemma — Unplayed / Cut Interrogation AggressionDetroit: Become Human - Behind the scenes and data-mined script fragments discussed in fan documentation

2016-2018 development

Early script drafts and motion-capture outtakes (discussed by Neil Newbon in interviews) included a more extended physical altercation where Gavin was to interrogate a deviant himself, showcasing his brutality. The sequence was cut/shortened to keep focus on Connor. The remnant is his observation-room heckling. This illustrates developer intent to make Gavin the human mirror to Connor's evolving empathy. No in-game consequence; informs interpretation of Gavin as intentionally written as foil, not nuanced anti-hero. Cut content is not canon but cited in actor interviews.

Gavin Reed is a romanceable character in Detroit: Become Human. — No romance path with Gavin exists in the released game. Connor cannot romance anyone. The popular Gavin/Connor and Gavin/RK900 ships are entirely fan-created (fanfiction, mods). A PC mod 'Reed900' adds dialogue but is not canon.

Gavin Reed is an android or RK900 himself. — Gavin is explicitly human — he has no LED, is not scanned as android by Connor, and is affected by human limitations. RK900 is a separate, unimplemented android model glimpsed only as a menu silhouette.

Gavin Reed appears throughout the game as a main character. — He appears in only 3-4 chapters of Connor's arc, with roughly 10-15 minutes of total screen time and many optional ambient lines. He is a supporting antagonist, not a protagonist.

Gavin Reed is based on a real Detroit police officer or folklore figure. — He is an original fictional creation by David Cage for a near-future sci-fi setting, with no folkloric or historical antecedent. The name is generic contemporary American.
