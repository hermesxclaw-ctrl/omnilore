Who It Is

Before the devil had a pitchfork, he had a whisper. Aka Manah is the 'Evil Mind' — the ancient Zoroastrian demon whose entire purpose is to corrupt your thoughts, plant wicked intentions, and turn you from the path of truth. He is the original voice of temptation in your head, predating the Christian devil by centuries. He is so potent that he once tried to recruit the prophet Zoroaster himself with the promise of world kingship — and was repelled by a single prayer. For over 3,000 years, this demon of bad ideas has haunted the human psyche, from the sacred fires of ancient Persia to the pages of the Persian national epic, where he reappears as a monstrous, king-kidnapping brute who can hurl heroes into the sea. He is the demon of every bad decision you've ever almost made.

Aka Manah's primary function is to corrupt human thought and will. He is the demon of evil purpose, base desires, and wicked intentions. He works to turn humans away from righteousness (asha) and towards lies (druj). In the Vendidad, he directly tempts the prophet Zoroaster, offering him worldly kingship to abandon his mission. In the Shahnameh, Akvan Div is a violent, destructive force who kidnaps kings and terrorizes the land.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Avestan: aka- (bad, evil) + manah- (mind, thought). Literally 'Evil Mind' or 'Evil Purpose'. In Middle Persian: Akem Manah; in New Persian: Akoman (Akvan).

Pronunciation — AH-kah MAH-nah (Avestan); AH-koh-mahn (Persian)

Aliases — Akem Manah, Akoman, Akvan, Aka Manah (Avestan), Akem Manah (Middle Persian), Akoman (New Persian)

Ownership — Zoroastrianism (ancient Iranian religion); also appears in Persian epic literature (Shahnameh) and later Islamic-era Persian folklore.

Powers
Corruption of Thought

Aka Manah's core power is to plant evil thoughts, doubts, and wicked intentions in the minds of humans, leading them away from truth and righteousness.

Limit: Can be repelled by the recitation of the Ahuna Vairya prayer and by steadfast faith in Ahura Mazda.

Temptation

Directly tempts holy figures, such as Zoroaster, offering worldly power and riches in exchange for abandoning their divine mission.

Limit: Fails against those with unwavering faith.

Physical Might (in Shahnameh)

As Akvan Div, possesses superhuman strength, able to seize kings, hurl heroes, and cause great destruction.

Limit: Is ultimately defeated by the cunning and strength of the hero Rostam.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Avesta, Aka Manah is an abstract, spiritual being, not described with a physical form. In later Middle Persian texts (Bundahishn), he is one of the arch-demons, often depicted as a dark, shadowy, or foul-smelling entity. In the Shahnameh, Akvan Div is a monstrous, powerful div (demon) with immense strength, capable of hurling the hero Rostam into the sea.

Symbols — Darkness, foul stench, the shadow of doubt, the whisper of temptation, the impulse towards greed and cruelty. In the Shahnameh, he is associated with the wilderness and the sea, where he attempts to drown Rostam.

Domains — The mind and will of mortals; the realm of evil thoughts; the temptation towards wickedness; in later tradition, a demon of discord and destruction.

Accounts I
The Temptation of ZoroasterVendidad (Fargard 19), Denkard (Book 7)

c. 3rd–10th century CE (textual record of older oral tradition)

Shortly after Zoroaster's birth and again during his early ministry, Aka Manah confronts the prophet. He tries to turn Zoroaster away from Ahura Mazda, offering him the kingship of the world and immense wealth. Zoroaster, strengthened by his faith, recites the Ahuna Vairya (the most sacred Zoroastrian prayer), which causes Aka Manah to flee in defeat. Zoroaster's victory over Aka Manah establishes his authority as the prophet of Ahura Mazda and demonstrates the power of faith over evil.

Accounts II
Rostam and Akvan Div (Akvan-e Div)Shahnameh (Book of Kings) by Ferdowsi

c. 1010 CE

The demon Akvan Div, a monstrous and powerful div, kidnaps King Kay Kavus of Iran. The hero Rostam is summoned to rescue the king. After a long search, Rostam finds Akvan sleeping. He attempts to kill the demon, but Akvan wakes, seizes Rostam, and hurls him into the sea. Rostam survives and, using his wits, feigns to be asleep on the shore. When Akvan approaches to inspect him, Rostam leaps up and kills the demon, rescuing the king. The demon is slain, the king is rescued, and Rostam's heroic reputation is further cemented.

Aka Manah is the same as Ahriman (Angra Mainyu). — While closely related, Aka Manah is a distinct demon in Zoroastrian theology, the chief lieutenant of Angra Mainyu, not the supreme evil spirit himself.

Aka Manah is only a character in the Shahnameh. — He is first and foremost a theological concept in the Avesta, the sacred scripture of Zoroastrianism, appearing millennia before the Shahnameh.

Aka Manah's power is purely physical. — In his original conception, Aka Manah's power is primarily mental and spiritual — he corrupts thought and will. His physical might is a later literary addition.
