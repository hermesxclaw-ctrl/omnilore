Form

In the Avesta, Aka Manah is an abstract, spiritual being, not described with a physical form. In later Middle Persian texts (Bundahishn), he is one of the arch-demons, often depicted as a dark, shadowy, or foul-smelling entity. In the Shahnameh, Akvan Div is a monstrous, powerful div (demon) with immense strength, capable of hurling the hero Rostam into the sea.

Powers

Corruption of Thought Aka Manah's core power is to plant evil thoughts, doubts, and wicked intentions in the minds of humans, leading them away from truth and righteousness. Avestan and Pahlavi texts Can be repelled by the recitation of the Ahuna Vairya prayer and by steadfast faith in Ahura Mazda. None significant.

Temptation Directly tempts holy figures, such as Zoroaster, offering worldly power and riches in exchange for abandoning their divine mission. Vendidad, Denkard Fails against those with unwavering faith. None.

Physical Might (in Shahnameh) As Akvan Div, possesses superhuman strength, able to seize kings, hurl heroes, and cause great destruction. Shahnameh (epic literature) Is ultimately defeated by the cunning and strength of the hero Rostam. This is a literary development, not present in the earlier abstract theological texts.

Behavior & Habitat

Aka Manah's primary function is to corrupt human thought and will. He is the demon of evil purpose, base desires, and wicked intentions. He works to turn humans away from righteousness (asha) and towards lies (druj). In the Vendidad, he directly tempts the prophet Zoroaster, offering him worldly kingship to abandon his mission. In the Shahnameh, Akvan Div is a violent, destructive force who kidnaps kings and terrorizes the land.

Accounts

Shortly after Zoroaster's birth and again during his early ministry, Aka Manah confronts the prophet. He tries to turn Zoroaster away from Ahura Mazda, offering him the kingship of the world and immense wealth. Zoroaster, strengthened by his faith, recites the Ahuna Vairya (the most sacred Zoroastrian prayer), which causes Aka Manah to flee in defeat.

The demon Akvan Div, a monstrous and powerful div, kidnaps King Kay Kavus of Iran. The hero Rostam is summoned to rescue the king. After a long search, Rostam finds Akvan sleeping. He attempts to kill the demon, but Akvan wakes, seizes Rostam, and hurls him into the sea. Rostam survives and, using his wits, feigns to be asleep on the shore. When Akvan approaches to inspect him, Rostam leaps up and kills the demon, rescuing the king.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Aka Manah as the ancient Iranian personification of the 'evil thought' that pops into your head before you do something bad. He's not a tempter with a forked tongue; he's the bad idea itself. In the Zoroastrian cosmic courtroom, he's the prosecutor for the side of lies (druj), and his only job is to make you choose the wrong path. Imagine the 'shoulder angel' from cartoons, but instead of a tiny angel and devil, it's the Good Mind (Vohu Manah) and the Evil Mind (Aka Manah) — and the Evil Mind is a whisper so seductive that it once offered a prophet the world to get him to quit his job.
