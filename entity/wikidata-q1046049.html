Who It Is

A man without a name, without a country, without a past — Captain Nemo is the embodiment of the sea's own mystery, a figure of immense intellect and burning vengeance who chooses to live beneath the waves rather than submit to the tyranny of the surface world. His story, spun from the imagination of Jules Verne, is a voyage into the depths of the ocean and the human soul.

Nemo is introspective, stoic, and deeply principled. He is compassionate towards the oppressed and his crew, but also capable of ruthless violence against his enemies. He is intellectually brilliant, with expertise in engineering, marine biology, music, and languages. He avoids dry land and surface society, preferring the solitude of the ocean. He is secretive and rarely reveals his emotions, but his grief for his lost family and his hatred of imperialism are evident.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Nemo is Latin for 'no one' or 'nobody', a direct translation of the Greek 'Outis' (Nobody), the pseudonym used by Odysseus in Homer's Odyssey to trick the Cyclops Polyphemus. This etymology is confirmed in the text when Nemo states 'I have no name!'.

Pronunciation — NAY-moh

Aliases — Prince Dakkar

Ownership — Primarily French literary origin, created by Jules Verne. The character's backstory involves Indian heritage and anti-imperialist sentiments, but the cultural ownership is conteste

Powers
Master Engineer and Inventor

Nemo designed and built the Nautilus, including its electric propulsion, navigation, and life support systems. He also invented a new language for his crew and various underwater tools.

Limit: His technology is bound by the knowledge of his time; no supernatural or futuristic abilities.

Expert Navigator and Sailor

He navigates the Nautilus through treacherous waters, including under the Antarctic ice and through the Suez tunnel, demonstrating exceptional skill.

Limit: He is dependent on the Nautilus's capabilities and the availability of sea routes.

Polyglot

Nemo is fluent in French, English, German, Latin, and several Indian languages (Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam) and Greek. He also created a unique language for his crew.

Limit: No known limits; his linguistic ability is exceptional but not supernatural.

Scientific Knowledge (Marine Biology, etc.)

He has an exhaustive knowledge of marine biology and is respected by Professor Aronnax. He also has knowledge of geology, physics, and other sciences.

Limit: His knowledge is contemporary to the 19th century.

Combat and Survival Skills

He personally fights sharks and giant squids, and leads his crew in emergencies, showing physical courage and skill.

Limit: Human limitations; he can be injured or killed.

Wealth and Resources

He gathers treasure from shipwrecks, making him immensely wealthy, which he uses to fund his expeditions and aid others.

Limit: His wealth is finite and dependent on finding wrecks.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In 'Twenty Thousand Leagues', Nemo is described by Aronnax as a tall, self-contained man with a straight nose, broad brow, and wide-set eyes, 'certainly the most wonderful physical specimen' he had ever met. His age is indeterminate, between 35 and 50. In 'The Mysterious Island', he is in his late sixties with a long white beard. He is often depicted in illustrations wearing a captain's uniform, sometimes with a cap.

Symbols — The Nautilus submarine is the primary symbol associated with Nemo, representing his technological mastery and isolation. His motto is 'Mobilis in mobili' (moving within a moving element), inscribed on the Nautilus. The sea itself is a symbol of freedom and independence for him.

Domains — His domain is the ocean, specifically the Nautilus. He avoids dry land except for uninhabited islands and Antarctica. He is at home in all seas, from the Arctic to the Antarctic, and navigates underwater passages like the Suez tunnel.

Accounts I
Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the SeasJules Verne, 1870

1866-1867 (contemporary to publication)

Professor Aronnax, his servant Conseil, and harpooner Ned Land are aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln, hunting a sea monster. They discover the Nautilus and are captured by Captain Nemo. They travel around the world underwater, witnessing wonders of the deep, fighting giant squids, and visiting Atlantis. Nemo's mysterious past and hatred of imperialism are hinted at. The novel ends with the Nautilus caught in the Maelstrom, and the trio escapes. The castaways escape, and Nemo's fate is left uncertain until the sequel.

Accounts II
The Mysterious IslandJules Verne, 1875

1865-1869 (Civil War era)

Five Union prisoners escape in a balloon and land on an uncharted island in the Pacific. They are aided by a mysterious benefactor who provides them with supplies and rescues them from dangers. At the end, they discover Captain Nemo, now old, in the Nautilus inside a grotto. He reveals his identity as Prince Dakkar and his backstory, then dies. The Nautilus is submerged, and the island is destroyed by a volcanic eruption. The castaways are rescued, and Nemo's legacy is revealed. The island's destruction is a dramatic finale.

Journey Through the ImpossibleJules Verne and Adolphe d'Ennery, 1882

1882 (play premiere)

A fantastical play where characters travel through impossible worlds, including a meeting with Captain Nemo and the Nautilus. It is a cameo appearance, not a full story. None significant to the canon.

Nemo is a villain in the original novels. — In Verne's works, Nemo is a complex anti-hero, not a clear-cut villain. He is sympathetic and principled, though his methods are violent.

Nemo's submarine is powered by a nuclear reactor. — The Nautilus is powered by electricity, generated from sodium-mercury batteries, not nuclear power. Nuclear power was not yet discovered.

Nemo is a pirate. — He is not a pirate; he does not attack merchant ships for plunder, but targets warships and gathers treasure from wrecks.

Nemo is from Atlantis. — He is not from Atlantis; he merely visits the ruins of Atlantis in the novel.
