She is the protector of the cosmos, the slayer of demons (particularly Mahishasura, Shumbha, and Nishumbha), and the bestower of boons. She is also associated with the Vindhya mountains, where she is known as Vindhyavasini. She presides over victory, courage, and the destruction of evil, as well as fertility, family, and protection. In a broader sense, she represents the creative and destructive power of the universe.

Form

In the Devi Mahatmya, Ambika is described as a radiant, beautiful goddess with a dark or luminous complexion, often depicted with multiple arms. When she emerges from the gods' combined energy, she is described as having a divine, moon-like face, and a form that is both terrifying and beautiful. In art, she is typically depicted as a warrior goddess, riding a lion or tiger, carrying weapons such as a trident, sword, discus, and bow, and often shown in the act of slaying the buffalo demon Mahishasura. In her more benign, motherly aspect, she is shown with a gentle smile, holding a lotus and a child, or with her hands in a boon-granting gesture (varada mudra). Her complexion is often described as golden or dark blue, depending on the regional tradition.

Powers

Cosmic Creation and Dissolution As a form of the Great Goddess (Devi), Ambika is capable of creating, preserving, and destroying the universe. She is the ultimate reality (Brahman) manifest as the feminine principle. Devi Mahatmya None stated. This power is absolute and inherent to her nature. None within the text, but this universal power is often downplayed in favor of her more localized warrior aspect.

Bestowal of Boons (Varada) Ambika is a goddess of grace who grants boons to her devotees. In the Devi Mahatmya, she promises to answer the prayers of those who worship her, especially during times of distress. Devi Mahatmya, Chapters 11-13 Boons are granted according to her will and the devotee's devotion; they are not automatic. None.

Invincibility in Battle When the gods are defeated by demons, they create Ambika from their combined energy. She is described as being invincible, a warrior who cannot be defeated by any asura (demon). She defeats Mahishasura, Shumbha, and Nishumbha, who had previously defeated the gods. Devi Mahatmya, Chapters 2, 5 Her invincibility is a function of her divine nature. She is not invincible against her own devotees' lack of faith. None.

Shape-shifting and Illusion (Maya) Ambika can create and manipulate illusion (maya). In her battle with Shumbha and Nishumbha, she creates an army of goddesses (the Sapta Matrikas) from her own being, and she and her forms can change their appearance at will. Devi Mahatmya, Chapter 8 Her illusions are ultimately for the purpose of cosmic order, not mere deception. None.

Granting of Moksha (Liberation) As the supreme goddess, Ambika can grant liberation from the cycle of birth and death (moksha) to her devotees. Devi Mahatmya, Chapter 12 This is a spiritual power, not a physical one. None.

Behavior & Habitat

Ambika embodies the dual nature of the divine feminine: she is the nurturing, benevolent mother who grants boons and protects her devotees, and the fierce, untamable warrior who annihilates evil. She is described as being both gentle and terrible, a paradox that is central to her character. In the Devi Mahatmya, she is shown as a strategic and ruthless fighter, but also as a compassionate mother who responds to the prayers of her devotees. She is not a passive deity; she is actively engaged in the cosmic order, restoring balance by destroying demons.

Accounts

The gods, defeated by the buffalo demon Mahishasura, go to the trinity (Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva) for help. Their collective anger and power coalesces into a brilliant light, from which the Goddess emerges. She is given weapons by the gods and a lion as her mount. She fights Mahishasura's army, and after a long and fierce battle, she slays him. This story is the foundation of the Durga Puja festival.

The demons Shumbha and Nishumbha usurp the gods' positions. Ambika, now residing in the Himalayas, is spotted by the demons' messenger, Sugriva, who is smitten by her beauty. The demons attempt to woo her, but she declares she will only marry the one who can defeat her in battle. This leads to a series of battles, where Ambika and her emanations (Kali, the Sapta Matrikas) decimate the demon armies. Shumbha and Nishumbha are ultimately slain by Ambika.

Ambika is the daughter of the King of Kashi. She and her sisters, Amba and Ambalika, are abducted by Bhishma for his half-brother Vichitravirya. She marries Vichitravirya, but he dies without issue. To continue the dynasty, her mother-in-law, Satyavati, asks the sage Vyasa to impregnate Ambika and Ambalika through the practice of niyoga. Ambika is terrified of Vyasa's appearance and closes her eyes. As a result, her son, Dhritarashtra, is born blind.

Scholarly Debates
Independence vs. Consort
Ambika is an independent, self-born goddess, not a consort of any male deity. ⇌ Ambika is a form of Parvati, the consort of Shiva.
Evidence for A: Devi Mahatmya, where she is created from the gods' combined energy and is not depicted as a wife.
Evidence for B: Shiva Purana and other Shaivite texts, where Parvati is explicitly called Ambika.
Identity with Durga
Ambika and Durga are the same goddess. ⇌ Ambika is a specific form of the Goddess, distinct from Durga.
Evidence for A: In the Devi Mahatmya, the goddess is called both Ambika and Durga, and the text says she is known by many names.

The Human Ambika
The Ambika of the Mahabharata is a separate, human figure. ⇌ The human Ambika is a mortal incarnation of the goddess.
Evidence for A: The Mahabharata does not identify her as divine.
Evidence for B: Some later devotional traditions have retroactively identified her as an avatar of the goddess.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Ambika as the ultimate 'boss' character in the video game of the cosmos. She's the final, unbeatable entity that appears when all the other 'player characters' (the gods) have been defeated. She has multiple 'forms' or 'skins' (Parvati, Durga, Kali) depending on the 'level' you're on, and she drops the ultimate loot: cosmic order and liberation. But unlike a video game boss, she's also the 'NPC' who gives you the main quest and the 'healer' who restores your health. She's the whole game in one character.
