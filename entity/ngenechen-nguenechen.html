Who They Are

Ngenechen is the supreme creator deity of the Mapuche people of southern Chile and Argentina. He is a unique, fourfold being composed of an old man (Fücha), an old woman (Kude), a young man (Weche), and a young woman (Üllcha). Ngenechen governs the universe from the sky realm (Wenu Mapu), sending the Ngillatún (the great Mapuche prayer ceremony) as a gift to humanity, and watching over the moral order of the world.

Cultural Significance

Ngenechen remains the central figure of Mapuche religion, which has survived over 500 years of Spanish colonization and Chilean state pressure. The Ngillatún ceremony — which can last for days and involves prayers, dances, animal sacrifices, and community feasting — is still performed by Mapuche communities in Chile and Argentina. The fourfold nature of Ngenechen has fascinated anthropologists and scholars of comparative religion, as it represents a sophisticated theological concept of a deity who contains all stages and genders of human existence within a single divine being.
