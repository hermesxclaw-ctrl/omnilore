Who It Is

Verethragna (literally 'Smiting of Resistance') is the yazata of victory — the hypostasis of triumph in battle and the embodiment of the warrior ideal in Zoroastrian Iran. In the Bahram Yasht (Yasht 14), he reveals himself to Zarathustra in ten separate incarnations: as the wind (Vata), the bull (with golden horns), the white horse, the rutting camel, the ferocious boar, the perfect fifteen-year-old youth, the falcon (the Vargan bird that strikes prey from above), the ram, the buck, and the armed warrior with a golden sword. Each form emphasizes a different quality of victory: speed, endurance, ferocity, beauty, or tactical precision. The most iconic of these is the boar (varāza) — a creature of such uncompromising fury that it strikes without fear, and the boar's head became a common royal and martial symbol throughout Achaemenid and Sasanian Iran. Verethragna was also syncretized with the Hellenic Heracles and the Mesopotamian Nergal in the Seleucid and Parthian periods. His name was adopted by several Sasanian kings (Bahram I–V).
