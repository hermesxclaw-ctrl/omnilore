Egil Skallagrímsson

The Warrior-Poet, Wolf-Biter, Rune-Master of Borg

Who It Is
Form

Egil Skallagrímsson is described in 'Egils saga' as a man of striking and formidable appearance. He was tall and broad-shouldered, with a large, well-proportioned frame that was both powerful and intimidating. His face was characterized by a broad forehead, thick eyebrows, and a prominent, somewhat hooked nose, which was said to be so large that it was compared to a bird's beak. He had a thick, dark beard and a stern, often scowling expression that could shift to a fierce, wolf-like grin when angered. His eyes were described as dark and piercing, capable of instilling fear in his enemies. In his later years, Egil became bald, which is reflected in his patronymic 'Skallagrímsson' (son of the bald-masked one), and he was known for his physical strength even in old age. The saga also notes that his skull was unusually thick, a detail that was later 'confirmed' when his remains were allegedly exhumed and measured, adding to his legendary physicality.

Powers

Berserker Fury Egil was a berserker, capable of entering a trance-like state of heightened strength and aggression in battle. This state, known as 'berserkgangr', made him nearly invincible, but it also led to reckless behavior and a loss of control. The saga describes him fighting with superhuman ferocity, often ignoring wounds and striking down multiple enemies. This ability was both a gift and a curse, as it could be triggered by stress or anger, and it sometimes left him exhausted afterward.

Runic Magic Egil was a master of runes, using them for both protective and harmful purposes. He carved runes on objects, such as a drinking horn, to detect poison, and he used rune spells to heal illness or curse enemies. In one famous episode, he carved runes on a bedpost to cure a sick woman, and in another, he used runes to break a curse placed by a rival. His knowledge of runes was considered a form of sorcery, and he was feared for his ability to manipulate fate through these symbols.

Skaldic Poetry Egil was one of the greatest skalds (poets) of his time, composing complex verses in Old Norse meter. His poetry was not only an art form but also a tool for political and personal expression. He used his poems to praise kings, lament his losses, and even to mock his enemies. His most famous work, 'Sonatorrek' (The Loss of Sons), is a deeply emotional elegy that showcases his poetic genius and his ability to articulate grief. His poetry was so powerful that it could influence the mood of a court, and he often used it to gain favor or avoid punishment.

Accounts

As a young man, Egil was challenged by a berserker named Ljot the Pale, who had been terrorizing a farm. Egil, though outnumbered, accepted the duel. He used his cunning to avoid Ljot's initial charge, then struck him with a single blow, killing him. This act established Egil's reputation as a formidable warrior and earned him the epithet 'Wolf-Biter'.

A young woman named Helga was ill, and her father sought help from a local healer who carved runes incorrectly, worsening her condition. Egil, visiting the farm, examined the runes and realized they were the wrong ones. He carved new runes, placed them under her pillow, and she recovered. This story highlights Egil's skill as a rune-master and his willingness to use his knowledge for good.

Egil's brother, Thorolf, was killed in battle while serving King Athelstan of England. Egil, grieving and enraged, blamed the king for not protecting Thorolf. He composed a poem in Thorolf's honor and then demanded compensation from the king. Athelstan, impressed by Egil's poetry and fearing his wrath, gave him a large sum of gold and a valuable ring. Egil accepted but never forgave the king, and he later used the gold to fund his own ventures.

Scholarly Debates
Historical Existence
Egil Skallagrímsson was a real historical figure, as evidenced by his mention in Landnámabók and the saga's detailed genealogy. ⇌ Egil is a fictional character, created by the saga's author to embody Norse ideals, with no independent historical evidence.
Authorship of Egil's Saga
Snorri Sturluson wrote the saga, as traditionally attributed, based on stylistic similarities with his other works. ⇌ The saga was written by an unknown author, possibly a contemporary of Snorri, due to differences in style and perspective.
Egil's Christianity
Egil converted to Christianity late in life, as the saga describes his baptism, reflecting the historical Christianization of Iceland. ⇌ Egil remained pagan at heart, as his reliance on runic magic and his burial mound suggest a continued adherence to old beliefs.
What We Don't Know

Further research is needed to determine the historical accuracy of Egil's life, as the saga is the primary source. The exact location of his burial mound at Borg has not been conclusively identified, and the skull allegedly exhumed in the 19th century has not been scientifically analyzed. The authorship of the saga remains unresolved, and more comparative studies with other sagas could shed light on its composition. Additionally, the extent of Egil's runic magic and its cultural context is not fully understood, and a deeper analysis of Old Norse magical practices would be beneficial.
