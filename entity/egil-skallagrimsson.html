Who It Is

Egil Skallagrímsson is the protagonist of perhaps the greatest of the Íslendingasögur (Sagas of Icelanders) — a 10th-century Icelandic warrior, poet, farmer, and rune-master who embodies the paradoxes of the Viking Age. Born with a huge, ugly head, Egil killed his first man at age seven (splitting his skull with an axe during a ball game), composed his first poem at age three, and spent his life oscillating between acts of incredible violence and verses of profound beauty. He was a berserker — his father Skallagrímr and grandfather Kveld-Úlfr (Evening-Wolf) were shapeshifters who became uncontrollably violent at dusk, and Egil inherited the 'hamrammr' (shape-strong) nature, his body contorting and swelling during battle fury. At the same time, Egil was the greatest skald (poet) of his age: his poem 'Höfuðlausn' (Head-Ransom) saved his life when he composed it in one night for his enemy, King Eiríkr Bloodaxe of York, who was so impressed he released Egil despite wanting him dead. His greatest poem, 'Sonatorrek' (The Loss of Sons), is a raw, desperate elegy for his drowned son Böðvar — one of the earliest and most powerful expressions of individual grief in European literature, in which Egil rages against the god Odin for taking his son. Egil was also a master of runes: he heals a sick girl by correcting a botched runic inscription, and he carves a curse-pole (níðstöng) against King Eiríkr and Queen Gunnhildr, driving them from Norway. Egil represents the fullest fusion of warrior, poet, and magician in saga literature — the berserker who writes the lyric, the killer who weeps in verse.
