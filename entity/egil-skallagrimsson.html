Who It Is

Did you know that Egil Skallagrímsson, a Viking warrior-poet, once used runes to cure a sick girl, but also composed a poem so powerful that it saved his own life from a king's wrath? His story is a rollercoaster of violence, poetry, and magic, making him one of the most unforgettable figures of the Viking Age.

Egil Skallagrímsson is portrayed as a complex and contradictory figure. He is a skilled poet, capable of composing intricate verses that display deep emotion and wit, yet he is also prone to violent outbursts and ruthless behavior. He is fiercely independent, often defying authority, including kings, and is driven by a strong sense of honor and family loyalty. Egil is vengeful, holding grudges for years, as seen in his feud with King Eirik Bloodaxe, but he is also capable of deep love and grief, as shown in his lament for his son. He is pragmatic and cunning, using trickery and runic magic

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Egil' is derived from Old Norse 'Egill', which is related to the word for 'edge' or 'point', possibly referencing a weapon or sharpness. The patronymic 'Skallagrímsson' means 'son of Skallagrímr', where 'Skallagrímr' itself is a compound of 'skalli' (bald head) and 'grímr' (mask or helmet)

Pronunciation — EH-gil SKAL-lah-GREEM-son (approximate Old Norse pronunciation: 'EH-gil' with a hard 'g', 'SKAL-lah-GREEM-son' with a rolled 'r')

Aliases — Egill Skallagrímsson, Egil Skallagrimsson, Egil the Warrior-Poet, Wolf-Biter, Rune-Master of Borg

Powers
Berserker Fury

Egil was a berserker, capable of entering a trance-like state of heightened strength and aggression in battle. This state, known as 'berserkgangr', made him nearly invincible, but it also led to reckless behavior and a loss of control. The saga describes him fighting with superhuman ferocity, often ignoring wounds and striking down multiple enemies. This ability was both a gift and a curse, as it could be triggered by stress or anger, and it sometimes left him exhausted afterward.

Runic Magic

Egil was a master of runes, using them for both protective and harmful purposes. He carved runes on objects, such as a drinking horn, to detect poison, and he used rune spells to heal illness or curse enemies. In one famous episode, he carved runes on a bedpost to cure a sick woman, and in another, he used runes to break a curse placed by a rival. His knowledge of runes was considered a form of sorcery, and he was feared for his ability to manipulate fate through these symbols.

Limit:

Skaldic Poetry

Egil was one of the greatest skalds (poets) of his time, composing complex verses in Old Norse meter. His poetry was not only an art form but also a tool for political and personal expression. He used his poems to praise kings, lament his losses, and even to mock his enemies. His most famous work, 'Sonatorrek' (The Loss of Sons), is a deeply emotional elegy that showcases his poetic genius and his ability to articulate grief. His poetry was so powerful that it could influence the mood of a court, and he often used it to gain favor or avoid punishment.

He was tall and broad-shouldered, with a large, well-proportioned frame that was both powerful and intimidating. His face was characterized by a broad forehead, thick eyebrows, and a prominent, somewhat hooked nose, which was said to be so large that it was compared to a bird's beak. Symbols — Egil is often associated with symbols of the warrior-poet: a sword, a shield, and a rune-carved staff. His iconic weapon is a sword named 'Dragvandil', which he inherited from his father, and he is also linked to the axe, a common Viking weapon. In iconography, he is depicted with a rune stone or a rune-inscribed object, emphasizing his role as a rune-master. The wolf is a recurring symbol, referencing his epithet 'Wolf-Biter'

Domains — Egil's primary domain is the farmstead of Borg in Iceland, which he established after his family fled from Norway. Borg is located in the Borgarfjörður region, and it serves as his base of operations and a symbol of his legacy. He also spends significant time in Norway, where he engages in raids and courtly politics, and in England, where he participates in battles and encounters with kings. Accounts I
The Slaying of the BerserkerEgils saga

c. 9th century

As a young man, Egil was challenged by a berserker named Ljot the Pale, who had been terrorizing a farm. Egil, though outnumbered, accepted the duel. He used his cunning to avoid Ljot's initial charge, then struck him with a single blow, killing him. This act established Egil's reputation as a formidable warrior and earned him the epithet 'Wolf-Biter'. Egil gained fame and respect, but also made enemies among Ljot's kin. This event foreshadowed his lifelong pattern of violence and vengeance.

Accounts II
The Runic Healing of HelgaEgils saga

c. 10th century

A young woman named Helga was ill, and her father sought help from a local healer who carved runes incorrectly, worsening her condition. Egil, visiting the farm, examined the runes and realized they were the wrong ones. He carved new runes, placed them under her pillow, and she recovered. This story highlights Egil's skill as a rune-master and his willingness to use his knowledge for good. Egil's reputation as a healer grew, and he was sought after for his magical abilities. This episode also demonstrates the importance of correct rune usage in Norse culture.

The Death of Thorolf and Egil's RevengeEgils saga

c. 10th century

Egil's brother, Thorolf, was killed in battle while serving King Athelstan of England. Egil, grieving and enraged, blamed the king for not protecting Thorolf. He composed a poem in Thorolf's honor and then demanded compensation from the king. Athelstan, impressed by Egil's poetry and fearing his wrath, gave him a large sum of gold and a valuable ring. Egil accepted but never forgave the king, and he later used the gold to fund his own ventures. Egil's relationship with Athelstan was strained, and he became more cynical about alliances with kings. This event also deepened his melancholy, as he lost his closest brother.
