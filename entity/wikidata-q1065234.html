Who It Is

In the tangled genealogies of Greek myth, few figures are as elusive as Pleisthenes—a name that appears in the shadows of the House of Atreus, sometimes as the father of Agamemnon and Menelaus, sometimes as a son of Atreus, and sometimes as a victim of his own family's curse. His story, pieced together from scholia and fragments, reveals a man whose very existence is a matter of scholarly debate, a figure who dies young and is remembered only through the lives of others.

In the tradition where he is the father of Agamemnon and Menelaus, he is said to have 'done nothing of note' and died young. In Hyginus's version of the plot for Euripides' play, he is an unwitting victim, killed by his own father. No personality traits are recorded.

Accounts I
Pleisthenes as Father of Agamemnon and MenelausHesiod, Aeschylus, Porphyry, Tzetzes, scholium on Euripides' Orestes

Archaic to Classical

In this tradition, Pleisthenes is the father of Agamemnon and Menelaus (and sometimes Anaxibia), but he dies young, and his sons are raised by their grandfather Atreus. This explains why Homer calls them Atreids. The mother is variously Aerope, Cleolla, or Eriphyle. In the version where Aerope is the mother, she is given to Pleisthenes by Nauplius after being exiled by her father Catreus. Agamemnon and Menelaus become the leaders of the Greek forces at Troy; their patrimony is ambiguous.

Pleisthenes as Son of PelopsScholia to Pindar's Olympian 1

Classical or later

A son (or bastard son) of Pelops is named Pleisthenes. Some scholars identify this figure with the father of Agamemnon and Menelaus, creating a genealogy where Pelops is the grandfather of the Atreides. If identified with the father of Agamemnon, it places him one generation earlier than the usual genealogy.

Accounts II
Pleisthenes as Victim of Atreus's RevengeHyginus, Fabulae 86 (possible plot of Euripides' 'Pleisthenes')

1st century CE (or later)

Thyestes, after being banished for adultery with Aerope, sends Atreus's son, Pleisthenes (whom he had raised as his own), to Atreus to be killed. Atreus, believing the boy is Thyestes' son, kills him unknowingly. Atreus unknowingly kills his own son, adding to the family's curse.

Pleisthenes as Son of Thyestes and AeropeInferred from other sources (e.g., Aeschylus's 'Agamemnon' mentions the banquet of Thyestes)

Archaic

Pleisthenes is one of the infant sons of Thyestes and Aerope whom Atreus kills and serves to Thyestes at a banquet. This act of cannibalism is part of the curse on the House of Atreus.

Pleisthenes as Son of Menelaus and HelenEuripides, Helen and Iphigenia in Tauris; possibly other sources

5th century BCE

A son of Menelaus and Helen, named Pleisthenes, travels with a son of Helen and Paris to Cyprus. The timing is usually placed during Helen's stay in Troy, but some scholars, like Jonathan Burgess, suggest it may have occurred after the war. This figure is obscure and may be a later invention to explain the presence of a son.

Pleisthenes is always the father of Agamemnon and Menelaus. — In the canonical Homeric epics, Atreus is the father. The tradition of Pleisthenes as father is a variant found in later sources.

Pleisthenes is a major character in Greek mythology. — He is a minor and obscure figure, often a name in genealogies, with no significant myths of his own.

The name 'Pleisthenes' is unique to one individual. — There are multiple figures with this name in the House of Tantalus, leading to confusion.
