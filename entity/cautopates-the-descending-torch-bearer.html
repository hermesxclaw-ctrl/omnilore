Who It Is

Cautopates is the second dadophoros, standing opposite Cautes with his torch pointed downward toward the ground. The lowered torch signifies descent — the setting sun, night, darkness, death, and winter. Together the pair Cautes-Cautopates embody the Zoroastrian-influenced dualism that pervades Mithraic cosmology: the cosmic polarity that Mithras, as the 'unconquered' mediator, holds in balance. In some interpretations, the three figures — Cautes (dawn), Cautopates (dusk), and Mithras (zenith) — encode Mithras as the sun at the climax of its daily course, the 'noonday sun' that rules from the middle.
