Cautopates (the Descending Torch-Bearer)

Who It Is
Form

Cautopates is often depicted as a bearded man holding a torch with the flame pointing downwards, typically in the company of Cautes and the god Mithras.

Powers

Torch-bearing Cautopates is said to possess the power of carrying a torch, which represents the descent of the soul into the underworld.

Accounts

In this myth, Cautopates is said to represent the descent of the soul into the underworld, while Cautes represents the ascent of the soul into the afterlife.

In this story, Cautes and Cautopates are said to have engaged in a contest to see who could carry their torches the farthest, with Cautopates ultimately emerging victorious.

Etymology
The name Cautopates is derived from the Greek word καυτός (kaútōs), meaning 'burning' or 'fiery'. ⇌ Some scholars argue that the name Cautopates may be derived from the Persian word کاو (kāv), meaning 'fire' or 'flame'.
