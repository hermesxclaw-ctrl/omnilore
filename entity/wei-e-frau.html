Who It Is

The Weiße Frau (White Lady) is a ghostly figure said to appear in the castles of several German and Central European noble families — most famously the Hohenzollerns (Prussian royal house) — as a harbinger of death. She manifests as a pale woman in flowing white robes, sometimes carrying keys, a candle, or a child. The most famous White Lady is identified as Kunigunde von Orlamünde, a 14th-century noblewoman who, according to legend, murdered her two children when she mistakenly believed they prevented her marriage to a Hohenzollern prince. Cursed to wander, she became the death-omen of the house. The White Lady was reportedly seen before the deaths of numerous Hohenzollern rulers: she appeared in the Berlin Palace before the deaths of several Prussian kings and was sighted at the Plassenburg before the death of Margrave Christian Ernst in 1712. Multiple noble houses across Germany, Bohemia, and Austria claimed their own Weiße Frau — the Habsburgs, the Rosenbergs, and others — making her a pan-aristocratic ghost archetype. Unlike the rural Dame Blanche, the Weiße Frau is specifically aristocratic: her appearances are documented in court chronicles, letters, and even official reports, lending her an unusual degree of historical attestation for a ghost. She represents the guilt of dynastic violence — murder, betrayal, and usurpation — haunting the very families who benefited from such crimes.
