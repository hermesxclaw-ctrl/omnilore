Who It Is

On a chilly November afternoon at Kyoto in 1986, a dark bay filly who had been dismissed as a quiet foal with bad hocks did what no Japanese filly had ever done — she surged past the downhill at the third corner, held off a desperate late charge by a neck, and made history as the first winner of Japan's Triple Tiara. Mejiro Ramonu was not the flashiest horse of her generation, but for six straight graded races she was unbeatable, and her name still marks the line between before and after for fillies' racing in Japan.

Contemporary human observers described a consistent temperament: quiet and unassuming as a foal, leading to low expectations; tractable in training at Hakodate and Miho Training Center; but prone to anxiety/nervousness on race days, especially at Tokyo Racecourse. Documented incidents: agitation after bumping at Keisei Hai Sansai Stakes (Nov 1985) leading to 4th; anxiety before Queen Cup (Feb 1986) leading to 4th; anxiety at Oka Sho but able to settle mid-pack. Powers
Late-closing acceleration / sustained finishing kick

Core racing weapon: ability to settle mid-pack or behind and unleash a prolonged sustained run from the third corner through the final stretch, often along the rail or outside. Demonstrated in Hochi Hai Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (recovered from near-last after being blocked to win by neck), Oka Sho (mid-pack to 1 3/4 length win), Yushun Himba (stumbled start, rail run to 2 1/2 length win), and QEII Cup (took lead at 800m downhill and held).

Limit: Required clear running room; vulnerable when blocked (Hochi Hai incident) or when pace/turf compromised closing traction (Rose Stakes turf comment by Kawachi). Less effective when forced to race on or near lead without cover.

Mile-to-middle-distance versatility (1400m-2400m)

Won at 1400m dirt (debut), 1600m (Oka Sho, TV Tokyo Sho), 1800m-2000m trials, and 2400m (Oaks, QEII Cup). Time 1:34.9 for 1600m at age 2 was historically fast in Japan at the time, matched only by Maruzensky and Colonel Lancer among 2yos then.

Limit: Never tested beyond 2500m; final start at Arima Kinen 2500m resulted in 9th behind Dyna Gulliver, suggesting stamina limit or condition decline late in arduous season. Summer heat intolerance noted August 1986.

Cardiorespiratory capacity (inferred from conformation)

Groom Kozo Kojima's observation of large abdomen/barrel as proxy for heart/lung volume, used to predict potential despite unimpressive overall appearance. Retrospectively linked to ability to sustain long drives.

Limit: Subjective conformation assessment, not measured VO2 or heart score; cannot be quantified from available records. Modern veterinary science would require echocardiography.

Mental sensitivity / race-day temperament

Highly sensitive to pre-race environment; anxiety could negate ability (two 4th-place finishes at Tokyo). When settled, able to perform. Handler familiarity (Kawachi in Kansai) seemed to help settling.

Limit: Temperament was a vulnerability, not a power; contributed to inconsistency at Tokyo before Oaks trial adaptation.

Durability and recovery (with limits)

Managed 12 starts in 14 months, including 6 consecutive graded wins (then-record for a filly). Recovered from juvenile hock issues, colic and periostitis after Keisei Hai (late 1985) to win next two starts within weeks.

Limit: Durability failed in autumn 1986: required bloodletting treatment for heat, showed lameness in training, entered QEII Cup at reported 70% condition per Okuhira, and faded in Arima Kinen. Career ended after Arima; no attempt at 4yo campaign.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Mejiro Ramonu was a bay mare (kayage) foaled 9 April 1983 at Mejiro Stud, Date, Hokkaido. Official height/weight not widely published in English sources; contemporary accounts emphasize not a standout yearling — small, quiet, with juvenile hock (tarsal) issues that resolved by age 2. Accounts I
Quiet foal with bad hocks at Mejiro Stud (1983-1985)Mejiro Stud history and JBIS breeding notes; Okuhira and Kojima recollections via Yushun

1983-04-09 to 1985-07

Foaled at Mejiro Stud in Date, Hokkaido, sired by French-import Mogami out of Mejiro Hiryu (six wins, half-sister to Kyoto Shimbun Hai winner Mejiro Eagle). As a foal she exhibited hock problems and a docile, inconspicuous demeanor, leading staff to have low expectations. No other trainer would take her and another filly, so she became the first Mejiro horse entrusted to newly licensed trainer Shinji Okuhira. Her hock issues resolved as she matured to age 2. In July 1985 she was named Mejiro Ramonu after Aiguille de l'Amone and sent to Hakodate Racecourse for early training, then to Miho Training Center that autumn. Groom Kozo Kojima alone flagged her large abdomen as a sign of elite cardiopulmonary potential. Established the underdog narrative that defined her later fame; placed her with Okuhira, whose career became linked to hers; set up physical basis for later stamina.

Two-year-old debut and the 1:34.9 mile (1985)JBIS/netkeiba race logs; Yushun December 1985 report

1985-10-13 to 1985-12-08

Debuted 13 Oct 1985 in a 1400m dirt maiden at Tokyo with Futoshi Kojima, winning handily. Entered Keisei Hai Sansai Stakes (G3) 3 Nov as favorite over colt Daishin Fubuki after undefeated Dyna Actress scratched; bumped after start, became agitated, finished 4th in a five-horse blanket finish. Suffered colic and periostitis post-race but recovered quickly. Won an allowance race by one length, then won the TV Tokyo Sho Sansai Himba Stakes (now Artemis Stakes, G3) in 1:34.9 for 1600m — at the time only the third 2yo in Japanese history to break 1:35 for a mile, alongside Maruzensky and Colonel Lancer. This established her as a top filly prospect despite the Keisei Hai disappointment. Validated Kojima's physical assessment; earned first graded win; set time record that highlighted miler speed; positioned her for classic contention the following spring.

Oka Sho — first classic and first Mejiro classic (April 6, 1986)JRA race video and official result; Yushun May 1986; netkeiba result

1986-02 to 1986-04-06

Started 3yo season in Queen Cup at Tokyo (Feb) as favorite but finished 4th after pre-race anxiety; jockey Masatsugu Kashiwazaki said he didn't understand the loss, while owner Miya Kitano said she anticipated it from the horse's demeanor. Shifted to Kansai for the Hochi Hai Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (Hanshin, Oaks trial) with new jockey Hiroshi Kawachi (a Kansai-based rider, chosen because Kansai jockeys would ride Kansai classics). Blocked at the third corner by a tiring horse and dropped to near-last entering the stretch, she unleashed a sustained outside run to beat Chuo Sally by a neck. At the Oka Sho (Japanese 1000 Guineas, Hanshin 1600m) she was 1.6 favorite, settled mid-pack despite anxiety, moved up to take the lead in the stretch and won by 1 3/4 lengths over Mayano Jo O. It was Mejiro Stud's first classic win and Kawachi's first Oka Sho win. First leg of Triple Tiara secured; proved she could win at Hanshin/Kyoto after Tokyo failures; cemented Kawachi as her classic partner; made her the filly to beat for the Oaks.

Accounts II
Yushun Himba (Japanese Oaks) — overcoming Tokyo (May 18, 1986)JRA official result; Yushun June 1986; netkeiba

1986-04 to 1986-05-18

After Oka Sho, connections worried about Tokyo, where she had lost twice. Entered the SanSpo Sho Yonsai Himba Tokubetsu (Tokyo 1800m, Oaks trial) specifically to test Tokyo and face Dyna Actress for the first time (Dyna Actress had skipped Oka Sho). Ramonu passed Dyna Actress in the final stretch to win, exorcising the Tokyo jinx. In the Yushun Himba (Tokyo 2400m) she stumbled at the start and raced from behind along the rail, gradually advancing and taking the lead in the final stretch to win by 2 1/2 lengths over Yu Miroku. She became the first filly since Titania (1976) to win both Oka Sho and Oaks, and the first to win two legs of the newly formalized Triple Tiara. Sent to Mejiro Farm to recuperate after the race. Second Tiara leg; proved stamina at 2400m and ability to win at Tokyo; made Triple Tiara clinch possible; elevated her earnings and reputation to national level.

Autumn trials, heat, lameness and the Queen Elizabeth II Cup — Triple Tiara completed (August-November 1986)JRA result; Yushun Dec 1986; Okuhira interview recollections

1986-08 to 1986-11-02

Planned autumn return via Queen Stakes at Hakodate, but suffered in summer heat, was sent back to Miho and given bloodletting treatment (a traditional veterinary measure for heat/condition), and showed lameness in training, delaying restart. Resumed not at Hakodate but directly in Kansai at the Rose Stakes (then 2000m, Kyoto/Hanshin) in October, where she led early but struggled on the turf going, dueling neck-and-neck with Pot Tesco Lady before prevailing; Kawachi blamed turf condition. Trainer Okuhira later said she was at '70%' on the day of the Queen Elizabeth II Cup (Kyoto 2400m, then the third Tiara leg; Shuka Sho did not yet exist). Despite being overwhelming favorite, she raced in the third pack, took the lead on the downhill at the third corner (~800m out), held the lead into the stretch, briefly slowed and was nearly caught by Super Shot, but held by a neck. She became the first filly in JRA history to win the Triple Tiara (Oka Sho-Yushun Himba-QEII Cup), surpassed 300 million yen in earnings (record for a filly at the time), and set a then-record six consecutive graded win

Arima Kinen and retirement — the final bow (December 1986 - February 1987)JRA Arima Kinen result 1986; JRA awards Jan 1987; Yushun Feb 1987

1986-11 to 1987-02-08

After the QEII Cup, some connections and fans wanted a Japan Cup start, but owner Miya Kitano had already decided Arima Kinen would be her final race before retirement. She entered the Arima Kinen (Nakayama 2500m) as third-highest vote-getter behind Miho Shinzan and Sakura Yutaka O, but finished 9th behind winner Dyna Gulliver, clearly below her spring/autumn peak and possibly feeling the long season and 2500m distance. She was retired immediately after. In January 1987 she was voted Saiyushu Yonsai Himba (Best 4yo Filly, now Best 3yo Filly). Despite expectations she might become the first filly Horse of the Year since Tomei (1971), the title went to Dyna Gulliver (Tokyo Yushun winner who beat her at Arima). Columnist Keijiro Okawa claimed he asked JRA to consider dual Horse of the Year or a Special Award for Ramonu, but JRA declined. Her retirement ceremony was held 8 Feb 1987 at Tokyo Racecourse where she paraded in her QEII Cup bib. Later that year she was inducted into the JRA Hall of Fame alongside Symboli Rudolf. The Triple Tiara has existed since the early 20th century like the colts' Triple Crown — The JRA Triple Tiara (Oka Sho, Yushun Himba, Queen Elizabeth II Cup) was only formalized in its current JRA Grade I form in the 1970s-1980s; the Shuka Sho replaced QEII Cup as third leg in 1996. Mejiro Ramonu in 1986 was the first to win the JRA Triple Tiara as defined then.

Mejiro Ramonu was undefeated or dominant at Tokyo — She lost twice at Tokyo before her Oaks win: 4th in Keisei Hai Sansai Stakes and 4th in Queen Cup, both attributed to anxiety/bumping. She only conquered Tokyo in the SanSpo Sho trial and Oaks after specific preparation.

Her foals were also champions — None of her 11 direct foals won a major title; her produce record is considered disappointing. Later female-line success came from distant descendants (Field Rouge, Glory Vase) generations later.

The Uma Musume character's personality reflects the real horse — The game/anime character is entirely fictional — elegant girl with invented dialogue and friendships. The real horse was a sensitive thoroughbred whose relationships were with handlers and on-track rivals.
