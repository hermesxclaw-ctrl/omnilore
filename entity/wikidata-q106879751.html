The Approach

In the dust of the Trojan plain, an Achaean warrior whose name is barely a whisper in the ancient texts meets his end at the hands of an Amazon queen. Eilissus, a shadow among the heroes, is remembered only because his death was dealt by the legendary Penthesilea.

Content advisory. Depictions of violence and death in mythological context.

Etymology

The Greek name Εἰλισσόν (Eilissos) may be related to the verb εἰλίσσω (eilissō), meaning 'to roll, wind, or turn', possibly suggesting a connection to a river or a winding motion. However, the etymology is speculative and not attested in ancient sources.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Greek mythology, specifically the epic cycle of the Trojan War as preserved in Quintus Smyrnaeus's 'Posthomerica'.

Disputed Identifications

Eilissus is sometimes confused with the river Ilissus (or Ilisos) in Athens, but they are distinct. The name appears only in Quintus Smyrnaeus and is not attested in other ancient authors.

Stories & Myths

Death at the Hands of Penthesilea

Source Quintus Smyrnaeus, The Fall of Troy, Book 1.Culture Ancient GreekPeriod Late Antiquity (4th century AD) for the text; the story is set during the Trojan War (traditional date 12th or 13th century BC).

Summary

During the final battles of the Trojan War, the Amazon queen Penthesilea arrives to aid the Trojans. She fights fiercely and kills several Achaean warriors, including Eilissus. His death is mentioned in passing as part of a list of casualties.

Eilissus's death contributes to the Achaean losses but is not a turning point. Penthesilea is later killed by Achilles.
