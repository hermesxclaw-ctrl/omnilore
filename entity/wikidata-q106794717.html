Etymology

The name 'Miranda' is derived from the Latin 'mirandus', meaning 'worthy of admiration' or 'wonderful'. It was coined by Shakespeare for the character in 'The Tempest', from the Latin gerundive of 'mirari' (to wonder at).

Stories & Myths

Miranda in The Tempest

Source William Shakespeare's play 'The Tempest'Culture English RenaissancePeriod 1610-1611

Summary

Miranda is the daughter of Prospero, the rightful Duke of Milan, who was exiled to a remote island. She has grown up on the island with only her father and the spirit Ariel for company. When a shipwreck brings Ferdinand, the Prince of Naples, to the island, Miranda falls in love with him. Her innocence and compassion are central to the play's themes of forgiveness and reconciliation.
