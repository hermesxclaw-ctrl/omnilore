Who It Is

In the moonlit maize fields of the Yucatán, a farmer might hear a sharp whistle cutting through the night—not a bird, not a thief, but a tiny guardian no taller than a child's knee. This is the alux, a spirit of the land that can be a blessing or a curse, depending on how it is treated.

Mischievous, capable of helpfulness and harm. They play tricks, ask for offerings, and can protect or cause illness. They are associated with natural features and can be summoned or sealed.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'alux' is Mayan, with the plural 'aluxo'ob'. The Nahuatl terms 'Chanekeh' or 'Chaneque' are also used. The exact etymology within Mayan languages is not provided in the source.

Pronunciation — Mayan: [aˈluʃ], plural: [aluʃoˀːb]

Aliases — Aluxo'ob (plural), Alux Kʼat, Chanekeh (Nahuatl), Chaneque (Nahuatl), Duende (sometimes used interchangeably)

Ownership — Yucatán Peninsula, Belize, and Guatemala; associated with Maya peoples.

Powers
Invisibility

Aluxo'ob are generally invisible but can become visible to communicate with or frighten humans.

Limit: Not specified

Shape-shifting

They can assume physical form, but the extent of shape-shifting is not detailed.

Limit: Not specified

Agricultural assistance

Help corn grow, summon rain, and patrol fields at night, whistling to scare off predators or thieves.

Limit: This assistance lasts for seven years after which the alux must be sealed in its house.

Causing mischief

Play pranks such as putting out fires, throwing pots and pans, and wreaking havoc if refused offerings.

Limit: Mischief is often a response to disrespect or lack of offerings.

Causing illness

Can spread illness if offerings are refused.

Limit: Not specified

Bringing good luck

Can protect a person from thieves or bring good luck if treated with respect.

Limit: Depends on respectful treatment.

Summoning

Saying their name aloud can summon a disgruntled alux from its home.

Limit: It is considered not good to name them aloud.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Small, about knee-high, resembling miniature traditionally dressed Maya people. Generally invisible but can assume physical form.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography are mentioned in the source.

Domains — Forests, caves, stones, fields, and maize fields (milpas). They can be enticed to move through offerings. They may inhabit shrines (kahtal alux) considered 'houses of the alux'.

Accounts I
Creation of the AluxMaya tradition (as per Wikipedia)

Ancient (pre-Columbian)

The aluxo'ob were created with mud, leaves, and divine breath by the ancient Mayan gods. The aluxo'ob are spirit beings associated with nature.

Accounts II
The Farmer's HelperMaya tradition (as per Wikipedia)

Contemporary

A farmer builds a little house on his property, often in a maize field. For seven years, the alux helps the corn grow, summons rain, and patrols fields. After seven years, the farmer must seal the alux inside; otherwise, it runs wild. If sealed, the alux is contained; if not, it becomes mischievous.

Offerings to the AluxMaya tradition (as per Wikipedia)

Contemporary

Aluxo'ob occasionally stop and ask farmers or travelers for offerings. If refused, they cause havoc and illness. If given, they provide protection and good luck. Respect leads to help; disrespect leads to harm.
