The Approach

In the moonlit maize fields of the Yucatán, a farmer might hear a sharp whistle cutting through the night—not a bird, not a thief, but a tiny guardian no taller than a child's knee. This is the alux, a spirit of the land that can be a blessing or a curse, depending on how it is treated.

Identity

Alux

Known As

Etymology

The term 'alux' is Mayan, with the plural 'aluxo'ob'. The Nahuatl terms 'Chanekeh' or 'Chaneque' are also used. The exact etymology within Mayan languages is not provided in the source.

Cultural Ownership

Yucatán Peninsula, Belize, and Guatemala; associated with Maya peoples.

Disputed Identifications

Some suspect the belief developed through contact with Spanish or British pirates in the 16th century, but Maya claim pre-Columbian origins. There is no direct evidence for pre-Columbian existence, though similar concepts appear in pre-Columbian art.

Description

Appearance

Small, about knee-high, resembling miniature traditionally dressed Maya people. Generally invisible but can assume physical form.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are mentioned in the source.

Behaviour & Personality

Mischievous, capable of helpfulness and harm. They play tricks, ask for offerings, and can protect or cause illness. They are associated with natural features and can be summoned or sealed.

Domains & Habitats

Forests, caves, stones, fields, and maize fields (milpas). They can be enticed to move through offerings. They may inhabit shrines (kahtal alux) considered 'houses of the alux'.

Version Differences

The source notes that they are similar to leprechauns or Brownies. Some contemporary Maya consider shrines to be alux houses. There is no detailed version differentiation in the provided text.

The Farmer's Helper

Source Maya tradition (as per Wikipedia)Culture MayaPeriod Contemporary

Summary

A farmer builds a little house on his property, often in a maize field. For seven years, the alux helps the corn grow, summons rain, and patrols fields. After seven years, the farmer must seal the alux inside; otherwise, it runs wild.
