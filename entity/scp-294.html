Who It Is

SCP-294 is a standard coffee vending machine, identical in appearance to thousands of such machines found in offices and break rooms worldwide. The only distinguishing feature is an entry keypad where users can type in the name of any beverage — or, as the Foundation discovered, any liquid substance at all. The machine dispenses exactly what is requested, no matter how impossible. Water dispenses water. Coffee dispenses excellent coffee. But 'liquid gold' dispenses molten gold. 'Antimatter' dispenses — briefly — a cup of antimatter (the resulting containment failure destroyed the testing room). 'The perfect drink' dispensed something that the testing researcher described as 'transcendent' before being amnesticized for safety. Most disturbingly, 'a cup of Joe' dispensed a cup of blended human remains that DNA testing matched to a Foundation researcher named Joseph ██████, who was found missing the following day. The machine's capabilities extend to abstract concepts: 'music' dispensed a cup of something that vibrated with a perfect C-major chord; 'knowledge' dispensed a liquid that, when consumed, implanted complete fluency in an unknown language. SCP-294 appears to draw its contents from somewhere — possibly a parallel dimension, possibly from wherever the requested substance exists in the universe. The mechanism is unknown; the machine's internal components appear entirely mundane upon disassembly. The Foundation has restricted access to SCP-294 following several incidents, including the Joe incident and an attempt by a researcher to request 'the cure for cancer' (the machine dispensed a cup of clear liquid that the researcher refused to consume, citing ethical concerns; the liquid evaporated before testing could be performed). SCP-294's experiment log is one of the longest and most entertaining on the wiki, documenting hundreds of increasingly creative requests and their often surprising results. The machine represents the classic SCP formula: take something utterly mundane and make it terrifying by making it work too well.
