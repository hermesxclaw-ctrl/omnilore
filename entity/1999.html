I found the tape. It was in a box in my parents' attic. I watched it for the first time in fifteen years. At the end, Mr. Bear looked directly at the camera and said my name.

Who It Is

1999 is a creepypasta written in the style of a personal blog, recounting the author's childhood memories of a mysterious local access television channel in Caledon, Ontario, called 'Caledon Local 21.' The channel primarily broadcasted a children's show called 'Mr. Bear's Cellar,' hosted by a man in a bear costume. The show was low-budget and eerie, featuring Mr. Bear in a dimly lit basement, talking directly to children, sometimes painting, sometimes 'playing games.' The author recalls that certain episodes grew increasingly disturbing. In one segment, Mr. Bear drew a picture of a crying child and said, 'This is what happens when you don't watch.' On another occasion, the screen cut to a live feed of an empty playground at night. The most infamous detail involves a contest where children were invited to write to Mr. Bear; the author did, and received a Polaroid photograph in the mail — a picture of themselves, sleeping in their own bed, taken from inside their room. The story concludes with the revelation that the police became involved, and the man behind Mr. Bear was never found. The format — a personal recollection of disturbing childhood television — directly presaged the analog horror movement. The idea of a local broadcast so wrong and so personal that it couldn't be a coincidence creates a particularly chilling kind of horror: not cosmic, not supernatural, but human and predatory. 1999 has been frequently cited as an influence on series like Local 58 and Gemini Home Entertainment, and it remains one of the creepypasta canon's most grounded and unsettling entries.
