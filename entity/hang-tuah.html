Etymology — Hang Tuah's name is derived from the Malay words 'tuha' or 'toh', which refer to a type of warrior or a silat master.

Pronunciation — HAHN too-ah

Aliases — The Undefeated Warrior, Admiral of Malacca

Ownership — Hang Tuah is a legendary figure in Malay folklore, specifically in the Malay states of Malaysia and Indonesia.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Hang Tuah is often depicted as a tall, muscular man with a strong and confident demeanor. He is typically shown wearing traditional Malay clothing and carrying a sword or a keris.

Symbols — Hang Tuah is associated with the silat martial art and the keris, a traditional Malay dagger. He is also often depicted with a sword or a shield.

Domains — Hang Tuah's stories take place in the Malay world, specifically in the Malay states of Malaysia and Indonesia.

Accounts I
The Story of Hang TuahHikayat Hang Tuah

15th century

Hang Tuah is a legendary warrior and admiral who lived in Malacca during the reign of Sultan Mansur Shah. He is known for his bravery, loyalty, and wisdom, and is often depicted as a just and fair leader. Hang Tuah's bravery and military conquests help to establish Malacca as a major power in the Malay world.

The Legend of Hang TuahMalay folklore

15th century

Hang Tuah is a legendary figure in Malay folklore, known for his bravery, loyalty, and wisdom. He is often depicted as a just and fair leader who is loved by his people. Hang Tuah's legend helps to inspire and motivate the people of Malacca to work together and achieve great things.
