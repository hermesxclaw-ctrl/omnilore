The Approach

On a September morning in 1274, over 900 ships slipped out of Masan's harbor, carrying an army of Mongols and Koreans intent on conquering Japan. The fleet never returned victorious. Seven centuries later, the same port city became the spark that ignited a nationwide uprising against dictatorship. Masan is not just a place on a map; it is a stage where the tides of history have repeatedly turned.

Content advisory. Contains descriptions of political violence, police brutality, and death.

The name 'Masan' (마산) is of Korean origin, but its exact etymological roots are not definitively documented in the provided sources. The hanja for Masan is 馬山, meaning 'horse mountain', but this is not confirmed in the source material.

Description

Appearance

Masan is a coastal city with a natural harbor, surrounded by mountains and islands. The urban area is divided into two districts (Masanhappo-gu and Masanhoewon-gu) and includes a central business district, residential neighborhoods, industrial zones, and a free-trade zone. Notable geographic features include Dotseom Island, Jeodo Island, and the Masan Port.

Symbols & Iconography

No official symbols are mentioned in the source material. The city is associated with the sea, its port, and the Mongol Well (Mongojeong), a historical landmark.

Behaviour & Personality

Masan has historically been a politically active and left-leaning city, with a strong tradition of democratic protest. It is also known for its fishing industry and culinary specialty, agujjim.

Domains & Habitats

Masan is a port city and administrative region within Changwon, South Korea. It includes urban districts, rural myeons, and islands.

The Masan Uprising of 1960

Source Wikipedia article on MasanCulture South KoreanPeriod 1960

Summary

On March 15, 1960, a protest against electoral corruption in Masan led to violent clashes with police, resulting in seven student deaths and fifty injuries. The discovery of student Kim Ju-yul's body with grenade fragments in his skull further inflamed public anger, leading to mass protests of up to 40,000 residents. The events in Masan helped spark the April 19 Movement, which led to the resignation of President Syngman Rhee.

Source Wikipedia article on MasanCulture South KoreanPeriod 1979

Summary

In October 1979, protests broke out in Masan and Busan against the regime of President Park Chung Hee, following a brutal police crackdown on a sit-in strike of female textile workers. Workers in Masan's Free-Export Zone created four labour unions.
