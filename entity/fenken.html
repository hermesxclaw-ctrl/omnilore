Who It Is

Der Fenken is a restless night spirit from the folklore of Vorarlberg, Austria's westernmost state. Described as a tall, shadowy figure wrapped in chains and tattered rags, the Fenken stalks the dark streets of villages and the lonely passes of the Bregenzerwald at night. His most distinctive feature is the sound he makes: the loud, rhythmic clanking and rattling of heavy iron chains dragging behind him, mixed with a low, mournful groaning. The Fenken's appearance is always preceded by the sound of chains — giving those who hear it a brief moment to flee. Despite his terrifying description, the Fenken is not overtly malevolent in the traditional sense; he is a 'doom warning' spirit. His presence portends some tragedy — a death, a devastating fire, or an avalanche — and he is said to be the ghost of a sinner (often a miser or a corrupt judge) condemned to walk the earth warning of disaster until Judgment Day. Encountering the Fenken is not necessarily fatal, but it is considered a dire omen. Hiding in a church or crossing oneself three times while saying a specific blessing is said to dispel him. In some traditions, the Fenken is associated with the Wild Hunt (Wildes Heer), a spectral procession of the dead that sweeps across the winter sky. The Fenken shares similarities with the Dutch Kludde (which also drags chains), the German Nachtjäger, and the Wild Huntsman figure common across Germanic folklore.
