Who It Is

A friend sends you a file called smile.jpg. You open it. It's a photograph of a Siberian Husky, but something is wrong. The dog is smiling — not a dog smile, a human smile, stretched too wide, showing too many teeth. The background is black. The dog's eyes have no pupils. And then the screen flickers. The image doesn't go away when you close the file. It's burned into your desktop. Into your phone. Into the back of your eyelids. You start having the dreams: a dark room, a red glow, a voice that whispers 'SPREAD THE WORD.' The dog is telling you to show the image to someone else. If you do, it lets you live. If you don't… you've seen the smile. You know what happens.

Smile Dog (also known as Smile.jpg) is a creepypasta that emerged in the late 2000s about a cursed image file that destroys the mind of anyone who sees it. The image itself is described as a photograph of a Siberian Husky in a dark room, smiling an unnervingly human smile full of gleaming white teeth. A disembodied human hand is visible in the corner of the frame, as if beckoning. The image cannot be deleted once viewed — it persists on hard drives, burns itself into phones, and eventually appears in the viewer's dreams. The dreams always feature the same elements: darkness, a red glow, and the dog, which speaks in a distorted voice demanding 'SPREAD THE WORD.' The viewer must show the image to someone else. If they do, they are spared — for now. If they refuse or delay too long, the dreams intensify. The dog appears in waking life. Seizures, madness, and death follow.

The Smile Dog mythos is built on the concept of the memetic hazard: an idea or image that harms you simply by being perceived. This is a deeply modern fear — in an age of viral content, where one click can expose you to things you can't unsee, Smile Dog literalizes the horror. The smiling dog image (wholesome, familiar) corrupted into something deeply wrong (the human smile, the wrong teeth) is uncanny in the most technical sense: the familiar made strange. The demand to 'spread the word' transforms victims into vectors, creating a chain letter from Hell. Smile Dog is not just a monster — it is an infection, and every pair of eyes that sees it becomes another carrier.

“A friend sends you a file called smile.”
Emergence

The original Smile Dog creepypasta was posted to 4chan's /x/ board around 2008-2009. The story was presented as a first-person account: 'I saw an image that ruined my life' — the narrator describes receiving smile.jpg, the dreams, the spreading compulsion, and the eventual fate of those who refuse to share the image. The actual 'Smile Dog' photo that accompanied the post was a photograph of a Siberian Husky, modified to give it an unnaturally wide, human-like grin with gleaming white teeth, set against a dark background with a human hand in the frame. The photo's origin is disputed — it may have been a stock photo or a candid shot manipulated in Photoshop. The creepypasta spread through the early internet, inspiring fan art, audio adaptations, and video readings.

Form

The Smile Dog image is a photograph of a Siberian Husky (or similar breed) in a dark room, lit by an unknown red or orange light source. The dog's head is tilted slightly, and its mouth is stretched into a grotesque, human-like smile — too wide, too deliberate, showing a full set of gleaming white teeth that no dog should have. The eyes are blank — no pupils, no irises, just empty white or black voids. In the bottom corner of the frame, a human hand is visible, curled in a beckoning gesture. The image quality is grainy, dark, and unsettling. In dreams, Smile Dog appears in full form: a large black canine shape in a red-tinted void, its human smile the only clearly visible feature.

Memetic Contagion (Psychological Infection) The primary and only attested ability. Viewing the 'smile.jpg' image causes a memetic infection: the image becomes 'stuck' in the viewer's mind, causing recurring intrusive thoughts, nightmares, and paranoia. The infection is psychological, not physical, but is described as deeply distressing and potentially debilitating. Original creepypasta story (c. 2009-2010) The infection requires direct visual contact with the image. The original story does not specify if it can be transmitted through descriptions, secondary images (e.g., a screenshot of a webpage containing the image), or dreams. The infection's duration and long-term effects are not specified; the story ends with the narrator still afflicted. Some later versions claim the infection can be spread through description alone, or that it causes physical harm (e.g., sleep deprivation leading to death). These are not attested in the original story.

Intrusive Presence (Psychological Warfare) The infected individual experiences a persistent sense of being watched, along with auditory hallucinations (e.g., a dog's growl, footsteps) and visual hallucinations (e.g., glimpsing the dog in peripheral vision). The entity does not physically manifest. Original creepypasta story These effects are entirely psychological. The original story does not suggest the entity can interact with the physical world. Later versions sometimes depict the entity as capable of physical harm (e.g., attacking in dreams, causing objects to move). This is not supported by the original text.

Dream Incursion The infected individual experiences recurring nightmares featuring the Smile Dog. These dreams are described as vivid and terrifying, but do not result in physical harm. Original creepypasta story The dreams are a symptom of the infection, not an independent ability of the entity. The entity does not 'enter' dreams; it is a recurring theme within them. Some versions claim the entity can kill people in their dreams (a 'Freddy Krueger' style ability). This is not attested.

Behavior & Habitat

Smile Dog is a parasitic meme. It uses human hosts to propagate itself, demanding that viewers show the image to others. This creates a chain of transmission that spreads exponentially. Those who comply are spared the worst effects — though they continue to have nightmares — while those who refuse are tormented, driven mad, and eventually killed. Smile Dog does not have complex goals or motivations beyond propagation. It is a virus of information, and like any virus, its only drive is to spread. The psychological torment is the cost of resistance.

Weaknesses & Wards

Smile Dog's primary weakness is that it depends on transmission. A viewer who destroys all digital devices, isolates themselves, and refuses to spread the image might theoretically starve the memetic entity — but the story implies this is nearly impossible, as the image persists supernaturally. The compulsion to spread can be resisted through sheer willpower, though at terrible cost. In some versions, destroying the original image file or the server it resides on might break the curse. The image may have a limited 'range' — if no one in a given network has seen it, it cannot spread. In some fan interpretations, the chain can be broken by someone who accepts the torment and refuses to pass it on, sacrificing themselves to stop the cycle.

Accounts

A first-person narrator recounts receiving an email from a friend named 'Kyle' containing a single attachment: 'smile.jpg'. The narrator opens the file and sees a Siberian Husky with an unnaturally wide, human-like smile. Initially dismissing it as a bad photoshop, the narrator soon finds the image 'stuck' in their mind. They experience nightmares, paranoia, and the sensation of being watched. The narrator attempts to delete the image and contact Kyle, but receives no response. The story ends with the narrator claiming the image has 'infected' them and that they can 'see' the dog even when not looking at the image.

A collection of loosely connected stories that attempt to build a backstory for Smile Dog. Common elements include: (1) The dog was once a normal pet that was possessed by a demon or an alien entity. (2) The image was created by a cult or a secret government experiment. (3) The image is a 'snapshot' of a parallel dimension. (4) Viewing the image is a 'summoning' that allows the entity to enter our world. These stories are not consistent with each other.

Field Notes

⁙ The original Smile Dog photo is of a real Siberian Husky — the human-like teeth and blank eyes were added through photo manipulation.

⁙ The hand in the corner of the Smile.jpg image is said to be beckoning the viewer — 'come closer, look deeper, spread the word.'

⁙ In some versions of the story, the phrase 'SPREAD THE WORD' is spoken in a voice that sounds like a recording played backwards.

⁙ Smile Dog's memetic nature means that even describing it to someone who hasn't seen the image might be enough to start the curse in some versions.

⁙ The creepypasta spawned a spin-off entity: Smile Cat (Grinning Cat), a similar cursed image featuring a feline with the same unsettling human smile.

Cultural Significance

Smile Dog is a foundational text of the creepypasta genre — one of the first internet horror stories to become widely known. It defined the 'cursed image' subgenre and the concept of the memetic hazard: the idea that information itself can be dangerous. This concept has deep resonance in the internet age, where viral content spreads at the speed of clicks and exposure to traumatic material is a genuine concern. Smile Dog has inspired countless imitations and spin-offs (Jeff the Killer, the Russian Sleep Experiment, Candle Cove) and helped establish creepypasta as a distinct form of digital folklore.

Modern Interpretations

Smile Dog has been adapted into short films, YouTube readings (creepypasta narrators like MrCreepyPasta and CreepsMcPasta have millions of views on Smile Dog readings), fan art, and indie games. The 2022 film 'Smile' — about a curse transmitted through gruesome smiles — borrows heavily from Smile Dog's memetic horror concept (though the film involves a human entity, not a dog). Smile Dog remains one of the most recognizable creepypasta characters, with its image (the human-like grin on a dog) instantly iconic. The concept has influenced modern horror video games, particularly those dealing with 'cursed media' (videotapes, image files, lost episodes).
