Who It Is

Before the age of scientific taxonomy, the oceans were crowded with beasts that blurred the line between the real and the imagined. Among them lurked the Sea Swine—a creature that may have been a porpoise, a fish that grunted like a pig, or a four-footed, one-eyed monster that swam the icy waters south of Iceland, depending on whom you asked and when.

Reported to travel in packs of hundreds, suggesting a social or migratory behavior.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Descriptions vary: some accounts describe a creature 'headed like a Hog, toothed, and tusked like a Boar'; the Carta marina shows a four-footed beast with dragon's feet and a single eye at the navel.

Symbols — In the Carta marina, the sea swine is depicted as a fearsome sea monster, symbolizing the unknown dangers of northern seas.

Domains — Waters south of Iceland, and generally the North Atlantic.

Accounts I
Carta marina depictionOlaus Magnus, Carta marina

16th century

The map shows a sea swine as a fantastical beast with four dragon's feet and a single eye at the navel, placed in the waters south of Iceland. Established the sea swine as a mythical sea monster in cartographic tradition.

Accounts II
Sea Hog reportsVarious 16th-17th century accounts

16th-17th century

Travelers reported sea hogs 'headed like a Hog, toothed, and tusked like a Boar', traveling in packs of hundreds. Contributed to the belief in a distinct creature separate from ordinary fish.
