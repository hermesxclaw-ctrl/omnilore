Who It Is

In the shadow of Metz Cathedral, a dragon once made its lair in the Roman amphitheater, its venomous breath so foul that it poisoned the very air of the city. This is the Graoully, a creature of medieval legend whose story is intertwined with the triumph of Christianity over paganism, and whose image still glowers from the walls of Lorraine's churches.

The Graoully was said to be a ferocious and destructive beast. It lived in the ruins of the Roman amphitheater, and its venomous breath was so poisonous that it killed anyone who came near, filling the city with a deadly miasma. It demanded tribute from the inhabitants, often in the form of livestock or even children, though this detail is not consistently attested. Upon seeing Saint Clement, it became docile and followed him, indicating that it was susceptible to divine power.

Powers
Venomous Breath

The Graoully's breath was so toxic that it poisoned the air around it, causing death to anyone who inhaled it. This made the city of Metz unsafe for its inhabitants.

Limit: The power was neutralized by the presence of a holy figure (Saint Clement), suggesting that it was ineffective against those protected by divine power.

Physical Might

As a dragon, the Graoully possessed immense strength, capable of destroying buildings and killing people with its claws and teeth.

Limit: It was subdued by the sign of the cross, indicating that it was vulnerable to holy symbols.

Control over lesser creatures

In some accounts, the Graoully commanded a host of smaller serpents or dragons that followed it, adding to the horror.

Limit: This ability is not present in the earliest sources.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Graoully is typically described as a dragon of enormous size, with a scaly body, bat-like wings, and a long, sinuous tail. It is often shown with a large, swollen belly, symbolizing its greed and gluttony. Some accounts give it multiple heads or a crest of spikes. Symbols — In Christian iconography, the Graoully represents the forces of paganism, evil, and spiritual corruption. Its defeat by Saint Clement symbolizes the triumph of Christianity over the old beliefs. The dragon is also a symbol of the city of Metz, appearing on its coat of arms and in local heraldry. In modern times, it has become a mascot of sorts for the city, with statues and souvenirs.

Domains — The Graoully's lair was the Roman amphitheater of Metz (known as the 'Amphithéâtre de Metz' or 'Arènes de Metz'). It also roamed the surrounding countryside, and its presence made the city uninhabitable. The river Seille, where it was drowned, is also part of its habitat.

Accounts I
Saint Clement and the GraoullyVita Sancti Clementis, Chronicon of Metz

Medieval (legend set in the 3rd or 4th century AD, recorded from the 10th century onward)

When Saint Clement arrived in Metz to evangelize the region, he found the city terrorized by a dragon called the Graoully. The creature lived in the Roman amphitheater and its venomous breath was killing the inhabitants. Clement prayed, and then approached the dragon, making the sign of the cross. The dragon became docile and allowed Clement to lead it by a cord to the river Seille, where it was drowned. The city was freed, and the people converted to Christianity. The dragon's death led to the conversion of the local population and the establishment of the Christian church in Metz. Saint Clement became the patron saint of the city.

The Graoully was a real animal that was killed by Saint Clement. — There is no evidence that the Graoully was a real creature. It is a legendary dragon, and the story is a moral allegory.

The Graoully is the same as the Tarasque or Gargouille. — They are distinct dragons from different regions of France. While similar, they have separate legends and are not interchangeable.

The Graoully was a fire-breathing dragon. — The primary source describes its venomous breath, not fire. Fire-breathing is a common dragon trait but is not attested for the Graoully in early sources.
