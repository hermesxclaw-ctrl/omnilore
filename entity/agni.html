Who It Is

In the beginning, there was neither day nor night, only the god Prajapati. From his forehead sprang Agni, the first fire, and with him came light, and with light came the division of time itself. Agni is not merely a god of fire; he is the mouth of the gods, the messenger who carries human offerings to the divine, the witness to sacred vows, and the transformative power that burns away ignorance to reveal knowledge. His presence is felt in the crackling hearth, the lightning's flash, and the sun's life-giving rays, a tripartite existence that binds the cosmos together.

Agni is characterized as a voracious consumer, a messenger, a purifier, and a witness. He is described as youthful, radiant, and swift. He is also seen as a mediator between gods and humans, and a protector of households. He is known to hide and play tricks, but also to be generous with his blessings.

Powers
Fire Manipulation

Agni embodies fire in all its forms: sacrificial fire, domestic fire, cremation fire, lightning, and solar fire. He can consume, transform, and purify.

Limit: His power depends on offerings; without fuel, he diminishes.

Messenger of the Gods

Agni carries offerings from humans to the deities, acting as a divine intermediary.

Limit: He can only convey what is offered; he cannot intercede on his own.

Witness to Vows

Agni serves as a witness to sacred rituals, especially marriage (Saptapadi) and initiation (Upanayana).

Limit: His role is symbolic; he does not enforce vows.

Purification

Agni has the power to purify, both physically and spiritually, burning away impurities.

Limit: Purification is not absolute; it requires proper ritual context.

Knowledge and Enlightenment

In post-Vedic thought, Agni symbolizes the transformative power of knowledge that dispels ignorance.

Limit: This is metaphorical, not a literal power.

Seven Tongues

Agni has seven tongues of flame (Saptajihvi) that consume oblations rapidly.

Limit: The number seven is symbolic; no specific limitations are described.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Agni is typically depicted as a red or ruddy man with two faces, three legs, seven arms, and seven tongues of flame. He rides a ram or a chariot drawn by red horses. His hair, eyes, and beard are fiery, and he carries a flaming spear or a sacrificial ladle. In some depictions, he has a pot-belly and is accompanied by his consort Svāhā.

Symbols — Fire, the sacrificial altar (vedi), the sacred ladle (sruva), the flaming spear, the ram, the color red, the southeast direction, the number seven (seven tongues, seven flames).

Domains — He resides in the southeast quarter, in the fire altar, in the home hearth, in the cremation ground, in lightning, in the sun, and in the waters (where he hides). As a guardian of the southeast, he is often represented in that corner of Hindu temples.

Accounts I
The Birth of AgniKaṭhaka Saṃhitā, Maitrāyaṇī Saṃhitā

Vedic

In the beginning, there was only Prajapati. Agni sprang from Prajapati's forehead, bringing light and creating day and night. Agni becomes the firstborn of the cosmos, the source of light and time.

Agni Hides in the WatersShatapatha Brahmana 1.2.3.1

Vedic

Three previous Agnis died while serving as Hotri priests. The fourth Agni, fearing a similar fate, hid in the waters. The deities discovered him and brought him back, making him the fire of the present age. Agni becomes the current fire god, and his hiding explains why fire can be found in water (as latent heat).

Accounts II
Agni as MessengerRigveda

Vedic

Agni is repeatedly invoked as the messenger who carries offerings to the gods. He is the mouth of the gods, consuming the oblation and conveying it to the divine realm. Establishes the ritual of homa (fire sacrifice) as a means of communication with the divine.

The Falcon MythIndo-European mythology

Prehistoric

A falcon brings fire from the deities to humans, often along with an elixir of immortality. Agni is associated with this myth, sometimes described as the 'heavenly falcon'. Fire is a divine gift, and Agni is a bringer of civilization.

Agni is the same as the Greek god Hephaestus. — While both are fire gods, they have distinct mythologies and cultural contexts.

Agni is only a god of physical fire. — Agni also represents transformative energy, knowledge, and the sun.

Agni is no longer worshipped. — Agni remains central in Hindu rituals like marriage and yajnas.
