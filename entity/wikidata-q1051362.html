The Approach

In the shadows of Roman Helvetia, a god of battle reigned—Caturix, the Battle-King, whose name echoes through inscriptions but whose stories have been lost to time.

Content advisory. None
Identity

Caturix

Pronunciation KA-tu-riks (reconstructed Gaulish)

Known As

Mars CaturixCaturix MarsCaturix Toutatis

Etymology

Gaulish 'catu-rix' meaning 'battle-king' or 'battle-lord', from 'catu-' (combat, battle) and 'rix' (king).

Cultural Ownership

Helvetii (Gaulish/Celtic), later Gallo-Roman

Disputed Identifications

Possibly an epithet of Toutatis; also associated with Cicollus and Caisivus.

Description

Appearance

No direct descriptions survive. As Mars Caturix, likely depicted in Roman martial style: armed warrior, possibly with helmet, spear, shield. No Celtic iconography confirmed.

Symbols & Iconography

Mars association implies spear, shield, helmet. No unique symbols attested. The name itself is the primary symbol.

Behaviour & Personality

Warrior god, protector in battle. Likely fierce and martial, but specifics unknown.

Domains & Habitats

War, battle, possibly tribal protection. Temples at Aventicum (Avenches), Nonfoux, and likely Yverdon (Eburodunum). Also venerated at Böckingen, Germany.

Version Differences

Gaulish Caturix vs Roman Mars Caturix: Roman interpretation adds Mars attributes. Possible original Celtic aspects lost.
