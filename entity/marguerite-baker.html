Who It Is

Marguerite Baker is the most viscerally disgusting and terrifying member of the Baker family in Resident Evil 7 — a once-kindly Southern matriarch who has been twisted by the Mold (the fungal bioweapon created by the E-Series) into a monstrous, insect-breeding horror. Before Eveline (the bioweapon 'daughter' who infected the Bakers) arrived, Marguerite was a normal, loving wife and mother. After infection, she becomes obsessed with 'family,' preparing grotesque meals of human remains ('Dinner' sequence, one of RE7's most infamous scenes), and protecting Eveline at all costs. Her mutation is the most drastic of the Bakers: her body becomes a living hive for giant, aggressive insects that erupt from her whenever she takes damage or transforms.

In her first appearance, Marguerite seems the most 'normal' of the Bakers — a middle-aged woman in a floral dress and apron who greets Ethan Winters with disturbingly polite hospitality. But the illusion shatters quickly: she shrieks and crawls after Ethan with insect-like speed, her spine twisting unnaturally, her vocalizations shifting between human rage and insectoid chittering. In her boss fight, she transforms fully: her lower body becomes distended, spidery, and egg-laying, her arms elongate into grasping tendrils, and she vomits swarms of flying insects that chase Ethan through the greenhouse. The fight is a masterclass in body horror — watching Marguerite's body contort and birth insects in real time is profoundly unsettling.

What makes Marguerite memorable beyond her boss fight is the tragedy beneath the horror. Before infection, she was genuinely kind. In the game's 'Daughters' DLC, the player sees the night Eveline arrived and began slowly corrupting the Bakers — Marguerite is the first to resist, before the Mold overwhelms her. Her dialogue in the main game ('Stay with us, Ethan! We're family now!') carries a perverted version of her original maternal love. The Mold didn't create a monster from nothing — it corrupted a real person, and what remains of Marguerite is still in there, twisted beyond recognition.

Marguerite's design was heavily influenced by Japanese horror (particularly the contorted movements from films like Ringu and Ju-on) and Southern Gothic aesthetics. Her 'insect queen' transformation drew from Cronenberg body horror and practical effects horror films. The scene where she shoves Ethan's face into a pot of rotten food while her husband saws off his hand is one of the most talked-about sequences in modern horror gaming, and her boss fight in the greenhouse (a dilapidated, humid, corpse-strewn space) is frequently ranked as one of RE7's highlights. Marguerite Baker proved that the most effective horror doesn't need cosmic monsters — it just needs to take something familiar (a mother, a home, a family dinner) and make it wrong.
