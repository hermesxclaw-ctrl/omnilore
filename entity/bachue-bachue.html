Who It Is

From the dark waters of Lake Iguaque, a woman emerged carrying an infant—the first mother of humanity, whose story still echoes in the mountains of Colombia.

Bachué is a nurturing and generative figure, the mother of humanity. She is also a guide, sometimes returning from the underworld to advise her people. Her actions are purposeful, focused on populating the earth and establishing social order.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Bachué is derived from the Chibcha language, meaning 'the one with the naked breast' or 'the woman with the bare breast.' The alternative name Furachogua means 'the good woman.'

Pronunciation — bah-CHWAY (approximate Spanish pronunciation)

Aliases — Furachogua, Bachue, Bachué (Chibcha: 'the one with the naked breast')

Ownership — Muisca (Chibcha) culture, indigenous people of the Altiplano Cundiboyacense in present-day Colombia.

Powers
Creation of humanity

Bachué gave birth to the human race, populating the earth. She emerged from the lake with a child who became her husband, and together they had many children who spread across the land.

Limit: None recorded; this is a foundational myth.

Transformation into serpents

After fulfilling her role, Bachué and her husband transformed into serpents and returned to the sacred lagoon, symbolizing the cycle of life and death.

Limit: This transformation is a one-time event, not a recurring power.

Guidance from the underworld

Bachué is believed to sometimes return from the underworld to guide her people, acting as a protector and counselor.

Limit: Not a constant presence; she appears only when needed.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Bachué is described as a beautiful woman, often depicted with bare breasts, symbolizing motherhood and fertility. In some artistic representations, she is shown with a child in her arms. No detailed physical description survives in primary sources.

Symbols — Associated with water, lakes, serpents, fertility, and motherhood. The serpent is a key symbol, as she transforms into a serpent at the end of her earthly life. In modern Colombian art, she is often depicted as a maternal figure with indigenous features.

Domains — Lake Iguaque (in present-day Boyacá, Colombia) is her primary sacred site. She is also associated with the underworld (the realm of the dead) and with the earth itself as a mother goddess.

Accounts I
The Emergence of Bachué and the Origin of HumanityPedro Simón, Noticias Historiales (1626)

Pre-Columbian, recorded in early 17th century

Bachué emerged from the waters of Lake Iguaque holding a baby boy. The child grew to become her husband, and together they had many children, who populated the earth. After achieving this, they transformed into serpents and returned to the lake, where they continue to reside. The Muisca believed Bachué sometimes returned to guide them. The establishment of human society and the sacredness of Lake Iguaque as a pilgrimage site.
