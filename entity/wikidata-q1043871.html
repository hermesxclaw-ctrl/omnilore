The Approach

In the quiet of a Roman doorway, an obscure goddess named Carna watched over hinges and human health alike—until her identity blurred with another divine guardian, leaving scholars to untangle a knot of ancient confusion.

Content advisory. None
Identity

Carna

Pronunciation KAR-nah

Etymology

The name Carna may derive from Latin 'caro' (flesh) or 'cardo' (hinge), reflecting her dual associations with bodily health and doorways.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Roman religion

Disputed Identifications

Carna is often confused or identified with Cardea, another Roman deity of hinges and health. Some ancient sources treat them as separate, while others merge them.

Description

Appearance

No specific iconography is attested. She is not depicted in surviving art.

Symbols & Iconography

A branch of whitethorn (crataegus) is associated with her, used in rituals to protect thresholds.

Behaviour & Personality

As a protective deity, she was invoked to safeguard health and doorways, warding off harmful forces.

Domains & Habitats

She presided over physical well-being and the protection of entrances, particularly in Roman households.

Version Differences

Ancient sources vary: some treat Carna as a distinct deity of flesh and health, while others merge her with Cardea, the goddess of hinges.

Powers & Abilities

Protection of health

Source Version Ovid, Fasti

Description

Carna was believed to protect the internal organs and overall physical health of individuals.

Limitations

Contradictions

Source Version Ovid, Fasti

Description

She used a whitethorn branch to drive away evil spirits and protect doorways.

Limitations

Stories & Myths

The Origin of Carna

Source Ovid, FastiCulture RomanPeriod Augustan

Summary

Ovid tells that Carna was a nymph named Cranaë, beloved by Janus. She was granted power over doorways and health, and given a whitethorn branch to repel evil.

Participants

Carna

Janus

Consequences

Carna became a protector of thresholds and bodily health.

Variants

Ovid's account is the only detailed narrative; other sources simply list her as a deity.

Relationships

Version Note

Cardea

Description

Carna's identity is often confused with Cardea, another deity of hinges and health. They may be the same or distinct.

Version Note

Some authors conflate them, others distinguish.

Conflicting Accounts

Ancient sources disagree on whether Carna and Cardea are separate deities.

Reception & Afterlife

Carna Botnet

Medium internetCreator Unknown researchersYear 2012Role namesake

Notes

The Carna Botnet was a network of insecure devices used to map the IPv4 address space. It was named after the Roman goddess, possibly due to her association with protection and boundaries.

Contradictions & Disputes

Identity of Carna vs Cardea

Position A

Carna and Cardea are distinct deities.

Position A — evidence

Some ancient lists separate them.

Position B

Carna and Cardea are the same deity.

Position B — evidence

Ovid and others conflate them or use the names interchangeably.

Scholarly Consensus

Scholars are divided; the evidence is insufficient to settle the matter.

Primary Evidence

Ovid, Fasti

Type textDate 8 CELocator Book 6, June 1Language Latin

Translation

Ovid describes Carna as a goddess who protects the body's vital organs and uses a whitethorn branch to ward off evil from thresholds.

Macrobius, Saturnalia
