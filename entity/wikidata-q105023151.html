The Approach

In the candle-lit circles of Brazilian Umbanda, a figure emerges from the spiritual crossroads—Caboclo São Sarava—a name that fuses indigenous ancestry with Catholic sainthood and Afro-Brazilian healing. He is not found in any orthodox hagiography, yet his presence is invoked in countless terreiros, a living testament to the syncretic genius of a people's faith.

Content advisory. This entry concerns Afro-Brazilian religious practices, which have historically been marginalized and misunderstood. Readers should approach with respect for living traditions. Some descriptions of ritual practice may be unfamiliar; no sensitive or violent material is included.
Identity

Caboclo São Sarava

Pronunciation kah-BOH-kloo sah-oo sah-RAH-vah

Known As

Caboclo SaravaSão SaravaCaboclo das Matas

Etymology

'Caboclo' derives from Tupi, referring to people of mixed indigenous and European descent, and in Umbanda denotes a spirit of indigenous ancestry. 'São' is Portuguese for 'Saint', indicating syncretic Catholic influence. 'Sarava' is a common greeting in Afro-Brazilian religions, derived from Yoruba 'sàrávà' meaning 'to greet' or 'to salute', often used to hail spirits.

Cultural Ownership

Afro-Brazilian, particularly Umbanda and related traditions; also present in some Candomblé-influenced contexts, though more characteristic of Umbanda's Caboclo line.

Disputed Identifications

Some practitioners identify 'São Sarava' as a syncretism of Saint Benedict or Saint Sebastian, but this is not universal. Others see 'Sarava' as an epithet rather than a specific saint, making 'Caboclo São Sarava' a composite title rather than a unique entity.

Description

Appearance

In ritual descriptions, Caboclo spirits are often depicted as indigenous warriors or healers, wearing feathered headdresses and often smoking a pipe or cigar. They may appear with a bow and arrow, symbolizing hunting and protection. For 'Caboclo São Sarava', specific visual details are not documented, but he likely follows this archetype.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with the forest, arrows, tobacco, and healing herbs. The term 'Sarava' is a ritual greeting, often accompanied by a hand gesture of respect. No specific iconographic representation is widely known.

Behaviour & Personality

Caboclos are typically described as serious, wise, and direct, with a strong connection to nature and healing. They are often invoked for protection, guidance, and curing. 'São Sarava' likely embodies these traits, though individual mediums may report variations.

Domains & Habitats

Primarily associated with forests, rivers, and natural landscapes, as Caboclos are considered spirits of the Brazilian wilderness. In ritual, they are called upon in the 'linha dos caboclos' (line of caboclos) within Umbanda.

Version Differences

In Umbanda, Caboclos are distinct from Pretos Velhos (spirits of enslaved Africans) and Exus (trickster spirits). Some traditions may blend Caboclo traits with Catholic saint attributes, leading to variations in how 'São Sarava' is perceived.

The Syncretic Emergence

Source Oral tradition and scholarly interpretationCulture Afro-BrazilianPeriod 20th century

Summary

The figure 'Caboclo São Sarava' likely emerged from the consolidation of Umbanda in the early 20th century, when diverse African, indigenous, and Catholic elements were blended. The name reflects this fusion: 'Caboclo' for indigenous ancestry, 'São' for Catholic sanctity, and 'Sarava' for Afro-Brazilian ritual greeting.

Participants

Umbanda founders and mediums

Consequences

Created a new spiritual identity that resonates with many Brazilians of mixed heritage.

Variants

Some terreiros may use 'São Sarava' as a specific entity, while others use it as an honorific for any Caboclo.
