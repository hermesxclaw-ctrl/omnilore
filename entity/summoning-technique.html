Who It Is

A space-time ninjutsu that allows the user to summon animals or objects through a blood contract. The user signs a contract in blood with a specific animal species, then uses hand signs to summon them. Toads, snakes, and slugs are among the most common summons used by the Sannin.

Powers

The Approach

In the hidden villages of the shinobi world, the Summoning Technique is more than a jutsu—it is a pact. A single hand seal, a drop of blood, and a contract sealed in ink can call forth a giant toad, a serpent, or a hawk from across dimensions. But this power is not free: it demands a bond, a price, and a will strong enough to command a beast that could just as easily devour its summoner.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of violence, death, and the ethical implications of using sentient creatures as tools in combat.
Identity

Summoning Technique

Pronunciation Koo-chee-yo-seh no Joot-soo

Known As

Kuchiyose no JutsuSummoning JutsuSummoningSpace-Time SummoningAnimal Summoning Technique

Etymology

The Japanese term 'Kuchiyose' (口寄せ) literally means 'calling forth' or 'invoking', often used in the context of spiritual summoning. 'Jutsu' (術) means 'technique' or 'art'.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Masashi Kishimoto for the 'Naruto' manga series, first published in 1999 by Shueisha. The technique is a central element of the Naruto universe's ninja combat system.

Disputed Identifications

In Western fandom, the technique is sometimes confused with the 'Summoning: Impure World Reincarnation' (Edo Tensei), which is a separate forbidden jutsu that resurrects the dead. They are different techniques, though both involve summoning.

Description

Appearance

The technique itself is invisible, but its activation is marked by a puff of smoke and a swirl of chakra. The summoned creature appears from a portal of swirling energy, often accompanied by a burst of smoke. The user's hand traces a pattern of blood on their palm or a surface, which then glows with chakra.

Symbols & Iconography

The summoning contract is a scroll with the user's name and blood signature. Each summoning clan has its own unique scroll, often adorned with clan symbols. The hand seals used are typically the Boar, Dog, Bird, Monkey, and Ram, though variations exist.

Behaviour & Personality

The technique is a negotiation, not a mere spell. The summoned creature retains its free will and personality. The user must command respect; otherwise, the summon may refuse to cooperate or even turn on the user. The bond between summoner and summon is built on trust and mutual benefit.

Domains & Habitats

Summoned creatures come from various realms, often referred to as 'summoning realms' or 'animal realms'. These are separate dimensions where the creatures live in societies. For example, Mount Myoboku is the home of the toads, Ryuchi Cave is the home of snakes, and the Forest of Death is home to many creatures.

Version Differences

In the original manga, the technique is primarily used for combat. In the anime, there are additional filler episodes showing more elaborate summoning rituals. In the sequel, 'Boruto: Naruto Next Generations', the technique is less prominent, but still used by some characters.

Powers & Abilities

Summoning Contract

Source Version Manga and anime

Description

A blood contract signed with a specific animal clan allows the user to summon any member of that clan, provided they have enough chakra and skill.

Limitations

Requires a pre-existing contract, blood sacrifice, and enough chakra to summon a creature of the desired size.

Contradictions

Some characters can summon without a visible contract, such as Orochimaru, but it is implied they have made secret contracts.

Summoning: Impure World Reincarnation

Source Version Manga and anime

Description

A forbidden technique that summons the souls of the dead into a living vessel, effectively resurrecting them. This is a separate technique, but often confused with the basic Summoning Technique.

Limitations

Requires a living sacrifice and a piece of the deceased's DNA. The resurrected are bound to the summoner's will.

Contradictions

This technique is classified as a Summoning Technique, but its mechanics and purpose are vastly different from the standard animal summoning.

Summoning: Rashomon Gates

Source Version Manga and anime

Description

A defensive summoning that calls forth massive, demonic gates to block attacks.

Limitations

Requires a high level of chakra and is often used by Orochimaru.

Contradictions

None

Summoning: Living Doll

Source Version Manga and anime

Description

A technique used by Kankuro to summon his puppet, which is a combat tool rather than a living creature.

Limitations

Requires a puppet and the user's chakra strings.

Contradictions

This is a different application of summoning, as it summons an object rather than a creature.

Stories & Myths

Naruto's First Summoning

Source Naruto, Chapter 94Culture Japanese mangaPeriod 2001

Summary

During the battle with Gaara, Naruto is desperate to gain an advantage. He signs a contract with the toads and performs the Summoning Technique, but instead of the boss toad Gamabunta, he accidentally summons a small tadpole. After several attempts, he finally summons Gamabunta, who is initially uncooperative but eventually agrees to help Naruto fight Shukaku.

Ethics of Summoning

Source Naruto manga

Description

Summoned creatures are sentient and can be forced to fight against their will. Some, like Manda, are hostile and require domination. The technique is essentially a form of enslavement, though some bonds are respectful.

Scholarly Context

This raises questions about the morality of using animals in warfare, a topic debated in real-world military ethics.

Edo Tensei

Source Naruto manga

Description

The Impure World Reincarnation is a forbidden jutsu that forcibly resurrects the dead, turning them into immortal puppets. This is a violation of the natural order and causes immense suffering.

Scholarly Context

This technique is analogous to necromancy in other mythologies and is universally condemned in the series.

Contradictions & Disputes

Chakra cost of summoning

Position A

Summoning large creatures requires enormous chakra reserves.

Position A — evidence

Naruto initially struggles to summon Gamabunta due to his low chakra control.

Position B

Some characters with low chakra can summon large creatures with ease.

Position B — evidence

Kakashi can summon his dogs without apparent strain, despite having limited chakra.

Scholarly Consensus

The chakra cost likely scales with the creature's size and the user's skill, but the series is inconsistent.

Can summoned creatures be killed permanently?

Position A

Summoned creatures can be killed and stay dead.

Position A — evidence

Manda is killed by Sasuke and does not appear again.

Position B

Summoned creatures are not truly dead when killed; they return to their realm.

Position B — evidence

Gamabunta is injured but survives, and other toads are shown alive after being defeated.

Scholarly Consensus

The series is ambiguous; some creatures die, but others may be able to retreat.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

The exact rules of summoning realms are not fully explored in the series. The chakra mechanics are inconsistent. The extent of the technique's use outside the main cast is unknown. Some summoning clans are only mentioned in guidebooks, not depicted. No real-world equivalent exists, so cultural context is limited to the Naruto franchise. This dossier relies on the manga and anime; supplementary materials like novels and spin-offs were not exhaustively reviewed.

Research Scope

This dossier focuses on the Summoning Technique (Kuchiyose no Jutsu) as depicted in the manga and anime series 'Naruto' by Masashi Kishimoto, including its variations, users, and cultural impact. It does not cover other fictional summoning systems unless explicitly noted.
