Etymology

From Italian 'biscione' (augmentative of 'biscia', meaning grass snake or adder), thus 'big snake'.

Cultural Ownership

Italian, specifically Milanese/Lombard; it is the emblem of the Visconti and Sforza families and of the city of Milan.

Disputed Identifications

The creature is sometimes identified as a dragon, a wyvern, or a serpent; its exact species is debated. Some interpret the child it swallows as a Moor, a Saracen, or an infant, with varying legends explaining its origin.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the heart of Milan, a serpent with a human head coils across coats of arms, its jaws open wide to swallow a small figure whole. This is the biscione, a heraldic dragon-serpent that has become one of the most recognizable symbols of the city — a creature of medieval legend, dynastic pride, and enduring cultural identity.

The great blue serpent coils in the heart of Milan, devouring the enemy of the house. So it has been since the Visconti first raised their standard, and so it shall remain.

Who It Is

The Biscione (literally 'big grass snake') is a legendary heraldic serpent-dragon that serves as the emblem of Milan and the House of Visconti and Sforza. Depicted as a blue serpent in the act of devouring a red or black human figure (a 'Saracen' or child), the Biscione is one of the most famous heraldic charges in Italy. The origin of the emblem is recounted in medieval chronicles: during the First Crusade, Ottone Visconti defeated a Saracen prince in single combat. The prince's helmet bore the image of a serpent, which Ottone adopted as his personal device. However, an older legend ties the Biscione to the foundation of Milan: according to the 12th-century chronicler Galvano Fiamma, the city was founded by a Celtic chief named Belloveso, whose battle standard was a serpent that had saved his life by feeding him milk. The creature is sometimes identified as a vipera ('viper'), though the Biscione is actually a constrictor snake in heraldic terms. The image of the serpent devouring a human is known as 'la bissa' in Lombard dialect and appears throughout Milan on the Duomo, the Sforza Castle, the city crest, and on the logo of the Alfa Romeo automobile company (which adopted the Biscione as its corporate symbol). Legend holds that the Biscione will one day awaken and devour Milan if the city ever loses its virtue. The beast represents both Milan's fierce independence and the dynastic power of its ruling families.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

The biscione is typically depicted as a large serpent or dragon with a human head, often crowned. It is usually shown in the act of swallowing a human figure, typically a child, emerging from its mouth. The serpent's body is coiled or undulating, and it is often colored blue (azure) on a silver (argent) field, though variations exist. The human head is usually shown with a beard and sometimes has a crown.

Symbols & Iconography

The biscione is a symbol of power, cunning, and protection. The swallowing of a human figure is often interpreted as the devouring of enemies or as a symbol of strength and domination. The crown represents sovereignty. In heraldry, it is classified as a 'serpent' or 'dragon' and is often blazoned as 'a serpent in pale, swallowing a child'.

Behaviour & Personality

As a heraldic beast, the biscione has no independent personality; its meaning is derived from its use as a symbol. It conveys ferocity, vigilance, and the power of the ruling family.

Domains & Habitats

It is not a creature with a natural habitat; it exists primarily in heraldry, on flags, coats of arms, and architectural elements throughout Milan and territories once under Visconti/Sforza rule.

Version Differences

Variations include the color of the serpent (often blue, but sometimes green), the presence or absence of a crown, the sex of the human head (usually male), and the identity of the swallowed figure (sometimes an adult, sometimes a child, sometimes a Moor). The blazon also varies: some depict the serpent as a dragon with legs, others as a legless serpent.

Symbolic power

Description

As a heraldic emblem, the biscione represents the authority and legitimacy of the Visconti and Sforza dynasties and the city of Milan. It is used on flags, coins, and buildings to assert political and military dominance.

Limitations

Its power is purely symbolic and not supernatural.
