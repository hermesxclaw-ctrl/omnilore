Who It Is

Lac Long Quan (Lạc Long Quân, 'Dragon Lord of Lac') is the legendary progenitor of the Vietnamese people, a dragon king of the sea who, together with the mountain fairy Au Co, founded the Vietnamese nation. According to the traditional origin myth, Lac Long Quan was the son of Kinh Duong Vuong, the first Hung King, and a descendant of the dragon clan of the sea. He was a being of extraordinary powers: he could transform between human and dragon form, commanded the creatures of the deep, and demonstrated superhuman strength, slaying monstrous creatures that terrorized early human communities, including a giant fish (Ngu Tinh), a nine-tailed fox (Ho Tinh), and a demonic tree spirit (Moc Tinh). These deeds established him as a divine civilizer who made the land safe for human habitation. The pivotal event of the myth is his marriage to Au Co, a fairy (tiên) of the mountains, descended from the bird clan. Their union of dragon and fairy, sea and mountain, produced a miraculous birth: Au Co laid a sac of 100 eggs, from which hatched 100 sons. However, the fundamental difference in natures soon became apparent—Lac Long Quan was a creature of water and could not long abide on the highlands, while Au Co was a creature of the mountains. The couple parted amicably: Lac Long Quan took 50 sons to the coastal lowlands, teaching them to fish and farm the deltas, while Au Co took 50 sons to the highlands, teaching them forest crafts. From these 100 sons, the Vietnamese people—the 'con Rồng cháu Tiên' (children of the dragon, grandchildren of the fairy)—are descended. This myth encodes deep cultural truths about Vietnamese identity: the hybrid origin from sea and mountain, the unity-in-duality of Vietnamese geography, and the inherent legitimacy of the Vietnamese people as heirs to both divine dragon and fairy lineages.
