The Approach

In the shadowed annals of Japanese imperial myth, Hosuseri stands as a figure of thwarted ambition and divine consequence—a deity whose story is intertwined with the very origins of the imperial line, yet whose name is often eclipsed by his more famous brothers.

Content advisory. Contains references to mythological violence and divine retribution.
Identity

Hosuseri

Pronunciation HOH-soo-seh-ree

Etymology

The name Hosuseri (火須勢理) is often interpreted as 'fire-rushing' or related to fire, possibly linking to his role in myth. The kanji 火 (hi) means 'fire', 須 (su) is phonetic, and 勢理 (seri) may relate to 'pushing' or 'rushing'.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have equated Hosuseri with other deities like Ho no Susori, but this is debated; the identification is not universally accepted.

Description

Appearance

Not described in detail in the sources; likely depicted as a divine being in classical Japanese art, but no specific iconography is attested.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are recorded for Hosuseri.

Behaviour & Personality

In the myth, Hosuseri is portrayed as impulsive and arrogant, challenging his younger brother to a contest of strength and wits, and later suffering the consequences of his hubris.

Domains & Habitats

As a deity, he is associated with the divine realm, but after his defeat, he is said to have retreated to the sea or to the underworld, depending on the version.

Version Differences

The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki differ in details: in one, he is the eldest and fails to retrieve the lost fishhook; in another, he is the one who challenges and loses, leading to his exile.

Stories & Myths

The Lost Fishhook

Source Kojiki and Nihon ShokiCulture JapanesePeriod Mythological age

Summary

Hosuseri, the elder brother, challenges his younger brother Hoderi to a fishing contest. Hoderi catches a fish, but Hosuseri loses his fishhook in the sea. Enraged, he demands it back, and Hoderi, unable to find it, offers his own hook, but Hosuseri refuses. Eventually, the sea god's aid is sought, and the hook is recovered, but Hosuseri's arrogance leads to his downfall as he is cursed to be subservient to his brother.

Participants

Hosuseri

Hoderi

Hoori (the third brother, though not directly involved in the contest)

Consequences

Hosuseri is defeated and becomes subject to his brother, establishing the dominance of the imperial line.

Variants

In the Nihon Shoki, the roles are slightly different, and Hosuseri's fate is less clear, with some versions saying he drowned.
