Who It Is

Le Anguane are freshwater spirits from the folklore of northeastern Italy, particularly the Veneto, Friuli, and Lombardy Alpine regions. They are the Italian equivalent of the Celtic and Germanic water nymphs, likely derived from pre-Roman Celtic populations of Cisalpine Gaul (the Gallia Cisalpina). Anguane are described as beautiful young women with long, flowing hair — often green or golden — who inhabit waterfalls, mountain springs, and deep woodland lakes. They are occasionally depicted with fish tails (like mermaids) or with the legs of goats, suggesting a hybrid nature. Anguane possess enchanting voices and frequently sing or play harps to lure humans into the water. Their behavior toward humans is ambivalent: they may grant prophecies, healing, and good fortune to those who treat them with respect, but they drown those who offend them or attempt to capture them. Like the wild women (Dames Vertes) of French and Alpine tradition, Anguane are protectors of wild nature and punish those who harm sacred springs or cut certain trees. They leave offerings of flowers, ribbon, or coins at the water's edge. In Friulian tradition, the Anguane are specifically guardians of the region's pristine mountain springs (the 'fountains of life'). The name 'Anguana' is often linked to Latin 'anguis' (serpent), connecting them to the serpent-tailed Melusine tradition, or alternatively to the Celtic goddesses of springs (Matronae).
