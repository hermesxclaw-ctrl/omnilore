Identity

Darius

Pronunciation də-RY-əs (English); dah-REE-us (Latinized); Dārayavauš (Old Persian)

Etymology

The name Darius derives from the Old Persian Dārayavauš, meaning 'he who holds firm the good' or 'possessor of goodness'. It is a common name among Persian kings, most notably Darius I and Darius III of the Achaemenid Empire.

Cultural Ownership

Persian/Iranian; also used in Greek and later Western contexts as a historical royal name.

Disputed Identifications

The metadata labels this as a fictional character, but the name is overwhelmingly associated with historical Persian kings. It is possible that this refers to a fictionalized version of a historical Darius, or a character in a specific work not identified here. No such fictional character could be confirmed.

Description

Appearance

No specific appearance is attested for a fictional Darius. Historical depictions of Darius I show him with a beard, royal robes, and a crenellated crown, as on the Behistun relief.

Symbols & Iconography

Royal symbols of Achaemenid Persia: the winged sun disc, the royal spear, the bow, and the lion-griffin.

Behaviour & Personality

Historical Darius I was portrayed as a capable administrator and military leader, but also as cunning and ruthless in securing power. Darius III is often depicted as tragic and less competent. Any fictional version would likely draw on these traits.

Domains & Habitats

Persian Empire, specifically the royal court at Persepolis and Susa, and the battlefield.

Version Differences

Multiple historical figures named Darius exist; no single fictional version is known. Differences between Darius I and Darius III are significant in character and historical role.

Stories & Myths

Rise of Darius I

Source Herodotus, Histories; Behistun InscriptionCulture Persian/GreekPeriod c. 522-486 BCE

Summary

Darius I seizes the throne after the death of Cambyses, overcoming the usurper Gaumata and suppressing numerous rebellions. He then expands the empire and organizes it into satrapies.

Participants

Darius I

Gaumata

Rebel leaders

Consequences

Establishes the Achaemenid dynasty's peak; builds Persepolis; invades Greece (Battle of Marathon).

Variants

Herodotus's account differs from Darius's own inscription, especially regarding Gaumata's identity.

Fall of Darius III

Source Arrian, Anabasis of AlexanderCulture Greek/RomanPeriod c. 336-330 BCE

Summary

Darius III, the last Achaemenid king, faces Alexander the Great. After defeats at Issus and Gaugamela, he flees, is betrayed by his satrap Bessus, and is killed.

Participants

Darius III

Alexander the Great

Bessus

Consequences

End of the Persian Empire; Alexander becomes ruler of Asia.

Variants

Later Roman sources embellish the story with tragic elements.
