Accounts I
The Sinking of Nuestra Señora de las MercedesHistorical records, contemporary accounts

1804

During the Napoleonic Wars, the Spanish frigate Nuestra Señora de las Mercedes was returning from South America with a cargo of silver and gold when it was intercepted by a British squadron off the coast of Portugal. The British demanded surrender, but the Spanish refused, leading to an attack. The frigate exploded and sank, killing most of its crew and losing its treasure. The wreck lay undiscovered for centuries until Odyssey Marine Exploration located it in 2007 and recovered a large amount of silver, sparking a legal battle between the salvage company, Spain, and Peru. The sinking resulted in loss of life and treasure. The subsequent salvage dispute led to a landmark U.S. Supreme Court decision in 2012, ruling that the treasure belonged to Spain. The incident highlighted issues of maritime heritage and sovereign immunity.

The Rise of Mercedes-BenzCorporate history, automotive archives

1926–present

The Mercedes-Benz brand was formed in 1926 by the merger of Daimler-Motoren-Gesellschaft and Benz & Cie. The name 'Mercedes' came from Emil Jellinek's daughter, Mercedes, and was used for a series of high-performance cars. The brand became synonymous with luxury and innovation, introducing technologies like anti-lock brakes and airbags. In motorsport, Mercedes has a dominant record in Formula One, winning numerous constructors' and drivers' championships. Mercedes-Benz has become one of the world's most valuable automotive brands, influencing global transportation and engineering standards.

Directed by Yousry Nasrallah, 'Mercedes' is a drama that explores social issues in contemporary Egypt. The film follows a young woman named Mercedes who becomes involved in prostitution, reflecting on poverty and survival. It was critically acclaimed for its honest portrayal of marginalized life. The film received awards and contributed to Nasrallah's reputation as a socially conscious filmmaker.
