From the union of heaven and earth, through the patience of the bear and the wisdom of heaven's son, the first kingdom was born on the slopes of Taebaek.

Who It Is

Dangun Wanggeom is the legendary founder of Gojoseon, the first Korean kingdom, and the central figure of Korea's national foundation myth. According to the Samguk Yusa, the story begins in the heavenly realm, where Hwanin, the Lord of Heaven, had a son named Hwanung who yearned to live on earth and govern humanity. Hwanin granted his son's wish and sent him down to Mount Taebaek (in present-day North Korea) with three heavenly seals and 3,000 followers. There, Hwanung established the divine city of Sinsi and promulgated laws, teaching humanity agriculture, medicine, and the arts of civilization. A bear and a tiger, living in a cave on the mountain, prayed to Hwanung, begging to become human. Hwanung gave them twenty cloves of garlic and a bundle of mugwort, instructing them to eat only these and avoid sunlight for 100 days. The tiger, unable to endure the ordeal, abandoned the test after about twenty days. The bear, however, persevered and after 100 days was transformed into a beautiful woman named Ungnyeo. She later married Hwanung and gave birth to Dangun Wanggeom. Dangun ruled Gojoseon for over 1,500 years before retiring to become a mountain spirit (sanshin) at the age of 1,908. The Dangun myth serves as the foundational narrative of Korean ethnic identity and national consciousness. October 3rd (Gaecheonjeol, 'Heaven Opening Day') is celebrated as National Foundation Day in South Korea. The Dangun myth weaves together shamanistic, animistic, and early state-formation elements: the descent from heaven, the bear transformation, and the establishment of civilization all reflect core themes in Korean indigenous religion.
