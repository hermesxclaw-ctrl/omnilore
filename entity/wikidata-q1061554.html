Who It Is

The name Polydorus echoes through Greek myth and history like a refrain—now a blinded prince, now a Theban king, now a sculptor whose chisel shaped the agony of Laocoön. Each bearer of this 'many-gifted' name carries a distinct fate, yet together they weave a tapestry of tragedy, heroism, and artistry.

Each mythological Polydorus has distinct traits: the son of Phineus is a victim of false accusation; the Theban prince is a royal heir; the Argive is a warrior; the Trojan prince is a young victim; the Ceteian warrior is a minor combatant; the suitor is an antagonist. Historical figures: the Spartan king is known for reforms; the tyrant of Pherae is a power-seeker.

Powers
None (mythological figures)

The mythological Polydori possess no supernatural powers; they are mortals involved in heroic or tragic narratives.

Limit: They are subject to fate and mortality.

Kingship (historical)

Polydorus of Sparta was a king; Polydorus of Pherae was a tyrant.

Limit: Political power, not supernatural.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description is given for any of the mythological Polydori. In art, the name is associated with the Laocoön group, where the figures are muscular and idealized.

Symbols — No distinctive symbols are associated with the name. In art, the Laocoön group is a symbol of suffering and divine punishment.

Domains — Various: Thrace (son of Phineus), Thebes (son of Cadmus), Argos (son of Hippomedon), Troy (Trojan prince), Ceteius (warrior), Ithaca (suitor), Sparta (king), Pherae (tyrant).

Accounts I
Blinding of Phineus's SonsPseudo-Apollodorus, Hyginus

Mythic

Phineus, blinded by the gods, has his sons by Cleopatra blinded after their stepmother Idaea falsely accuses them of rape. The sons are blinded; later the Argonauts punish Phineus.

Theban Royal LinePseudo-Apollodorus

Mythic

Polydorus, son of Cadmus and Harmonia, becomes king of Thebes and fathers Labdacus with Nycteis. Continues the Theban royal line leading to Oedipus.

Epigoni Attack on ThebesPausanias

Mythic

Polydorus, son of Hippomedon, is one of the Epigoni who successfully attack Thebes to avenge their fathers. Thebes is sacked; the Epigoni are victorious.

Accounts II
Death of Trojan PolydorusHomer, Virgil (Aeneid)

Mythic

Priam's youngest son is sent to Thrace with gold; Polymestor kills him for the gold. In some versions, he is killed during the Trojan War. His ghost appears in the Aeneid; his death is avenged by Hecuba.

Death of Ceteian PolydorusQuintus Smyrnaeus

Late Antiquity

A Ceteian ally of Troy is killed by Odysseus. Minor death in the Trojan War.

Slaying of the SuitorsHomer, Odyssey

Mythic

Polydorus of Zacynthus is one of Penelope's suitors; he is killed by Odysseus. Odysseus reclaims his household.
