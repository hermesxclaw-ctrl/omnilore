Who It Is

Cultural Significance

Dracula embodies Victorian anxieties about reverse colonization (an Eastern aristocrat invading England), sexual predation, disease, and the erosion of Christian faith. The novel's epistolary structure — diary entries, letters, telegrams — makes it an early example of found-footage horror.
