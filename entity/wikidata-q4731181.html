Who It Is

In the shadowed halls beneath the world, where the roots of mountains drink the dark, there sits a queen whose name most have never heard. She is Allani, the Lady of the Dark Earth, the keeper of the gate that no living soul may pass and return unchanged. While the storm gods thunder above and the sun god blazes in his course, Allani rules the silent realm of the dead — and even the greatest of the gods must descend to her throne when they need the wisdom that only the earth's deep places can give.

Allani is depicted as a powerful, authoritative deity who presides over the underworld. In the Song of Ullikummi, she is shown as a goddess who can be petitioned and who receives the gods when they descend to the underworld. She is not depicted as malevolent, but rather as a stern and just ruler of the dead. In some texts, she is called upon in rituals to protect the living from the dangers of the underworld.

Powers
Sovereignty over the Underworld

Allani is the queen of the underworld, ruling over the realm of the dead. She has the power to admit or deny entry to the underworld, and to release souls from it. In the Song of Ullikummi, the god Ea must descend to her realm to seek her counsel.

Limit: Her power is generally confined to the underworld. She does not typically act in the world of the living, except through rituals and intermediaries.

Control over the Dead

As queen of the underworld, Allani has authority over the spirits of the dead. She can be petitioned to keep the dead from harming the living, and to ensure the proper passage of souls to the afterlife.

Limit: Her control over the dead is absolute, but she is not a psychopomp — she does not guide souls to the underworld. That role belongs to other deities.

Purification and Protection

Allani is invoked in Hittite purification rituals to remove impurity and protect the living from the contamination of death. She is called upon to 'open the gate of the dark earth' to allow impurities to be carried away to the underworld.

Limit: This power is ritualistic and requires the performance of specific ceremonies by a priest or ritual practitioner.

Accounts I
The Song of UllikummiHittite text (CTH 345), translating a Hurrian original

c. 1400-1200 BCE

In this central myth of the Kumarbi cycle, the god Ea descends to the underworld to consult Allani. He finds her in her palace, and she provides him with the ancient wisdom needed to defeat the stone giant Ullikummi. She is depicted as a wise, powerful, and indispensable figure who holds knowledge that even the great gods do not possess. Allani's counsel enables Ea to find the ancient copper-cutting tool that is used to separate Ullikummi from the shoulder of the giant Upelluri, leading to the stone giant's downfall. This establishes Allani as a key figure in the cosmic order.

Accounts II
Ritual of the Eagle of AllaniHittite ritual texts

c. 1400-1200 BCE

In purification rituals, the 'Eagle of Allani' is invoked to carry away impurity and evil to the underworld. The eagle is a symbol of Allani's power and her ability to bridge the worlds of the living and the dead. The ritual involves the symbolic release of an eagle or the recitation of spells invoking its power. The ritual purifies the individual or household, restoring them to a state of ritual cleanliness and protecting them from the dangers of the underworld.

Allani as Spouse of Nergal (Akkadian tradition)Akkadian god-lists and literary texts

c. 1300-600 BCE

In the Akkadian tradition, Allatum (Allani) is identified with Ereshkigal and is the spouse of Nergal, the god of war and plague. The myth of Nergal and Ereshkigal describes how Nergal descends to the underworld and becomes its king, ruling alongside Allatum/Ereshkigal. This syncretism merges the Hurrian Allani with the Sumerian Ereshkigal, creating a composite goddess who rules the underworld with Nergal.
