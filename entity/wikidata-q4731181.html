She is the queen of the underworld, ruler of the realm of the dead. She also has a role in purification rituals, where she is invoked to keep the dead from troubling the living.

Form

No definitive visual representation of Allani has been identified with certainty. She is not depicted on any known seal or relief with a clear label. Based on her role as underworld queen, she may have been depicted similarly to other underworld goddesses — seated on a throne, possibly with a double-headed eagle or other chthonic symbols. This is speculative. In texts, she is described as 'the queen of the dark earth' and 'the lady of the underworld', suggesting a regal, imposing presence.

Powers

Sovereignty over the Underworld Allani is the queen of the underworld, ruling over the realm of the dead. She has the power to admit or deny entry to the underworld, and to release souls from it. In the Song of Ullikummi, the god Ea must descend to her realm to seek her counsel. Hurrian, Hittite, Akkadian Her power is generally confined to the underworld. She does not typically act in the world of the living, except through rituals and intermediaries. In the Song of Ullikummi, she is visited by Ea, who comes to her. In some texts, she is described as being subordinate to the supreme god Tessub or to Ereshkigal, while in others she is fully independent.

Control over the Dead As queen of the underworld, Allani has authority over the spirits of the dead. She can be petitioned to keep the dead from harming the living, and to ensure the proper passage of souls to the afterlife. Hurrian, Hittite Her control over the dead is absolute, but she is not a psychopomp — she does not guide souls to the underworld. That role belongs to other deities. In some rituals, Allani is invoked alongside the 'Eagle of Allani' to carry away impurities, suggesting a more active role in the world of the living than her primary domain would suggest.

Purification and Protection Allani is invoked in Hittite purification rituals to remove impurity and protect the living from the contamination of death. She is called upon to 'open the gate of the dark earth' to allow impurities to be carried away to the underworld. Hittite This power is ritualistic and requires the performance of specific ceremonies by a priest or ritual practitioner. None found.

Behavior & Habitat

Allani is depicted as a powerful, authoritative deity who presides over the underworld. In the Song of Ullikummi, she is shown as a goddess who can be petitioned and who receives the gods when they descend to the underworld. She is not depicted as malevolent, but rather as a stern and just ruler of the dead. In some texts, she is called upon in rituals to protect the living from the dangers of the underworld.

Accounts

In this central myth of the Kumarbi cycle, the god Ea descends to the underworld to consult Allani. He finds her in her palace, and she provides him with the ancient wisdom needed to defeat the stone giant Ullikummi. She is depicted as a wise, powerful, and indispensable figure who holds knowledge that even the great gods do not possess.

In purification rituals, the 'Eagle of Allani' is invoked to carry away impurity and evil to the underworld. The eagle is a symbol of Allani's power and her ability to bridge the worlds of the living and the dead. The ritual involves the symbolic release of an eagle or the recitation of spells invoking its power.

In the Akkadian tradition, Allatum (Allani) is identified with Ereshkigal and is the spouse of Nergal, the god of war and plague. The myth of Nergal and Ereshkigal describes how Nergal descends to the underworld and becomes its king, ruling alongside Allatum/Ereshkigal.

What We Don't Know

Limited primary evidence. No definitive visual representation identified. The Ugaritic reference to 'Allaniyu' is ambiguous and requires further study. The Urartian evidence is sparse. The relationship between Allani and the 'Eagle of Allani' is not fully understood. The full extent of her role in Hurrian mythology is unknown due to the fragmentary nature of the texts.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Allani as the ancient Near Eastern equivalent of Hades or Persephone — a powerful, regal figure who rules the underworld. But unlike the Greek gods, who are often depicted as capricious, Allani is a figure of stability and order. She is the one who ensures that the dead stay in their place, that the boundary between the living and the dead is maintained, and that the cosmic order is preserved. She is not a villain, but a necessary part of the universe — the dark counterpart to the bright gods of the sky.
