Who It Is

Lucifer is one of the most complex and culturally resonant figures in Western religious tradition — the most beautiful of angels whose pride led to his catastrophic fall and transformation into Satan, the Adversary of God and humanity. The name 'Lucifer' originates from the Latin Vulgate translation of Isaiah 14:12, rendering the Hebrew Helel ben Shahar ('shining one, son of the dawn') as 'Lucifer' (light-bearer), a passage originally directed at the defeated king of Babylon but reinterpreted by early Christian exegetes as describing Satan's primordial fall. Jesus in Luke 10:18 declares, 'I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven,' reinforcing this cosmic narrative. The full mythology of Lucifer's rebellion against God and expulsion from heaven with a third of the angelic host was developed through apocryphal texts (particularly the Book of Enoch), patristic writings (Origen, Augustine), and reached its most influential literary form in John Milton's Paradise Lost (1667). In Milton's epic, Lucifer emerges as a tragic anti-hero whose defining characteristic is pride: 'Better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven.' This characterization profoundly influenced the Romantic poets — Blake and Shelley celebrated Lucifer as a symbol of rebellion against tyranny — and generations of literary and pop-cultural portrayals. Theologically, Lucifer represents the origin of evil as a corruption of good: he was created perfect but freely chose rebellion. His sin is identified as pride (superbia), the desire to exalt himself above God. In Islamic tradition, Iblis (Satan) similarly refuses to bow before Adam out of pride, arguing his creation from smokeless fire makes him superior to the clay-formed human. Lucifer's transformation from radiant archangel to the serpent of Eden and the dragon of Revelation maps a tragic arc from highest to lowest, light to darkness — a fall so total that his very name becomes a paradox: the light-bearer who dwells in darkness. Modern representations from Gaiman's Sandman to the television series Lucifer continue to mine the rich tension between Lucifer as cosmic villain, tragic rebel, and sympathetic anti-hero.
