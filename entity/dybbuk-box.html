Who It Is

In 2003, a wine cabinet listed on eBay ignited an international obsession, its seller claiming it contained a malevolent spirit known as a dybbuk. The box, allegedly purchased from a Holocaust survivor's estate, would go on to inspire a Hollywood horror franchise and spark endless debate: Is it a genuine artifact of the supernatural, a masterful piece of performance art, or something in between? The story of the Dybbuk Box is a modern legend, a collision of Jewish folklore, internet culture, and the enduring human desire to believe in the unseen.

The box is not a sentient being, but it is said to exhibit paranormal activity: emitting foul odors, causing nightmares, attracting flies, and influencing negative emotions in those nearby. It is believed to house a malevolent dybbuk that seeks to possess or torment individuals.

Powers
Possession

The box is said to contain a dybbuk that can possess individuals, causing them to act erratically, speak in strange voices, or exhibit unnatural strength.

Limit: The dybbuk's ability to possess may be limited to those who come into direct contact with the box or who are emotionally vulnerable.

Causing nightmares and disturbing dreams

Owners and visitors report vivid, terrifying nightmares, often involving shadowy figures or dying relatives.

Limit: No known limitations; subjective experience.

Emanating foul odors

The box is said to emit smells of rotting meat, cat urine, and jasmine flowers, often without any physical source.

Limit: Odors are intermittent and not always present.

Attracting negative entities or energy

The box is believed to attract other spirits or malevolent forces, amplifying negative emotions and events in its vicinity.

Limit: Unverified; anecdotal only.

Causing physical harm

Some accounts claim that the box causes scratches, bruises, or even serious injuries to those who disrespect it.

Limit: Reported incidents are rare and often unverified.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Dybbuk Box is a wooden wine cabinet, approximately 2 feet tall, with two doors and a drawer. It is described as having a vintage, mid-century design, possibly from the 1940s or 1950s. The wood is dark, and the interior contains two shelves and a small drawer. It is not inherently sinister in appearance; its reputation comes from its alleged supernatural inhabitants.

Symbols — The box itself bears no known symbols, but it is often depicted in media with Hebrew inscriptions or mystical imagery, though these are likely artistic embellishments. The concept of a 'dybbuk' has become iconic in horror, symbolizing unresolved trauma and the supernatural.

Domains — The box's physical domain is wherever it is kept. It has allegedly moved between owners: Kevin Mannis, Jason Haxton, and currently Zak Bagans, who displays it at his Haunted Museum in Las Vegas. The box's spiritual domain is the liminal space between the living and the dead, as per Jewish folklore.

Accounts I
The eBay ListingKevin Mannis's eBay auction

2003

Kevin Mannis, a Portland, Oregon antique dealer, listed a wine cabinet for sale on eBay, claiming it was haunted by a dybbuk. He described a series of disturbing events: nightmares, strange smells, and a sense of dread. The listing went viral, and the box was eventually sold to Jason Haxton. The listing launched the Dybbuk Box into internet fame, leading to media coverage, a book, and a film.

Accounts II
Jason Haxton's OrdealThe Dibbuk Box (book)

2003-2012

Jason Haxton, a medical professional, purchased the box and experienced a series of health problems, nightmares, and paranormal activity. He researched the box's origins, allegedly tracing it to a Holocaust survivor and a dybbuk that had been trapped. He eventually contacted a rabbi to perform an exorcism, but later transferred the box to Zak Bagans. Haxton's book documented his experiences and brought the box to wider attention.

Zak Bagans's AcquisitionThe Haunting of... The Dybbuk Box

2014-present

Zak Bagans, host of Ghost Adventures, acquired the box and displayed it in his Haunted Museum. He investigated it and claimed to have experienced paranormal phenomena. The box remains on display. The box became a permanent attraction, cementing its status as a modern horror icon.
