Who It Is

The Flat Earth is a fascinating and complex concept that has captured the imagination of people for centuries. From its origins in ancient Greece to its modern-day manifestations, the Flat Earth has been a source of debate, controversy, and intrigue.

The Flat Earth is often portrayed as a stubborn and unyielding concept, resistant to evidence and reason. In some stories, the Flat Earth is depicted as a malevolent force, seeking to deceive and mislead people.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Flat Earth is often depicted as a flat disc, with the horizon visible from any point on the surface. In some depictions, the Flat Earth is shown with a firmament above it, separating the Earth from the sky.

Symbols — The Flat Earth is often associated with the image of a flat disc, surrounded by a ring of water or a firmament. Other symbols associated with the Flat Earth include the image of a ship or a boat, representing the idea of a flat world.

Domains — The Flat Earth is often associated with the domains of mythology, folklore, and pseudoscience. It is also found in the domains of art, literature, and music.

Accounts I
The Story of Christopher Columbusstr.org, Columbus and the Flat Earth Myth

15th century

Christopher Columbus set sail across the Atlantic, facing disputes over the shape of the Earth. He believed in a spherical Earth, while many people believed in a flat Earth. Columbus's voyage led to the discovery of the Americas and the establishment of European colonies.

Accounts II
The Story of Galileo GalileiWikipedia, Galileo Galilei

17th century

Galileo Galilei challenged the idea of the flat Earth, using evidence from astronomy and mathematics to prove that the Earth is a sphere. Galileo's work led to the acceptance of the spherical Earth and the rejection of the flat Earth theory.
