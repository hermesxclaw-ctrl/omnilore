Who It Is

In the vast pantheon of Japanese kami, Ame no Oshihi is a ghost—a name whispered once in the ancient Kojiki and then lost to the centuries. Imagine a deity so obscure that his entire existence rests on a single line of text from 712 CE, a divine figure who accompanied the legendary first emperor Jimmu on his conquest of Yamato, yet left no other trace in myth, ritual, or art. He is the cosmic equivalent of an extra in a blockbuster film: present, purposeful, but utterly forgotten.

The Kojiki does not describe Ame no Oshihi's personality or behavior directly. As a supporting deity in the Jimmu myth cycle, he likely served as a protector or guide to the first emperor, but this is inference from the context rather than explicit statement. The role of Ame no Oshihi in the Jimmu narrative is consistent with a broader pattern in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki: heavenly kami dispatch help — birds, weapons, guidance, and lesser deities — to smooth the path of the imperial founder. Form & Signs

Appearance — No physical description of Ame no Oshihi survives in any known source. As a kami (deity) in the Shinto tradition, he would likely be depicted in anthropomorphic form in later artistic renderings, but no such depiction is attested. Given the solar element in his name, he may have been conceptualized as a radiant or luminous being, but this is pure speculation.

Symbols — No symbols are attested for Ame no Oshihi. Given the solar element in his name, the sun itself or solar imagery might be associated with him, but this is not confirmed by any source.

Domains — Based on the etymology, Ame no Oshihi may have been a solar deity or a celestial kami associated with endurance and perseverance. However, his actual domain of influence is not documented. He could have been a tutelary deity of a specific clan or a minor kami invoked for protection during journeys.

Accounts I
The Eastern Expedition of Emperor JimmuKojiki (712 CE)

Legendary period (traditionally dated to 660 BCE, but likely composed centuries later)

Ame no Oshihi appears as a minor divine figure in the epic narrative of Emperor Jimmu's journey from Kyushu to Yamato, where he established the imperial line. The Kojiki records that Ame no Oshihi was among the deities who accompanied the emperor, but does not elaborate on his specific actions. This is a single, brief mention in a much larger myth cycle. The mention of Ame no Oshihi contributes to the broader narrative of divine support for the imperial house, reinforcing the legitimacy of the Japanese imperial line.

Accounts II
The Guardian at the Kumano CrossingKojiki; Nihon Shoki; Chamberlain; Aston; Philippi.

Mythic age of Japan's first emperor; recorded in the eighth-century chronicles.

During Emperor Jimmu's eastward campaign from Kyushu toward Yamato, the imperiled expedition found aid from heavenly beings. At the dangerous crossing and the mountain approaches, divine assistance—often embodied by guardian kami and sacred birds—guided the emperor's forces through hostile terrain and provided sign and blessing. Ame no Oshihi, as a minor heavenly kami in this cycle, is associated with the protective presence that shadowed the imperial army, embodying the heavenly mandate (tenson) that sanctioned Jimmu's conquest and the founding of the Yamato state. The story grounds the Yamato dynasty's legitimacy in the blessing of the heavenly deities, showing that the perilous march to the east was underwritten by divine protection from the outset.
