The Approach

In the spring of 1993, a chestnut colt with a face like his great-damsire and a body like his sire stood at the starting gate of the Tōkyō Yūshun, carrying the weight of a jockey's 19-year dream. He would not just win that day; he would etch his name into the heart of Japanese racing as one-third of a legendary rivalry—the 'BNW' trio—and live long enough to become a living memory of an era.

Content advisory. No dark material; contains mention of equine injury and death.
Identity

Winning Ticket

Pronunciation WIN-ning TIK-et (Japanese: U-i-nin-gu Chi-ket-to)

Known As

ウイニングチケットUiningu Chiketto

Etymology

English phrase 'winning ticket', likely chosen for its auspicious meaning in a betting context.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese thoroughbred racing; part of the 'BNW' trio of the 1993 Triple Crown.

Disputed Identifications

None known; the name is unique to this horse.

Description

Appearance

Chestnut coat, with a body shape nearly identical to his sire Tony Bin and a face nearly identical to his great-damsire Tesco Boy. No other distinctive markings are recorded.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols; in Umamusume, his character design often includes a ticket motif.

Behaviour & Personality

In real life, no detailed personality is recorded beyond his racing demeanor, which was noted for his strong finish. In Umamusume, he is portrayed as straightforward, highly spirited, and prone to emotional outbursts, including crying.

Domains & Habitats

Raced primarily at Japanese tracks: Hakodate, Nakayama, Tokyo, Kyoto, and Hanshin. Stood at stud in Hokkaido, and retired to Urakawa Yushun Village AERU.

Version Differences

Real horse vs. Umamusume character: the latter is a fictionalized, anthropomorphic depiction with exaggerated personality traits.

Powers & Abilities

Racing speed and stamina

Source Version 1993 Tōkyō Yūshun

Description

Winning Ticket demonstrated strong speed and stamina, particularly in the Tōkyō Yūshun, where he ran the second-fastest time up to that point (2:25.5).

Limitations

Injuries (sesamoiditis as a yearling, tendinitis later) limited his career.

None.

Stories & Myths

The 1993 Triple Crown Rivalry

Source Contemporary race reportsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1993

Summary

Winning Ticket, Biwa Hayahide, and Narita Taishin formed the 'BNW' trio, dominating the Triple Crown races. Winning Ticket won the Yayoi Sho and Tōkyō Yūshun, while Biwa Hayahide won the Satsuki Shō and Kikuka-shō.
