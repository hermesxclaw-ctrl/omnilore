Who It Is

In the spring of 1993, a chestnut colt with a face like his great-damsire and a body like his sire stood at the starting gate of the Tōkyō Yūshun, carrying the weight of a jockey's 19-year dream. He would not just win that day; he would etch his name into the heart of Japanese racing as one-third of a legendary rivalry—the 'BNW' trio—and live long enough to become a living memory of an era.

In real life, no detailed personality is recorded beyond his racing demeanor, which was noted for his strong finish. In Umamusume, he is portrayed as straightforward, highly spirited, and prone to emotional outbursts, including crying.

Powers
Racing speed and stamina

Winning Ticket demonstrated strong speed and stamina, particularly in the Tōkyō Yūshun, where he ran the second-fastest time up to that point (2:25.5).

Limit: Injuries (sesamoiditis as a yearling, tendinitis later) limited his career.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Chestnut coat, with a body shape nearly identical to his sire Tony Bin and a face nearly identical to his great-damsire Tesco Boy. No other distinctive markings are recorded.

Symbols — No specific symbols; in Umamusume, his character design often includes a ticket motif.

Domains — Raced primarily at Japanese tracks: Hakodate, Nakayama, Tokyo, Kyoto, and Hanshin. Stood at stud in Hokkaido, and retired to Urakawa Yushun Village AERU.

Accounts I
The 1993 Triple Crown RivalryContemporary race reports

1993

Winning Ticket, Biwa Hayahide, and Narita Taishin formed the 'BNW' trio, dominating the Triple Crown races. Winning Ticket won the Yayoi Sho and Tōkyō Yūshun, while Biwa Hayahide won the Satsuki Shō and Kikuka-shō. Winning Ticket's Tōkyō Yūshun victory was a career highlight, and the rivalry became legendary in Japanese racing.

Accounts II
Retirement and LongevityUrakawa Yushun Village AERU

2005-2023

After retiring from stud, Winning Ticket lived at Urakawa Yushun Village AERU, becoming the oldest living G1 winner after Legacy World's death in 2021. He died at age 32 due to colic.
