Who It Is

Sir Bors (Bohort) is one of the three Grail achievers — alongside Galahad and Percival — and the only one who returns to Camelot to tell the tale. He is the younger son of King Bors of Gaul (Gaunnes) and the brother of Sir Lionel. Unlike the celestially pure Galahad and the personally transformed Percival, Bors is the Grail knight who achieves his quest through sheer moral discipline: he makes the right choices, passes the tests, and resists temptation through willpower rather than inherent sanctity. He is, in essence, the attainable human ideal — a man who is chaste, devout, and loyal, but who struggles with doubt and grief like any mortal.

Bors's Grail Quest is defined by two agonizing tests. First, he encounters a lady who claims she will kill herself and her maidens if Bors will not lie with her. She and her ladies climb a tower and threaten to jump. Bors, torn between his vow of chastity and his chivalric duty to save women, prays for guidance. The tower and ladies vanish in a burst of flame — it was a demonic illusion. Second, Bors must choose between rescuing his brother Lionel, who is being beaten by two knights, and a maiden who is being dragged away by another knight. Bors chooses the maiden — the more helpless victim — and prays for Lionel. Lionel survives but is enraged at what he perceives as Bors's betrayal and later attacks his brother. Bors refuses to fight back, even when Lionel nearly kills him, and a divine intervention (a hermit struck dead by a fireball) finally stops the fratricide.

Bors's third great trial is the vision of the Grail itself. At Corbenic, he witnesses the Grail Mass but, unlike Galahad, is not permitted to enter the deepest mysteries — only to see them and return. He returns to Camelot as the sole surviving Grail knight (Galahad dies in ecstasy, Percival becomes a hermit and dies shortly after). His testimony validates the quest: the Grail is real, the spiritual realm is real, and a mortal man — not a demigod, not a saint — can approach it.

After the Grail Quest, Bors remains a pillar of the dying Round Table. He supports Arthur in the war against Lancelot with great reluctance, knowing Lancelot to be his friend and the greatest knight living. After Camlann, he joins Lancelot in the hermitage, and at Lancelot's death, Bors buries his friend beside his brother Lionel. He then goes to the Holy Land, where he dies fighting against the Saracens — a crusader ending appropriate to the Grail knight who was always the warrior-saint rather than the mystic.

Bors's moral complexity — his status as a good man among saints, a chaste man among virgins, a survivor among martyrs — makes him one of the most human and sympathetic of the Arthurian knights. He is the Grail companion who has to work for his purity, and his reward is not rapture but the burden of testimony.
