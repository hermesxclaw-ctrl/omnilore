The Approach

The two letters 'vi' open more doors than almost any other pair in the digital world: they launch a text editor used by millions of programmers, name a champion in one of the most popular games on Earth, and echo through history as a Roman numeral, a chemical ghost, and a dozen different people and places. This page untangles the many lives of 'Vi' — from a 1970s Californian programmer's tool to a modern telecom brand, from a jazz saxophonist to a Vietnamese playwright.

Identity

Vi

Pronunciation In English, 'vi' is typically pronounced as 'vee' or 'vye' depending on context (e.g., 'vee-eye' for the editor, 'vee' for the name). In Latin, it is pronounced 'vee'.

Known As

VIviV.I.v.i.

Etymology

The term 'vi' has multiple origins: as a text editor, it is an abbreviation of 'visual' (from the visual mode of the ex editor); as a Roman numeral, it represents the number 6; in Latin, 'vide infra' means 'see below'; as a chemical symbol, it was proposed for the element now known as francium (from 'virginium'); as a given name, it is a diminutive of Violet.

Cultural Ownership

The term 'Vi' is not owned by any single culture; it is used globally in computing, linguistics, entertainment, and business.

Disputed Identifications

The abbreviation 'VI' can be ambiguous, referring to the British Virgin Islands, the United States Virgin Islands, or other entities depending on context. The element abbreviation 'Vi' was rejected and is not in current use.

Symbols & Iconography

The text editor 'vi' is often symbolized by its command-line interface; the League of Legends character Vi is depicted with large mechanical gauntlets; the telecom Vi uses a red and magenta logo.

Behaviour & Personality

The text editor is known for its modal interface and steep learning curve; the character Vi is known for her tough, street-fighter personality.

Domains & Habitats

The text editor exists in Unix-like operating systems; the character Vi exists in the fictional world of Runeterra; the telecom operates in India.

Version Differences

The text editor has many clones and derivatives (e.g., Vim, Neovim); the character Vi has different appearances across League of Legends, Arcane, and other media.

Stories & Myths

Origin of the vi text editor

Source Bill Joy's development at UC BerkeleyCulture Computer sciencePeriod 1976

Summary

Bill Joy created vi as a visual mode for the ex editor, which became part of BSD Unix. It became one of the most influential text editors in history.

Participants

Bill Joy

Consequences

vi became standard on Unix systems, influencing countless editors and spawning clones like Vim.

Variants

Vim, Neovim, Elvis, etc.
