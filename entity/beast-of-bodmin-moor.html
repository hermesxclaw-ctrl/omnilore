There's something on the moor that isn't supposed to be there. It's black as night, moves like liquid shadow, and it kills sheep -- not like a dog does, but like a big cat. Clean. Efficient.

Who It Is

The Beast of Bodmin Moor is Britain's most famous 'Alien Big Cat' (ABC) -- a large, predatory feline reported from the bleak moorlands of Cornwall. Described as a panther-like creature with sleek black fur, a long tail, and a size comparable to a large dog or small leopard (approximately 3 to 5 feet in body length), the Beast has been blamed for numerous livestock killings, particularly sheep, since the late 1970s. The first modern sighting wave began in 1978, and by the 1990s, reports had become so frequent that the UK government launched an official investigation. In 1995, the Ministry of Agriculture, Fisheries and Food conducted a six-month inquiry into the Beast of Bodmin, examining carcasses, plaster casts of tracks, and a famous 1994 video purportedly showing a large black cat on the moor. The official report concluded there was 'no verifiable evidence' of a big cat living wild on Bodmin Moor, though it acknowledged that an escaped exotic pet could theoretically survive. Despite the official conclusion, sightings and livestock predation attributed to the Beast have continued into the 21st century. The Beast of Bodmin is part of a broader UK phenomenon of 'Alien Big Cats' -- large, non-native felines reportedly sighted across Britain since the 1960s, following the 1976 Dangerous Wild Animals Act, which led some exotic pet owners to release their animals rather than comply with new licensing requirements. Skeptics attribute sightings to large domestic cats, dogs (especially black Labrador retrievers seen at a distance), foxes, or optical illusion.
