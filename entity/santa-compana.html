Do not look at the light. Lie face down upon the earth. We pass through your village because we never left it — we are your dead, and we are still your neighbors.

Who It Is

The Santa Compaña (Holy Company) is a spectral procession of the dead that wanders the rural roads and forest paths of Galicia at midnight. Led by a living person carrying a cross and a cauldron of holy water — usually cursed to this role as penance — the Compaña consists of shrouded souls holding candles whose light can be seen from afar but emits no heat. They march in silence, and anyone who encounters them must either join the procession (if they lock eyes with the leader) or avoid them by lying face-down, drawing a protective circle, or carrying a black cat. The Compaña visits the homes of the soon-to-die, circling them three times, and the doomed person's soul joins the procession after death. The leader is freed only when they pass the cross and cauldron to another unfortunate soul. Unlike the Wild Hunt of Germanic folklore (aggressive, punitive), the Santa Compaña is more mournful and inevitable — it is the community of the dead maintaining its connection to the living, a village of ghosts processing through the same paths they walked in life. The tradition is deeply embedded in Galician identity, connected to the region's Celtic heritage, its dense forests, and its foggy, melancholic landscape. The Santa Compaña is also called 'Estadea' or 'As da Noite' (Those of the Night), and its rituals of avoidance remain a living folk tradition in rural Galicia.
