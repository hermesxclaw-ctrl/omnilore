Who It Is

In the dark forests of the Hunsrück, in what is now western Germany, a Roman-era traveler once scratched a plea into stone: 'To the god Arsilunnus.' That is all. No image, no myth, no list of miracles — just a name, echoing in the silence of two thousand years. Arsilunnus is a ghost in the Celtic pantheon, a deity known from a single broken altar, a reminder that for every famous god like Cernunnos or Lugus, there were hundreds of local spirits whose stories were lost when their last worshipper died. He is less a god to be understood and more a mystery to be contemplated.

Unknown. As a theonym attested only in a votive inscription, no myths or behavioral traits are recorded.

Appearance — No iconographic representation of Arsilunnus survives. The deity is known solely from the epigraphic record. Any description of appearance is purely conjectural.

Symbols — None attested. The absence of any accompanying symbol or iconography is a significant limitation.
