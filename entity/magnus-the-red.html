Who It Is

He was the Emperor's brightest son, a sorcerer-king who sought to master the Warp itself—and in doing so, damned his Legion, his homeworld, and ultimately himself. Magnus the Red's fall is a tragedy of pride, forbidden knowledge, and a bargain struck with a god of change.

Magnus is portrayed as highly intelligent, learned, and arrogant. He is a scholar and sorcerer, valuing knowledge above all. He genuinely cares for his sons, the Thousand Sons, and seeks to protect them from their genetic flaw (the Flesh Change). His hubris leads him to believe he can control the Warp and bargain with Tzeentch without being corrupted. He is tragic in that his actions, intended to help the Imperium, ultimately doom it.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Magnus' is Latin for 'great', fitting his immense psychic power and stature. 'The Red' refers to his crimson skin and his Legion's red armor. 'Cyclops' derives from his single, all-seeing eye.

Pronunciation — MAG-nus thuh RED

Aliases — The Crimson King, The Cyclops, The Red Cyclops, Primarch of the Thousand Sons, Daemon Primarch of Tzeentch

Powers
Psychic Mastery

Magnus is one of the most powerful psykers in the galaxy, second only to the Emperor. He can manipulate the Warp on a massive scale, cast devastating psychic powers, and communicate across interstellar distances.

Limit: Psychic powers can be dangerous and attract daemonic attention. Magnus's own hubris and the nature of Tzeentch's bargain limit his true potential.

Daemonic Ascension

After his transformation into a Daemon Prince of Tzeentch, Magnus gains immortality, supernatural durability, and the ability to manifest in the material realm through rituals or when summoned.

Limit: As a daemon, his physical form can be banished but not permanently destroyed. He is bound to the whims of Tzeentch and the Warp.

Immense Physical Prowess

Like all primarchs, Magnus possesses superhuman strength, speed, and resilience, making him a formidable warrior even without his psychic powers.

Limit: His physical abilities are secondary to his psychic might.

Master Sorcerer

He knows countless arcane rituals and spells, including those that manipulate time, space, and reality. He can create warp rifts, teleport, and alter the minds of others.

Limit: Sorcery often requires sacrifices and risks daemonic possession.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Magnus is a towering primarch, standing well over nine feet tall. His skin is a deep red, and he possesses a single, cyclopean eye that glows with psychic power. His hair is long and black, often depicted flowing. He wears ornate power armor, typically crimson and gold, adorned with arcane symbols and scrollwork. As a Daemon Primarch, his form is mutable and often wreathed in psychic fire, with wings and other daemonic features. He carries a mass

Symbols — The Eye of Horus (ironic), the symbol of the Thousand Sons (a stylized eye), the Pyramid of Photep, the Crimson King iconography, and Tzeentch's sigils. His armor often bears Egyptian-like motifs, reflecting his homeworld's culture.

Domains — Originally from the planet Prospero, a world of scholars and psykers. After his fall, he resides in the Warp, specifically the realm of Tzeentch, the Crystal Labyrinth. He also has a presence on the daemon world of Sortiarius, the Planet of the Sorcerers, which was once Prospero.

Accounts I
The Rise of Magnus and the Thousand SonsCodex: Thousand Sons, A Thousand Sons

Great Crusade (30k)

Magnus is discovered on Prospero by the Emperor. He becomes the primarch of the Thousand Sons Legion. His Legion suffers from the Flesh Change, a genetic mutation that turns them into mutants. Magnus seeks to cure them through sorcery, despite the Emperor's prohibition on psychic powers. He discovers the existence of the Warp and daemons, and believes he can control them. The Emperor warns Magnus against using sorcery. Magnus's dabbling leads to a pact with Tzeentch, who offers to cure the Flesh Change in exchange for his loyalty.

The Council of NikaeaA Thousand Sons, The Horus Heresy novels

30k

The Emperor convenes a council to decide the fate of psykers in the Legions. Magnus argues for the use of psychic powers, but the anti-psyker faction, led by Mortarion, wins. The Emperor decrees that the Legions must disband their Librarius divisions. Magnus reluctantly complies but continues his research in secret. This deepens Magnus's resentment and drives him to further secrecy.

Accounts II
The Warning to TerraA Thousand Sons, The Burning of Prospero

30k

Magnus learns of Horus's betrayal and attempts to warn the Emperor via a psychic projection. However, his spell is intercepted by Tzeentch, and he inadvertently breaches the Emperor's wards on the Golden Throne, causing catastrophic damage. This act is seen as a betrayal by the Emperor. The Emperor orders Leman Russ and the Space Wolves to bring Magnus to Terra, but Russ is tricked into destroying Prospero instead.

The Burning of ProsperoThe Burning of Prospero, Prospero Burns

30k

Leman Russ, believing Magnus has turned traitor, leads the Space Wolves in a devastating attack on Prospero. Magnus, torn between defending his world and avoiding a war against the Emperor, eventually fights back but is defeated. Rather than be captured, he makes a final bargain with Tzeentch, transforming into a Daemon Prince and fleeing into the Warp. Prospero is destroyed, the Thousand Sons are scattered and corrupted, and Magnus becomes a Daemon Prince of Tzeentch.

The Siege of TerraThe Horus Heresy novels (e.g., Fury of Magnus)

30k

During the Horus's siege of Terra, Magnus confronts the Emperor. He is offered a chance to return to the loyalist side, but he refuses, remaining loyal to Tzeentch. He battles the Emperor and is ultimately banished. Magnus's defeat solidifies his status as a Daemon Primarch.
