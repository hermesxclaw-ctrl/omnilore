Who It Is

Paul Klee was a Swiss-German artist whose playful, childlike paintings belied a rigorous mind—a musician who heard color, a philosopher who drew with wit, and a Bauhaus master whose theories reshaped modern art.

Klee was known for his dry wit, intellectual curiosity, and disciplined work ethic. He was deeply introspective, keeping diaries and writing extensively on art theory. He was also a talented musician, and music profoundly influenced his art. He was politically cautious but morally principled, as evidenced by his response to the Nazi regime.

Powers
Color Theory Mastery

Klee developed a sophisticated understanding of color, influenced by Delaunay and others. He taught color theory at the Bauhaus and wrote extensively on the subject, exploring the emotional and symbolic potential of color.

Limit: His early work showed a lack of natural color sense, which he struggled to overcome.

Drawing and Draftsmanship

Klee was a natural draftsman, skilled in line and volume from an early age. His drawings are precise and expressive, often blending caricature with abstraction.

Limit: He initially felt inadequate as a painter, focusing on drawing.

Musicality

Klee was a talented violinist, and his art often reflects musical structures, rhythms, and harmonies. He used visual elements to evoke musical qualities.

Limit: He chose visual arts over music as a career.

Art Theory and Pedagogy

Klee's writings and lectures on art theory, compiled in the Paul Klee Notebooks, are considered seminal, comparable to Leonardo da Vinci's treatise. He taught at the Bauhaus and influenced generations of artists.

Limit: His theoretical writings are complex and require careful study.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Klee was of slight build, with a distinctive mustache and often wore round-rimmed glasses. He had a thoughtful, introspective demeanor, often appearing serious in photographs, but his art reveals a playful and humorous side.

Symbols — Klee's visual language includes abstract forms, geometric shapes, fish, birds, plants, and musical motifs. He often used letters and numbers as graphic elements. His work is characterized by a childlike perspective and a sense of whimsy, yet it is underpinned by careful composition and color theory.

Domains — Klee lived and worked in Switzerland and Germany, particularly in Munich, Weimar, Dessau, and Düsseldorf. He traveled to Italy, Tunisia, and Paris, which influenced his use of color and light. He was associated with the Bauhaus school and later the Düsseldorf Academy.

Accounts I
Early Life and Musical BeginningsBiographies, diaries

1879-1898

Klee was born into a musical family and showed early talent on the violin. Despite parental pressure to pursue music, he chose visual arts, partly out of rebellion and partly because he found modern music lacking. He began studying art in Munich in 1898. Set the stage for his artistic career and lifelong engagement with music and art.

The Tunisian Turning PointDiaries, letters

1914

Klee traveled to Tunisia with August Macke and Louis Moilliet. The intense light and color of North Africa transformed his approach to color, leading him to write, 'Color has taken possession of me.' This trip is considered a pivotal moment in his artistic development. Klee embraced color as a primary expressive element, evolving toward abstraction.

Accounts II
Bauhaus YearsBiographies, Bauhaus records

1921-1931

Klee taught at the Bauhaus school in Weimar and Dessau, where he collaborated with Kandinsky and others. He developed his color theory and published his notebooks. His work during this period is characterized by geometric abstraction and playful forms. Klee became a leading figure in modern art education and produced many iconic works.

Persecution and Later LifeBiographies, historical records

1933-1940

After the Nazis came to power, Klee was labeled a 'degenerate artist,' lost his teaching position, and had works removed from museums. He moved to Switzerland, where he continued to work despite declining health. His later works reflect a darker, more somber tone. Klee's reputation was suppressed in Germany, but he continued to create art until his death in 1940.
