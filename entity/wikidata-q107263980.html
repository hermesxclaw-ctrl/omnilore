The Approach

Paul Klee was a Swiss-German artist whose playful, childlike paintings belied a rigorous mind—a musician who heard color, a philosopher who drew with wit, and a Bauhaus master whose theories reshaped modern art.

Content advisory. No dark material directly related to Klee's life or work is presented. However, his art and writings are situated within the context of early 20th-century European culture, including colonial-era attitudes and the rise of Nazism, which led to his art being labeled 'degenerate.'
Identity

Paul Klee

Pronunciation German: [ˈpaʊ̯l ˈkleː]

Known As

Paul Klee (birth name)KleeP. Klee

Etymology

The surname 'Klee' is of German origin, meaning 'clover' (from Middle High German 'kle').

Cultural Ownership

Swiss-born German artist; associated with German Expressionism, Bauhaus, and modern art. His nationality is often described as Swiss-German, reflecting his Swiss birth and German artistic career.

Disputed Identifications

There is no significant dispute over Klee's identity, though some may misidentify him as purely Swiss or purely German. His artistic style is sometimes conflated with other Bauhaus artists, but his unique visual language is well documented.

Description

Appearance

Klee was of slight build, with a distinctive mustache and often wore round-rimmed glasses. He had a thoughtful, introspective demeanor, often appearing serious in photographs, but his art reveals a playful and humorous side.

Symbols & Iconography

Klee's visual language includes abstract forms, geometric shapes, fish, birds, plants, and musical motifs. He often used letters and numbers as graphic elements. His work is characterized by a childlike perspective and a sense of whimsy, yet it is underpinned by careful composition and color theory.

Behaviour & Personality

Klee was known for his dry wit, intellectual curiosity, and disciplined work ethic. He was deeply introspective, keeping diaries and writing extensively on art theory. He was also a talented musician, and music profoundly influenced his art. He was politically cautious but morally principled, as evidenced by his response to the Nazi regime.

Domains & Habitats

Klee lived and worked in Switzerland and Germany, particularly in Munich, Weimar, Dessau, and Düsseldorf. He traveled to Italy, Tunisia, and Paris, which influenced his use of color and light. He was associated with the Bauhaus school and later the Düsseldorf Academy.

Version Differences

Klee's artistic style evolved over time, from early etchings and satirical works to later abstract and color-focused pieces. His time in Tunisia marked a turning point in his use of color. His later works, created under the shadow of Nazi persecution, became more somber and symbolic.

Powers & Abilities

Color Theory Mastery

Source Version Bauhaus period and later

Description

Klee developed a sophisticated understanding of color, influenced by Delaunay and others. He taught color theory at the Bauhaus and wrote extensively on the subject, exploring the emotional and symbolic potential of color.

Limitations

His early work showed a lack of natural color sense, which he struggled to overcome.

None noted.

Early Life and Musical Beginnings

Source Biographies, diariesCulture Swiss-GermanPeriod 1879-1898

Summary

Klee was born into a musical family and showed early talent on the violin. Despite parental pressure to pursue music, he chose visual arts, partly out of rebellion and partly because he found modern music lacking. He began studying art in Munich in 1898.

The Tunisian Turning Point

Source Diaries, lettersCulture European, North AfricanPeriod 1914

Summary

Klee traveled to Tunisia with August Macke and Louis Moilliet. The intense light and color of North Africa transformed his approach to color, leading him to write, 'Color has taken possession of me.' This trip is considered a pivotal moment in his artistic development.

Participants

Paul Klee

August Macke

Louis Moilliet

Consequences

Klee embraced color as a primary expressive element, evolving toward abstraction.

Variants

Some accounts emphasize the influence of Macke and Moilliet.

Bauhaus Years

Source Biographies, Bauhaus recordsCulture GermanPeriod 1921-1931

Summary

Klee taught at the Bauhaus school in Weimar and Dessau, where he collaborated with Kandinsky and others. He developed his color theory and published his notebooks. His work during this period is characterized by geometric abstraction and playful forms.

Participants

Persecution and Later Life

Source Biographies, historical recordsCulture German, SwissPeriod 1933-1940

Summary

After the Nazis came to power, Klee was labeled a 'degenerate artist,' lost his teaching position, and had works removed from museums. He moved to Switzerland, where he continued to work despite declining health. His later works reflect a darker, more somber tone.

Participants

Consequences

Klee's reputation was suppressed in Germany, but he continued to create art until his death in 1940.

Degenerate Art Label

Source Historical records, biographies

Description

The Nazi regime condemned Klee's art as 'degenerate' (Entartete Kunst), leading to the removal of his works from German museums and his dismissal from the Düsseldorf Academy.

Scholarly Context

This was part of a broader Nazi campaign against modern art, which they viewed as un-German and morally corrupt.

Reception & Afterlife

Klee's works have been exhibited worldwide, including major retrospectives at the Museum of Modern Art, New York, and the Zentrum Paul Klee, Bern.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nationality

Position A

Klee is often described as Swiss.

Position A — evidence

He was born in Switzerland and later returned there.

Position B

Klee is described as German due to his artistic career in Germany.

Position B — evidence

He studied, worked, and taught in Germany for most of his life.

Scholarly Consensus

He is generally referred to as Swiss-German, acknowledging both his Swiss birth and German artistic identity.

Artistic Style

Position A

Klee is categorized as an Expressionist.

Position A — evidence

His involvement with the Blaue Reiter and his emotional use of color.

Position B

Klee is associated with Cubism and Surrealism.

Position B — evidence

His use of geometric abstraction and dreamlike imagery.

Scholarly Consensus

Klee is seen as a unique figure who drew from multiple movements but defied simple categorization.
