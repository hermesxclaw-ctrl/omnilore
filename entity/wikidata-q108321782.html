Who It Is

On a Fremantle street corner in 2007, two buskers with more ambition than gear set out to buy a horn section one coin at a time — and ended up building a nine-piece funk-hip-hop brass orchestra that would sell out 800-cap rooms, win Western Australia's biggest music prizes, and turn a busking hustle into one of Perth's most ecstatic live acts.

Attested behavior: jam-based origins evolving into structured funk, hip-hop, electronic and dance fusion; described as a live-focused act with residency and festival strategy. Won both 'Best Funk Act' (WAM) and 'Best Hip-Hop Act' / 'Best Live Act' (PDMAs), indicating genre-fluid reception. Accounts I
Busking Duo Origins and Gear HustleBand career chronology (supplied source material)

2007-2008

Nicholas Owen and Karri Harper Meredith begin as a busking duo in Fremantle's business district in 2007, with the explicit aim of raising money for expensive musical equipment, lights, and PA gear 'excessive to the band's member ratio.' They play largely jam-based material and early versions of songs that later enter The Brow's touring repertoire. Their first major show is the band competition AmpFest, which serves as a networking tool for local gigs. Establishes DIY funding model and jam-based songwriting foundation; enables expansion to full band and secures local gig network.

National Campus Band Competition RunBand career chronology

2008-2009

In 2008, Owen and Harper Meredith add Alex Vaughan aka Quills (later Emcee Rezide) and guitarist/bassist Rhian Todhunter to compete in the National Campus Band Competition. The four-piece reaches state finals in 2008. Expanded to seven with Sky Eaton (trumpet/vocals/synths), Angelique Ross (clarinet/tenor sax), and Lyndon Blue (violin/bass/guitar/vocals), they return in 2009 to win the Western Australian state final and are flown to Sea World on the Gold Coast, Queensland for the national finals. State win provides first significant interstate exposure and solidifies expanded horn/string lineup; directly precedes formalization as Brow Horn Orchestra.

Becoming the Brow Horn Orchestra - RTRFM BreakthroughBand career chronology; RTRFM Seriously Sound System festival record

Late 2009 - mid 2010

In late November 2009 drummer Ben Lanzon joins and the band renames to The Brow Horn Orchestra. Debut with new name/lineup at RTRFM's 'Seriously Sound System' summer dance festival (Dec 2009) where they debut 'Goliath,' later their first leading single. They build momentum via Western Australian Circus Festival (2010) and a month-long residency at Leederville Hotel's Funk Club (March 2010). Lauren Todhunter (alto sax/percussion) and Jake Chaloner (electric guitar) join; Wafi Zainal (tuba) becomes regular guest. In May 2010 they are nominated and asked to perform at the West Australian Music Industry Awards, winning Best Funk Act (industry-nominated). Demo 'Dreams Do Come & Go' receives Triple J airtime ahead of the awards. In July 2010 Rhian Todhunter departs; Jake and Lyndon absorb bass/guitar duties. In August 2010 they win the Next Big Thing Competition (previous winners John Butler, Tame Impala, Snowman), with street press calling them Perth's next big thing regardless. They are taken on by JumpClimb Music and Management after a Coopers Sunday Green (lawn bowls + live music) show

Two Fires EP and National Showcase CircuitBand career chronology; MGM Distribution record

Early 2012 - late 2012

With new 5-piece core (Owen, Harper Meredith, Lanzon, Eaton, Fear), the band spends early 2012 writing follow-up EP 'Two Fires,' described as more production-heavy due to lineup changes. Released late June 2012 with lead single 'Fade' receiving press and Triple J rave reviews. EP picked up by MGM Distribution for national physical/digital distribution and receives positive media/blog coverage. Band undertakes several national tours supporting the record and plays showcase gigs at industry conferences Fuse (Adelaide) and BigSound (Brisbane). Second EP extends national distribution; showcase appearances position band for industry exposure beyond Western Australia; production-heavy direction confirmed.

2013-present (chronology ends 2013)

After touring 'Two Fires' through 2013 across festivals and national shows, The Brow Horn Orchestra changes name back to The Brow ahead of recording their next record. No further releases, awards, or tours are detailed in supplied material after 2013, creating a gap from 2013 to present.
