Who It Is

Imagine a god who lives in the sky, a silent, powerful force watching over his people from the heights of a volcano. This is Achamán, the supreme deity of the Guanches, the indigenous people of Tenerife. He was a creator, a protector, and a judge, a being of immense power who could call down the sun or imprison a fire demon within the earth. His story is a testament to the rich, complex spiritual life of a people who were tragically conquered and their culture nearly erased.

Achamán was believed to be a benevolent creator and protector, who provided for the Guanches and maintained the cosmic order. He was invoked in times of need, particularly during droughts or conflicts. He was also a moral authority, rewarding the righteous and punishing the wicked, though the specifics of this judgment are not well-documented.

Powers
Creation

Achamán was credited with the creation of the world, the heavens, and the Guanches themselves. He was the ultimate source of all life and existence.

Limit: The creation narrative is not detailed in surviving sources, so the exact scope and method of his creation are unknown.

Providence and Judgment

He was believed to intervene in human affairs, providing rain, fertility, and protection. He also acted as a moral judge, rewarding the virtuous and punishing the wicked, both in life and in the afterlife.

Limit: The mechanisms of his judgment and the criteria for reward/punishment are not detailed in the sources.

Antagonism with Guayota

Achamán was locked in a cosmic struggle with Guayota, an evil being associated with fire and the underworld. Achamán eventually triumphed, imprisoning Guayota within the volcano Teide.

Limit: This narrative is fragmentary and likely contains later Christian-influenced elements of a devil figure.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No physical representation of Achamán is attested in any source. The Guanches likely did not create images of him, as their worship was centered on natural elements and celestial bodies. He was conceived as an invisible, omnipresent force residing in the sky, rather than an anthropomorphic figure.

Symbols — The sky itself and the sun were the primary symbols associated with Achamán. The almogarens, open-air sanctuaries, are also considered symbols of his worship. No specific animal or object is definitively linked to him, though the sun (Magec) is sometimes seen as an aspect or manifestation of his power.

Domains — Sky, heavens, creation, cosmic order, divine providence, justice

The Imprisonment of GuayotaPost-conquest folklore, chronicles such as Galindo

Pre-conquest (myth), recorded post-conquest

Guayota, a malevolent being, was said to have stolen the sun (Magec), plunging the world into darkness. The Guanches prayed to Achamán for help. Achamán confronted Guayota, defeated him, and imprisoned him inside the volcano Teide. He then restored the sun to its proper place in the sky. This myth explained the presence of volcanic activity in Teide, which was seen as Guayota's frustrated efforts to escape. It also established Achamán as the ultimate protector of the cosmic order.

Accounts II
The Creation of the GuanchesPost-conquest chronicles, oral tradition

Pre-conquest (myth), recorded post-conquest

Achamán was credited with the creation of the first Guanches. The exact details of this creation are lost, but he was seen as their divine progenitor and protector. This belief gave the Guanches a strong sense of divine origin and a special relationship with their god, who was intimately connected to their land and identity.
