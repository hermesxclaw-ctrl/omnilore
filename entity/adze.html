The name 'Adze' is from the Ewe language; its exact linguistic roots are not well-documented in available sources.

Disputed Identifications

Some sources conflate the Adze with other vampiric entities in African folklore, but it is distinct in its firefly form and specific cultural context.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the twilight forests of the Ewe people, a flickering light among the trees might not be a firefly—it could be the Adze, a vampiric being that shifts between human and insect form, feeding on the lifeblood of the unwary.

The adze is the vampire of the Ewe people of Ghana and Togo, and its genius is its size: it flies through the night not as a bat or a shadow but as a firefly — a drifting, pretty spark that slips through any crack, under any door, past any defense sized for something monstrous. Inside the house it feeds, drinking blood from sleepers — children above all — who then sicken and waste with what the modern clinic calls malaria and the old knowledge called the adze's kiss: the insect-borne night-death mapped onto folklore with terrible accuracy.

Who It Is

The adze is the vampire of the Ewe people of Ghana and Togo, and its genius is its size: it flies through the night not as a bat or a shadow but as a firefly — a drifting, pretty spark that slips through any crack, under any door, past any defense sized for something monstrous. Inside the house it feeds, drinking blood from sleepers — children above all — who then sicken and waste with what the modern clinic calls malaria and the old knowledge called the adze's kiss: the insect-borne night-death mapped onto folklore with terrible accuracy. Caught in the act — trapped in a jar, swatted from the air — the adze transforms into its true shape: a hunched, black-skinned humanoid horror. And here the adze becomes something darker than a monster: in its human-linked form it possesses, riding witches (adzetsi) within the community, so that the hunt for the firefly became, historically, a hunt for neighbors. The adze cannot be bargained with and has no taste for anything but blood, palm oil, and coconut water — the pantry of the region reimagined as a vampire's diet. Its lesson is the one the mosquito net finally answered: the deadliest thing in the dark is small, glowing, and already inside.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

The Adze is described as a humanoid witch who can transform into a firefly-like insect, often emitting a glowing light. In human form, it may appear as an ordinary person, but its true nature is revealed at night.

Symbols & Iconography

Firefly imagery is central; the Adze is associated with darkness, night, and the forest.

Behaviour & Personality

The Adze is malevolent, preying on humans, especially children, to drink their blood. It is cunning and deceptive, often luring victims with its light. It can be captured and forced to reveal its human identity.

Domains & Habitats

Forests and rural areas of Ewe territory; active at night.

Version Differences

Variants exist where the Adze is a spirit rather than a witch, or where it can be controlled by a human master. Some accounts say it can be killed by burning its human form.

Death & Rising

Ewe witchcraft-and-vampire tradition of the Volta region, encoding insect-vector disease, child mortality, and communal witchcraft anxiety into a single luminous predator.

Attested in Ewe oral traditionEra Precolonial to present

Archive Dossier — Powers

Shapeshifting

Source Version General Ewe folklore

Description

The Adze can transform between human and firefly forms, and possibly other animals.

Limitations

Transformation is often limited to night; in human form, it may be vulnerable.

Contradictions

Some accounts say it can only shift at night; others suggest it can do so anytime.

Blood consumption

Source Version General Ewe folklore

Description

The Adze feeds on human blood, often from sleeping victims, sometimes causing illness or death.

Limitations

Must feed periodically; may be weakened if deprived.

Contradictions

None noted.

Flight

Source Version General Ewe folklore

Description

In firefly form, the Adze can fly, allowing it to travel quickly and evade capture.

Limitations

Flight is limited to insect form.

Contradictions

None noted.

Form

A firefly's wandering spark in the dark house; cornered, a squat black manikin with hooked fingers — and in its social form, the ordinary face of a possessed neighbor.

Powers

Insect-form infiltration through any gap, blood-feeding that wastes children, possession of human hosts, and the seeding of witchcraft accusation — a monster that attacks the body and the community in the same night.

Weaknesses & Wards

It must be caught transformed to be killed; nets and sealed rooms defeat the firefly; and communal rites cleanse the possessed — remedies that map, one to one, onto screens, sanitation, and reconciliation.

Accusations of witchcraft

Source Meyer 1999

Description

People, often women, were accused of being Adze and faced violence or ostracism.

Scholarly Context

Witchcraft accusations in Ewe society have been studied as a form of social control and scapegoating.

The Firefly Witch

Source Oral tradition, collected by EllisCulture EwePeriod 19th century

Summary

A woman suspected of being an Adze is caught when a hunter captures a firefly that enters her house; the firefly transforms back into the woman, revealing her identity.

Variants
