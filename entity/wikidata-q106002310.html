The Approach

In the early 1990s, a chestnut colt with a machine-like precision stormed through Japan's racing world, winning seven consecutive races and capturing the nation's imagination. Known as the 'Cyborg' and the 'Chestnut Super Express,' Mihono Bourbon came within a single length of achieving the Japanese Triple Crown, only to be denied by a rival who would become his eternal counterpart. His story is one of remarkable speed, tragic injury, and a legacy that transcends the racetrack.

Identity

Mihono Bourbon

Pronunciation MEE-hoh-noh BOUR-bon (Japanese: ミホノブルボン, Mihono Burubon)

Known As

Mihono Burubon (Japanese transliteration)CyborgChestnut Super Express

Etymology

Named by owner Miura Shoji after the Bourbon dynasty that ruled France in the 16th century. The name 'Mihono' likely derives from the owner's prefix or farm name.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese thoroughbred racing culture, specifically the early 1990s era. The horse is a beloved figure in Japanese horse racing history.

Disputed Identifications

None known. The name is unique to this racehorse, though the anthropomorphized character in Umamusume is a separate fictional representation.

Description

Appearance

Chestnut coat, hence the nickname 'Chestnut Super Express.' No other physical details are provided in the source.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are documented. His nickname 'Cyborg' reflects his perceived machine-like racing style.

Behaviour & Personality

During training, he disliked people approaching during meals and preferred solitude. On the track, he was a front-runner, maintaining the lead and handling slopes with seemingly machine-like ability.

Domains & Habitats

Trained at Ritto Training Center, raced at various Japanese racecourses including Chukyo, Tokyo, Nakayama, and Kyoto. Retired to Hidaka Light Horse Farming Cooperative and later Smile Farm.

Version Differences

The real horse differs from the fictional Umamusume character, who is an anthropomorphized girl with a robotic demeanor. The source notes this adaptation.
