The Approach

The name Lycus—Greek for 'wolf'—echoes through myth, geography, and modern fiction, belonging to heroes, tyrants, rivers, and even a Marvel villain. But who was the first Lycus, and why does this name persist across millennia?

Content advisory. Contains mythological violence, including murder, betrayal, and usurpation, as found in ancient Greek sources.
Identity

Lycus

Pronunciation LY-kus (English), LOO-kos (classical Greek)

Known As

LykosLycos

Etymology

From Ancient Greek Λύκος, meaning 'wolf'.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology and ancient geography; also used in modern fiction.

Disputed Identifications

Multiple distinct mythological figures share the name; some may be conflated in later sources.

Description

Appearance

As a mythological figure, no consistent physical description is given; he is typically depicted as a mortal man, often a king or hero. In art, he may appear as a warrior.

Symbols & Iconography

The wolf is the primary symbol associated with the name, though not necessarily depicted with each figure.

Behaviour & Personality

Varies by figure: some are virtuous heroes, others are tyrants or villains.

Domains & Habitats

Various locations in Greece and Asia Minor, including Thebes, Athens, and Lycia.

Version Differences

There are at least five distinct mythological Lycus figures, each with different genealogies and stories.
