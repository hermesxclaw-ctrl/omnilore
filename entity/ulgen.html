Who It Is

High in the celestial vault of the Turkic cosmos, where the sky meets the sacred mountain, dwells Ülgen—the radiant creator, the giver of life, the judge of souls. Unlike the chthonic Erlik who rules the underworld, Ülgen is the benevolent sky god whose light nurtures the world and whose judgment guides the spirits of the dead. His presence is felt in the thunder that shakes the heavens, in the rain that blesses the steppe, and in the shaman's ecstatic flight to his golden throne.

Ülgen is benevolent, wise, and just. He is the creator of the world, the giver of life, and the protector of humanity. He rewards the good and punishes the wicked, both in life and after death. He is approachable through shamanic rituals, but he is also distant and transcendent. He is not capricious but acts according to cosmic order.

Powers
Creation

Ülgen created the earth, the sky, and all living beings. In the Altai creation myth, he dives into the primordial sea to bring up earth, or he commands a bird or a devil to do so. He breathed life into the first human, or formed him from clay or reeds.

Limit: The act of creation is sometimes shared with a subordinate being (e.g., Erlik or a bird), and the material for creation is often obtained from the devil, implying that Ülgen is not omnipotent but works with pre-existing matter.

Weather Control

Ülgen controls thunder, lightning, rain, and wind. He sends storms as punishment or to cleanse the world. Shamans invoke him to bring rain during droughts.

Limit: His weather powers are sometimes delegated to lesser spirits (e.g., the 'yzyr' or 'kut' spirits). He may withhold rain if displeased.

Judgment of Souls

After death, souls ascend to Ülgen's realm to be judged. The righteous dwell in his heavenly abode, while the wicked are sent to Erlik's underworld.

Limit: The judgment is not described in detail; it may be a simple division based on one's deeds. Some traditions say that a shaman must guide the soul to Ülgen.

Granting Shamanic Power

Ülgen is the source of shamanic power. Shamans ascend to his realm to receive their calling, their drum, and their spirit helpers. He may also grant them the ability to heal, divine, and communicate with the spirits.

Limit: Not all shamans are chosen by Ülgen; some are called by Erlik or other spirits. The shaman's power is conditional on his or her purity and adherence to tradition.

Blessing and Fertility

Ülgen blesses families with children, livestock, and prosperity. He is invoked in rituals for fertility and abundance.

Limit: His blessings are contingent on proper worship and sacrifice.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ülgen is generally not described in anthropomorphic detail, but when he appears, he is envisioned as an old man with a white beard, often seated on a golden throne in the highest heaven. He may be surrounded by light or fire. In some shamanic invocations, he is said to have a shining face and to ride a white horse or a celestial eagle. The Yakut Yryn-ai-tojon is described as a white-bearded old man in white clothing, dwelling in the east.

Symbols — Key symbols associated with Ülgen include: the sky and the celestial vault; the sun and the moon; the sacred mountain (often the world mountain); the white horse (sacrificed to him); the birch tree (used in shamanic rituals to reach his realm); the eagle (as a messenger or his steed); the color white (purity, holiness); and the number nine (the nine heavens).

Domains — Ülgen dwells in the highest of the nine (or seventeen) heavens, on a golden mountain, near a golden palace. He is the lord of the sky, the celestial realm, and all that is above the earth. He controls the weather, especially thunder, lightning, and rain. He is also the ruler of the upper world, where the souls of the righteous go after death.

Accounts I
The Creation of the WorldAltai oral tradition, recorded by Radlov and others

Unknown (pre-Islamic, recorded in 19th century)

In the beginning, there was only water. Ülgen, or God, flew over the water in the form of a black goose or a bird. He saw a devil (Erlik) swimming, and commanded him to dive into the water and bring up mud. Erlik brought up mud, but hid some in his mouth. Ülgen took the mud and created the earth. When Erlik tried to spit out the hidden mud, it created mountains, swamps, and other imperfections. Ülgen then created the first human, either from clay or from a reed, and breathed life into him. Erlik, jealous, tried to corrupt the human, leading to the origin of evil and death. The world is created, but with imperfections due to Erlik's deception. Humanity is mortal and susceptible to evil. The stage is set for the eternal conflict between good and evil.

Accounts II
The First ShamanAltai oral tradition

Unknown

Ülgen created the first shaman and gave him a drum. The shaman ascended to the heavens to receive his power. In some versions, the shaman was sent to combat Erlik's evil. The shaman became the intermediary between humans and the gods. Shamanism is established as the religious institution. Shamans can communicate with the divine and the spirits.

The FloodAltai oral tradition, recorded by Verbitsky

Unknown

Ülgen, angered by the wickedness of humanity, sent a great flood to destroy the world. However, he saved a few righteous people, along with pairs of animals, by placing them on a raft or a ship. After the flood, humanity repopulated the earth. The world is cleansed, and humanity gets a second chance. This story shows Ülgen's role as a moral judge.

Ülgen is a purely benevolent god with no dark side. — While Ülgen is generally benevolent, he can be wrathful and punishing. He sends floods, storms, and diseases as punishments. He is not a 'love and light' deity but a just and powerful ruler.

Ülgen is the same as the Greek Zeus or the Norse Odin. — Ülgen is a sky god, but his mythology and cultural context are unique to Turkic peoples. He is not a thunderbolt-wielding patriarch in the same way as Zeus, nor a wisdom-seeking wanderer like Odin. Comparisons are superficial.

Ülgen was a major deity in all Turkic cultures historically. — Ülgen is primarily attested among Siberian Turkic peoples, especially Altai. In Western Turkic groups (e.g., Oghuz), the supreme deity was Tengri, and Ülgen is not prominent in their historical records.

Ülgen is a god of the moon or the sun. — Ülgen is associated with the sky as a whole, not specifically with the sun or moon. He is sometimes said to have created the sun and moon, but he is not identified with them.
