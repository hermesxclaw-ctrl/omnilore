Etymology

The name Ülgen is thought to derive from the Old Turkic root 'ül-' meaning 'to distribute' or 'to share', or from 'ülüg' meaning 'portion, share, fate'. This etymological connection suggests a role as the distributor of fates and fortunes. Alternative theories propose a connection to the Mongolic word 'ölgii' (cradle) or to the verb 'öl-' (to die), but these are less widely accepted.

Cultural Ownership

Pan-Turkic, with particular prominence in Altai, Khakas, Shor, and other Siberian Turkic traditions. Also found among the Yakut (Sakha) as 'Yryn-ai-tojon' and in various forms among other Turkic peoples. The deity is not exclusive to one nation but is a shared heritage of Turkic-speaking peoples, with regional variations.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have equated Ülgen with the Tengri of the broader Turkic and Mongolic sky-god tradition, while others see him as a distinct deity or an aspect of Tengri. In the Yakut tradition, the analogous deity is often called 'Yryn-ai-tojon' or 'Aiyy-Toyon', but the exact correspondence is debated. The name 'Ülgen' itself may be a title rather than a proper name, and some sources use 'Bai-Ülgen' (Rich Ülgen) or 'Ülgen Khan'.

Archive Dossier — Approach

High in the celestial vault of the Turkic cosmos, where the sky meets the sacred mountain, dwells Ülgen—the radiant creator, the giver of life, the judge of souls. Unlike the chthonic Erlik who rules the underworld, Ülgen is the benevolent sky god whose light nurtures the world and whose judgment guides the spirits of the dead. His presence is felt in the thunder that shakes the heavens, in the rain that blesses the steppe, and in the shaman's ecstatic flight to his golden throne.

Ülgen sits on the golden mountain in the white sky, and the shaman climbs the birch tree to seek his blessing.

Who It Is

Ülgen (also Ulgan, Ulgen Khan) is a benevolent sky deity of the Turkic peoples, second only to Kayra Han (or in some traditions, the supreme god himself). He is the god of goodness, light, and abundance, who dwells in the sixteenth layer of the sky, seated on a golden mountain. Ülgen created the sun, the moon, the stars, and the first humans alongside Kayra Han. He is the protector of shamans and the source of shamanic power: a shaman who receives Ülgen's blessing can travel to the upper world, heal the sick, and restore balance. Ülgen is depicted as a wise old man with a long white beard, dressed in white, carrying a golden staff. He rides a white horse and is associated with white swans, horses, and the cosmic birch tree (the shaman's path between worlds). The shamans' ritual ascent to Ülgen's realm involved a journey through seven or nine watches (layers) of the sky, each guarded by spirits. Offerings to Ülgen consisted of white horses (whose souls were sent to the sky while their bodies remained on earth), mare's milk, and white foods. Ülgen represents the positive, life-affirming aspect of the Turkic supernatural world — the force of growth, health, and cosmic order. His name is cognate with the Old Turkic 'ülüg' (portion, share, destiny).

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Ülgen is generally not described in anthropomorphic detail, but when he appears, he is envisioned as an old man with a white beard, often seated on a golden throne in the highest heaven. He may be surrounded by light or fire. In some shamanic invocations, he is said to have a shining face and to ride a white horse or a celestial eagle. The Yakut Yryn-ai-tojon is described as a white-bearded old man in white clothing, dwelling in the east.

Symbols & Iconography

Key symbols associated with Ülgen include: the sky and the celestial vault; the sun and the moon; the sacred mountain (often the world mountain); the white horse (sacrificed to him); the birch tree (used in shamanic rituals to reach his realm); the eagle (as a messenger or his steed); the color white (purity, holiness); and the number nine (the nine heavens).

Behaviour & Personality

Ülgen is benevolent, wise, and just. He is the creator of the world, the giver of life, and the protector of humanity. He rewards the good and punishes the wicked, both in life and after death. He is approachable through shamanic rituals, but he is also distant and transcendent. He is not capricious but acts according to cosmic order.

Domains & Habitats

Ülgen dwells in the highest of the nine (or seventeen) heavens, on a golden mountain, near a golden palace. He is the lord of the sky, the celestial realm, and all that is above the earth. He controls the weather, especially thunder, lightning, and rain. He is also the ruler of the upper world, where the souls of the righteous go after death.

Version Differences

In Altai tradition, Ülgen is a distinct supreme deity, often contrasted with Erlik, the lord of the underworld. Among the Yakut, the analogous deity is often 'Aiyy-Toyon' or 'Yryn-ai-tojon', but there are multiple 'aiyy' deities, and the supreme creator may be 'Urun Aiyy Toyon' (White Creator Lord). In some Turkic groups, Ülgen is merged with or replaced by Tengri. The number of heavens and the details of his family vary: some say he has seven sons and seven daughters, others say nine. The exact hierarchy among the celestial gods also varies.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Creation

Source Version Altai, Yakut

Description

Ülgen created the earth, the sky, and all living beings. In the Altai creation myth, he dives into the primordial sea to bring up earth, or he commands a bird or a devil to do so. He breathed life into the first human, or formed him from clay or reeds.

Limitations

The act of creation is sometimes shared with a subordinate being (e.g., Erlik or a bird), and the material for creation is often obtained from the devil, implying that Ülgen is not omnipotent but works with pre-existing matter.

Contradictions

Some versions say Ülgen created the world alone; others say he ordered Erlik to dive into the water, and Erlik concealed some earth in his mouth, leading to the creation of mountains and evil.

Weather Control

Source Version Altai, Yakut

Description

Ülgen controls thunder, lightning, rain, and wind. He sends storms as punishment or to cleanse the world. Shamans invoke him to bring rain during droughts.

Limitations

His weather powers are sometimes delegated to lesser spirits (e.g., the 'yzyr' or 'kut' spirits). He may withhold rain if displeased.

Contradictions

Some traditions attribute weather control to other deities (e.g., the Yakut 'Uluu Tojon' or the thunder god 'Syge toyon'), and Ülgen's role may be secondary.

Judgment of Souls

Source Version Altai, Yakut

Description

After death, souls ascend to Ülgen's realm to be judged. The righteous dwell in his heavenly abode, while the wicked are sent to Erlik's underworld.

Limitations

The judgment is not described in detail; it may be a simple division based on one's deeds. Some traditions say that a shaman must guide the soul to Ülgen.

Contradictions

In some Turkic traditions, the afterlife is not clearly divided between Ülgen and Erlik, and other deities or ancestors may play a role.

Granting Shamanic Power

Source Version Altai, Yakut

Description

Ülgen is the source of shamanic power. Shamans ascend to his realm to receive their calling, their drum, and their spirit helpers. He may also grant them the ability to heal, divine, and communicate with the spirits.

Limitations

Not all shamans are chosen by Ülgen; some are called by Erlik or other spirits. The shaman's power is conditional on his or her purity and adherence to tradition.

Contradictions

The Yakut tradition distinguishes between 'white shamans' (who serve the aiyy, or celestial deities) and 'black shamans' (who serve the abaasy, or evil spirits). Ülgen is associated with white shamans.

Blessing and Fertility

Source Version Altai

Description

Ülgen blesses families with children, livestock, and prosperity. He is invoked in rituals for fertility and abundance.

Limitations

His blessings are contingent on proper worship and sacrifice.

Contradictions

Some fertility functions are attributed to the goddess Umay, who is sometimes seen as his wife or daughter.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

Kayra Han

Type predecessor or fatherSource Altai mythology, various ethnographers

Description

In some Altai traditions, Kayra Han is the supreme creator god, and Ülgen is his son or an aspect of him. Kayra Han is often described as the initial creator, while Ülgen is the active ruler of the world.

Version Note

The relationship is not consistent; some sources make them identical, others distinct.

Conflicting Accounts

Some scholars argue that Kayra Han is a later invention or a variant of Tengri, and that Ülgen is the original supreme deity.

Erlik

Type antagonistSource Altai, Yakut traditions

Description

Erlik is the lord of the underworld and the adversary of Ülgen. He is the source of evil, disease, and death. He tempts humans to sin and claims their souls after death. Ülgen and Erlik are brothers in some myths, with Erlik's jealousy leading to his fall.

Version Note

In some versions, Erlik is a fallen angel or a demigod who challenged Ülgen's authority.

Conflicting Accounts

In some Yakut traditions, the evil spirit is not a single entity but a host of 'abaasy', and the supreme deity may not have a direct antagonist.

Umay

Type consort or daughterSource Pan-Turkic mythology

Description

Umay is a goddess of fertility, childbirth, and children. She is often associated with Ülgen, either as his wife, daughter, or companion. She protects women and children and is invoked for blessings.

Version Note

The exact relationship varies; some sources make her a separate deity, others an aspect of Ülgen's creative power.

Conflicting Accounts

Some scholars see Umay as a pre-Islamic goddess who was later integrated into the celestial hierarchy.

Humanity

Type creator and judgeSource Altai, Yakut traditions

Description

Ülgen created humans and continues to watch over them. He rewards the righteous and punishes the wicked. He is accessible through prayer and sacrifice.

Version Note

In some traditions, humans are seen as children of Ülgen, while in others they are created from earth and are subject to both Ülgen and Erlik.

Conflicting Accounts

The Yakut concept of 'aiyy' includes a pantheon of celestial deities, and the relationship between the creator and humans is more complex.

Shamans

Type chosen intermediariesSource Altai, Yakut traditions

Description

Shamans are the chosen servants of Ülgen. They ascend to his realm to receive their powers and act as healers, diviners, and guides for the dead.

Version Note

Only 'white shamans' serve Ülgen; 'black shamans' serve Erlik.

Conflicting Accounts

Some traditions hold that all shamans are called by both good and evil spirits, and the distinction is not absolute.
