Who It Is

The Daedric Prince of dusk and dawn, vanity, and egotism. Also known as the Mother of the Rose and Queen of the Night Sky. Unlike many Daedra, Azura is concerned for her followers' well-being. Her realm is Moonshadow, a plane of impossible beauty. Her artifact is Azura's Star. She cursed the Chimer to become the Dunmer (Dark Elves) for breaking their oath to her.

The Approach

In the pantheon of the Daedric Princes, Azura stands apart—a goddess of twilight and transition, whose gaze pierces the veil between day and night, and whose vengeance is as swift as the setting sun. She is the Queen of Dawn and Dusk, the Mother of the Rose, and the patron of those who walk the edge of change. Yet her story is not one of gentle benevolence; it is a tale of prophecy, betrayal, and the unyielding consequences of pride.

Content advisory. Contains themes of divine retribution, curses, and references to the enslavement of a people (the Dunmer) as punishment, which may be distressing to some readers.

Etymology

The name 'Azura' may derive from the word 'azure', meaning sky blue, reflecting her association with twilight and the color of her realm. Alternatively, it could be a corruption of 'Azurah', a name used by the Khajiit.

Cultural Ownership

Azura is a Daedric Prince in the fictional universe of The Elder Scrolls, created by Bethesda Game Studios. She is worshipped by various races, most notably the Dunmer (Dark Elves), who consider her one of the 'Good Daedra'.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars within the lore debate whether Azura is the same entity as the Khajiit goddess Azurah, but this is generally accepted as a cultural interpretation of the same Daedric Prince.

Description

Appearance

Azura is most often depicted as a beautiful woman with dark blue or purple skin, flowing black hair, and eyes that glow with a violet light. She is sometimes shown with feathered wings or accompanied by a glowing star. Her appearance is often associated with the colors of dusk and dawn.

Symbols & Iconography

Her symbol is a crescent moon and star, representing the twilight. The star is often depicted as a glowing gem, and the crescent moon is sometimes shown with a rose. She is also associated with the color purple and the concept of transition.

Behaviour & Personality

Azura is portrayed as proud, enigmatic, and deeply concerned with the fate of her followers. She is known to be both benevolent and vengeful, rewarding those who serve her faithfully and cursing those who betray her. She is a master of prophecy and often manipulates events to fulfill her visions.

Domains & Habitats

Azura's domain is Moonshadow, a realm of twilight and beauty, said to be filled with gardens of roses and glowing crystals. She is associated with the liminal times of dawn and dusk, and her influence is felt in matters of change, prophecy, and vanity.

Version Differences

In different games, her portrayal varies slightly. In Morrowind, she is a central figure in the Dunmer religion, while in Skyrim, she is more of a peripheral deity. In ESO, she appears in more quests and has a more active role. Her appearance also changes slightly, but her core attributes remain consistent.

Powers & Abilities

Prophecy

Source Version All games, particularly Morrowind

Description

Azura can foresee future events and often communicates her visions to her chosen mortals. She is known for her accurate prophecies, such as the Nerevarine prophecy.

Limitations

Her prophecies are often cryptic and can be misinterpreted.

Contradictions

None known.

Cursing

Source Version Morrowind, ESO

Description

Azura can curse individuals or entire races. The most famous example is her curse on the Chimer, transforming them into the Dunmer with grey skin and red eyes.

Limitations

Her curses are permanent and cannot be lifted by mortal means.

Contradictions

Some sources suggest the curse was a punishment for the murder of Nerevar, but others claim it was a result of the Tribunal's use of the Heart of Lorkhan.

Source Version Oblivion, Skyrim, ESO

She grants blessings to her followers, such as increased luck, protection, or special abilities. In the games, praying at her shrine can grant temporary boosts.

Limitations

Blessings require devotion and can be lost if the follower displeases her.

Stories & Myths

The Chimer and the Curse

Source Various in-game books, including 'The War of the First Council' and 'Nerevar at Red Mountain'Culture DunmerPeriod First Era

Summary

After the death of Nerevar, the Tribunal used the Heart of Lorkhan to achieve godhood. Azura appeared to them and cursed the Chimer, turning their skin grey and their eyes red, declaring that they would be 'Dunmer' forever. This curse was a punishment for their betrayal of Nerevar's trust.

Participants

Azura

Tribunal (Vivec, Almalexia, Sotha Sil)

Nerevar

Consequences

The Chimer became the Dunmer, and Azura became one of the 'Good Daedra' to the Dunmer, who still worship her despite the curse.

Variants

Some accounts suggest the curse was a result of the Tribunal's actions, while others claim it was a punishment for the murder of Nerevar by the Tribunal.

The Nerevarine Prophecy

Source Morrowind main questCulture DunmerPeriod Third Era

Summary

Azura prophesied that a reincarnation of Nerevar would come to Morrowind to defeat the Tribunal and Dagoth Ur. The Nerevarine, the player character, fulfills this prophecy, with Azura guiding them throughout their journey.

Participants

Azura

Nerevarine

Tribunal

Dagoth Ur

Consequences

The Tribunal loses their divinity, Dagoth Ur is defeated, and Azura's prophecy is fulfilled.

Variants

The prophecy is a central plot point in Morrowind, and Azura's role is well-documented.

The Star of Azura

Source Skyrim (The Black Star quest)Culture NordPeriod Fourth Era

Summary

Azura tasks the Dragonborn with retrieving her star, which has been corrupted and turned into the Black Star by the mage Malyn Varen. The Dragonborn must decide whether to restore the star to Azura or keep it for themselves.

Participants

Relationships

Nerevar

Type worshipperSource Morrowind lore

Description

Nerevar was a mortal champion of Azura, and she chose him as her representative. She was angered by his death and cursed the Tribunal for their role in it.

Version Note

Nerevar is a historical figure in the lore, and his relationship with Azura is well-documented.

Conflicting Accounts

Some sources suggest Nerevar was a devotee of Azura, while others imply he was merely a political leader.

The Tribunal

Type enemySource Morrowind

Description

Azura cursed the Tribunal and the Chimer for their use of the Heart of Lorkhan and the death of Nerevar. She opposes their false godhood.

Version Note

The Tribunal are the primary antagonists in Morrowind, and Azura's curse is a key plot point.

Conflicting Accounts

The Tribunal's own accounts claim they were justified in their actions, which contradict Azura's view.

Dunmer

Type worshipperSource Morrowind, ESO

Description

The Dunmer worship Azura as one of the 'Good Daedra', despite her cursing them. They see her as a guide and protector.

Version Note

Azura is a central figure in Dunmer religion, and her influence is seen in their culture.

Conflicting Accounts

Some Dunmer sects may reject Azura in favor of the Tribunal, but most revere her.

Khajiit

Type worshipperSource ESO

Description

The Khajiit worship Azura as Azurah, a goddess who shaped them. She is considered one of their ancestral spirits.

Version Note

In Khajiit mythology, Azurah is a creator figure, which differs from her role in other cultures.

Conflicting Accounts

The Khajiit's creation myth involving Azurah is distinct and may conflict with other accounts of Azura's nature.

Dark History
Content advisory. References to divine punishment, enslavement, and the transformation of a people as a curse.

Curse on the Chimer

Source Morrowind, ESO

Description

Azura cursed the entire Chimer race, altering their appearance and marking them as 'Dunmer'. This act was a punishment for the Tribunal's actions, but it affected all Chimer, including those who had no part in the betrayal.

Scholarly Context

This curse is a central element of Dunmer identity and is seen as both a punishment and a symbol of Azura's power.

Vengeance on Betrayers

Source ESO

Description

Azura is known to exact vengeance on those who betray her, sometimes in cruel ways. For example, in ESO, she punishes a follower who tried to use her power for selfish ends.

Scholarly Context

Her vengeance is often seen as justified by her followers, but it can be harsh.

Contradictions & Disputes

Reason for the Curse

Position A

Azura cursed the Chimer because the Tribunal murdered Nerevar.

Position A — evidence

In-game texts like 'Nerevar at Red Mountain' suggest the murder was the cause.

Position B

Azura cursed the Chimer because the Tribunal used the Heart of Lorkhan to become gods.

Position B — evidence

Other sources, such as 'The War of the First Council', imply the use of the Heart was the primary offense.

Scholarly Consensus

Most lore scholars accept that both reasons are intertwined; the Tribunal's actions were a betrayal of Nerevar and a violation of divine law.

Azura's Role in Khajiit Creation

Position A

Azura is the same as Azurah, the Khajiit goddess who created the Khajiit race.

Position A — evidence

The Khajiit creation myth in ESO describes Azurah as a form of Azura.

Position B

Azura and Azurah are separate entities, with Azurah being a distinct Khajiit deity.

Position B — evidence

Some Khajiit texts treat Azurah as a separate spirit, not directly equated with Azura.

Scholarly Consensus

The general consensus is that Azurah is the Khajiit interpretation of Azura, but the distinction is culturally significant.
