Who It Is

In the quiet intervals between battles, Hijiri Kuina is remembered as a master of the blade whose name carries weight in the world of One Piece. Yet her story is one of promise cut short, a legacy that lingers in the memory of a young swordsman who once crossed blades with her.

Kuina is portrayed as proud, competitive, and deeply passionate about swordsmanship. She is shown to be frustrated by the limitations placed on her as a girl, believing that women cannot become the world's greatest swordsman. Despite this, she is kind and encouraging to Roronoa Zoro, her rival, and ultimately inspires him to pursue his dream.

Swordsmanship

Kuina was an exceptionally skilled swordswoman, capable of defeating Zoro in their duels, despite being younger. She had mastered several techniques, though specific named techniques are not shown.

Limit: As a child, she had not yet developed her full potential, and her skills were limited by her physical strength and lack of experience.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the flashback, Kuina is depicted as a young girl with short, dark hair and a determined expression. She wears a simple kimono or training attire, and is often seen holding a katana. Her physical appearance is consistent with a child around 11 years old.

Symbols — She is associated with the sword, specifically the katana, which she wields with skill. Her image is tied to the Shimotsuki Village dojo, where she trained.

Domains — She lived in Shimotsuki Village on an island in the East Blue, where she trained at the local dojo owned by her father, Koshiro.

Accounts I
The Rivalry with ZoroOne Piece manga, chapter 5

Pre-canon, set in the East Blue

Kuina and Zoro were childhood rivals at the Shimotsuki Village dojo. They sparred countless times, with Kuina winning every match. After one final duel, Zoro challenged her to a promise: that one of them would become the world's greatest swordsman. Kuina, however, expressed despair, saying that as a girl, she would inevitably become weaker and be surpassed. She then gave Zoro her prized katana, Wado Ichimonji, and asked him to fulfill their dream. Shortly after, she died in an accident, leaving Zoro to carry on her legacy. Zoro inherited Wado Ichimonji and dedicated his life to becoming the world's greatest swordsman, vowing never to lose again. Her death deeply affected him and motivated his training.
