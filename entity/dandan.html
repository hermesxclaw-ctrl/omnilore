Who It Is

The Dandan (also Dandān or Dandan al-Bahrayn) is a sea monster of Persian Gulf folklore — a giant fish or dragon-fish said to inhabit the waters between Bahrain, Basra, and the coast of Oman. Described in medieval Arabic maritime texts and sailors' lore, the Dandan is a fish of immense size with a dragon-like head, sharp teeth, and a body covered in pearl-like scales. It is said to grow pearls within its flesh — not from sand-grain irritants like ordinary oysters, but as natural excretions of its supernatural body. The finest pearls in the Gulf were said to come from the Dandan's corpse, washed ashore to be harvested by lucky pearl-divers. The Dandan attacks ships that sail too close to its territory, particularly during the pearl-diving season, and its roar can be heard for miles across the water, causing storms and whirlpools. In some legends, the Dandan guards the undersea entrance to a jinn kingdom or a lost city (possibly a folk memory of Dilmun, the ancient Bahraini civilization associated with paradise and the sweet-water springs beneath the sea). The Dandan also appears in the Arabian Nights and related maritime tales, where it is one of the 'seven great sea monsters' that sailors must contend with on long voyages. The Dandan represents the Arabian Gulf as a dangerous but fabulously wealthy space — the sea that yields pearls also contains monsters, and the line between the pearl-diver's fortune and his death is as thin as a breath of air. The Dandan also connects to the broader Indian Ocean sea-monster complex, drawing on both Arabian and South Asian maritime lore.
