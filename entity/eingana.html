Who It Is

Eingana is a profound and terrifying Dreamtime figure from northern Australian Aboriginal mythology — a snake goddess who is simultaneously the creator of all life and the bringer of death. She holds within her body the thousands of life-threads that connect every living being to existence. Each creature — human, animal, plant — is tethered to Eingana by an invisible cord. At birth, she releases a thread, and the being lives. At death, she pulls the thread back into herself, and the being returns to her. She is thus the beginning and the end, the womb and the grave. Eingana's myth is one of the more philosophically complex expressions of the life-death cycle in world mythology. In some versions, she gave birth to all the creatures of the world in a single great labor, and the landscape — the gorges, creeks, and rock formations — is the scar tissue of her birth. She is described as an immense snake, larger than any earthly serpent, resting in the deepest waterholes or coiled beneath the earth. Unlike the Rainbow Serpent, who is primarily associated with water and creation, Eingana is associated with the preconditions of existence itself — the boundary between being and non-being. Her worship involved elaborate rituals, and some accounts suggest that initiates had to symbolically die to her (have their thread pulled back) before being reborn with new sacred knowledge. Academic sources on Eingana are limited, as her traditions were suppressed early and unevenly recorded, but what survives suggests a deity of extraordinary metaphysical depth whose theology anticipated certain existentialist and process-philosophy concepts by millennia.
