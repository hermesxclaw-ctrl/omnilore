Who It Is

Alex Chen doesn't read minds — she feels them until they hurt. In the mountain town of Haven Springs, every grief, rage, and terror bleeds into the air as colored fire only she can see, and when she steps too close, it takes her over. Her gift is not cool telepathy; it is the lived experience of a foster kid who learned to scan every room for danger, turned supernaturally literal.

Attested personality: introverted but wry, observant, self-deprecating humor as defense, deeply loyal to chosen family. Due to eight years in foster care and instability with her power, Alex is hypervigilant, anticipates rejection, struggles with trust and self-worth, and initially suppresses her empathy as a curse. Kuan and Mori describe her as 'chill, wry, and down-to-earth' when not overwhelmed, with a love of music (songwriting, record store culture). Morally good at core per writers, but player choice allows assertive, compassionate, or withdrawn responses. She journals, text

Powers
Aura Sight (Empathic Clairvoyance)

Passive perception of strong emotions as colored auras surrounding people and objects. Colors correspond to emotion families: red/orange for anger, violet/purple for fear, blue for sadness/grief, yellow/gold for joy, green for anxiety/disgust variants. Allows Alex to identify who is emotionally charged and what emotion dominates. Functions at range, through walls at close proximity when emotion is intense.

Limit: Requires line-of-sight or proximity; faint emotions are invisible; cannot read specific thoughts without deeper focus; overwhelmed by multiple strong auras simultaneously; suppressed when Alex is emotionally dysregulated herself

Emotion Absorption / Empathic Immersion

When Alex focuses on an aura, she absorbs the emotion, experiencing it as if it were her own with full physiological and psychological force. The world transforms into a surreal manifestation of that person's inner state (e.g., Charlotte's anger turns the world to fire and ash; Duckie's sadness creates a silent, color-drained bar). Alex feels their memories and physical sensations. This is involuntary when emotions are explosive nearby.

Limit: High cost: Alex loses self-regulation, risks being trapped in the emotion, experiences after-effects (lingering anger/fear/sadness that colors her own mood). Cannot easily exit without resolving the source emotion or being pulled out by another person. Prolonged immersion can cause collapse, nosebleed, or dissociation (implied). Requires consent to fully resolve - she cannot force someone to let go.

Emotional Projection and Manipulation (Nova)

After absorbing an emotion, Alex can project it outward as a concussive 'Nova' burst that forces nearby people to feel the same emotion intensely, or conversely, can calm/soothe an emotion in a target by taking it away. Used offensively (e.g., to make Riley's fear visible, to force Mac to feel anger) and therapeutically (to relieve Eleanor's fear/confusion, to help Charlotte release anger). In gameplay, presented as dialogue choices and a radial blast in climax.

Limit: Ethically fraught - projection without consent is violation; can cause harm if misused. Requires absorbed charge; cannot create emotion ex nihilo. Large novas exhaust her. Typhon climax shows she can turn Jed's suppressed guilt/fear back on him, but at great personal risk.

Memory Dive / Psychometry via Emotion

By touching aura-charged objects (e.g., Gabe's jacket, Jed's mining artifacts, Charlotte's bracelet), Alex can experience vivid memory echoes tied to strong emotions imprinted on the object, seeing past events from the owner's perspective.

Limit: Only works on objects with strong emotional residue; memories are fragmented and subjective to the owner's feeling, not objective truth; Alex cannot change the past

Musical Regulation and Resilience (Non-supernatural skill)

Alex uses songwriting, guitar, and record-store knowledge as emotional regulation tools. While not supernatural, the game ties this to her power mastery - performing 'Creep' or original songs allows her to ground herself after immersion. This is framed as learned coping, not magic.

Limit: Requires safe environment; fails when trauma is acute; dependent on supportive audience

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested in-game model: young adult Asian-American woman, age 21 at start of True Colors (born c. 2000). Shoulder-length dark brown/black hair with faded teal/aqua dyed underlayer, brown eyes, light-to-medium skin tone. Wardrobe is casual, layered, and music-inflected: denim jacket with patches/pins, plaid shirts, graphic tees, skinny jeans, boots, beanie. Height approx. 5'4" based on motion capture actor. No supernatural visual change in normal

Symbols — Core symbols: color-coded auras (primary visual language), the cracked geode / raw amethyst imagery associated with Haven's mining history, the lanterns of the Spring Festival (Chapter 2), the vinyl record and guitar (music as coping and connection), the black lantern/rose imagery in marketing, and the 'Helping Hands' group home paperwork as bureaucratic symbol. Typhon Mining's logo (mountain silhouette) recurs as antagonistic

Domains — Primary habitat: Haven Springs, Colorado - fictional former mining town (population ~ small town), built around Typhon Mining Company, with Black Lantern bar, record store (Wavelengths), dispensary, park, and mountain trails. Prior habitats: Portland, Oregon (brief period before game), and Helping Hands group foster home (location unspecified, implied urban Pacific Northwest) where she lived for eight years after separation from parents and broth

Accounts I
The Helping Hands Years - Eight Years in Foster CareLife is Strange: True Colors - collectible texts, SMS messages, Alex's journal, and developer interviews (Felice Kuan on Tristan consultation)

c. 2013-2021 (Alex ages ~13-21), revealed via flashbacks and documents during base game

After her mother abandons the family and her father John Chen is unable to care for them, Alex and older brother Gabe are separated. Gabe is adopted while Alex remains a ward of Helping Hands group home for eight years, moving between placements. She learns to hide her overwhelming empathy, which is treated as behavioral problems by caregivers and peers. Texts from social worker and journal entries show she feels like an outsider 'not just from Haven, but from people in general.' Gabe's letters and eventual invitation to Haven Springs offer the first stable reconnection. This backstory is not a playable chapter but is delivered through environmental storytelling and dialogue, informing why Alex initially views her power as a curse and fears attachment. Establishes Alex's core wound, hypervigilance, and musical coping; motivates her desperate need to make the Haven move work and her devastation at Gabe's death; frames later acceptance arc

Chapter 1: Side A - Reunion and Death in the MineLife is Strange: True Colors Chapter 1: Side A

2021 (game release contemporary)

Alex arrives in Haven Springs after years apart to live with Gabe, who now works at the Black Lantern and is dating Charlotte Harmon, mother to Ethan Lambert. During a welcome hike with Gabe and Ethan to explore an old mine, Typhon Mining detonates scheduled explosives despite Gabe's call to stop - Ethan is missing on the trail. Gabe dies trying to save Ethan when the blast triggers a collapse. Alex experiences Gabe's final fear and then overwhelming grief, her aura sight flaring uncontrollably. Typhon, led locally by Jed Lucan, covers up the incident as an accident and denies the call was ignored. Alex, using her power on Mac Loudon (Typhon employee who received the call), senses his fear and guilt and becomes convinced Gabe's death was preventable and covered up. Inciting incident for entire game; Alex decides to stay in Haven to investigate despite being offered a chance to leave; establishes Typhon as antagonist and Alex's power as investigative tool

Chapter 2: Lanterns - The Weight of Jed's Fear and the Spring FestivalLife is Strange: True Colors Chapter 2: Lanterns

2021, days after Chapter 1, during Haven's annual Spring Festival (LARP and lantern release)

Haven holds its Spring Festival to honor Gabe. Alex investigates Typhon while navigating festival events. She becomes entangled with Jed Lucan, the beloved town patriarch and Ryan's father, whose warm exterior masks deep fear. When Alex uses her power on Jed after he recounts the mining disaster of his past, she is pulled into a manifestation of his suppressed terror - a dark mine filled with whispers. She also helps Eleanor Lethe, the elderly shop owner showing early cognitive decline, whose fear of being a burden manifests as a shifting, paper-filled shop where words disappear. Alex learns to soothe Eleanor by absorbing and releasing her fear, a compassionate use of power. Clues point to Typhon's second blast cover-up and a secret second call. Alex proves she can use empathy to heal, not just suffer; deepens suspicion of Jed; establishes mechanic of helping vs harming with power; foreshadows Jed's culpability

Chapter 3: Monster or Mortal - Charlotte's AngerLife is Strange: True Colors Chapter 3: Monster or Mortal

2021, following Typhon's attempt to discredit Alex

Alex is accused of being unstable after confronting Mac. She seeks evidence in Charlotte's dispensary. Charlotte, grieving Gabe and angry at Alex for stirring up pain and at herself for not protecting Ethan, radiates overwhelming red anger. Alex absorbs it and is trapped in an apocalyptic version of Haven where ash falls, fires burn, and Charlotte appears as a raging, isolated figure. To escape, Alex must confront Charlotte's memory of Gabe and help her articulate grief beneath anger, choosing whether to take Charlotte's anger away (leaving her numb but functional) or leave it (respecting her process). Simultaneously, Alex breaks into Typhon's offices with Steph and Ryan, discovering Diane Jacobs is Typhon's compliance officer sent to bury the report. Major ethical choice about consent and emotional labor; reveals Typhon's systematic cover-up; Charlotte's fate branches based on Alex's intervention, affecting later town support

Chapter 4: Flicker - Ryan's Fear and Eleanor's ForgettingLife is Strange: True Colors Chapter 4: Flicker

2021, night of the Haven council vote on Typhon expansion

Alex's investigation fractures her friendships when Ryan, who trusted Jed, feels betrayed and afraid that Alex is projecting her trauma onto the town. Alex absorbs Ryan's fear - manifesting as a dark forest where Ryan is frozen, convinced he will become his father and fail everyone. She must guide him through it. In parallel, Eleanor's dementia worsens; her fear of forgetting Riley manifests as a shop where names vanish. Alex can again intervene. The chapter culminates in Alex finding proof that Jed ordered the blast to continue despite Gabe's call, sacrificing Gabe to protect Typhon's mining expansion and his own legacy. Jed attempts to manipulate Alex by appealing to her desire to belong. Ryan relationship can be repaired or broken; Eleanor's arc resolves; Alex obtains definitive evidence of Jed's guilt; sets up final confrontation

Chapter 5: Side B - Confrontation, Nova, and Two FuturesLife is Strange: True Colors Chapter 5: Side B

2021, Haven Springs town council meeting / abandoned mine

At the council meeting, Jed publicly paints Alex as a troubled outsider. When Alex presents evidence, Jed confesses privately in the mine: in 2008 he ordered a similar blast that killed seven miners, including his own son's friends, and covered it up; he repeated the choice with Gabe to protect the town's economy and his myth. He tries to kill Alex in the mine collapse. Alex, overwhelmed by Jed's suppressed guilt and her own grief and rage, unleashes a massive Nova - forcing Jed to feel the full weight of his victims' terror and her own pain. Jed collapses, consumed. Alex then faces the town, her power now witnessed. In the aftermath, Haven decides Typhon's fate based on Alex's testimony and whether she earned community trust. Final choice: Alex can stay in Haven Springs, accepting belonging and continuing to help (taking over the Black Lantern/record store, continuing relationship with Steph or Ryan), or leave Haven to pursue music on the road, honoring Gabe's memory by living freely. Alex can read minds and control people like a traditional telepath — She senses and shares emotions, not specific thoughts. She cannot implant false memories or command actions; projection forces feeling, not obedience. Game consistently shows she feels what they feel, with limited ability to soothe or amplify, and consent is narratively central.

Alex is the first Asian-American protagonist in video games — She is a milestone for AAA narrative adventure leads and for Life is Strange, but not the first Asian-American protagonist overall (e.g., Faith Connors in Mirror's Edge (2007, Asian-coded), Chloe Price is not, but games like Sleeping Dogs (2012) starred Wei Shen). Her significance is as a nuanced, non-stereotyped Asian-American woman with foster care background in a story-driven AAA game.

mxmtoon voiced Alex entirely — Split performance: Erika Mori is the speaking voice and performance capture for all dialogue and motion; mxmtoon (Maia) is only the singing voice for diegetic songs. Mori also sings in some non-musical vocalizations, but credited singing is mxmtoon.

Alex's power is a curse that she permanently removes at the end — Arc is about acceptance, not removal. In both endings she retains power and chooses how to use it. No ending cures or removes empathy; dialogue explicitly reframes it as strength. Player cannot opt to lose power.
