Who It Is

Whiro is the Māori god of darkness, death, evil, and chaos — the supreme antagonist in the Māori divine pantheon. He is the eldest son of Ranginui (Sky Father) and Papatūānuku (Earth Mother), and from the beginning, he opposed Tāne's plan to separate their parents, preferring the comfort of darkness. When the separation occurred, Whiro became Tāne's eternal enemy, vowing to destroy all that Tāne created — light, forests, birds, and humanity. He fled to the underworld (Rarohenga), where he now rules over disease, death, and decay. Whiro is associated with the lizard and the dark of the moon (the last phase before the new moon, when the moon is invisible). His influence is blamed for sickness, misfortune, and evil deeds. Māori tohunga (priests/healers) developed elaborate rituals to combat Whiro's influence, and the concept of tapu (sacred prohibition) functions partly as a barrier against his contaminating presence. However, Whiro is not purely a devil figure in the Christian sense: like many indigenous deities of darkness, he is a necessary part of cosmic balance. Death and decay are not evil in themselves — they are what allow the cycle of life to continue. Whiro's evil lies in his malice, his active desire to destroy what others create. In some traditions, Whiro accumulated so much power that he devoured his own body, leaving only spirit — making him a purely spiritual malevolence. He represents the shadow side of the Māori cosmos, the darkness against which the culture heroes (especially Māui) define themselves through struggle.
