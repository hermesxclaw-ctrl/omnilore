Who It Is

Hold out your hand and you hold a palm; look to the horizon in any hot country and you see another — the same word for the hollow that holds water and the tree that promises it. For 6,000 years humans have called both by the same sound, and for 6,000 years we have cut, worshipped, burned, planted, and killed for palms.

As plants, palms are long-lived, slow-growing monocots with distinct phenology: dioecious or monoecious flowering, often mast fruiting, reliance on specific pollinators (beetles, bees, wind) and dispersers (birds, bats, humans, ocean currents for coconuts). Many are ecological keystones of oases and tropical forests. Behaviorally, human-palm interaction is intensive: palms require human-assisted pollination (date palms since antiquity), irrigation, and fire management. Powers
Caloric and Nutritional Provision

Palms are among humanity's most important food plants. Date palm fruits are 60-70% sugar, storable for years; coconut provides flesh, milk, water, and oil; oil palm yields the highest oil per hectare of any crop; peach palm (Bactris gasipaes), sago palm (Metroxylon sagu), palmyra (Borassus), and areca nut are staples. Sago starch extraction yields 150-300kg per trunk.

Limit: Highly seasonal; many fruits require processing (oil palm must be milled within 24h; areca is psychoactive and carcinogenic; sago requires leaching of toxins). Monoculture vulnerability to disease (e.g., lethal yellowing, Bayoud disease).

Material and Structural Provision

Trunks, leaves, and fibers provide thatch, matting, baskets, rope (coir from coconut, piassava from Attalea), wax (carnauba Copernicia prunifera), timber (palm wood used in construction), paper, and fuel. Leaves are universal roofing and weaving material from the Amazon to the Pacific.

Limit: Palm 'wood' is not true wood, lacks strength for large beams; susceptible to weevils (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus). Overharvesting threatens wild species (e.g., Tahina spectabilis).

Hydraulic and Ecological Keystone Function

Oasis palms create microclimates, shade, windbreak, and aquifer stabilization enabling agriculture in deserts. Mangrove palm Nypa stabilizes coastlines. Palms support specialized fauna: palm swifts, palm cockatoos, pollinating weevils, and humans. Coconut is the ultimate oceanic disperser, viable after 110 days floating in seawater.

Limit: Palms are poor at cold tolerance; most cannot survive hard frost. Require abundant water despite desert image - date palms need irrigation.

Symbolic and Ritual Power

Palm frond confers status: victory wreath, martyr's palm, peace offering, apotropaic protection. In Judaism, waving lulav effects atonement and rain prayers at Sukkot. In Christianity, blessed palms on Palm Sunday are sacramentals, burned to ash for next year's Ash Wednesday. In antiquity, palm ash and fronds marked sacred space.

Limit: Symbolic power is culturally contingent; in northern Christianity, substitute species dilute botanical specificity.

Economic and Industrial Dominance (Oil Palm)

Elaeis guineensis produces ~35% of world's vegetable oil on ~10% of oil-crop land. Palm oil is in ~50% of supermarket products, biodiesel, and cosmetics due to high yield, oxidative stability, and low cost. PaLM (Google) and Palm OS borrow the name for connotations of handy, tropical ease.

Limit: Yield comes at extreme ecological cost (see dark history). Name borrowing creates confusion; PaLM has no botanical capability.

Domains — Arecaceae is pantropical and subtropical, with highest diversity in Southeast Asia and South America. Accounts I
The City of Palms - Jericho and ElimHebrew Bible: Exodus 15:27, Deuteronomy 34:3, Judges 1:16, 2 Chronicles 28:15; also Josephus, Jewish War

Iron Age - Second Temple period (texts 8th-2nd century BCE)

After crossing the Red Sea, Israelites reach Elim with 12 springs and 70 palm trees, a perfect oasis mirroring tribes and elders. Later, Jericho is repeatedly called 'the city of palm trees' (ir ha-temarim), whose palms signify its wealth and divine blessing as an oasis in the Jordan Rift. The prophetess Deborah judges Israel 'under the Palm of Deborah' between Ramah and Bethel. Date palms are both landscape and metaphor for the righteous: 'The righteous shall flourish like the palm tree' (Psalm 92:12). Established palm as biblical symbol of righteousness, fertility, and promised land abundance; influenced Christian and Islamic oasis imagery; Jericho's palms became proverbial for luxury and were taxed by Herod and Cleopatra.

Leto, Apollo, and the Palm of DelosHomeric Hymn to Apollo (3), Callimachus Hymn to Delos, Euripides, and Odyssey 6.162-163 (Odysseus to Nausicaa)

Archaic - Classical (7th-5th century BCE)

The pregnant Leto, pursued by Hera, finds refuge on barren Delos. She grasps a palm tree (phoinix) on the island and gives birth to Apollo and Artemis. The Delian palm becomes sacred, adorned with votives. Centuries later, Odysseus flatters Nausicaa by comparing her to 'a young palm shoot springing up beside Apollo's altar at Delos' - the most elegant compliment in Homer, linking female beauty, youth, and sacred botany. Delian palm becomes panhellenic sacred tree; pilgrims dedicated bronze palms (e.g., Nicias' palm at Delos, 417 BCE). The simile establishes palm as standard of slender, graceful beauty in Western poetry.

The Phoenix and the PalmHerodotus Histories 2.73, Ovid Metamorphoses 15.392-407, Pliny Natural History 10.2, Physiologus, and medieval bestiaries

5th century BCE - Middle Ages

The phoenix (phoinix) lives 500 years, builds a nest of spices atop a palm tree (also phoinix) at Heliopolis, dies in fire, and is reborn from ash. The wordplay is intentional: bird and tree share name and fate - both die and regenerate. Pliny says the palm's name comes from its Phoenix-like regrowth after cutting. Christians allegorized the palm-phoenix as Resurrection: the palm's evergreen fronds and the bird's immortality signify Christ. Created enduring conflation of palm tree and phoenix bird in art (palm as phoenix perch in mosaics, e.g., Aquileia Basilica 4th century). Gave English 'palm' and 'phoenix' as doublets. Modern Phoenix, Arizona named for rebirth, with palm iconography.

Accounts II
The Palm Tree of the Virgin - Mary and the Date PalmQur'an Surah Maryam 19:22-26; Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew 20; The Golden Legend (Jacobus de Voragine)

7th century CE (Qur'an), 7th-13th century Christian apocrypha

In labour, Maryam (Mary) withdraws to a remote place. The pains drive her to the trunk of a date palm. A voice calls: 'Shake the trunk of the palm toward you, it will drop fresh ripe dates upon you. Eat and drink and cool your eye.' The palm miraculously provides nourishment. The Christian apocryphal Gospel of Pseudo-Matthew tells a parallel: during Flight into Egypt, the infant Jesus commands a palm to bend and feed Mary, and a spring appears at its roots. One of the most depicted Qur'anic scenes in Islamic miniature painting; establishes date palm as maternal, merciful tree in Islamic tradition. Christian version justifies veneration of palms at Christmas and Palm Sunday; palm-date imagery enters Marian iconography.

Palm Sunday - The Triumphal EntryGospels: Matthew 21:1-11, Mark 11:1-11, Luke 19:28-40, John 12:12-19; liturgical sources: Apostolic Constitutions, Egeria's Itinerarium

1st century CE (event), 4th century CE (liturgical attestation)

Jesus enters Jerusalem before Passover riding a donkey. Crowds cut branches (Greek baia, stibas; John specifies phoinix) and spread cloaks, shouting 'Hosanna! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!' The palm branches echo Maccabean victory celebrations (1 Maccabees 13:51, 2 Maccabees 10:7) where palms marked liberation. By the 4th century, Jerusalem Christians reenacted the procession from Mount of Olives with palm fronds; Egeria describes children waving branches. Instituted Palm Sunday, opening Holy Week, one of Christianity's major feasts. Palms blessed and distributed, later burned for Ash Wednesday ashes, creating annual cycle. Palm becomes martyr symbol: 'palm of martyrdom.' Artistic motif of Christ on donkey with palms appears in earliest Christian sarcophagi.

The Palm-Wine Drinkard and the Deads' TownAmos Tutuola, The Palm-Wine Drinkard (1952); oral Yoruba palm-wine folklore

Traditional folklore, literary fixation 1952

A lazy man who drinks only palm wine employs an expert tapper who taps 150 kegs daily. When the tapper falls from a palm and dies, the Drinkard journeys to Deads' Town to retrieve him, traversing forests of bizarre spirits, with palm wine as both sustenance and supernatural currency. The novel draws on Yoruba tales where palm wine (emu) is the drink of ancestors and spirits, and the palm tree is axis between worlds. First African novel published in English to gain international renown; introduced Yoruba palm cosmology to world literature; influenced Dylan Thomas, Chinua Achebe. Established palm wine as literary symbol of excess, tradition, and modernity. UNESCO heritage of palm-wine music (highlife) linked.

All palms are coconut palms or date palms; 'palm tree' = coconut tree on a beach. — Arecaceae has ~2600 species; only one is coconut (Cocos nucifera) and one is date (Phoenix dactylifera). Most palms look nothing like beach coconuts - e.g., rattans are spiny vines, Nypa is a mangrove, Chamaedorea are small understory shrubs. The beach coconut is an introduced, human-dispersed species in many places.

Palm oil comes from coconut palms. — Palm oil comes from African oil palm Elaeis guineensis (mesocarp oil) and palm kernel oil from its seeds; coconut oil is distinct from Cocos nucifera. They have different fatty acid profiles and supply chains.

Palm Sunday palms are always true palm fronds. — In temperate Europe, true palms do not grow; churches use substitutes - willow (pussy willow in Slavic countries), box, yew, olive - still called 'palms' (e.g., German Palmkatzchen = willow catkins). Blessed 'palms' burned for Ash Wednesday may be willow.

Palm reading (palmistry) is an ancient, accurate science of predicting the future from palm lines. — Palmistry (chiromancy) is a folk divination tradition with roots in South Asian samudrika shastra and Greco-Roman thought, but no empirical predictive validity. Lines are flexion creases formed in utero, variable and not linked to fate. Modern palmistry is entertainment or spiritual practice, not science.

Palm trees were always in Los Angeles and the American Southwest - they are native desert icons. — Only one palm is native to California: Washingtonia filifera (California fan palm) in desert oases. All tall Mexican fan palms (Washingtonia robusta) and date palms lining LA streets were introduced in late 19th-20th century for exotic effect. Phoenix, Arizona's palms are also largely introduced.
