Who It Is

The Wati-kutjara (literally 'Two Men' in Pitjantjatjara) are twin creator beings who feature prominently in the mythology of the Western Desert peoples of Central Australia. These two ancestral lizard men traversed the desert landscape during the Dreamtime, their travels creating the waterholes, rock formations, mountain ranges, and sacred sites that define the geography of the Western Desert. Unlike a single supreme creator, the Wati-kutjara represent the principle of duality in creation — two beings working together, sometimes in harmony, sometimes in conflict, to shape the world. Their adventures include battles with monstrous beings, encounters with other Dreaming ancestors, and the establishment of ritual practices. In some versions, the Wati-kutjara are responsible for the creation of many of the prominent geological features of the Musgrave and Mann Ranges in northern South Australia. They are also associated with the introduction of specific ceremonies, songs, and body-painting designs that are still used in Western Desert ritual life. The Wati-kutjara stories emphasize the Aboriginal understanding that the landscape is not a passive backdrop but a record of intentional ancestral action — every feature of the desert is a trace left by the Two Men. Their dual nature — two beings, not one — reflects a sophisticated conceptualization of creation as a process requiring relationship, complementarity, and sometimes productive tension, rather than a single, solitary act of will. In contemporary Pitjantjatjara communities, the Wati-kutjara songlines continue to be performed and the sites associated with their travels remain central to cultural and ceremonial life.
