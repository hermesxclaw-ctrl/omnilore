Who It Is

Springtrap is the central antagonist of the Five Nights at Freddy's franchise — a decayed, moldering animatronic rabbit suit that serves as the undying vessel for the series' ultimate villain: William Afton, the serial child murderer whose crimes set the entire FNAF story in motion. The backstory (pieced together by fans across years of lore analysis) is that Afton murdered at least six children at Freddy Fazbear's Pizza locations in the 1980s, stuffing their bodies into the animatronic suits. When the vengeful spirits of the children later cornered him in the abandoned restaurant's safe room, Afton attempted to hide inside the Spring Bonnie suit — an older 'springlock' model prone to catastrophic mechanical failure. The springlocks triggered, and Afton was crushed to death inside the suit in one of the most gruesome deaths in gaming lore. Thirty years later, his corpse — now fused to the endoskeleton and fur of Spring Bonnie — was discovered and brought to Fazbear's Fright, a horror attraction based on the Freddy's legend, where he awakens.

Physically, Springtrap is a uniquely horrific design: a greenish-gold, decaying rabbit animatronic with torn fabric revealing a metal endoskeleton beneath, and between the metal, the visible, mummified remains of Afton's human body — desiccated flesh, visible organs, and a grinning, lipless human skull visible through the torn jaw of the rabbit head. The design is masterful body horror: the juxtaposition of the friendly children's-entertainment mascot with the rotting corpse of a serial killer creates a visual that is simultaneously grotesque and tragic.

In FNAF 3, Springtrap is the only animatronic that can kill the player (the others are 'phantom' hallucinations). His behavior is more cunning than previous animatronics: he learns from audio lures, avoids certain traps, and stalks the player with intelligent persistence. He is William Afton — a human intelligence in a monster's body. In subsequent games, Afton survives multiple apparent deaths, mutates into new forms (Scraptrap in Pizzeria Simulator, Glitchtrap — a digital consciousness — in Help Wanted, and Burntrap in Security Breach), and eventually returns as a full-body organic regeneration with a new, monstrous design. His catchphrase (and the series' most iconic line) is 'I always come back' — a statement that is both a threat and a meta-commentary on the franchise itself.

Springtrap/William Afton has become one of the most recognizable horror characters of the 2010s, rivaling legacy icons in younger generations. The 2023 FNAF film adaptation (starring Matthew Lillard as Afton) brought the character to mainstream audiences. His story — a man who committed unspeakable crimes, died horrifically as a direct consequence, and yet somehow persists — is a genuinely compelling horror tragedy, elevated by years of community-driven lore excavation that turned a simple jumpscare game into one of gaming's most elaborate mythologies.
