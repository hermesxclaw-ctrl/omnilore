Form

Ao Run is typically depicted as a majestic dragon in full imperial regalia, often shown with a dragon's head, human-like body, and a long, serpentine tail. He wears elaborate robes embroidered with wave patterns and pearls, and holds a ceremonial tablet (hu) or a pearl (the 'Dragon Pearl' or 'Flame Pearl') representing his power over the sea and weather. In some depictions, he appears as a fully anthropomorphic figure with a dragon headdress.

Powers

Hydrokinesis Ao Run has absolute control over the waters of the Western Sea, including the ability to create or calm storms, summon tsunamis, and manipulate currents. All versions His power over water is limited to his own realm. He cannot command the seas of other Dragon Kings without their permission. In some texts, he can control all water on earth; in others, only his specific sea.

Weather Manipulation He can summon rain, wind, and storms. This is a key function of his role as a Dragon King, as he is responsible for providing rain for agriculture. Folk religion, Fengshen Yanyi This power is often depicted as being subject to the approval of the Jade Emperor. He cannot create weather that goes against celestial decree. In some myths, the Dragon Kings have full autonomy over weather; in others, they are strictly bound by heavenly orders.

Shapeshifting Like all Dragon Kings, Ao Run can take on a fully human form, a fully draconic form, or a hybrid form. All versions The transformation is often depicted as being uncomfortable or requiring a specific artifact (like a dragon robe) to maintain. None found.

Immortality & Longevity As a divine being, Ao Run is immortal, or at least has a lifespan measured in millennia. All versions He is still subject to the laws of heaven and can be punished or killed by more powerful deities. None found.

Authority over Marine Life He commands all creatures of the sea, from the smallest fish to the largest whales, and can summon them as an army or as servants. Folk religion, Fengshen Yanyi This authority is limited to the creatures of his sea. None found.

Behavior & Habitat

Ao Run is a dignified and powerful deity, but his behavior is often portrayed as cautious and politically minded. Unlike his more impulsive brother Ao Guang (Dragon King of the East Sea), Ao Run is more likely to seek celestial arbitration than to take direct action. He is a guardian of his realm, but also a vassal who understands the importance of not overstepping his bounds.

Accounts

After the young hero Nezha kills Ao Bing, the son of Ao Guang (Dragon King of the East Sea), Ao Guang summons his three brothers — Ao Qin (South), Ao Run (West), and Ao Shun (North) — to seek revenge. They collectively confront Nezha and his father, Li Jing. Ao Run, as the Dragon King of the West, is portrayed as a formidable ally, but the Dragon Kings are ultimately outmaneuvered by Nezha's cleverness and his connection to the celestial sage Taiyi Zhenren. The Dragon Kings are forced to appeal to the Jade Emperor for justice, but Nezha's teacher intervenes, and the matter is resolved in a way that humiliates the Dragon Kings.

The Dragon King of the West Sea appears as a minor character, often providing assistance or information to Sun Wukong and his companions. He is depicted as a bureaucratic official of the celestial court, managing his realm and responding to requests from more powerful figures.

In many coastal and riverine communities, Ao Run is venerated as a rain-bringer and protector against floods. Local officials and villagers would perform rituals at his temples, offering sacrifices and processions to beseech him for good weather and bountiful harvests. These rituals often involved the symbolic 'awakening' of the dragon from his slumber.

Scholarly Debates
Name and Identity
Ao Run is the canonical name for the Dragon King of the West Sea. ⇌ The Dragon King of the West Sea is sometimes unnamed or given a different name in various texts and adaptations.
Evidence for A: Fengshen Yanyi explicitly names him Ao Run.
Evidence for B: Journey to the West does not consistently name him, and some local traditions use different names.
Power and Authority
Ao Run is a powerful deity with significant autonomy over his realm. ⇌ Ao Run is a minor celestial bureaucrat with limited power, subject to the Jade Emperor's will.
Evidence for A: Folk traditions depict him as a direct intervener in human affairs, controlling weather and granting blessings.
Evidence for B: In Journey to the West, he is a minor functionary who must follow orders.
Role in the Nezha Story
Ao Run is a vengeful adversary who seeks to punish Nezha. ⇌ Ao Run is a reluctant participant, dragged into the conflict by his brother Ao Guang.
Evidence for A: In some versions, he actively attacks Nezha.
Evidence for B: In others, he is more passive, following the lead of his brother.
What We Don't Know

The primary evidence for Ao Run is literary and folkloric. There is limited archaeological or epigraphic evidence specifically naming 'Ao Run' as opposed to a more generic 'Dragon King of the West Sea'. Further research into local temple records and regional gazetteers could provide more detailed accounts of his veneration. The exact nature of his cult in different historical periods is not fully documented. The relationship between the literary character and the folk deity requires further scholarly analysis.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Ao Run as the CEO of a major ocean corporation. He has immense power over his domain (the Western Sea), commands a vast workforce (marine life), and can influence the global economy (weather and trade). However, he is ultimately answerable to the board of directors (the Jade Emperor and the celestial bureaucracy) and must navigate complex political alliances with his rival CEOs (the other Dragon Kings). He is not a rogue titan like Poseidon; he is a powerful, but constrained, executive.
