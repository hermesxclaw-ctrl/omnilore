Bergbúi

I have lived in this mountain since before the land was named. Your farm is at my feet. Bring me butter, greet me when you pass — and I will send driftwood to your shore and keep the rockfall from your sheep.

Who It Is

The Bergbúi (literally 'mountain-dweller') is a mountain spirit or troll of Icelandic folklore, distinct from the giant Bergrisi of Norse mythology — the Bergbúi is smaller, more solitary, and more intimately tied to specific local landscape features. He lives inside a particular mountain, cliff, or large rock formation, and is the supernatural owner-occupant of that feature. The Bergbúi is not inherently hostile but territorial: he punishes those who quarry his rock, mock his home, or fail to give proper greeting when passing. He may cause rockfalls, sudden fog, or strange echoes to warn away intruders. In some legends, the Bergbúi emerges at night to visit nearby farms, warming himself by the hearth (leaving behind a pile of stones or gravel as evidence), or he may exchange gifts with respectful farmers — a chunk of gold-bearing quartz for a bowl of milk. A famous Bergbúi legend tells of a farmer who heard a voice from the mountain asking for butter; when he obliged, the Bergbúi rewarded him with a steady supply of driftwood (precious in treeless Iceland) washing up on his shore. The Bergbúi can also be petitioned for knowledge of underground water sources, mineral deposits, or lost sheep. He is closely related to the Huldufólk (Hidden People) and Landvættir, but is more localized and chthonic — tied to a specific rock, not the land generally. The Bergbúi represents the Icelandic perception that the landscape itself is occupied: every mountain has a resident, every cliff a guardian, and human use of the land is always conditional on negotiation with these invisible neighbours.
