Who It Is

SCP-3000 is a colossal serpentine entity dwelling in the depths of the Bay of Bengal, measuring an estimated 600 to 900 kilometers in length — making it the largest known living organism in the Foundation universe. Its body is eel-like, dark gray, and nearly featureless, with no visible eyes, mouth, or other distinct anatomical features along its length. The head, which has only been observed via deep-sea submersibles, is vaguely humanoid in shape — an enormous, gaunt face with closed eyes, suspended in the abyssal darkness. SCP-3000 was discovered during a Foundation investigation into mass disappearances of fishing vessels. The entity's presence induces extreme paranoia, panic, and memory loss in nearby humans — an effect the Foundation has weaponized. When processed, the thick, viscous fluid (designated Y-909) secreted by SCP-3000's body can be refined into powerful amnestics used throughout the Foundation to erase memories of anomalous events from civilian populations. This makes SCP-3000 critically important — it is the primary source of Foundation amnestics, and without it, the entire veil of secrecy protecting humanity from knowledge of the anomalous would collapse. The entity appears to be in a state of permanent torpor, perpetually consuming and digesting something unknown on the ocean floor. Its psychic emissions, while dangerous up close, are theorized to be the byproduct of its digestive process or possibly its dreams. The Foundation operates a permanent harvesting facility built into SCP-3000's body, manned by personnel in heavily shielded suits who extract Y-909 while monitoring the entity's slow, rhythmic movements. The name Anantashesha is drawn from Hindu mythology — Ananta-Shesha, the endless serpent upon which Vishnu rests in the cosmic ocean. Whether this entity is the source of that myth or merely coincidentally similar is unknown. SCP-3000 represents the terrifying scale of the anomalous universe: something so vast that the Foundation's entire operation depends on it, yet so incomprehensible that it can never be fully understood or controlled.
