Who It Is

Before the first sunrise, before the first bird sang, there was only a silent, frozen world. Then Yhi opened her eyes — and that simple act of waking became the dawn of all life. She is the Sun Mother of the Gamilaraay people, a creator whose power is not in thunderbolts or world-shattering force, but in the gentle, persistent warmth that calls every living thing from its slumber. She is the cosmic alarm clock of an entire continent, and her story is a reminder that creation can be as quiet as a seed cracking open in the soil.

Yhi is a creator and life-giver. She acts with deliberate, loving intent, waking the sleeping life-forms of the earth with her warmth and light. She is a patient, nurturing figure who does not force life but rather invites it to stir and grow. Her journey across the sky is a daily act of re-creation, a cycle of awakening that sustains all living things.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Yhi is recorded in the Gamilaraay (Kamilaroi) language of southeastern Australia. The precise linguistic root is not definitively established in available sources, but the name is consistently associated with the sun and light in the recorded traditions.

Pronunciation — EE-hee (approximate; the Gamilaraay 'y' is pronounced like the English 'y', and the 'hi' is a short syllable)

Aliases — Yhi the Sun Goddess, Yhi the Sun Mother

Powers
Solar Radiance

Yhi embodies the sun's light and warmth. Her primary power is her ability to bring life and activity to the world through her radiance. This is her defining attribute.

Limit: Her power is tied to the cycle of day and night; she must travel across the sky, and her absence brings darkness and dormancy.

Life Awakening

Yhi's light awakens the dormant life-forms of the earth. Each plant and animal that stirs is a direct result of her touch. She is the catalyst for the world's vitality.

Limit: She awakens what is already there; she does not create life from nothing, but rather rouses it from its primordial sleep.

Cosmic Journey

Yhi's daily journey across the sky is a cosmic act of creation, as her light re-awakens the world each morning. This journey is a fundamental part of her nature and power.

Limit: Her journey is cyclical and predictable, not a free-willed act of travel.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Yhi is described as a radiant, benevolent feminine presence who emerges from the darkness of the primordial void. She is not described in physical terms like a humanoid figure; rather, she is the sun itself, a source of warmth and light. Her appearance is one of pure incandescence, a blinding brightness that brings life to the dormant earth.

Symbols — The sun itself, light, warmth, the dawn, the act of waking, the sprouting of plants, the stirring of animals. Her role as a 'mother' is symbolic of her life-giving and nurturing power.

Domains — Sun, light, creation, life, warmth, awakening, the sky, the daily cycle of day and night, the seasonal cycle of growth and dormancy.

Accounts I
The Sun Mother's AwakeningGamilaraay oral tradition, recorded by K. Langloh Parker in 'Australian Legendary Tales' (1896)

Pre-colonial (recorded 1896)

In the beginning, the earth was a cold, dark, and lifeless void. Yhi, the Sun Mother, slept in this darkness. When she awoke, she opened her eyes, and her light flooded the world. She traveled across the sky, and wherever her light touched the ground, life began to stir. Plants sprouted, animals woke, and the world became vibrant and full of activity. Yhi's journey across the sky became the daily cycle of day and night, and her warmth became the source of all life. The world becomes populated with life. The cycle of day and night is established. Yhi's role as the life-giving mother of all creation is affirmed.

Accounts II
The Descent of Yhi: Bringing Light to the Dark WorldAboriginal Australian oral traditions (various nations)

The Dreaming

In the Dreamtime, the world lay in darkness, cold, silent, and still. Yhi, the Sun Goddess, slept beneath the earth in deep dreaming. The other spirit beings grew tired of the eternal darkness and called out to her, urging her to wake and bring light to the world. Stirred by their calls, Yhi opened her eyes for the first time, and light flooded across the land. She rose from her underground resting place and began to walk across the sky, her radiance transforming everything it touched. Where her light fell, dormant seeds sprouted, flowers opened their petals, and animals frozen in the darkness stretched and began to move. Yhi walked across the entire sky, bringing warmth, growth, and life to every corner of the world. At the end of her journey, she descended again beneath the earth and darkness returned, but now the world knew light, and all living things waited through the night for her return. Yhi is a pan-Aboriginal deity. — Yhi is specifically a Gamilaraay figure. Different Aboriginal nations have their own distinct creation stories and spiritual figures. She is not a universal Aboriginal sun goddess.

Yhi is the same as other sun gods like Ra or Amaterasu. — While there are thematic parallels (a sun deity associated with creation and life), Yhi is a unique figure within her own cultural context. These are comparative observations by outsiders, not claims made within Gamilaraay tradition.

The stories as recorded by Parker are the definitive, original versions. — Parker's recordings are a colonial-era mediation. They are a valuable record, but they are not a direct, unaltered transcript of the oral tradition. The original stories may have been longer, more detailed, or different in emphasis.
