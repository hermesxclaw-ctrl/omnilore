Who It Is

Imagine a world without the sun. Not a cloudy day, but a universe where the source of all light and warmth has simply... closed the door, gone inside, and refused to come out. That is the setting of the Amano-Iwato myth, where Japan's sun goddess, Amaterasu, heartbroken by her brother's rampage, retreats into a cave and plunges the cosmos into eternal night. The gods must throw a party so loud, so lewd, and so hilarious that she just has to peek outside.

Amaterasu's personality is complex. She is primarily depicted as a benevolent, nurturing figure who brings order and light to the world. She is the divine weaver, creating the fabric of the cosmos in her sacred weaving hall. However, she also displays a capacity for profound grief, withdrawal, and even a degree of petulance when deeply wounded by her brother's actions. Her retreat into the rock-cave is a moment of deep vulnerability, and her re-emergence is a triumph of communal cunning and joy. Powers
Solar Radiance

Amaterasu's fundamental power is her ability to generate and control light. Her very presence illuminates the heavens and the earth. When she withdrew to the rock-cave, the world was plunged into darkness. Her light is not just physical but also spiritual, representing truth, order, and life.

Limit: Her light is not a weapon but a life-giving force. It is intrinsically tied to her presence and will.

Divine Weaving

Amaterasu is depicted as a weaver, creating the garments of the kami and, symbolically, the fabric of the cosmos. This is a creative, ordering power that aligns her with other world-creating deities who use craft as a form of creation.

Limit: This is a domestic, creative power, not a martial one. It is disrupted by Susanoo's violence.

Authority over the Heavenly Realm (Takamagahara)

As the ruler of Takamagahara, Amaterasu holds supreme authority over the heavenly kami. She commands them, delegates tasks, and makes final judgments on matters of cosmic importance. This authority is the model for imperial rule on earth.

Limit: Her authority is challenged by Susanoo, but she prevails through a combination of strategy and the support of the other kami.

Granting the Imperial Mandate

Amaterasu holds the power to bestow the right to rule the earthly realm. She sent her grandson, Ninigi, to pacify and rule Japan, a mandate that was passed down to the imperial line. This is her most politically consequential power.

Limit: This power is exercised once, in the mythological past, and its legitimacy is a matter of historical and political interpretation.

Hidden in the Rock-Cave (Amano-Iwato)

Amaterasu's ability to withdraw her light from the world is a unique and dramatic power. It is a form of cosmic withdrawal that demonstrates the absolute dependence of the world on her presence. This is not a deliberate weapon but an act of profound grief and withdrawal.

Limit: She is lured out by a combination of music, dance, and the sight of her own reflection, demonstrating that her withdrawal can be reversed.

Symbols — The sun disc is her most fundamental symbol. The Yata no Kagami (Eight-Span Mirror) is her most sacred symbol and one of the Three Imperial Regalia. The jewel (magatama) is also associated with her, particularly in the Amano-Iwato episode. The Ise Shrine itself, with its architectural simplicity and use of plain wood, is a symbol of her purity and permanence. The Chrysanthemum Throne is a political symbol derived from her legacy. Domains — Amaterasu's primary domain is the sun, light, and the heavens (Takamagahara, the High Plain of Heaven). She is also the patron deity of the imperial family and the Japanese state. Her domains extend to weaving, agriculture (particularly rice cultivation, as the sun is essential for crops), and the fundamental cosmic order. As the ancestress of the imperial line, she is also a deity of sovereignty, legitimacy, and national identity.

Accounts I
The Birth of the Sun GoddessKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (Kamiyo)

After returning from the underworld (Yomi), the creator god Izanagi performed a purification ritual in the river Woto in Tsukushi. As he washed his left eye, the goddess Amaterasu was born. In the Kojiki, she is born from his left eye. The Nihon Shoki offers multiple variants: she is born from his left eye, from a bronze mirror he held, or from his hand. She was radiant and beautiful, and Izanagi, overjoyed, gave her a necklace of jewels and decreed that she should rule the High Plain of Heaven (Takamagahara). Amaterasu is established as the ruler of the heavens and the most powerful of the 'Three Precious Children' born from Izanagi's purification, alongside her brothers Tsukuyomi (moon) and Susanoo (storm).

Accounts II
The Amano-Iwato (Rock-Cave) EpisodeKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (Kamiyo)

Amaterasu's brother, Susanoo, the storm god, was given dominion over the seas but wept and wailed, causing great calamities. When Amaterasu asked why, he said he wished to go to the land of his mother, Izanami. He then went to Takamagahara to say farewell, but his behavior became destructive and violent. He destroyed Amaterasu's rice fields, defecated in her palace, and, most grievously, flayed a heavenly horse and threw it through the roof of her sacred weaving hall, killing one of her weaving maidens. In grief and fury, Amaterasu retreated into the rock-cave (Amano-Iwato), plunging everything into darkness. The eight hundred countless kami gathered to devise a plan. They placed a rooster at the cave's entrance, gathered a mirror (Yata no Kagami) and jewels, and the goddess Ame-no-Uzume performed a lewd and boisterous dance on an overturned tub, causing the kami to laugh uproariously. Curious, Amaterasu opened the cave door a crack. The Dispatched Grandson (Tenson Kōrin)Kojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (Kamiyo)

After the pacification of the earthly realm (Ashihara no Nakatsukuni) by the deities Ōkuninushi and others, Amaterasu decided to send her grandson, Ninigi-no-Mikoto, to rule Japan. She gave him the three sacred treasures: the Yata no Kagami (mirror), the Yasakani no Magatama (jewel), and the Kusanagi no Tsurugi (sword, which was originally found by Susanoo). She told Ninigi that the mirror was to be worshipped as her own spirit. Ninigi descended to the peak of Takachiho in Hyūga (southern Kyushu) and established the divine mandate for the imperial line. This story is the mythological foundation for the legitimacy of the Japanese imperial family, establishing a direct divine lineage from the sun goddess to the Emperor. The Three Imperial Regalia become the symbols of this legitimacy.

Amaterasu is the 'creator' of the universe. — Amaterasu is a major deity, but she is not the creator of the universe. The world was created by Izanagi and Izanami. Amaterasu was born later and given dominion over the heavens. She is a ruler, not a creator.

Amaterasu is the most powerful deity in Shinto. — While she is the most prominent and politically important deity, 'power' in Shinto is not a simple hierarchy. Deities have different domains and roles. Izanagi and Izanami, as creators, hold a different kind of fundamental power. Her political supremacy is a result of her connection to the imperial line.

Amaterasu and the Sun are the same thing. — Amaterasu is a personified deity who is associated with the sun. She is not the sun itself, but its divine essence or ruler. The sun is her symbol and her domain.

Amaterasu is a purely benevolent and peaceful goddess. — While primarily benevolent, her mythology includes moments of withdrawal, grief, and indirect violence. She is not a passive figure but a powerful ruler who can be wounded and who makes decisive, sometimes harsh, judgments (e.g., her anger at Tsukuyomi for killing Ukemochi).
