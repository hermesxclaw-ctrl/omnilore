Who It Is

Chakreshvarī is the Yakshini (attendant goddess) of the 1st Tīrthaṅkara Rishabhanatha (Ādinātha), and traditionally considered the first and highest among the 24 Yakshinis. Her name means 'Queen of the Discus' or 'She of the Wheel,' and she is depicted with a discus (chakra) as her distinguishing emblem. She is typically shown seated on a lotus or a vulture/garuda, with four arms holding a discus, a conch, a mace, and a lotus (in Śvetāmbara tradition) or seated on a vehicle with the same attributes (Digambara tradition). In some depictions, she has a thousand-spoked golden wheel behind her head. As the attendant of the first Tīrthaṅkara, Chakreshvarī occupies a special position in the Jain pantheon. She is worshipped for protection, the removal of obstacles, and success in endeavors. Her discus is a weapon that destroys ignorance and evil forces. Like the other Yakshinis, she represents the accessible, responsive aspect of the divine — unlike the Tīrthaṅkaras, who are beyond interference in worldly affairs, she actively intervenes in the lives of her devotees. Chakreshvarī is particularly associated with prosperity and victory, and her temples are found across India, especially in Gujarat and Rajasthan.

Origins

Chakreshvarī is the Yakshini of Rishabhanatha (Ādinātha), and thus the first of the 24 Yakshinis. She was appointed by the gods to serve as the protector and attendant of the first Tīrthaṅkara. Her discus represents the wheel of dharma that the Tīrthaṅkara sets in motion.

Attested in Jain Āgamas / Digambara and Śvetāmbara TraditionsEra c. 5th century CE onward
