The Approach

In the luminous pantheon of Jain goddesses, Chakreshvarī stands as the radiant attendant of the first Tīrthaṅkara, Ṛṣabhanātha, her four arms bearing the emblems of royal power and spiritual victory. She is the divine embodiment of the cosmic order that upholds the Jain path, a figure whose worship bridges the earthly realm of kings and the celestial sphere of liberated souls.

Content advisory. None. This figure is a benevolent deity in the Jain tradition; no violent or disturbing material is associated with her.

Etymology

From Sanskrit 'chakra' (wheel, discus) and 'īśvarī' (queen, goddess), meaning 'Goddess of the Discus' or 'Queen of the Wheel'.

Cultural Ownership

Jainism, particularly the Digambara and Śvetāmbara traditions, with significant worship in Western and Central India.

Disputed Identifications

Sometimes conflated with the yakshī Ambikā, but they are distinct attendants of different Tīrthaṅkaras. No major disputes.

Description

Appearance

Chakreshvarī is typically depicted as a beautiful goddess with four arms, seated on a lotus or standing, with a serene expression. She holds a chakra (discus) in one hand, and often a lotus, conch, and mace or goad in the others. She is adorned with elaborate jewelry and a crown, and is often shown with a small figure of Ṛṣabhanātha on her head or in her crown.

Symbols & Iconography

The chakra (discus) is her primary attribute, symbolizing the cosmic law (dharma) and the wheel of time. The lotus represents purity, the conch represents the primordial sound, and the mace or goad represents power and control. Her four arms signify omnipresence and the four directions.

Behaviour & Personality

As a yakshī, she is a benevolent guardian and protector of the Jain faith. She is believed to bestow blessings, grant boons, and protect devotees from harm. She is also associated with wealth and prosperity.

Domains & Habitats

She resides in the celestial abode of the yakshas and yakshīs, but is often invoked in temples and shrines dedicated to Ṛṣabhanātha. She is worshipped in Jain homes and temples, particularly in Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Karnataka.

Version Differences

In Śvetāmbara iconography, she is often shown with a child (her son) and is more pacific. In Digambara depictions, she is more martial, sometimes riding a lion or an elephant, and holds weapons. The number of arms may vary from two to four.

Granting boons and wishes

As a yakshī, she can grant material and spiritual benefits to her devotees, including wealth, health, and success.

Limitations

Her powers are subordinate to the Tīrthaṅkara and the teachings of Jainism; she cannot grant liberation (moksha).

Stories & Myths

Attendant of Ṛṣabhanātha

Source Jain mythology, likely derived from the Āvaśyaka-niryukti and later commentarial worksCulture JainPeriod Unknown; possibly early centuries CE

Summary

Chakreshvarī is the yakshī (attendant goddess) assigned to Ṛṣabhanātha, the first Tīrthaṅkara. She was created by the gods to protect the Tīrthaṅkara and his teachings. She is often depicted alongside him in sculptures.

Participants

Chakreshvarī

Ṛṣabhanātha

Consequences

She became a prominent object of worship in Jainism, especially among the laity.

Variants

In some texts, she is said to have been a queen in a previous life who earned her position through merit.
