The primary domain is that of music, particularly the lyre, and its power to shape the physical world. This connects to broader domains of civilization, city-building, civic order, and the arts. As a female figure, she might also be associated with the Muses and the patron goddesses of poetry and song. Her domain is the threshold where art becomes action, where sound becomes stone.

Form

There is no verified physical description of a female 'Amphiona'. If we extrapolate from the iconography of the male Amphion, who is typically depicted as a young, beardless man holding a lyre, a female counterpart would likely be depicted as a beautiful, muse-like figure, perhaps in the style of a Terpsichore or a kitharistria (female lyre player), often seen in classical vase painting. She would likely be shown in a long chiton, holding a lyre or kithara, possibly with the walls of Thebes in the background as a symbol of her power. This is a reconstruction based on artistic parallels, not an attested description.

Powers

Musical Construction (Theurgy of the Lyre) The ability to move stones, shape stone, and construct massive architectural works through the power of music played on the lyre. This is the defining power of the male Amphion, and by extension, of the female Amphiona. It is a form of sonic or musical magic that imposes harmony and order onto the physical world. Attested for the male Amphion in Apollodorus (Bibliotheca 3.5) and many other sources. The power is not simply a metaphor for 'inspiring' workers; it is a literal, magical act where the stones move of their own accord. The power is only attested for the construction of the walls of Thebes. It is not shown to be a general-purpose power. It seems to be a specific, perhaps one-time, gift from the gods (Hermes gave the lyre) to accomplish a specific task. The limits of the power (duration, scale, ability to destroy as well as create) are never tested in the myths. There is no contradiction on the core power, but the exact mechanism is debated by scholars: was it the 'pure' music, the specific notes, or the divine will of Hermes channeled through the lyre? The myth is vague, leaving room for interpretation.

Divine Patronage (Gift of Hermes) Amphion was granted the lyre by Hermes, the god of music, invention, and trickery. This gift is the source of his power. For a female Amphiona, this patronage would be a direct connection to a major Olympian god, elevating her from a mortal musician to a divinely empowered figure. Attested in Apollodorus (Bibliotheca 3.5) and other sources. The gift is central to the myth. The power is contingent on the divine gift. It is not an innate, self-taught skill. This implies a dependency on the gods and a specific purpose for the gift. None. The source of the power is consistently attributed to Hermes.

Behavior & Habitat

In the absence of any narrative about a female Amphiona, we can only infer behavior from the male figure's attributes. The key behavioral trait is the use of music not just for entertainment, but as a constructive, even magical, force. This Amphiona would be a civilizing figure, using her art to build, to order, and to create harmony from chaos. She would be the antithesis of brute force, embodying the power of intellect and art over raw strength.

Accounts

Amphion and his twin brother Zethus, sons of Zeus and Antiope, were raised by shepherds after their mother was persecuted. When they came of age, they decided to build the walls of the city of Thebes. Zethus, the strongman, carried huge stones on his back, while Amphion, the musician, played his lyre. The stones, charmed by his music, moved of their own accord and fitted themselves into place. The city was thus built with a unique blend of brute strength and divine art.

Amphion married Niobe, the daughter of Tantalus. Niobe was proud of her many children (the Niobids) and mocked the goddess Leto, who had only two children (Apollo and Artemis). In revenge, Apollo and Artemis killed all of Niobe's children. Amphion, in some versions, is also killed by the gods, or dies of grief. Niobe, in her eternal grief, was turned into a rock on Mount Sipylus, weeping forever.

Scholarly Debates
The Nature of the Musical Power
The power is literal: the stones moved of their own accord due to the magic of the music. ⇌ The power is metaphorical: the music was so inspiring that it motivated the workers to build more efficiently, and the 'moving stones' is a poetic exaggeration.
Evidence for A: Apollodorus and most mythographers state it as a literal fact. The myth is a 'just-so story' explaining the city's magic
Evidence for B: Modern rationalist interpretations, common in the 19th and 20th centuries, sought to demythologize the story.
The Existence of a Female 'Amphiona'
'Amphiona' is a distinct, albeit rare, female figure, a personification of the lyre or of musical power. ⇌ 'Amphiona' is nothing more than a grammatical variant or a scribal error for the male 'Amphion', and does not represent a distinct figure.
Evidence for A: A few late antique/Byzantine scholia and lexica use the name in a way that could be interpreted as a personification.
Evidence for B: There is no narrative, no cult, and no artistic depiction of a female Amphiona. All major sources use the male form.
Amphion's Death
Amphion was killed by Apollo as part of the punishment for Niobe's hubris. ⇌ Amphion went mad with grief and killed himself, or died of a broken heart.

What We Don't Know

The primary gap is the lack of any substantial ancient source for a distinct female figure named 'Amphiona'. The dossier is a reconstruction and a creative extrapolation from the male figure of Amphion. Further research is needed to: 1) Exhaustively search late antique and Byzantine scholia and lexica for any other uses of the name 'Amphiona'. 2) Investigate any possible syncretic connection with the Muses or other female musical figures. 3) Examine the reception of the myth in modern feminist retellings, which might have created a female Amphiona in contemporary literature or art. 4) The iconography of the male Amphion is well-studied, but a comprehensive search for any female figure with a lyre in the context of the Theban myth cycle is needed.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Amphion as the ultimate 'creative director' of antiquity. Zethus is the project manager who moves the heavy equipment, but Amphion is the visionary whose design and 'vibe' literally make the building materialize. If Orpheus is the musician who makes nature weep, Amphion is the one who makes nature work.
