Who It Is

In the fierce world of the Ulster Cycle, where warriors boasted and bled for honor, Cet mac Mágach of Connacht was a name that made even the bravest Ulster heroes pause. His deeds were written in blood and severed heads, and his rivalry with Conall Cernach was the stuff of legend—a rivalry that would end in a ford, with both men near death, but only one walking away.

Cet is portrayed as proud, boastful, and ruthless, typical of Ulster Cycle heroes. He is cunning (stealing the brain of Mesgegra) and physically powerful. He shows respect for worthy opponents, as seen in his interaction with Conall at the feast.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Cet' may derive from Old Irish 'cét', meaning 'hundred' or 'first', but this is uncertain. 'Mac Mágach' means 'son of Mágach', a patronymic.

Pronunciation — Approximately 'Ket mak MAH-gakh' (with a guttural 'ch' sound at the end) in reconstructed Old Irish.

Aliases — Cet, Cét

Powers
Combat prowess

Cet is a champion warrior, capable of killing twenty-seven men in a single raid and defeating many Ulster heroes in single combat.

Limit: None specified, but he is ultimately defeated by Conall Cernach.

Sling mastery

Cet is skilled with a sling, using it to shoot the brain-ball of Mesgegra into Conchobar's head with lethal precision.

Limit: Only appears in this tale.

Boasting

Cet is adept at verbal combat, shaming opponents by recounting his victories over them.

Limit: He is ultimately outdone by Conall's greater boasts.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No explicit physical description of Cet survives in the texts. He is depicted as a formidable warrior, likely of noble bearing, but specific details are unknown.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography are associated with Cet in the sources. His identity is tied to his deeds and his rivalry with Conall.

Domains — Cet is associated with Connacht, but no specific dwelling or territory is mentioned. He travels for raids and feasts.

Accounts I
The Story of Mac Dá Thó's PigManuscripts at Trinity College Dublin and Bodleian Library

Medieval (12th century manuscript, but story likely older)

At a feast, warriors of Connacht and Ulster compete for the champion's portion. Cet shames all Ulster warriors by recounting how he defeated them. When Conall Cernach arrives, he boasts of defeating Cet's brother Anlúan, and Cet concedes, but says if Anlúan were present, he would outdo Conall. Conall then throws Anlúan's severed head at Cet. Cet is humiliated and the feast descends into a general battle (not detailed in this summary).

Accounts II
The Death of ConchobarMultiple manuscripts (at least five)

Medieval

Cet steals the calcified brain of Mesgegra, king of Leinster, which Conall had taken as a trophy. He shoots it from his sling, embedding it in Conchobar's head. The doctors cannot remove it without killing him, so they sew it up, warning him not to get excited. Years later, Conchobar hears of the crucifixion of Christ, flies into a rage, and the brain bursts from his head, killing him. Conchobar dies, fulfilling a prophecy. Cet's act is seen as a successful assassination.

The Death of Cet mac MágachBook of Leinster (Edinburgh manuscript)

Medieval

Cet raids Ulster, killing twenty-seven men and taking their heads. Conall Cernach pursues him through snow, catches up at a ford, and after a ferocious combat, kills Cet. Conall is left near death himself. Cet is dead, and Conall is gravely wounded but survives. This is the definitive end of their rivalry.
