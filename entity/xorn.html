Xorn

The Stone Glutton, The Gem-Hungry, Rock Shark

TypeElemental
CultureDungeons & Dragons (Wizards of the Coast / Hasbro)
RealmElemental Plane of Earth / The Underdark
ScaleIndividual / Small Clusters
AlignmentNeutral (hunger-driven)

We do not want your flesh. We want what's in your pocket. Give us the shiny. Give us the precious. Then we leave.

Who It Is

The Xorn is D&D's strangest elemental predator — a creature from the Plane of Earth whose entire body is a marvel of bizarre anatomy. Imagine a three-legged, radially symmetrical barrel of rocky flesh, about 5 feet tall and 5 feet wide, with a single enormous mouth on top and three arms spaced around its body, each ending in a circular, lamprey-like mouth of its own. The Xorn has no face in the traditional sense — its central maw is ringed by small, gem-like eyes giving it 360-degree vision. Its stony hide is rough and gray-brown, making it indistinguishable from a boulder when it remains still.

The Xorn's signature ability is its capacity to swim through solid stone and earth as easily as a fish moves through water. It phases through rock without leaving a tunnel, its body merging with and passing through solid matter via its elemental nature. This makes Xorns nearly impossible to trap, track, or contain. Their diet consists entirely of gems, precious metals, and minerals — a Xorn can consume a fist-sized diamond in seconds, digesting it in a furnace-like internal heat. They are indifferent to flesh and organic matter entirely; a Xorn will pass through a room full of people to reach a single gold coin behind them.

Xorn are not inherently hostile, but their hunger for treasure inevitably brings them into conflict with adventurers. A Xorn that detects a hoard of gold or a magic ring through solid stone will follow it relentlessly, waiting for an opportunity to snatch it. They can be negotiated with — a clever party might bribe a Xorn with a handful of gems to leave peacefully, or even to scout ahead through solid rock in exchange for payment. However, a starving Xorn is as dangerous as any predator, its three secondary mouths capable of biting with crushing force.

The Xorn is classic D&D weirdness — not an adaptation of folklore but a purely original creation from the game's 'anything goes' early design philosophy. Its design is simultaneously alien and logical: an elemental from a plane of solid stone would need to 'swim' through rock and eat minerals. Its strange radial symmetry and multi-mouthed anatomy feel genuinely otherworldly while still being mechanically coherent. Xorns appear in Baldur's Gate 3 (as optional Underdark encounters) and across D&D media, and their concept of a rock-swimming creature has influenced other fantasy settings and games.
