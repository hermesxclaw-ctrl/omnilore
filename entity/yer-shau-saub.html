Who They Are

Yer Shau (also called Saub or Siv Yis) is the supreme creator god in Hmong mythology, who fashioned the cosmos, humanity, and all living things. He established the spiritual order that governs the relationship between the seen world (ntuj no) and the spirit world (yeeb ceeb), and provided the rituals and knowledge that allow Hmong shamans to navigate between realms.

Origins

In the beginning, according to Hmong cosmology, there was only darkness and water — a formless void. Yer Shau existed alone in this primordial expanse. He first created light by separating the darkness, then formed the sky and the earth from the cosmic waters. Using his immense power, he fashioned the sun, moon, stars, mountains, rivers, and forests. Dissatisfied with the emptiness of the world, Yer Shau took clay from the riverbank and molded the first human figures — a man and a woman. He breathed into their nostrils, and they came to life. These first humans prospered and multiplied, but chaos entered the world through the malevolent spirit Ndu Nyong, who brought disease, death, and discord. Yer Shau responded by establishing the spiritual hierarchy, appointing guardian spirits (dab) for every household, and teaching the first shamans how to navigate the spirit world. He also created the qeej, the sacred bamboo instrument whose music can cross between worlds, and established the twelve-year cycle of rituals that maintain cosmic harmony.

Powers

Yer Shau possesses absolute creative power, capable of bringing matter and life into existence through will alone. He can manipulate the fabric of reality, dividing and arranging the cosmos according to divine order. He has dominion over life and death — granting souls to humans at birth and judging their fate after death. He hears all prayers and can dispatch spirits to intervene in mortal affairs. Through his authority, shamans are empowered to undertake soul-retrieval journeys, communicate with ancestors, and combat malevolent spirits. He controls the weather and the seasons, and can both bestow blessings of fertility and prosperity or withdraw them as punishment. His most important power is maintaining the cosmic balance between the seen and unseen worlds.

Cultural Significance

Yer Shau is central to Hmong spiritual life. His name is invoked in all major ceremonies: births, weddings, funerals, and the annual New Year rituals (Noj Peb Caug). Hmong shamans (txiv neeb) undergo years of training to master the chants that recount Yer Shau's creation, and their authority derives from his original mandate. The elaborate Hmong funeral rites — which can last days and involve multiple animal sacrifices, qeej music, and the ritual crossing of nine bridges — are understood as following the instructions Yer Shau gave at the dawn of time. The Hmong diaspora, particularly after the Vietnam War, has carried these traditions to communities in the United States, France, Australia, and elsewhere, where Yer Shau remains a living presence in family altars and community rituals.
