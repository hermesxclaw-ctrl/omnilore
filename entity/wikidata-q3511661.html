Who It Is

Before the Victoria River carved its way through the Northern Territory, a colossal serpent-woman dragged her body across the land, her scales flashing with all the colors of the sky. Her name was Abeguwo. She is not a gentle mother goddess, but a force of nature — a creator who can flood the world and a law-giver who punishes the unworthy. She is the Dreaming made flesh, a reminder that the land is alive, powerful, and demands respect.

Abeguwo is a powerful and often dangerous ancestral being. She is not a benign, nurturing mother goddess. She is associated with the raw power of nature — storms, floods, and the life-giving but also destructive force of water. She is a creator, but her creation is often violent and transformative. She is a figure of immense spiritual power, requiring respect and adherence to the correct ceremonial protocols. She is a guardian of sacred sites, particularly waterholes, and can be angered by those who break the law or desecrate these places.

Powers
Landscape Creation

Abeguwo shaped the physical landscape of the Victoria River District. Her movements, her body, and her actions created rivers, waterholes, hills, and other geological features. The specific features she created are known to the Ngarinyman people.

Limit: Her creative power is tied to the Ngarranggarni; she created the landscape in that time, but she is not continuously creating new landforms.

Control over Rain and Storms

Abeguwo is a rain-maker. She controls the storms and the rains, and her anger can bring devastating floods. She is also associated with the rainbow, which is her sign or her physical form.

Limit: Her control over the weather is not capricious; it is often in response to the actions of humans, particularly the breaking of laws or the desecration of sacred sites.

Shape-shifting

Abeguwo can change her form. She is most often described as a giant snake, but she can also appear as a human woman. This ability allows her to interact with humans and to travel across the landscape.

Limit: The extent and rules of her shape-shifting are not fully documented in public sources.

Guardianship of Sacred Sites

Abeguwo is the guardian of the waterholes and other sacred sites she created. She protects these places from desecration and punishes those who violate them.

Limit: Her guardianship is localized to her specific sites.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Abeguwo is described as a colossal, snake-like being, though her exact form can vary between stories. She is often depicted as an immense python or a water serpent, but she is also described as having the ability to take on a human (female) form. In her snake form, she is associated with the rainbow, and her body is sometimes said to shimmer with all the colors of the rainbow. She is a being of immense size and power, capable of shaping the lands

Symbols — The primary symbols associated with Abeguwo are the rainbow, water, and the serpent. The rainbow is seen as her physical manifestation or a sign of her presence. Waterholes are her body or her home. In contemporary art, she is often depicted as a large snake with rainbow-colored scales, sometimes with a human head or other anthropomorphic features. Her iconography is a powerful symbol of Ngarinyman identity and connection to country.

Domains — Abeguwo is primarily associated with water, rain, storms, and the rainbow. Her primary habitats are waterholes, rivers, and wetlands. She is also a sky-being, connected to the clouds and the rain. In the Ngarinyman landscape, specific waterholes and rock formations are her creation and are considered her sacred sites. Accounts I
The Creation of the Victoria RiverNgarinyman oral tradition

Ngarranggarni (The Dreaming)

Abeguwo's journey across the landscape created the Victoria River, a vital water source and central feature of Ngarinyman country. The river is not just a geographic feature; it is her creation and a living testament to her power. The creation of the Victoria River provided the resources for Ngarinyman life. The river itself became a sacred site, and Abeguwo became its eternal guardian.

Accounts II
Abeguwo and the Punishment of the LawbreakersNgarinyman oral tradition

Ngarranggarni (The Dreaming)

This story serves as a moral and legal lesson, demonstrating the consequences of breaking the law. Abeguwo is not a passive or benevolent figure; she is an active enforcer of the Dreaming law. The lawbreakers were destroyed, and the law was reaffirmed. The site of the punishment became a sacred, and potentially dangerous, place.

Abeguwo is a 'rain goddess' who is worshipped. — Abeguwo is a Dreaming ancestor, not a 'goddess' in the Western sense. She is a powerful being who controls rain and water, but she is not 'worshipped' in a way that is analogous to, say, Greek or Hindu deities. The relationship is one of respect, obligation, and adherence to law, not worship.

Abeguwo is just another name for the Rainbow Serpent. — While Abeguwo shares the serpentine form and rainbow/water association with the 'Rainbow Serpent', she is a distinct being unique to the Ngarinyman people. She is not a 'version' of a generic figure. Her stories, her name, and her cultural role are specific to the Ngarinyman.

Abeguwo is a benevolent, nurturing mother figure. — Abeguwo is a powerful and dangerous being. She is a creator, but she is also a destroyer and a punisher. She is not a 'mother goddess' in a gentle, nurturing sense. She demands respect and can be angered, with deadly consequences.
