Who It Is

From a Siberian workshop to the skies over a war-torn homeland, Antonov is the name behind the largest fixed-wing aircraft ever built—and a symbol of Ukrainian resilience. This is the story of a design bureau that turned colossal ambition into flying reality.

Known for innovation in very large aircraft and rough-field operations. Historically state-owned, with a reputation for engineering robustness.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Not applicable (company, not a physical entity). The company's products are aircraft, ranging from small utility planes to the massive An-225.

Symbols — The Antonov logo features a stylized wing and the company name. Aircraft carry the 'An-' prefix.

Domains — Headquartered in Kyiv, Ukraine. Production and design facilities in Ukraine, with historical sites in Novosibirsk and other Soviet locations.

Accounts I
Foundation and early yearsOfficial history, Oleg Antonov's memoirs

1946–1952

Established in Novosibirsk as OKB-153, moved to Kyiv in 1952. Became a major Soviet aircraft design bureau.

Accounts II
The An-225 MriyaAviation press

1980s–2022

Built the world's largest aircraft, the An-225 Mriya, as part of the Soviet space program. Destroyed in 2022 during the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Symbol of Ukrainian pride; plans for reconstruction announced.

Post-Soviet transitionNews reports

1991–present

After Ukrainian independence, Antonov became a Ukrainian state enterprise, facing financial challenges and seeking international partnerships. Integration into Ukroboronprom; ongoing efforts to revive production.
