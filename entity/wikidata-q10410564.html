Who It Is

On a dark, drizzly evening in the Korean countryside, a traveler might glimpse a hunched figure hopping along the muddy path—a single leg propelling it with uncanny speed, a conical hat shadowing two glowing eyes. This is the dokkakgwi, the one-legged ghost of Korean folklore, a creature that is less a malevolent spirit and more a mischievous, unpredictable force of nature. It challenges strong men to wrestling matches, rewards the kind with magical gifts, and punishes the greedy with cruel irony. Its story weaves together ancient Chinese mountain spirits, Korean shamanistic beliefs, and a rich tradition of morality tales, offering a window into a world where the supernatural is never far from the everyday.

Dokkakgwi are mischievous, amoral, and unpredictable. They are not purely evil but are known for playing pranks, leading travelers astray, and hiding belongings. They famously challenge humans to wrestling matches (ssireum), which they almost always win due to immense strength. They can be generous to those who please them and punitive to those who annoy them.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'dokkakgwi' (독각귀) is composed of the Korean words 'dok' (독) meaning 'one' or 'single', 'gak' (각) meaning 'leg', and 'gwi' (귀) meaning 'ghost' or 'spirit'. It literally translates to 'one-legged ghost' or 'one-legged demon'. The Chinese equivalent, 'Dujiaogui' (独脚鬼), has the same meaning.

Pronunciation — DOK-kahk-gwee

Aliases — Dujiaogui (Chinese), Dok-kak-gwi, One-Legged Ghost, One-Legged Demon

Ownership — The dokkakgwi is primarily a creature of Korean folklore, but its origins are linked to Chinese mythology, specifically the one-legged mountain spirits like the Kui and Wutong Shen

Powers
Superhuman Strength

Dokkakgwi possess immense physical strength, allowing them to easily defeat humans in wrestling matches.

Limit: None mentioned, but they can be defeated if their weakness (single leg) is exploited.

Magical Objects

Dokkakgwi are associated with magical items: the dokkaebi gamtu (a cap that grants invisibility) and the dokkaebi bangmangi (a club that can summon anything when struck on the ground).

Limit: The objects' powers may be limited by the wielder's intent or the dokkakgwi's control.

Shapeshifting or Illusion

Some accounts suggest dokkakgwi can lead humans astray, implying an ability to confuse or create illusions.

Limit: Not explicitly stated as a power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The dokkakgwi is typically described as a grotesque, hairy humanoid with a single powerful leg, allowing it to move with remarkable speed. It is often depicted wearing a satgat (conical bamboo hat) and a dorongi (straw raincoat), appearing on dark, drizzly days. Two glowing eyes peer from beneath the brim of its hat. In some variations, it has only one glaring eye and a fearsome expression.

Symbols — The single leg is the most distinctive symbol. The satgat and dorongi associate it with rural, agricultural settings and rainy weather. The dokkaebi gamtu (invisibility cap) and bangmangi (magical club) are common symbols of its magical nature.

Domains — They are associated with mountains, rural villages, and wilderness areas. They appear on dark, drizzly days, and are often encountered at night or in secluded places.

Accounts I
The Bump on the ChinKorean folktale

Traditional (oral tradition, collected in modern era)

A kind old man with a large bump on his chin stumbles upon a dokkaebi celebration. He joins in their singing, and the delighted dokkaebi remove his bump as a reward. A greedy neighbor with a similar bump tries to imitate him, but his unpleasant singing irritates the dokkaebi, who punish him by attaching the first man's bump to his own, leaving him with two. The kind man is rewarded and healed; the greedy man is punished with an additional bump.

Accounts II
The Man Who Defeated a DokkaebiKorean folktale

Traditional

A man is persistently challenged to a wrestling match by a dokkaebi. Learning that the creature's weakness lies in its single leg (or left side), he manages to defeat it. Depending on the version, the dokkaebi either flees in humiliation, leaving behind its magical club, or befriends the man and uses its powers to bring him wealth. The man gains wealth or a magical club, and the dokkaebi is either defeated or becomes an ally.
