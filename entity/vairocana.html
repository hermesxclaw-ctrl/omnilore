Who It Is

Vairocana is the Cosmic Buddha, the supreme deity of esoteric (Vajrayana) Buddhism and the central figure of the Avatamsaka Sutra, one of the most philosophically profound texts of the Mahayana tradition. His name, meaning 'The Illuminator' or 'He Who Is Like the Sun,' designates him as the source of all light and wisdom, the primordial Buddha from whom all other Buddhas emanate. In the Mahayana Trikaya (Three Bodies) doctrine, Vairocana represents the Dharmakaya—the Truth Body, the formless, all-pervading ultimate reality of Buddhahood itself, as distinct from the historical Buddha Shakyamuni (the Nirmanakaya, or Emanation Body) and the celestial Buddhas like Amitabha (the Sambhogakaya, or Enjoyment Body). The Avatamsaka Sutra describes a vast, infinitely interconnected cosmos in which every particle of dust contains entire world-systems, and Vairocana sits at the center of an inconceivably vast lotus universe, simultaneously present in every atom. His mudra is distinctive: in the esoteric tradition, he forms the 'wisdom fist' (vajra mudra or bodhyagri mudra), with the left index finger grasped by the right fist, symbolizing the union of method and wisdom. The colossal Daibutsu of Nara, Japan (the Todai-ji Buddha, completed in 752 CE), is a representation of Vairocana and was the largest bronze statue in the world for centuries. In the Shingon school of Japanese Buddhism founded by Kukai (Kobo Daishi), Vairocana is the central deity, and all of reality is understood as his cosmic body—mountains, rivers, and human beings are all manifestations of Vairocana's teaching. The famous Borobudur temple in Java, the largest Buddhist monument in the world, is also structured as a three-dimensional mandala with Vairocana at its summit.
