Who It Is

The infant stage of the Mimic life cycle. Crawlers are spider-like organisms, approximately 30cm in diameter, that seek out living hosts to which they attach, feeding parasitically on body heat and bioelectricity. Hosts often do not notice the attachment for days — the Crawler secretes a local anesthetic. Once attached, the Crawler slowly metabolizes the host's tissue, replacing it with Carnis tissue, and eventually detaches to enter the Meat Snake stage. Hosts rarely survive the process, and those who do are fundamentally changed — their replaced tissue continues to exhibit Carnis-like properties.

Form

A palm-sized arachnid with eight jointed legs, a bulbous central body of raw red muscle tissue, and a ventral mouth surrounded by small, flexible feeding tendrils. The legs end in microscopic hooks that anchor into flesh. When attached, the Crawler flattens against the host's skin, blending almost perfectly with surrounding tissue.

Powers

Parasitic attachment to living hosts

Anesthetic secretion

Tissue replacement with Carnis cells

Stage progression to Meat Snake
