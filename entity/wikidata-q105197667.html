Who It Is

In the quiet corridors of a Japanese high school, a girl with a cochlear implant walks alone, her silence a shield against a world that has already judged her. Momo Nishimiya, the deaf protagonist of 'A Silent Voice,' is not a legend of swords or sorcery, but a hero of a different kind—one whose battle is fought in the space between hearing and being heard, and whose victory is measured not in glory, but in the fragile, hard-won connections of friendship and forgiveness.

Shōko is gentle, kind, and deeply empathetic, often apologizing for things that are not her fault. She is persistent in trying to communicate, even when others reject her. She has a quiet, introverted nature but is not passive; she shows resilience in the face of bullying. She is also self-blaming and struggles with low self-esteem, which leads to her depression and suicide attempt. Her personality develops as she learns to value herself and accept friendship.

Powers
Sign language and written communication

Shōko communicates primarily through Japanese Sign Language (JSL) and written notes in a notebook. She is fluent in JSL and uses it with her family and later with Shōya as he learns to sign. She also lip-reads to some extent.

Limit: Her communication methods are limited by others' willingness to learn or read. She cannot hear spoken language, and her speech is difficult for others to understand, so verbal communication is a challenge.

Lip-reading

Shōko can lip-read, but it is imperfect and tiring. She often misunderstands or misses what others say, which leads to social difficulties.

Limit: Lip-reading requires facing the speaker, clear articulation, and good lighting. It is not reliable in group settings or when speakers turn away.

Empathy and emotional perception

Shōko is highly perceptive of others' emotions, often sensing when someone is upset or lying. This is not a supernatural ability but a heightened sensitivity developed through her reliance on non-verbal cues.

Limit: Her empathy can lead to self-blame and emotional burden, as she often internalizes others' negative feelings.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Shōko Nishimiya is a petite girl with short, light-brown hair and large, expressive brown eyes. She wears a hearing aid in each ear, and later in the story, she uses a cochlear implant on one side. She is often depicted in her school uniform, and her appearance is designed to convey vulnerability and quietness. Her facial expressions are limited due to her difficulty with verbal communication, but her eyes and gestures convey her emotions.

Symbols — The most significant symbols associated with Shōko are the hearing aids and the cochlear implant, which represent her disability and her connection to the hearing world. The 'X' marks that protagonist Shōya Ishida places over people's faces in his perception are a visual metaphor for his emotional isolation, and Shōko is initially marked with an 'X' before it is removed as he begins to accept her. Domains — The story is set in contemporary Japan, primarily in a suburban city. Key locations include the elementary school where the bullying occurs, the high school where the characters reunite, and the riverbank and bridge where Shōko and Shōya often meet. The film also features a school festival and a trip to a zoo, which are settings for key emotional moments.

Accounts I
The Bullying and IsolationA Silent Voice, manga volume 1; film opening.

Elementary school years (early 2010s).

Shōko transfers to a public elementary school and is immediately targeted by classmates, particularly Shōya Ishida, for her deafness. She is subjected to mockery, physical harassment, and social exclusion. Teachers fail to intervene effectively, and Shōko's attempts to befriend her classmates are rebuffed. The bullying escalates until Shōko is forced to transfer to another school. Shōko develops deep emotional scars and a tendency to blame herself. Shōya becomes the target of bullying himself after being exposed as the main perpetrator, leading to his own social isolation and guilt.

Accounts II
The Reunion and RedemptionA Silent Voice, manga volumes 2-7; film middle and climax.

High school years (mid-2010s).

Years later, Shōya, now a high school student, is consumed by guilt and seeks out Shōko to apologize. He learns sign language and gradually builds a friendship with her. Their relationship is complicated by Shōya's own social anxiety and Shōko's low self-worth. A series of misunderstandings and conflicts with old classmates lead to a crisis where Shōko attempts suicide, but Shōya saves her. The story culminates in a school festival where Shōya experiences a panic attack but is supported by Shōko and friends, symbolizing his acceptance into a community and his forgiveness of himself. Shōko and Shōya develop a deep, healing friendship. Shōya learns to forgive himself and connect with others. Shōko begins to value herself and finds a supportive social circle.

Shōko is mute. — Shōko is deaf, not mute. She can speak, but her speech is difficult to understand, and she chooses to communicate via sign language and writing.

The story is a romance. — While there are romantic undertones, the core of the story is about bullying, redemption, and self-acceptance. The creator has stated it is not a straightforward love story.

Shōko is a passive victim. — Shōko is resilient and actively tries to connect with others, despite the bullying. She is not merely a victim; she is a survivor who eventually finds strength.
