Mama Cocha

I am the water that surrounds your world. From Titicaca I rose; to the sea I return. Every fish is my child — take only what you need, and offer thanks.

Who It Is

Mama Cocha (Mama Qucha, 'Mother Sea') is the Inca goddess of the sea, lakes, and all bodies of water — the divine feminine force of the aquatic realm. She is the sister and consort of Viracocha (the creator) in some traditions, and together they are the parents of Inti (the sun) and Mama Quilla (the moon). Her domain encompasses the Pacific Ocean (which the Incas called 'Mama Cocha' — a single word for both the goddess and the sea itself), Lake Titicaca (the sacred lake from which Viracocha emerged), and all rivers, springs, and water sources. She was the patroness of fishermen, coastal communities, and anyone whose life depended on the bounty of the sea — a critical sector of the Inca economy given the rich fisheries of the Humboldt Current. Fishermen offered coca leaves, maize, and shells to Mama Cocha before setting sail, and her shrines dotted the Peruvian and Chilean coasts. She is also the mother of the tuna (the fish), whose appearance and disappearance govern the coastal seasons. Mama Cocha's worship was particularly important among the coastal peoples (Yunga) whom the Inca incorporated into their empire, and her veneration represented the empire's recognition of maritime as well as highland spiritual traditions. Unlike Pachamama (whose worship was ubiquitous and enduring), Mama Cocha was more localized to coastal contexts and has faded more from active practice. However, the name 'Mama Cocha' survives in contemporary Quechua to refer to the sea, and her legacy persists in the reverence for Lake Titicaca as a sacred site. She represents the Andean understanding that water — whether the vast Pacific, the high-altitude lakes, or the mountain streams — is a living, maternal presence that must be honored and propitiated.
