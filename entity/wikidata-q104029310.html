Who It Is

Before she became a name on a birth certificate, Melissa was a bee—and a goddess, a nymph, a priestess, and a hive. From the honey-fed infant Zeus to the silent initiate torn apart by angry women, the figure of Melissa buzzes through millennia of myth, her story as layered as the honeycomb she discovered.

As a nurturing nymph, she fed Zeus honey. As a priestess, she was silent and faithful to her vows, even under threat of death. As a goddess, she was a mediator and a source of regeneration.

Etymology — From Greek μέλισσα (mélissa), 'bee', derived from μέλι (meli), 'honey'. Hittite melit also means 'honey'.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Not explicitly described in the source material. As a nymph, she may be depicted as a beautiful young woman; as a bee, she would have insect form. In art, bee imagery is associated with her.

Symbols — Bees, honey, honeycomb, hive. Also associated with the moon and with souls (as bees symbolized souls).

Domains — Cretan caves (associated with Zeus's infancy), forests, and wild places where bees dwell. Also associated with the underworld (chthonian) and the moon.

Accounts I
Nurse of ZeusGreek mythology

Mythic

Melissa, a nymph and daughter of King Melisseus of Crete, helped raise the infant Zeus. She fed him honey, and in some versions, bees brought honey to his mouth. She was sister to Amaltheia, who fed him goat's milk. Melissa became the name for all nymphs who cared for Zeus as a baby. She was honored as the discoverer of honey.

Accounts II
The Priestess of DemeterPorphyry

Classical Antiquity

An elderly priestess of Demeter, named Melissa, was initiated into the mysteries by the goddess herself. When neighbors tried to force her to reveal the secrets, she remained silent. In anger, they tore her to pieces. Demeter sent a plague, and bees were born from her dead body. Bees were created from her body, and the priestesses of Demeter were called Melissae (bees).

The Hive of VenusOrphic fragment quoted by Natalis Comes

Ancient

Melitta is called the 'hive of Venus' (Aphrodite), a mighty fountain from which all kings and winged Loves descended. She is also identified with Seira and Demeter. Melitta is seen as a mother goddess, the source of all life.
