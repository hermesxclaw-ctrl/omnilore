Who It Is

In the high, cold valleys of the Colombian Andes, before the Spanish conquest, the Muisca people told of a beautiful, rebellious woman who challenged the strict moral order of their world. She was Huitaca, a goddess of drunkenness, pleasure, and liberation—a figure who defied the stern teachings of the civilizing hero Bochica and paid a terrible price for her defiance.

Huitaca is portrayed as rebellious, willful, and hedonistic. She defied the moral order imposed by Bochica, advocating for pleasure and freedom from strict rules. She is also depicted as persuasive and charismatic, able to sway people away from virtuous paths.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The meaning of 'Huitaca' is uncertain, but it is often associated with drunkenness, pleasure, or rebellion. Some sources suggest it may be related to the Muisca word for 'intoxication' or 'revelry,' though this is speculative.

Pronunciation — wee-TAH-kah

Aliases — Huytaca, Huecaca, Huitaca, the Goddess of Joy

Ownership — Muisca people, indigenous to the Altiplano Cundiboyacense in present-day Colombia

Powers
Influence over human behavior

Huitaca could inspire feelings of joy, pleasure, and intoxication in humans, leading them to abandon strict moral codes. She was said to encourage revelry and disregard for Bochica's laws.

Limit: Her influence was opposed by Bochica, who countered her effects with his own teachings.

Transformation

In some accounts, Huitaca had the ability to transform herself into an owl, a symbol of wisdom and night. However, this power was used against her as punishment, as she was turned into an owl by Bochica.

Limit: Her transformation was imposed by Bochica, not self-willed.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Huitaca is described as exceptionally beautiful, with long hair and a captivating presence. She is often depicted as a young woman, sometimes with ornaments or clothing typical of Muisca nobility. In some artistic renderings, she is shown with a baton or staff, symbolizing her authority.

Symbols — The owl is a primary symbol associated with Huitaca, as she was transformed into an owl by Bochica. She is also associated with the moon, due to her connection to Chía, and with elements of intoxication and festivity.

Domains — Huitaca is associated with the earth and the moon, and her domain includes drunkenness, pleasure, and liberation. She is said to have lived among the Muisca people in the highlands of Colombia, possibly in the region of Bogotá.

Accounts I
The Rebellion of HuitacaPedro Simón, Lucas Fernández de Piedrahita

Pre-Columbian, recorded in 17th century

Huitaca, a beautiful and influential woman, opposed the teachings of Bochica, a civilizing hero who brought moral laws to the Muisca. She convinced the people to indulge in pleasure, drunkenness, and festivities, undermining Bochica's authority. In response, Bochica transformed her into an owl as punishment, condemning her to roam the night. Huitaca's transformation served as a warning against disobedience and excess. The owl became a symbol of her punishment and a reminder of the consequences of defying divine order.
