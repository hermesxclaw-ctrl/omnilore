Who It Is

The Pillan (plural; singular Pillán) are the powerful ancestral spirits of the Mapuche people, associated with volcanoes, fire, thunder, and righteous punishment. When a great Mapuche ancestor, warrior, or machi (shaman) dies, they may ascend to become a Pillan — a spirit that dwells in the volcanoes or the sky and exercises enormous spiritual power. The Pillan are not gods in the Western sense but apotheosized ancestors who continue to influence the living world, particularly through natural phenomena: volcanic eruptions, lightning strikes, and earthquakes are all manifestations of Pillan power. They punish moral transgressions (especially violations of the admapu — the Mapuche moral code), and their anger can bring drought, illness, and crop failure. However, they also protect the righteous and can be petitioned through the machi for aid. Each volcano in Mapuche territory — Villarrica, Llaima, Lanín, Osorno, and many others — is the home of a specific Pillan, and the spectacular volcanic landscape of southern Chile is thus not empty wilderness but a densely populated spiritual geography. The Mapuche relationship with the Pillan reflects a broader indigenous pattern in which ancestral spirits are not distant or abstract but immediate, powerful presences in the land itself. During the centuries of warfare against the Spanish and, later, the Chilean and Argentine states, the Pillan were invoked for protection, and volcanic eruptions were sometimes interpreted as signs of spirit support for Mapuche resistance.
