Who It Is

In the quiet between worlds, a messenger descends—winged, radiant, and terrifying. Angels are the bridge between the mortal and the divine, appearing in the visions of prophets and the dreams of the faithful. But these beings of light are not merely gentle guardians; they are warriors, judges, and sometimes rebels. From the fiery seraphim to the fallen Lucifer, the angelic host embodies humanity's deepest hopes and fears about the unseen.

Angels are generally portrayed as obedient servants of God, carrying out divine commands. They can be messengers, warriors, protectors, and guides. They are often depicted as lacking free will in Jewish tradition, but in Christianity, some angels chose to rebel, indicating a degree of free will. They are usually benevolent, but can also execute judgment and punishment.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Angels are typically depicted as humanoid beings of extraordinary beauty, often with bird-like wings and halos, radiating divine light. However, biblical descriptions vary: seraphim have six wings (Isaiah 6:2), cherubim have four faces and four wings (Ezekiel 1:6), and some angels appear as ordinary men (Genesis 18:2). In art, they are often shown as beautiful youths or androgynous figures, but some texts describe them as fearsome or inhuman, wit

Symbols — Wings, halos, trumpets, swords, scales, and divine light. In Christian art, angels are often depicted with white robes, golden halos, and feathered wings. In Islamic tradition, angels are described as having wings in pairs, triplets, or quadruplets (Quran 35:1).

Domains — Heaven, the divine realm, and sometimes the earthly realm as intermediaries. They are considered spiritual beings that inhabit the celestial sphere. In Jewish and Christian cosmology, they reside in the presence of God, while in Islam, they are created from light and inhabit the heavens.

Accounts I
The AnnunciationLuke 1:26-38

1st century CE

The angel Gabriel appears to Mary to announce that she will conceive Jesus by the Holy Spirit. Mary accepts her role, leading to the birth of Jesus.

Jacob's LadderGenesis 28:10-22

c. 2nd millennium BCE (traditional dating)

Jacob dreams of a ladder connecting heaven and earth, with angels ascending and descending. Jacob receives a blessing and names the place Bethel.

Accounts II
The Fall of SatanRevelation 12:7-9, Isaiah 14:12-15 (interpreted)

1st century CE (Revelation), 8th century BCE (Isaiah)

A war in heaven results in Satan and his angels being cast out. Satan becomes the adversary of humanity, leading to temptation and evil in the world.

The Angelic LiturgyDead Sea Scrolls (4Q400-407)

c. 2nd century BCE

A liturgical text describing the praise offered by angels in the heavenly temple, which the community joins. It emphasizes the communion between the earthly and heavenly realms.

Zoroaster's VisionGathas (Yasna 31)

c. 1000 BCE

Vohu Manah (Good Purpose) reveals to Zoroaster the true nature of Ahura Mazda. Zoroaster begins his prophetic mission.

Angels are always depicted with wings. — In the Bible, angels often appear as ordinary humans without wings (e.g., Genesis 18). Wings are more common in art and later traditions.

All angels are benevolent. — Some angels are malevolent or fallen, and even good angels can execute harsh judgments.

Angels are the souls of deceased humans. — In most theological traditions, angels are a separate order of creation, distinct from humans.

Satan was always an evil angel. — In the Hebrew Bible, Satan ('the adversary') is a title for an angel who tests humans, not inherently evil.
