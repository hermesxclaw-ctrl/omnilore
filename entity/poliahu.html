Poli'ahu

You think this is a land only of fire? I wrap the summit in white. The tropics hold ice — and I am its queen.

Who It Is

Poli'ahu is the Hawaiian snow goddess who lives atop Mauna Kea, the highest mountain in the islands (13,796 ft / 4,207 m) — and one of the only places in the tropical Pacific to receive regular snowfall. She is one of four sister snow goddesses (along with Lilinoe, Waiau, and Kahoupokane) who preside over the mountain, but Poli'ahu is the most beautiful and powerful. In her most famous myth, Poli'ahu and her sisters came down from the summit to engage in hōlua sledding (an ancient Hawaiian sport using narrow wooden sleds on grassy slopes). As they raced, they encountered a beautiful stranger who challenged Poli'ahu to a race. She accepted — and immediately recognized her opponent as Pele in disguise. A cosmic race ensued between snow and fire, with Poli'ahu speeding down the mountain on her sled, leaving a trail of snow that Pele's lava could not follow. Eventually, Pele retreated in frustration, but the geological border between the two goddesses' domains remains visible today: the snow line of Mauna Kea versus the lava flows of Mauna Loa and Kīlauea. Poli'ahu represents the startling reality that even in the middle of the Pacific, winter and snow are part of the sacred landscape. She is a reminder that Hawaiian spirituality encompasses the full range of climate zones, from coral reefs to alpine summits. Her white mantle (poli = bosom, 'ahu = garment/cloth) is the snow cover of Mauna Kea — ephemeral, beautiful, and sacred.
