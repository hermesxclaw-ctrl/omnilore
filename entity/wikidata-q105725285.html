The Approach

In the sprawling metropolis of Tokyo, a seemingly unremarkable high school student hides a terrifying secret—Haruta Shigemo, a boy whose casual cruelty masks a demonic power that threatens to consume him and everyone around him.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of violence, psychological manipulation, and dark supernatural themes.
Identity

Haruta Shigemo

Pronunciation Hah-roo-tah Shee-geh-moh

Etymology

The name 'Haruta' (春太) is a common Japanese given name, literally meaning 'spring' (haru) and 'thick/fat' (ta). 'Shigemo' (重茂) is a rare surname, possibly meaning 'heavy' (shige) and 'luxuriant' (mo). No canonical etymology is provided in the source material.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese popular culture; created by Gege Akutami for the manga 'Jujutsu Kaisen', serialized in Shueisha's Weekly Shonen Jump.

Disputed Identifications

None known.

Description

Appearance

Haruta is a young man with short, messy dark hair and sharp, narrow eyes. He typically wears a dark school uniform with an unbuttoned collar, giving him a slovenly appearance. His expression often carries a smug or dismissive smirk, reflecting his arrogant personality.

Symbols & Iconography

No distinctive symbols associated with Haruta. His cursed technique, 'Miracle', is visualized through subtle probability manipulation rather than overt iconography.

Behaviour & Personality

Haruta is arrogant, sadistic, and deeply narcissistic. He believes he is blessed by fortune and looks down on others, especially those he considers weaker. He enjoys toying with his victims, showing no remorse for his actions. Despite his bravado, he becomes cowardly when faced with genuine danger, as seen when he begs for his life before Itadori.

Domains & Habitats

Haruta operates in urban environments, primarily within the Shibuya district during the Shibuya Incident. He is a student at a jujutsu technical college, though he shows little respect for its rules.

Version Differences

The anime adaptation closely follows the manga, with no significant differences in Haruta's characterization or role. Some minor dialogue changes occur in the English dub, but they do not alter the plot.

Powers & Abilities

Miracle (Cursed Technique)

Source Version Manga and anime

Description

Haruta's innate cursed technique allows him to unconsciously manipulate probability in his favor. In critical moments, unlikely events occur to save him from harm, such as a car crashing into an attacker or a sharp object being just out of reach. The technique is passive and unpredictable, seemingly granting him 'luck'.

Limitations

The technique is not consciously controlled; it activates based on Haruta's desperation and survival instinct. It cannot guarantee outcomes, and it failed to save him from Yuji Itadori's overwhelming strength.

Contradictions

The exact mechanics of the technique are not fully explained in the source material, leading to fan speculation. Some fans interpret it as a form of fate manipulation, while others see it as a simple luck-based ability.

Basic Jujutsu Combat

Source Version Manga and anime

Description

Haruta is a sorcerer with basic combat training, capable of using cursed energy to enhance his physical abilities and perform simple attacks. He is not a top-tier fighter and relies on his technique for survival.

Limitations

His combat skills are average compared to other sorcerers; he is easily overpowered by stronger opponents like Itadori.

Contradictions

None.

Stories & Myths

The Shibuya Incident

Source Jujutsu Kaisen manga, chapters 83-86, 121-123Culture Japanese manga culturePeriod 2018-2021 (real-world publication)

Summary

During the Shibuya Incident, Haruta and his companion, a cursed user named Mimiko Hasaba, are tasked with hunting down Yuji Itadori. Haruta encounters Itadori and attempts to kill him, but his Miracle technique fails to protect him from Itadori's raw power. He is brutally beaten and left incapacitated. Later, he is killed by Mahito, a cursed spirit, as a means to prevent him from interfering.

Consequences

Haruta's death serves as a demonstration of Mahito's ruthlessness and the chaotic nature of the Shibuya Incident. His failure highlights the limits of his luck-based ability.

Variants

The anime adapts these events faithfully, with minor pacing changes.
