Who It Is

A vampiric female ghost in Malay and Indonesian folklore, said to be the spirit of a woman who died while pregnant or in childbirth. The pontianak takes the form of a beautiful woman with long black hair, a white dress, and a gaping hole in her back. She lures men who then discover her true nature — the sweet floral fragrance of her presence turning to the stench of rotting corpse. In some versions, she manifests as a ball of flame or a disembodied woman's head trailing entrails (similar to the penanggalan). The pontianak announces her presence through the sound of a crying baby; if the crying is loud, she is far away; if soft, she is terrifyingly close. She preys on men and pregnant women.
