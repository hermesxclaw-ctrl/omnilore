The Approach

Scarface is a name that cuts across genres and eras: from a real-life Prohibition-era gangster to a fictional Cuban drug lord, from a Batman villain to a beloved children's story cat. This dossier explores the many faces of 'Scarface'—a moniker that has become synonymous with power, violence, and the American Dream's dark side.

Content advisory. This dossier contains references to organized crime, violence, drug trafficking, murder, and gangster culture. Some content may be disturbing to sensitive readers. Discretion is advised.
Identity

Scarface

Etymology

The name 'Scarface' is a compound of 'scar' (a mark left on skin) and 'face', referring to a facial scar. It is often used as a nickname for someone with such a scar, most notably Al Capone, who was nicknamed 'Scarface' due to scars on his face from an altercation.

Cultural Ownership

The name 'Scarface' is used across multiple cultures and media, with no single owner. It is most famously associated with the 1983 film and its protagonist Tony Montana, as well as the historical Al Capone.

Disputed Identifications

There is no major dispute over the identity of 'Scarface' as a general term, but the specific identity of 'Scarface' varies by context. For example, in comics, 'Scarface' is a dummy used by the Ventriloquist, not a separate character. In the 1983 film, Tony Montana is nicknamed 'Scarface' due to a scar, but the film does not explain its origin clearly.

Description

Appearance

The appearance of 'Scarface' varies by version. Al Capone was a heavyset man with a scar on his left cheek. Tony Montana, as portrayed by Al Pacino, has a scar on his face, is of Cuban descent, and often wears flashy suits. In comics, Scarface is a ventriloquist dummy with a scar. The lion named Scarface has a prominent scar on its face.

Symbols & Iconography

The scar is the central symbol, representing a violent past. In the 1983 film, the 'World is Yours' globe is iconic. The character is often associated with wealth, power, and excess.

Behaviour & Personality

Al Capone was a ruthless gangster. Tony Montana is ambitious, violent, and paranoid. The Ventriloquist's Scarface dummy is aggressive and criminal. The lion Scarface was a dominant male known for its resilience.

Domains & Habitats

Al Capone operated in Chicago during Prohibition. Tony Montana in Miami and Cuba. The Ventriloquist in Gotham City. The lion in the Maasai Mara, Kenya.

Version Differences

The 1932 film is a loose adaption of Capone's story, while the 1983 film is a remake with a Cuban protagonist. The comic character is unrelated to the films. The lion is a real animal, not a fictional character.
