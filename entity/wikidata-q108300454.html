Who It Is

He did not need a Devil Fruit or a sword to erase a nation. Spandine — the smiling bureaucrat in a crisp white suit who ordered Ohara burned — shows how One Piece makes state violence look banal: a man with a snail transponder, a lie about scholars, and the legal authority to call down battleships on a library.

Attested behavior is that of a cunning, sycophantic, and morally hollow intelligence officer. Spandine presents as polite and deferential to superiors (the Five Elders) and to Professor Clover initially, using flattery and false promises of clemency to extract information. When Clover reveals he knows the name of the Ancient Kingdom, Spandine abruptly drops the pretense, panics, and triggers the Buster Call to silence him — showing cowardice, careerism, and willingness to commit genocide to protect his standing. Powers
Cipher Pol Command Authority (CP9 Chief)

As chief of CP9 22 years ago, Spandine held top-secret authority to command CP9 agents, conduct interrogations, seize Poneglyph rubbings, and directly request a Buster Call via Golden Den Den Mushi. He could order Marines and Government agents without Marine rank. This is institutional power, not personal combat power.

Limit: Authority is delegated and revocable by the Five Elders and Commander-in-Chief Kong. He cannot order the Admirals directly without Buster Call authorization. After retirement, he holds no active command; his son Spandam inherits the position but not his competence.

Intelligence, Interrogation and Political Manipulation

Skilled interrogator and deceiver. He successfully infiltrated Ohara's scholarly community, feigned negotiation with Professor Clover ('If you stop your research, you will be pardoned'), and used Nico Olvia's capture to lure the Ohara scholars into a trap. He is adept at bureaucratic self-promotion, turning the Ohara genocide into career capital.

Limit: Not a master strategist — Clover sees through him and forces him to reveal the Government's fear of the Void Century. His panic leads to premature Buster Call, which later embarrasses the Government when Robin survives. He underestimates Jaguar D. Saul's defection and Kuzan's later sympathy.

Buster Call Authorization via Golden Den Den Mushi

Possession and use of the Golden Transponder Snail, which when activated summons ten warships, five Vice Admirals, and thousands of Marines to annihilate an island. Spandine uses it on Ohara, making him one of only a handful of characters to have legally triggered a Buster Call (alongside Spandam on Enies Lobby).

Limit: Single-use device; after activation it cannot be recalled. Requires Five Elders' pre-authorization (implied). Spandine does not control the fleet tactically — Vice Admirals (Sakazuki, Kuzan) execute the bombardment. He remains off-island and cannot stop the excesses (Sakazuki destroying the evacuation ship).

No Superhuman Combat Ability (Explicit Lack)

Unlike CP9 agents under him (Rob Lucci, Kaku, etc.), Spandine displays no Rokushiki, Haki, Devil Fruit, or notable physical strength. He is a pure bureaucrat. Oda deliberately contrasts his weakness with his agents' lethality to emphasize institutional evil over martial prowess.

Limit: Cannot defend himself in combat; relies entirely on subordinates and Marines. This is a narrative limitation, not a power.

Form & Signs

Accounts I
The Ohara Infiltration and the Trap for Nico OlviaOne Piece manga Chapters 392-393, anime Episodes 275-276

In-universe: c. 1500-1502 (22 years before present, circa 22 B.C. - Before Current timeline); publication 2005

Spandine, as CP9 chief, is tasked by the Five Elders to investigate the scholars of Ohara who are illegally researching the Poneglyphs and the Void Century. He captures archaeologist Nico Olvia during her attempt to warn Ohara after her ship is seized for carrying Poneglyph rubbings. He imprisons Olvia and uses her as bait, allowing her to return to Ohara under surveillance while CP9 agents infiltrate the island. He presents himself to Professor Clover as a negotiator, claiming the Government will pardon Ohara if they cease research. Olvia reunites briefly with her daughter Nico Robin, whom she has not seen in years, before Spandine's forces surround the Tree of Knowledge. Establishes Spandine's deceptive methodology and sets the stage for the Buster Call. Olvia's capture reveals the Government's prior knowledge of Ohara's research and the personal cost to Robin's family. The infiltration justifies the later claim that Ohara scholars are 'criminals.'

Confrontation with Professor Clover and the Name of the Ancient KingdomOne Piece manga Chapter 395-396, anime Episode 277

In-universe: 1502, day of the Buster Call; publication 2005

Inside the Tree of Knowledge, Professor Clover, the world's greatest archaeologist and director of Ohara's library, confronts Spandine. Clover states that Ohara has deciphered the Poneglyphs and knows the name of the Great Kingdom that opposed the Twenty Kingdoms 800 years ago. He accuses the World Government of erasing history to hide its founding crime. Spandine, initially smug, turns pale when Clover begins to utter the kingdom's name (rendered as '—' in the manga, censored by a gunshot). Terrified that the Five Elders will punish him for letting the name be spoken, Spandine shoots Clover and immediately activates the Golden Den Den Mushi, shouting 'Buster Call!' He justifies it as silencing dangerous scholars who seek to revive Ancient Weapons. Clover is mortally wounded and dies during the bombardment, but his speech is later echoed by Vegapunk in Chapter 1066. Spandine's shooting and Buster Call order directly cause the genocide. Succession: From Spandine to Spandam and the Enies Lobby LegacyOne Piece manga Chapter 398, anime Episode 266-267 (Spandam flashback)

In-universe: c. 1510-1514 (8-4 years before present); publication 2006

Years after Ohara, an aging Spandine grooms his son Spandam to succeed him as chief of CP9. In Chapter 398, Spandam is shown receiving the Golden Den Den Mushi from his father, with Spandine boasting about Ohara as his great achievement. Spandam, far less competent and more cowardly than his father, inherits not only the position but also the same Golden Snail, which he later uses to accidentally trigger the second Buster Call on Enies Lobby during the Straw Hats' rescue of Robin. Spandine appears in a brief flashback panel patting Spandam's head, and later in a cover story (CP9's Independent Report) seen walking with his son after Enies Lobby's fall, implying retirement but continued influence. Creates a hereditary bureaucratic dynasty that parallels the Celestial Dragons' inherited power. Spandam's incompetence contrasts with Spandine's calculated cruelty, showing institutional decay. The second Buster Call on Enies Lobby mirrors Ohara, with Robin again at the center, completing a generational cycle of trauma. Posthumous Reckoning: Vegapunk's Broadcast and the Will of OharaOne Piece manga Chapters 1066-1067, 1115-1116, 1120

In-universe: 1522 (present); publication 2022-2024

During the Egghead Island arc, Dr. Vegapunk reveals that the books of Ohara were not destroyed: Jaguar D. Saul survived (with Kuzan's help) and giants from Elbaf rescued the library's books from the lake, preserving them on Elbaf. Vegapunk admits he visited Ohara after the Buster Call, met the young Robin's legacy, and was inspired by Clover and Olvia. He broadcasts to the world that Ohara's will lives on. Spandine is not present but is named in retrospectives as the man who ordered the Buster Call, and his crime is reframed as part of the World Government's 800-year censorship, now linked to Imu and the Void Century. The Five Elders (now revealed as immortal Mythical Zoan users loyal to Imu) are shown as the true architects; Spandine was their willing instrument. Retroactively deepens Spandine's historical guilt: his Buster Call failed to erase knowledge, and the rescued books become the foundation for the final saga's revelations about the Ancient Kingdom, Joy Boy, and the Will of D. Robin's survival is validated as Ohara's victory over Spandine. No punishment for Spandine is shown

The Empty Throne and the World Government System Spandine ServedOne Piece manga Chapters 395-397, 906-908, 1084-1086 (World Government lore)

In-universe: 800-year history; publication 2005-2023

Though Spandine never enters Pangaea Castle's Empty Throne room, his actions exemplify the system it represents. The source material explains that the World Government was founded by 20 kings, whose descendants (Celestial Dragons) rule from Mary Geoise, with the Five Elders as public heads and Imu as secret king on the Empty Throne. Spandine as CP9 chief is part of the Government's 'dark' enforcement arm (Cipher Pol), alongside Marines and later Seraphim. His willingness to commit genocide to protect the Empty Throne's secret (the Void Century) illustrates how mid-level bureaucrats sustain the system without ever seeing Imu. His story is thus a case study in how the World Government maintains power through compartmentalized, deniable violence. Contextualizes Spandine not as an aberration but as a functionary of a structure designed to erase history. His Ohara Buster Call is one of several erasures (also God Valley, Lulusia). Understanding Spandine helps readers understand why the Government fears Robin and the Poneglyphs.
