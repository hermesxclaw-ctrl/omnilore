Who It Is

He drops from the white sea clouds with a bazooka that breathes fire — not a god, not a pirate, but a Shandian warrior whose name means 'praying mantis' and who chooses to charge an invincible lightning god anyway.

Attested (limited panel time): Brave, impulsive, loyal to Wyper and the Shandian cause. Shown as eager to fight Enel's forces and to reclaim Upper Yard (the lost half of Jaya). In Ch. 262-264 he charges ahead despite Wyper's caution, demonstrating hot-headedness relative to Braham and Genbo. Shows camaraderie with fellow warriors and concern for Aisa. No extended dialogue or backstory monologue; personality inferred from action rather than exposition. Disputed: fan wikis sometimes assign him a stoic or calm trait, but primary manga shows more aggression than stoicism. Unknown: age

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese 蟷螂・鎌切 kamakiri 'praying mantis' (Mantodea). Attested in Japanese since at least the Heian period. Compound often analyzed as kama 'sickle' + kiri 'cutter', referring to the raptorial forelegs. The orthography 蟷螂 is ateji (phonetic kanji), while 鎌切 is folk-etymological. The name follows One

Pronunciation — Japanese: kamakiri [ka.ma.kʲi.ɾʲi], pitch accent kaMAkiri (Nakadaka). English dub: kuh-mah-KEE-ree.

Aliases — Kamakiri the Shandian Warrior, Kamakiri of Shandia

Ownership — Character owned by Eiichiro Oda / Shueisha / Shonen Jump as part of One Piece (1997-present). Linguistic term kamakiri belongs to general Japanese language and folklore; not owned.

Powers
Shandian Warrior Combat Training

Proficient spear fighter and guerrilla warrior, trained in Upper Yard's cloud terrain and dial warfare. Demonstrates agility on cloud surfaces and coordination in squad attacks with Wyper, Braham, and Genbo.

Limit: No superhuman Devil Fruit or Mantra (Observation Haki) attested for Kamakiri specifically; unlike Aisa and Wyper, he shows no Mantra. Vulnerable to Enel's lightning and to priest trials.

Burn Bazooka (Breath Dial + Flame Dial Bazooka)

Shoulder-mounted cannon incorporating a Breath Dial (stores and releases compressed wind) to propel an incendiary blast, augmented by a Flame Dial. Fires a large, sustained column of flame capable of burning forest and damaging Upper Yard ruins. Used in assault on Upper Yard and against Enel's forces.

Limit: Requires dial charge; limited shots; recoil and reload time. Ineffective against Logia intangibility (Enel's lightning body) and against opponents with superior speed. Can be dodged or countered by Mantra users. Cloud environment can disperse flame.

Dial Utility (General)

As Shandian warrior, presumed access to other dials (Impact, Axe, etc.) common to Skypiea warfare, though only Burn Bazooka is attested in use by Kamakiri on-panel.

Limit: No evidence he uses Reject Dial (Wyper's unique, dangerous dial) or Mantra dials.

Endurance and Survival

Survives Enel's lightning judgment and the Survival Game (Enel's 6-day death game), including being struck down and later recovering after Enel's defeat — demonstrating notable durability typical of One Piece combatants.

Limit: Not immortal; was incapacitated by Enel and required the arc's resolution to recover. No rapid healing factor shown.

Accounts I
The 400-Year War: Shandia's Exile and the Struggle for Upper YardOne Piece manga Ch. 249-254, 286-290 (Robin’s Poneglyph exposition, Gan Fall flashback); anime Ep. 164-174

2003 (publication); in-universe 400 years (c. 1122-1522 in One Piece calendar)

Four hundred years before the present, half of Jaya (with the golden city Shandora) is shot into the sky by the Knock-Up Stream, becoming Upper Yard. The Shandians are expelled and live as exiles on Angel Island's clouds, waging a generations-long guerrilla war against the Skypieans who settle Upper Yard as 'God's land.' Kamakiri belongs to the current generation of warriors under Wyper, inheriting this war. No individual origin story for Kamakiri is given; he embodies the collective exile narrative. Frames Kamakiri's motivation: reclaim ancestral homeland and ring the Golden Bell (proof of Shandora's existence). Explains his willingness to ally with or oppose outsiders based on that goal.

Contact: Straw Hats Meet the Shandian WarriorsOne Piece manga Ch. 249-255; anime Ep. 163-167

2003

After the Straw Hats arrive in Skypiea and are criminalized for not paying entry, they encounter the Shandian warriors. Kamakiri appears with Wyper's squad intercepting both Skypieans and Straw Hats in the White Sea. Initial contact is hostile: Shandians see all Upper Yard intruders as enemies. The Straw Hats are scattered, setting up parallel invasions of Upper Yard. Establishes Kamakiri as part of the third faction (Shandians) distinct from Straw Hats and Enel's forces. Sets up later uneasy cooperation.

Assault on Upper Yard: Burn Bazooka Against the Priests' TrialsOne Piece manga Ch. 262-275; anime Ep. 168-175

2003

Wyper leads the Shandian warriors, including Kamakiri, in a full assault on Upper Yard to reach the ruins and defeat Enel. Kamakiri uses his Burn Bazooka to blast through forest and against Enel's subordinates. The group navigates the Four Trials (Balls, String, Iron, Swamp) set by Enel's priests. Kamakiri is shown fighting alongside Braham and Genbo, demonstrating coordinated dial warfare. Shows Kamakiri's combat role and the limits of conventional dial weapons against Mantra-enhanced priests. Narrows the field for the later Survival Game.

Accounts II
The Survival Game: Enel's Judgment and the Warriors' FallOne Piece manga Ch. 274-287; anime Ep. 179-183

2003

Enel announces a Survival Game: all combatants (Straw Hats, Shandians, priests, Skypieans) must survive three hours while he hunts them with lightning. Kamakiri, with other Shandians, is struck down by Enel's El Thor / lightning judgment after challenging him directly. The scene emphasizes Enel's Logia invincibility and the despair of conventional warriors against a godlike Devil Fruit user. Kamakiri is shown incapacitated, not yet confirmed dead. Removes Kamakiri and most Shandians from the final confrontation, leaving Wyper and Luffy to face Enel. Creates the misconception that Kamakiri died here (corrected in aftermath).

After the Fall of God: Ringing the Bell and ResettlementOne Piece manga Ch. 290-300, especially 298-300; anime Ep. 187-195

2003-2004

Luffy defeats Enel, rings the Golden Bell of Shandora, fulfilling the 400-year vow between Noland and Calgara. In the aftermath, the Shandians, Skypieans, and Straw Hats celebrate. Kamakiri is shown among the surviving Shandian warriors recovering and participating in the peace, with Upper Yard now shared. The war ends; Shandia and Skypiea begin coexistence. Kamakiri's final panels are group celebration, not individual epilogue. Resolves the exile arc; Kamakiri transitions from guerrilla to settler. No further individual story for Kamakiri after Skypiea; he does not reappear in later arcs as of 2026.

The Mantis in Japanese Folklore: Background Tale (Non-character, Contextual)Wakan Sansai Zue (1712), Konchu narratives, and Edo netsuke / folk belief corpus

Edo period (1603-1868); compiled 1712

Separate from the One Piece character, the insect kamakiri appears in Japanese folk knowledge as a familiar, somewhat ominous predator — admired for its stillness and sudden strike, sometimes associated with sharpness and protection against evil, but not a major yokai. Unlike kamaitachi, kamakiri has no canonical yokai tale cycle; its folklore is entomological and proverbial ('kamakiri no ono' — mantis's axe, futile resistance). Included to prevent misattribution of yokai lore to the character. Explains why modern fiction borrows kamakiri as a name for sharp, cutting characters without implying a traditional deity or monster lore.

Kamakiri is a traditional Japanese yokai or god of mantises — Kamakiri in this dossier is a modern One Piece character (2003) whose name borrows the common noun for mantis. There is no major traditional yokai named Kamakiri with a story cycle; the insect appears in proverbs and natural histories, not as a deity. Confusion arises from the yokai kamaitachi (sickle weasel).

Kamakiri dies in Skypiea and never appears again — He is incapacitated by Enel but survives. Post-war chapters show Shandian warriors alive and celebrating. He simply has no further individual plot after Skypiea, which is misread as death.

Kamakiri is a mantis-man or has mantis powers — No transformation or insect powers are attested. He is a human Shandian warrior with a flame bazooka; the name is thematic, not biological. Mantis-themed transformations belong to unrelated tokusatsu monsters.

Kamakiri is a major recurring character throughout One Piece — He is a supporting arc character exclusive to Skypiea (Ch. 249-300). He does not join the Straw Hats, does not appear in later sagas, and is not in the main cast. His role is important thematically but limited in panel time.
