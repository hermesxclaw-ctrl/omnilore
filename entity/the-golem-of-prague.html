A clay giant animated by Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel (the Maharal of Prague) in the 16th century, using Kabbalistic formulas and the word 'EMET' (truth) inscribed on its forehead. Created to defend the Jewish ghetto from pogroms, the Golem grows increasingly powerful and destructive until the Rabbi erases the first letter, changing EMET to MET (death), returning it to inert clay.

Who It Is

A clay giant animated by Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel (the Maharal of Prague) in the 16th century, using Kabbalistic formulas and the word 'EMET' (truth) inscribed on its forehead. Created to defend the Jewish ghetto from pogroms, the Golem grows increasingly powerful and destructive until the Rabbi erases the first letter, changing EMET to MET (death), returning it to inert clay.

Attested in Various (most famously Gustav Meyrink's 'Der Golem' 1915)

Cultural Significance

The Golem embodies the tension between protection and destruction, creation and hubris. It is also deeply tied to Jewish experiences of persecution and the desperate measures taken for survival. The legend has spawned a rich literary and cinematic tradition.
