Who It Is

The classic European werewolf of medieval and early modern folklore. A person who transforms — voluntarily through witchcraft or involuntarily through a curse — into a wolf or wolf-human hybrid. Transformations were believed to be triggered by the full moon (a relatively late addition), by wearing a wolf-skin belt, or by anointing the body with a magical salve. The European werewolf trials of the 16th-17th centuries were as deadly as the witch trials: figures like Peter Stumpp (the 'Werewolf of Bedburg,' 1589), Gilles Garnier (the 'Werewolf of Dole,' 1573), and the Beast of Gévaudan cases (1764-1767) resulted in executions. Medical theories linked werewolves to clinical lycanthropy (a psychiatric delusion), hypertrichosis (excessive hair growth), and rabies.
