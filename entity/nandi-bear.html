Who It Is

The Nandi Bear, called Chemosit by the Nandi people, is one of Africa's most feared cryptids -- a powerful nocturnal predator reported from the highlands of western Kenya. Described as a massive, hyena-like animal with a bear-like posture, the Nandi Bear stands 4 to 6 feet at the shoulder, has a sloping back (like a hyena), powerful forequarters, a short neck, a bear-like face with a short snout, and dark brown or reddish fur. It is said to be an extremely aggressive man-eater that preys on livestock and humans, especially children. Nandi oral traditions describe it raiding villages at night, tearing through the walls of huts to seize victims, and specifically targeting the brains of its kills. European colonists in the early 20th century recorded numerous terrifying accounts. In 1905, a railway engineer named Hickes reported a close encounter. In 1912, a supposed Nandi Bear was shot and examined -- it was determined to be an unusually large spotted hyena, but some researchers questioned whether it was the same creature described by natives. Colonial administrator G.W. Hickes documented attacks in the 1920s. Cryptozoologists have proposed several possible identities: a surviving population of the giant short-faced hyena (Pachycrocuta brevirostris, extinct ~500,000 years ago), a giant forest-dwelling hyena, a surviving chalicothere (a prehistoric ungulate with claws), a giant baboon, or an unknown bear species -- though bears are not native to sub-Saharan Africa. Skeptics attribute sightings to exaggerated accounts of known predators (spotted hyenas, honey badgers, aardvarks seen in poor light). The Nandi Bear remains one of Africa's most enduring cryptid mysteries.
