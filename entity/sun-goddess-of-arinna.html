Form

The appearance of the Sun-Goddess of Arinna is not well-documented in ancient sources, but she is often depicted in modern representations as a powerful and radiant figure, surrounded by symbols of her association with the sun and fertility. In some Hittite artifacts, she is shown with a sun disk on her head, emphasizing her connection to the celestial body. Her appearance is often compared to that of other ancient Near Eastern goddesses, such as Ishtar or Hathor, who were also associated with the sun and fertility. However, the Sun-Goddess of Arinna's unique cultural context and mythological significance set her apart from these other figures. The Hittites believed that the Sun-Goddess of Arinna was a beautiful and powerful goddess, with the ability to bring light and life to the world. Her appearance was likely an important aspect of her cult worship, with devotees seeking to emulate her radiance and fertility through rituals and ceremonies.

Powers

Control over the sun The Sun-Goddess of Arinna was believed to have the power to control the sun, bringing light and life to the world. The Hittites believed that she could use this power to ensure the fertility of the land, the success of the royal family, and the well-being of the kingdom as a whole.
