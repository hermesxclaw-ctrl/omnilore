The Approach

In the spring of 1983, a bay colt with an unassuming name—born from a farm's initials—thundered into Japanese racing history, becoming only the third horse to sweep the coveted Triple Crown. His name was Mr. C. B., a horse whose legacy would gallop far beyond the track, into the hearts of fans and the pixels of a modern gaming phenomenon.

Content advisory. No dark material found. Note: The subject is a real racehorse; some fictionalized portrayals in media may include dramatic elements.
Identity

Mr. C. B.

Pronunciation Misutā Shī Bī (Japanese)

Etymology

The initials 'C. B.' stand for Chigira Bokujō (Chigira Farm), his birthplace. The name was given when he entered racing at age 3.

Behaviour & Personality

Known for a solid waist and stable rear-oriented center of gravity, unlike many offspring of Tosho Boy. Described as 'a horse that inherited only the good traits of Tosho Boy.'

Domains & Habitats

Stories & Myths

Triple Crown Victory

Source WikipediaCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1983

Summary

Mr. C. B. became the third horse to win the Japanese Triple Crown, after St Lite and Shinzan. He was ridden by jockey Masato Yoshinaga in all his starts, a unique feat for a Triple Crown winner.

Consequences

Named Japanese Horse of the Year for 1983 and inducted into the JRA Hall of Fame in 1986.
