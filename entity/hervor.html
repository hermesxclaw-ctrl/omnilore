Who It Is

Hervör is the fierce shieldmaiden protagonist of Hervarar saga ok Heiðreks — a defiant warrior-woman who does what even legendary heroes fear: she enters the burial mound of her dead father Angantýr to demand the cursed sword Tyrfing. Raised as a warrior despite attempts to give her a 'proper' female upbringing, Hervör spends her youth raiding as a viking (the word could apply to women too — she is a 'vikingr'), dressed in men's clothes and calling herself 'Hervarðr.' When she learns that her father was one of the twelve berserker sons of Arngrímr who died on the island of Sámsey (Samsø, Denmark), she sails there alone. At sunset, as the barrow-fires burn over the mounds (a phenomenon called 'haugaeldr' — mound-fire, the supernatural flames that mark a draugr's barrow), Hervör approaches the largest mound and calls her father's name. The dialogue between Hervör and the undead Angantýr is one of the greatest scenes in Norse literature: she demands the sword Tyrfing, forged by the dwarves Dvalinn and Durinn, cursed so that it must kill a man every time it is drawn, and which will cause three great evils before it is finally laid to rest. Angantýr warns her of the curse, begs her not to take it, but ultimately yields. Hervör takes Tyrfing and goes on to have further adventures, including a peaceful period as a wife and mother — but the curse plays out through her descendants, confirming the doom Angantýr foretold. Hervör represents the shieldmaiden archetype at its most powerful: a woman who claims her paternal inheritance by force of will, confronting the dead and defying the curse, reshaping her own destiny even within the constraints of fate.
