Who It Is

Papa-tū-ā-nuku (Earth Mother Spread Wide) is the primordial earth mother in Māori cosmology, the partner of Rangi-nui (Sky Father) and mother of all gods and living beings. She and Rangi were locked in a tight embrace, creating a world of darkness where their children — the departmental gods — lived pressed between them. When Tāne pushed Rangi away, separating his parents, Papa groaned in pain and loss, and her body became the earth. She provides for all her children, offering them food, shelter, and the physical ground of existence. In some accounts, when the gods were starving after the separation, Papa helped Tāne find the kūmara (sweet potato) to feed them. In Hawaiian tradition, she is known as Papahānaumoku ('Papa Who Gave Birth to Islands'), the earth mother who, with Wākea (Sky), gave birth to the Hawaiian Islands. The Papahānaumokuākea Marine National Monument is named after her. Papa represents the feminine principle as earth: nurturing, patient, abundant, but also the one who bears the weight of the sky's loss and the one whose body we inhabit.

Origins

Papa and Rangi emerged from Te Kore (the Void). They embraced and had children who lived in darkness. After Tāne separated them, Papa turned toward the underworld and became the earth that sustains all life. In Hawaiian tradition, Papahānaumoku gave birth to the islands and kalo (taro).
