Description

Appearance

Myagrus species are typical longhorn beetles (family Cerambycidae), subfamily Lamiinae. They are characterized by elongated bodies, long antennae (often longer than the body), and robust legs. The elytra (wing covers) are usually patterned with spots or markings, as suggested by species names like 'alboplagiatus' (white-spotted) and 'irroratus' (spotted or speckled). Coloration is generally brownish or dark, often with lighter markings. Size ranges are not specifically documented in available sources but likely fall within the moderate range typical of Lamiinae.

Behaviour & Personality

As with most longhorn beetles, adults are likely xylophagous in larval stage, feeding on wood, and adults may feed on nectar, sap, or leaves. They are typically nocturnal or crepuscular. Specific behavioral traits for Myagrus species are not documented in the available sources.

Domains & Habitats

Myagrus species are found in Southeast Asia, including Indonesia (Java, Penang) and possibly other regions. They inhabit tropical forests, where their larvae bore into dead or living wood. Specific habitat preferences are not detailed in the available literature.

Taxonomic history

Source Various taxonomic publicationsCulture ScientificPeriod 1866-1957

Summary

The genus Myagrus was established by Pascoe in 1866 with the description of Myagrus vinosus. Subsequent species were added by Pascoe (1878), Gahan (1888), Heller (1924), and Breuning (1957), expanding the genus to five species. The genus is placed within the tribe Lamiini or similar, but exact tribal placement may require verification.
