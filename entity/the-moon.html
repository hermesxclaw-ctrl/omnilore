The Moon

His Throne, The Great Eye, The Night Watcher

RealmEarth's Moon (but wrong), Night Sky

Who It Is

Local 58 is widely considered the first true analog horror series, and its most terrifying episode — 'Skywatching' — reveals something profoundly wrong with the Moon. In the episode, a local television broadcast presents itself as a simple astronomy show about viewing the Moon. But as the host describes what viewers should see, the narration becomes increasingly disturbed: 'Look at the Moon. Do you see it? That's no moon. That's His throne.' The Moon is revealed to be not a natural satellite but something else entirely — the seat or eye of a cosmic entity, watching Earth. In the episode 'Contingency,' the Moon is associated with a mass suicide event in which the United States government broadcasts a message to 'face the Moon' and 'reject the lies of the Moon' — implying that the Moon's influence is a known, ongoing threat. In another episode, 'Real Sleep,' a subliminal messaging tape is designed to suppress dreams, because dreaming about the Moon allows it to reach into human minds. The horror of Local 58 is built entirely on implication and atmosphere: we never see the entity behind the Moon, we never learn its name, and the exact nature of its threat is never explicitly stated. What we get instead is the creeping realization that something has been wrong with the night sky for a very long time, and the institutions meant to protect us — the government, the media — are either complicit or helpless. The Moon as a malevolent entity is a deeply primal concept: the Moon is one of the most universal and constant features of human experience, present in every culture's mythology. To corrupt it — to make it not a source of light and tides but the eye of a predator — is to make the entire night sky unsafe. Local 58's influence on the analog horror genre cannot be overstated; it established the aesthetic, the pacing, and the thematic concerns that series like Gemini Home Entertainment and the Mandela Catalogue would build upon.
