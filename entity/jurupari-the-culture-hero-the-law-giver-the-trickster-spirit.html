Who It Is

Jurupari (or Yurupary) is a complex and widespread figure of Amazonian mythology — a culture hero, trickster, and founding lawgiver whose myth complex spans the Tupi-Guarani, Arawak (Baniwa, Tariana), and Tukanoan peoples of the northwest Amazon (the Rio Negro and Vaupés basins). The Jurupari complex is most prominent as the cycle of the 'sacred flutes' — a system of male initiation rites from which women are rigorously excluded. In the myth, Jurupari was born from the fruit of the pucanga tree (or from a palm nut) impregnated by the sun (in some versions) and raised by his mother; he grew into a prodigiously powerful man who established the laws of the tribe — the division of labor, food taboos, marriage regulations, and the rule that women must never see the sacred flutes. Women who broke this taboo were said to be devoured by the earth. Jurupari himself was killed and burned by men jealous of his power, but he was resurrected in the form of the sacred flute, whose sound is his continuing presence in the men's house. The Jurupari myth encodes the Amazonian social logic of male-female complementarity and hierarchy: the power of the flutes is the power of ancestral law.
