Who It Is

The mythical alchemical substance capable of transmuting base metals (lead, mercury) into gold or silver, and producing the Elixir of Life that grants rejuvenation and immortality. Known also as 'the tincture,' 'the powder,' 'the red lion' (for gold), and 'the white lion' (for silver). For centuries, the Philosopher's Stone was the central goal of alchemy — the magnum opus. Its creation was described through the stages of nigredo (blackening), albedo (whitening), citrinitas (yellowing), and rubedo (reddening), each corresponding to psychological and spiritual transformations. In the Islamic world, Jabir ibn Hayyan (Geber, 8th century) developed systematic theories, and in China, alchemists sought the 'Golden Elixir.' The Stone functions on three levels: material (metal transmutation), biological (the Elixir of Life), and spiritual (the perfection of the soul).
