The Approach

Imagine a writhing knot of rats, their tails fused together in a grotesque tangle, moving as one monstrous mass. This is the rat king—a rare and chilling phenomenon that has haunted European folklore for centuries, and which has slithered its way into modern horror and fantasy. But is it a real biological anomaly, a product of terrified imaginations, or a symbol of corruption and chaos? The truth is as tangled as the tails themselves.

Content advisory. Contains descriptions of animal suffering, decay, and grotesque imagery. Some fictional portrayals include violence and body horror.

Etymology

The term 'rat king' is a direct translation of the German 'Rattenkönig'. The origin of the term is uncertain, but it may derive from the French 'roi des rats' or from the idea of a king among rats, as the entangled rats were sometimes thought to be led by a larger, smarter rat. Another theory suggests it comes from the Latin 'rex ratum', but this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily associated with Central and Northern European folklore, particularly Germany and France. The phenomenon has been documented since the 16th century, and the term 'Rattenkönig' has been used in German-speaking regions. However, the concept has spread globally and has been adapted in various modern media.

Disputed Identifications

There is debate over whether the rat king is a genuine biological phenomenon or a man-made artifact. Some researchers argue that the tails of rats can become entangled due to a combination of factors like sticky substances (e.g., sap, blood, feces) and cold temperatures, while others claim that no verifiable specimen has been found, and most historical examples are hoaxes or misidentifications. Additionally, the term 'rat king' has been used metaphorically to describe political or social corruption, and in Japan, it is used as a slang term for a yakuza boss.

Description

Appearance

A rat king is a group of rats whose tails are entangled in a knot, often with the bodies arranged in a radial pattern, with their heads pointing outward. The tails are typically intertwined with each other and sometimes with other materials like hair, twigs, or dirt. The rats are usually dead or severely debilitated, and the knot is often tightly bound, making it impossible for the rats to free themselves.

Symbols & Iconography

In folklore, the rat king is often seen as a symbol of chaos, corruption, and disease. It has been depicted in art and literature as a monstrous creature, sometimes with a crown or other royal attributes, emphasizing its 'king' status. In modern media, it is often portrayed as a horrifying entity, representing the dark side of nature or a plague-like menace.

Behaviour & Personality

In folklore, the rat king is often described as a single creature with a collective intelligence, leading its rat subjects. It is sometimes said to be aggressive and dangerous, spreading disease and causing misfortune. In reality, if a rat king occurs, the rats are likely to be in distress, unable to move freely, and would eventually die from starvation or predation. There is no evidence of any coordinated behavior or intelligence.

Domains & Habitats

Rat kings have been reported primarily in Europe, particularly in Germany, France, and Switzerland, in areas with high rat populations, such as barns, cellars, and sewers. They are most commonly found in cold climates, as it is hypothesized that cold temperatures cause the rats to huddle together, and their tails can become frozen or glued together by ice or other substances.

Version Differences

Historical accounts vary in the number of rats involved, ranging from a few to dozens. Some accounts describe the rats as being alive and active, while others describe them as dead. The appearance of the knot also varies, with some being simple tangles and others being complex, tightly woven masses. In fiction, the rat king is often depicted as a single, monstrous creature, sometimes with supernatural powers, which is quite different from the real phenomenon.

Stories & Myths

The Strasbourg Rat King

Source Historical account, 19th centuryCulture French/GermanPeriod 19th century

Summary

One of the most famous rat king specimens was discovered in 1828 in a miller's house in Strasbourg. The specimen consisted of 32 rats whose tails were tightly entangled. It was preserved and displayed in the Strasbourg museum. The find was widely reported and sparked scientific and public interest.

Participants

32 rats

Miller who found them

Museum curators

Consequences

The specimen became a popular attraction and was later studied by naturalists. It remains one of the most cited examples of a rat king.

Variants

Some accounts claim the rats were found alive, others say they were dead. The exact circumstances of the discovery are unclear.

The Rat King of Dollendorf

Source Historical account, 16th centuryCulture GermanPeriod 16th century

Summary

An early account from 1564 describes a rat king found in Dollendorf, Germany. The account claims that the rats were found in a nest and that they were considered a bad omen, associated with witchcraft and evil.

Consequences

The rat king was destroyed, and the event was recorded in local chronicles as a sign of impending disaster.

Variants

Animal Suffering

Source Historical accounts and scientific papers

Description

The rat king phenomenon involves rats that are trapped and unable to free themselves, leading to starvation, injury, and death. Historical accounts often describe the rats as being in a pitiful state.

Scholarly Context

This is a natural phenomenon, but it raises ethical questions about the treatment of animals and the display of such specimens.

Folklore and Fear

Source Folklore and historical chronicles

Description

The rat king has been associated with fear, disease, and evil omens. In some cultures, it was seen as a manifestation of the devil or a witch's curse.

Scholarly Context

The fear of rats and the diseases they carry has been a persistent theme in human history, and the rat king amplified this fear.

Misrepresentation and Hoaxes

Source Scientific investigations

Description

Some alleged rat king specimens have been shown to be hoaxes, created by humans by tying rat tails together. This has led to skepticism about the existence of the phenomenon.

Scholarly Context

The possibility of hoaxes has made it difficult to verify the authenticity of rat king specimens, and some researchers argue that all examples are man-made.

Contradictions & Disputes

Existence of the phenomenon

Position A

The rat king is a real, natural phenomenon, with multiple documented specimens.

Position A — evidence

Historical accounts and preserved specimens in museums, such as the Strasbourg specimen.

Position B

The rat king is a hoax or misidentification, with no verifiable specimens.

Position B — evidence

Lack of recent verified occurrences, and the ease with which rat tails can be artificially tied.

Scholarly Consensus

Most modern scientists are skeptical, but the phenomenon cannot be completely ruled out.

Cause of the entanglement

Position A

The tails become entangled due to natural causes, such as cold weather and sticky substances.

Position A — evidence

Reports of rats found alive and entangled in nests, suggesting natural causes.

Position B

The tails are deliberately tied by humans, either as a hoax or for some other purpose.

Position B — evidence

Some specimens show clean, deliberate knots that are unlikely to occur naturally.

Scholarly Consensus

There is no consensus; both possibilities exist.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

The veracity of the rat king phenomenon remains uncertain. There is a lack of recent, well-documented cases. The exact mechanism of tail entanglement is not fully understood. Many historical accounts are vague and lack details. The cultural significance of the rat king in non-European regions is under-researched. The fictional portrayals are numerous, but a comprehensive list is not included here.
