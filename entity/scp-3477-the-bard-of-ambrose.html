Who It Is

A human male who claims to be William Shakespeare — and can prove it. He speaks in perfect iambic pentameter, has intimate knowledge of Elizabethan England that no historian possesses, and can recite entire plays that Shakespeare was 'working on' before his 'escape.' The Foundation's conclusion: he is either the genuine article, transported through time by unknown means, or an impossibly detailed copy. He is content in containment, having been provided with a laptop ('a magic slate of wondrous utility') on which he has written 47 new plays. The Foundation's literary analysis unit is still processing them.

Form

A middle-aged man of English appearance, balding, with a well-trimmed beard. He dresses in modern clothing provided by the Foundation but refers to jeans as 'strange hose' and sneakers as 'silent shoes.'

Powers

Encyclopedic knowledge of 16th-century England

Perfect iambic pentameter speech

Production of historically authentic Elizabethan literature
