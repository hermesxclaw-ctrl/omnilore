Who It Is

He is the priest who tried to drown you in a cloud you could sink into, then forgot why he was fighting you in the first place — Gedatsu, Enel's most dangerously absent-minded enforcer, whose Trial of the Swamp turned Skypiea's fluffy paradise into a suffocating trap, and whose second life as an accidental hot-spring tycoon is one of One Piece's strangest redemptions.

Attested personality is defined by extreme forgetfulness, airheadedness, and literal-minded comedy, even by One Piece standards. He repeatedly forgets his own Trial's rules, forgets to breathe while sinking, forgets that he removed his own eyelids' protection, and must be reminded of basic plans. Despite this, he is dutiful and devout toward Enel, shows no hesitation in attempting to kill intruders, and displays surprising earnestness and work ethic in the cover story. Powers
Swamp Cloud (Numa Kumo) - Swamp Dial Technology

Not a Devil Fruit. Gedatsu uses a Swamp Dial? Actually manga depicts him manipulating Numa Kumo (Swamp Cloud), a specially processed Island Cloud that behaves like viscous swamp: victims who step on it sink and suffocate, and it can be expelled to engulf opponents. He can generate and control masses of this cloud to trap Chopper and create sinking fields. The cloud does not give him intangibility; it is an environmental weapon.

Limit: Requires pre-prepared cloud or Dial storage; can be countered by not touching it, by using solid footing (Chopper uses cloud skates / waver?), or by drying/freezing. Gedatsu himself is not immune and comically sinks into his own swamp if careless. Finite volume.

Jet Dial-Enhanced Mobility and Combat

Gedatsu has Jet Dials (Jetto Daiaru) surgically embedded in his elbows and the soles of his feet. By venting stored air at explosive pressure, he achieves high-speed linear charges, mid-air dashes, and rocket-assisted punches and kicks. Combined with his mass, this yields a battering-ram fighting style: Jet Punch, Jet Kick. He also uses a numbing Swamp Cloud punch that rots? Actually his punch can cause opponent to sink on contact.

Limit: Jet Dials have limited charge and recoil; trajectory is mostly straight-line, making him predictable and prone to crashing. He frequently miscalculates and slams into walls. Requires solid footing to launch. No ranged energy attack like Enel's lightning.

Physical Strength and Endurance (Priest-level)

As a God's Priest, Gedatsu possesses superhuman strength and durability well above average Blue Sea humans, though portrayed as the weakest of the four priests. He can shatter stone, survive being launched by Chopper's Heavy Point, and endure falling from Skypiea to the Blue Sea without death (largely comedic resilience).

Limit: Significantly weaker than Ohm and Shura; defeated by pre-Gear Chopper with clever use of environment. Vulnerable to blunt force, and his forgetfulness creates self-inflicted openings.

Trial Administration and Survival Game Authority

Institutional power: as priest, Gedatsu can judge intruders, command lower-ranking Divine Soldiers (Enel's enforcers), and invoke the 0-100% survival rate ordeals. This is political/religious authority under Enel's theocracy, not supernatural.

Limit: Authority evaporates with Enel's defeat; no independent following. Other priests do not obey him.

Accounts I
The Four Priests and the Survival GameOne Piece manga Ch. 249-254; anime Ep. 162-164

2003 publication; in-world: during Straw Hat exploration of Skypiea, Age of Enel's six-year rule

After the Straw Hats arrive in Skypiea and are sentenced as criminals for illegal entry, Enel's priesthood is introduced. Gedatsu appears alongside Satori, Shura, and Ohm as one of four priests who administer the Ordeals. Conis explains that each priest oversees a lethal trial with a historically low survival rate. Gedatsu is assigned the Ordeal of Swamp (survival rate 50%, later stated as 0% for his specific ruins). He waits in the Upper Yard ruins for intruders. This establishes the theocratic structure: Enel as God, priests as judges. Frames Gedatsu as a gatekeeper; sets up the Straw Hats splitting up and each facing a trial. Establishes that priests are not to be underestimated despite comedic designs.

Trial of the Swamp vs. Tony Tony ChopperOne Piece manga Ch. 255-257, 261; anime Ep. 165-167, 170

2003

Separated from the crew, Tony Tony Chopper falls into Gedatsu's domain: a forest where the clouds themselves are swamp. Gedatsu demonstrates the Numa Kumo by sinking a bird and then Chopper's leg. He explains that struggling only makes you sink faster. Chopper, in Brain Point, analyzes the cloud's properties while Gedatsu comically forgets his own plan, forgets to attack, and sinks into his own swamp. The battle is a mix of tension and slapstick: Gedatsu uses Jet Dials to launch high-speed punches, Chopper uses his transformations (Heavy Point, Horn Point) and the environment. Chopper ultimately outwits him by using the swamp's buoyancy and Gedatsu's forgetfulness. Gedatsu's Trial is broken; Chopper proves his growth as a fighter without Monster Point. Demonstrates that Dial technology, not Devil Fruits, powers Sky Island combat.

Defeat and the Fall from the SkyOne Piece manga Ch. 261, 275, 298; anime Ep. 170-171

2003

Chopper defeats Gedatsu by knocking him into the swamp and then, with a final blow, sending him flying off the island's edge. Unlike Satori who is defeated earlier, Gedatsu is launched clear off Skypiea, plummeting through the White Sea clouds toward the Blue Sea below — a fall that should be fatal. He is not seen again in the main Skypiea battle. When Enel is later defeated by Luffy and his Ark Maxim crashes, the remaining priests' regime collapses. Gedatsu's absence from the final celebration underscores that his story is not over but displaced. Removes one priest from the board before the climactic war between Straw Hats, Shandians, and Enel's forces. Sets up the cover story by physically removing Gedatsu from the sky.

Accounts II
Gedatsu's Accidental Blue Sea Life - The Fall and Rescue by GoroOne Piece manga cover story Ch. 314-322 (Gedatsu's Accidental Blue Sea Life, parts 1-9)

2004-2005 publication; in-world: shortly after Skypiea's liberation

The 30-part cover serial follows Gedatsu after his fall. He survives impact in the Blue Sea, washing up near Alabasta (identified by desert, later confirmed as near Yuba). He is found unconscious by an elderly man named Goro, a tunnel-digger who has spent 20 years trying to dig a tunnel with his nephew Toto (Koza's father). Gedatsu, still in priest robes, is nursed back to health. In his typical forgetful fashion, he tries to help dig but causes collapses, yet his Jet Dials prove useful for excavation. He befriends Goro and the mole-like forest animals. Reframes Gedatsu from antagonist to accidental immigrant; explains his survival and introduces Alabasta aftermath without Straw Hat involvement. Establishes his post-priest identity as laborer.

Construction of the Ukkari Hot Spring (Ukkari Onsen)One Piece manga cover story Ch. 323-343 (parts 10-30)

2004-2005

While digging with Goro, Gedatsu accidentally breaches an underground aquifer, causing a massive geyser that floods the tunnel. Instead of disaster, the hot water creates a natural hot spring. Recognizing an opportunity, Gedatsu and Goro, joined by Toto, Koza, and other Alabasta residents (including former Baroque Works-affiliated villagers), build the Ukkari Hot Spring Resort around it. Gedatsu becomes the proprietor (referred to as Boss Gedatsu), wearing a yukata and managing the baths. The final panels show the resort thriving, with Koza and Vivi's allies visiting, and Gedatsu happily serving customers — a comedic atonement for his former role as a trial priest. The spring's name 'Ukkari' (meaning 'careless/accidental') puns on Gedatsu's personality. Provides closure: Gedatsu finds a peaceful, productive life contributing to desert community's water scarcity solution. The resort is later referenced in fanbooks as a canonical Alabasta location. Marks one of Oda's most complete villain-to-civilian rehabilitations via cover story.

Legacy in Skypiea's Liberation and Priesthood's EndOne Piece manga Ch. 298-303, 722 (referenced); databooks

2003-2004; retrospective mentions 2014

After Enel's defeat, Skypiea is liberated, Gan Fall is restored as God, and the priests' ordeals are abolished. While Ohm, Shura, and Satori are shown being defeated and later (in other cover stories) attempting to atone or being stranded, Gedatsu alone successfully integrates into Blue Sea society. His absence is noted in later Skypiea retrospectives (e.g., during Dressrosa when Urouge? No, but Skypiea is recalled). His story is used by Oda to show that not all of Enel's followers were irredeemable and that Sky Island technology (Dials) can be repurposed for peaceful use, as seen in the hot spring's construction using Dial-assisted drilling. The priesthood as an institution ends; Gedatsu's hot spring becomes a symbol of Dial technology's civilian potential. His survival is later cited in official fanbooks as proof that falls from Sky Islands are survivable with cloud cushioning.

Gedatsu is a Devil Fruit user (Swamp Man). — His swamp is Numa Kumo, a type of Island Cloud manipulated via Dials, and his jets are Jet Dials. No Devil Fruit is involved; he is vulnerable to standard attacks and can drown in his own swamp.

Gedatsu died when he fell from Skypiea. — He survived the fall, was rescued in the Blue Sea, and became the owner of the Ukkari Hot Spring Resort in Alabasta, as shown in the manga cover story 'Gedatsu's Accidental Blue Sea Life' (Ch. 314-343).

Gedatsu was the strongest priest. — Narratively and in fanbook power scaling, Gedatsu is portrayed as the weakest of the four priests, defeated by Chopper alone pre-timeskip, while Ohm required Zoro and Shura required Wyper/Gan Fall. His strength is comedic rather than formidable.
