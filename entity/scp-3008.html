Who It Is

Imagine walking into a familiar IKEA—fluorescent lights, flat-pack furniture, the comforting promise of meatballs—and then realizing, hours later, that the exit has vanished and the store never ends. In SCP-3008, that moment of dawning horror stretches into an entire civilization: towns built from bookshelves, barricades of sofas, and faceless staff who politely announce closing time before hunting you through the dark. It is retail architecture turned into a labyrinth, customer service rewritten as a death sentence, and the everyday act of shopping recast as a portal into an uncaring, possibly infinite space.

Attested: SCP-3008 as a location exhibits no explicit sentience; its anomalous behavior is spatial and temporal. The interior pocket dimension translocates entrants once they pass out of sight of the main doors, and exits appear unpredictably, suggesting a disorienting, possibly non-Euclidean topology. SCP-3008-1 has a day-night cycle governed by overhead lighting: during "day" the lights are on, and SCP-3008-2 are generally passive, wandering aimlessly and ignoring humans unless attacked. Name & Tongue

Etymology — The designation "SCP-3008" follows the SCP Foundation’s numerical cataloging system for anomalous objects and locations. The subtitle "A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old IKEA" is an ironic description emphasizing the apparent normality of the building’s exterior contrasted with its anomalous interior.

Pronunciation — Ess-See-Pee Three-Double-Oh-Eight; "A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old EE-kay-uh" (IKEA commonly pronounced "eye-KEE-uh" or "ee-KAY-uh" depending on regional usage).

Powers
Spatial translocation at entrance

Attested: Individuals entering SCP-3008 through the main entrance and passing out of sight of the doors are translocated to SCP-3008-1, the interior pocket dimension. This displacement is seamless and typically goes unnoticed until the person attempts to return to the entrance, which is no longer accessible. The phenomenon functions as a one-way portal triggered by crossing a threshold and losing visual contact with the doors.

Limit: Attested: The translocation appears limited to the main entrance; attempts to breach walls or other external surfaces reveal non-anomalous interior sections consistent with a normal IKEA store. Entry is thus controlled by the Foundation by guarding the doors. The effect does not visibly alter the entrant’s body or immediate surroundings, making it difficult to detect in real time.

Seemingly infinite interior space

Attested: SCP-3008-1 resembles the interior of an IKEA store but extends far beyond the building’s physical limits. Measurements indicate an area of at least 10 km² with no visible external terminators. Laser rangefinder tests yield inconclusive results, leading to speculation that the space may be infinite.

Limit: Attested: The Foundation has not conclusively proven infinity; the space is at minimum extremely large and disorienting. Human exploration is limited by survival constraints, hostile entities, and the difficulty of navigation.

Day-night cycle controlled by lighting

Attested: SCP-3008-1 has a rudimentary day-night cycle determined by overhead lights turning on and off at times consistent with the opening and closing hours of a normal IKEA store. During "day" the environment is brightly lit; during "night" the lights switch off, plunging the store into darkness.

Limit: Attested: The cycle appears regular and predictable, allowing human survivors to plan defenses. It is unclear whether the cycle can be disrupted or manipulated by the Foundation.

Automatic replenishment of food (meatballs)

Attested/adaptation-specific: Food courts within SCP-3008-1 serve meatballs and other items despite the absence of visible kitchen staff. Fan databases note that meatball stores replenish themselves automatically, providing theoretically limitless food for survivors.

Limit: Attested: The replenishment appears limited to certain food items and locations; other resources (water, tools, medical supplies) must be scavenged. The mechanism is unexplained.

Hostile behavioral shift of SCP-3008-2 at night

Attested: SCP-3008-2, the faceless staff entities, are generally passive during the day, wandering without reacting to humans unless attacked. At night, they become violently hostile, attacking any humans they encounter while repeating the phrase "Excuse me, the store is now closed. Please exit the building." Their aggression increases when parts of another instance are nearby, suggesting a collective or contagion-like behavioral amplification.

Limit: Attested: SCP-3008-2 can be fought and killed by humans and Foundation forces, as evidenced by Incident 3008-1. They are physically strong and persistent but not invincible. Their hostility ceases when the lights turn back on.

Unpredictable exits from SCP-3008-1

Attested: SCP-3008-1 contains one or more exits that appear at random locations and times. These exits are difficult to find due to the store’s vastness and shifting spatial cues. A small number of humans (14 in the original article) have successfully exited since containment began.

Limit: Attested: Exits are not under human control and may vanish or relocate. Survivors often stumble upon them by chance rather than systematic search.

Accounts I
SCP-3008 Main Containment NarrativeSCP-3008 - SCP Foundation (Mortos)

2010s–present (initial publication 2017, ongoing readership)

The main SCP-3008 article presents the Foundation’s discovery and containment of a spatially anomalous IKEA store. After purchasing the retail park and converting it into Site-██, the Foundation redirects roads and guards the entrance. The description details the translocation effect at the doors, the vast interior SCP-3008-1, the presence of trapped civilians who have built settlements, and the hostile faceless staff entities SCP-3008-2. Special Containment Procedures outline monitoring, detaining and amnesticizing humans who exit, and terminating non-human entities. The article frames SCP-3008 as a Euclid-class anomaly requiring ongoing observation and limited exploration. The Foundation’s containment prevents public access to SCP-3008, reducing the number of new victims. However, many civilians remain trapped inside, forming a rudimentary civilization under constant threat from SCP-3008-2. The article implies ongoing ethical and logistical challenges: rescuing survivors is difficult, and the anomaly’s nature remains only partially understood.

Accounts II
Incident 3008-1: Foundation Report and Survivor DiarySCP-3008 - SCP Foundation (Incident 3008-1 addendum); SCP Foundation Wikia summary

2017 (within article); later fan retellings

Incident 3008-1 is presented in two parts: a formal Foundation report and the diary of a man who escaped SCP-3008-1. The report states that on a specific date at 12:37 AM, a man exited the building but was killed by an instance of SCP-3008-2 shortly afterward; the entity was then terminated by Foundation personnel. The diary, recovered from the man’s belongings, recounts his experiences inside SCP-3008-1: he wanders through endless aisles, encounters other survivors, and eventually settles in a town called "Exchange". He describes daily life, defenses against the staff, and glimpses of Foundation drones. Memory gaps among residents—such as ignorance of the Statue of Liberty—suggest that many come from alternate universes, explaining the lack of missing-person reports in any single world. Exchange is eventually destroyed by SCP-3008-2, and the diarist’s escape ends in his death outside the store. The incident confirms that escape from SCP-3008-1 is possible but extremely dangerous. Community and Fan Game Narratives (e.g., Roblox Infinite IKEA experiences)WikiHow article; various fan game descriptions and community posts

Late 2010s–2020s

SCP-3008 has inspired numerous fan-made games, particularly on platforms like Roblox, where players are trapped in an infinite IKEA and must survive attacks by staff-like entities. These games typically feature base-building from furniture, resource gathering, and cooperative defense against nightly assaults. While not canonical, they translate the SCP-3008 concept into interactive survival horror, often adding mechanics such as hunger, stamina, and crafting. These narratives extend SCP-3008’s reach beyond the SCP website, embedding it in gaming culture and reinforcing its status as a recognizable internet-horror archetype. They also shape popular understanding of SCP-3008, sometimes overshadowing the original article.
