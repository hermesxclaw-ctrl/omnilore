SCP-3008: A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old IKEA

Pronunciation Ess-See-Pee Three-Double-Oh-Eight; "A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old EE-kay-uh" (IKEA commonly pronounced "eye-KEE-uh" or "ee-KAY-uh" depending on regional usage).

Known As

Infinite IKEAUr-IKEASCP-3008-1 (interior pocket dimension)A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old IKEA

Etymology

The designation "SCP-3008" follows the SCP Foundation’s numerical cataloging system for anomalous objects and locations. The subtitle "A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old IKEA" is an ironic description emphasizing the apparent normality of the building’s exterior contrasted with its anomalous interior. "Infinite IKEA" and "Ur-IKEA" are fan and database nicknames highlighting the store’s seemingly endless interior and archetypal status among SCP retail anomalies.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-3008 is a collaborative-fiction entity originating on the SCP Foundation website, written by the author credited as Mortos. It exists within the broader SCP Foundation mythos, a community-driven internet-horror and speculative fiction project governed by Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licensing. The setting borrows the real-world IKEA brand as a recognizable cultural touchstone but transforms it into an anomalous, extra-dimensional horror environment. The narrative and lore are primarily owned and curated by the SCP community and derivative fan creators, not by IKEA itself.

Disputed Identifications

There is no major dispute about SCP-3008’s identity as an anomalous IKEA-branded retail unit and its interior pocket dimension SCP-3008-1. Minor fan discussions debate whether SCP-3008-1 is truly infinite or merely extremely large, and whether it should be classified as a separate SCP in its own right. Some fan wikis treat SCP-3008-1 as a distinct entry, but this is a taxonomic rather than ontological dispute.

Archive Dossier — Approach

Imagine walking into a familiar IKEA—fluorescent lights, flat-pack furniture, the comforting promise of meatballs—and then realizing, hours later, that the exit has vanished and the store never ends. In SCP-3008, that moment of dawning horror stretches into an entire civilization: towns built from bookshelves, barricades of sofas, and faceless staff who politely announce closing time before hunting you through the dark. It is retail architecture turned into a labyrinth, customer service rewritten as a death sentence, and the everyday act of shopping recast as a portal into an uncaring, possibly infinite space.

The store is now closed. Please exit the building. [Distorted, repeated in a continuous loop]

Who It Is

SCP-3008 is an IKEA retail furniture store that, from the outside, appears identical to any standard IKEA location. The entrance and exit function normally during daytime hours. However, once an individual enters deeper into the store and loses sight of the entrance for an extended period, they become trapped in an extradimensional space. The interior of SCP-3008 is vastly larger than its external dimensions suggest — estimates place it at a minimum of 10 square kilometers, with some survivors reporting seemingly infinite expanse. The interior contains all the expected features of an IKEA showroom: display rooms, cafeteria areas, warehouse sections, and endless aisles of flat-pack furniture. A day-night cycle exists within, simulated by the store's lighting systems dimming and brightening on a 24-hour cycle. During the 'night' period, the store's 'Employees' emerge. These humanoid entities, designated SCP-3008-2, resemble IKEA staff members — wearing the standard blue and yellow uniform — but their proportions are wrong. Their faces are blank and featureless, their limbs are slightly too long, and they speak only in distorted, garbled approximations of IKEA product names and employee phrases. SCP-3008-2 instances are uniformly hostile to humans after dark, attempting to capture or kill anyone they encounter. Survivors within SCP-3008 have formed small communities, constructing fortified settlements from furniture, establishing trade networks, and developing survival strategies. The Foundation maintains a permanent presence at the store's entrance, cordoning it off from the public while allowing monitored exploration. Interview logs with long-term survivors reveal a desperate existence of resource management, territorial disputes with other survivor groups, and constant vigilance against the Employees. SCP-3008 is particularly notable for its deceptively mundane exterior — shoppers enter unknowingly and may never be seen again. The entity plays on the universally recognized surrealism of large IKEA stores, where disorientation and the feeling of being trapped are part of the designed experience. The Foundation estimates that several thousand individuals may currently be lost within SCP-3008.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Attested: SCP-3008 appears externally as a large, modern retail unit branded as an IKEA furniture store, located within a retail park acquired and converted into Site-██ by the SCP Foundation. The building’s exterior dimensions are consistent with a typical large-format IKEA store, with parking lots and access roads. Inside, SCP-3008-1—the anomalous interior space—resembles a standard IKEA showroom and warehouse: aisles of flat-pack furniture, lighting fixtures, textiles, and signage marking sections such as "Living Room", "Exchange", "Checkouts", and "Trolley". However, the interior extends far beyond the physical limits of the building, with measured areas of at least 10 km² and no visible terminus, giving the impression of an endless maze of furniture and shelving. The environment includes food courts serving meatballs and other IKEA-style food, despite the absence of visible kitchens or staff. SCP-3008-2, the humanoid entities, resemble distorted IKEA employees: they wear yellow shirts and blue trousers consistent with the IKEA uniform, but their bodies are elongated or shortened, with inconsistent proportions and no facial features. Their clothing appears integrated into their skin rather than removable attire.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: SCP-3008’s iconography is dominated by IKEA branding—blue-and-yellow color schemes, section signs, product labels, and directional arrows. Within SCP-3008-1, human settlements adopt the store’s signage as toponyms: towns named "Exchange", "Checkouts", and "Trolley" are built from scavenged furniture, turning commercial signage into civic markers. The faceless staff (SCP-3008-2) visually echo real-world retail employees but become uncanny symbols of corporate anonymity and dehumanized labor. Their repeated phrase during night attacks—"Excuse me, the store is now closed. Please exit the building."—functions as a ritualized, weaponized customer-service script. In fan art and derivative works, SCP-3008 is often represented by endless aisles under fluorescent lights, faceless staff silhouettes, and the juxtaposition of the familiar IKEA logo with horror aesthetics.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-3008 as a location exhibits no explicit sentience; its anomalous behavior is spatial and temporal. The interior pocket dimension translocates entrants once they pass out of sight of the main doors, and exits appear unpredictably, suggesting a disorienting, possibly non-Euclidean topology. SCP-3008-1 has a day-night cycle governed by overhead lighting: during "day" the lights are on, and SCP-3008-2 are generally passive, wandering aimlessly and ignoring humans unless attacked. During "night" the lights switch off, and SCP-3008-2 become violently hostile, hunting humans and repeating the closing-time phrase. Their behavior suggests a programmed or instinctual enforcement of store hours rather than individual personality. Humans trapped inside form communities, build fortifications, and develop survival strategies, displaying a range of personalities and coping mechanisms—from cooperative town-building to desperate isolation. The diary in Incident 3008-1 portrays survivors as pragmatic, traumatized, and occasionally humorous in the face of absurd horror.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-3008-1 is an extra-dimensional pocket space whose domain is retail architecture—specifically, an IKEA furniture store interior. Its habitat includes endless aisles, showrooms, warehouse racks, food courts, and scattered settlements built from furniture. SCP-3008-2 inhabit this space, appearing throughout the store and congregating more densely at night. Human survivors establish towns near food courts or resource-rich sections, using furniture to construct walls, watchtowers, and barricades. The environment automatically replenishes certain resources, notably meatballs in the food courts, suggesting a self-sustaining or reset mechanism. The SCP Foundation treats the surrounding retail park and building as a containment site, redirecting roads and monitoring the entrance to prevent accidental entry.

Version Differences

Attested vs adaptation-specific: The core SCP Foundation article establishes SCP-3008 as a Euclid-class anomalous IKEA store with an interior pocket dimension, faceless staff entities, a day-night cycle, and human settlements. Fan wikis (SCP Foundation Wikia, SCP Database Wiki, SCP3008 Wiki) largely reproduce this description but add clarifying details, such as the theory that SCP-3008-1 may be infinite, the naming of specific towns (Exchange, Checkouts, Trolley), and notes about automatic meatball replenishment. The WikiHow article emphasizes SCP-3008’s popularity, explains its mechanics in lay terms, and connects it to fan-made games (e.g., Roblox experiences) and broader SCP fandom. Some adaptations, especially games, exaggerate or simplify elements: staff may be more numerous or more grotesque, exits may be gamified, and survival mechanics may be foregrounded. These are adaptation-specific and not strictly canonical to the original SCP article. The notion that many residents come from alternate universes—hinted at by memory gaps and lack of missing-person reports—is a fan-interpretive layer derived from Incident 3008-1 rather than an explicit, fully developed canonical doctrine.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Spatial translocation at entrance

Source Version SCP-3008 original article (Description section); corroborated by fan summaries.

Description

Attested: Individuals entering SCP-3008 through the main entrance and passing out of sight of the doors are translocated to SCP-3008-1, the interior pocket dimension. This displacement is seamless and typically goes unnoticed until the person attempts to return to the entrance, which is no longer accessible. The phenomenon functions as a one-way portal triggered by crossing a threshold and losing visual contact with the doors.

Limitations

Attested: The translocation appears limited to the main entrance; attempts to breach walls or other external surfaces reveal non-anomalous interior sections consistent with a normal IKEA store. Entry is thus controlled by the Foundation by guarding the doors. The effect does not visibly alter the entrant’s body or immediate surroundings, making it difficult to detect in real time.

Contradictions

Disputed: It is unclear whether the translocation moves individuals into a separate dimension or reconfigures space within the same universe. Some fan sources describe SCP-3008-1 as a "pocket dimension"; others treat it as a spatially impossible interior. The original article leaves the exact metaphysical nature unresolved.

Seemingly infinite interior space

Source Version SCP-3008 original article (Description); SCP Database Wiki elaboration.

Description

Attested: SCP-3008-1 resembles the interior of an IKEA store but extends far beyond the building’s physical limits. Measurements indicate an area of at least 10 km² with no visible external terminators. Laser rangefinder tests yield inconclusive results, leading to speculation that the space may be infinite.

Limitations

Attested: The Foundation has not conclusively proven infinity; the space is at minimum extremely large and disorienting. Human exploration is limited by survival constraints, hostile entities, and the difficulty of navigation.

Contradictions

Disputed: Some sources and fans assert that SCP-3008-1 is truly infinite; others emphasize that this is speculative. The article’s cautious language supports an agnostic position.

Day-night cycle controlled by lighting

Source Version SCP-3008 original article; fan wikis; WikiHow explanation.

Description

Attested: SCP-3008-1 has a rudimentary day-night cycle determined by overhead lights turning on and off at times consistent with the opening and closing hours of a normal IKEA store. During "day" the environment is brightly lit; during "night" the lights switch off, plunging the store into darkness.

Limitations

Attested: The cycle appears regular and predictable, allowing human survivors to plan defenses. It is unclear whether the cycle can be disrupted or manipulated by the Foundation.

Contradictions

Unknown: No major contradictions; some adaptations may alter cycle length for gameplay purposes.

Automatic replenishment of food (meatballs)

Source Version SCP Database Wiki (Attributes); WikiHow article; implied by SCP-3008 narrative.

Description

Attested/adaptation-specific: Food courts within SCP-3008-1 serve meatballs and other items despite the absence of visible kitchen staff. Fan databases note that meatball stores replenish themselves automatically, providing theoretically limitless food for survivors.

Limitations

Attested: The replenishment appears limited to certain food items and locations; other resources (water, tools, medical supplies) must be scavenged. The mechanism is unexplained.

Contradictions

Unknown: The original SCP article does not explicitly describe the replenishment mechanism; fan sources extrapolate from narrative hints.

Hostile behavioral shift of SCP-3008-2 at night

Source Version SCP-3008 original article; SCP3008 Wiki; SCP Foundation Wikia laconic summary.

Description

Attested: SCP-3008-2, the faceless staff entities, are generally passive during the day, wandering without reacting to humans unless attacked. At night, they become violently hostile, attacking any humans they encounter while repeating the phrase "Excuse me, the store is now closed. Please exit the building." Their aggression increases when parts of another instance are nearby, suggesting a collective or contagion-like behavioral amplification.

Limitations

Attested: SCP-3008-2 can be fought and killed by humans and Foundation forces, as evidenced by Incident 3008-1. They are physically strong and persistent but not invincible. Their hostility ceases when the lights turn back on.

Contradictions

Minor: Some fan depictions exaggerate their strength, speed, or intelligence for dramatic effect. Canonically, they are dangerous but not unstoppable.

Unpredictable exits from SCP-3008-1

Source Version SCP-3008 original article; SCP Foundation Wikia; SCP3008 Wiki.

Description

Attested: SCP-3008-1 contains one or more exits that appear at random locations and times. These exits are difficult to find due to the store’s vastness and shifting spatial cues. A small number of humans (14 in the original article) have successfully exited since containment began.

Limitations

Attested: Exits are not under human control and may vanish or relocate. Survivors often stumble upon them by chance rather than systematic search.

Contradictions

Unknown: Some adaptations treat exits as more predictable or as objectives in game design; canon emphasizes randomness and disorientation.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

SCP-3008-1 (interior pocket dimension)

Type locationSource SCP-3008 original article; SCP Database Wiki.

Description

SCP-3008-1 is the interior space accessed by entering SCP-3008 and passing out of sight of the main doors. It is effectively the "true" anomalous environment, while SCP-3008’s exterior building serves as the portal and containment shell.

Version Note

Canonical SCP Foundation description; fan databases treat SCP-3008-1 as a distinct entry but subordinate to SCP-3008.

Conflicting Accounts

Minor taxonomic disagreement over whether SCP-3008-1 should be cataloged as a separate SCP.

SCP-3008-2 (faceless staff entities)

Type hostileSource SCP-3008 original article; SCP Foundation Wikia; SCP3008 Wiki.

Description

SCP-3008-2 are humanoid entities inhabiting SCP-3008-1. They resemble IKEA employees but have distorted proportions and lack facial features. They are passive during the day and violently hostile at night, attacking humans and enforcing a ritualized closing-time script.

Version Note

Canonical description with minor elaborations in fan sources.

Conflicting Accounts

Some adaptations depict SCP-3008-2 as more intelligent or monstrous than in the original article; these are creative variations.

SCP Foundation

Type creator|wielder|containment authoritySource SCP-3008 original article (Special Containment Procedures).

Description

Within the fiction, the SCP Foundation discovers SCP-3008, purchases the retail park, and converts it into Site-██. The Foundation monitors the entrance, detains and amnesticizes humans who exit, and terminates non-human entities. It is responsible for documenting SCP-3008 and attempting to understand and contain its anomaly.

Version Note

Canonical organizational relationship within SCP lore.

Conflicting Accounts

Fan fiction sometimes portrays the Foundation as more interventionist, launching rescue operations or exploiting SCP-3008; these are speculative expansions.

Civilians trapped inside SCP-3008-1

Type victim|inhabitantSource SCP-3008 original article; Incident 3008-1 diary.

Description

An unknown number of civilians are trapped within SCP-3008-1, having entered the store before containment or through accidental breaches. They form settlements, build fortifications, and develop social structures to survive. They are victims of the anomaly and of SCP-3008-2’s nightly attacks.

Version Note

Canonical depiction of survivor communities.

Conflicting Accounts

Fan works vary in estimating population size and in depicting the complexity of their societies.

IKEA (real-world brand)

Type name-use|setting inspirationSource SCP-3008 original article; WikiHow explanation.

Description

SCP-3008 uses IKEA’s branding, architecture, and cultural associations as the basis for its horror setting. The store is described as previously owned by and branded as IKEA, a popular furniture retail chain. This relationship is fictional and does not imply endorsement or participation by the real company.

Version Note

Canonical within the SCP narrative; legally framed under Creative Commons and transformative fiction.

Conflicting Accounts

None in lore; in real-world discourse, some readers mistakenly assume official involvement by IKEA, which is not supported by evidence.
