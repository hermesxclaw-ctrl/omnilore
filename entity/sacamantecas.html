Archive Dossier — Approach

In the dark corners of the Spanish imagination, there lurks a figure whose very name promises a gruesome trade: the Sacamantecas, the 'fat extractor.' A bogeyman whispered about to frighten children, he is said to roam the countryside, seeking human fat for his nefarious purposes—whether to grease machinery, craft ointments, or satisfy a cannibalistic hunger. But is he merely a folkloric phantom, or does he have roots in real crimes that once haunted Spain?

Don't stay out after dark, or El Sacamantecas will catch you. He has long fingernails and a knife, and he takes the fat from your bones to sell in the market.

Who It Is

El Sacamantecas (literally 'the fat extractor' or 'the lard remover') is a terrifying bogeyman figure from Spanish folklore, particularly prevalent in Basque Country, Navarre, and northern Spain. Unlike many mythological creatures with ancient origins, the Sacamantecas emerged as a distinct folk figure in the 19th century, inspired by real-life serial killers whose crimes horrified the rural population. The creature is described as a tall, gaunt man dressed in black rags, with long fingernails, hollow eyes, and a curved knife. He lurks at crossroads, forest paths, and the edges of villages, waiting for lone children or women. His name derives from his gruesome modus operandi: he captures his victims, kills them, and extracts their body fat (manteca), which he then sells for use in soaps, cosmetics, or medicinal ointments. The most infamous historical figure associated with the Sacamantecas legend was Manuel Blanco Romasanta, a 19th-century Galician serial killer who claimed he was possessed and who was convicted of murdering 13 people (women and children) and selling their fat. Another was Juan Díaz de Garayo (known as 'El Sacamantecas de Vitoria'), who murdered six women in the 1870s. The Sacamantecas legend was used by parents to control children — the threat that 'El Sacamantecas will come for you if you wander too far from home' was a powerful deterrent. The figure remains one of Spain's most enduring bogeyman legends, a reflection of historical social anxieties about strangers and the exploitation of the vulnerable.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Often described as a gaunt, sinister man, sometimes elderly, with a hunched posture and a sack or container for collecting fat. Some versions depict him as a butcher or a vagrant.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with knives, hooks, and containers of fat. His image is used in warnings to children.

Behaviour & Personality

He is said to lurk in isolated areas, waiting to capture children or travelers. He extracts their fat, sometimes killing them in the process. He is portrayed as cunning and predatory, preying on the vulnerable.

Domains & Habitats

Rural areas, forests, and roadsides; often near villages where he can find victims.

Version Differences

Some versions say he sells the fat for industrial purposes, others for cooking or as a cure. In some, he is a solitary criminal; in others, he is part of a network.
