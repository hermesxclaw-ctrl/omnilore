The Approach

Renard is a name that slips through languages and genres—from a sly fox of medieval fables to a French privateer ship, a Bond villain, and a Canadian diamond mine. This dossier untangles the threads of a name that has been woven into folklore, engineering, and pop culture alike.

Identity

Renard

Pronunciation /rəˈnɑːr/ or /ˈrɛnərd/ in English; /ʁə.naʁ/ in French

Known As

ReynardReynardineRenard the FoxLe Renard

Etymology

From Old High German 'Reginhart' meaning 'strong counsel' or 'brave counsel', composed of 'ragin' (counsel) and 'hart' (strong, hard). In French, 'renard' replaced the older word for fox, 'goupil', after the popularity of the Roman de Renart.

Cultural Ownership

The name is most famously associated with the European folkloric fox Reynard, a figure of medieval French, Dutch, and German literature. The name is also used across various cultures in modern media and commerce.

Disputed Identifications

The name 'Renard' is shared by many unrelated entities, leading to potential confusion. For instance, the folkloric fox is distinct from the Bond villain or the aircraft manufacturer. Additionally, some sources conflate 'Renard' with 'Reynardine', a different folkloric figure.

Description

Appearance

The folkloric Renard (Reynard) is typically depicted as an anthropomorphic fox, with reddish-orange fur, a bushy tail, and often wearing clothing or standing upright. In modern media, appearances vary widely, from the sly fox of the animated film 'Robin Hood' to the humanoid Renard in 'Grimm'.

Symbols & Iconography

Renard the fox symbolizes cunning, trickery, and wit. In heraldry, a fox may represent intelligence and a willingness to outsmart opponents. In the Stravinsky opera, the fox is a central character in a burlesque tale.

Behaviour & Personality

The folkloric Renard is clever, deceitful, and self-serving, often outwitting other animals and humans. He is a trickster archetype, sometimes sympathetic but often amoral. In other contexts, characters named Renard may have varying personalities, from villainous (Bond villain) to heroic (Maria Renard).

Domains & Habitats

The folkloric fox is found in European forests and countryside, both in literature and in reality. The name is also attached to geographic locations such as Cape Renard in Antarctica and Renard Island in Papua New Guinea.

Version Differences

The folkloric Reynard is a medieval literary figure, while the webcomic character 'Reynardine' is a modern reinterpretation. The Bond villain Renard is a terrorist, unrelated to the fox. The Stravinsky opera features a fox as a trickster in a Russian folk setting.

Stories & Myths

Roman de Renart

Source Medieval French literatureCulture FrenchPeriod 12th-13th century

Summary

A collection of satirical fables about Reynard the Fox, who outwits other animals, particularly the wolf Isengrim, to survive and prosper. The stories critique medieval society, church, and nobility.

Participants

Reynard

Isengrim

King Noble the Lion

Chanticleer the Rooster

Consequences

The tales became immensely popular and spread across Europe, influencing later literature and the word 'renard' replacing 'goupil' in French.

Variants

There are many versions and continuations, including Dutch, German, and English adaptations.

Renard (Stravinsky opera)

Source Igor StravinskyCulture Russian/FrenchPeriod 1916

Summary

A burlesque for four male singers and chamber orchestra, based on Russian folk tales. The fox (Renard) tries to catch a rooster, but is outwitted by the rooster's friends, the cat and the ram.

Participants

Renard

Rooster

Cat

Ram

Consequences

The opera was premiered by the Ballets Russes and remains a notable work in Stravinsky's oeuvre.

Variants

The libretto was written by Stravinsky and is based on Russian folk tales collected by Alexander Afanasyev.
