Who It Is

Renard is a name that slips through languages and genres—from a sly fox of medieval fables to a French privateer ship, a Bond villain, and a Canadian diamond mine. This dossier untangles the threads of a name that has been woven into folklore, engineering, and pop culture alike.

The folkloric Renard is clever, deceitful, and self-serving, often outwitting other animals and humans. He is a trickster archetype, sometimes sympathetic but often amoral. In other contexts, characters named Renard may have varying personalities, from villainous (Bond villain) to heroic (Maria Renard).

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Old High German 'Reginhart' meaning 'strong counsel' or 'brave counsel', composed of 'ragin' (counsel) and 'hart' (strong, hard). In French, 'renard' replaced the older word for fox, 'goupil', after the popularity of the Roman de Renart.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The folkloric Renard (Reynard) is typically depicted as an anthropomorphic fox, with reddish-orange fur, a bushy tail, and often wearing clothing or standing upright. In modern media, appearances vary widely, from the sly fox of the animated film 'Robin Hood' to the humanoid Renard in 'Grimm'.

Symbols — Renard the fox symbolizes cunning, trickery, and wit. In heraldry, a fox may represent intelligence and a willingness to outsmart opponents. In the Stravinsky opera, the fox is a central character in a burlesque tale.

Domains — The folkloric fox is found in European forests and countryside, both in literature and in reality. The name is also attached to geographic locations such as Cape Renard in Antarctica and Renard Island in Papua New Guinea.

Accounts I
Roman de RenartMedieval French literature

12th-13th century

A collection of satirical fables about Reynard the Fox, who outwits other animals, particularly the wolf Isengrim, to survive and prosper. The stories critique medieval society, church, and nobility. The tales became immensely popular and spread across Europe, influencing later literature and the word 'renard' replacing 'goupil' in French.

Accounts II
Renard (Stravinsky opera)Igor Stravinsky

1916

A burlesque for four male singers and chamber orchestra, based on Russian folk tales. The fox (Renard) tries to catch a rooster, but is outwitted by the rooster's friends, the cat and the ram. The opera was premiered by the Ballets Russes and remains a notable work in Stravinsky's oeuvre.

The World Is Not Enough (1999)James Bond film

1999

Renard, a former MI6 agent turned terrorist, plans to cause a nuclear meltdown in Istanbul. Bond must stop him while protecting Elektra King, who is revealed to be Renard's lover and accomplice. Renard is killed by Bond, and the plot is thwarted.
