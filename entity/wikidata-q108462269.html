19th-21st century (post-colonial folklore)

Stories of a 'white lady' - a female spirit in white dress appearing near rivers, balete trees, or old houses - are ubiquitous in Central Luzon. She is variously a mourning mother, a victim of colonial violence, or a diwata. The Tagalog/Kapampangan word 'puti' (white) appears in many spirit names. These stories are NOT attested as Duhen Puti stories, but are included to warn against conflating a generic 'white' spirit epithet with the specific Wikidata entity Duhen Puti. Demonstrates high risk of false identification if 'Puti' is used as sole identifier

Context: The Loss of Kapampangan Oral Literature After Pinatubo and Colonial Conversion - Meta-narrative explaining absenceScholarship on Kapampangan cultural history (see citations: Larkin, Cannell, Center for Kapampangan Studies)

1571-present (Spanish colonization to 1991 Pinatubo eruption)

Spanish Augustinian missionization (from 1571), American period schooling, and the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo (sacred to Apung Malyari) caused massive displacement and loss of ritual specialists (mangagamot, katalonan). Many local anito names survive only in fragmentary word lists compiled by early ethnographers (Beyer, Miller) without accompanying narratives. The absence of a story for Duhen Puti fits this documented pattern of archival loss rather than indicating the deity never had stories. Explains why a deity might be reduced to a name in a database; frames research gap as historical, not ontological
