Who It Is

In the grand pantheon of Greek gods — Zeus with his thunderbolt, Athena with her owl, Apollo with his lyre — there is no Acmena. The name rings with the clarity of an ancient Greek epithet, evoking the peak of a mountain, the sharp edge of a blade, the full bloom of youth. Yet when you search the vast archives of mythology — the dusty volumes of Smith's dictionary, the digital halls of Theoi, the inscriptions of a thousand temples — you will find nothing. No statue, no hymn, no votive offering. The only Acmena that exists is a tree: the lilly pilly, a rainforest evergreen of Australia and Southeast Asia, bearing edible pink fruits and white flowers like small explosions of cream. This is the curious case of a name that sounds divine but belongs to a berry. Botanical characteristics (genus Acmena)
