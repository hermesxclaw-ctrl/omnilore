The Approach

In the shadow of Odysseus' heroic saga stands a name barely whispered in ancient texts: Chalcomedusa, the guardian of bronze, whose very title links her to the age of heroes and the craft that forged their weapons. Though she never speaks or acts in any surviving tale, her presence anchors the lineage of Ithaca's most famous king.

Identity

Chalcomedusa

Pronunciation /ˌkælkoʊməˈduːsə/ (kal-koh-mə-DOO-sə)

Known As

Khalkomedousa

Etymology

From Ancient Greek χαλκός (chalkós, 'copper' or 'bronze') and μέδουσα (médousa, 'guardian' or 'protectress'). The name thus signifies 'guardian of bronze' or 'protectress of copper/bronze work'.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Greek mythology, specifically the island of Ithaca and the Homeric cycle.

Disputed Identifications

Some later genealogical traditions may conflate her with other figures or omit her entirely. The name is rare and appears mainly in scholia and later mythographic compilations.

Description

Appearance

No ancient descriptions of Chalcomedusa's physical appearance survive. As a queen and mother of Laertes, she would have been depicted as a noble matron in later artistic traditions, but nothing is attested.

Symbols & Iconography

Her name connects her to bronze/copper and guardianship, possibly associating her with protective deities or crafts, but no specific iconography is known.

Behaviour & Personality

No narratives provide information on her personality or behavior. She exists solely as a genealogical figure.

Domains & Habitats

As queen of Ithaca, she would have resided in the royal palace on Ithaca, but no specific domain is attributed to her.

Version Differences

Some traditions may omit her or name a different mother for Laertes (e.g., a daughter of Autolycus in some accounts), but Chalcomedusa is the most commonly cited mother in surviving mythological handbooks.

Stories & Myths

Genealogy of Odysseus

Source Scholia, Hyginus, Hesiodic fragmentsCulture Ancient GreekPeriod Archaic to Roman

Summary

Chalcomedusa is the wife of Arcesius, king of Ithaca, and mother of Laertes, who becomes the father of Odysseus. Her role is purely genealogical, providing the maternal line that connects Odysseus to the ruling house of Ithaca.

Participants

Consequences

Establishes Odysseus' noble lineage and his claim to the kingship of Ithaca.

Variants

Some traditions make Laertes' mother a daughter of Autolycus (possibly Amphithea), but Chalcomedusa is the standard figure in mythographic sources.
