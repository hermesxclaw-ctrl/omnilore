Who It Is

Tlazolteotl ('Filth Eater,' from tlazolli = filth/refuse and teotl = god) is the profound and purging Aztec goddess who embodies the paradox that the same force which creates sin can also consume and absolve it. As the 'Eater of Filth,' she devours the moral 'trash' of humanity — the miscalculations, betrayals, lusts, and failures that accumulate in the soul over a lifetime. The Aztec confession ritual (teochihualiztli) was addressed to her: the penitent confessed to a priest who 'ate' the sin by consuming the filth-symbolized as a morsel of food — after which the sinner was purified, though only once in a lifetime (unlike the repeatable Christian confession). Tlazolteotl is also the goddess of the spindle and the loom, of midwifery, and of the dark moon. Her four aspects (youth, maturity, elder, crone) mirror the fourfold division of the earth-mother and the phases of the moon.
