Who It Is

In the frozen wilds of Hokkaido, where the Ainu people tell of gods who walk among animals and humans, there is a figure known as Kanna Kamui—a name that carries the weight of thunder and the fury of the sky. But who exactly is this being? Is it a single deity, a class of spirits, or a name borrowed by modern storytellers for their own creations? The answer is as layered as the snow that blankets the northern island.

In Ainu mythology, Kanna Kamui is a powerful and sometimes capricious deity. He is responsible for thunder and storms, which can be destructive, but he is also seen as a guardian of the sky. He may punish humans for wrongdoing or be appeased with offerings. In 'Dragon Maid,' Kanna is portrayed as a kind, cute, and somewhat naive dragon child who is deeply attached to her human friend Kobayashi.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — In the Ainu language, 'kamuy' (also spelled 'kamui') means 'deity' or 'spirit.' 'Kanna' is often interpreted as 'above' or 'upper,' suggesting a sky or thunder deity. Thus, Kanna Kamui is commonly glossed as 'Thunder God' or 'God of Thunder.'

Pronunciation — Approximately /ˈkanna ˈkamuɪ/ (kah-nah kah-moo-ee) in English approximation; in Ainu, the 'u' is short and the 'i' is distinct.

Aliases — Kanna-kamuy, Kanna-kamui, Kanna Kamuy, Kanna no Kamui

Powers
Control over thunder and lightning

As a thunder god, Kanna Kamui can summon thunder and lightning, causing storms and striking targets.

Limit: Limited to weather phenomena; may be appeased or avoided by proper ritual.

Shape-shifting

Kanna Kamui can take the form of a bird or human to interact with the human world.

Limit: Not explicitly detailed; shape-shifting is common among kamuy.

Dragon abilities (modern)

The fictional Kanna can transform into a dragon, breathe electricity, and has superhuman strength and durability.

Limit: She cannot use her powers in public easily; she needs to charge up by consuming electricity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In traditional Ainu lore, Kanna Kamui is not consistently described in anthropomorphic terms. He may appear as thunder and lightning, or as a bird of prey (such as a hawk or eagle) associated with the sky. In some tales, he takes human form when visiting the earth. In the modern fictional 'Dragon Maid,' Kanna Kamui appears as a young girl with white hair, red eyes, and small horns, often wearing a school uniform.

Symbols — Traditional symbols include thunder, lightning, and the sky. The Ainu often depicted kamuy through carved wooden sticks (ikupasuy) and other ritual objects. In modern fiction, Kanna is often associated with dragon imagery, electricity, and a fang motif.

Domains — Traditional Kanna Kamui resides in the upper world (the sky), but he descends to the earth during thunderstorms. The Ainu believe that all natural phenomena have kamuy, and Kanna Kamui is the kamuy of thunder. In modern fiction, Kanna lives on Earth after being exiled from the dragon world, and she stays in Kobayashi's apartment.

Accounts I
The Thunder God and the HunterChamberlain, 'Aino Folk-Tales' (1888)

Pre-modern (oral tradition recorded in 19th century)

A hunter encounters Kanna Kamui in the mountains. The god is angered by the hunter's arrogance and strikes him with lightning, but the hunter is saved by a wise old woman who offers a sacrifice to the god. The hunter learns humility and the Ainu learn to respect the thunder god.

Kanna Kamui as Creator of the World (variant)Batchelor, 'The Ainu and Their Folklore' (1901)

19th century

Some Ainu traditions credit Kanna Kamui with creating the world or shaping the land, but this is not universal. Establishes Kanna Kamui as a major creative force in some cosmologies.

Kanna Kamui in Miss Kobayashi's Dragon MaidCoolkyoushinja, manga (2013-present)

2013-present

Kanna is a dragon girl who is exiled from her world for being too playful. She is taken in by Kobayashi, a programmer, and becomes like a daughter to her. She attends school and tries to live a normal life while hiding her dragon nature. Kanna forms a found family and learns about human society.
