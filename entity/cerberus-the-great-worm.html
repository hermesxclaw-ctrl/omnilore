Who It Is

A three-headed, dog-like beast who guards the Third Circle (Gluttony). Dante's Cerberus is not merely a guard but an active tormentor — he claws, flays, and tears the gluttonous souls apart while howling eternally. Virgil silences him by throwing fistfuls of mud into his three throats.

Attested in Inferno (Canto VI), Divine Comedy (1320)

Cultural Significance

Cerberus embodies gluttony itself — never sated, eternally consuming, a living metaphor for the sin he guards. The detail of Virgil throwing mud into his mouths echoes the classical Sibyl's trick for passing Cerberus with drugged honey cakes.
