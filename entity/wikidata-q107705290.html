Who It Is

In a universe crowded with planet-busting Chojin, masked knights and devil princes, Yosaku is defiantly ordinary — a small Japanese boy in a cap who cheers from the sidelines while 30-meter superhumans level stadiums. He has no Killer Move, no mask that revives the dead, and no ambition to be king. That ordinariness is exactly why Yosaku matters: he is the audience surrogate from Kinnikuman's earliest, strangest days, before wrestling took over the world.

Attested: Yosaku is cheerful, supportive, and slightly mischievous, typical of Shōnen Jump 'kid sidekick' archetype of the late 1970s. He cheers for Kinnikuman even when Kinnikuman is failing as a bumbling superhero, and reacts with exaggerated fear/awe when monsters or Chojin appear. He is not a fighter and shows cowardice or awe-struck paralysis in danger, then comic relief. Disputed/Unknown: No deep personality arc, internal monologue, or backstory is provided. Later fan summaries sometimes ascribe him loyalty or naive optimism, but this is interpretive rather than textually so

Powers
No Chojin Power (Human Civilian)

Yosaku is a normal human child with no Chojin Kyodo, Chojin Power, or supernatural ability. He does not possess superhuman strength, durability, or wrestling techniques. His role is purely supportive/spectator.

Limit: Cannot fight Chojin or kaiju; must flee or be rescued. No Burning Inner Strength (Kajiba no Kuso Djikara) or other Kinniku clan abilities.

Cheering / Moral Support

Narrative function: cheering for Kinnikuman and other Justice Chojin, providing audience reaction shots and comic commentary. In early gag structure, his cheers occasionally motivate Kinnikuman's garlic-fueled transformations, but not as a formal power.

Limit: No measurable effect on match outcomes after series shifts to wrestling rules; purely diegetic encouragement.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested: Yosaku is depicted as a small Japanese elementary-school-aged boy, short stature, simple rounded face, often wearing a baseball cap or short hair, casual 1970s-80s children's clothing (T-shirt, shorts). He is drawn in Yudetamago's early gag-manga style - less muscular detail than Chojin, large expressive eyes, simplified limbs. No costume, mask, or Chojin anatomy. In anime, color design gives him dark hair, light skin, yellow/orange cap

Symbols — None attested. Yosaku carries no personal emblem, weapon, or Chojin mark. His narrative symbol is that of the 'ordinary spectator' - the human crowd that contrasts with Chojin spectacle. In group scenes he may hold small flags or cheer banners for Kinnikuman, but these are generic, not character-specific iconography.

Domains — Attested: Lives in Tokyo, Japan (specifically the neighborhood where Kinnikuman operates as a resident superhero before Planet Kinniku reveal). Seen in streets, parks, schoolyards, and later in Chojin Olympics stadium audience. No connection to Planet Kinniku, Chojin realms, or other dimensions. His habitat is strictly human civilian Japan, grounding the early series before it expands to interplanetary wrestling tournaments.

Accounts I
Debut - Monster Extermination DaysYudetamago, Kinnikuman, Weekly Shōnen Jump 1979, Jump Comics Vol.1

1979 (Showa 54) - Monster Extermination Arc

In the earliest episodic chapters where Kinnikuman is a parody Ultraman-like hero who exterminates giant monsters for pay, Yosaku appears as one of the neighborhood children who watches and comments on Kinnikuman's incompetent heroics. In these stories Kinnikuman grows to 30 meters after eating garlic, fails comically, and is scolded by Meat Alexandria. Yosaku and other kids (including Yoshigai) serve as the peanut gallery, reacting to monsters like Eraginesu and others. Establishes Yosaku as part of the human civilian world before the series retcons Kinnikuman as a Chojin prince. No lasting consequences; arc is episodic and later de-emphasized.

The Garlic Giant GagYudetamago, Kinnikuman Chapter 1-10 range

1979

Kinnikuman's signature early gag - eating garlic to grow giant - is witnessed by Yosaku and friends. Yosaku's reaction (awe, then mockery when Kinnikuman fails) exemplifies the series' early tone: superhero pastiche where the hero is lazy and cowardly. Meat often has to explain Chojin matters to the children. Reinforces Kinnikuman's initial characterization before training under Prince Kamehame and learning the 48 Killer Moves. Yosaku does not influence plot.

Accounts II
Transition to Chojin Wrestling - The Audience EraYudetamago, Kinnikuman, 20th Chojin Olympics build-up (1980)

1980

As Yudetamago pivots from gag monster stories to wrestling tournament structure, human spectators become less relevant. Yosaku is last seen in crowd scenes around the announcement of the 20th Chojin Olympics, where Terryman, Robin Mask, Ramenman and others are introduced. The narrative focus shifts to Idol Chojin (Terryman, Robin Mask, Warsman, Brocken Jr., Wolfman) and the concept of Justice Chojin vs Brutal/Devil Chojin. Marks Yosaku's narrative obsolescence; human children are replaced by adult human supporters like Natsuko and later Bibinba. No further speaking roles attested after this period.

20th Chojin Olympics - Crowd WitnessYudetamago, Kinnikuman Vol. 3-5 (20th Chojin Olympics Arc)

1980-1981

During the 20th Chojin Olympics, where Kinnikuman defeats Ramenman (who had murdered Brockenman) and later Robin Mask in the finals, Yosaku appears only as an anonymous member of the stadium audience. He witnesses Ramenman's brutality and Kinnikuman's Burning Inner Strength comeback. His face is not individually highlighted; he is part of the 'humanity watching Chojin' motif. The arc establishes the brutal tone that will define later Devil Chojin arcs (Seven Devil Chojin, Golden Mask). Yosaku's presence underscores that humans are now spectators, not participants.

Absence and Legacy - The Missing CivilianYudetamago, Kinnikuman post-1981 arcs through Kinnikuman Nisei and 2011 revival

1981-present

From the Seven Devil Chojin arc onward (where Buffaloman splits Meat into seven pieces), through the Golden Mask, Dream Chojin Tag Team Tournament (where Terryman partners with Geronimo then becomes Kinnikuman Great), Scramble for the Throne, and Perfect Origin arcs, Yosaku does not appear. His narrative function is fully absorbed by Meat Alexandria (trainer), Natsuko (Terryman's wife), and later by new human characters. In Kinnikuman Nisei (1998), the next generation focuses on Kinniku Mantaro, Terry the Kid, Kevin Mask, and Meat's cryogenic return - Yosaku is not revived or referenced. Demonstrates how early gag characters were retired when Kinnikuman became a pure wrestling epic. Yosaku remains a footnote, illustrating the series' evolution from pastiche to sport-drama.

Yosaku is a Justice Chojin or Idol Chojin who fights alongside Kinnikuman. — Yosaku has no Chojin abilities, no wrestling moves, and never fights. He is a human child spectator from the earliest gag chapters, phased out before the Idol Chojin team (Terryman, Robin Mask, Warsman, Brocken Jr., Wolfman, Geronimo) was formalized after the 21st Chojin Olympics and Seven Devil Chojin arc.

Yosaku is an important recurring character throughout Kinnikuman and appears in Ultimate Muscle (Nisei). — Yosaku appears only in the first ~2 volumes / ~20 anime episodes (1979-1980) and is absent from all major wrestling arcs, films, games, Nisei, and the 2011 revival. His role was replaced by Natsuko, Bibinba, and Meat.

Yosaku has a signature move or Chojin Power level. — No source attributes any technique, power level (Chojin Kyodo), or finishing move to Yosaku. Early concept of Chojin Power measurement (e.g., Kinnikuman 950,000, Buffaloman 10,000,000) does not apply to humans.
