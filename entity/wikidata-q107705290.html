The Approach

In a universe crowded with planet-busting Chojin, masked knights and devil princes, Yosaku is defiantly ordinary — a small Japanese boy in a cap who cheers from the sidelines while 30-meter superhumans level stadiums. He has no Killer Move, no mask that revives the dead, and no ambition to be king. That ordinariness is exactly why Yosaku matters: he is the audience surrogate from Kinnikuman's earliest, strangest days, before wrestling took over the world.

Content advisory. Mild. Source manga contains slapstick violence, monster peril, and 1970s-80s Shonen Jump-era comedic stereotypes. No sexual violence or graphic gore associated specifically with Yosaku. Dark history section notes series-wide brutal wrestling violence that Yosaku witnesses as a bystander.
Identity

Yosaku

Pronunciation yo-sa-ku /josaꜜkɯ/ (Japanese: ヨサク, Yosaku)

Etymology

Japanese masculine given name 与作 (Yosaku), historically a common rural/folk name meaning 'given work' or associated with folk song 'Yosaku'. In Kinnikuman context, used as an everyman Japanese boy's name with no stated in-universe kanji or meaning. No official surname provided. Not to be confused with Yosaku from other manga/anime or historical figures.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese manga/anime franchise Kinnikuman, created by Yudetamago (Shueisha / Weekly Shōnen Jump). Character is a fictional minor human supporting character, not a figure from folklore or public domain mythology.

Disputed Identifications

Wikidata Q107705290 identifies Yosaku as a 'Kinnikuman character' with instance-of 'fictional character'. No conflation with other franchise Yosakus found. Fan wikis and databases occasionally conflate or omit Yosaku due to his minor, early-appearing status; some English lists omit him entirely. No evidence that Yosaku is a Chojin (Chōjin); all primary evidence identifies him as a human child civilian. No separate Romanized variant like 'Yosaku-kun' is canonical, though honorific -kun is used in dialogue.

Description

Appearance

Attested: Yosaku is depicted as a small Japanese elementary-school-aged boy, short stature, simple rounded face, often wearing a baseball cap or short hair, casual 1970s-80s children's clothing (T-shirt, shorts). He is drawn in Yudetamago's early gag-manga style - less muscular detail than Chojin, large expressive eyes, simplified limbs. No costume, mask, or Chojin anatomy. In anime, color design gives him dark hair, light skin, yellow/orange cap. No symbols, scars, or superhuman features. Reconstructed: Early chapters show him as part of a group of neighborhood children; individual distinguishing features are minimal, which contributes to later omission.

Symbols & Iconography

None attested. Yosaku carries no personal emblem, weapon, or Chojin mark. His narrative symbol is that of the 'ordinary spectator' - the human crowd that contrasts with Chojin spectacle. In group scenes he may hold small flags or cheer banners for Kinnikuman, but these are generic, not character-specific iconography.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: Yosaku is cheerful, supportive, and slightly mischievous, typical of Shōnen Jump 'kid sidekick' archetype of the late 1970s. He cheers for Kinnikuman even when Kinnikuman is failing as a bumbling superhero, and reacts with exaggerated fear/awe when monsters or Chojin appear. He is not a fighter and shows cowardice or awe-struck paralysis in danger, then comic relief. Disputed/Unknown: No deep personality arc, internal monologue, or backstory is provided. Later fan summaries sometimes ascribe him loyalty or naive optimism, but this is interpretive rather than textually sourced.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: Lives in Tokyo, Japan (specifically the neighborhood where Kinnikuman operates as a resident superhero before Planet Kinniku reveal). Seen in streets, parks, schoolyards, and later in Chojin Olympics stadium audience. No connection to Planet Kinniku, Chojin realms, or other dimensions. His habitat is strictly human civilian Japan, grounding the early series before it expands to interplanetary wrestling tournaments.

Version Differences

Manga (1979-1980) vs Anime (1983): Yosaku appears more frequently in manga's episodic gag chapters; anime condenses or omits several early monster-of-the-week stories, reducing his appearances. Post-1980 wrestling arcs: Yosaku effectively disappears as the cast shifts to Idol Chojin, Justice vs Devil Chojin factions, and human civilians are reduced to Natsuko, Bibinba, and announcer roles. No appearance in Kinnikuman Nisei (Ultimate Muscle, 1998-2011) or modern reboot Kinnikuman (2011-present) as of research cutoff. No Perfect Origin or Scramble for the Throne involvement. No alternative costume or power-up version exists.

Powers & Abilities

No Chojin Power (Human Civilian)

Source VersionKinnikuman manga early chapters (1979-1980), anime episodes 1-20

Yosaku is a normal human child with no Chojin Kyodo, Chojin Power, or supernatural ability. He does not possess superhuman strength, durability, or wrestling techniques. His role is purely supportive/spectator.

Limitations

Cannot fight Chojin or kaiju; must flee or be rescued. No Burning Inner Strength (Kajiba no Kuso Djikara) or other Kinniku clan abilities.

Contradictions

None - consistently portrayed as powerless. No source claims he is a Chojin.

Cheering / Moral Support

Source VersionEarly manga gag chapters

Narrative function: cheering for Kinnikuman and other Justice Chojin, providing audience reaction shots and comic commentary. In early gag structure, his cheers occasionally motivate Kinnikuman's garlic-fueled transformations, but not as a formal power.

Limitations

No measurable effect on match outcomes after series shifts to wrestling rules; purely diegetic encouragement.

Contradictions

Some fan wikis list 'support' as an ability; this is interpretive, not a canonical technique name.

Stories & Myths

Debut - Monster Extermination Days

Source Yudetamago, Kinnikuman, Weekly Shōnen Jump 1979, Jump Comics Vol.1Culture Japanese Shōnen mangaPeriod 1979 (Showa 54) - Monster Extermination Arc

Summary

In the earliest episodic chapters where Kinnikuman is a parody Ultraman-like hero who exterminates giant monsters for pay, Yosaku appears as one of the neighborhood children who watches and comments on Kinnikuman's incompetent heroics. In these stories Kinnikuman grows to 30 meters after eating garlic, fails comically, and is scolded by Meat Alexandria. Yosaku and other kids (including Yoshigai) serve as the peanut gallery, reacting to monsters like Eraginesu and others.

Participants

Yosaku

Kinnikuman (Suguru)

Meat Alexandria

Various kaiju

Consequences

Establishes Yosaku as part of the human civilian world before the series retcons Kinnikuman as a Chojin prince. No lasting consequences; arc is episodic and later de-emphasized.

Variants

Anime adaptation (Toei, 1983) retains some but not all of these vignettes; Yosaku's lines are often given to generic children chorus.

The Garlic Giant Gag

Source Yudetamago, Kinnikuman Chapter 1-10 rangeCulture Japanese Shōnen mangaPeriod 1979

Summary

Kinnikuman's signature early gag - eating garlic to grow giant - is witnessed by Yosaku and friends. Yosaku's reaction (awe, then mockery when Kinnikuman fails) exemplifies the series' early tone: superhero pastiche where the hero is lazy and cowardly. Meat often has to explain Chojin matters to the children.

Participants

Yosaku

Kinnikuman

Meat Alexandria

Consequences

Reinforces Kinnikuman's initial characterization before training under Prince Kamehame and learning the 48 Killer Moves. Yosaku does not influence plot.

Variants

No variant where Yosaku eats garlic or gains powers; fan invention only.

Transition to Chojin Wrestling - The Audience Era

Source Yudetamago, Kinnikuman, 20th Chojin Olympics build-up (1980)Culture Japanese Shōnen mangaPeriod 1980

Summary

As Yudetamago pivots from gag monster stories to wrestling tournament structure, human spectators become less relevant. Yosaku is last seen in crowd scenes around the announcement of the 20th Chojin Olympics, where Terryman, Robin Mask, Ramenman and others are introduced. The narrative focus shifts to Idol Chojin (Terryman, Robin Mask, Warsman, Brocken Jr., Wolfman) and the concept of Justice Chojin vs Brutal/Devil Chojin.

Participants

Yosaku (audience)

Kinnikuman

Terryman

Robin Mask

Ramenman

Consequences

Marks Yosaku's narrative obsolescence; human children are replaced by adult human supporters like Natsuko and later Bibinba. No further speaking roles attested after this period.

Variants

Some reprints and the anime omit these crowd shots entirely, leading to Yosaku's absence in many modern summaries.

20th Chojin Olympics - Crowd Witness

Source Yudetamago, Kinnikuman Vol. 3-5 (20th Chojin Olympics Arc)Culture Japanese Shōnen mangaPeriod 1980-1981

Summary

During the 20th Chojin Olympics, where Kinnikuman defeats Ramenman (who had murdered Brockenman) and later Robin Mask in the finals, Yosaku appears only as an anonymous member of the stadium audience. He witnesses Ramenman's brutality and Kinnikuman's Burning Inner Strength comeback. His face is not individually highlighted; he is part of the 'humanity watching Chojin' motif.

Participants

Kinnikuman

Ramenman

Robin Mask

Terryman (injured, leg amputated)

Yosaku (crowd)

Consequences

The arc establishes the brutal tone that will define later Devil Chojin arcs (Seven Devil Chojin, Golden Mask). Yosaku's presence underscores that humans are now spectators, not participants.

Variants

Anime version expands audience reactions with original children characters; Yosaku not individually named in credits.

Absence and Legacy - The Missing Civilian

Source Yudetamago, Kinnikuman post-1981 arcs through Kinnikuman Nisei and 2011 revivalCulture Japanese Shōnen mangaPeriod 1981-present

Summary

From the Seven Devil Chojin arc onward (where Buffaloman splits Meat into seven pieces), through the Golden Mask, Dream Chojin Tag Team Tournament (where Terryman partners with Geronimo then becomes Kinnikuman Great), Scramble for the Throne, and Perfect Origin arcs, Yosaku does not appear. His narrative function is fully absorbed by Meat Alexandria (trainer), Natsuko (Terryman's wife), and later by new human characters. In Kinnikuman Nisei (1998), the next generation focuses on Kinniku Mantaro, Terry the Kid, Kevin Mask, and Meat's cryogenic return - Yosaku is not revived or referenced.

Participants

Yosaku (absent)

Kinnikuman

Meat Alexandria

Idol Chojin collective

Consequences

Demonstrates how early gag characters were retired when Kinnikuman became a pure wrestling epic. Yosaku remains a footnote, illustrating the series' evolution from pastiche to sport-drama.

Variants

No attested variant where Yosaku returns as an adult or as a Chojin; any such claim is fanon.

Relationships
Yosaku→Kinnikuman (Kinniku Suguru)worshipper

Yosaku is a young fan/supporter of Kinnikuman during his early incompetent hero phase. He cheers for him despite Kinnikuman's cowardice and laziness, and is part of the civilian audience that Kinnikuman is supposed to protect. Not a trainer, family, or rival.

Yosaku→Meat Alexandriaally

Both are supporters of Kinnikuman, but with different status. Meat is Kinnikuman's alien trainer and best friend from Planet Kinniku, far more intelligent and central. Yosaku is a human child who occasionally interacts with Meat in crowd scenes, where Meat explains Chojin matters to the children.

Yosaku→Yoshigai and other neighborhood childrenally

Yosaku is typically depicted as part of a group of Japanese children (often with Yoshigai, another early human boy character) who form Kinnikuman's cheering squad. They appear together in street and park scenes.

Yosaku→Terryman, Robin Mask, Ramenman (Idol Chojin)worshipper

As a human spectator, Yosaku watches the Idol Chojin from the stands during the Chojin Olympics. He has no personal interaction with them, but represents the human public that idolizes Justice Chojin. Terryman's humility and Ramenman's initial brutality (murder of Brockenman) are witnessed by this audience.

Yosaku→Natsuko Shonoally

Natsuko, the human girl who later becomes Terryman's wife and mother of Terry the Kid, succeeds Yosaku as the primary human civilian perspective character. Where Yosaku was a child fan, Natsuko becomes an adult supporter/reporter figure. They never interact on-page; the relationship is one of narrative succession.

Yosaku→Yudetamago (creators)creator

Yosaku was created by Yudetamago (Shimada Takashi and Nakai Yoshinori) as part of the early gag-manga ensemble before the series found its wrestling identity. Like many early human characters, he was gradually phased out as the authors focused on Chojin lore.

Warning: Witness to Brutal Chojin Violence — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Witness to Brutal Chojin Violence

Source Kinnikuman Vol.3-5 (Ramenman vs Brockenman, Warsman vs Ramenman)

Description

Although Yosaku himself commits no violence, the stories he appears in transition into extreme brutality. Ramenman murders Brockenman in cold blood during the 20th Chojin Olympics (tearing him apart), Warsman stabs Ramenman in the temple leaving him vegetative, and later arcs feature Buffaloman splitting Meat into seven pieces. Yosaku as a child spectator is present in-universe for this tonal shift from slapstick to graphic wrestling gore, a notable dissonance for a children's manga.

Scholarly Context

Critics and retrospectives (e.g., Shamoon, 'The Superhero as Wrestler') note Kinnikuman's shift around 1980 as a deliberate move to capture the wrestling boom, with increased brutality that would be controversial today for a series still marketed to children. Yosaku's disappearance coincides with this shift, possibly to remove child witnesses from increasingly gory content.

Contradictions & Disputes

Is Yosaku a Chojin or Human?

Position A

Yosaku is a normal human child (civilian)

Position A — evidence

Early manga depicts him as small, powerless, living in Tokyo, with no Chojin Power, mask, or superhuman feat; official encyclopedias list him under 'humans' not 'Chojin'.

Position B

Misconception that all named Kinnikuman characters are Chojin

Position B — evidence

Because the series is known for Chojin, casual fans assume any named character is a Chojin; fan wikis sometimes auto-categorize him with Chojin template.

Scholarly Consensus

Consensus among primary sources: human. No evidence for Chojin status.

Continuity of Yosaku after 1980

Position A

Yosaku disappears after the early arcs and never returns

Position A — evidence

Systematic review of tankōbon Vol.6 onward, Toei anime episode guides, and Nisei cast lists show no named Yosaku appearance; character is absent from official Perfect Origin cast charts.

Position B

Some fan summaries claim he appears in later crowd scenes or Nisei

Position B — evidence

Anonymous crowd children in later stadium scenes are sometimes misidentified as Yosaku by fans eager to find cameos; no caption or credit confirms this.

Scholarly Consensus

Attested absence is the documented position; cameo claims are unverified and should be flagged as unknown.

Name Spelling and Identity

Position A

Canonical name is Yosaku (ヨサク), no surname

Position A — evidence

Japanese tankōbon furigana and Wikidata label use ヨサク / Yosaku.

Position B

Variant romanizations Yosaku-kun, Yosakku appear in fan translations

Position B — evidence

Fan scanlations add -kun honorific or double consonant for emphasis.

Scholarly Consensus

Yosaku is correct; honorific is not part of canonical name.
