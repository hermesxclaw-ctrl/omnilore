Etymology

The name 'Sho' is likely derived from the Tibetan word for 'dice' or 'game', though the exact etymology is uncertain. In Tibetan, 'sho' (ཤོ) can mean 'dice' or 'game of dice', and the board game is also known as 'miksha' (མིག་ཚད) meaning 'eye measurement'.

Cultural Ownership

Tibet, with historical and cultural significance in Tibetan Buddhist culture. The game is played in Tibet and among Tibetan diaspora communities.

Description

Appearance

Sho is a board game played on a wooden board with a grid of lines, typically 9x9 or 11x11. The pieces are small discs or stones, often made of wood or bone, with one side black and the other white. The board may have decorative carvings.

Symbols & Iconography

The board's grid may symbolize the cosmos or a battlefield, with the center sometimes marked as a special point. The pieces are plain, but the game's symbolism is tied to strategy and conflict.

Behaviour & Personality

The game is a two-player strategy game, similar to chess or checkers, involving capturing opponent's pieces. It is known for its depth and is often played in monasteries as a mental exercise.

Domains & Habitats

Sho is traditionally played in Tibet, in monasteries and homes. It is also played in Tibetan communities in exile, such as in India and Nepal.

Version Differences

There are variations of the game rules across regions, but the core mechanics remain similar. Some versions may use dice to determine movement, while others are purely strategic.
