The Approach

In the shadows of Mycenaean legend, a prince named Lysinomus stands among the forgotten sons of Electryon—a life cut short in a dynastic feud that echoes through the generations, ultimately paving the way for the greatest hero of Greek myth, Heracles.

Content advisory. Contains themes of violence, fratricide, and dynastic conflict as found in ancient Greek mythology.
Identity

Lysinomus

Pronunciation /laɪˈsɪnəməs/ (ly-SIN-ə-məs)

Etymology

The name Lysinomus (Λυσίνομος) may be derived from Greek λύσις (lysis, 'release') and νόμος (nomos, 'law' or 'custom'), possibly meaning 'releasing from law' or 'one who dissolves customs'. However, the etymology is uncertain and not explicitly explained in ancient sources.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically the Mycenaean heroic age as recorded in classical sources.

Disputed Identifications

There is no known dispute over the identification of Lysinomus. He is a minor figure, and no ancient author provides conflicting details.

Description

Appearance

No specific physical description of Lysinomus survives in ancient sources. As a Mycenaean prince, he would have been of noble bearing, likely depicted in art as a young warrior, but this is conjectural.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are associated with Lysinomus. He is not individually represented in surviving ancient art.

Behaviour & Personality

No direct characterization of Lysinomus exists. As one of Electryon's sons, he is part of a group of brothers who are collectively described as defending their father's kingdom. They are portrayed as brave but ultimately fall in battle.

Domains & Habitats

Mycenae, the legendary city ruled by his father Electryon. He is associated with the Argolid region in the Peloponnese.

Version Differences

There are no known variant versions of Lysinomus's story. His name appears only in Apollodorus's catalogue of Electryon's children.

As a Mycenaean prince, Lysinomus would have been trained in combat, but no specific feats are recorded. His death in battle against the Taphians implies he fought alongside his brothers.

Stories & Myths

The Death of Electryon's Sons

Source Pseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.5-6Culture Ancient GreekPeriod Heroic Age (Bronze Age), as recorded in classical literature

Summary

Lysinomus was one of the sons of Electryon, king of Mycenae, and Anaxo. When the sons of Pterelaus, king of the Taphians, came to claim the kingdom based on their descent from Mestor (Electryon's brother), a dispute arose. In the ensuing conflict, Lysinomus and all his brothers were killed.

Consequences

The death of his sons led Electryon to wage war against the Taphians. Before leaving, he entrusted his kingdom and daughter Alcmena to Amphitryon, who accidentally killed Electryon. This series of events ultimately led to the birth of Heracles, son of Zeus and Alcmena.

Variants

No significant variants of this episode are known. Other sources, such as Hesiod's Catalogue of Women, mention Electryon's children but do not list Lysinomus individually.

Mass killing of princes

Source Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.6

Description

Lysinomus and his brothers were all killed in a dynastic conflict. The ancient sources report this without moral commentary, reflecting the heroic ethos of the time.

Scholarly Context

This episode illustrates the theme of kin conflict and the fragility of royal lineages in Greek mythology.
