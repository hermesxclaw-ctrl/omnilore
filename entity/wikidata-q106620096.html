Who It Is

Ianthe, a name that glimmers through Greek mythology and literature, evokes the delicate beauty of a violet flower and the tender bonds of friendship and love. Yet behind this gentle epithet lies a tapestry of distinct figures—from Oceanid nymphs to tragic lovers—whose stories have been woven and rewoven across millennia, each thread colored by the era that spun it.

In Ovid, Ianthe is portrayed as gentle, loving, and loyal; in mythographic lists, no personality traits are given.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Greek ἴον (ion) 'violet' and ἄνθος (anthos) 'flower', meaning 'violet flower' or 'violet blossom'.

Pronunciation — eye-AN-thee (English), ee-AHN-thee (reconstructed Ancient Greek: /i.án.tʰɛː/)

Aliases — Ianthe (Greek: Ἰάνθη), Ianthe of Crete, Ianthe the Oceanid, Ianthe the Nereid (disputed)

Ownership — Greek mythology and classical literature; later adopted in Western art and English poetry.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Not described in extant sources; as a nymph, imagined as a beautiful young woman, possibly adorned with violets.

Symbols — Violet flower; in art, often depicted as a graceful maiden, sometimes with floral attributes.

Domains — As an Oceanid, dwells in the sea or freshwater; as a Cretan maiden, associated with the human world of Phaistos, Crete.

Archaic to Classical

Ianthe is named as one of the three thousand Oceanid nymphs, daughters of Oceanus and Tethys, but no individual story is told. Her inclusion establishes her as a minor divine figure.

Accounts II
Iphis and IantheOvid, Metamorphoses

Augustan

In Crete, Iphis is born a girl but raised as a boy by her mother, who hid the child's sex to avoid her husband's order to kill any female child. Iphis falls in love with Ianthe, and they are betrothed. Fearing the wedding, Iphis prays to the gods, and Isis transforms her into a man. The two marry and live happily. The story explains the origin of a ritual at Phaistos where a man and woman exchange clothes, and it celebrates same-sex love transformed into a heterosexual union.
