Who It Is

Luna is the moon-deity of Mithraic iconography, appearing as a female bust (or crescent and chariot) in the celestial arc that frames every tauroctony relief. She is paired with Sol — Sol in his quadriga (four-horse chariot) on the left ascending arc, Luna in her biga (two-horse chariot) on the right descending arc. Together they establish the cosmic frame within which the tauroctony occurs: the bull-slaying is a solar-lunar event that regulates the rhythm of the cosmos. Luna's crescent often ornaments the shoulders of Mithras or appears in the upper register between the zodiac signs. The Sol-Luna pairing mirrors the Cautes-Cautopates pairing that is reproduced at the level of the dadophoroi within the tauroctony — a fractal dualism that structures all of Mithraic cosmology.
