I am the light that pulls the tides and swells the womb. The sea knows my face, and the farmer plants by my phases.

Who It Is

Bulan is the moon deity in Bicolano mythology, distinct from the Tagalog Mayari. Bulan is described as a beautiful, gentle deity who controls the tides, the menstrual cycles of women, and the rhythm of sea-based agriculture. In the Bicolano epic Ibalon (the oldest surviving full narrative from pre-colonial Philippines), Bulan is the son of the supreme god Gugurang. He is associated with Mount Mayon — the volcano's perfect cone is said to be Bulan's eternal tribute to his lost love (in some versions) or his place of rest. The Bicolano people believed eclipses occurred when a giant sea serpent (Bakunawa) tried to swallow Bulan. During lunar eclipses, they would beat drums and gongs to scare the serpent away. Bulan was also the patron of fishermen, who prayed to him for calm seas and good catches. Offerings to Bulan included white chickens, coconut oil, and silver coins. The name Bulan simply means 'moon' in many Philippine languages, and the deity's gentle, protective nature contrasts with the more martial Mayari of the Tagalogs. In some versions, Bulan is female; in others, male — the moon's gender varies by region.
