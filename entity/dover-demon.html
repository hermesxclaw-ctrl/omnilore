Who It Is

The Dover Demon is one of cryptozoology's most unusual and briefly-seen creatures. Over two nights in April 1977, three independent teenage witnesses in the small town of Dover, Massachusetts reported seeing a strange humanoid creature. On the evening of April 21, 17-year-old Bill Bartlett was driving with friends when he spotted a figure on a stone wall by the roadside: about 3 to 4 feet tall, with a disproportionately large, watermelon-shaped head, no visible nose or mouth, and two large, glowing orange eyes. Its body was thin with spindly arms and legs ending in long, thin fingers that wrapped around the rocks. Bartlett described the skin as peach-colored and rough-textured, like shark skin. Hours later that same night, 15-year-old John Baxter saw a similar creature while walking home. The following night, 15-year-old Abby Brabham reported seeing an identical entity. All three gave remarkably consistent descriptions despite not knowing each other. The local police investigated but found no evidence. Cryptozoologist Loren Coleman coined the name 'Dover Demon' and documented the case extensively. Skeptical explanations include a foal (baby horse), a moose calf, or an owl, but none of these match the consistent descriptions of the large head, glowing orange eyes, and spindly limbs. The creature was never seen again, making it one of cryptozoology's most tantalizing one-off encounters. Its bizarre morphology -- neither clearly extraterrestrial, animal, nor folkloric -- has made it a unique entry in cryptid catalogs.
