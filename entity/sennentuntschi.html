Who It Is

Sennentuntschi is a chilling figure from Swiss Alpine folklore — an artificial woman created by lonely herdsmen (Sennen) during their long summer isolation on high mountain pastures. According to legend, a group of cowherds, starved for female company, built a life-sized female figure out of wood, straw, hay, and old clothes, and in their loneliness, began to treat her as if she were real. One herdsman even took her as his 'wife.' The situation crossed from pathetic to horrifying when the figure mysteriously came to life — either from dark magic, a pact with the devil, or sheer concentrated longing. The living Sennentuntschi was beautiful but ruthless, exacting revenge on her creators for their crude treatment and for violating the boundary between creation and cruelty. She would torment, drive mad, or kill the herdsmen who made her, one by one. The Sennentuntschi story is an Alpine variation of the Pygmalion myth — but with a dark, cautionary twist: what is created in loneliness and without love will bring destruction. The legend persists in Swiss cultural memory and inspired the 2016 German-Swiss horror film 'Sennentuntschi.'
