Who It Is

Sennentuntschi is a chilling figure from Swiss Alpine folklore — an artificial woman created by lonely herdsmen (Sennen) during their long summer isolation on high mountain pastures. According to legend, a group of cowherds, starved for female company, built a life-sized female figure out of wood, straw, hay, and old clothes, and in their loneliness, began to treat her as if she were real. One herdsman even took her as his 'wife.' The situation crossed from pathetic to horrifying when the figure mysteriously came to life — either from dark magic, a pact with the devil, or sheer concentrated longing. The living Sennentuntschi was beautiful but ruthless, exacting revenge on her creators for their crude treatment and for violating the boundary between creation and cruelty. She would torment, drive mad, or kill the herdsmen who made her, one by one. The Sennentuntschi story is an Alpine variation of the Pygmalion myth — but with a dark, cautionary twist: what is created in loneliness and without love will bring destruction. The legend persists in Swiss cultural memory and inspired the 2016 German-Swiss horror film 'Sennentuntschi.'

The Approach

In the remote alpine pastures of Switzerland, where the mountains swallow sound and the fog rolls in like a shroud, herders whispered of a creature born from loneliness and sin. They called her Sennentuntschi, the Alpine witch, a being conjured not by ancient gods but by the desperate hands of men who had been alone too long. Her story is a dark mirror of human isolation, guilt, and the terrifying power of belief.

Identity

Sennentuntschi

Etymology

The name is Swiss German, derived from 'Senner' (an alpine herdsman) and 'Tuntschi' (a doll or puppet). Thus, literally 'herdsman's doll'.

Cultural Ownership

Alpine regions of Switzerland, particularly the cantons of Appenzell and Graubünden. Also known in parts of Austria and South Tyrol.

Disputed Identifications

Some sources equate Sennentuntschi with other Alpine spirits like Perchta or the Golem, but these are distinct figures. The Sennentuntschi is a unique creation of Swiss Alpine folklore, often conflated with the generic 'Alp' (nightmare spirit).

Description

Appearance

Sennentuntschi is typically described as a life-sized female doll, often made of wood, straw, and old clothes. In some tales, she is animated and appears as a beautiful but eerie woman. Her appearance varies: sometimes she has a painted face, sometimes a blank one. She may appear disheveled or dressed in traditional Alpine attire.

Symbols & Iconography

The doll is often associated with alpine huts, lonely pastures, and the act of creation through desperation. In modern depictions, she is shown with a haunting, empty gaze, often in a rustic setting.

Behaviour & Personality

The Sennentuntschi is a product of her creation. In stories, she is initially innocent and childlike, but as she gains sentience, she becomes vengeful and cruel. She is driven by a desire for revenge against her creators for the abuse they inflict upon her. She is cunning, manipulative, and ultimately deadly.

Domains & Habitats

Alpine meadows, remote mountain huts, and high pastures where herdsmen spend months alone.

Version Differences

There are two main versions: In one, the doll is a harmless companion that later turns malevolent. In another, she is created as a joke, but then comes to life and punishes the herdsmen for their sins. Some versions have her as a demonic entity summoned by the herdsmen's depravity.

Stories & Myths

The Lonely Herdsmen's Creation

Source Oral traditionCulture Swiss AlpinePeriod Unknown, likely 18th-19th century

Summary

A group of herdsmen, isolated for months in a remote alpine hut, create a doll out of boredom and loneliness. They dress it in old clothes and give it a painted face. As time passes, they begin to treat it as a real woman, even sharing their meals and conversations. One night, the doll comes to life. At first, she is innocent and childlike, but the herdsmen, driven by lust and cruelty, abuse her. Enraged, she turns on them, driving them to madness and death, one by one.

Participants

Herdsmen

Sennentuntschi

Consequences

The herdsmen perish, and the doll disappears into the mountains.

Variants

In some versions, only one herdsman is involved, and he is the sole victim.

The Punishment for Sin

Source Oral traditionCulture Swiss AlpinePeriod Unknown

Summary

Herdsmen create a doll to mock a fellow herdsman who is particularly sinful. They place the doll in his bed as a prank. To their horror, the doll comes to life and begins to torment the sinful man, exposing his crimes and driving him to suicide. The doll then vanishes.

Participants

Sinful herdsman

Other herdsmen

Sennentuntschi

Consequences

The sinful man dies, and the others are left in terror.

Variants

In some versions, the doll kills all the herdsmen as punishment for their collective sins.
