Who It Is

El Coco (also Cuco, Coca, or Coconut Man) is the Iberian bogeyman — a shapeless, lurking terror used by parents for centuries to frighten children into obedience. The famous Spanish lullaby begins: 'Duérmete niño, duérmete ya, que viene el Coco y te comerá' (Sleep child, sleep now, or the Coco will come and eat you). The Coco's form is deliberately undefined — he is a head with a gaping mouth, or a shadow, or a figure carrying a sack for naughty children. This shapelessness is his power: he can be whatever the child most fears. The name 'Coco' derives from the Portuguese/Spanish word for 'coconut,' which, with its three dark holes on a brown sphere, resembles a grinning, monstrous head. Portuguese explorers brought coconut imagery back to Europe where the resemblance to a bogeyman's face was immediately noted — giving the coconut its name while also reinforcing the monster myth. The Coco migrated to Latin America with colonization, evolving into local variants like the Brazilian 'Cuca' (a crocodile-witch) while remaining a staple bogeyman across the Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking world. In the Iberian Peninsula, regional variants include the Catalan 'Home del Sac' (Man of the Sack) and the Galician 'Sacamanteigas' (Fat-Sucker). The Coco represents the most primal form of supernatural enforcement — the parent-deployed monster who enforces bedtime, obedience, and the boundaries of the home against the darkness outside.
