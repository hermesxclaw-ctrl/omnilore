Who It Is

The Lady of the Lake is a fascinating and complex cultural entity that has captivated audiences for centuries. Her magical powers, beautiful appearance, and deep connection to the natural world make her a compelling figure in the story of King Arthur.

The Lady of the Lake is often portrayed as a powerful, wise, and mysterious figure, with a deep connection to the natural world and the cycles of life and death.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Lady of the Lake' is derived from the medieval literature and mythology associated with the legend of King Arthur.

Pronunciation — Lay-dee of the Lake

Aliases — Nimue, Viviane, Ninienne, Keeper of Excalibur, Enchantress of Avalon

Ownership — The Lady of the Lake is a cultural entity owned by the collective unconscious of European folklore, particularly in the Matter of Britain.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Lady of the Lake is often depicted as a beautiful, anthropomorphic woman with magical powers, sometimes with a connection to the natural world.

Symbols — The Lady of the Lake is associated with the sword Excalibur, the island of Avalon, and the natural world, including lakes, forests, and mountains.

Domains — The Lady of the Lake inhabits the realm of the faeries, the natural world, and the spiritual realm.

The Lady of the Lake provides King Arthur with the sword Excalibur, which becomes a symbol of his power and authority. The sword Excalibur becomes a central symbol of King Arthur's power and authority.

Accounts II
The Lady of the Lake and MerlinThe Matter of Britain

Medieval

The Lady of the Lake eliminates the wizard Merlin, who had become a threat to King Arthur's power. Merlin's elimination allows King Arthur to consolidate his power and authority.
