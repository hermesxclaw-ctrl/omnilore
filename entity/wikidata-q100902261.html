The Approach

Minene Uryū is not the kind of terrorist who blows up buildings for a cause—she does it to spite God. A survivor of a Middle Eastern war that killed her parents, she turned her grief into a crusade against all religion, becoming the Ninth Diary Holder in a deadly battle royale. But when a police detective named Masumi Nishijima refuses to give up on her, she finds something she never expected: a reason to live. Her story is a brutal, tragic, and ultimately redemptive arc that cuts to the heart of Future Diary's themes of fate, love, and the cost of playing god.

Content advisory. Violence, terrorism, murder, suicide bombing, religious extremism, war trauma, child death, psychological trauma, and graphic injuries.
Identity

Minene Uryū

Pronunciation MEE-neh-neh OO-ryuu (Japanese: 雨流 みねね, Uryū Minene)

Known As

NinthNinth Diary HolderThe Terrorist

Etymology

The name 'Minene' (みねね) is a given name, while 'Uryū' (雨流) is a surname; the kanji for 'Uryū' literally mean 'rain flow'. The character is named after Minerva, the Roman goddess of wisdom, strategic warfare, and crafts.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese pop culture; created by Sakae Esuno for the manga series Future Diary (published by Kadokawa Shoten), later adapted into an anime by Asread.

Disputed Identifications

None known; the character is unique to the Future Diary franchise and not based on any pre-existing mythological figure beyond the naming convention.

Description

Appearance

Minene is a young woman with short, light-colored hair (blonde in the anime, light brown in the manga) and often wears a red hooded jacket, a white shirt, and black shorts. She has a distinctive scar over her left eye (which she later loses, leaving a closed eyelid). She is frequently seen carrying explosives and firearms. Her attire is practical for combat and reflects her rugged, anti-establishment lifestyle.

Symbols & Iconography

Her primary symbol is the number '9' (her diary holder number), often displayed on her clothing or equipment. She is associated with explosives and the concept of 'escape' (reflecting her diary's power). Her red hoodie can be seen as a symbol of her rebellious and dangerous nature.

Behaviour & Personality

Minene is fiercely independent, pragmatic, and ruthless when pursuing her goals. She is an atheist who harbors deep hatred for religion, particularly monotheistic beliefs, which she blames for her parents' death. Despite her violent exterior, she shows moments of vulnerability and compassion, especially toward children and those who remind her of her past. She initially views others as tools but gradually forms genuine bonds, particularly with Yukiteru Amano and Masumi Nishijima. She is highly resourceful, intelligent, and capable under pressure, but also impulsive and prone to emotional outbursts.

Domains & Habitats

Minene operates in urban environments, often hiding in abandoned buildings, rooftops, and other out-of-the-way locations. She is a fugitive and constantly on the move to avoid capture. In the Third World, she is shown living a peaceful suburban life with Nishijima and their children.

Version Differences

In the manga, Minene's backstory is more detailed, and her relationship with Nishijima is more developed. The anime condenses some of these elements but remains largely faithful. The side-story Mosaic provides additional context not present in the main series. Minor differences in dialogue and scene ordering exist between the two versions.

Powers & Abilities

Escape Diary (逃亡日記)

Source Version Main series (manga and anime), side-story Mosaic.

Description

A Future Diary that predicts the best course of action for Minene to escape from any dangerous situation. It manifests as a handheld device (a smartphone-like gadget) that displays step-by-step instructions for evading threats.

Limitations

The diary only provides escape routes if there is a feasible chance of escape; if no such route exists, it fails. It also cannot predict events that are not directly related to Minene's escape, making it less useful for offensive actions. Additionally, it does not account for the actions of other diary holders who can predict the future.

Stories & Myths

The Ninth Diary Holder's Introduction

Source Future Diary manga and anime, episode 2.Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod 2006-2012

Summary

Minene is introduced as a notorious terrorist who infiltrates Yuki's school to kill the Eighth Diary Holder, Kamado Ueshita. She sets off bombs and engages in a firefight with police. Yuki and Yuno intervene, and Minene is forced to flee. This encounter establishes her as a dangerous antagonist and sets the stage for her later alliance with Yuki.

Consequences

Minene's attack results in multiple casualties and brings the Diary Game to the attention of the authorities. It also leads to her first encounter with Masumi Nishijima, who becomes a recurring figure in her life.

Variants

The anime episode 2 closely follows the manga chapter 2, with minor differences in pacing and dialogue.

The Failed Assassination and Deus's Intervention

Source Future Diary: Mosaic and main series (flashback).Culture Japanese mangaPeriod 2006-2010

Summary

Minene attempts to assassinate a Catholic Cardinal in a cathedral. The assassination fails, and she is pursued by security forces. On the verge of being captured or killed, she falls from a high place. Deus ex Machina appears, offering to save her life if she participates in the Diary Game. She accepts, becoming the Ninth Diary Holder.

Participants

Consequences

Minene gains the Escape Diary and enters the Diary Game. Her hatred for religion intensifies, and she sees the game as an opportunity to destroy all religion if she wins.

Variants

The Mosaic side-story expands on this event, providing more detail than the brief flashback in the main series.

Alliance with Yukiteru and the Battle Against the Eleventh

Source Future Diary manga and anime, episodes 20-24.Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod 2011-2012

Summary

After the death of Masumi Nishijima, Minene is devastated but resolves to avenge him. She teams up with Yuki and his friends to invade the twin tower buildings where the Eleventh Diary Holder, John Bacchus, is hiding. During the assault, she discovers that Yuki had been using them as shields and had let Nishijima die. Enraged, she confronts Yuki, but he shoots her. Mortally wounded, she decides to help Yuki by sacrificing herself to blow open the vault where John is hiding. She detonates a bomb, but survives due to Deus having transferred half his power to her earlier. She then accompanies Yuki to the Third World to stop Yuno, and survives the final battle.

Minene's actions are crucial in defeating the Eleventh. She loses her left eye and nearly dies, but ultimately survives. Her relationship with Yuki is strained but eventually repaired. She finds happiness in the Third World with Nishijima.

Source Future Diary manga and anime, final episode.Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod 2011-2012

In the Third World, Minene lives with the Third World's Masumi Nishijima, and they have children who inherit her godly powers (including the ability to fly). She remains in contact with Deus and Murumuru. This ending shows her finding peace and happiness after a life of violence and loss.

Parents' death in war

Source Future Diary: Mosaic, main series flashbacks.

Description

As a child, Minene and her parents were caught in a conflict in the Middle East (hinted to be the Israeli-Palestinian conflict). Her parents were killed in the crossfire. This event is the catalyst for her hatred of God and religion, leading her to become a terrorist.

Scholarly Context

The depiction of war trauma and its psychological effects is a common theme in Japanese media, often explored through characters with tragic backstories.

Terrorist activities

Source Main series, Mosaic.

Description

Minene engages in bombings, assassinations, and other violent acts against religious institutions and figures. She shows no remorse for her actions, viewing them as a necessary crusade against God. This includes the bombing of a school and the assassination attempt on a cardinal.

Scholarly Context

The character embodies a critique of religious extremism, but also serves as a cautionary tale about the cycle of violence.

Suicide bombing

Source Main series, manga and anime.

Description

In a desperate act to avenge Nishijima and defeat the Eleventh, Minene attempts to blow herself up. She survives due to Deus's intervention, but the act demonstrates her willingness to sacrifice herself.

Scholarly Context

Suicide bombing is a real-world phenomenon, and its portrayal in fiction often raises ethical questions. In this context, it is a narrative device to show Minene's commitment and desperation.

Loss of eye

Source Main series, manga and anime.

Description

During a confrontation with Yuki, he shoots her in the left eye, blinding it. This injury is a permanent reminder of her violent life.

Scholarly Context

Physical disfigurement is often used in fiction to symbolize a character's suffering and transformation.
