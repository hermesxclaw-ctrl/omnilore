Who It Is

He was the champion of champions, a titan of Persian legend whose mace shattered armies and whose name still thunders across the Iranian world. But for all his might, Rostam's fate was woven with tragedy: he unknowingly slew his own son Sohrab, and was himself murdered by the treachery of a jealous half-brother. His story, told in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh, is a saga of heroism, loyalty, and the cruel ironies of destiny.

Rostam is characterized by his immense strength, courage, and loyalty to the Kayanian kings. He is also proud and sometimes overconfident. He is a king-maker and a faithful general, yet his tragic fate involves unintended filicide and betrayal.

Powers
Superhuman Strength

Rostam possesses immense physical strength, capable of slaying a mad elephant with a single blow and defeating entire armies.

Limit: Despite his strength, he is vulnerable to treachery and magic.

Master Warrior

Trained in warfare by his father Zāl, Rostam is an unparalleled fighter on horseback and with various weapons, especially his mace.

Limit: None specified.

Divine Protection

Rostam's horse Rakhsh has an incredibly long life due to divine protection, and Rostam himself lives for over 600 years.

Limit: This protection does not prevent his death by treachery.

Leadership and Kingship

Rostam is a faithful military general and king-maker for the Kayanian dynasty, commanding armies and shaping the fate of the kingdom.

Limit: He is not a king himself but serves the throne.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Rostam is depicted as a colossal, superhumanly strong hero. He wears the zīn-i palang, a panther-skin garment, and carries a mace. He rides the legendary stallion Rakhsh.

Symbols — Panther-skin garment (zīn-i palang), mace, and his horse Rakhsh. In art, he is often shown wearing a distinctive helmet and armor.

Domains — He is a native of Zabulistan (historical region in southern Afghanistan), and his mother was from Kabul. He serves as Marzban (frontier guardian) of Sistan. He travels across Iran and beyond, including Mazandaran (a mythical land, not the modern province).

Accounts I
Birth and Early LifeShahnameh

Mythical antiquity

Rostam is born to Zāl and Rudaba after a difficult labor, saved by the Simurgh's instruction on a Caesarean section (Rostamzad). As a child, he slays a mad elephant and conquers the fortress of Mt. Sipand. Rostam's early feats establish his reputation as a hero and secure his position as a warrior.

The Seven Labours (Haft Khan)Shahnameh

Mythical antiquity

Rostam undertakes a perilous journey to rescue King Kay Kāvus, who has been captured by the Divs of Mazandaran. He faces seven trials, including battling a lion, a dragon, and the White Demon (Div-e Sepid). Rostam rescues the king and defeats the demons, showcasing his prowess.

Accounts II
Rostam and SohrabShahnameh

Mythical antiquity

Rostam unknowingly fights and kills his son Sohrab, whom he had with Tahmineh, princess of Samangan. The tragedy is a central episode in the epic. Rostam is devastated by the loss, and the story highlights themes of fate and tragedy.

Rostam and EsfandiyārShahnameh

Mythical antiquity

Rostam fights the invincible Esfandiyār, who has a prophecy that he can only be killed by a special arrow. Rostam, guided by the Simurgh, uses a tamarisk arrow to strike Esfandiyār's eye, killing him. Esfandiyār's death leads to further conflict and consequences for the kingdom.

Death of RostamShahnameh

Mythical antiquity

Rostam is killed by his jealous half-brother Shaghad, who digs a pit filled with poisoned spears. Rostam, Rakhsh, and his brother Zavareh fall into the trap. Rostam, even in death, manages to kill Shaghad. Rostam's death marks the end of an era.
