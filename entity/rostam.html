Who It Is

Rostam is the greatest hero of Persian mythology, the central figure of Ferdowsi's Shahnameh — a semi-divine warrior of superhuman strength and courage who defends the kingdom of Iran for over 600 years. The son of the sage Zal and the princess Rudaba (whose labor required a Caesarean section performed by the Simurgh), Rostam was born with the strength of an adult. His most famous feats include the Seven Trials (Haft Khan-e Rostam) — a series of increasingly difficult battles against monsters, sorcerers, and demons to rescue a trapped king — and his epic battles against the Turanian champion Sohrab, not knowing that Sohrab is his own son. The tragedy of Rostam and Sohrab is one of the most heartbreaking passages in world literature: Rostam kills Sohrab in battle, only to realize from the boy's armband that he is his own son, fathered years before and now unknowingly slain. Rostam also defeats the White Demon (Div-e Sepid), the demon king of Mazandaran, and the dragon-tyrant Zahhak is chained before his time. Rostam carries the mace of the bull-headed Gop, his horse Rakhsh (the legendary red stallion), and the Simurgh's feather for aid. He represents the Persian ideal of javānmardi (chivalry, manly virtue) — the warrior who serves the king, protects the land, and upholds the cosmic order, even as his own fate is one of tragic sorrow.
