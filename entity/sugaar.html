Who It Is

In the storm-tossed highlands of the Basque Country, a serpent of fire and thunder slithers through the sky, summoned by the ancient goddess Mari to forge the tempests that shape the land. This is Sugaar, the male half of a pre-Christian divine pair, a figure whose very name whispers of his serpentine nature and whose legends, though scarce, hint at a powerful and enigmatic presence.

Sugaar's primary role is to periodically join with Mari in the mountains to generate storms. He is also said to punish disobedient children. In Betelu, he is considered a demon.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Suga(a)r is derived from Basque 'suge' (serpent) and '-ar' (male), thus 'male serpent'. The alternative suggestion of 'su' (fire) and 'gar' (flame) is considered folk etymology. 'Sugoi' may be 'suge' + 'o[h]i' (old serpent) or 'su' + 'goi' (high fire). The etymology of 'Maju' is unknown.

Pronunciation — soo-GAH-ar (approximate)

Aliases — Sugar, Sugoi, Suarra, Maju

Ownership — Basque

Form & Signs

Appearance — Sugaar is normally imagined as a dragon or serpent. He is also described as appearing as a fire-sickle or fireball when crossing the sky.

Symbols — Fire, sickle, serpent/dragon, storms, thunder.

Domains — In Ataun, he has two homes: the caves of Amunda and Atarreta. He travels through the sky, between mountains such as Balerdi and Elortalde in Betelu. He meets Mari on Fridays (the day of the akelarre or sabbat) in Azkoitia.
