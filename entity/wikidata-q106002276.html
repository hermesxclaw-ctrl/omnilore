The Approach

In the spring of 1985, on a small family farm in Hokkaido, a foal was born who couldn't stand. His leg was bowed, his mother's milk was scarce, and he survived on weeds. They named him 'Hatsuratsu'—'lively'—a hopeful name for a horse that seemed destined for obscurity. Five years later, that same horse would be the most beloved idol in Japanese horse racing, a gray-haired monster from the countryside who changed the sport forever.

Etymology

Oguri Cap (オグリキャップ) is a Japanese name. 'Oguri' is likely a name component, while 'Cap' may be a reference to his sire Dancing Cap. His early name 'Hatsuratsu' (活発) means 'lively' or 'vigorous'.

Cultural Ownership

Japan (Japanese horse racing)

Disputed Identifications

None noted.

Description

Appearance

Grey stallion. As a foal, he was thin and unappealing due to malnutrition and a bowed front left leg. He grew into a healthy, fully capable horse by his second year.

Symbols & Iconography

Statues at Yushun Memorial Park and Kasamatsu Racecourse; his mane is displayed at Kasamatsu Racecourse.

Behaviour & Personality

Massive appetite, known for eating weeds as a foal. In popular culture, depicted as introverted and simple-minded with a large appetite.

Domains & Habitats

Hokkaido, Japan (birth and stud); raced at Kasamatsu Racecourse and national tracks.

Version Differences

Real-life horse vs. anthropomorphized character in Umamusume: Pretty Derby.

Powers & Abilities

Racing ability

Source Version Real life

Description

Won 22 of 32 races, including 4 Grade I victories. Notable wins: 1989 Mile Championship, two Arima Kinen (1988, 1990), 1990 Yasuda Kinen.

Limitations

Missed Japanese Triple Crown due to late registration.

From Countryside to National Fame

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1985-1991

Summary

Born with a bowed leg and malnutrition, Oguri Cap overcame his humble beginnings to become a national racing star. His late entry into national racing sparked a rule change in the JRA.
