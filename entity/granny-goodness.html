Who It Is

In the fiery crucibles of Apokolips, where hope is a crime and cruelty is a craft, there is no figure more terrifying—or more maternal in her monstrosity—than Granny Goodness. She is the orphanage matron who turns children into weapons, the smiling grandmother whose lullabies are screams, and the sadistic architect of some of the DC Universe's most broken warriors. To understand the New Gods is to understand Granny, for she is the dark mother who forges the future of Apokolips in the fires of abuse.

Granny Goodness is sadistic, manipulative, and utterly devoted to Darkseid. She derives pleasure from breaking the will of others, especially children, and molding them into loyal soldiers. She is cunning, patient, and enjoys psychological torture as much as physical. Despite her cruelty, she maintains a facade of grandmotherly warmth, which she uses to disarm her victims. She is fiercely loyal to Darkseid but also self-serving, often seeking to increase her own power and influence within Apokolips.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Granny Goodness' is an ironic inversion of the archetypal 'good grandmother' figure, highlighting her deceptive and cruel nature. 'Goodness' is a direct contrast to her malevolent actions, and 'Granny' evokes a false sense of warmth and care.

Pronunciation — GRAN-ee GOOD-ness

Aliases — Goodness, Granny, The Goodness, G-Goodness, The Granny

Ownership — DC Comics (Warner Bros. Discovery), created by Jack Kirby

Powers
New God Physiology

As a New God, Granny Goodness possesses superhuman strength, durability, and longevity. She is virtually immortal and can survive in the vacuum of space. Her physical abilities are far beyond those of ordinary humans, though she is not as powerful as Darkseid or other top-tier New Gods.

Limit: She is not invulnerable and can be injured by sufficiently powerful attacks.

Master Manipulator and Psychological Torturer

Granny Goodness is a genius at psychological manipulation, able to break the strongest wills and recondition individuals to serve Darkseid. She uses a combination of physical abuse, psychological conditioning, and technological means.

Limit: Her methods can fail on individuals with exceptionally strong willpower or outside help.

Combat Expertise

Despite her aged appearance, Granny Goodness is a formidable hand-to-hand combatant, trained in various martial arts and skilled in using her staff or cane as a weapon.

Limit: She prefers to avoid direct confrontation, relying on her Furies or technology.

Technological Access

Granny Goodness has access to Apokoliptian technology, including boom tubes for teleportation, and various devices for mind control and torture.

Limit: Her access is subject to Darkseid's approval.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Granny Goodness is typically depicted as an elderly woman with a hunched posture, gray hair pulled into a bun, and a wrinkled face. She wears a high-collared, long-sleeved dress often in shades of purple or green, with a cape and a distinctive headpiece that resembles a widow's peak or a crown. Symbols — Her symbol is often associated with the Anti-Life Equation, representing her devotion to Darkseid. She is frequently depicted with a staff or a cane, which she uses as both a support and a weapon. Her orphanage, the Granny Goodness Orphanage, is a recurring location symbolizing her domain of child abuse and indoctrination.

Domains — Granny Goodness primarily operates on Apokolips, where she runs the Granny Goodness Orphanage, a facility for training future Female Furies and other soldiers. She also has access to Darkseid's stronghold, Armagetto, and other military installations. She has been known to travel to Earth and other planets to retrieve or manipulate individuals, especially those with metahuman potential.

Accounts I
The Origin of the Female FuriesThe New Gods (vol. 1) #7 (1972)

1970s

Granny Goodness is introduced as the head of an orphanage on Apokolips where she trains young girls to become the Female Furies, a team of elite warriors serving Darkseid. She selects the most promising and subjects them to brutal conditioning, turning them into ruthless killers. The story follows the creation of the original Furies, including Big Barda, who eventually rebels against Granny. Big Barda's rebellion leads to her escape to Earth and her marriage to Mister Miracle, setting up a recurring conflict between Granny and her former pupil.

The Hunger DogsJack Kirby's The Hunger Dogs (1985)

1980s

In this graphic novel, Granny Goodness plays a role in Darkseid's plans to conquer Earth, using her orphanage to create a new generation of warriors. She is shown as a loyal but scheming lieutenant, and her methods are depicted as even more brutal than before. The story ends with a temporary defeat for Darkseid, but Granny escapes punishment.

Accounts II
Seven Soldiers: Mister MiracleSeven Soldiers: Mister Miracle #1-4 (2005-2006)

2000s

In this modern reinterpretation by Grant Morrison, Granny Goodness is reimagined as a sinister force operating on Earth, running a cult-like organization. She captures Shilo Norman, the new Mister Miracle, and subjects him to psychological torment, using her 'goodness' as a weapon to break his spirit. Shilo eventually escapes and defeats Granny, but the experience leaves lasting trauma.

Justice League Unlimited: 'The Ties That Bind'Justice League Unlimited (2004-2006)

2000s

In this episode, Granny Goodness is shown as the head of the orphanage where Big Barda was raised. She attempts to capture Barda and Mister Miracle, but they are rescued by the Justice League. The episode highlights her cruel methods and her vendetta against Barda. Granny is defeated and forced to retreat, but she vows to return.
