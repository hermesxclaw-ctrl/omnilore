Who It Is

On Dead Bird Studio's burning soundstage, a short, shouting owl in a striped conductor's uniform slams his baton, derails his own train, and declares war on a penguin in a tuxedo — all while a child in a top hat tries to steal his hourglasses. The Conductor is not a gentle maestro. He is Gears for Breakfast's portrait of obsessive, old-school showmanship turned feral, and the reason Chapter 3 of A Hat in Time feels like a Looney Tunes feud directed by Wes Anderson.

Attested personality: hot-tempered, fiercely competitive, nostalgic, and theatrical. He speaks in loud, clipped, often shouting delivery, with a working-class, slightly Brooklyn/NYC-inflected accent. He is obsessed with winning the Annual Bird Movie Award against DJ Grooves, to the point of sabotage and self-sabotage. He values practical stunts, real trains, real owls, and 'old-fashioned' filmmaking over Grooves' CGI and penguin glamour. Powers
Showmanship and Directorial Authority

Controls Dead Bird Studio's sets, crew of owls, and the Owl Express. Can order set changes, cue stunts, and command owl subordinates. Authority is social/institutional, not supernatural.

Limit: Authority is limited to his studio; owls will disobey if scared or bribed by Grooves. He cannot control Hat Kid or DJ Grooves' penguins.

Baton Combat and Train Operation

Wields baton as melee weapon in boss-adjacent sequences; operates and can overdrive the Owl Express steam engine to dangerous speeds (Train Rush act).

Limit: No superhuman strength; baton does minimal damage, train can derail if misused. Hat Kid can outrun and outmaneuver him.

Cinematic Trap-Setting

Designs elaborate film-set traps: collapsing scenery, rolling boulders, timed flamethrowers, and murder-mystery scenarios (Murder on the Owl Express).

Limit: Traps are clearly artificial and telegraphed for platforming; they require crew setup and can malfunction.

Resilience (Cartoon Logic)

Survives being hit by trains, falling props, and explosions with only comical injury, consistent with game's slapstick physics.

Limit: Not invulnerability in lore; played for comedy. No canonical explanation.

Accounts I
Train RushA Hat in Time - Chapter 3, Act 1

2017

Hat Kid arrives at Dead Bird Studio seeking Time Pieces. The Conductor and DJ Grooves are feuding over who will win the Annual Bird Movie Award. The Conductor has hijacked the Owl Express and is racing it recklessly around the studio to film a practical stunt. Hat Kid must platform across the speeding train cars, dodging obstacles the Conductor has placed, to reach the engine and claim the Time Piece. The Conductor shouts directions and threats over loudspeakers throughout. Introduces the central rivalry and establishes The Conductor's preference for practical effects. Hat Kid obtains one Time Piece. The train is damaged, setting up later acts.

Dead Bird Studio (Film Set Duel)A Hat in Time - Chapter 3, Act 2

2017

The studio lot becomes a competitive film shoot. The Conductor and DJ Grooves each demand Hat Kid's help filming their movie. The player can choose to help either director by collecting props and performing scenes. If helping The Conductor, Hat Kid films gritty western/train heist scenes with owl actors, while The Conductor directs loudly with baton and megaphone, yelling at penguin saboteurs. The act ends with a joint screening where the winner is decided by player choice. Player's choice determines which director gives Hat Kid the Time Piece and which cutscene plays. Establishes branching narrative; both directors get alternate victory/defeat animations. No canonical winner is declared by developers.

Murder on the Owl ExpressA Hat in Time - Chapter 3, Act 3

2017

A murder-mystery film shoot turns into a whodunit. The Conductor stages a murder on the Owl Express with Hat Kid as detective. Passengers (owls and penguins) are suspects. The Conductor plays the victim/director, found 'murdered' in his cabin, while Hat Kid interrogates crows, owls, and DJ Grooves. The solution reveals the murder was staged for the film, but the Conductor's temper leads to real chaos when the train's brakes fail. The act parodies Murder on the Orient Express. Awards a Time Piece upon solving the mystery. Deepens lore of Owl Express as a luxury liner and of Conductor's love of classic cinema. Frequently cited by reviewers as the game's best level.

Accounts II
Picture PerfectA Hat in Time - Chapter 3, Act 4

2017

The Conductor needs publicity photos. Hat Kid is tasked with taking photos of the studio's birds for his poster, while DJ Grooves wants glamour shots of penguins. The Conductor's version involves wrangling uncooperative owls who hide, sleep, or attack. He shouts photo directions and complains about Grooves' photoshopped penguins. The act is non-violent and comedic. Grants a Time Piece. Shows softer, more exasperated side of Conductor as he deals with talent. No major plot consequences.

The Big ParadeA Hat in Time - Chapter 3, Finale Act

2017

The Annual Bird Movie Award parade. Both directors have built massive parade floats that march through Dead Bird Studio. The Conductor's float is a giant Owl Express engine spewing confetti and practical effects; Grooves' is a disco penguin palace. Hat Kid must infiltrate both floats, disabling traps, while the directors hurl insults over PA systems. The Conductor, in a rare moment, admits he just wants his films to be remembered. The finale ends with Hat Kid awarding the Time Piece to one director, while the other throws a tantrum. Concludes Chapter 3 arc. No canonical winner; game explicitly leaves rivalry unresolved for replay. Unlocks Time Rift levels. The Conductor's float remains in the hub as scenery afterward.

The Conductor's Cameo Legacy (Post-Studio References)A Hat in Time - Seal the Deal DLC, Nyakuza Metro DLC, and official comics/promo art

2018-2019

After Chapter 3, The Conductor does not appear physically in new levels but is referenced via posters in the Arctic Cruise, photos in Hat Kid's spaceship, and in Gears for Breakfast's 2018 Halloween comic where he and Grooves cameo as judges. Developers have stated in interviews they intentionally left his story open for potential sequel appearances. Maintains character presence without new story; fuels fan speculation about sequel. No new Time Pieces associated.
