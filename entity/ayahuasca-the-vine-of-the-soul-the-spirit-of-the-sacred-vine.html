Who It Is

Ayahuasca is the living spirit of the sacred psychoactive vine Banisteriopsis caapi — the center of Amazonian shamanic medicine and the most important plant-teacher (vegetalista tradition) in the western Amazon. In the cosmology of the Shipibo-Conibo, Ashaninka, Shuar, and dozens of other Amazonian peoples, Ayahuasca is a female spirit — a wise, ancient, and often terrifying presence who is the mother of all healing plants. She appears to the shaman in visions either as a serpentine woman (half-woman, half-vine, whose body coils like the Banisteriopsis stem) or as a jaguar. The ayahuasca brew (which combines the MAOI-containing vine with the DMT-rich leaves of Psychotria viridis or Diplopterys cabrerana) is not merely a drug but a 'person' — a sentient being who calls specific individuals to become shamans, who teaches the icaros (healing songs), who shows the origin of illness and the location of lost objects or souls. To call her 'Ayahuasca' or 'Mother Ayahuasca' is to acknowledge her as a teacher, not a tool. The visions she grants are considered 'real' — a journey into the 'true world' behind the veil of ordinary perception, where jaguars can speak and where the geometric patterns of Shipibo design (kené) are the foundational structure of the universe.
