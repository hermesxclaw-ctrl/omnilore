Who It Is

The Simurgh (also Simorgh, Simurg) is a magnificent mythical bird in Persian mythology, a wise and benevolent creature of immense size and age who nests in the Tree of All Seeds at the center of the world or on the peaks of Mount Qaf (the cosmic mountain at the edge of the world). She has the body of a peacock, the head of a dog, and the claws of a lion — a composite creature symbolizing her mastery over the animal kingdom. The Simurgh is said to possess all knowledge and is the nurse of creation: in Zoroastrian tradition, she protects the sacred tree and, when she flies, the seeds of all plants scatter across the earth. She is the patron of heroes: in Ferdowsi's Shahnameh (the Persian Book of Kings), the Simurgh raises the abandoned hero Zal, teaches him wisdom, and grants him a feather to summon her in times of great need — a feather that saves Zal's son Rostam in his most desperate battles. In Attar's Conference of the Birds, the Simurgh is the ultimate goal of the birds' quest — the king they seek — and they discover that the Simurgh is themselves (a pun on 'si murgh' meaning 'thirty birds'). The Simurgh represents the highest wisdom, the mother of all knowledge, and the unity of the seeker with the sought. She is a symbol of Iran itself — ancient, wise, and eternal.

Attested in Avesta (as Saēna, 'the bird'); Shahnameh (Ferdowsi); Attar's Conference of the Birds; Persian miniaturesEra Avestan period (c. 1500–1000 BCE) through Islamic Persian literature
