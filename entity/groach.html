Groac'h

The Water Witch, Lake Hag, Sorceress of the Depths

I am the old woman of the lake. To your eyes, I am beauty; to the water, I am bone and weed. My jars hold the souls of those who came too close — come closer, and yours will join them.

Who It Is

The Groac'h (also Groah, Groac'had, literally 'old woman' in Breton) is a malevolent water witch who dwells in lakes, ponds, and marshes across Brittany. She appears as a hideous old crone with long, lank hair, webbed hands and feet, greenish skin, and fish-like eyes — but she can also transform into a beautiful young woman to lure victims. The Groac'h drowns travellers, particularly young men, dragging them into her underwater lair where she keeps their souls in sealed jars or cages. She also steals children who wander too close to the water. The Groac'h is associated with storms, floods, and the dangerous deep pools that dot the Breton landscape. Her power can be countered by Christian symbols (crosses, holy water), iron, and by invoking the name of a Breton saint, particularly Saint Anne (patron saint of Brittany) or Saint Yves. In some tales, a brave young man defeats the Groac'h by tricking her — offering to comb her hair (a task she cannot refuse because of her vanity about her long, snake-like locks), during which he binds her with her own hair and cuts off her head. The Groac'h's severed head continues to prophesy and curse. The Groac'h represents the dangerous waters of the Breton interior — the stagnant ponds, the treacherous marshes, the deep lakes — in a region that is as much water as land. She is related to the broader European 'lake hag' type (cf. the Slavic Baba Yaga and the Scottish Cailleach Bheur), but localized to the specific watery landscape of inland Brittany with its thousands of ponds and meres.
