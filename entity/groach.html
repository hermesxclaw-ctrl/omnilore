Groac'h

The Water Witch, Lake Hag, Sorceress of the Depths

also known asGroac'h of the Isle of LokWater WitchLake HagSorceress of the DepthsGroahGroach
TypeWitch / Water Spirit
CultureBreton
Who It Is
Form

The Groac'h is typically described as an old woman, often with a haggard and withered appearance, resembling a witch or an ogress. She is sometimes depicted with walrus teeth, a distinctive feature that sets her apart from other fairy figures. In some accounts, she appears as a beautiful young woman to lure victims, but her true form is revealed as an aged crone. She is often seen by night, near bodies of water, and may be accompanied by a faint glow or mist. Her clothing is usually ragged and dark, blending with the shadows of her cavernous dwellings. In Souvestre's tale, she is described as having long, tangled hair and piercing eyes that reflect her malevolent nature. The Groac'h's appearance is mutable, as she can shape-shift into various forms, including animals or inanimate objects, to deceive humans.

Powers

Shape-shifting The Groac'h can change her form at will, transforming into a beautiful woman, an animal, or even an inanimate object. This ability is used to deceive and lure victims, as she often appears as a damsel in distress to attract men.

Transformation of humans She has the power to turn humans into fish, as depicted in Souvestre's tale. This transformation is permanent unless reversed by a specific counter-spell or the destruction of the Groac'h.

Control over nature The Groac'h has dominion over natural forces, particularly water and weather. She can summon storms, create whirlpools, or calm the sea to aid or hinder sailors and fishermen.

Illusion and glamour She can create illusions to make her cavern appear as a luxurious palace or to hide her true appearance. This power is often used to entrap victims or to test visitors.

Immortality and longevity As a fairy, the Groac'h is ageless and immortal, though she can be killed by specific means, such as being struck with an iron object or having her magical net destroyed.

Accounts

A young fisherman named Houarn Pogamm and his cousin Bellah are in love. To seek their fortune, Houarn sets out, and Bellah gives him a magic bell and a knife for protection. He arrives at the Isle of Lok, where he encounters the Groac'h, who appears as a beautiful woman. She invites him to her underwater palace, where he is seduced and then transformed into a fish. Bellah, worried, uses the magic bell to locate him and sets out to rescue him. She finds the Groac'h's cavern and, using the knife, kills the Groac'h. The spell is broken, and all the transformed men are restored, including Houarn. Bellah and Houarn marry and live happily, using the Groac'h's treasure to enrich their village.

A poor fisherman helps an old woman who is actually a Groac'h in disguise. She rewards him by granting him abundant catches and a bag of gold, but warns him not to be greedy. The fisherman initially prospers but becomes greedy and demands more, leading the Groac'h to curse him, causing his boat to sink. He survives but loses everything, learning a lesson about contentment.

A Groac'h terrorizes a coastal village, drowning fishermen and stealing children. Saint Gildas, a Breton saint, confronts her. He uses his staff to part the sea, exposing her cavern. He sprinkles holy water, which burns her skin, and she is forced to flee. The saint then places a stone on the entrance to her cavern, sealing her forever. The village is saved, and the Groac'h is never seen again.

Scholarly Debates
Nature of the Groac'h
The Groac'h is inherently malevolent, as depicted in Souvestre's tale, where she kills and eats men. ⇌ Some oral traditions present the Groac'h as a neutral or even benevolent figure who rewards visitors with gifts, suggesting a more ambiguous morality.
Appearance
The Groac'h is always an old hag with walrus teeth, as described in many sources. ⇌ In some accounts, she appears as a beautiful young woman, using her allure to deceive, indicating that her appearance is not fixed.
Habitat
The Groac'h lives in underwater caverns beneath the sea or lakes. ⇌ Some tales place her in caves under beaches or even in freshwater springs, suggesting a broader range of habitats.
Relationship with humans
The Groac'h is hostile to humans, actively seeking to harm them. ⇌ Other stories show her as a solitary fairy who avoids humans unless provoked, and may even help those who show her kindness.
What We Don't Know

There is limited primary documentation of the Groac'h in pre-19th-century sources, as most knowledge comes from literary retellings by Souvestre and later folklorists. The oral traditions that may have existed are largely unrecorded, and the extent to which Souvestre embellished or invented the character is unknown. Additionally, the regional variations within Brittany are not well-documented, and the Groac'h's relationship to other Celtic water spirits remains under-researched. Further study could explore the Groac'h's role in Breton identity and her representation in modern media.
