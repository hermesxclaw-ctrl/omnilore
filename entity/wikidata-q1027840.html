The Approach

In the sprawling saga of the Highlander franchise, most Immortals are defined by their swordsmanship, their centuries of life, or their battles for the Prize. But Graham Ashe—a figure whose name surfaces only in the shadows of the franchise's expanded lore—represents something rarer: an Immortal whose story is told not through epic duels, but through the quiet, deadly work of assassination. His existence raises unsettling questions about the nature of Immortality itself, and his methods challenge the very code that binds the Quickening.

Content advisory. Contains themes of assassination, violence, and death, consistent with the Highlander franchise's action-fantasy genre.

Etymology

The name 'Graham' is of Scottish origin, meaning 'gravel homestead' or 'grey home'. 'Ashe' likely derives from the Old English 'æsc', meaning 'ash tree'. The combination may suggest a connection to Scotland or England, consistent with the franchise's Celtic roots.

Stories & Myths

The Valkyrie

Source Highlander: The Series, Season 5, Episode 12, 'The Valkyrie'Culture Western (franchise)Period 1997 (airdate); in-story, contemporary

Summary

In this episode, Graham Ashe is hired to assassinate a woman named Cassandra, who is an Immortal. He tracks her and attempts to kill her, but she escapes. Duncan MacLeod intervenes, protecting Cassandra. Ashe later confronts MacLeod, revealing that he is an Immortal himself. A duel ensues, and MacLeod ultimately beheads Ashe, taking his Quickening. Ashe's motivations are revealed to be purely professional: he was paid to eliminate Cassandra, and he had no personal vendetta against MacLeod.

Ashe's death results in his Quickening being absorbed by MacLeod. The episode reinforces MacLeod's role as a protector of the innocent and highlights the moral complexities of Immortal life.
