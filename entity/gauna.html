Who It Is

Gauna (also //Gauwa or !Gauwa) is the San deity of death, sickness, and darkness — Kaggen's opposite and adversary. While Kaggen creates and sustains life, Gauna consumes it. He is described as a shadowy figure who lurks in the dark places of the bush, sending disease, misfortune, and death to humans. The San believe that illness is caused by Gauna's invisible arrows, which strike the body and can only be removed by a trance healer (n/om k'xao) who battles Gauna's influence in the spirit realm during the healing dance. Unlike Kaggen, who is identified with the light and life of day, Gauna is associated with night, the cold, and the grave. He is sometimes conflated with the spirits of the angry dead who have not been properly buried or remembered. In San cosmology, the relationship between Kaggen and Gauna is not a simple good-versus-evil duality: Gauna's existence is accepted as a necessary part of the cosmic order — death is what gives life its urgency and meaning. Still, he is actively resisted through ritual, dance, and the community's collective spiritual energy. The ethnographic records of Wilhelm Bleek's interviews with the Xam man //Kabbo provide the most detailed surviving account of Gauna's mythology, preserved from a culture that faced near-extermination during the colonial era.

Form

Gauna is not described with a fixed physical form in the ǀXam narratives. He is a shape-shifter and a disembodied presence, often associated with darkness, the bush at night, and the wind. He can manifest as a whirlwind, a shadow, or a monstrous figure. In some stories, he appears as a giant or a being with animalistic features, but his essence is more about his terrifying presence than a fixed appearance.

Powers

Death Manipulation Gauna is the primary agent of death in ǀXam mythology. He kills people, often by shooting them with invisible arrows or by stealing their hearts. He is the lord of the underworld, where the souls of the dead reside. Attested in multiple ǀXam narratives from the Bleek-Lloyd collection. Gauna is not omnipotent. He can be tricked, fought, and even defeated by clever heroes like /Kaggen. He is also bound by certain rules, such as the need to be invited or to find a victim's 'scent'. The exact mechanics of his killing are sometimes vague. In some stories, he shoots an arrow; in others, he simply 'takes' the person.

Shape-shifting Gauna can transform his appearance, taking on forms that are terrifying or deceptive. He may appear as a whirlwind, an animal, or a shadowy figure. Attested in narratives where he pursues or deceives his victims. His transformations are often described as being incomplete or monstrous, revealing his true nature. The extent of his shapeshifting abilities is not systematically defined.

Supernatural Strength and Ferocity Gauna is a formidable physical opponent, capable of overpowering and killing humans with ease. He is a creature of immense power and aggression. Implied in all his confrontations with humans and /Kaggen. He can be overpowered by /Kaggen when the trickster uses his wits and magical abilities. None explicitly recorded.

Mastery over the Hunt (Negative Aspect) Gauna can blight the hunt, making it unsuccessful or dangerous. He scares away game or leads hunters astray. Attested in stories about hunters in the bush. His influence can be countered by rituals and the power of the shamans. This is a less prominent aspect of his power compared to his death-dealing abilities.

Behavior & Habitat

Gauna is a malevolent being who actively seeks to harm, kill, and devour. He is associated with death, disease, and misfortune. He is a predator, stalking his victims in the wild. He is also a thief, stealing the life-force or the 'heart' of his victims. Unlike /Kaggen, who is a bumbling but ultimately creative trickster, Gauna is a cunning and dangerous adversary. He is not easily tricked and is a formidable opponent.

Accounts

A man is dying. His family calls for /Kaggen (the Mantis) to save him. /Kaggen comes and sees that the man's heart has been taken by Gauna. /Kaggen, using his trickster abilities, travels to the underworld or confronts Gauna to retrieve the heart. The story details the struggle between the life-giving power of the creator and the death-dealing force of Gauna.

Gauna is associated with the whirlwind, a powerful and dangerous natural phenomenon. The whirlwind is seen as a manifestation of his presence, a force that can pick up dust, debris, and even people, carrying them away to their doom. People are warned to be careful when a whirlwind appears, as it may be Gauna seeking a victim.

A common theme is Gauna's method of killing. He steals the heart of his victim, which is the seat of life and vitality. Once the heart is taken, the person begins to sicken and die. A shaman or /Kaggen must then intervene to track down Gauna and recover the stolen heart.

Scholarly Debates
Gauna as 'Evil God'
Early ethnographers and missionaries interpreted Gauna as a devil-like figure, an evil god in a dualistic cosmology. ⇌ Modern scholars argue that ǀXam cosmology is not dualistic. Gauna is a powerful, dangerous being, but he is not a 'god of evil' opposing a 'god of good'. He is a force of nature and death, an inherent part of the cosmos.
Evidence for A: Early missionary accounts and some of Bleek and Lloyd's initial interpretations.
Evidence for B: Detailed analysis of the narratives by scholars like Hewitt and Guenther, who show the nuanced role of Gauna.
Gauna's Physical Form
Some accounts suggest Gauna has a monstrous, physical form. ⇌ Other accounts portray him as a disembodied force, a wind, a shadow, or a presence.
Evidence for A: Stories where he physically grapples with /Kaggen or humans.
Evidence for B: Stories where he is associated with the whirlwind or the darkness of the night.
Gauna's Role in the Underworld
Gauna is the active ruler and tormentor of the dead. ⇌ The underworld is a passive, shadowy place, and Gauna's role is more about bringing death to the living than governing the dead.
Evidence for A: Descriptions of the underworld as a place of fear and cold.
Evidence for B: The focus of most narratives is on Gauna's actions in the world of the living.
What We Don't Know

1. The original ǀXam names and stories may have nuances that are lost in translation. 2. The cultural context of the narratives, including rituals and beliefs, is only partially understood. 3. There is a lack of comparative material from other San groups regarding Gauna, as the ǀXam are extinct as a living culture. 4. The relationship between Gauna and other malevolent spirits in San folklore is not fully clear. 5. The archaeological record (e.g., rock art) may contain depictions of Gauna, but this is a matter of interpretation.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Gauna not as a 'devil' with pitchfork and horns, but as the embodiment of the wild, untamed, and lethal aspects of nature. He is the apex predator of the spirit world, the reason why you don't wander into the bush alone at night. If /Kaggen is the clever, bumbling gardener of the cosmos, trying to coax life into being, Gauna is the wildfire that consumes it, the drought that withers it, the final silence that follows the last heartbeat. He is the ultimate 'other'—the force that defines the boundary of life by being its end. In modern terms, he is the final boss, the ender of runs, the permadeath that makes the game matter.
