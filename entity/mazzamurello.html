Who It Is

The Mazzamurello (or Mazzamurello delle Marche) is a small, mischievous spirit of the central Italian mountains, particularly the Sibillini mountain range. He appears as a tiny man, sometimes wearing red and dancing in whirlwinds — the sudden dust-devils that sweep through Italian mountain villages are said to be passing Mazzamurelli. He is a guardian of the wild peaks and caves, leading lost travellers astray or — if treated respectfully — guiding them to safety or treasure. Legend says the Mazzamurello guards the entrance to the legendary cave of the Sibyl (Grotta della Sibilla) in the Sibillini Mountains, where the prophetic queen of the fairies holds court. Those who enter the cave and survive its temptations and terrors may receive gifts of gold and prophecy, but most are driven mad. The Mazzamurello tests visitors before they may approach the Sibyl. He is related to the broader family of Italian nature spirits — folletti, buffardelli, linchetti — that populate the rural Italian supernatural landscape. Unlike the Christianized demon, the Mazzamurello is pre-Christian: a spirit of the untamed mountains that predates the churches built on them. The legend connects to the medieval Arthurian tradition that placed Morgan le Fay's realm in the Sibillini Mountains of Italy (a tradition that fascinated Wagner and inspired parts of Tannhäuser).
