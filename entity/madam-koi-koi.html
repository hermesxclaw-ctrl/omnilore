Who It Is

In the dark of a Nigerian boarding-school corridor, fear has a sound before it has a face: the slow, deliberate click of a high heel on bare concrete—koi, koi, koi—drawing closer with every breath. For generations of students, that sound has meant one thing: Madam Koi Koi is walking, and anyone foolish enough to meet her gaze may never be seen again. Whether she is a vengeful ghost, a memory of an abusive teacher, or a story invented to keep teenagers in their beds, her red heels have carved a permanent groove in West African school folklore.

Madam Koi Koi is consistently characterized as hostile and vengeful. In origin stories, she is a strict, sometimes sadistic teacher who beats students for minor infractions, slaps them, and enjoys inflicting pain. After death, her ghost continues this pattern: she haunts dormitories and toilets at night, punishing students who break curfew, wander the halls, or use the bathroom after lights-out. Powers
Auditory haunting ("koi-koi" footsteps)

Her most consistent and attested ability is to announce her presence through the distinct clicking of high-heeled shoes on hard floors. The sound travels down corridors, often without a visible source, and grows louder as she approaches. This auditory phenomenon functions as both warning and terror mechanism, conditioning students to remain in bed and avoid forbidden spaces at night.

Limit: The sound is typically confined to school buildings, especially dormitories and toilets, and is most often reported at night after lights-out. Some accounts imply that only those who are out of bounds or awake at inappropriate hours hear it.

Physical assault (slapping, beating)

In both her living and ghostly forms, Madam Koi Koi is said to slap and beat students. Origin stories describe her as a teacher who inflicts severe corporal punishment, including a slap that permanently damages a student’s ear. After death, she continues to strike students who encounter her, sometimes leaving marks or causing injury.

Limit: Punishment is usually directed at students who break rules—wandering at night, leaving dormitories, or using toilets after curfew—though some versions portray her as attacking any student she encounters.

Causing disappearance or death

Several versions claim that any student who sees Madam Koi Koi’s face disappears, dies, or is found dead the next morning. In one widely circulated story, a student who helped kill her later hears her footsteps nightly, investigates, and is beaten to death, his body discovered the following day.

Limit: These consequences are typically tied to direct visual contact or deliberate pursuit of the sound, rather than incidental hearing.

Institutional enforcement of discipline (symbolic power)

Beyond supernatural abilities, Madam Koi Koi functions as a disciplinary tool. Teachers, prefects, and older students invoke her name to keep younger boarders in bed, discourage late-night conversations, and enforce curfews. The fear of her footsteps becomes a mechanism of social control within the school.

Limit: This power is symbolic rather than literal; it depends on belief and the circulation of stories among students and staff.

Haunting specific spaces (toilets, corridors, dormitories)

She is said to appear or be heard in particular liminal spaces—school toilets, long corridors, stairwells, and dormitory entrances—especially when students are alone. These locations amplify vulnerability and fear, making routine activities like using the bathroom at night fraught with anxiety.

Limit: Her activity is temporally limited to night or liminal times (very early morning, late evening). Daytime sightings are rare and often dismissed as exaggerations.

Psychological influence (archetypal fear)

Scholarly and popular analyses interpret Madam Koi Koi as a psychological archetype embodying fear of authority, punishment, and isolation in boarding schools. Her legend shapes student behavior, dreams, and memories, and persists long after graduation as a shared cultural reference.

Limit: This is an interpretive, not supernatural, power; it describes her impact on human psychology rather than an in-universe ability.

Accounts I
The cruel teacher fired after injuring a studentWikipedia; Grokipedia; independent folklore essay "Madam Koi Koi: Nigeria's Vengeful Boarding-School Ghost"

Late 20th century (story circulation); set in an unspecified postcolonial secondary school

In this widely attested origin story, Madam Koi Koi is a beautiful, stylish secondary-school teacher known for her red high heels and harsh discipline. She routinely beats students for minor infractions and once slaps a girl so hard that the student’s ear is permanently damaged. The school management finally dismisses her. Humiliated and enraged, she leaves the school in her red heels, vowing revenge. On her way home she dies in a road accident. After her death, students begin hearing the "koi-koi" sound of heels in the dormitory corridors at night. The ghostly teacher returns to punish students, and anyone who sees her face is said to vanish or die. The school becomes haunted; students fear leaving their beds after lights-out. The story reinforces rules about curfew and movement, and the teacher’s cruelty is transformed into a supernatural threat that outlives her. The legend spreads to other schools as students transfer or share stories.

The students’ revenge and the fatal heelWikipedia; Oriire article; independent folklore essay

Late 20th century (story circulation)

In another major variant, Madam Koi Koi is an extremely wicked teacher who flogs students at every opportunity. Frustrated by the school’s failure to discipline her, a group of students decide to take matters into their own hands. One night they corner her as she leaves the school, gag her to prevent screaming, and beat her mercilessly, using one of her own red heels to deliver the fatal blow. Panicking, they throw her body over the back fence, hoping people will assume she was killed by robbers. Over time, students involved in the attack begin to disappear, except for one boy who struck her with the shoe. He claims to hear her heels in the hostel every night. When he finally investigates, he is beaten to death; his body is found the next morning. The school is eventually shut down, and students are transferred elsewhere, carrying the legend with them. The students’ act of vigilante justice leads to a curse: they disappear or die, and the school closes. Accounts II
Madam Moke of Ghana: the lost heelOriire article; Wikipedia references to Ghanaian variant

Late 20th to early 21st century

In Ghana, a closely related figure known as Madam Moke haunts boarding schools. She is similarly described as a stylish woman in high heels. In one common story, she dies in a car accident while chasing a student, leaving one of her heels behind. After her death, students report hearing the sound of a single heel clicking through corridors at night. In some versions, she roams the school searching for the missing shoe, and anyone who encounters her or helps her is endangered. The legend reinforces rules about obedience and movement, warning students not to provoke teachers or wander at night. It also localizes the Nigerian story into Ghanaian school culture, adapting details to fit local language and imagery.

The unseen face and vanishing studentsIndependent folklore essay; oral accounts; Mythlok summary

Late 20th–21st century

A recurring motif in Madam Koi Koi stories is that students who see her face do not live to describe it. In many tellings, she is glimpsed only from behind or at a distance, her red heels visible but her face obscured. Those who claim to have seen her face are said to vanish, die mysteriously, or be found dead after a night-time encounter. This motif explains why descriptions of her face are rare and emphasizes the taboo of direct confrontation. The motif intensifies fear and discourages students from investigating strange sounds or apparitions. It also preserves the mystery of her appearance, making her more terrifying and less concretely defined.

School toilets and early-arriving studentsWikipedia; Grokipedia; oral accounts

Late 20th–21st century

Beyond boarding-school dormitories, Madam Koi Koi is said to haunt school toilets and target students who arrive too early or leave too late. Children who come to school before official opening hours or linger after closing may hear her heels in empty bathrooms or corridors. The legend warns that she punishes those who violate temporal boundaries of school life, reinforcing the idea that school spaces are dangerous when unsupervised. The story discourages unsupervised presence on school grounds and reinforces official schedules. It also extends her domain beyond boarding schools, making her relevant to day-school students.
