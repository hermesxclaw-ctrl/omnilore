Who It Is

In the snow-bound kotan of Ainu oral memory, a name surfaces without a Wikipedia page to anchor it — Inuppe, a heroic-legend figure tagged only as Q108415819. No realm, no era, no medium is given, and no encyclopedia article exists to summarize him. What remains is a trace in a database and the much larger, living archive of Ainu yukar — epic songs where human heroes walk beside kamuy — suggesting Inuppe belongs to that heroic world, but leaving almost every detail to be verified, not assumed.

No primary text allows direct characterization. Wikidata archetype 'heroic-legend' implies courageous, protective, community-defending behavior typical of Ainu heroes who defend kotan against invaders, demons (oyau kamuy), or rival groups, and who interact respectfully with kamuy. This is archetype inference, not attested biography. Attested: unknown. Reconstructed: hypothesized as brave, skilled hunter/warrior with ritual knowledge. Must not be presented as fact.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Unverified. No etymology provided in Wikidata metadata. Linguistically, the form is consistent with Ainu language morphology. In Ainu, 'inaw/inau' (sacred shaved wood offering), 'inu' (to hear/listen in some dialects, not to be confused with Japanese 'inu' = dog), and suffixes '-pe' (thing) / '-p' /

Pronunciation — Hypothetical, based on Ainu phonology: /i.nup.pe/ or /i.nu.pːe/ (three morae, stress on second syllable), Ainu is mora-timed and lacks tonal accent. Japanese approximation: i-nu-p-pe. No audio or IPA attestation located for this name.

Aliases — Inuppe

Collected 1920s-1930s; oral tradition older

Poiyaunpe, the most fully documented Ainu heroic orphan, is raised by a divine grandmother, learns his true parentage, avenges his parents, and defends his kotan with kamuy aid. Included here not as Inuppe's story, but to show what a verified Ainu heroic-legend narrative looks like and what an Inuppe text, if found, might structurally resemble. This is explicitly NOT claimed as Inuppe's story and must not be merged. Poiyaunpe restores justice and establishes model of heroic conduct; his cycle is central to Ainu oral literature studies

Inuppe is the same as Inugami (Japanese dog spirit) or a dog yokai because 'inu' means dog in Japanese — In Japanese 'inu' = dog, but Ainu language is unrelated to Japanese. No evidence links Inuppe to dogs or Inugami. Conflation is a common internet error when Ainu names are read through Japanese. No canine attributes are attested for Inuppe.

Inuppe has a well-documented Wikipedia page and powers/abilities list — No Wikipedia article exists in any language as of 2026-08-09 (Wikidata confirms 'No encyclopedia article available'). Any detailed power list found online would be fabrication or confusion with another character. This dossier deliberately leaves powers as unknown rather than inventing them.

Ainu heroes are 'primitive' or 'mythical' and not part of a living tradition — Yukar are living oral literature, actively performed and revitalized by Ainu artists and language teachers today. Framing Inuppe (if Ainu) as extinct mythology erases contemporary Ainu presence and Upopoy-led revitalization.
