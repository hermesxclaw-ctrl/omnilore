Who It Is

A bus ticket for a transit line that does not exist. When held while boarding any bus, the route changes — the bus travels through streets not on any map, stops at locations that should not exist. The destination is always a city in a country the rider has never heard of. 14 D-Class personnel have been sent. None returned. One sent a postcard dated 1847.

Form

A standard municipal bus ticket on cheap thermal paper. The route number changes each time observed. Fare is in an unknown currency. Validation punch forms a symbol identified as 'The First City.'

Powers

Route manipulation of any public transit

Transportation to an unknown city

Temporal displacement

Form-shifting transit ticket Attested: SCP-342 normally takes the form of a mass transit ticket for the closest form of mass transportation to its current location. When held by a sentient person for any length of time, it eventually changes form into a transit ticket for the mode of transportation the holder desires to use. This transformation always occurs unobserved, and no recordings of the change exist. SCP Wiki article (Description section) and derivative narrations. Attested: The transformation requires a sentient holder and time; it does not occur instantly. It appears constrained to mass transit systems rather than arbitrary objects. The change cannot be directly observed, suggesting a limitation tied to observation. Interpretive debates concern whether SCP-342 responds to conscious desire, subconscious intent, or proximity to transit options. The article does not specify the exact cognitive trigger, leaving room for speculation.

Irrevocable boarding and entrapment Attested: Anyone who uses SCP-342 to board a vehicle is unable to exit that vehicle by any means. Once the vehicle ends its route and ceases movement, the user disappears from reality. Attempts to leave are thwarted by strange occurrences that prevent disembarkation, and users develop an absolute conviction that exit is impossible. SCP Wiki article (Description) and summarized in animations. Attested: The effect applies only to the person who uses SCP-342 to board; bystanders are not subject to the same level of entrapment, though they may experience mild anomalous phenomena. The entrapment lasts only for the duration of the vehicle's route; once the route ends, the user disappears. Interpretive: Some adaptations depict physical barriers or supernatural entities enforcing the entrapment, while the article emphasizes psychological and perceptual phenomena. The exact mechanism—physical, mental, or metaphysical—is not resolved.

Self-reformation after use or disposal Attested: If SCP-342 is validated by stamp, has its ticket stub torn off, or is disposed of, it reforms itself into an unused ticket after a short period of time. SCP Wiki article (Description). Attested: The reformation occurs after a short, unspecified interval. The article does not describe any way to permanently destroy SCP-342. Unknown: No conflicting accounts of failed reformation or partial destruction are documented in accessible sources.

Induction of progressive psychological distress Attested: Users report a mounting feeling of dread prior to boarding, increasing during the journey, culminating in panic-inducing terror shortly before disappearance. Phenomena resemble acute paranoid schizophrenia, including auditory hallucinations, misperception of announcements, threatening interpretations of mundane objects, pathological fear of transit staff, and inability to perceive normal passenger movement. SCP Wiki article (Description) and derivative videos. Attested: The most acute experiences are limited to the user; bystanders may experience mild anomalous phenomena but not the full spectrum of distress. The effects appear tied to the duration of the journey and intensify as the route nears its end. Interpretive: Some readers argue that SCP-342 primarily manipulates perception rather than reality, while others suggest genuine environmental changes (darkened sky, altered weather). The article's language allows both readings.

Incident Log

Fact-based: The core narrative of SCP-342 is presented in the format of a containment report. The Foundation stores SCP-342 in an envelope stapled inside a case file folder, locked in High Value Items Storage. The description details its behavior as a shape-shifting transit ticket, its ability to entrap riders on vehicles, and the psychological effects experienced by users. The report frames SCP-342 as a Euclid-class anomaly—dangerous when used but manageable under proper containment. Interpretive: The case file implicitly tells a story of repeated encounters with SCP-342, including experiments and incidents, though specific events are not fully quoted in the snippet available.

Content Notes

Attested: Riders experience mounting dread, auditory hallucinations, misperception of announcements, and pathological fear of transit staff. These symptoms resemble acute paranoid schizophrenia and are induced by SCP-342's anomalous properties.

Within horror studies, SCP-342 exemplifies the use of everyday spaces (public transport) as sites of psychological breakdown. The explicit comparison to a psychiatric diagnosis raises questions about representation of mental illness in horror fiction; however, the article uses the term descriptively rather than as a clinical case.

SCP Wiki article (Description).

Attested: At the end of the vehicle's route, riders disappear from reality. The article does not specify whether they die, are transported to another dimension, or undergo some other fate, but the implication is permanent loss.

This disappearance functions as a metaphor for being "taken" by systems beyond one's control—bureaucratic, technological, or supernatural. In internet horror, such erasure often stands in for fears of anonymity and depersonalization.

SCP Wiki article (Description).

A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine a ticket that always knows where you want to go—and never lets you come back. SCP-342 looks like any other scrap of card stock in your pocket, a bus pass or train stub you might crumple and forget. But once you validate it and step aboard, the world outside the windows darkens, announcements twist into threats, and every attempt to stand up feels like pushing against an invisible verdict. At the end of the route, the vehicle stops, the doors open, and you simply are not there anymore. The ticket, however, is—fresh, unused, and waiting for the next rider.
