Who It Is

In the vast, star-scattered sea of Japanese horse racing, few names carry the weight of Yutaka Take — a jockey whose career reads like a legend etched in turf and thunder, spanning decades and defying the limits of human endurance.

Known for his calm demeanor, strategic mind, and relentless work ethic; respected for his professionalism and longevity in a physically demanding sport.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese surname 'Take' (武) meaning 'warrior' or 'military'; given name 'Yutaka' (豊) meaning 'abundant' or 'bountiful'.

Pronunciation — YOO-tah-kah TAH-keh

Aliases — Take Yutaka, Yutaka-kun, The Emperor of Racing, Take

Ownership — Japanese; associated with Japanese horse racing culture; widely recognized in Japan and internationally.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Yutaka Take is a Japanese male, typically seen in jockey silks during races; physical appearance: lean, athletic build, short black hair, often focused expression.

Symbols — His racing silks often feature distinctive colors; associated with victory poses and the number of wins; represented in media as a symbol of Japanese racing excellence.

Domains — Primarily Japanese racecourses (Tokyo, Nakayama, Hanshin, etc.); also international races (Dubai, Hong Kong, France, etc.); home base in Japan.

Accounts I
Record-breaking careerJRA statistics

1987-present

Yutaka Take became the youngest jockey to win the Japanese Derby in 1992, and has accumulated over 4,000 wins in JRA races, a record. Set benchmarks for future jockeys; inspired many.

Accounts II
International successSports news

1990s-present

Take has won races in France, Dubai, Hong Kong, and other countries, showcasing Japanese jockeys' competitiveness globally. Raised profile of Japanese racing internationally.

The 'Take' phenomenonMedia coverage

1990s-2000s

Take's popularity transcended sport, appearing in commercials, TV shows, and becoming a household name in Japan. Increased public interest in horse racing.
