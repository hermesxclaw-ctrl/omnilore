The Approach

In the shadow of Mount Othrys, where the Hellenic tribes traced their descent from the fire-stealer Prometheus, there lived a prince whose name was almost lost to time: Neonus, son of Hellen, father of Dotus, and a quiet thread in the tapestry of Greek mythological genealogy. His story is not one of epic deeds, but of lineage—a reminder that even the most obscure figures anchor the identities of peoples and places.

Content advisory. None
Identity

Neonus

Pronunciation neh-OH-nus (English approximation), neh-OH-noos (classical Greek)

Etymology

The name Neonus (Νεώνου) may derive from the Greek word 'νεών' (neon), meaning 'young' or 'new', but this is speculative. The genitive form suggests a patronymic or toponymic origin.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically Thessalian regional tradition

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have suggested that Neonus might be a variant or misreading of another name, but no concrete evidence supports this. The name appears only once in extant literature, making identification difficult.

Description

Appearance

No physical description of Neonus survives. He is known only from genealogical lists, so his appearance is unknown.

Symbols & Iconography

No symbols or iconography are associated with Neonus. He does not appear in any known artistic representations.

Behaviour & Personality

No specific behaviors or personality traits are recorded. His role is purely genealogical.

Domains & Habitats

Neonus was a Phthian prince, living in the region of Phthia in Thessaly, a land associated with Hellenic origins and the Myrmidons.

Version Differences

No alternative versions of Neonus exist. The only mention is in Stephanus of Byzantium.

Stories & Myths

Genealogy of Neonus

Source Stephanus of ByzantiumCulture GreekPeriod Byzantine era (6th century AD), preserving earlier mythological traditions

Summary

Neonus is listed as a son of Hellen, the eponymous ancestor of the Hellenes, and the father of Dotus, who gave his name to the city Dotium in Thessaly. This genealogy establishes Neonus as a link in the chain from the mythological founder of the Greek people to a specific geographic location.

Participants

Neonus

Hellen (father)

Dotus (son)

Consequences

Through this lineage, Neonus becomes an ancestor figure for the inhabitants of Dotium, providing a mythological charter for the city's name and identity.

Variants

No variants are recorded. Other sons of Hellen are well-attested, but Neonus appears only here.

Contradictions & Disputes

Existence and genealogy

Position A

Neonus is a genuine mythological figure, son of Hellen, as recorded by Stephanus.

Position A — evidence

Stephanus's entry provides a clear genealogical statement.

Position B

Neonus may be a later invention or a textual corruption, given his absence from earlier sources.

Position B — evidence

No other ancient author mentions Neonus, and his name is rare.

Scholarly Consensus

Most scholars treat Stephanus as a reliable compiler of earlier traditions, but the lack of corroboration leaves room for doubt. The figure is generally accepted but considered obscure.

His mother is unknown with certainty, and his own life story is entirely absent. The etymology of his name is speculative. Research is limited to a single textual mention.
