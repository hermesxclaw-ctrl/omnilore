Who It Is

In the pantheon of Tamriel's Daedric Princes, Meridia stands apart as a being of paradox—a Daedra who despises the undead, a deity of 'living light' who commands the energies of the sun itself. Her followers whisper of a Lady of Infinite Energies, a being who once walked among the Aedra before her fall, and whose obsessions with purity and order have shaped the fates of mortals and immortals alike. To understand Meridia is to peer into the heart of a cosmic contradiction: a Prince of Oblivion who wages war against the very essence of death.

Meridia is portrayed as a proud, haughty, and uncompromising deity. She is obsessed with purity and order, and abhors the undead and necromancy. She is willing to manipulate mortals to achieve her goals, often using them as champions to cleanse tainted places. She is not malevolent in the traditional sense, but her methods can be ruthless. She values loyalty and obedience, and is quick to punish those who defy her. She is also known to be somewhat vain, expecting worship and reverence.

Pronunciation — meh-RID-ee-ah

Powers
Control over Light and Solar Energy

Meridia can command the power of the sun and light, using it to sear enemies, illuminate dark places, and even restore life. She can channel this energy through her champions, granting them abilities like the Dawnbreaker's fiery explosions.

Limit: Her power is less effective in the complete absence of light or in realms of pure darkness. She cannot create light from nothing; she must draw upon existing solar energy or the ambient light of Mundus.

Purification and Banishment of Undead

Meridia has a profound hatred for the undead and can banish or destroy them with a touch or a command. Her power is especially potent against zombies, skeletons, and other reanimated corpses. She can also cleanse areas tainted by necromantic energy.

Limit: Her power may be less effective against powerful undead beings, such as vampires or liches, who may resist her influence. Some undead are immune to her banishment if they are bound to a stronger power.

Granting Artifacts and Blessings

Meridia grants powerful artifacts to her champions, such as the Dawnbreaker, a sword that burns undead, and the Ring of Khajiit, which enhances stealth. She also bestows blessings that increase a follower's combat effectiveness against undead.

Limit: Artifacts are only granted to those who prove themselves worthy through quests. They may lose their power if the wielder falls out of favor with Meridia.

Manipulation of Mortals

Meridia can communicate with mortals through dreams, visions, and direct speech. She can also influence their wills, though she often prefers to persuade or command rather than possess. She has been known to 'purify' mortals, stripping them of free will to serve her.

Limit: She cannot directly control a mortal's actions for extended periods without their consent or a powerful artifact. Some mortals are resistant to her influence due to strong will or magical protection.

Realm Manipulation (Colored Rooms)

As a Daedric Prince, Meridia has absolute control over her Oblivion realm, the Colored Rooms. She can shape the environment, create creatures, and alter the laws of physics within her domain.

Limit: Her power is limited to her own realm; she cannot alter Mundus directly. She must act through intermediaries or artifacts.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Meridia is typically depicted as a beautiful female figure bathed in brilliant, golden light. In Skyrim, she appears as a glowing, spectral woman with a serene face, often surrounded by radiant particles. In ESO, she is shown as a more solid figure with pale skin, golden eyes, and flowing white hair, adorned in ornate armor that seems to be made of light. Her form is often partially obscured by intense luminosity, making her appear otherworldly a

Symbols — Meridia's symbol is a stylized sunburst, often depicted as a golden disc with radiating rays. She is associated with the color gold, light, and the sun. Her artifacts often bear this symbol, and her statues are typically surrounded by glowing light. She is also associated with the concept of 'living light' and the pure energy of the sun.

Domains — Meridia's realm in Oblivion is called the Colored Rooms, a plane of shifting, vibrant colors and crystalline structures. It is a place of intense light and beauty, but also of danger. She is associated with the sun, light, and life, and is often invoked by those who oppose the undead. Her influence is felt throughout Tamriel, with shrines and temples dedicated to her in various provinces.

Accounts I
The Fall of the Magna-GeThe Elder Scrolls Online loading screen and lore books

Dawn Era

According to some legends, Meridia was originally a Magna-Ge, a spirit that helped create Mundus but fled before its completion. She was cast out of Aetherius for her role in the creation, and became a Daedric Prince. This story explains her affinity for light and her disdain for the undead, as she is said to have been 'tainted' by the mortal world. Meridia's fall from grace is said to have fueled her hatred of death and decay, as she seeks to restore purity to the world.

The Purge of the Undead in the Iliac BayThe Elder Scrolls II: Daggerfall

Third Era

Meridia aided the Breton knight Sir Amiel in cleansing the Iliac Bay region of a powerful undead army. She granted him the Dawnbreaker to fight the horde. The quest involves recovering the sword and using it to destroy the undead threat. The region was freed from undead, and Meridia's influence in the area grew. The Dawnbreaker became a famous artifact.

Accounts II
The Champion of Meridia in CyrodiilThe Elder Scrolls IV: Oblivion

Third Era

The Champion of Cyrodiil encounters Meridia's shrine and is tasked with recovering her stolen artifact, the 'Mace of Molag Bal'. After defeating the necromancer who stole it, the Champion is rewarded with the Dawnbreaker. This quest highlights Meridia's hatred of undead and her willingness to use mortals as instruments. The Champion gains a powerful weapon and Meridia's favor. The necromancer is destroyed.

The Break of DawnThe Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim

Fourth Era

The Dragonborn finds Meridia's Beacon and is summoned to her temple. She tasks them with cleansing her temple of undead and retrieving the Dawnbreaker. The quest culminates in the destruction of Malkoran, a necromancer who defiled her temple. Meridia rewards the Dragonborn with the Dawnbreaker. The temple is purified, and the Dragonborn gains a powerful artifact. Meridia's influence in Skyrim is established.

The Light of Meridia in the Clockwork CityThe Elder Scrolls Online: Clockwork City DLC

Second Era

Meridia plays a role in the Clockwork City storyline, where she helps the Vestige combat the machinations of Sotha Sil and Nocturnal. She provides guidance and assistance, showing a more cooperative side to her nature. The Vestige is aided in their quest, and Meridia's motivations are further explored.
