The Approach

A train pulls away from a small station, snow still clinging to the platform—this is the image that has made 'Nagoriyuki' one of Japan's most beloved folk songs, a melody that has traveled from a 1974 album track to a cinematic theme, a station jingle, and countless covers.

Identity

Nagoriyuki

Pronunciation na-go-ri-yu-ki

Known As

なごり雪NokoriyukiRemaining Snow

Etymology

'Nagoriyuki' (なごり雪) literally means 'remaining snow'—snow that lingers after the season has turned, evoking a sense of lingering memory or parting.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese folk music tradition; composed by Shōzō Ise, originating from Tsukumi, Ōita Prefecture.

Disputed Identifications

There is no significant dispute over the song's identity, but it is sometimes confused with the fictional character Nagoriyuki from the video game Guilty Gear, which is unrelated.

Behaviour & Personality

The song is characterized by a gentle, melancholic melody and reflective lyrics about a farewell in a snowy setting.

Domains & Habitats

Japanese popular music, folk music, seasonal songs

Version Differences

Kaguyahime's original version is more folk-oriented; Iruka's cover is more polished and widely known. Numerous other covers exist, varying in arrangement and interpretation.
