Olokun

God of the Deep Ocean, Lord of the Abyss, Keeper of Unfathomable Wealth

Above the waves, you see the surface. Below, I keep what the world has forgotten — the drowned gold, the sunken truth, the darkness that births all light.

Who It Is

Olokun is the Yoruba orisha of the deep ocean — the abyssal depths below Yemaya's surface waters, a realm of immense pressure, darkness, mystery, and unfathomable treasure. Where Yemaya governs the life-giving surface ocean, Olokun rules what lies beneath: the realm of sunken ships, drowned civilizations, primordial secrets, and the ocean floor's unimaginable wealth. Olokun is a deity of profound power and inscrutable motives, representing the ocean's most terrifying and magnificent aspects.

In Yoruba tradition, Olokun's gender is fluid and varies by region — in some areas, Olokun is male; in others, female; in yet others, androgynous. In the Benin Kingdom (Edo tradition), Olokun is a male deity associated with the divine right of kings. In Cuban Santería, Olokun is most often conceived as male or androgynous, a mysterious and somewhat terrifying figure who must be approached with great care. Physically, Olokun is rarely depicted directly in traditional art — his true form is considered too powerful and alien for human eyes. When represented, he appears as a figure emerging from deep blue or indigo water, sometimes wearing a mask, often associated with chains, anchors, and treasure chests.

Olokun's central myth involves a challenge to Olodumare, the supreme deity. In some versions, Olokun — jealous of the dry land created by Obatala and envying humanity's devotion to the sky god — sent great floods to overwhelm the earth, challenging Olodumare's supremacy. Olodumare remained calm, and the other orishas (particularly Obatala) bound Olokun with chains and cast him into the deepest abyss, where he remains — immensely powerful but restrained. Other versions describe Olokun as the primordial ocean itself, existing before creation, from whom all waters emerged and to whom all waters will eventually return.

Despite his terrifying aspects, Olokun is also the source of great blessing. His realm contains pearls, coral, sunken gold, and all the wealth of the sea. Devotees petition Olokun for prosperity, but also for emotional depth, psychological insight, and the courage to face the unknown. In Santería, Olokun is syncretized with Our Lady of Regla alongside Yemaya, and his rites — conducted in deep blue rooms or near the ocean — are among the most solemn and secretive. Offerings include melon, dark molasses, whole fish, and blue objects. His children are said to be deeply emotional, intensely private, and often drawn to the sea or to psychology. Olokun reminds us that some truths exist only in the depths, and wisdom sometimes requires descending into darkness before surfacing with treasure.
