Who It Is

Orang Pendek ('short person' in Indonesian) is a cryptid primate reported from the remote mountain forests of western Sumatra. Described as a bipedal, ground-dwelling ape standing 3 to 5 feet tall, covered in short dark or reddish-brown hair, with a human-like face, broad shoulders, and powerful arms. Unlike the larger Yeti or Bigfoot, Orang Pendek is consistently described as a relatively small, shy herbivore that feeds on fruits, roots, and grubs. The Suku Anak Dalam (Orang Rimba) indigenous people have known of this creature for centuries, regarding it as a real, corporeal animal of the forest. Dutch colonists in the early 20th century documented consistent native accounts, and the creature drew serious scientific attention in the 1990s when British conservationist Debbie Martyr launched a long-term research project in Kerinci Seblat National Park. Martyr and her team have collected multiple eyewitness accounts and cast footprints showing a divergent big toe -- a feature diagnostic of non-human primates. In 2003, the discovery of Homo floresiensis ('the Hobbit') on nearby Flores demonstrated that small bipedal hominids coexisted with modern humans until remarkably recently, lending unexpected credibility to the Orang Pendek hypothesis. Some researchers suggest it may represent a surviving population related to Homo floresiensis or an undiscovered orangutan subspecies. Two British tourists reported close-range sightings in the 1990s, describing a powerfully built ape walking on two legs.
