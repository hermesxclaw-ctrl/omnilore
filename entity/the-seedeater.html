Who It Is

The Seedeater is a creepypasta entity associated with the autumn harvest season, particularly active in the rural Midwest during October. It is described as a towering humanoid figure, approximately 9 feet tall, composed of dried corn husks, twisted vines, and desiccated plant matter. Its head resembles a withered pumpkin or gourd, with features that vaguely suggest a face — hollow eyes, a carved mouth — but the expression is frozen and unreadable. The Seedeater appears in cornfields during the harvest, and its presence is heralded by a sudden silence — all birdsong stops, all insect sounds cease, and the air becomes unnaturally still. The entity's behavior is seasonal and ritualistic: it 'plants' its victims in the earth, burying them alive in shallow graves with only their heads exposed, as if they were crops waiting to be harvested. The Seedeater then returns at the next full moon to 'harvest' them. The survivors of encounters describe the experience of being planted as one of absolute terror — the entity's touch is cold and dry, its movements are slow and deliberate, and during the burial process, it makes a soft sound like the rustling of dry leaves that some interpret as singing. The Seedeater is considered one of the more atmospheric creepypasta entities, relying less on shock value and more on the eerie, folk-horror aesthetic of the harvest season. It draws on the same tradition as scarecrows and harvest spirits — the sense that the land itself can be hostile, and that the rituals of agriculture hide older, darker practices. The entity has been featured in numerous YouTube narrations and is frequently cited as an underrated gem in the creepypasta canon.
