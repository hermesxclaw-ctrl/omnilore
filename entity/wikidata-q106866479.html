Who It Is

In the open skies of the Americas, a small bird with a fierce heart reigns not by size but by sheer audacity—the kingbird, whose name in Latin, Tyrannus, hints at its unyielding dominance over much larger foes.

Prefer semi-open or open areas. They perch on exposed branches and sally out to catch insects in flight. They aggressively defend breeding territories, often chasing away much larger birds, including hawks.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Small passerine birds with long pointed wings and large broad bills. Plumage varies by species; many have gray or black upperparts and white underparts, some with yellow or red crowns.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography; in ornithology, they are recognized by their aggressive behavior and aerial insect-catching.

Domains — Native to the Americas, from North to South America, in semi-open or open habitats such as fields, savannas, and forest edges.

Kingbird versus Red-tailed Hawk (2009)Photographic documentation

2009

A kingbird was photographed defending its young by landing on a red-tailed hawk's back, sinking its talons and pecking its skull until the hawk gave up and flew away. The hawk retreated; the young were protected.
