The Approach

In the autumn of 2004, a bay colt with a single white foot tore through Japan's most prestigious races, seizing the Triple Crown of the Turf and the hearts of 125,000 fans at Nakayama. Zenno Rob Roy, a son of the legendary Sunday Silence, didn't just win—he rewrote records and left a legacy that galloped far beyond the track.

Content advisory. None. This is a factual account of a racehorse's life and career.

Zenno Rob Roy

Pronunciation ZEN-no ROB-roy (Japanese: ゼンノロブロイ, Hepburn: Zenno Robu Roi)

Etymology

The name 'Zenno' likely derives from the owner's name or stable prefix (Zenno is a common Japanese horse name prefix), while 'Rob Roy' references the Scottish folk hero and outlaw Rob Roy MacGregor.

Cultural Ownership

Japan (bred and raced), with American sire and dam lines.

Disputed Identifications

None known.

Description

Appearance

Bay horse with one white foot, standing just under 16.2 hands (approximately 1.68 meters).

Symbols & Iconography

None specific; his white foot is a distinguishing mark.

Behaviour & Personality

Known for his strong finish and resilience; often held up early in races and produced a powerful late run. He was a consistent performer who placed in many races.

Domains & Habitats

Trained in Japan, primarily at the Ritto Training Center; raced at major Japanese tracks including Tokyo, Nakayama, Hanshin, and Kyoto. Also raced at York Racecourse in England.

No significant version differences, as he is a real horse.

Stories & Myths

2004 Autumn Triple Crown triumph

Source Racing records and contemporary news reportsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod Autumn 2004

Summary

Zenno Rob Roy won the autumn Tennō Shō, Japan Cup, and Arima Kinen consecutively, becoming the first horse to sweep the Autumn Triple Crown. His Arima Kinen victory set a record time of 2:29.5.
