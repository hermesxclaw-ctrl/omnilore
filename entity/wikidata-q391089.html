Who It Is

Before the spears clash, before the shields meet, there is a sound — a raw, collective roar that tears through the air and turns fear into fury. The ancient Greeks gave that sound a name, and then they gave that name a mother. She is Alala, daughter of War himself, the brief, terrible goddess of the moment when an army decides to charge. She has no face, no temple, no myth — only a voice, and that voice is the beginning of everything.

Alala is the embodiment of the war-cry itself — the collective shout of soldiers as they charge. She is the sound that precedes the clash of spears, the audible threshold between peace and violence. As a personification, she does not act; she is the act.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From the ancient Greek verb ἀλαλάζω (alalázō), meaning 'to raise a war-cry' or 'to shout loudly'. The name is onomatopoeic, directly imitating the sound of a battle cry — akin to 'hurrah!' or 'a-la-la!'

Pronunciation — ah-lah-LAH (ἀ-λᾰ-λᾱ́)

Aliases — Ἀλαλά, Alalá, Alale

Ownership — Ancient Greek religion and mythology. A minor daemon or personification (daimona) of the war-cry, daughter of Polemos (War personified).

Embodiment of the War-Cry

Alala is the personification of the battle-shout. Her 'power' is the power of the cry itself: to rally allies, to intimidate enemies, to mark the beginning of combat.

Limit: She is a sound, not a physical force. She has no agency beyond the cry she represents.

Psychopomp of Courage

The war-cry, and thus Alala, serves to summon courage in the hearts of soldiers and to strike fear into the enemy. This is a reconstructed psychological function based on the purpose of the war-cry in ancient warfare.

Limit: No direct ancient source attributes this function to Alala specifically.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No physical description survives in any ancient source. Alala is a sound made manifest, a voice without a body. Later artistic depictions (rare) imagine her as a young woman with her mouth open in a shout, sometimes holding a war trumpet (salpinx) or a spear.

Symbols — The war-cry itself, the spear (as she is the 'prelude of the spears'), the trumpet (salpinx), the open mouth.

Domains — Warfare, the battle-cry, the moment of attack, the psychological dimension of combat (fear, courage, intimidation).

Accounts I
The Daughter of PolemosPindar, Dithyrambs (fragment 78)

c. 5th century BCE

Pindar, in a fragment of a dithyramb (a choral hymn to Dionysus), names Alala as the daughter of Polemos (War). He calls her the 'prelude of the spears' (ἐγχεῖον κορυφά), meaning she is the first sound of battle, the cry that precedes the clash of weapons. He also notes that 'men sacrifice to her in war' — a poetic way of saying that the war-cry is the first offering made in battle. Establishes Alala as a minor but real figure in the Greek divine pantheon, a personification of a sound rather than a physical deity.

Accounts II
The War-Cry in Greek Military PracticeThucydides, History of the Peloponnesian War; Xenophon, Anabasis; Plutarch, Life of Alexander

Classical to Hellenistic period (5th-4th century BCE)

While Alala herself appears only in Pindar's fragmentary poetry, the war-cry she personified was a central element of Greek military culture. Greek armies advanced into battle raising the paean—a rhythmic chant or shout that served both to terrify the enemy and to synchronize the hoplite line's advance. Thucydides describes Athenian and Spartan soldiers raising their war-cries before the clash of shields at battles such as Mantinea and Delium. Xenophon, in his Anabasis, recounts how the Ten Thousand Greek mercenaries shouted their battle cry as they charged across the plains of Mesopotamia. The war-cry was not merely an emotional release but a tactical tool: it signaled the moment of engagement, masked fear, and created a psychological shockwave that could cause less disciplined enemy formations to waver before the first spear was thrown. In this context, Alala was not just a poetic abstraction but a genuine presence on the battlefield—the sound itself was the goddess, and to raise the cry was to invoke her power. Alala is a major goddess of war like Athena or Ares. — She is a very minor personification of a single sound — the war-cry. She has no myths, no cult sites, and no major appearances in Greek literature beyond a single fragment.

Alala is the same as Enyo or a sister of Ares. — She is the daughter of Polemos, not a sibling of Ares. Enyo is a separate, more prominent war goddess.

The word 'alala' was a specific, formalized battle-cry. — It was a general onomatopoeic term for a loud shout or cry, used in both military and ritual contexts. It was not a specific, codified chant.
