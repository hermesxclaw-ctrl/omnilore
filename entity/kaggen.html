Who It Is

Kaggen (also Cagn or |Kaggen) is the supreme trickster-creator deity of the San people, one of the world's oldest continuous mythological traditions. He most frequently manifests as a praying mantis — a small, seemingly insignificant insect that nonetheless embodies immense creative and destructive power. Together with his wife Coti, their adopted daughter the Porcupine, and their grandson Ichneumon (the mongoose-like tracker), Kaggen's family forms a divine clan. Unlike transcendent creator gods of monotheistic traditions, Kaggen is fallible, emotional, and deeply involved in the daily affairs of existence. He makes mistakes, gets into fights, is sometimes humiliated, and can be killed — only to be restored. He created the eland antelope (the San's most sacred animal) from a shoe, and he gave the San their hunting knowledge and trance dances. In the myths collected by Wilhelm Bleek and Lucy Lloyd from Xam prisoners in Cape Town in the 1870s, Kaggen emerges as a profoundly nuanced figure — not an omnipotent lawgiver but a being who exists in the same difficult world as humans, modeling resilience, creativity, and survival through his endless transformations. The San believe Kaggen can appear as any animal and that his creative energy (num) resides in all living things, accessible through the trance dance.
