Who It Is

He never raised his voice in court — he didn't need to. Gregory Edgeworth, the defense attorney who had never lost, died not with a closing argument but with a gunshot in a dark, shaking elevator, his nine-year-old son watching. For fifteen years his murder was remembered as a tragedy of oxygen and panic; the truth was colder, more deliberate, and it shaped two generations of lawyers who followed.

Attested personality: calm, principled, soft-spoken, and unwaveringly ethical. Described by Raymond Shields as a lawyer who 'never turned away a client and never forged evidence,' and by Manfred von Karma as an irritant who 'tarnished perfection' by giving him his only penalty in 15 years. He is shown as a doting father: he brought nine-year-old Miles to the courthouse on December 28, 2001 to watch closing arguments, encouraged Miles' early interest in defense work, and is implied to have taught Miles the classroom defense that later saves Phoenix Wright. Powers
Undefeated Defense Advocacy

As a defense attorney, Gregory maintained a perfect win record until DL-6, renowned for acquitting clients even against Manfred von Karma, who had a 15-year perfect record until Gregory forced a penalty. His skill lay in meticulous evidence analysis, calm cross-examination, and ethical fact-finding rather than theatrics.

Limit: Not supernatural; bound by legal procedure and evidence. Could not prevent extrajudicial murder. His perfection was precisely what provoked von Karma's lethal retaliation. No attested loss before DL-6, but record is stated in dialogue, not shown case-by-case.

Ethical Lawyering and Mentorship

Refused to use forged evidence or coercion. Mentored younger attorneys including Robert Hammond and Raymond Shields, teaching them to prioritize client truth. His mentorship directly inspired Miles' childhood aspiration to become a defense attorney before trauma redirected him to prosecution.

Limit: Mentorship was informal and truncated by his early death; Shields notes Gregory's ideals were difficult to uphold in corrupt system.

Parental Influence and Moral Exemplar

Not a power in supernatural sense, but narrative ability: his memory acts as moral compass for Miles. Miles' eventual rejection of 'guilty at all costs' prosecution is framed as return to father's values. Phoenix Wright's own defense philosophy is indirectly linked via Miles' childhood classroom trial defense, which Miles learned from watching Gregory.

Limit: Influence is posthumous and psychological; he cannot intervene directly. Miles misremembers his death for 15 years, showing limits of memory.

Accounts I
The Undefeated Defense Attorney — Pre-DL-6 ReputationPhoenix Wright: Ace Attorney Episode 4 (Turnabout Goodbyes) and Ace Attorney Investigations 2 Case 5

Fictional late 1990s-2001 (canon timeline), created 2001

Before his death, Gregory Edgeworth was one of the most respected defense attorneys in the district, known for never losing a case. He took on difficult, unpopular defendants and won through thorough investigation. His most significant professional collision was with prosecutor Manfred von Karma, who had maintained a perfect 15-year record of guilty verdicts. In Yanni Yogi's murder trial (the case that became DL-6), Gregory's defense was effective enough to force von Karma to receive his first and only penalty for mishandling evidence — a humiliation von Karma never forgave. Colleague Raymond Shields later keeps Gregory's desk and files as a shrine, describing him as a man who believed the defense's job was to find truth, not merely to win. Established Gregory as von Karma's motive for murder; established ethical ideal that later defines Miles' redemption arc; provides in-universe precedent for Phoenix Wright's own idealistic defense style.

The Classroom Turnabout — Father as ModelPhoenix Wright: Ace Attorney Episode 4 flashback dialogue (Miles Edgeworth testimony)

Fictional 2001 (Miles age 9), created 2001

As a child, Miles Edgeworth idolized his father and aspired to be a defense attorney. In elementary school, Miles successfully defended his classmate Phoenix Wright when Phoenix was falsely accused of stealing lunch money (actually taken by Miles himself, though Miles' guilt is revealed later). Miles explicitly states he learned how to defend by watching his father at work. This childhood trial, inspired by Gregory, is the foundational link between the Edgeworth and Wright families and the reason Phoenix later becomes a defense attorney to 'see Miles again.' Creates thematic lineage: Gregory → Miles → Phoenix. Explains Miles' initial career aspiration and Phoenix's motivation. Payoff 15 years later when Miles and Phoenix meet as prosecutor vs defense.

The DL-6 Incident — Death in the ElevatorPhoenix Wright: Ace Attorney Episode 4: Turnabout Goodbyes — DL-6 case file, testimony of Yanni Yogi, Miles Edgeworth, and Manfred von Karma

Fictional December 28, 2001, 15 years before game's present (2016); created 2001

On December 28, 2001, after closing arguments in Yanni Yogi's trial, a massive earthquake struck the courthouse. The power failed while an elevator containing Gregory Edgeworth, his son Miles (who had come to watch), and court bailiff Yanni Yogi was between floors. Oxygen rapidly depleted. In the darkness and panic, Manfred von Karma — who had been in the elevator as well (original script) or who opened the elevator shaft and shot from outside during blackout (depending on translation staging) — fired a pistol. The bullet struck Gregory in the heart, killing him instantly. Nine-year-old Miles, suffering oxygen deprivation, witnessed his father's shooting but repressed the memory, later believing he himself had shot his father when Yanni Yogi threw the pistol to him. Yanni Yogi suffered severe brain damage from oxygen loss, losing his memory and later his position and fiancée. The case was recorded as unsolved (DL-6) due to lack of evidence and conflicting survivor testimony. The pistol was disposed of in the courthouse lake. Accounts II
Posthumous Frame — Yanni Yogi's Revenge and Robert Hammond's MurderPhoenix Wright: Ace Attorney Episode 4: Turnabout Goodbyes (2016 present-day case)

Fictional December 2016 (15 years after DL-6), created 2001

Fifteen years after DL-6, Yanni Yogi — now living as a reclusive caretaker of the Kurain-adjacent lake, mentally diminished and having lost his fiancée Polly Jenkins and career — seeks revenge against those he blames for ruining his life. He murders defense attorney Robert Hammond (who had been Gregory's colleague and who had defended Yanni Yogi after Gregory's death but later abandoned him) on the lake. He then frames Miles Edgeworth for the murder, using Miles' presence on the lake (Miles was meeting Hammond to discuss DL-6). Gregory's death is thus the inciting historical crime that enables the present-day murder; Phoenix Wright's defense of Miles requires exhuming DL-6 and proving von Karma, not Miles, killed Gregory. Exoneration of Miles; exposure of von Karma; Yanni Yogi's arrest; Miles' psychological liberation and career reorientation; establishes Gregory's death as structural lynchpin of franchise's first game climax.

Legacy Through Raymond Shields — The Forgotten Turnabout ParallelAce Attorney Investigations 2: Prosecutor's Gambit, Case 4: The Forgotten Turnabout and Case 5: The Grand Turnabout

Fictional 2011 (in-universe), created 2011 (Japan) / 2024 (worldwide)

Defense attorney Raymond Shields, Gregory's former apprentice, idolized Gregory and kept his office exactly as Gregory left it, including his fountain pen and case files. Shields reveals Gregory's philosophy: 'A defense attorney's job is to believe in his client until the end.' Shields' own defense of the IS-7 incident's victim (the confectioner) parallels Gregory's defense of Yanni Yogi, and his confrontation with prosecutor Manfred von Karma's legacy (through von Karma's daughter Franziska and protege Sebastian Debeste) mirrors Gregory's. Miles, now a prosecutor questioning his path, learns from Shields that Gregory never sought perfection for its own sake but sought truth, prompting Miles to finally articulate his own definition of prosecution as seeking truth rather than guilty verdicts. Deepens Gregory's characterization beyond 'perfect victim' to ethical teacher; provides Miles with alternative father figure who transmits Gregory's values; reinforces franchise theme that true justice transcends win/loss records.

Spiritual Absence — Why Gregory Never ReturnsPhoenix Wright: Ace Attorney: Justice for All, Trials and Tribulations, Spirit of Justice (Fey channeling mechanics)

2002-2016 (franchise development)

Unlike Mia Fey, Gregory Edgeworth never appears as a channeled spirit via the Kurain channeling technique, despite his son's central role. The narrative reason is that his death was not tied to the Fey spiritual lineage and his spirit was never summoned; the meta reason is that Shu Takumi reserved channeling for the Fey family to keep its rules consistent. His absence is itself a story point: Miles must remember his father through evidence and testimony, not supernatural reunion, emphasizing memory and record over spirit intervention. Fans have noted this absence makes his death feel more final than Mia's. Reinforces thematic distinction between legal truth (documents, memory) and spiritual truth (channeling); preserves Gregory's death as irreversible historical fact rather than reversible plot device.
