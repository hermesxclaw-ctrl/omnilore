Who It Is

Juracán (from which the English word 'hurricane' descends, via Spanish huracán) is the Taíno spirit of the great storm — the primordial wind-force whose cyclonic energy shaped both the physical geography of the Caribbean and the seasonal rhythm of its peoples. In Taíno creation thought, Juracán was present at the beginning of the world, when only the sky (Turey) and the still-unformed sea (the 'first water') existed; Juracán's winds stirred the waters into form, sculpting the islands, mountains, and valleys of the Greater Antilles. Separately from Guabancex (who was the 'infuriated' manifestation of the storm), Juracán is the more abstract, cosmic principle of cyclonic wind — the raw force of meteorological transformation. The Taíno observed the approach of hurricanes by reading the behavior of the sea, the flight of birds, and the clouds; their word hurákān passed into Spanish, then into the European languages, as the universal term for these tropical cyclones. Juracán was neither purely evil nor good — like all natural forces, he was powerful and ambivalent, capable of destruction but also of clearing the way for new growth and of revealing the hidden geography of the islands.
