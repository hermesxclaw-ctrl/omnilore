Who It Is

In a world where the sky still glows from the fires of a colony drop, a fifteen-year-old scavenger who has never seen a Newtype steals the most powerful weapon left on Earth — not because he believes in destiny, but because a girl in a white dress whispered for help inside his head. Garrod Ran is Gundam's most deliberately ordinary hero: no psychic powers, no military training, no noble bloodline, just stubborn optimism and a stolen Gundam that can call down a beam from the moon.

Attested across episodes 1-39: brash, impulsive, fiercely optimistic, and emotionally direct. Raised as an orphan Vulture in the wasteland, he is street-smart, mechanically handy, and unafraid to bluff. Unlike many Gundam protagonists, he is not introspective or traumatized at the start; he masks insecurity with bravado and flirts openly (especially with Tiffa). Core traits: absolute refusal to abandon Tiffa, distrust of abstract ideals like 'Newtype supremacy,' and belief that 'you decide who you are.' Grows from selfish scavenger ('I'll sell the Gundam')

Powers
Mobile Suit Piloting - General

Exceptionally talented Oldtype pilot despite no formal training. Masters the GX-9900 Gundam X within days, later adapts to GX-9900-DV Gundam X Divider (close-combat loadout without Satellite Cannon) and GX-9901-DX Gundam Double X (twin Satellite Cannon). Demonstrates high spatial awareness, reflexes, and improvisation in debris-filled ruins and desert terrain.

Limit: No innate Newtype precognition; relies on visual tracking and Tiffa's warnings. Early episodes show poor energy management and over-reliance on Satellite Cannon. Cannot operate Flash System alone.

Satellite Cannon Operation via Proxy Flash System

The Gundam X's signature weapon requires a Newtype's Flash System to request microwave energy from the lunar D.O.M.E. solar power satellite. Garrod, as a non-Newtype, cannot trigger it alone. He fires it by having Tiffa Adill (or later Jamil Neate) act as a Newtype conduit — Tiffa senses Garrod's will and relays the request. Later the Double X's Twin Satellite Cannon uses the same principle but with enhanced G-Controller built from Jamil's data.

Limit: Requires clear line-of-sight to moon, several minutes charging, reflector panels must deploy leaving unit immobile and vulnerable, and a willing Newtype co-operator. Cannot fire during cloudy/overcast conditions or underground. If Tiffa is incapacitated or unwilling, weapon is unusable. Divider form removes cannon entirely.

Vulture Survival and Mechanical Improvisation

Scavenging, jury-rigging, desert navigation, bartering, and hand-to-hand brawling learned as a freelance Vulture. Can repair and hotwire mobile suits, pilot Vulture hovercrafts, and survive alone in wasteland. Shown disassembling MS-06 Borjarnon parts and maintaining Gundam X with Kid Salsamille's help.

Limit: Not an engineer on Kid's level; needs Kid for major overhauls. Survival skills are practical, not superhuman.

Interpersonal Charisma and Moral Influence

Unusually persuasive for a Gundam lead: convinces Tiffa to trust him, reforms Ennil El, earns loyalty of Roybea, Witz, and even former enemy Carris Nautilus. His refusal to accept Newtype determinism directly influences Tiffa, Jamil, and ultimately D.O.M.E.'s judgment on humanity. Narrative power rather than supernatural.

Limit: Charisma fails with Frost brothers (Shagia and Olba), who view his idealism as naïve. Does not prevent tragedy (e.g., Carris's injuries).

Gundam Double X Twin Satellite Cannon and G-Falcon Integration

In final arc, pilots Gundam Double X which can dock with G-Falcon support fighter for extended flight and fire twin linked Satellite Cannons. Requires synchronized Newtype support (Tiffa + Jamil's recorded brainwaves via G-Controller). Demonstrated to destroy the colony laser and damage the New UNE's space fortress.

Limit: Same lunar dependency plus greater energy demand; twin firing risks overload. G-Falcon docking requires skilled support pilot (Pala Sys).

Accounts I
The Vulture and the Stolen Gundam - Encounter with TiffaAfter War Gundam X Episodes 1-3 'Is the Moon Out?' / 'Nice to Meet You, Tiffa' / 'My Mount is Dangerous'

After War 0015 (in-universe); broadcast 1996

In the wasteland, Vulture Garrod Ran is hired by rival Vultures to capture a Newtype girl held by the Alternative Company. Infiltrating their base, he finds Tiffa Adill in a cell and hears her telepathic plea. He steals the mysterious GX-9900 Gundam X to escape, accidentally triggering its Satellite Cannon when Tiffa unconsciously guides the Flash System — wiping out an island. Pursued by mercenary Witz Sou and Roybea Loy, Garrod refuses to hand Tiffa over for money, choosing to protect her despite having just met her. Establishes Garrod's core motivation (protect Tiffa, not profit) and the Gundam X's devastating power. Leads to his recruitment onto the Freeden. Introduces the series' central theme: Oldtype protecting Newtype rather than exploiting her.

Joining the Freeden - Becoming Jamil's PupilAfter War Gundam X Episodes 4-10

After War 0015

Garrod is captured by the land battleship Freeden, captained by ex-Newtype Jamil Neate. Jamil, who lost his Newtype abilities and his lover Lucille Liliard in the 7th Space War, sees his younger self in Garrod and takes him as a pupil despite Garrod's lack of discipline. Garrod clashes with crew, fails his first formal mission, and learns he cannot fire the Satellite Cannon without Tiffa. Episode 10 'I Am You, You Are Me' forces Garrod to confront that Tiffa's visions are not convenience but trauma. Transition from lone Vulture to team pilot. Jamil becomes surrogate father/mentor; Garrod learns restraint and that power requires responsibility. Freeden becomes his home.

Ennil El - The Stalker in the WastelandAfter War Gundam X Episodes 11-15, especially Ep.13 'Think, My Gun is Calling'

After War 0015

Vulture pilot Ennil El, a young woman abandoned by her lover and obsessed with Garrod after he spares her, stalks him across the wasteland in her custom Jenice and later Correl. She attempts to kill Tiffa out of jealousy, then tries to force Garrod to need her. Garrod defeats her without killing her and ultimately offers her a chance to leave the cycle of revenge. She later redeems herself by saving the Freeden. Major character development for Garrod: first time he must deal with someone whose love is possessive and violent, contrasting his protective love for Tiffa. Ennil's redemption arc becomes a recurring ally thread. Content advisory: stalking, attempted murder-suicide.

Accounts II
Carris Nautilus and the Ghost of the Newtype LabAfter War Gundam X Episodes 16-23 'Believe in My Power' through 'My Dream is Real'

After War 0015

At the abandoned Newtype Laboratory, the Freeden encounters Carris Nautilus, a 15-year-old artificial Newtype who pilots the RMSN-008 Bertigo, a mobile armor that overwhelms Garrod through superior psychic control. Carris believes Newtypes must lead humanity and sees Garrod as an Oldtype relic. Garrod, stripped of his Satellite Cannon (Gundam X is heavily damaged and refitted into the Divider), must win without his superweapon. Using pure piloting and Tiffa's emotional support rather than psychic power, he defeats Carris and convinces him that being human matters more than category. Carris survives crippled and later aids the Freeden. Gundam X is destroyed and rebuilt as Gundam X Divider (no Satellite Cannon), forcing Garrod to grow as a pilot without a crutch. Thematically central: Garrod proves Oldtype will can match Newtype power. Carris becomes an ally and foreshadows the Frost brothers' ideology.

Fort Severn and the Frost Brothers - The Category F TrapAfter War Gundam X Episodes 24-32

After War 0015

The Freeden heads to Fort Severn, a New UNE base where Newtypes are classified. The Frost brothers — Shagia and Olba Frost, twins classified as 'Category F' (pseudo-Newtypes who register as Newtypes but cannot use Flash System) — manipulate both sides. They resent being denied Newtype status and plan to restart war to prove the system wrong. Garrod is captured, tortured psychologically, and told Tiffa would be better off without him. He escapes by rejecting their determinism: 'Tiffa is Tiffa, not a category.' The Freeden is forced to scuttle and crew transfers to Freeden II. Reveals the Frost brothers as final antagonists and critiques Newtype essentialism. Fort Severn's fall pushes the story into space. Garrod's Gundam X Divider is further damaged, leading to acquisition of Gundam Double X.

D.O.M.E. and the Moon Will Always Be There - The Truth of NewtypesAfter War Gundam X Episodes 33-39 (Final Arc)

After War 0015

In space, Garrod pilots the newly restored GX-9901-DX Gundam Double X, built from the original X's spare parts and Jamil's G-Controller. At the lunar base D.O.M.E. (Depths Of Mind Elevating), the party learns the truth: D.O.M.E. is the preserved brain of the first Newtype, who declares Newtypes are not a new species but a human potential that war perverted. The Frost brothers attempt to fire a colony laser to force humanity to evolve through war. Garrod, Jamil, Tiffa, and Carris unite; Garrod and Tiffa together fire the Double X's Twin Satellite Cannon to destroy the laser. In the final episode, D.O.M.E. asks Garrod — as an Oldtype who loves a Newtype — whether humanity should be allowed to continue. Garrod answers that people can understand each other without being Newtypes. D.O.M.E. spares humanity and the Satellite System is shut down. Series conclusion: Garrod's philosophy — that understanding is a choice, not a psychic power — is validated by the first Newtype itself. Garrod is a Newtype like Amuro or Kamille. — He is explicitly an Oldtype. His ability to fire the Satellite Cannon comes entirely from Tiffa Adill (or Jamil) acting as a psychic conduit. The series' central theme is that an Oldtype can protect and understand a Newtype without being one.

Gundam X is part of the Universal Century timeline. — After War Gundam X is an Alternate Universe (AU) with its own calendar (After War). It reuses UC concepts (Newtypes, Gundams) but is not connected to UC chronology. The 7th Space War is not the One Year War.

Garrod stole the Gundam X because he wanted to be a hero. — He stole it initially for money — Vultures offered a bounty for the Gundam and for Tiffa. He only decides to protect Tiffa after hearing her telepathic plea and seeing her exploitation. His heroism is accidental and then chosen, not innate.
