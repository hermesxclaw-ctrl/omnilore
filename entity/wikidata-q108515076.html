Who It Is

From a Norman valley town on the Meuse to the Florida Territory, from a gallows at Tyburn to a billboard in Vice City — the name Duval has traveled farther than any single family. Literally 'of the valley,' it is not one lineage but a topographic label taken up independently by French peasants, Norman knights recorded in the Domesday Book, and their descendants across five continents, now carried by governors, highwaymen, suffragettes, and a Grand Theft Auto protagonist.

No inherent personality trait. Onomastic studies note the name's high frequency made it a 'generic French surname' in Anglophone fiction to signal French origin without specific regional marking. Bearers span the full social spectrum, from aristocratic magistrates (Jean-Jacques Duval d'Eprémesnil) to anarchist burglars (Clément Duval).

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Toponymic surname from Old French du val 'of the valley' (Latin vallis 'valley'). In Norman and Picard forms, also associated specifically with the town of Déville (Seine-Maritime, formerly Deville, Ardennes in some sources) — hence the early Norman form 'Devall' / 'de Eyvill'. The preposition + art

Pronunciation — French: /dy.val/ (doo-VAHL), two syllables, final 'l' pronounced; Anglicized: /duːˈvɔːl/ (doo-VAWL) or /ˈdjuːvəl/ (JEW-vəl) in North America, especially as Duvall. Norman medieval Latin renderings: de Valle, de Eyvilla.

Aliases — DuVal, Duvall, Deval, Devall, Deville, DeVille

Ownership — French and Norman origin, with parallel establishment in England after 1066. Widely diffused in Francophonie (France, Quebec, Haiti, Mauritius, Belgium, Louisiana) and Anglophonie

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a surname, Duval has no corporeal appearance. In heraldic tradition, multiple unrelated Duval/Duvall families bore distinct coats of arms. Example: Norman Duval d'Eprémesnil arms - Azure, three fleurs-de-lis Or (variant). English Duvall of Dorset - Gules, a fess between three cinquefoils Argent. Symbols — Toponymic iconography centers on the valley (vallis): heraldic charges occasionally include chevrons representing hills, wavy fesses for rivers in valleys, or towers for Déville. No religious or mythic symbol intrinsically attached. In popular culture, the name evokes Frenchness/Creole/Louisiana associations (e.g., Duval County, Florida, named for William Pope Duval; Duval Street in Key West).

Claude Duval, the Gentleman HighwaymanWilliam Pope, Memoirs of Monsieur Du Vall (1670); Alexander Smith, History of the Lives of the Most Noted Highway-men (1714); contemporary news pamphlets and trial records

1643-1670 (Restoration England)

Born in Domfront, Normandy, Claude Duval came to England as a boy in the household of the Duke of Richmond. After the Civil War he became a notorious highwayman on the roads around London, famed for gallantry toward women and speaking French. Arrested for multiple robberies, he was hanged at Tyburn on 21 January 1670 aged 27. His romanticized legend grew immediately after death, with ballads and a mock memoir presenting him as a chivalrous robber in contrast to brutal contemporaries. Established the archetype of the 'gallant highwayman' in English folklore; later literary recycling in Harrison Ainsworth's Rookwood (1834) and popular idiom 'to dance with Duval' for robbery.

William Pope Duval and Florida TerritoryUS Territorial Papers; James D. Horan, The McIntosh and Weatherford Creek Indian Leaders; Florida Historical Quarterly

1784-1854, governorship 1822-1834

Virginia-born lawyer William Pope Duval, veteran of the War of 1812, was appointed by President Monroe as first civilian governor of Florida Territory after its cession from Spain. He established Tallahassee as capital (choosing a midpoint between Pensacola and St. Augustine), negotiated uneasily with Seminole leaders, and presided over the territory's early legal code. Washington Irving fictionalized him as 'Ralph Ringwood' and 'the Governor' in his frontier sketches. Duval County (Jacksonville) and Duval Street (Key West) named for him; his sons Burr (killed at Goliad, 1836) and John C. (author of Early Times in Texas, 1892) extended family lore into Texas Revolution literature.

Elsie and Una Duval - Suffragette Hunger StrikersSuffragette Fellowship Collection (Museum of London); Emmeline Pankhurst, My Own Story (1914); Home Office prison records

1908-1919

Sisters Elsie Duval (1892-1919) and Una Duval (1879-1975, née Dugdale) were militant members of the Women's Social and Political Union. Elsie was imprisoned three times, hunger-struck, forcibly fed, and smuggled from Holloway under the Cat and Mouse Act. Una, married to Victor Duval, founded the Animal Defence Society connection and was also imprisoned. Elsie died at 27 from heart failure attributed to force-feeding damage. Became emblematic martyrs for suffragette militancy; Elsie's letters and Una's later marriage reform activism cited in suffrage historiography.

Accounts II
Jeanne Duval, Baudelaire's Black VenusCharles Baudelaire, Les Fleurs du mal (1857) and letters; Jeanne Duval biographical research by Michaël Pakenham and François Livi

c.1820-c.1862, relationship 1842-1860s

Jeanne Duval (c.1820-1862), Haitian-born actress and dancer in Paris, was the long-term mistress of poet Charles Baudelaire from 1842. Addressed as the 'Vénus noire' in poems including 'Le Balcon,' 'Parfum exotique,' and 'Sed non satiata,' their tumultuous relationship involved financial support, jealousy, illness (syphilis), and eventual estrangement. She later lived in poverty, possibly with Baudelaire's support until his death. Central to Baudelaire scholarship on race, exoticism, and modernity; feminist and postcolonial re-readings debate whether Baudelaire exoticized or humanized her; very few direct records by Duval herself survive.

Clément Duval and IllegalismClément Duval, Memorie autobiografiche (1929, published as Outrage); Richard Parry, The Bonnot Gang (1987); French judicial archives

1850-1935, burglary 1886, trial 1887

Clément Duval (1850-1935), wounded veteran of the Franco-Prussian War turned anarchist, committed a burglary in Paris in 1886 during which he stabbed policeman Rossignol. At his 1887 trial he declared 'La propriété, c'est le vol' and justified theft as reparation. Sentenced to death, commuted to life in the penal colony of Devil's Island (Cayenne), he escaped after 14 years and fled to New York, where he wrote memoirs advocating 'illegalism' — the doctrine that theft from the bourgeoisie is legitimate. Became a foundational figure for anarcho-illegalism influencing the Bonnot Gang; his case cited in debates on penal colony reform.

Maria Duval and the Psychic Mail FraudUS Department of Justice, FTC complaints; US Postal Inspection Service case 2016; CNN, The Guardian investigative reporting 2014-2018

1990s-2016 (peak 2000s)

Maria Carolina Gamba, operating as 'Maria Duval' (Italian-born psychic, b. 1930s), licensed her name and image to a mass-mailing astrology scam. Letters promising personalized psychic help for a fee were sent to millions, disproportionately elderly, in the US, Europe, and Australia, generating an estimated $200 million. US authorities shut down the operation in 2016; Duval herself claimed she was also exploited by contractors, while investigators found she profited directly. Led to US-Canada-Europe consumer protection actions and the 2018 settlement requiring restitution; became case study in psychic fraud and elder exploitation.

Duval means 'of the devil' or is related to 'du Devil' — No relation to devil (French diable). The val element is Latin vallis 'valley', not diabolus. Confusion arises from English misreading of Duval and variant Devill/Divell spellings which are scribal variants of valley, not devil.

All Duvals descend from one noble Norman family recorded in Domesday — The Domesday de Eyvill tenants represent one Norman lineage, but the surname's high frequency in France indicates many independent adoptions by unrelated valley dwellers. Most modern Duvals have no demonstrable link to the Domesday family.

The psychic Maria Duval was a genuine clairvoyant persecuted by skeptics — Investigations by US DOJ, FTC, and French authorities found no evidence of psychic ability and documented a mass-mail fraud operation that purchased victim lists and sent identical 'personalized' letters. She was not merely a face but a beneficiary and licensor.

Duval is exclusively French and always pronounced 'Doo-VAHL' — While French origin, the name has been fully nativized in English, Spanish, and Creole contexts with anglicized/hispanicized pronunciations (Doo-VAWL, Doo-VAHL, JEW-vəl). Bearers in Mauritius, Haiti, Mexico, and the US may not identify as French.
