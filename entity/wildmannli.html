Who It Is

Die Wildmannli (also Wilde Männli — 'Little Wild Men') are small, shaggy, dwarf-like creatures from Swiss Alpine folklore, particularly in the Bernese Oberland, Valais, and Graubünden regions. They are described as tiny humanoids covered in thick, shaggy fur or mossy hair, with long beards, small horns, and wearing simple clothing made of leaves and animal skins. Wildmannli live in high mountain caves and rock crevices above the treeline, emerging in summer when the snow melts. They are fundamentally benevolent toward humans: they help alpine herders by guiding lost cows back to the barn, mending broken fences, churning butter, and protecting livestock from bears and wolves. In return, they ask only for a small portion of milk, cheese, or bread left at the cave entrance. Wildmannli are particularly known for their love of music — if a herder plays an alpenhorn or a zither, the Wildmannli will come out to dance and reward the musician with cheese or gold. However, they are intensely shy and will never show themselves fully to humans, working only at night or when no one is looking. If a human tries to capture a Wildmannli or steal its tools (especially its tiny ax or milk pail), the creature will disappear forever, and the cows will stop producing milk. In some traditions, the Wildmannli are believed to be the spirits of pagan Alpine peoples who retreated into the mountains when Christianity came, or the remnants of an ancient pre-human race. They share features with the Scandinavian Tomte, the German Kobold, and the British brownie. The Wildmannli are distinct from the Wild Man (Wilder Mann) of German tradition, who is a solitary, giant, hairy figure — the Wildmannli are specifically small, communal, and helpful.
