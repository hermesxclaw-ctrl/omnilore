Who It Is

An ancient turtle-like Pokemon that crystallizes the energy of the Terastal phenomenon. In its Stellar Form, Terapagos becomes a massive, crystalline entity with a shell that resembles a planet surrounded by rings of pure Terastal energy. It is the source of the Terastal phenomenon that defines Paldea's unique Pokemon battles.

The Approach

When the Terastal phenomenon reaches its absolute zenith, the ancient turtle Terapagos unveils its true form—a crystalline being whose shell becomes a crown of shimmering jewels, drawing the very light of the stars themselves. This is the Stellar Form, a state that transcends the ordinary types and harnesses the raw, elemental energy of Paldea itself.

Etymology

'Terapagos' likely derives from 'Terastal' (the phenomenon) and 'Galapagos' (the islands), referencing its turtle-like appearance and connection to the Terastal phenomenon. The 'Stellar' form name references its association with the Stellar type and its radiant, star-like appearance.

Cultural Ownership

The Pokémon franchise is owned by Nintendo, Game Freak, and Creatures Inc. Terapagos is a fictional creature from the Pokémon series, specifically from the ninth generation of games.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans have speculated that Terapagos is the 'Hidden Treasure of Area Zero' mentioned in the DLC's title, but this is not officially confirmed. The Stellar Form is one of two forms of Terapagos; the other is its Normal Form. There is no dispute about its existence, but its exact nature and connection to the Terastal phenomenon are subject to fan interpretation.

Description

Appearance

In its Stellar Form, Terapagos becomes a large, majestic turtle with a crystalline shell that resembles a crown or a cluster of jewels. Its body is predominantly white and blue, with glowing, star-like patterns. The shell is adorned with a large, central crystal and multiple smaller crystals that emit a radiant, multicolored light. Its eyes glow with a similar energy, and its overall appearance is ethereal and otherworldly.

Symbols & Iconography

The Stellar Form is associated with the Terastal phenomenon, and its design incorporates star motifs, crystals, and a crown-like shell, symbolizing its role as a 'king' or 'origin' of the phenomenon. The Stellar type is represented by a special Tera Jewel that is rainbow-colored, and Terapagos's Stellar Form is the only Pokémon that can use the Stellar-type Terastallization.

Behaviour & Personality

Terapagos is an ancient and powerful Pokémon, described as having existed since the beginning of the world. It is said to be peaceful and wise, but it will defend itself if threatened. In the game, it is encountered as a boss battle, where it uses its Stellar Form to overwhelming effect. It is implied to be the source of the Terastal phenomenon, and it has a deep connection to Area Zero.

Domains & Habitats

Terapagos is found in Area Zero, a mysterious and dangerous region in Paldea. The Stellar Form is only seen during the final battle with Terapagos, after it absorbs the energy of the Tera Orbs. It is not found in the wild in this form; it must be transformed.

Version Differences

Terapagos has two forms: Normal Form and Stellar Form. The Normal Form is a small, blue turtle with a hexagonal shell. The Stellar Form is significantly larger and more powerful. The Stellar Form is only available in Pokémon Scarlet and Violet after completing the main story and the DLC, and it is the only form that can be used in battle with the Stellar type.

Powers & Abilities

Stellar Type

Source Version Pokémon Scarlet and Violet: The Indigo Disk

Description

Terapagos Stellar Form is the only Pokémon that can have the Stellar type, which is a special type that is not a traditional type but rather a Tera Type. When Terapagos Terastallizes into its Stellar Form, it gains a boost to all its moves, and it can change its Tera Type to any type, but only once per battle.

Limitations

The Stellar type is not a permanent type; it is a Tera Type. Terapagos must Terastallize to access it. Also, the ability to change Tera Type is limited to once per battle.

Contradictions

None known, as it is a unique mechanic.

Tera Shift

Source Version Pokémon Scarlet and Violet: The Indigo Disk

Description

Terapagos has the ability Tera Shift, which automatically Terastallizes it into its Stellar Form when it enters battle.

Limitations

This ability only works once per battle, and it requires Terapagos to be in its Normal Form. After Terastallizing, it remains in Stellar Form for the rest of the battle.

Contradictions

None known.

Tera Starstorm

Source Version Pokémon Scarlet and Violet: The Indigo Disk

Description

A signature move that Terapagos can learn. It deals damage and is super effective against all Pokémon that have Terastallized. Its power is boosted by the Stellar type.

Limitations

This move is only available to Terapagos in its Stellar Form. It uses the user's higher attacking stat.

Contradictions

None known.

High Base Stats

Source Version Pokémon Scarlet and Violet: The Indigo Disk

Description

Terapagos Stellar Form has exceptionally high base stats, particularly in Special Attack and Special Defense, making it a formidable opponent.

Limitations

Its base stats are fixed and cannot be modified except through EVs and IVs.

Contradictions

None known.

Contradictions & Disputes

Terapagos's role as the source of the Terastal phenomenon

Position A

The game states that Terapagos is the source of the Terastal phenomenon.

Position A — evidence

In-game dialogue from the DLC, where characters explain that Terapagos's power is the origin of the phenomenon.

Position B

Some fans theorize that the phenomenon may have been caused by the Tera Orbs or by Area Zero's energy, not directly by Terapagos.

Position B — evidence

Speculation based on the fact that the Tera Orbs are used to Terastallize Pokémon, and Area Zero is filled with Tera crystals.

Scholarly Consensus

There is no scholarly consensus, as this is a fictional creature. The official canon is that Terapagos is the source.
