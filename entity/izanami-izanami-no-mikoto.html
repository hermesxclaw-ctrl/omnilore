Who It Is

Izanami is the primal female creator kami of Japanese myth, the sister-wife of Izanagi who with him created the Japanese archipelago and gave birth to the first generation of kami. She died giving birth to the fire kami Kagutsuchi — her body was so badly burned that she descended to Yomi, the land of the dead. When Izanagi followed to retrieve her, he found her putrefied body covered in maggots, pursued by the hags of the underworld and the thunder kami that had grown from her rotting flesh. In her shame and rage at being seen in this state, she pursued Izanagi but failed to catch him before he sealed the entrance to Yomi with a massive boulder. From behind the boulder, Izanami declared she would kill 1,000 people a day; Izanagi responded he would ensure 1,500 births a day to counter her. Izanami is thus both the mother goddess who created life and the queen of the dead who calls souls to the underworld. She represents the cycle of birth and death — the inevitable end that comes from the same source as life. She is worshipped as a dread goddess of death at some shrines near the supposed entrance to Yomi.

Origins

Izanami was born as the seventh generation of heavenly kami alongside Izanagi. She gave birth to the islands of Japan and numerous kami. When she bore Kagutsuchi (fire), she was fatally burned. She descended to Yomi, where she was transformed into a rotting corpse-goddess, and was sealed in the underworld by Izanagi.
