Who It Is

In the beginning, when the world was only a brine-soaked void beneath a floating bridge of heaven, two deities stood upon that bridge and stirred the sea with a jeweled spear. From the dripping salt, an island was born, and from that island, all of Japan. Yet the same goddess who gave birth to the archipelago would also give birth to death itself, and her descent into the shadowy land of Yomi would split the world of the living from the world of the dead forever. This is the story of Izanami, the Female-Who-Invites, whose love and wrath shaped the cosmos.

Izanami is portrayed as a creator goddess who is proactive in creation, but also as a figure who becomes wrathful and vengeful after being abandoned by Izanagi. She is determined and assertive, as seen in her decision to eat the food of the underworld and her curse to kill 1,000 people a day.

Powers
Creation

Izanami, along with Izanagi, created the Japanese archipelago and many deities.

Limit: None stated

Death and Underworld Dominion

After her death, Izanami became the ruler of Yomi, the land of the dead. She has the power to cause death to the living, as seen in her curse to kill 1,000 people per day.

Limit: She cannot leave Yomi because she has eaten the food of the underworld.

Cursing

Izanami cursed the living to die at a rate of 1,000 per day.

Limit: Izanagi countered by promising to give life to 1,500 per day.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Kojiki, Izanami is described as beautiful and graceful before her death. After her descent into Yomi, she becomes a rotting form of flesh with maggots and foul creatures crawling over her body. In art, she is often depicted as a beautiful woman in traditional Japanese attire, sometimes with a comb in her hair.

Symbols — Izanami is associated with the jeweled spear (Amenonuhoko), the pillar of heaven (Ame-no-mihashira), and the palace Yahiro-dono. In the underworld, she is linked to the peach that Izanagi threw to drive away demons, and the boulder (Chibiki no iwa) that separates the living and dead.

Domains — Izanami is the goddess of both creation and death. She resides in Yomi, the shadowy land of the dead, after her death. Before that, she lived on Onogoroshima and later on the island of Awaji, according to the Nihon Shoki.

Accounts I
Creation of the Japanese ArchipelagoKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (mythological)

Izanagi and Izanami, the last of seven generations of primordial deities, were tasked with creating the first land. They used the jeweled spear Amenonuhoko to churn the sea from the floating bridge of heaven, and the dripping salt formed Onogoroshima. They descended to this island, built a pillar and palace, and after a failed first marriage ritual, they successfully united and gave birth to the Great Eight Islands of Japan and many deities. The Japanese archipelago is created, and the progenitors of the imperial line are born.

Accounts II
Death of Izanami and Descent to YomiKojiki

Age of the Gods (mythological)

Izanami died giving birth to the fire deity Kagu-tsuchi. Izanagi, grief-stricken, followed her to Yomi. He found her but she had already eaten the food of the underworld and could not leave. When Izanagi lit a torch and saw her rotting body, he fled in horror. Izanami, enraged, sent demons and foul women to chase him. Izanagi escaped by throwing a peach, which drove them away, and sealed the entrance to Yomi with a boulder. Izanami then declared she would kill 1,000 people per day, and Izanagi responded by promising to give life to 1,500. The separation of the living and dead realms, the origin of death, and the purification ritual (harai) performed by Izanagi.

Birth of the Three Noble DeitiesKojiki

Age of the Gods (mythological)

After returning from Yomi, Izanagi performed a purification ritual. From his left eye, Amaterasu (sun goddess) was born; from his right eye, Tsukuyomi (moon god); and from his nose, Susanoo (storm god). The creation of the highest deities in the Shinto pantheon, and the establishment of purification rituals.

Izanami is a shinigami (death god) in the same sense as modern fictional portrayals. — Izanami is a goddess of creation and death, but the concept of shinigami as a distinct class of death gods is largely a modern invention. In mythology, she is the ruler of Yomi, but she does not personally reap souls.

Izanami and Izanagi are purely mythological and have no connection to the imperial family. — In Shinto belief, they are considered the direct ancestors of the Japanese imperial family, as Amaterasu is their descendant and the imperial line claims descent from Amaterasu.

The peach that Izanagi threw was a magical weapon unique to the myth. — The peach is a Chinese-inspired amulet fruit, and the narrative reflects Chinese influence on Japanese mythology.
