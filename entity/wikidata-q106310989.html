Who It Is

In the celestial courts of Chinese myth, three sisters guard the very gate through which every soul enters the world. Qiongxiao, the middle of the Three Celestial Maidens, once wielded a golden gourd that could capture immortals and a pair of scissors that could sever a dragon's spine—yet her greatest legacy is the quiet, unyielding power of birth itself, a power so profound that even gods must bow before it.

In the novel, Qiongxiao is portrayed as emotionally expressive and loyal to her brother. She is the first to urge revenge, weeps openly at her brother's death, and nearly faints from grief. She is also depicted as a capable warrior, more agile than her sisters. In folk religion, she is a benevolent goddess who protects women and children.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Qiongxiao (瓊宵) combines 'qiong' (瓊), meaning 'jade' or 'precious', and 'xiao' (宵), meaning 'night' or 'firmament'. The title Niangniang (娘娘) is an honorific for goddesses, often translated as 'Lady' or 'Madam'. The full title can be rendered as 'Lady of the Jade Firmament'.

Powers
Hunyuan Gold Gourd (混元金斗)

A powerful treasure that can capture beings and strip them of their cultivation, reducing their spiritual power. In the novel, it is used to capture Chan Sect immortals.

Limit: Can be overcome by higher-level deities such as Yuanshi Tianzun and Laozi, who dismantle the formation and neutralize the gourd.

Golden Dragon Scissors (金蛟剪)

A pair of scissors that can transform into two golden dragons to attack enemies. It is a lethal weapon capable of killing powerful beings.

Limit: Not effective against the highest-level immortals; the weapon is ultimately destroyed or rendered useless when the formation is broken.

Martial agility

Qiongxiao is depicted as particularly agile in battle, allowing her to avoid injuries that strike her sisters.

Limit: Despite her agility, she is ultimately killed by Xianhe Tongzi wielding the Three Treasures Wish-Granting Scepter.

Fertility and childbirth blessing

In folk religion, the Three Celestial Maidens, including Qiongxiao, are believed to grant children, ensure safe pregnancies, and protect infants.

Limit: These powers are not explicitly attributed to Qiongxiao individually in the novel, but are a later development in worship.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The provided source does not give a detailed physical description of Qiongxiao. In traditional iconography, she is often depicted as a beautiful woman in celestial robes, sometimes with a swan as her mount. In the novel, she is described as agile and martial. Specific visual details vary by temple and artist.

Symbols — Key symbols include the Hunyuan Gold Gourd (混元金斗), a gourd that can capture and strip immortals of their cultivation; the Golden Dragon Scissors (金蛟剪), a pair of scissors that can be transformed into dragons to attack enemies; and the swan, her divine mount. In folk worship, the gourd also symbolizes childbirth, as it is believed all beings pass through it before birth.

Domains — Qiongxiao resides on Sanxian Island (三仙岛) with her sisters in the celestial realm. In local folklore, she is associated with Qiongxiao Mountain on Mount Taibai in Shaanxi, where she is said to have been transformed after her defeat.

Accounts I
The Vengeance of the Three SistersInvestiture of the Gods

Ming dynasty (16th century)

After their brother Zhao Gongming is killed by Lu Ya Zhenren, Qiongxiao is the first to urge her sister Yunxiao to avenge him. When his body is returned, she weeps and nearly faints. The sisters then join the Shang forces and set up the Grand Nine-Curves Yellow River Formation to capture Chan Sect immortals. The formation captures several immortals and strips the Twelve Golden Immortals of much of their cultivation. Yuanshi Tianzun and Laozi intervene, dismantle the formation, and kill the sisters. After the Zhou victory, Jiang Ziya appoints them as the 'Ganying Suishi Xiangu' (感应随世仙姑), setting the stage for their later worship.

Accounts II
The Transformation into MountainsLocal folklore in Shaanxi

Unknown

Local tradition holds that after the sisters were defeated in the Battle of the Nine-Curves Yellow River Formation, they were transformed into three peaks on Mount Taibai: Qiongxiao Mountain, Yunxiao Mountain, and Bixiao Mountain. The peaks face the main summit, Baxiantai, in a bowing posture, symbolizing their submission. The peaks become a local landmark and a site of worship, honoring the sisters' mythological significance.

Qiongxiao is a purely fictional character with no religious significance. — While she originated in a novel, she and her sisters have been integrated into Chinese folk religion and Daoist ritual as deities of childbirth and fertility.

The Three Celestial Maidens are the same as Zhusheng Niangniang and Songzi Niangniang. — They are often merged in practice, but they are distinct deities with different origins and iconography.
