Who It Is

A name that echoes through centuries, Asterius belongs to many—a legendary king, a feared monster, and a forgotten saint. But who was the real Asterius? The answer may be as elusive as the labyrinth he once guarded.

The Minotaur is depicted as a savage, man-eating creature, confined to the labyrinth. The king Asterion is a wise ruler, while the giant Asterion is a fierce opponent. Christian saints are portrayed as pious and martyred.

Aliases — Asterion, Asterius (Minotaur), Asterius of Crete

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Minotaur Asterius is typically depicted with the body of a man and the head of a bull, though some ancient art shows a fully human figure with a bull's head or a bull's body with a human head. The king Asterion of Crete is described as a handsome mortal, while the giant Asterion is said to be a son of the river god or a giant with a hundred arms. Christian saints named Asterius have no specific physical descriptions.

Symbols — The labyrinth is the primary symbol associated with the Minotaur. In art, the Minotaur is often shown in combat with Theseus. The name 'Asterius' itself evokes stars, but no specific star-related iconography is attached to the mythological figures.

Domains — The Minotaur dwells in the labyrinth of Crete, built by Daedalus. King Asterion rules Crete. The giant Asterion is associated with various mythic locales. Saints are associated with their respective cities.

Accounts I
The Birth of the MinotaurBibliotheca (Pseudo-Apollodorus), Metamorphoses (Ovid)

Classical antiquity

King Minos of Crete prayed to Poseidon for a bull to sacrifice, but when a magnificent bull emerged from the sea, Minos kept it. As punishment, Poseidon made Pasiphaë, Minos's wife, fall in love with the bull. She coupled with it, giving birth to the Minotaur, a creature with a human body and a bull's head. Minos, ashamed, had Daedalus build the labyrinth to contain him. The Minotaur was imprisoned, and Athens was forced to send seven youths and seven maidens every nine years as tribute to be devoured.

Accounts II
Theseus and the MinotaurVarious (Plutarch, Ovid, etc.)

Classical antiquity

Theseus, son of King Aegeus of Athens, volunteered to be among the tribute to Crete. With the help of Minos's daughter Ariadne, who gave him a ball of thread, Theseus entered the labyrinth, slew the Minotaur, and found his way out. Theseus escaped with Ariadne, but later abandoned her on Naxos. He forgot to change his ship's sails from black to white, causing his father Aegeus to commit suicide.

The Martyrdom of Asterius of OstiaButler's Lives of the Saints

3rd century CE

Asterius of Ostia was a Christian martyr who was put to death during the persecution of Emperor Alexander Severus. He was killed for his faith, and his relics were venerated. He was recognized as a saint and martyr.
