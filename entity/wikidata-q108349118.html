Who It Is

They do not walk so much as they glide — three and a half metres of painted history swaying above the crowd, their huge heads turning slowly to survey the plaça. When the gralles strike up and the Gegants de Vilafranca del Penedès begin to dance, the whole Festa Major seems to breathe with them: children darting between their skirts, geganters sweating beneath the wooden frames, and a town that has carried giants on its shoulders for more than four centuries.

As objects, they have no autonomous personality, but performance conventions assign them dignified, slow, benevolent demeanour. Protocol: they bow (reverència) to authorities, to the church (Santa Maria), and to other gegants when meeting. They dance in prescribed figures (passeig, ball pla, contrapàs adapted for giants) and interact gently with the public - leaning to 'greet' children. Powers
Processional Presence and Civic Blessing

The primary 'power' is symbolic: their appearance legitimizes and inaugurates the festival. Their entry into the plaça signals the start of the Seguici and draws the community together. Historically, presence at Corpus Christi was believed to honour the Eucharist and display municipal prestige.

Limit: Effect is social-ritual, not supernatural; requires authorized performance and musical accompaniment; cannot appear without carriers and permission.

Ball de Gegants (Ritual Dance)

Choreographed dances to live gralla, tabal and brass: slow turns, bows, and figure-eight paths. The dance is the giants' 'animation', requiring coordination between two giants and musicians.

Limit: Limited by carrier endurance (15-30 kg frame, poor ventilation), heat, and crowd density; dance duration typically 5-10 minutes per performance with rests.

Intergenerational Transmission and Pedagogy

Through Gegantons and children's workshops, the giants transmit festive knowledge, dance steps, music and town identity to new generations. The colla gegantera functions as custodian school.

Limit: Dependent on volunteer recruitment and funding; knowledge is embodied and at risk if not documented.

Apotropaic and Communal Protection (Folk Attribution)

In older popular belief across Catalonia, gegants and other entremesos were sometimes ascribed apotropaic value - warding off evil and bringing good harvest or fortune by their imposing size and noise. No specific Vilafranca miracle is attested; this is a generalized folk interpretation reported by Amades.

Limit: Folk belief, not doctrinal; not actively claimed today; should be labelled as reconstructed interpretation.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As festive effigies, the Gegants de Vilafranca are 3 to 4 metres tall, constructed on a lightweight internal frame (historically wood and wicker, now aluminium and fibreglass) covered with papier-mâché / cartró pedra heads and hands, textile costumes, and a concealing skirt (faldilla) that hides the carrier (geganters) inside. The head is oversized with painted features, wig and crown or headdress; hands are enlarged and gesturing. Two principal

Symbols — Gegants embody civic identity and festive authority. They open the cercavila (processional parade) and preside over the Seguici Festiu, symbolizing the town itself, its history and its hospitality. Their royal dress evokes medieval town foundation and comital power (Vilafranca as royal town), not a specific mythological narrative. They carry no weapons; attributes are crowns, sceptres or floral elements. In iconography they are paired male/female

Domains — Domain is strictly festive-urban: streets and squares of Vilafranca del Penedès, especially Plaça de la Vila, Plaça de Santa Maria, La Rambla, and the route of the Seguici Festiu during Festa Major (29 August - 3 September, around Sant Fèlix). Habitat when not performing is storage: the casa dels gegants / local of the Agrupació de Geganters (Cal Figarot area) and occasionally VINSEUM displays. Accounts I
First Documented Gegants for Corpus and Festa Major (17th Century)Arxiu Comarcal de l'Alt Penedès, Fons Municipal, Llibres de Clavaria / Llibres d'Acords; cited in Grau i Pujol and Balanza; Generalitat Inventari notes 'documented since 17th c.'

Early modern, c. 1637-1700 (disputed exact year)

Municipal accounts record payments for making, dressing and carrying gegants for the Corpus Christi procession and later for the Festa Major de Sant Fèlix. Like elsewhere in Catalonia, the town council funded entremesos as part of civic-religious display, alongside the Àliga, Drac and Mulassa. The early giants were likely one pair, stored by the town and repaired annually. Their appearance marked Vilafranca's status as a vegueria capital. Established the gegants as a permanent element of the Seguici Festiu, creating a continuity that survives today. Provided template for later expansions of the festive retinue.

19th-Century Consolidation and Costume RenewalGrau i Pujol, La Festa Major de Vilafranca; press archives (Setmanari del Penedès) and municipal programmes; Amades comparative notes

19th century, especially 1850-1890

During the Renaixença revival of Catalan culture, Vilafranca's Festa Major was reorganized and the Seguici codified. The gegants received new costumes reflecting 19th-century historicist taste (velvet, crowns, ermine), and their dance was set to modern gralla repertoire. Photographs and programmes from the late 1800s show the pair presiding over the cercavila alongside Nans and Capgrossos. Fixed the visual identity that would be remembered as 'traditional' and photographed into the 20th century; reinforced Festa Major as the main annual ritual after Corpus declined.

Civil War, Suppression and Post-War Reconstruction (1936-1950s)Grau i Pujol; VINSEUM / Arxiu Comarcal oral histories; Ajuntament historical summary of Festa Major

1936-1955

Like many Catalan festive elements, the gegants suffered during the Civil War (1936-1939): figures were hidden, damaged or destroyed, and public Catalan festivals were suppressed or Hispanized under early Francoism. After 1939, the town slowly restored its Seguici. The Gegants Vells were repaired or remade in the 1940s-50s by local artisans using cartró pedra, reintroducing the giants to the plaça as a sign of cultural resilience, albeit under tight censorship of Catalan language and symbols. Physical survival of the tradition but with material discontinuity; current Gegants Vells are reconstructions, not original 17th-century objects. Story is central to local memory of cultural resistance.

Accounts II
Creation of the Gegants Nous and the Gegantons (Late 20th Century)Agrupació de Geganters de Vilafranca; Ajuntament Festa Major programmes 1980-2000; premsa comarcal

1980s-1990s

To preserve the fragile Gegants Vells and to allow more dynamic dancing, the town commissioned a second pair - the Gegants Nous - lighter and more robust, with new costumes. Simultaneously, Gegantons (child-sized giants) were created so that children could learn to be geganters. The new figures were baptized in a public ceremony (bateig) with godparent giants from other towns, following Catalan geganter custom, and quickly became beloved alongside the old pair. Doubled the town's giant heritage, reduced wear on historic figures, expanded participation to women and youth, and increased invitations to trobades de gegants across Catalonia.

Festa Major de Sant Fèlix Today: The Seguici Festiu and UNESCO-Adjacent RecognitionAjuntament de Vilafranca Festa Major 2023-2024 programmes; Generalitat declaration Festa patrimonial d'interès nacional 2010; UNESCO intangible heritage context for Catalan festive traditions

2010-present

Each 29 August - 1 September, the Gegants - both Vells and Nous - perform multiple sortides: the Cercavila, the Seguici Festiu, the Ball de Gegants in Plaça de la Vila, and the processó de Sant Fèlix. They dance to specially composed pieces, bow to the saint and to the Castellers de Vilafranca, and are carried by a mixed colla of men and women. In 2010 the Generalitat declared Vilafranca's Festa Major a Festa patrimonial d'interès nacional, explicitly citing its Seguici including gegants as intangible heritage. The giants are now central to the town's cultural tourism and live-streamed events. Institutional protection, funding for conservation, and international visibility; increased pressure to balance authenticity, safety and mass tourism.

Trobades and Inter-Town GodparenthoodCoordinadora de Geganters de Catalunya; Agrupació de Geganters de Vilafranca social media and diaries; local press

1990s-present (contemporary layer)

Beyond Vilafranca, the gegants travel (by van, not on foot) to trobades de gegants - gatherings where dozens of towns' giants meet, dance and are displayed. Vilafranca's giants have godparents (padrins) and godchildren (fillols) among other Penedès and Catalan giants, creating kinship networks documented in bateig certificates. These stories are not ancient myths but living social narratives that townspeople recount as part of the giants' biography. Creates inter-town festive kinship and a contemporary mythology of giant families, often mistaken by outsiders for ancient legend.

Gegants de Vilafranca are ancient mythological giants from Catalan legend, like giants in folktales who live in mountains. — They are 17th-century festive effigies made of papier-mâché and textiles, carried by people. While Catalonia does have mythological gegants in legends, the Vilafranca gegants are civic festive figures (imatgeria festiva), not characters from myth. Their 'biography' is the town's festival history.

The current Gegants are centuries old, original 17th-century figures. — The tradition is centuries old, but the physical figures are reconstructions. The present Gegants Vells are mid-20th-century remakes after Civil War damage, and Gegants Nous are late 20th-century creations. Materials are periodically renewed for safety and weight.

Gegants dance spontaneously or have supernatural powers. — Every movement is produced by a trained carrier inside the frame, dancing to live gralla music in prescribed choreography. The 'powers' are performative and symbolic - inaugurating the festival, embodying town identity - not supernatural abilities. Stamina, heat and visibility are real limitations.

All Catalan gegants are the same; Vilafranca's are identical to Barcelona's or Tarragona's. — Each town's gegants have distinct construction, costumes, music, dance and history. Vilafranca's are specifically tied to its Festa Major de Sant Fèlix, its Seguici Festiu, and its Penedès identity (wine capital, casteller town). They have their own godparent networks and costume colours.
