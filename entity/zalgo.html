Who It Is

You're reading a forum post when the text begins to bleed. Letters drip downward, melting into characters you don't recognize from any human alphabet. The screen darkens. You hear a sound from your speakers — not music, not noise, but a voice. Many voices. A chorus of something that exists in the spaces between words, in the static between radio stations, in the blind spot of your peripheral vision. The bleeding text forms a single name: ZALGO. And you understand, with a clarity that turns your bowels to water, that merely reading that name has been enough. You have been seen. You have been marked. He comes.

Zalgo is a creepypasta entity and internet meme that first appeared in 2004 on the Something Awful forums in a comic by artist 'Shmorky' (David Lovelace). The comic, titled 'The Unnamed Feeling,' featured a horrifying, multi-eyed entity spilling out of a portal behind a character named Z̤͈̙̟̩̀̌̊̅̽A̱̥̟̟̝̰̰̘̔̏̌̃̾̒̔̏̌̃L̯̘̭̲͇̲̻ͣ̈̾G̝̪̺̹̜̪̙̒̀̇O̲̝̭̯̞̼̺̰̺̒̑̾. The image and the entity were picked up by 4chan's /x/ board, where the 'Zalgo text' meme was born: corrupted, 'bleeding' text created by stacking diacritical marks (c̶̸̷̛̛̕ǫ̪̦̗̘̳̞̰m̡̌ȇ̥̙̬̘̪̱̺ s ee) that represents Zalgo's influence corrupting reality. Zalgo 'speaks' through this corrupted text, his words bleeding into the fabric of the page.

Zalgo is described as 'he who waits behind the wall' — an incomprehensible, world-devouring entity who will one day break through into our reality and corrupt everything. He is a god of chaos, corruption, and madness, similar to Lovecraft's Great Old Ones but filtered through the anarchic, collaborative humor of the early internet. His followers (voluntary or compelled) 'invoke' him by posting Zalgo text, and the meme itself is framed as if every use of Zalgo text is a tiny breach in the wall between worlds. Zalgo is both a parody of Lovecraftian horror and a genuinely effective horror creation — the corrupted text, the multiform entity, the implication that merely reading about him invites his influence. 'He comes' is the standard response to a Zalgo appearance — a grim, fatalistic acknowledgment that nothing can stop him.

Emergence

Zalgo was created by David Lovelace ('Shmorky') for a Something Awful forum Photoshop Phriday contest in 2004. The original image was a comic titled 'The Unnamed Feeling,' showing a character posting Zalgo text on a forum and a multi-eyed, tendril-covered entity emerging from a portal behind him with the tagline 'Zalgo!' The comic was intended as a joke about Lovecraftian horror and forum culture. However, 4chan's /x/ board adopted Zalgo and expanded his mythology exponentially. The 'Zalgo text' technique — stacking Unicode combining characters to create corrupted, bleeding text — was developed to represent his influence, and the backstory of 'he who waits behind the wall' was collaboratively written. Zalgo is one of the few internet monsters to have a known creator, though Lovelace himself has expressed ambivalence about his creation's viral spread.

Form

Zalgo has no fixed form — he is described as a multiform entity of chaos and corruption. The original Shmorky comic depicted him as a mass of black tendrils, red eyes, and sharp teeth erupting from a dimensional rift. Fan depictions have expanded this to include seven mouths, seven eyes, multiple limbs, and a form that seems to be always in motion, always shifting, never fully comprehensible. His 'face' — if it can be called that — contains too many features in impossible configurations. His presence corrupts the space around him: text bleeds, images distort, reality itself seems to run like wet paint. He is sometimes depicted as a shadow, sometimes as a physical monstrosity, sometimes as a 'glitch' in the fabric of the universe. The one consistent feature is multiplicity: too many eyes, too many mouths, too many limbs — an excess of features that defies coherence.

Powers

Text Corruption (Zalgo Text) The ability to corrupt digital or written text, causing it to 'bleed' or 'drip' downward into illegible, corrupted characters. This is the most consistent and well-attested power. All versions Seems to only affect text. The original meme was a visual effect created by combining Unicode combining characters. Some versions claim it can corrupt any digital media; others limit it to text.

Reality Corruption The ability to corrupt physical reality, causing decay, madness, and transformation in those exposed to its influence. This is implied in later versions but not in the earliest. Post-2006 4chan versions Unclear; appears to require exposure to its 'text' or image. Some versions say direct exposure is required; others say mere knowledge is enough.

Fear Inducement Zalgo's presence or influence causes intense fear, paranoia, and madness in victims. Post-2006 versions May be a side effect of its other powers rather than a direct ability. None significant.

Corruption of Media Zalgo can corrupt images, videos, and audio, causing them to glitch, distort, or show disturbing imagery. Later creepypasta versions Not attested in the earliest sources. Some versions claim it can only corrupt text; others extend to all media.

Void Travel Zalgo can move between dimensions or 'behind the wall' and our reality. Post-2006 versions It is 'waiting'—it cannot fully cross over without an invitation or a breach. Some versions say it can cross at will; others say it is trapped.

Behavior & Habitat

Zalgo's only goal is to break through the wall between his dimension and ours and corrupt all of reality. He is patient — he has been waiting for eons and will wait for eons more. His influence manifests as 'Zalgo text' — corrupted, bleeding text that appears in forum posts, documents, subtitles, and anywhere text can exist. His followers (either worshipers or the possessed) spread his influence by posting Zalgo text, which is framed as a form of invocation. He does not negotiate, does not make deals, and does not have human motivations. He is not evil in the human sense — he is a force of chaos that will unmake reality simply by entering it, the way water dissolves sugar.

Weaknesses & Wards

Zalgo is currently trapped behind 'the wall' — a dimensional barrier that prevents him from fully entering our reality. The nature of this wall is unspecified, but it holds. He can be 'ignored' — denying him attention may weaken his influence, though this is practically impossible on the internet. His vessels (possessed humans) can be killed. In some fan interpretations, positive, 'pure' content — hope, love, genuine human connection — acts as a counterforce to his corruption. He may be opposed by other cosmic entities or by the collective will of humanity. But fundamentally, Zalgo has no known permanent weakness — like Lovecraft's Cthulhu, he is a force beyond human opposition, and the best strategy is to avoid attracting his attention.

Accounts

A user posts a block of text that appears normal at first, but as the reader scrolls, the text begins to 'bleed'—letters drip downward, forming corrupted characters. The post is accompanied by a description of a malevolent entity 'behind the wall' that is responsible for the corruption. This is the earliest known appearance of the Zalgo concept.

Users on /x/ expand on the Zalgo concept, adding a detailed physical description (tall, gaunt, bleeding eyes, lipless mouth) and the 'wall' metaphor. The entity is now explicitly named 'Zalgo' and is described as a malevolent, corrupting force. The 'Zalgo text' effect is codified as a method of summoning or attracting the entity. Stories are told of people who read the text and experienced nightmares, paranoia, or physical illness.

Writers on creepypasta wikis codify the 'canon' of Zalgo: a tall, gaunt humanoid with bleeding eyes and a lipless mouth, who corrupts text and reality. The entity is often described as a 'god of chaos' or 'void entity.' Stories are written about encounters with Zalgo, often involving a person finding a corrupted text file or image and being driven mad. The 'wall' metaphor is expanded: Zalgo is 'behind the wall' and is trying to break through.

Field Notes

⁙ Zalgo text is created by stacking Unicode combining characters on normal letters — the resulting corruption is technically valid Unicode, not random glitches.

⁙ David Lovelace ('Shmorky'), the creator, has stated that Zalgo was originally a placeholder name he got from a random name generator and decided to keep.

⁙ The phrase 'he who waits behind the wall' became part of Zalgo's mythology through 4chan anonymous contributions, not the original comic.

⁙ Speaking or typing Zalgo's name in 'clean' text is considered dangerous in the mythos — proper references should always use corrupted Zalgo text.

⁙ Zalgo has been compared to the King in Yellow, the fictional play from Robert W. Chambers that drives readers insane — both are 'dangerous texts' within their respective canons.

Cultural Significance

Zalgo represents the fear that the internet is not just a communication medium but a portal — that the things we share, the memes we spread, the attention we give might be feeding something we don't understand. The Zalgo text meme is a perfect expression of digital corruption: the familiar (readable text) made strange and horrifying through technical manipulation. Zalgo embodies the Lovecraftian horror of the information age: that some knowledge is dangerous not because of what it describes but because the act of knowing it changes you. 'He comes' is the internet's equivalent of 'The stars are right' — a catchphrase that acknowledges doom with gallows humor.

Modern Interpretations

Zalgo remains an active presence in internet horror culture. Zalgo text generators are widely available, and the corrupted text aesthetic is used in everything from creepypasta to YouTube video titles to Discord memes. Zalgo appears in fan fiction, art, and as a 'hidden lore' figure in various indie games. His influence can be seen in the 'glitch horror' aesthetic, the 'corrupted' subgenre of creepypasta, and the broader concept that internet content can be 'dangerous to know.' While Zalgo has not had the mainstream breakthrough of Slender Man or the Backrooms, he remains deeply embedded in internet horror culture as a foundational figure.

Content Notes

The primary horror of Zalgo is psychological: the corruption of reality, the fear of the unknown, and the descent into madness. Victims are often described as becoming paranoid, hallucinating, or losing their sanity.

This is a common trope in cosmic horror (Lovecraft) and internet horror (creepypasta).

All versions

Zalgo's appearance (bleeding eyes, lipless mouth, decaying skin) and its ability to corrupt bodies (causing wounds, lesions, or transformation) are examples of body horror.

Common in horror fiction, particularly in the 'body horror' subgenre.

Post-2006 versions

The idea that a simple text file or image can become a vector for a malevolent entity is a modern variation of the 'cursed object' trope.

Reflects anxieties about digital media and the loss of control over information.
