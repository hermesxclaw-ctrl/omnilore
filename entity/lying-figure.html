Who It Is

A human shape wrapped in its own skin, head forced downward, body convulsing as it spews burning fluid—this is the Lying Figure, the first monster James Sunderland meets in Silent Hill 2. It looks less like a predator than a patient in agony, a walking body bag that refuses to stay still. Every time it shambles out of the fog, you’re not just fighting an enemy; you’re confronting the image of a loved one dying in a hospital bed, turned into horror you can’t ignore.

Attested: The Lying Figure behaves as a hostile, semi-mindless monster. It shambles toward the player when upright, occasionally stopping to emit a stream or spray of yellow, acid-like fluid from its chest, which stuns and injures James. When prone, it moves quickly, scurrying across the ground and sometimes ramming into the player. In the remake and Otherworld variant, it can spew a poisonous cloud upon death, leaving hazardous pools of liquid. Its movements are jerky, pained, and desperate rather than calculated, suggesting agony more than predatory cunning.

Powers
Acidic Chest Spray

Attested: The Lying Figure can emit a stream or spray of yellow, acid-like liquid from vents or openings in its chest. In Silent Hill 2, this attack briefly stuns James and inflicts mild damage. In the remake, similar attacks are described as "Vomit" or "Vomit Spray," with the creature bending or rearing back before releasing a gushing stream of unknown liquid in front of it.

Limit: The spray has limited range and requires the creature to face the target. Players can avoid it by staying just outside its effective radius or dodging sideways. After spraying, the creature is briefly vulnerable to melee attacks.

Rapid Crawling Charge

Attested: When lying on the ground, the Lying Figure can move very quickly, scurrying toward the player and ramming into them. This behavior is emphasized in both original and remake guides, which note that the creature is more vulnerable but more dangerous in this state.

Limit: The crawling charge is linear and can be interrupted by well-timed melee strikes, especially with weapons like the Steel Pipe. Once stunned, the creature is open to follow-up attacks and finishing kicks.

Poisonous Death Cloud (Otherworld Variant)

Attested: In the remake’s Otherworld, Lying Figures sometimes spew vomit or a poisonous cloud upon dying, leaving pools that cause damage if the player remains within them for the first few seconds.

Limit: The hazard is localized and short-lived; players can avoid damage by stepping away from the pool immediately after the creature’s death.

Physical Body Check / Pinning Attack

Attested: Guides describe a "Body-Check" or pinning attack in which the Lying Figure stands its ground, shakes slightly, then leaps a short distance to collide with the player, potentially pinning them if they fail to evade.

Limit: The attack is telegraphed by the creature’s shaking and brief pause, allowing attentive players to dodge. Once dodged, the creature is vulnerable to counterattacks.

Resistance to Conventional Weapons

Attested: Lying Figures can withstand multiple hits from melee weapons and handgun fire, but are ultimately fragile compared to later monsters. Guides recommend the Steel Pipe or handgun as efficient tools, noting that three handgun shots often suffice.

Limit: They have relatively low durability; shotgun blasts are overkill. Once knocked down, they must be kicked to prevent recovery.

Accounts I
James Sunderland’s First Encounter in South ValeSilent Hill 2 (2001) – in-game narrative

Early 2000s

Fact-based: After receiving a mysterious letter apparently from his deceased wife Mary, James Sunderland travels to Silent Hill. Upon entering South Vale, he follows a trail of blood through the fog-shrouded streets to a tunnel. There, he sees a humanoid silhouette moving strangely. As he approaches, the figure emerges as a Lying Figure, its upper body bound in flesh, writhing in pain. James fights and kills it, only to find that more such creatures now roam the streets and interiors of the town. This first encounter marks the transition from eerie emptiness to overt monstrosity and signals the town’s manifestation of James’s inner turmoil. The encounter forces James into direct violence and establishes the Lying Figure as the most common enemy in his journey. It also foreshadows the revelation that the town’s monsters are manifestations of his subconscious, particularly his memories of Mary’s illness and death.

Lying Figures Across Silent Hill 2’s LocationsSilent Hill 2 (2001) – in-game progression

Early 2000s

Fact-based: As James explores Wood Side Apartments, Blue Creek Apartments, the Silent Hill Historical Society, Toluca Prison, the Labyrinth, and the Lakeview Hotel, Lying Figures appear repeatedly. They lurk in corridors, stairwells, and open spaces, often emerging from darkness or fog. Their presence in both mundane and symbolic locations reinforces the idea that James cannot escape his guilt and memories; every environment is contaminated by suffering bodies that resemble Mary’s condition. The repeated encounters contribute to the game’s oppressive atmosphere and to James’s psychological deterioration. They also serve as a mechanical tutorial for combat and resource management, shaping the player’s experience of fear and fatigue.

Accounts II
Maria’s Off-screen Battles with Lying FiguresSilent Hill Wiki synthesis of game events

2000s–2020s

Interpretive: Some descriptions note that Maria fights through several Lying Figures before meeting James in Rosewater Park. While the game does not show these battles directly, her appearance and dialogue imply that she has navigated hostile environments similar to James’s. This suggests that Lying Figures are part of the shared Otherworld experienced by both characters, though their exact encounters differ. This interpretation reinforces Maria’s role as a constructed figure who nonetheless suffers within the town’s nightmare, paralleling Mary’s suffering and James’s guilt.

Armless Man in Silent Hill (2006 film)Silent Hill (film) – cinematic adaptation

Mid-2000s

Fact-based: In the film, Armless Men—monsters clearly inspired by the Lying Figure—appear in foggy streets and industrial spaces. Their torsos are bound, arms absent, and they spew acidic fluid at protagonists. The film uses them to create set-piece moments of body horror and chase sequences, translating the game’s recurring enemy into a more spectacular cinematic threat. The Armless Men contribute to the film’s visual identity and help establish Silent Hill as a place of grotesque, suffering bodies. They also cement the Lying Figure’s image in broader popular culture beyond the game.

Lying Figures in Comics and Art FilmSilent Hill comics (e.g., Sinner’s Reward) and Fukuro

Late 2000s–2010s

Fact-based with some reconstruction: In comics and the art film Fukuro, Lying Figures or closely related monsters appear as visual motifs of suffering and confinement. They attack protagonists or haunt hospital-like spaces, often without detailed exposition. These appearances extend the creature’s symbolic role into new narratives, where it continues to represent pain, guilt, and bodily degradation. These transmedia uses reinforce the Lying Figure as an iconic Silent Hill monster and allow artists to experiment with its form and meaning.
