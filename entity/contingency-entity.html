Who It Is

In Local 58's most famous episode, 'Contingency,' a broadcast is transmitted through the Emergency Alert System. The screen displays the Presidential Seal, and a synthesized voice addresses the nation in the calm, measured tones of an official government message. The message informs the American public that the United States has been defeated by a foreign power and that all Americans must, as a final act of patriotism, voluntarily end their lives to deny the enemy the satisfaction of victory. Instructions are provided. The broadcast concludes: 'If you are a true patriot, you will do this for your country. 51st state is not a place.' The horror of 'Contingency' is that it presents itself as a real EAS broadcast — a format designed to be trusted absolutely — and uses that trust to deliver something monstrous. The entity at the center of this horror is never seen. It may be the Moon. It may be whatever controls the Moon. It may be the United States government itself, having been compromised or corrupted. The '51st state is not a place' line implies that whatever won the war, whatever the government surrendered to, was not a country but something else — a state of being, a dimension, or an entity. 'Contingency' is one of the most effective examples of format-native horror: it doesn't need a monster, special effects, or even a story. It just needs the viewer to believe, for a moment, that the broadcast might be real. The episode's power is in its ambiguity — we never learn what the threat is, only that the government's response to it was to tell everyone to kill themselves, which implies something far worse than death.
