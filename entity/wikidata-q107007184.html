Who It Is

In the sky above the earth, the high god Wulbari once dwelt so close that the first humans could reach up and touch the fabric of heaven itself. But a single act of human carelessness—an offending hand, a splash of dirty water—severed that intimacy forever, and Wulbari withdrew to a distant celestial realm, leaving humanity to toil below. This is the story of how the world lost its heavenly neighbor, and what the Krachi people of West Africa remember about the god who turned away.

Wulbari is portrayed as a beneficent but remote creator who originally lived close to humans, but after an offense, he withdrew to the sky, becoming distant and less directly involved in human affairs. He is not considered malicious, but rather justly offended and now inaccessible.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Wulbari' is often glossed as 'God' or 'the Supreme Being' in the Krachi language. The exact linguistic components are not clearly documented in available sources; it may be a compound or a proper name.

Pronunciation — Not recorded in available sources. Likely /wʊlˈbɑːri/ in English approximation, but this is speculative.

Aliases — Wulbari

Ownership — Wulbari is a sky god of the Krachi people, who live in the Oti Region of Ghana (formerly Volta Region) and parts of Togo. The Krachi language is a Guang language of the Niger-Congo

Powers
Creation

Wulbari is credited with creating the world and the first humans, though the details of creation are not fully recorded.

Limit: No explicit limitations documented.

Sky control

As a sky god, Wulbari controls the heavens, including rain and thunder, which affect agricultural and daily life.

Limit: After withdrawing, he no longer interacts directly with humans.

Withdrawal

Wulbari has the power to remove himself from human proximity, which he did after being offended.

Limit: This is a one-time act, not a recurring power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Wulbari is described as residing in the sky, and is often conceived anthropomorphically as a powerful, distant male figure. No specific physical form is detailed in available sources; he is more of a celestial presence than a visual deity.

Symbols — The sky itself is the primary symbol of Wulbari. He is associated with the heavens, rain, and thunder, though specific iconographic representations are not documented. In Krachi culture, he may be invoked in prayers for rain or good fortune.

Domains — Wulbari's domain is the sky, specifically the upper atmosphere. He is a celestial deity, not tied to a specific earthly location, though he is worshipped by the Krachi people.

Accounts I
The Withdrawal of WulbariOral tradition of the Krachi people; documented in secondary sources

Pre-colonial; exact dating unknown

In the beginning, Wulbari lived so close to the earth that the sky was within arm's reach of the first people. However, a woman (or in some versions, a man) was pounding fufu with a long pestle, and the pestle struck the sky, or a woman's hand touched the sky while she was working, and she wiped her dirty hands on it. Offended by this pollution, Wulbari withdrew to the distant heavens, leaving humanity to live apart from him. Since then, he has been remote and is only approached through prayers and rituals. The separation of heaven and earth; humans lost direct access to the divine; the need for intermediaries (priests, rituals) arose.
