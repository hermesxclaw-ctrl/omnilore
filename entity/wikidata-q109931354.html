Who It Is

In a sun-flooded Detroit mansion in 2038, an old painter who can no longer hold a brush without shaking asks his android caretaker to do what no machine is supposed to do: create something human. Carl Manfred does not build androids or lead revolutions — he simply sees Markus as a son — and that single act of recognition is the spark that will split Detroit in two.

Attested behavior: Carl is calm, observant, wryly humorous, and deliberately non-authoritarian toward Markus. He treats Markus as a companion and surrogate son rather than a tool, correcting Leo's dehumanizing language ("He's not your slave, he's ..." in 'Broken'). He encourages autonomy: in 'The Painter,' he tells Markus to paint what he feels, not to copy, and praises the result regardless of player choice (realistic copy, abstract, or android-themed). He is also blunt about human nature and art — telling Markus that humans are afraid of death

Powers
Painting and Artistic Mastery

Celebrated human painter in his prime; his works fill his mansion and are described in-game as valuable and exhibited. He understands composition, color theory, and abstract expressionism and can articulate artistic philosophy.

Limit: By 2038, physical disability (tremor, wheelchair use) prevents him from painting at his former level; he directs Markus rather than executes large works himself. No supernatural enhancement.

Cultural Capital and Wealth

Wealthy patron who owns a historic mansion, art collection, and a private android (Markus, model RK200, prototype). Can afford private medical care and to shield Markus from standard android treatment.

Limit: Wealth does not protect him from family estrangement, health decline, or the legal status of androids; he cannot prevent police from treating Markus as property if Markus is implicated in his death.

Mentorship and Emotional Influence

Ability to inspire and morally form Markus by treating him as a person, encouraging autonomous creativity and ethical reflection. His aphorisms ('Don't let anyone tell you who you are') become Markus's core memory triggers.

Limit: Influence is interpersonal and posthumous only; he cannot intervene after death except as memory. His parenting fails with Leo, showing limits of his approach.

Piano Appreciation (not performance)

Requests Markus to play piano; appreciates music. Not shown playing himself in 2038 due to disability.

Limit: No longer performs; relies on Markus or recordings.

No Superhuman or Android Abilities

Explicitly human; has no android strength, regeneration, interface ability, or network connection. Vulnerable to age, illness, and physical assault.

Limit: Mortality is central to his narrative function; his heart attack is unpreventable within game logic.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Carl Manfred is presented as an elderly white man in his late 70s to early 80s, with white-gray hair, weathered skin, and a frail, thin build. He uses a motorized wheelchair in all appearances and has limited mobility in his legs, consistent with an unspecified degenerative condition or sequelae of age and illness; his hands tremble, affecting his ability to paint. He wears elegant, muted clothing — often a dark sweater or robe, scarf, and tailor

Symbols — Carl's primary symbols are painting and the wheelchair. Canvases — especially his large abstract works and the blank canvas he offers Markus — symbolize creativity as a measure of humanity and free will. The wheelchair signifies human vulnerability, dependence, and interdependence, deliberately mirroring android servitude but from a position of privilege: Carl needs care but can afford to buy it. Accounts I
Shades of Color - Morning RitualDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'Shades of Color'

2038 (in-fiction); 2018 (publication)

The player, as Markus, awakens in Carl's mansion and performs caretaking tasks: opening curtains, administering Carl's medication, wheeling him to the dining area and studio, and serving breakfast. Carl greets Markus warmly, asks about his night, and treats the routine as companionship rather than servitude. The chapter establishes the domestic rhythm, Carl's disability and dependence, and his gentle authority. Optional interactions include playing piano for Carl, playing chess, and reading. Establishes Carl-Markus father-son bond that motivates Markus's later choices; introduces gameplay mechanics of obedience vs autonomy; sets up the house as a safe space that will be violated in 'Broken.'

The Painter - Paint What You FeelDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'The Painter'

2038 / 2018

Carl takes Markus to his studio, shows his own abstract paintings, and asks Markus to paint. He first asks Markus to copy a painting on the desk, then dismisses the copy and tells him to paint something from the heart, to imagine and create. Depending on player input, Markus paints a realistic copy, a portrait of Carl, abstract shapes, or android-themed imagery (statue, android oppression). Carl is moved regardless, saying Markus has talent and that being human is about choice and meaning. He calls Markus a son in all but blood. This is the canonical moment of Markus's deviancy trigger — Carl's validation allows Markus to imagine beyond programming. The painting Markus creates is later found by Jericho androids and influences how they perceive him. The scene is widely cited in reception as the emotional core of Carl's arc.

Broken - Leo's Intrusion and Carl's DeathDetroit: Become Human, Chapter 'Broken'

2038 / 2018

Carl's biological son Leo, estranged and addicted, arrives demanding money. Carl refuses and tells Leo he will not enable him. Leo becomes enraged, insults Markus, and attacks him, calling him a plastic toy. Carl intervenes verbally, defending Markus. Leo shoves Markus; Markus must choose to endure or push back. In either case, Leo knocks over Carl or the stress triggers Carl's heart attack. Carl collapses, clutching his chest. If Markus defended himself, Leo accuses Markus of killing Carl; if Markus did not, Leo blames the android's presence. Police arrive (called by neighbors or Leo), see an android over a dead/dying human, and shoot Markus if he does not flee. Carl dies in Markus's arms, telling him not to let anyone tell him who he is. Carl's death is the inciting trauma that leads Markus to the junkyard, deviancy, and leadership of Jericho. It frames the game's central theme of personhood vs property. The police shooting of Markus (if he stays) demonstrates systemic anti-android bias. Leo disappears after this chapter until a brief late-game cameo.

Accounts II
Legacy - The Painting and the InheritanceDetroit: Become Human, Chapters 'Jericho,' 'Crossroads,' 'Battle for Detroit' (posthumous references)

2038 / 2018

After Carl's death, Markus carries Carl's gift — the painting he made in 'The Painter' and Carl's words — into the android resistance. In Jericho, Markus can show the painting to other androids as proof of human empathy. In 'Crossroads,' Markus may return to Carl's house (now empty, with Leo absent or briefly present) and reflect at Carl's studio. Carl's voice is heard in Markus's memory during pivotal choices about violence vs pacifism. If Markus chooses the pacifist revolution, he explicitly invokes Carl's teaching that violence is not the answer. Carl becomes a moral anchor for the pacifist path; players who pursue violent revolution are shown diverging from Carl's ideals. The empty mansion symbolizes lost innocence. The painting becomes an icon in fan art.

The Art of Carl Manfred - Paratextual BackstoryThe Art of Detroit: Become Human (Titan Books, 2018) and in-game environmental storytelling (press clippings, gallery plaques in Carl's house)

2018 (publication); diegetic dates 2020s-2030s

Paratexts fill gaps left by the game: Carl was a successful contemporary painter in Detroit, known for large abstract canvases blending human figures with geometric fragmentation — suggesting themes of identity and fracture. Press clippings in his house mention exhibitions and critical acclaim before his retirement due to health. The artbook shows concept iterations where Carl was initially more severe, then softened to emphasize warmth. No detailed biography (birth year, family origin) is given; the artbook notes the design goal was to create 'a father figure who sees beyond the machine.' Provides the only semi-canonical backstory for Carl's career; informs scholarly and fan interpretation of his role as the 'good human' archetype. Also explains visual choices (wheelchair, mansion) as deliberate contrasts to android oppression.

Casting Carl - Lance Henriksen's PerformanceBehind-the-scenes featurettes and interviews (Quantic Dream YouTube, PlayStation Blog, 2018)

2016-2018 (production and promotion)

Quantic Dream cast Lance Henriksen (known for Aliens, Millennium) for his ability to convey fragility and gravitas. Motion capture and voice sessions were conducted in Paris. Henriksen described Carl as a man who 'has nothing left but love to give' and who sees Markus as the son Leo failed to be. Director David Cage described Carl as the moral opposite of the abusive humans elsewhere in the game, intentionally idealized to show that coexistence is possible. The featurette shows Henriksen in a wheelchair rig, performing the heart attack scene. Casting shaped reception: Henriksen's genre legacy brought intertextual weight (fans noted his previous android role as Bishop in Aliens, now playing a human who loves an android). Performance was praised in reviews as grounding the game's sentimentality.

Carl Manfred created or manufactured Markus. — Markus is a CyberLife RK200 prototype, gifted to or purchased by Carl. Carl is an artist and owner, not an engineer. Elijah Kamski and CyberLife created androids; Carl has no technical role.

Carl is an android himself or becomes one. — Carl is explicitly human, mortal, and dies of a heart attack. No scene suggests he is an android or is transferred. The confusion may arise from Lance Henriksen's prior role as android Bishop in Aliens.

You can save Carl if you choose the right dialogue options in 'Broken.' — All verified branches result in Carl's death. The choice affects whether Markus is blamed and whether he is shot by police, but not Carl's survival.

Carl Manfred is based on a real Detroit painter. — Carl is a fictional composite created by Quantic Dream. No real painter of that name with that biography is documented; his paintings are fictional works created by the game's art team.

Carl dislikes Leo and prefers Markus out of cruelty. — Carl is shown as pained by Leo's addiction and estrangement, setting boundaries while expressing love. His affection for Markus is not presented as rejection of Leo but as a different kind of bond; he defends both in different ways.
