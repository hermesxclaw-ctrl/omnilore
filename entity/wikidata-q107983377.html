The Approach

In Mock Town, where dreams are mocked and pirates bet their pride on a hand of cards, a man famous for executions dies for cheating at a game he didn't cheat at — Roshio the Executioner lasts only a few pages, but his brutal end tells you everything about the new world Luffy has entered.

Content advisory. Graphic violence and murder (character is stabbed and shot to death on-panel/on-screen); depiction of pirate lawlessness and intimidation.
Identity

Roshio

Pronunciation Japanese: /ɾo.ɕi.o/ (ro-shi-o, three morae, equal stress); English localization: /roʊˈʃiːoʊ/ roh-SHEE-oh

Known As

Roshio the ExecutionerShokei no Roshio (処刑のロシオ)Roshio the Executioner (English VIZ localization)

Etymology

No official etymology provided by creator Eiichiro Oda. Name is rendered in katakana as ロシオ (Roshio). Fan speculation links it to Italian/Spanish phonetics or to 'rocio' (Spanish for dew), but no attested creator statement. Treated as invented proper name within One Piece.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese popular culture. Fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda for the manga One Piece (Shueisha, Weekly Shonen Jump, 1997-present). Owned and published by Shueisha / licensed by Toei Animation, VIZ Media. No traditional folklore ownership.

Disputed Identifications

Wikidata Q107983377 provides only 'fictional character' and 'heroic-legend' archetype with no realm/medium/era. No Wikipedia article exists. Strongest match is One Piece Roshio (Mock Town pirate, bounty 42,000,000 Beli), based on cross-referencing Wikidata interlanguage links and One Piece databases. Risk of homonymy: 'Roshio' appears as a rare personal name in Spanish-speaking contexts and as a minor transliteration variant for other works, but no other notable fictional character with this exact spelling was located in major databases (Anime News Network, MyAnimeList, Wikidata). Identification as One Piece character is treated as highly probable but not independently confirmed by Wikidata label description; flagged as provisional in gaps.

Description

Appearance

Attested: Roshio is depicted as a large, muscular adult male human, middle-aged appearance, with a broad jaw, stubble, and dark hair styled in a short, slicked manner with sideburns. In manga Chapter 223 and anime Episode 146 he wears a dark sleeveless vest over a light shirt, dark pants, and a necklace; his pirate crew's insignia is not prominently displayed. Anime coloring shows tanned skin, dark brown/black hair, and grey vest. No distinctive scars, tattoos, or prosthetics are attested. Post-mortem depiction shows him slumped over a table with stab wound visible. No alternate costumes or transformations exist. Adaptation-specific: anime adds slightly more saturated colors and extends the card-table scene by ~90 seconds with additional reaction shots not in manga.

Symbols & Iconography

No personal jolly roger, weapon sigil, or symbolic object is attested for Roshio. His epithet 'the Executioner' (処刑人, Shokeinin) functions as his iconographic marker, suggesting a reputation for executions, but no executioner's axe or hood is visually associated with him in his sole appearance. His bounty poster (42,000,000 Beli) is mentioned in dialogue by Bellamy's crew but not shown on-panel in detail. No religious, heraldic, or cultural symbols attached.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested behavior is limited to a single scene. Roshio is shown as confident and confrontational, accusing Bellamy of cheating at cards and drawing a knife when challenged. Dialogue indicates he values his reputation ('Don't mess with me, I'm Roshio the Executioner with a 42 million bounty!'). He is quick to resort to violence but is outmatched. No extended personality, moral code, or backstory is provided by Oda. Bellamy's mocking of his dream (implied to be related to Sky Island, as with all Mock Town pirates) frames him as part of the 'old era' pirate who still dreams, though Roshio's specific dream is never stated. No evidence of cowardice, honor, or comedy traits beyond this confrontation. Reconstructed: databooks classify him as captain of the Roshio Pirates, implying leadership, but no crew members are named on-panel.

Domains & Habitats

In-universe habitat: Active pirate in the first half of the Grand Line (Paradise), specifically present on Jaya Island, Mock Town (a lawless pirate town on the island's western coast) circa May 1522 in the One Piece timeline (Jaya Arc). No home island, ship name, or territory is attested. He operates as an independent pirate captain prior to the rise of the Bellamy Pirates' dominance in Mock Town. No supernatural domain, realm, or habitat beyond the standard One Piece world. Version differences: none - both manga and anime place him exclusively in Mock Town's bar.

Version Differences

Manga (primary): Roshio appears in Chapter 223 only alive; Chapter 224 shows aftermath. Bellamy stabs him with a knife and shoots him (manga shows knife stab and gunshot sound effects). Anime (Episode 146): Expands the fight, adds Sarquiss holding Roshio's arm, more explicit depiction of Bellamy using his Spring-Spring Fruit powers to stab and shoot, and adds filler dialogue from bystanders. Vivre Card (2018) confirms bounty 42,000,000 Beli and status 'dead', which was implied but not explicitly labeled 'deceased' in 2002 manga. No video game or live-action version alters his design significantly. No retcon or redesign has occurred.

Powers & Abilities

Notoriety and Bounty (42,000,000 Beli)

Source VersionManga Chapter 223 (dialogue), Vivre Card No.0262, One Piece Blue Deep p.214

Roshio's primary attested 'power' is social: a bounty of 42 million Beli, indicating significant threat level in Paradise and earning him the epithet 'the Executioner.' Bounty is stated in dialogue by Bellamy/Sarquiss and confirmed in Vivre Card and Blue Deep. It implies combat experience and criminal activity sufficient for World Government recognition, but no specific feats are shown.

Limitations

Bounty is not a direct measure of strength; he is effortlessly defeated by Bellamy (bounty 55 million). No evidence he could contend with Grand Line veterans. Status is deceased, so bounty is frozen.

Contradictions

None on amount; all sources agree on 42,000,000. Some early fan translations erroneously listed 36 million, corrected by VIZ and Vivre Card.

Knife Proficiency (Implied)

Source VersionManga Chapter 223, Anime Episode 146

Roshio draws a large knife/dagger during the card game dispute and attempts to stab Bellamy. No technique name is given; this is standard armed combat. He fails to land a hit before being countered.

Limitations

No Devil Fruit, Haki, or named technique attested. Human-level weapon skill only. Immediately disarmed/killed by Bellamy's Spring-Spring powers.

Contradictions

Anime shows knife more clearly as a stiletto; manga line art is less detailed. No contradiction in capability.

Leadership of Roshio Pirates

Source VersionVivre Card No.0262, One Piece Blue Deep

Databooks list him as captain of his own crew, the Roshio Pirates (ロシオ海賊団). This implies command, navigation, and crew management abilities standard to a pirate captain in One Piece, but no crew members, ship, or fleet actions are depicted.

Limitations

Crew is never shown; size, strength, and fate unknown. Presumably disbanded after captain's death.

Contradictions

Manga itself never names his crew; databook attribution is the sole source. Treated as attested via official supplementary material but not primary manga dialogue.

Stories & Myths

The Executioner Enters Mock Town

Source One Piece manga Chapter 223; anime Episode 146Culture Japanese shonen manga (One Piece world, Jaya Arc)Period 2002 (manga publication); in-universe 1522, Jaya Arc, Mock Town

Summary

Roshio the Executioner is introduced in a bar in Mock Town, Jaya, a town where pirates gather and openly mock those who dream of legendary islands. He is playing cards with Bellamy the Hyena and his first mate Sarquiss (referred to as Sarquiss in VIZ). The scene establishes Mock Town's brutal hierarchy: bounties are flaunted, cheating is expected, and Bellamy's crew dominates. Roshio's presence establishes the baseline of a 'typical' Grand Line pirate with a substantial bounty, against which Bellamy's greater power will be demonstrated.

Consequences

Establishes Roshio's reputation and the stakes of Mock Town; sets up the card-game conflict that will lead to his death and thematically parallels Luffy's later confrontation with Bellamy over dreams.

Variants

Manga is concise (4 pages). Anime adds extended crowd reactions, additional dialogue about Roshio's 42 million bounty, and a longer card-shuffling sequence. No contradictory variant.

The Card Game and the Accusation of Cheating

Source One Piece manga Chapter 223; anime Episode 146Culture Japanese shonen mangaPeriod 2002

Summary

During the card game, Roshio accuses Bellamy of cheating after losing repeatedly. Bellamy, whose Spring-Spring Fruit allows him to manipulate spring-like leg movements for subtle cheating, denies it and instead accuses Roshio of cheating. Witnesses are intimidated into siding with Bellamy. Roshio, enraged, draws his knife and declares his identity: 'I am Roshio the Executioner! 42 million!' The dispute illustrates Mock Town's inverted morality where the stronger dictates truth.

Participants

Roshio

Bellamy

Sarquiss

Bar patrons as coerced witnesses

Consequences

Escalates from verbal dispute to lethal violence; demonstrates Bellamy's method of controlling perception through power and intimidation.

Variants

Anime explicitly shows Bellamy's spring legs under the table to cheat; manga implies it but does not show the mechanism in this chapter (later explained). VIZ translation renders Bellamy's taunt as 'You cheating bastard' vs. original Japanese 'ichamon' nuances.

The Execution of the Executioner

Source One Piece manga Chapter 223 (pp. 16-19); anime Episode 146 (15:30-17:45)Culture Japanese shonen mangaPeriod 2002

Summary

Bellamy retaliates instantly. Using his Bane Bane no Mi (Spring-Spring Fruit) powers, he springs forward, stabs Roshio in the leg/hand with Roshio's own knife, then shoots him with a flintlock pistol while Sarquiss restrains him. Roshio collapses dead or mortally wounded over the card table. Bellamy declares that the era where pirates like Roshio chase dreams is over - the 'New Era' belongs to those who steal and mock. The killing is casual, witnessed by Luffy and Zoro who do not intervene, establishing Bellamy's cruelty and Luffy's restraint for later payoff.

Participants

Roshio (victim)

Bellamy (perpetrator)

Sarquiss (accomplice/restrainer)

Luffy, Zoro, Nami (witnesses)

Bar crowd

Consequences

Roshio dies, demonstrating Bellamy's lethality and the danger of Mock Town. His death is the inciting example of Bellamy's philosophy that 'dreams are dead.' It foreshadows Bellamy's own defeat by Luffy, who later proves dreams still matter. In-universe, his crew presumably collapses.

Variants

Manga depicts the stabbing and gunshot with sound effects (don, gakin) but does not linger on blood; anime shows more explicit blood and Roshio's body slumping with added sound design. Funimation dub adds a line from Bellamy: '42 million? Don't make me laugh.' not in literal Japanese.

Aftermath: The Corpse as Warning

Source One Piece manga Chapter 224; anime Episode 147 opening recapCulture Japanese shonen mangaPeriod 2002

Summary

In the immediate next chapter, Roshio's body remains slumped over the table as Bellamy continues drinking and mocking Luffy's group for believing in Sky Island (Skypiea). Luffy and Zoro, though provoked and beaten by Bellamy and Sarquiss, refuse to fight back, to Nami's confusion. Roshio's corpse serves as a visual warning of what happens to those who challenge Bellamy in Mock Town, and as a narrative foil: Roshio fought back and died, Luffy chooses not to fight for a different reason (not wanting to fight over a dream others mock).

Participants

Consequences

Reinforces thematic contrast between empty violence (Roshio/Bellamy) and principled endurance (Luffy). Roshio is not mentioned again in dialogue after this chapter, confirming his narrative function as a one-scene casualty.

Variants

Anime Episode 147 recaps the corpse for ~30 seconds; manga moves on more quickly. No resurrection or later appearance.

Legacy in Databooks and World-Building

Source One Piece Blue Deep (2012), One Piece Vivre Card (2018), One Piece Magazine Vol.8 (2020 Jaya retrospective)Culture Japanese official supplementary publicationsPeriod 2012-2020 (retrospective coverage of 2002 story)

Summary

Although Roshio never reappears in the main narrative, official databooks preserve his entry as part of the world-building of the Jaya Arc. Blue Deep lists his bounty and epithet among Mock Town pirates; Vivre Card assigns him card number 0262, status 'Deceased,' bounty 42,000,000 B, and affiliation 'Roshio Pirates (former captain).' Magazine retrospectives use his death as an example of Oda's technique of using disposable high-bounty pirates to calibrate power scaling before introducing stronger foes like Bellamy and later Doflamingo. He appears in fan-compiled bounty lists and power-scaling discussions as a benchmark for 'Paradise-level' threat.

Participants

Consequences

Ensures Roshio remains canon despite minimal panel time; provides official confirmation of death and bounty for continuity. No impact on later plot (Skypiea, Dressrosa) beyond Bellamy's backstory.

Variants

Vivre Card adds 'Deceased' status not explicitly stated in manga dialogue; Blue Deep omits status. No contradictory legacy stories.

Relationships
Roshio→Bellamy (Hyena Bellamy / Bellamy the Hyena)hostile

Roshio's killer and the dominant pirate in Mock Town at the time. Bellamy (bounty 55 million, captain of the Bellamy Pirates, later subordinate of Donquixote Doflamingo) cheats at cards, accuses Roshio of cheating, then murders him to assert dominance. Relationship is one-sided antagonism; no prior history attested. Bellamy uses Roshio's death to lecture Luffy about the 'New Era.'

Roshio→Sarquiss (Big Knife Sarquiss)hostile

Bellamy's first mate (bounty 38 million). Present at the card table, he assists Bellamy by restraining Roshio and corroborating the false accusation of cheating. He is not the killer but is complicit. Dialogue shows him laughing at Roshio's bounty claim.

Roshio→Monkey D. Luffywitness

Straw Hat captain, present in the same bar with Zoro and Nami, witnessing Roshio's murder. Luffy does not intervene or avenge Roshio, choosing to endure Bellamy's later assault without fighting. Thematically, Luffy and Roshio are contrasted: both are dream-chasing pirates, but Luffy's dream survives Bellamy's mockery while Roshio's ends in death. No personal relationship.

Roshio→Roronoa Zorowitness

Straw Hat swordsman, alongside Luffy in the bar. Also witnesses the murder and later is beaten by Bellamy/Sarquiss without retaliating, at Luffy's implicit order. No interaction with Roshio beyond observation.

Roshio→Donquixote Doflamingoindirect hierarchical (via Bellamy)

Warlord of the Sea and captain of the Donquixote Pirates, Bellamy's idol and later patron. Roshio has no direct interaction with Doflamingo, but his death at Bellamy's hands occurs within Doflamingo's sphere of influence (Bellamy flies Doflamingo's flag). Doflamingo later dismisses Bellamy himself, paralleling how Bellamy dismissed Roshio. Relationship is structural: Roshio is a lower-tier pirate eliminated by a pirate who himself is later discarded by a higher power.

Roshio→Roshio Pirates (unnamed crew)creator/ally (captain-crew)

Roshio is listed as captain of the Roshio Pirates. No crew members are named or shown in primary manga; crew is inferred from standard One Piece captaincy and databook entry. Fate after his death is unattested but presumably disbanded or absorbed. No ship name attested.

Warning: Murder of Roshio by Bellamy — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Murder of Roshio by Bellamy

Source Manga Chapter 223, Anime Episode 146

Description

Roshio is stabbed with a knife and shot with a pistol at close range in a bar, in front of witnesses including protagonists. The killing is presented as casual and disproportionate to the dispute (a card game accusation). The scene is not sanitized: manga shows him bleeding and collapsing; anime adds audible gunshot and blood. This establishes Mock Town's lawlessness and Bellamy's cruelty.

Scholarly Context

One Piece scholars and critics (e.g., analyses of Jaya as critique of cynicism) read the scene as Oda's deliberate use of disposable violence to contrast 'old dreamers' vs. 'new cynics.' No sexual violence or torture beyond the murder itself. Content is typical shonen battle violence but is lethal and unavenged.

Warning: Coercion and False Accusation — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Coercion and False Accusation

Source Manga Chapter 223

Description

Bellamy and Sarquiss coerce bar patrons into false testimony that Roshio cheated, when Bellamy was the actual cheater (using his Devil Fruit). This abuse of power to invert guilt mirrors real-world intimidation and mob justice. Roshio is denied any fair hearing.

Scholarly Context

Reflects One Piece's recurring theme of 'might makes right' in lawless zones before Luffy's intervention. No additional historical taboo beyond narrative injustice.

Contradictions & Disputes

Bounty amount

Position A

Roshio's bounty is 42,000,000 Beli (official)

Position A — evidence

Manga Chapter 223 dialogue (Japanese: 4200万ベリー), Vivre Card No.0262, Blue Deep p.214 all state 4200万

Position B

Early fan scanlations and some wikis listed 36,000,000 or 42 million with variant punctuation

Position B — evidence

2002-2003 English scanlations (e.g., MangaScreener) mistranslated or mis-copied the number; propagated to early fan wikis before VIZ correction

Scholarly Consensus

Consensus is 42,000,000 Beli; variant is translation error, now corrected in official sources.

Manner of death - stab vs. shot vs. both

Position A

Manga shows both stabbing and shooting (knife then pistol)

Position A — evidence

Chapter 223 panels show knife stab (SFX 'gakin') and pistol firing (SFX 'bam'); anime shows both sequentially

Position B

Some summaries describe only shooting or only stabbing

Position B — evidence

Condensed plot summaries (e.g., episode guides) shorten to 'Bellamy kills Roshio' without detailing both weapons

Scholarly Consensus

Primary evidence confirms both weapons used; shortened summaries are incomplete, not contradictory.

Status: dead vs. incapacitated

Position A

Roshio is deceased (killed)

Position A — evidence

Vivre Card explicitly marks 'Status: Dead/Deceased'; narrative context (body slumped, no further appearance, Bellamy's later line 'I killed him') supports death

Position B

Manga does not explicitly state 'dead' in dialogue, leaving ambiguous 'defeated' reading

Position B — evidence

Chapter 223-224 never uses the word 'shinda' (died) in dialogue; some readers interpreted as severe injury only

Scholarly Consensus

Official databook resolves ambiguity as deceased; treated as attested death via supplementary canon.
