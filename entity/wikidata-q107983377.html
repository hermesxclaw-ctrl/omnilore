Who It Is

In Mock Town, where dreams are mocked and pirates bet their pride on a hand of cards, a man famous for executions dies for cheating at a game he didn't cheat at — Roshio the Executioner lasts only a few pages, but his brutal end tells you everything about the new world Luffy has entered.

Attested behavior is limited to a single scene. Roshio is shown as confident and confrontational, accusing Bellamy of cheating at cards and drawing a knife when challenged. Dialogue indicates he values his reputation ('Don't mess with me, I'm Roshio the Executioner with a 42 million bounty!'). He is quick to resort to violence but is outmatched. No extended personality, moral code, or backstory is provided by Oda. Name & Tongue

Etymology — No official etymology provided by creator Eiichiro Oda. Name is rendered in katakana as ロシオ (Roshio). Fan speculation links it to Italian/Spanish phonetics or to 'rocio' (Spanish for dew), but no attested creator statement. Treated as invented proper name within One Piece.

Pronunciation — Japanese: /ɾo.ɕi.o/ (ro-shi-o, three morae, equal stress); English localization: /roʊˈʃiːoʊ/ roh-SHEE-oh

Aliases — Roshio the Executioner, Shokei no Roshio (処刑のロシオ), Roshio the Executioner (English VIZ localization)

Ownership — Japanese popular culture. Fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda for the manga One Piece (Shueisha, Weekly Shonen Jump, 1997-present). Owned and published by Shueisha / licens

Powers
Notoriety and Bounty (42,000,000 Beli)

Roshio's primary attested 'power' is social: a bounty of 42 million Beli, indicating significant threat level in Paradise and earning him the epithet 'the Executioner.' Bounty is stated in dialogue by Bellamy/Sarquiss and confirmed in Vivre Card and Blue Deep. It implies combat experience and criminal activity sufficient for World Government recognition, but no specific feats are shown.

Limit: Bounty is not a direct measure of strength; he is effortlessly defeated by Bellamy (bounty 55 million). No evidence he could contend with Grand Line veterans. Status is deceased, so bounty is frozen.

Knife Proficiency (Implied)

Roshio draws a large knife/dagger during the card game dispute and attempts to stab Bellamy. No technique name is given; this is standard armed combat. He fails to land a hit before being countered.

Limit: No Devil Fruit, Haki, or named technique attested. Human-level weapon skill only. Immediately disarmed/killed by Bellamy's Spring-Spring powers.

Leadership of Roshio Pirates

Databooks list him as captain of his own crew, the Roshio Pirates (ロシオ海賊団). This implies command, navigation, and crew management abilities standard to a pirate captain in One Piece, but no crew members, ship, or fleet actions are depicted.

Limit: Crew is never shown; size, strength, and fate unknown. Presumably disbanded after captain's death.

Accounts I
The Executioner Enters Mock TownOne Piece manga Chapter 223; anime Episode 146

2002 (manga publication); in-universe 1522, Jaya Arc, Mock Town

Roshio the Executioner is introduced in a bar in Mock Town, Jaya, a town where pirates gather and openly mock those who dream of legendary islands. He is playing cards with Bellamy the Hyena and his first mate Sarquiss (referred to as Sarquiss in VIZ). The scene establishes Mock Town's brutal hierarchy: bounties are flaunted, cheating is expected, and Bellamy's crew dominates. Roshio's presence establishes the baseline of a 'typical' Grand Line pirate with a substantial bounty, against which Bellamy's greater power will be demonstrated. Establishes Roshio's reputation and the stakes of Mock Town; sets up the card-game conflict that will lead to his death and thematically parallels Luffy's later confrontation with Bellamy over dreams.

The Card Game and the Accusation of CheatingOne Piece manga Chapter 223; anime Episode 146

2002

During the card game, Roshio accuses Bellamy of cheating after losing repeatedly. Bellamy, whose Spring-Spring Fruit allows him to manipulate spring-like leg movements for subtle cheating, denies it and instead accuses Roshio of cheating. Witnesses are intimidated into siding with Bellamy. Roshio, enraged, draws his knife and declares his identity: 'I am Roshio the Executioner! 42 million!' The dispute illustrates Mock Town's inverted morality where the stronger dictates truth. Escalates from verbal dispute to lethal violence; demonstrates Bellamy's method of controlling perception through power and intimidation.

Accounts II
The Execution of the ExecutionerOne Piece manga Chapter 223 (pp. 16-19); anime Episode 146 (15:30-17:45)

2002

Bellamy retaliates instantly. Using his Bane Bane no Mi (Spring-Spring Fruit) powers, he springs forward, stabs Roshio in the leg/hand with Roshio's own knife, then shoots him with a flintlock pistol while Sarquiss restrains him. Roshio collapses dead or mortally wounded over the card table. Bellamy declares that the era where pirates like Roshio chase dreams is over - the 'New Era' belongs to those who steal and mock. The killing is casual, witnessed by Luffy and Zoro who do not intervene, establishing Bellamy's cruelty and Luffy's restraint for later payoff. Roshio dies, demonstrating Bellamy's lethality and the danger of Mock Town. His death is the inciting example of Bellamy's philosophy that 'dreams are dead.' It foreshadows Bellamy's own defeat by Luffy, who later proves dreams still matter. In-universe, his crew presumably collapses.

Aftermath: The Corpse as WarningOne Piece manga Chapter 224; anime Episode 147 opening recap

2002

In the immediate next chapter, Roshio's body remains slumped over the table as Bellamy continues drinking and mocking Luffy's group for believing in Sky Island (Skypiea). Luffy and Zoro, though provoked and beaten by Bellamy and Sarquiss, refuse to fight back, to Nami's confusion. Roshio's corpse serves as a visual warning of what happens to those who challenge Bellamy in Mock Town, and as a narrative foil: Roshio fought back and died, Luffy chooses not to fight for a different reason (not wanting to fight over a dream others mock). Reinforces thematic contrast between empty violence (Roshio/Bellamy) and principled endurance (Luffy). Roshio is not mentioned again in dialogue after this chapter, confirming his narrative function as a one-scene casualty.

Legacy in Databooks and World-BuildingOne Piece Blue Deep (2012), One Piece Vivre Card (2018), One Piece Magazine Vol.8 (2020 Jaya retrospective)

2012-2020 (retrospective coverage of 2002 story)

Although Roshio never reappears in the main narrative, official databooks preserve his entry as part of the world-building of the Jaya Arc. Blue Deep lists his bounty and epithet among Mock Town pirates; Vivre Card assigns him card number 0262, status 'Deceased,' bounty 42,000,000 B, and affiliation 'Roshio Pirates (former captain).' Magazine retrospectives use his death as an example of Oda's technique of using disposable high-bounty pirates to calibrate power scaling before introducing stronger foes like Bellamy and later Doflamingo. He appears in fan-compiled bounty lists and power-scaling discussions as a benchmark for 'Paradise-level' threat. Ensures Roshio remains canon despite minimal panel time; provides official confirmation of death and bounty for continuity. No impact on later plot (Skypiea, Dressrosa) beyond Bellamy's backstory.

Roshio was a member of the Bellamy Pirates or Donquixote Pirates — Roshio was captain of his own independent crew, the Roshio Pirates, and was Bellamy's victim, not subordinate. He had no affiliation with Donquixote Doflamingo beyond being killed by Doflamingo's admirer.

Roshio had a Devil Fruit power or was a major fighter — No Devil Fruit, Haki, or named technique is attested for Roshio. He fought with a normal knife and was easily defeated. His 42 million bounty reflects general threat, not superhuman ability.

Roshio appears later in Skypiea or Dressrosa arcs — Roshio appears only in Jaya/Mock Town (Ch.223-224 / Ep.146) and never reappears alive. He is not present in Skypiea, nor among Bellamy's crew in Dressrosa. His only later 'appearances' are flashback recaps and databook entries.

Roshio is based on a real historical executioner or folklore figure — No evidence Oda based Roshio on a specific historical person or folklore. Name and design appear original for One Piece's pirate world-building. No interview or SBS (Shitsumon o Boshu Suru) column links him to a historical model.
