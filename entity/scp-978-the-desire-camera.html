A Polaroid camera that captures what its subject most desires rather than what is in front of it. Test photos: SCP-999 wanting a family, SCP-682 wanting total annihilation, SCP-096 wanting to be seen.

Who It Is

A Polaroid camera that captures what its subject most desires rather than what is in front of it. Test photos: SCP-999 wanting a family, SCP-682 wanting total annihilation, SCP-096 wanting to be seen. An O5 Council member photographed showing an empty room — they desired nothing, having already lost everything.

Form

A Polaroid OneStep from the 1970s, beige and brown. The flash leaves afterimages lasting 30 minutes.

Powers

Desire-based photography

Subconscious truth revelation

Works on non-human and anomalous subjects

Desire-revealing photography Attested: When SCP-978 is used to photograph a living subject, the resulting instant photograph does not depict the subject’s actual appearance or activity at the moment of capture. Instead, it shows what the subject wanted to be doing at that time. The desires manifested can be trivial (e.g., wanting to go home, wear different clothing) or profound and disturbing (e.g., engaging in taboo acts), and may reflect conscious wishes or deeply suppressed impulses. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) Core SCP-978 article; SCP Foundation Wikia/Fandom summary; SCP Database Attested: The effect applies only to living beings; inanimate objects photographed alone do not exhibit anomalous output. The camera requires standard film and paper and operates mechanically like a normal Polaroid, implying dependence on physical media and user activation. The desire shown appears to be tied to the moment of capture, not a permanent or predictive future state. Some tests show minimal or no change (e.g., subjects whose desires are already aligned with their current activity), indicating that the camera does not force dramatic alterations. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978) Interpretive contradiction: Some derivative sources describe the output as the subject’s "greatest" or "innermost" desires, while the original article emphasizes a spectrum from simple impulses to deeply suppressed wishes. This creates tension between a maximalist reading (the camera reveals core, defining desires) and a situational reading (it reveals whatever the subject wants at that moment). No explicit in-universe resolution is provided.

Mixed-desire composite imaging Attested: When two or more subjects with contrasting desires are photographed together, SCP-978’s output appears to "mix" their desires, producing composite scenes that incorporate elements of each subject’s wish. This can result in visually complex or contradictory images, revealing interpersonal tensions or incompatible goals. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) Core SCP-978 article; Fandom and Database summaries Attested: The mixing effect is described qualitatively but not systematically quantified; the camera does not necessarily give equal weight to each subject’s desire, and the mechanism of combination is unknown. The phenomenon requires simultaneous multi-subject photography; separate photographs of each subject show individual desires instead. Unknown: No detailed test series is published that systematically explores multi-subject desire mixing, leaving open questions about whether dominance, proximity, or emotional intensity affects the composite image.

Cross-testing diagnostic function with other SCPs Attested: Extended test logs document SCP-978 being used on various SCP entities, including multiple SCP-001 proposals, SCP-4999, SCP-2273-1, and others. The resulting photographs provide insight into these entities’ desires—such as release from custody, reunion with loved ones, or transformation into non-anomalous human states—thus functioning as a diagnostic tool for understanding anomalous psychology or metaphysics. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978) SCP-978 Extended Test Logs; Fandom and Database summaries Attested: Testing with certain high-risk SCPs (especially SCP-001 variants) is heavily restricted, requiring O5-level approval, and resulting photographs are often destroyed or archived under extreme security. Some tests produce dangerous secondary effects (e.g., contamination by SCP-217, memetic kill agents), indicating that using SCP-978 on powerful anomalies can be hazardous. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs) Interpretive: While the logs suggest that SCP-978 reliably reveals desires, some entries are heavily redacted or destroyed, leaving ambiguity about whether the camera’s output is always interpretable or safe. The presence of memetic kill agents and contamination raises the possibility that the act of observation via SCP-978 can interact with other anomalies in unpredictable ways, but this is not fully theorized in canon.

Incident Log

Attested (derivative canon): At some point in the late 20th or early 21st century, local authorities encountered unexplained photographic phenomena associated with a Polaroid camera. The device was eventually turned over to the SCP Foundation, reportedly after being found in an abandoned warehouse at a redacted location. The Foundation designated it SCP-978, classified it as Safe, and stored it in security locker H-J-12 at Site-17. Subsequent documentation formalized containment procedures and testing protocols. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978) [[1]](https://grokipedia.com/page/SCP-978)

Attested: In May of an unspecified year, Foundation researchers conducted basic tests on human staff. Research assistants and doctors were photographed while posing neutrally and smiling for the camera. The resulting images revealed a range of desires: one assistant appeared tired and unprofessional, drinking from a flask; another was shown in casual clothes heading out for the night, admitting she wished to go home; a doctor appeared simply wearing a different tie. At least two results were redacted as [DATA EXPUNGED], one leading to a research assistant being placed on temporary psychiatric leave and another causing extreme embarrassment and apologies from a doctor. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Attested: After human testing, SCP-978 was used on animals, including a dog and its handler, mice, a rabbit, and other beings. The photographs revealed desires such as the dog wanting to play or be with its handler, and other animals exhibiting simple, instinctual wishes. These tests confirmed that SCP-978’s effect applies to non-human living beings and can capture relatively simple, immediate desires more easily than complex human psychological states. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Attested (derivative canon): After SCP-4999 appeared to a bedridden Stage IV cancer patient at a hospital, an individual holding SCP-978 took a photograph of SCP-4999 smoking a cigarette next to the patient. The resulting Polaroid showed the patient standing next to a man, while SCP-4999 was absent from the image. This suggests that the patient’s desire was to stand beside a non-anomalous companion, or perhaps to be healthy and upright, rather than to remain bedridden with SCP-4999 present. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Attested (derivative canon): SCP-978 was used on SCP-2273-1 while he sat on a bunk reading a novel. The resulting photograph showed two European men working on a farm as three women watched. Four of the older figures appeared related, while the youngest woman was seemingly identified as SCP-191 without her cybernetics. This image suggests a desire for a pastoral, familial life and for SCP-191 to exist as a non-modified human, highlighting themes of lost normalcy and bodily autonomy. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Attested: Extended logs describe SCP-978 being used on various SCP-001 instances and proposals, including SCP-001-EX, SCP-001-ARBELICT, SCP-001-CLEF, SCP-001-TG/AKTUS, SCP-001-AKTUS-I, SCP-001-AKTUS-II, SCP-001-LOCKE-Ω, and SCP-001-LOCKE-A. Results range from no change (SCP-001-EX) to images of release from Foundation custody, bowing before an unknown entity in an infinite white landscape, transformed non-anomalous human states, and dangerous effects such as photographs emitting intense brightness that converts exposed creatures into SCP-001-A instances. Some photographs are destroyed or heavily redacted, and testing is restricted to O5-approved scenarios. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Content Notes

Attested: Several test results are redacted as [DATA EXPUNGED], with one leading to a research assistant’s temporary psychiatric leave and another causing extreme embarrassment and apologies from a doctor. While the exact content is unknown, the narrative clearly implies that SCP-978 can reveal desires that are socially taboo, morally troubling, or personally devastating. The Foundation explicitly forbids use of SCP-978 for blackmail, entertainment, or personal reasons, acknowledging the potential for abuse and psychological harm. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Within horror studies and surveillance ethics, SCP-978 can be read as a critique of technologies that expose inner life—akin to lie detectors, data mining, or invasive therapy—highlighting the risk of weaponizing private desires. The redactions function as narrative ellipses that invite readers to imagine worst-case scenarios without glamorizing them.

Core SCP-978 article (testing log; containment procedures)

Attested: Research Assistant ███ ██████ is placed on temporary psychiatric leave after the content of their SCP-978 photograph (redacted) is revealed. This indicates that confronting one’s own desires, especially when they conflict with self-image or social norms, can trigger acute psychological distress. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

This episode aligns with psychological literature on shame, cognitive dissonance, and trauma associated with involuntary disclosure of private thoughts. SCP-978 dramatizes the potential harm of forced self-revelation in institutional settings.

Core SCP-978 testing log

Attested: Containment procedures explicitly state that SCP-978 is not to be used for blackmail, entertainment, or personal reasons, with an admonition "This means you" from O5-██. This implies that the camera’s outputs could be exploited to coerce, manipulate, or humiliate subjects by revealing compromising desires. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

The prohibition reflects awareness of institutional abuse risks. In broader cultural terms, SCP-978 can be read as a metaphor for surveillance capitalism and data-driven blackmail, where intimate information is extracted and weaponized.

Core SCP-978 containment procedures

Attested: The extended test logs include a memetic kill agent (978-001) activated for Overseer-level readers, with life signs confirmed. This indicates that information associated with SCP-978’s testing—particularly with SCP-001—can be lethal or cognitively hazardous, requiring protective measures that themselves involve violence (albeit fictional). [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Memetic kill agents are a recurring SCP trope representing dangerous knowledge. In the context of SCP-978, they underscore the idea that revealing desires of cosmic or foundational entities may be too dangerous to human minds, linking desire, knowledge, and death.

SCP-978 Extended Test Logs

Attested: In one extended test, a photograph produced by SCP-978 becomes contaminated by SCP-217 and is immediately incinerated. In another, a photo of SCP-001-LOCKE-Ω emits color and brightness equal to the original anomaly, transforming all exposed creatures into SCP-001-A instances. These incidents show that SCP-978’s outputs can become vectors for other anomalies, causing bodily transformation and mass harm. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

These episodes highlight the dark potential of images as carriers of contagion—memetic, technological, or biological. They resonate with media theory about the power of images to affect bodies and with horror traditions of cursed photographs.

A way to hold it in your mind

According to traditional accounts, a cheap red-and-black Polaroid camera sits in a locker at Site-17, indistinguishable from the disposable nostalgia of the late twentieth century—until you press the shutter and discover that the photograph shows not what you were doing, but what you wanted to be doing. In the SCP Foundation’s universe, SCP-978 turns every snapshot into a confession, exposing desires that range from a different tie to fantasies so disturbing they send researchers on psychiatric leave. The "Desire Camera" is horror by way of honesty: a mundane object that makes the inner life visible, and sometimes lethal, one instant print at a time.
