The Approach

A cheap red-and-black Polaroid camera sits in a locker at Site-17, indistinguishable from the disposable nostalgia of the late twentieth century—until you press the shutter and discover that the photograph shows not what you were doing, but what you wanted to be doing. In the SCP Foundation’s universe, SCP-978 turns every snapshot into a confession, exposing desires that range from a different tie to fantasies so disturbing they send researchers on psychiatric leave. The "Desire Camera" is horror by way of honesty: a mundane object that makes the inner life visible, and sometimes lethal, one instant print at a time.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses fictional but potentially distressing material, including exposure of taboo desires, psychological harm, implied sexual or violent fantasies, institutional coercion, memetic hazards, and bodily transformation. While SCP-978 and related entities are part of a collaborative horror fiction project, the themes may resonate with real-world experiences of shame, surveillance, and mental health crises. Readers sensitive to these topics should proceed with care.
Identity

SCP-978 — The Desire Camera

Pronunciation S-C-P nine-seven-eight; "desire camera" /dɪˈzaɪər ˈkæm(ə)rə/

Known As

SCP-978Desire Camera

Etymology

"SCP-978" follows the SCP Foundation’s cataloguing convention: "SCP" (Secure, Contain, Protect) plus a three‑digit numerical identifier. The epithet "Desire Camera" is descriptive, referring to its anomalous property of producing photographs that depict the subject’s desires rather than their actual state.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-978 is a collaboratively authored fictional object within the SCP Foundation project, an internet-based shared horror and speculative fiction universe published under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licensing. Authorship is distributed among multiple contributors rather than a single canonical creator, and the work is maintained by the SCP Foundation community and affiliated wikis.

Disputed Identifications

No major disputes exist over the identity of SCP-978 itself; it is consistently described as a Polaroid Supercolor 645 CL instant camera with desire-revealing photographic output. Minor interpretive differences concern whether the camera reveals "innermost" or "current" desires, and how complex or subconscious those desires may be, but these are debates over function rather than identity.

Description

Appearance

Attested: SCP-978 is described as a standard red-and-black Polaroid Supercolor 645 CL instant camera, visually indistinguishable from a non-anomalous consumer model of the Polaroid 600 series. It has a lightweight plastic body, integrated flash, viewfinder, and film slot, with no visible damage, modifications, or anomalous markings. It uses standard Polaroid 600-type film packs and produces square instant photographs. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[1]](https://grokipedia.com/page/SCP-978)
Reconstructed: Grokipedia and derivative databases elaborate typical technical specifications (fixed focus, automatic exposure, built-in flash, film battery) based on known properties of the Supercolor 645 CL line; these details are consistent with Polaroid documentation but not individually spelled out in the original SCP article. [[1]](https://grokipedia.com/page/SCP-978)

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: SCP-978 itself bears no unique sigils, logos, or anomalous markings beyond standard Polaroid branding. Its iconography in fan art and wiki imagery typically emphasizes the red-and-black color scheme and the unsettling contrast between mundane camera design and disturbing photographic outputs. The photographs produced by SCP-978 function as symbolic windows into desire, often depicting altered environments, changed clothing, or entirely different scenarios, which have become a recognizable trope in SCP-related visual culture. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978)

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-978 exhibits no sapience or personality; it behaves mechanically like a normal instant camera. Its anomalous behavior is limited to the content of the photographs it produces. The camera does not initiate actions on its own, communicate, or resist handling. All "behavior" is expressed through the images, which reveal the desires of subjects at the moment of capture. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)
Interpretive: Within the SCP narrative, the camera is often treated as an ethically charged diagnostic tool—its outputs can embarrass, disturb, or psychologically impact subjects, leading to commentary by Foundation staff about the dangers of exposing hidden desires. This is an interpretive layer built from test logs and meta discussion rather than explicit characterization of the object as sentient. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-978 is stored in security locker H-J-12 at Site-17, a major SCP Foundation facility. It is removed only for authorized testing by personnel with Level-2 clearance or higher, and its use is tightly regulated. Its "habitat" is thus a secure containment environment within the fictional Foundation infrastructure. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978)
Interpretive: Thematically, SCP-978 occupies the domain of psychological and ethical horror—its function intersects with surveillance, confession, and exposure of inner life. It is often deployed in cross-tests with other SCPs to explore their desires, situating it within a meta-domain of epistemic tools used by the Foundation to understand anomalies. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Some sources emphasize "innermost" or "greatest" desires, while the original article stresses that the effect can range from deeply suppressed desires to simple, momentary impulses. Powers & Abilities

Desire-revealing photography

Source Version Core SCP-978 article; SCP Foundation Wikia/Fandom summary; SCP Database

Description

Attested: When SCP-978 is used to photograph a living subject, the resulting instant photograph does not depict the subject’s actual appearance or activity at the moment of capture. Instead, it shows what the subject wanted to be doing at that time. The desires manifested can be trivial (e.g., wanting to go home, wear different clothing) or profound and disturbing (e.g., engaging in taboo acts), and may reflect conscious wishes or deeply suppressed impulses. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978)

Limitations

Attested: The effect applies only to living beings; inanimate objects photographed alone do not exhibit anomalous output. The camera requires standard film and paper and operates mechanically like a normal Polaroid, implying dependence on physical media and user activation. The desire shown appears to be tied to the moment of capture, not a permanent or predictive future state. Some tests show minimal or no change (e.g., subjects whose desires are already aligned with their current activity), indicating that the camera does not force dramatic alterations. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Contradictions

Interpretive contradiction: Some derivative sources describe the output as the subject’s "greatest" or "innermost" desires, while the original article emphasizes a spectrum from simple impulses to deeply suppressed wishes. This creates tension between a maximalist reading (the camera reveals core, defining desires) and a situational reading (it reveals whatever the subject wants at that moment). No explicit in-universe resolution is provided.

Mixed-desire composite imaging

Source Version Core SCP-978 article; Fandom and Database summaries

Description

Attested: When two or more subjects with contrasting desires are photographed together, SCP-978’s output appears to "mix" their desires, producing composite scenes that incorporate elements of each subject’s wish. This can result in visually complex or contradictory images, revealing interpersonal tensions or incompatible goals. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978)

Limitations

Attested: The mixing effect is described qualitatively but not systematically quantified; the camera does not necessarily give equal weight to each subject’s desire, and the mechanism of combination is unknown. The phenomenon requires simultaneous multi-subject photography; separate photographs of each subject show individual desires instead.

Contradictions

Unknown: No detailed test series is published that systematically explores multi-subject desire mixing, leaving open questions about whether dominance, proximity, or emotional intensity affects the composite image.

Cross-testing diagnostic function with other SCPs

Source Version SCP-978 Extended Test Logs; Fandom and Database summaries

Description

Attested: Extended test logs document SCP-978 being used on various SCP entities, including multiple SCP-001 proposals, SCP-4999, SCP-2273-1, and others. The resulting photographs provide insight into these entities’ desires—such as release from custody, reunion with loved ones, or transformation into non-anomalous human states—thus functioning as a diagnostic tool for understanding anomalous psychology or metaphysics. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs) [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Limitations

Attested: Testing with certain high-risk SCPs (especially SCP-001 variants) is heavily restricted, requiring O5-level approval, and resulting photographs are often destroyed or archived under extreme security. Some tests produce dangerous secondary effects (e.g., contamination by SCP-217, memetic kill agents), indicating that using SCP-978 on powerful anomalies can be hazardous. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Contradictions

Interpretive: While the logs suggest that SCP-978 reliably reveals desires, some entries are heavily redacted or destroyed, leaving ambiguity about whether the camera’s output is always interpretable or safe. The presence of memetic kill agents and contamination raises the possibility that the act of observation via SCP-978 can interact with other anomalies in unpredictable ways, but this is not fully theorized in canon.

Stories & Myths

Acquisition and containment at Site-17

Source SCP Foundation Wikia/Fandom; SCP Database; Grokipedia (partially extrapolated)Culture SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod Late 20th or early 21st century (in-universe; exact year redacted or unspecified)

Summary

Attested (derivative canon): At some point in the late 20th or early 21st century, local authorities encountered unexplained photographic phenomena associated with a Polaroid camera. The device was eventually turned over to the SCP Foundation, reportedly after being found in an abandoned warehouse at a redacted location. The Foundation designated it SCP-978, classified it as Safe, and stored it in security locker H-J-12 at Site-17. Subsequent documentation formalized containment procedures and testing protocols. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978) [[1]](https://grokipedia.com/page/SCP-978)

Participants

Local authorities (unnamed)

SCP Foundation acquisition team

Site-17 containment staff

Consequences

SCP-978’s containment enabled systematic study of its anomalous properties and integration into the Foundation’s broader research program. Its Safe classification reflects the object’s lack of autonomous hostility but does not eliminate ethical and psychological risks associated with its use.

Variants

Grokipedia provides a more detailed recovery narrative than the original SCP article, which does not explicitly describe the warehouse or local authorities. These details should be treated as secondary canon or fan elaboration rather than primary evidence.

Initial human testing and psychological impact

Source Core SCP-978 article testing logCulture SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod May of an unspecified year (late 20th or early 21st century, in-universe)

Summary

Attested: In May of an unspecified year, Foundation researchers conducted basic tests on human staff. Research assistants and doctors were photographed while posing neutrally and smiling for the camera. The resulting images revealed a range of desires: one assistant appeared tired and unprofessional, drinking from a flask; another was shown in casual clothes heading out for the night, admitting she wished to go home; a doctor appeared simply wearing a different tie. At least two results were redacted as [DATA EXPUNGED], one leading to a research assistant being placed on temporary psychiatric leave and another causing extreme embarrassment and apologies from a doctor. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Consequences

The tests demonstrated SCP-978’s capacity to reveal both mundane and disturbing desires, prompting ethical concerns and psychological consequences for subjects. The psychiatric leave and embarrassment underscore the potential harm of exposing hidden impulses, leading to explicit policy prohibiting use of SCP-978 for blackmail, entertainment, or personal reasons.

Cross-testing with SCP-4999

Source SCP Foundation Wikia/Fandom; SCP Database; Extended Test Logs (referenced)Culture SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod Post-introduction of SCP-4999 (21st-century in-universe)

Summary

Attested (derivative canon): After SCP-4999 appeared to a bedridden Stage IV cancer patient at a hospital, an individual holding SCP-978 took a photograph of SCP-4999 smoking a cigarette next to the patient. The resulting Polaroid showed the patient standing next to a man, while SCP-4999 was absent from the image. This suggests that the patient’s desire was to stand beside a non-anomalous companion, or perhaps to be healthy and upright, rather than to remain bedridden with SCP-4999 present. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Participants

SCP-4999

Stage IV cancer patient (unnamed)

Photographer (Foundation-affiliated, unnamed)

Consequences

The test implies that SCP-978 may not depict certain anomalous entities if they are not part of the subject’s desire, raising questions about the ontological status of SCP-4999 and the camera’s selective representation. It also underscores the poignancy of human desire for normalcy and companionship in the face of terminal illness.

Variants

The core description is consistent across Fandom and Database entries; the original SCP-978 article does not include this cross-test, so it should be treated as extended canon.

Testing with SCP-001 proposals and high-risk anomalies

Source SCP-978 Extended Test LogsCulture SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod Post-establishment of multiple SCP-001 proposals

Summary

Attested: Extended logs describe SCP-978 being used on various SCP-001 instances and proposals, including SCP-001-EX, SCP-001-ARBELICT, SCP-001-CLEF, SCP-001-TG/AKTUS, SCP-001-AKTUS-I, SCP-001-AKTUS-II, SCP-001-LOCKE-Ω, and SCP-001-LOCKE-A. Some photographs are destroyed or heavily redacted, and testing is restricted to O5-approved scenarios. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Variants

Dark History
Content advisory. SCP-978’s narrative includes psychologically disturbing content, exposure of taboo desires, implied sexual or violent fantasies, mental health crises, and dangerous interactions with other anomalies (including memetic kill agents and contamination). Readers should be aware of themes of coercion, surveillance, shame, and potential self-harm or harm to others implied by revealed desires.

Exposure of taboo and disturbing desires

Source Core SCP-978 article (testing log; containment procedures)

Description

Attested: Several test results are redacted as [DATA EXPUNGED], with one leading to a research assistant’s temporary psychiatric leave and another causing extreme embarrassment and apologies from a doctor. While the exact content is unknown, the narrative clearly implies that SCP-978 can reveal desires that are socially taboo, morally troubling, or personally devastating. The Foundation explicitly forbids use of SCP-978 for blackmail, entertainment, or personal reasons, acknowledging the potential for abuse and psychological harm. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Scholarly Context

Within horror studies and surveillance ethics, SCP-978 can be read as a critique of technologies that expose inner life—akin to lie detectors, data mining, or invasive therapy—highlighting the risk of weaponizing private desires. The redactions function as narrative ellipses that invite readers to imagine worst-case scenarios without glamorizing them.

Psychiatric impact and mental health

Source Core SCP-978 testing log

Description

Attested: Research Assistant ███ ██████ is placed on temporary psychiatric leave after the content of their SCP-978 photograph (redacted) is revealed. This indicates that confronting one’s own desires, especially when they conflict with self-image or social norms, can trigger acute psychological distress. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Scholarly Context

This episode aligns with psychological literature on shame, cognitive dissonance, and trauma associated with involuntary disclosure of private thoughts. SCP-978 dramatizes the potential harm of forced self-revelation in institutional settings.

Potential for blackmail and coercion

Source Core SCP-978 containment procedures

Description

Attested: Containment procedures explicitly state that SCP-978 is not to be used for blackmail, entertainment, or personal reasons, with an admonition "This means you" from O5-██. This implies that the camera’s outputs could be exploited to coerce, manipulate, or humiliate subjects by revealing compromising desires. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Scholarly Context

The prohibition reflects awareness of institutional abuse risks. In broader cultural terms, SCP-978 can be read as a metaphor for surveillance capitalism and data-driven blackmail, where intimate information is extracted and weaponized.

Memetic kill agents and lethal information

Source SCP-978 Extended Test Logs

Description

Attested: The extended test logs include a memetic kill agent (978-001) activated for Overseer-level readers, with life signs confirmed. This indicates that information associated with SCP-978’s testing—particularly with SCP-001—can be lethal or cognitively hazardous, requiring protective measures that themselves involve violence (albeit fictional). [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Scholarly Context

Memetic kill agents are a recurring SCP trope representing dangerous knowledge. In the context of SCP-978, they underscore the idea that revealing desires of cosmic or foundational entities may be too dangerous to human minds, linking desire, knowledge, and death.

Contamination and transformation via photographs

Source SCP-978 Extended Test Logs

Description

Attested: In one extended test, a photograph produced by SCP-978 becomes contaminated by SCP-217 and is immediately incinerated. In another, a photo of SCP-001-LOCKE-Ω emits color and brightness equal to the original anomaly, transforming all exposed creatures into SCP-001-A instances. These incidents show that SCP-978’s outputs can become vectors for other anomalies, causing bodily transformation and mass harm. [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Scholarly Context

These episodes highlight the dark potential of images as carriers of contagion—memetic, technological, or biological. They resonate with media theory about the power of images to affect bodies and with horror traditions of cursed photographs.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nature of "desire" revealed by SCP-978

Position A

SCP-978 reveals a subject’s "innermost" or "greatest" desires—deep, defining wishes that may be long-term or subconscious.

Position A — evidence

Derivative wikis (Fandom, SCP Database) describe SCP-978 as showing "innermost" or "greatest" desires, emphasizing depth and centrality. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Position B

SCP-978 reveals whatever the subject wants to be doing at the moment of capture, which may be trivial, situational, or fleeting.

Position B — evidence

The original SCP article states that the photograph shows "not what the subject was doing at the time of the photo, but rather what the subject wanted to be doing" and notes that the effect can range from deeply suppressed desires to simple changes or alterations, implying a spectrum rather than exclusively core desires. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Scholarly Consensus

Within SCP fandom, both readings coexist. A nuanced consensus recognizes that SCP-978 can reveal both deep and trivial desires, depending on the subject and context. The object is thus best understood as a situational desire-revealer rather than a pure "soul mirror."

Canon status of recovery narrative and technical details

Position A

SCP-978 was recovered from an abandoned warehouse after unexplained photographic incidents, and its technical specifications (focus range, exposure control, film type) are part of canonical description.

Position A — evidence

Grokipedia and some derivative databases describe recovery in an abandoned warehouse and provide detailed camera specs consistent with Polaroid documentation. [[1]](https://grokipedia.com/page/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978)

Position B

The original SCP article does not specify recovery location or detailed technical specs; these are extrapolations or secondary canon from commentary sites like Grokipedia.

Position B — evidence

The core SCP-978 article focuses on containment, anomalous effect, and testing, without explicit recovery narrative or technical breakdown beyond identifying the model as a Polaroid Supercolor 645 CL. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Scholarly Consensus

Careful readers treat the original SCP article as primary canon and Grokipedia-style elaborations as secondary or interpretive. The recovery story and technical details are plausible but not strictly canonical; they should be cited as commentary rather than original text.

Scope of cross-testing with other SCPs

Position A

Cross-tests with SCP-4999, SCP-2273-1, SCP-191, and multiple SCP-001 proposals are integral parts of SCP-978’s canon, defining its narrative role.

Position A — evidence

Fandom and Database entries, as well as the Extended Test Logs, present these cross-tests as established narrative episodes, often referenced in discussions of SCP-978’s significance. [[2]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-978) [[3]](https://scpdb.miraheze.org/wiki/SCP-978) [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Position B

These cross-tests are extended or derivative canon, not part of the original SCP-978 article, and may be subject to revision or debate.

Position B — evidence

The original SCP-978 article contains only a limited testing log focused on Foundation staff and animals; cross-tests with other SCPs appear in separate documents and fan compilations, indicating a layered canon structure.

Scholarly Consensus

Most SCP readers accept extended logs as part of SCP-978’s broader canon, but rigorous citation distinguishes between the main article and later additions. For scholarly work, it is important to specify which document each test comes from.

Ethical framing of SCP-978’s use

Position A

SCP-978 is primarily a research tool, ethically neutral, whose risks are manageable through containment procedures.

Position A — evidence

Containment procedures present SCP-978 as a Safe-class object with standard access controls, suggesting routine use in research. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978)

Position B

SCP-978 is inherently ethically fraught, functioning as a coercive surveillance device that can cause psychological harm and enable blackmail.

Position B — evidence

The explicit prohibition against blackmail and entertainment use, the psychiatric leave incident, and the embarrassment of staff indicate recognition of serious ethical risks. Extended logs with memetic kill agents and dangerous cross-tests further complicate the notion of "safe" use. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978) [[4]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-978-extended-test-logs)

Scholarly Consensus

Interpretive scholarship on SCP often reads SCP-978 as a critique of surveillance and institutional power. While the object is mechanically safe, its social and psychological effects are not, and the narrative leans toward a cautionary framing.

Known Gaps

1. Exact posting and revision dates for the original SCP-978 article and extended test logs are not clearly indicated in accessible versions; chronology is approximate.
2. The specific content of redacted [DATA EXPUNGED] photographs in the testing log is unknown; any reconstruction would be speculative and is therefore omitted.
3. Recovery details (abandoned warehouse, local authorities) and detailed technical specifications of the camera are drawn from secondary commentary (Grokipedia, databases) rather than the primary SCP article; their canonical status is limited.
4. Comprehensive documentation of fan art, memes, and tabletop uses of SCP-978 is dispersed across platforms and not centrally archived; reception analysis is necessarily general rather than exhaustive.
5. Some extended test log entries involving SCP-001 proposals are heavily redacted or destroyed, leaving gaps in understanding of SCP-978’s interaction with foundational anomalies.
6. No formal in-universe theoretical paper on the mechanism of desire revelation (e.g., cognitive, memetic, or metaphysical) is available; interpretations remain speculative.
7. Non-English adaptations or translations of SCP-978 may exist but were not systematically surveyed; this dossier focuses on English-language sources up to July 2026.
