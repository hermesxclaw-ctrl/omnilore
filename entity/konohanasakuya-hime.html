Konohanasakuya-hime, the blossom princess, whose beauty is as brief as cherry petals and whose heart proved stronger than fire.

Who It Is

Konohanasakuya-hime ('Princess of Tree Blossoms') is the Shinto kami of cherry blossoms, Mount Fuji, and volcanic fire. She is the daughter of the mountain kami Ōyamatsumi. The heavenly grandson Ninigi (Amaterasu's descendant) first asked for the hand of her elder sister Iwanaga-hime (Princess of Long-Rock), who was ugly but represented longevity. When Ninigi rejected Iwanaga-hime and chose the beautiful Konohanasakuya-hime instead, Ōyamatsumi warned that rejecting longevity meant the imperial lineage would be mortal — explaining why Japanese emperors are not immortal. Konohanasakuya-hime became pregnant after one night with Ninigi, and when he doubted her faithfulness, she proved her chastity by enclosing herself in a burning wooden hut (or by giving birth in a ring of fire), emerging unharmed with her children. This myth associates her with volcanic fire and with the difficult but triumphant nature of childbirth. She is worshipped as the goddess of Mount Fuji — the Fujisan Hongū Sengen Taisha is her head shrine. She embodies the delicate beauty of cherry blossoms and the fierce, purifying power of volcanic fire. She is also a protector of women in childbirth.
