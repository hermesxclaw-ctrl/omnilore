Who It Is

In the tempestuous world of Fengshen Yanyi, where gods and mortals clash in a cosmic war, Bixiao Niangniang stands as a fiery spirit of vengeance and devotion. The youngest of the Three Celestial Maidens, she is a figure of fierce loyalty and tragic downfall, whose story echoes through Chinese folk religion as a protector of childbirth.

Bixiao is portrayed as outspoken, impulsive, and fearless, in contrast to her elder sister Yunxiao's calm demeanor. She is fiercely loyal to her brother Zhao Gongming and her sect, and she is quick to act on her emotions, particularly when seeking revenge. This impulsiveness ultimately leads to her downfall.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Bixiao' (碧霄) combines 'bi' (碧), meaning 'green jade' or 'green-blue', and 'xiao' (霄), meaning 'firmament' or 'sky'. Thus, it translates to 'Green Firmament' or 'Green Sky'. 'Niangniang' (娘娘) is a honorific for goddesses or imperial consorts.

Pronunciation — Bìxiāo Niángniang (Mandarin: /pi˥˩ ɕjɑʊ̯˥ njɑŋ˧˥ njɑŋ˧˥/)

Aliases — Zhao Bixiao, Bixiao Xianzi, Lady of the Green Firmament, Sanxiao Shengmu (as one of the three), Sanxiao Niangniang (as one of the three), Ganying Suishi Xiangu (posthumous deification)

Ownership — Chinese folk religion, Taoism, and the literary tradition of Fengshen Yanyi.

Powers
Golden Dragon Scissors (金蛟剪)

A pair of scissors that transform into two golden dragons, capable of cutting through most magical defenses and killing powerful beings.

Limit: Not effective against beings of higher Daoist attainment, such as Yuanshi Tianzun.

Chaos-Origin Gold Gourd (混元金斗)

A gourd that can suck in enemies and dissolve them into blood, trapping even immortals.

Limit: Ineffective against those who are not within its range or who possess certain protective talismans.

Nine-Curves Yellow River Formation (九曲黄河阵)

A complex battle formation that confuses and traps enemies, causing them to lose their magical powers and become blinded.

Limit: Can be broken by powerful deities such as Yuanshi Tianzun.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the novel, Bixiao is not described in great physical detail. She is depicted as a young woman of divine bearing, often dressed in Taoist robes. In folk iconography, she is typically portrayed alongside her sisters, often holding a golden gourd or scissors, symbolizing her powers. In temple statues, she may appear with a serene or stern expression, reflecting her dual nature as a martial and maternal goddess.

Symbols — Golden Dragon Scissors (金蛟剪) - a pair of scissors made from two golden dragons, capable of cutting through almost anything. Chaos-Origin Gold Gourd (混元金斗) - a gourd that can trap and dissolve enemies. The Nine-Curves Yellow River Formation (九曲黄河阵) - a powerful battle formation that traps and blinds enemies.

Domains — In the novel, she resides on Sanxian Island (三仙岛) with her sisters. In folk religion, she is worshipped in temples dedicated to the Sanxiao Niangniang, often located in Fujian, Taiwan, and other regions with Chinese diaspora communities.

Accounts I
The Death of Zhao Gongming and the Vengeance of the Three SistersFengshen Yanyi, chapters 50-51

Ming Dynasty (novel set in the Shang-Zhou transition)

After Zhao Gongming, the brother of the Three Celestial Maidens, is killed by Lu Ya Zhenren, Bixiao urges her sisters to avenge him. They join Wen Zhong's forces and use their magical artifacts against the Zhou army. They set up the Nine-Curves Yellow River Formation, which traps many of the Zhou generals and immortals. The formation is eventually broken by Yuanshi Tianzun, who then seals Bixiao in a treasure chest, causing her to dissolve into blood. After the Zhou victory, Jiang Ziya deifies the sisters as the 'Ganying Suishi Xiangu'.
