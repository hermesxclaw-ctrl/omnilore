Who It Is

In the world of Japanese horse racing, where legends are often defined by Triple Crown glory, Matikanetannhauser carved a different kind of legacy: a horse of remarkable resilience and late-blooming brilliance, whose name, a delicate dance of letters and tradition, tells a story of its own. From record-breaking victories to a quiet retirement marked by a gentle friendship, his tale is one of perseverance, unexpected quirks, and a second life in popular culture as a clumsy but beloved anime character.

Real horse: known for being relaxed in retirement, unbothered by a playful companion except during feeding. In Umamusume: cheerful, hardworking, but clumsy, often causing accidents and nosebleeds.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Named after the Richard Wagner opera 'Tannhäuser', with the owner's prefix 'Machikane' (冠名) added. The name was shortened to 'Matikane' to comply with international naming rules.

Pronunciation — ma-chi-ka-ne-tan-hai-za

Aliases — Matikane Tannhauser, マチカネタンホイザ (Japanese), Machikanetanhoiza (Hepburn romanization)

Ownership — Japanese horse racing culture; bred and raced in Japan.

Powers
Racing stamina

Ability to run long distances, evidenced by wins in the Diamond Stakes (3,400m) and Meguro Kinen (2,500m).

Limit: Not a sprinter; lacked speed in shorter Grade 1 races like the Takamatsunomiya Hai (1,600m) where he finished 4th.

Come-from-behind racing style

Often raced from the back of the pack and made late charges, as in the American Jockey Club Cup win.

Limit: Dependent on pace; could be compromised if the field went too slow.

In Umamusume: 'Ei, ei, mun!'

A catchphrase that boosts morale; in-game, it may be a skill or personality trait.

Limit: Fictional; no real-world equivalent.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a real horse: a bay Thoroughbred, typical of the breed. In Umamusume, she is depicted as a girl with brown hair and red accents, often with a clumsy posture.

Symbols — In Umamusume, her catchphrase 'Ei, ei, mun!' is iconic. Her design may include horse-ear motifs common to the series.

Domains — Raced at various Japanese tracks (Chukyo, Tokyo, Hanshin, etc.). Retired to Breeders Stallion Station, then Machikane Farm, finally Kosuda Farm in Yamanashi.

Accounts I
Debut and early promiseRacing records

1991-1992

Won debut at Chukyo in record time, then placed in stakes, but was consistently behind Mihono Bourbon in classic trials. Established as a talented but not top-tier horse.

1993: Breakthrough in staying racesRacing records

1993

Won the Diamond Stakes in record time and the Meguro Kinen, beating Rice Shower. Later won the May Stakes and Fuji Stakes. Earned a reputation as a top stayer.

Accounts II
1995: American Jockey Club Cup and Takamatsunomiya HaiRacing records

1995

Won the American Jockey Club Cup (his first clockwise win) and later the Takamatsunomiya Hai, his fourth graded victory. Capped his career with a Grade 1 win.

Retirement and pensionBreeding records and news

1996-2013

Stood at stud, siring 45 foals with moderate success. Pensioned in 2006, moved to Kosuda Farm in 2010, where he lived peacefully until his death from colic in 2013. Remembered fondly for his gentle nature in retirement.
