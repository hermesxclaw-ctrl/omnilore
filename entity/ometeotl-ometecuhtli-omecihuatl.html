Who It Is

Before there was anything, there was the void — and out of that void, an act no other Aztec god performs: Ōmeteōtl created itself. This 'Two-God' sits at the zero-point of Aztec cosmology, the self-springing unity that then split into a lord and a lady to make the world, that dwells in the thirteenth heaven above the nine tiers, that midwives told every newborn had shaped their soul down in the 'place of duality.' Yet for all its grandness, this supreme creator is the most contested deity in the Aztec pantheon: one of the most-cited scholars turned him into the Aztec's 'Lord of Duality,' while a challenger argues the whole Two-God is an artifact of colonial mistranslation — and that the real being behind the name is a bone-lord closer to the god of the dead. The story of Ōmeteōtl is not just a creation myth; it is a live argument about how much of Aztec 'monotheism' the Spanish compilers

Powers
Self-creation / autogenesis

According to the Five Suns legend, Ōmeteōtl 'created itself' from the void that was the rest of the universe — the first deity, with no prior source. This positions Ometeotl as the unmoved first cause of Aztec cosmology (the 'First Cause' gloss in the Codex Ríos).

Limit: This is a cosmological abstraction; Ometeotl's actual cultic agency is largely mediated through other gods.

Bi-gendered reproduction of creation

Ōmeteōtl was one as the first divinity but became two — Ometecuhtli and Omecihuatl — in order to reproduce all creation, modeling the cosmos's generative male/female duality (a form of dialectical monism where oppositional pairs are expressions of one unity).

Limit: The pair's procreation is abstract/cosmogonic rather than the genealogical begetting found in other pantheons.

Creation of the four Tezcatlipocas

In the Five Suns creation narrative, Ōmeteōtl created the four Tezcatlipocas — Quetzalcoatl (west), Huitzilopochtli (south), Xipe Totec (east), and Tezcatlipoca (north) — who then undertake the world-construction and sun-creation that powers the successive cosmic ages.

Limit: This specific genealogical relationship is a feature of the Five Suns synthesis; other sources treat these gods as self-arising or created differently.

Making of human souls & life-giving

As Tōnacātēcuhtli and Tōnacācihuātl, the pair 'created human souls and sent them to earth.' Sahagún records midwives addressing each newborn as having been formed by Ōmetēcuhtli and Ōmecihuātl in the place of duality. Tōnacātēcuhtli is a creator and fertility god 'worshipped for populating the earth and making it fruitful.'

Limit: Soul-making is framed as a descent from Omeyocan; it does not encompass death (which subtracts souls), the domain of Mictlantecuhtli.

Turning Quaxolotl into a dog

The one direct narrative action recorded: when the goddess Quaxolotl offended Tōnacātēcuhtli, he transformed her into a dog (itzcuintli). This is a punitive metamorphosis consistent with creator-gods who discipline lesser deities.

Limit: Thinly attested; the story is preserved only as a brief note and is not developed in any surviving major cycle.

Patronage of time & calendar origin

As Tōnacātēcuhtli, he was patron of Cipactli, the first of the twenty day-signs, and presided over the trecena named 1 Cipactli — placing the creator at the temporal head of the ritual calendar, a key connection-hub role in Aztec cosmology.

Limit: Calendar patronage is functional rather than a 'power' in the active sense.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As Ōmeteōtl in the earliest layer, the being is described in largely abstract/cosmological terms — a self-created 'First Cause' at the apex of the cosmos rather than a figure with a fixed iconography. Symbols — Residence in Ōmeyōcān, the thirteenth and highest heaven (the 'Place of Duality' above the nine-tiered heavens). Ometecuhtli/Omecihuatl are associated with the abstract sign of duality / two; Tōnacātēcuhtli with abundance, sustenance, and procreation. In the calendar he patronizes Cipactli (the first day-sign) and the 1 Cipactli trecena. Pre-Columbian procreative scenes (copulating couples) are his iconographic signature; the Codex Borg

Domains — Ōmeyōcān, the uppermost of the thirteen heavens, the 'place above the nine-tiered heavens' and 'the mansion of the Lord of Abundance' (in tōnacātēuctli īchān). From this apex, souls of humans descended to earth; it is the vertical origin-point of the Aztec cosmos as opposed to Mictlan, the underworld at the bottom.

Accounts I
Autogenesis and the VoidFive Suns / Legend of the Suns (Codex Chimalpopoca complex)

Recorded 16th century; mythic cosmogony

In the beginning there was the void that was the rest of the universe. From that void Ōmeteōtl created itself, the first god. It then created the four Tezcatlipocas — Quetzalcoatl (west), Huitzilopochtli (south), Xipe Totec (east), Tezcatlipoca (north) — who begin the laborious cycle of world-building. This is Ometeotl's foundational story: the transcendent instantiation above the pantheon from which the cosmos and its agents spring. Establishes Ometeotl as the highest cosmological principle; the four Tezcatlipocas proceed to create/destroy the successive Sun-ages.

The Two-Lord and Two-Lady of the Thirteenth HeavenFlorentine Codex; Historia de los mexicanos por sus pinturas; Histoyre du Mechique; Codex Ríos

16th century

At the summit of the cosmos, in Ōmeyōcān ('place of duality'), dwell the creator pair Ometecuhtli and Omecihuatl — or Tōnacātēcuhtli and Tōnacācihuātl ('Lord and Lady of Our Sustenance/Abundance'). They were 'always in the thirteenth heaven; nothing was ever known of their beginning.' From this mansion, human souls descend to earth. Sahagún's midwives invoke the heavenly lady: 'Your father and mother—Ōmetēcuhtli and Ōmecihuātl, the heavenly lady—formed you, created you.' The pair function as the ultimate parental source of every individual. Establishes the mythic warrant for the midwife's blessing and for human life originating in the celestial dual-god. It also names Omeyocan as the residence that is also a 'mansion of the Lord of Abundance.'

Accounts II
Quetzalcoatl's prayers to the creator-pairCodex Chimalpopoca / Legend of the Suns

16th-century record

During creation and ordering rites, Quetzalcoatl prays to several pairs of creator-gods, explicitly including Tōnacātēcuhtli and Tōnacācihuātl. This shows the dual creator invoked within the ritual frame of the primary culture-hero god, linking the highest cosmogonic principle to the active mythic drama. Ties Ometeotl into the Quetzalcoatl-centered creation complex; a key relationship-locus.

The Punishment of QuaxolotlTōnacātēcuhtli article (colonial records)

Colonial-era record

When the goddess Quaxolotl offended Tōnacātēcuhtli, the creator turned her into a dog. It is the lone directly narrated act of the creator-god's punitive power, a metamorphosis echoing other Mesoamerican deities' transformations of malefactors. The story is preserved only briefly and does not figure in the major cosmogonic cycles. Shows Tōnacātēcuhtli exercising disciplinary authority over the secondary pantheon.

The Dialectical Unity underlying oppositionAztec philosophy (school reading, esp. as synthesized by León-Portilla and Maffie)

Modern interpretive reconstruction of colonial fragments

Philosophers present Ōmeteōtl as the unity underlying the universe that 'forms, shapes, and is all things'; even opposites — light/dark, life/death — are expressions of the same unity. The deity model is described as a dialectical monism comparable to logocentric unity concepts in Western and Eastern philosophy. Priests are said to have held a panentheistic view of the many gods as aspects of this single transcendent unity, while the masses kept polytheism. Ometeotl becomes the centerpiece of the 'Aztec monotheism/pantheism' thesis that dominates modern pop-psychology of Aztec religion and fuels debate (Maffie vs the transcendental-reading).

Did Ōmeteōtl exist at all? (two-god vs no-god)León-Portilla (and many after): Ōmeteōtl was the actual supreme creator deity, a mystical dual-natured being at the apex of Aztec religion.Haly (1992): There was no Ōmeteōtl/Ometecuhtli/Omecihuatl; the colonial sources do not support a god of duality, and the name reads better as bone-lord (omiteuctli).
Transcendent creator vs immanent pantheismLeón-Portilla: Ometeotl is a transcendent, separate creator above the pantheon (God of Duality).Maffie: Aztec religion is pantheistic — teōtl IS the immanent world-process; a separate transcendental 'God of Duality' is therefore contradictory by definition.
Two-god (ōme) vs bone-god (omi) etymologyŌme ('two') → Ōmeteōtl 'Two-God / Dual God'; Ōmeyōcān 'place of duality'.Haly: root is omi ('bone') → Omitecuhtli 'Bone-Lord', a name overlapping Tōnacātēcuhtli and Mictlantecuhtli (bone-creation gods).
Single being vs gendered pairŌmeteōtl is one being that 'became two' (Ometecuhtli + Omecihuatl) to reproduce.The sources speak of two distinct dwellings deities (Tōnacātēcuhtli & Tōnacācihuātl), which Sahagún names as mere 'epithets' of the Ōme pair — i.e. a possibly loose conflation of two coexisting creator-couples.
Creator of the four Tezcatlipocas vs independent godsFive Suns: Ōmeteōtl created the four Tezcatlipocas (Quetzalcoatl, Huitzilopochtli, Xipe Totec, Tezcatlipoca) who then drive cosmogony.Most other source-families present those four gods as self-originated or created otherwise; Ometeotl's 'creator of the four' role is specific to the Five Suns synthesis, not a universal Aztec doctrine.
Relation to Mictlantecuhtli: complement vs identityMictlantecuhtli & Mictēcacihuātl are the life/death 'opposites and complements' of Ometecuhtli & Omecihuatl (distinct but paired).Haly collapses them: 'Omitecuhtli' (bone-lord) is an epithet overlapping Mictlantecuhtli, making the supposed 'life-giving dual god' actually a chthonic bone-god in disguise.
Calendar head (Cipactli/1-Cipactli) attributionTōnacātēcuhtli is the undisputed patron of the first day-sign Cipactli and the 1 Cipactli trecena.Some schemes assign day-sign patronages differently; the exact pairing is not universal across colonial tonalamatl sources.
