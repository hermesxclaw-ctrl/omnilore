Who It Is

Yaya is a fascinating and complex figure who continues to captivate scholars and enthusiasts alike. His role in the creation of the world and his relationships with other gods and goddesses are just a few of the many mysteries surrounding this enigmatic figure.

Yaya is often depicted as a wise and just ruler, who is fair and compassionate towards his people. He is also shown as a powerful and authoritative figure, who is not to be trifled with.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The etymology of Yaya is unclear, but it is believed to be derived from the ancient Sumerian word 'ya' meaning 'to exist' or 'to be'. Some scholars suggest that Yaya may be related to the Egyptian god Atum, who was also considered the supreme being and creator god.

Pronunciation — The pronunciation of Yaya is unknown, but it is likely to be a series of guttural sounds similar to the Sumerian language.

Aliases — Yaya, The Supreme Being, Father of All, The All-Father

Ownership — Yaya is a figure from ancient Sumerian mythology and is considered a cultural artifact of the Sumerian people.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Yaya is often depicted as a powerful, bearded man with a stern expression. He is sometimes shown wearing a crown or holding a staff, symbolizing his authority and power.

Symbols — Yaya is associated with the symbol of the sun and the staff, which represent his power and authority. He is also associated with the number 12, which represents completeness and perfection.

Domains — Yaya is often associated with the heavens and the sky, representing his power and authority over the cosmos. He is also associated with the earth and the land, representing his connection to the natural world.
