Who It Is

Yaya ('the Highest Person' or 'Grandfather') is the supreme being of Taíno mythology — a distant, all-father figure who existed before creation and whose actions set the cosmic order in motion. According to the creation cycle recorded by Fray Ramón Pané (who lived among the Taíno of Hispaniola in the 1490s), Yaya's story begins with his son Yayael ('Son of Yaya'), who attempted to kill his father. Yaya killed Yayael instead and placed his bones in a gourd (the 'first container' of life). The bones became fish — the first beings to populate the water. When the four brothers of creation (the Guaíza, born from Yaya's body or a female figure) broke the gourd, the waters poured forth to fill the earth's hollows, forming the sea. Yaya then ascended to the highest heaven, a realm of pure light from which he rarely intervenes in human affairs. Yaya was not worshipped in temples or with images; he was the 'background' of the universe, the implicit source whose presence undergirded all the more actively-engaged deities.
