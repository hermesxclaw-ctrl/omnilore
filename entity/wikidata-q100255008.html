Who It Is

Before the word was a hex on a baseball diamond, it was a bird—a small, twisting wryneck—whose very name meant a spell. From ancient Greek sorcery to a 1911 American slang term, the jinx has fluttered between bird, curse, and bad luck, leaving a trail of superstition across centuries and continents.

A jinx is believed to bring bad luck to a person, object, or endeavor. It is often invoked when discussing a potential success (e.g., a no-hitter).

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Latin 'iynx', from Greek 'iunx' (wryneck), a bird used in magic; associated with a sorceress named Iynx. The English 'jynx' (charm or spell) dates to 1690s; the spelling 'jinx' first attested in American English in 1911.

Pronunciation — /dʒɪŋks/

Aliases — jynx, jyng, iynx, jinx (spelling variant), hoodoo

Ownership — Western folklore, particularly English and American; rooted in ancient Greek and Roman superstition.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No physical appearance; a concept or curse. In ancient Greek art, the wryneck bird was depicted on magical wheels (iynx wheels).

Symbols — The wryneck bird, often depicted on a spinning wheel (iynx) used in love magic; later, a 'hoodoo' or hex symbol.

Domains — Folklore, superstition, sports (especially baseball), maritime culture.

Accounts I
The Myth of IynxAncient Greek mythology (referenced in Ovid and other sources)

Classical antiquity

Iynx was a nymph or sorceress who cast a spell on Zeus to make him fall in love with Io. In punishment, Hera transformed her into a wryneck bird, which was then used in love magic. The bird became associated with love spells and divination.

Mr. Jinx in Ballou's Monthly MagazineBallou's Monthly Magazine, Vol. 6, p. 276

1857

A character named Mr Jinx appears, one of the earliest uses of the name in print. Establishes 'Jinx' as a name or epithet in American popular culture.

Accounts II
Jinks Hoodoo in Little PuckMusical comedy 'Little Puck' (1887)

1887

A character named Jinks Hoodoo, described as 'a curse to everybody, including himself', appears in the musical comedy. The name was quickly picked up by the press, popularizing the term.

The Jinx (1910) by Allen SangreeThe Jinx: Stories of the Diamond (1911)

1910

A baseball short story where a player puts a 'jinx' on an opposing team, and a cross-eyed girl is seen as a jinx. Popularized the term 'jinx' in baseball context.
