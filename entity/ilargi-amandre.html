Who It Is

In the moonlit valleys of the Basque Country, where ancient oaks whisper secrets to the wind, there exists a goddess whose name means 'Mother Moon' — Ilargi Amandre. She is not a distant celestial queen, but a nurturing presence woven into the very fabric of Basque folklore, guiding both the tides of the sea and the hearts of her people.

As a maternal figure, she is benevolent and protective, especially of women, children, and the home. She is seen as a calm, guiding presence that watches over the night. She is also associated with fertility and the natural cycles of growth and harvest.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — In Basque, 'ilargi' means 'moon' (from 'hil' (month) + 'argi' (light), literally 'light of the month'), and 'amandre' means 'mother' or 'grandmother', thus 'Mother Moon'.

Pronunciation — ee-LAR-gee ah-MAHN-dreh

Aliases — Ilargi, Amandre Ilargi, Mother Moon

Ownership — Basque people, primarily in the Basque Country (Euskal Herria) spanning northern Spain and southwestern France.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description is recorded. She is generally imagined as a beautiful woman with a pale, glowing face, often depicted in modern art as a motherly figure with a crescent moon on her brow. In traditional folklore, she is not anthropomorphized in detail; the moon itself is the object of veneration.

Symbols — The crescent moon, the full moon, the night sky, the sea (due to tidal influence), and the cycle of the month. She is also associated with the color silver and with water.

Domains — The moon, the night sky, and by extension the sea and the earth's waters. She is also present in rural landscapes, particularly in places where lunar rituals were performed, such as hills and fields.

Accounts I
The Moon and the SunOral tradition; recorded by Barandiaran

Pre-Christian to modern times

Ilargi Amandre is said to be the companion of Eguzki Amandre (Mother Sun). They take turns crossing the sky, one by day and the other by night. They are both seen as maternal figures who bring light and life to the world. This story explains the daily cycle of day and night, and reinforces the importance of both celestial bodies in Basque cosmology.

Accounts II
The Moon's Blessing on the FieldsAgricultural customs; recorded in Caro Baroja

Historical agricultural practice

Farmers would plant crops according to the phases of the moon, believing that Ilargi Amandre's light influenced the growth of seeds. They would also pray to her for a good harvest. This practice reflects the deep connection between the lunar cycle and agricultural life, and shows the goddess's role in sustaining the community.
