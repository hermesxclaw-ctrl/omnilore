Who It Is

Mithra is the most prominent yazata (divine being 'worthy of worship') in the Zoroastrian pantheon after Ahura Mazda himself. His fundamental domain is the covenant (Av. mitra-m, 'contract, agreement') — the moral and cosmic principle that truth-telling, promise-keeping, and social bonds hold the world together. Mithra is the 'Lord of the Wide Pastures,' a warrior-shepherd who surveys the entire created order from his high vantage point and punishes those who break their word. While Mithra is closely associated with the sun in post-Avestan tradition (where he becomes the sun-god proper), in the Gathas of Zarathustra he is more abstract: the principle of contractual fidelity that sustains the asha (truth/order) against the lie (druj). Mithra's later Hellenized form, Mithras, became the center of the Roman mystery cult, but the Iranian Mithra is a distinctly Zoroastrian figure — a righteous warrior-judge who rides at dawn to defend truth and punish the perfidious.
