Who It Is

Kon is an ancient coastal creator-god from pre-Inca mythology (particularly the central coast of Peru, the region of the Ichma and Chancay cultures), absorbed into the Inca pantheon as a minor but distinctive creator figure. Kon was described as a 'god without bones' — a being of light, wind, and movement rather than substance — who could fly across the sky at will. In one version of the myth, Kon created the first inhabitants of the Peruvian coast, giving them abundant fruit and vegetables but no water, forcing them to rely entirely on his providence. When the people angered him, he cursed the coast to become the rainless desert it is today, and he was supplanted by Pachacamac, a more powerful creator who brought a more balanced cosmic order. Kon's cult was centered in the Nazca and Ica valleys, where his 'light and boneless' nature may relate to the Nazca lines (figures visible only from above).
