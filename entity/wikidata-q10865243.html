Who It Is

Three Buddhas sit side by side and time itself becomes visible. On the left, the Buddha who lit the lamp in a world before memory; in the center, the Buddha who awakened beneath a tree in our own age; on the right, the Buddha who waits in a celestial palace, laughing, bag over his shoulder, not yet born. In Chinese temples they are called Sanshi Fo — the Buddhas of the Three Eons — past, present, and future. They are not three gods, but one promise stretched across kalpas: that awakening has happened, is happening, and will happen again.

As Buddhas, all three are by definition awakened, compassionate, and beyond personal idiosyncrasy, but hagiography and devotion assign temporal roles. Dipamkara is the validator: the ancient Buddha who recognizes the future Shakyamuni (then the ascetic Sumedha/Megha) and grants vyakarana (prophecy of future Buddhahood), embodying lineage continuity. Shakyamuni is the historical teacher: ascetic, compassionate, pragmatic, willing to teach in a defiled age (saha world) despite its obduracy. Maitreya is the awaited, patient, and — in Budai form — jovial and accessible: a bodhisattva currently

Powers
Vyakarana (Prophecy of Future Buddhahood)

Dipamkara's defining power is to perceive the karmic maturity of an aspirant and irrevocably prophesy his future Buddhahood. In the Sumedha story he declares the matted-hair ascetic will become Shakyamuni, fixing his destiny across kalpas.

Limit: Prophecy can only be given to one who has perfected paramitas over incalculable eons and makes the vow in presence of a living Buddha; not arbitrarily granted.

Anuttara Samyak Sambodhi (Perfect Awakening) and Ten Powers (Dasa-balani)

All three possess the standard Buddha powers: knowledge of what is possible/impossible, of karmic retribution, of meditative concentrations, of faculties of beings, of dispositions, of previous lives, divine eye/ear, knowledge of defilements, etc. They can teach Dharma appropriate to any audience and manifest countless emanations.

Limit: Buddhas cannot undo karma for others, cannot teach without a suitable vessel, and (in present-eon doctrine) Shakyamuni cannot prevent the eventual decline of his Dharma — hence need for Maitreya.

Tusita Abiding and Future Descent (Maitreya-specific)

Maitreya currently abides in Tusita heaven, teaching devas, and will descend when human lifespan reaches 80,000 years, attain awakening under a dragon-flower tree (naga-puspa), and hold three assemblies (longhua sanhui) saving 92, 94, and 96 koṭis of beings respectively.

Limit: He cannot descend until conditions ripen: Shakyamuni's Dharma must be forgotten, Jambudvipa must be purified, and King Sankha must be present. He remains a bodhisattva, not yet a Buddha, until then.

Illumination (Dipamkara-specific)

Dipamkara is 'Lamp-Maker' — his appearance illuminates worlds, symbolically and literally dispelling darkness. Hagiography says at his birth all lamps lit spontaneously; his Dharma is described as lighting lamps for future Buddhas.

Limit: Symbolic power; not depicted as combat miracle in canonical texts.

Interpenetration of the Three Times (Huayan philosophical power)

In Huayan (Avatamsaka) exegesis, the three eon Buddhas interpenetrate: past contains present and future, present contains past and future (the 'ten times' theory). Vairocana as Dharmakaya embodies all three simultaneously, so worship of one is worship of all.

Limit: Doctrinal interpretation, not a miraculous power attributed in early sutras; depends on Huayan metaphysics.

Accounts I
Sumedha and Dipamkara's Prophecy — The Lamp is LitBuddhavamsa 2, Mahavastu I, Divyavadana 18; Chinese *Taizi Ruiying Benqi Jing* (T.185) and *Xianyu Jing* (T.202)

Indic core 1st century BCE - 2nd century CE; Chinese translations 3rd-5th century

In a past eon, the ascetic Sumedha (Chinese Sumoti, also called Megha) lives in the time of Dipamkara Buddha. Learning Dipamkara will pass through his city, Sumedha buys lotus flowers to offer, then seeing a muddy patch on the road, spreads his matted hair over the mire so Dipamkara need not soil his feet. Dipamkara pauses, looks at the prostrate ascetic, and grants vyakarana: 'This ascetic will become Shakyamuni Buddha in the future, in the present Auspicious Eon.' He predicts his clan, parents, disciples, and lifespan. Sumedha, overjoyed, accepts the prophecy and vows to perfect the paramitas. This establishes the karmic lineage that makes Shakyamuni's present Buddhahood possible and defines Dipamkara as the past Buddha who guarantees the future. Foundational Jataka/Nidana story recited at ordinations; establishes doctrine of bodhisattva vow across kalpas; iconographically justifies Dipamkara's position as past Buddha in Sanshi Fo triads; provides scriptural basis for lamp offerings (Randeng).

Shakyamuni's Awakening in the Present Auspicious Eon*Buddhacarita* of Asvaghosa, *Lalitavistara*, Pali *Mahaparinibbana Sutta*, Chinese *Fobenxing Ji Jing* (T.190)

1st-4th century CE texts; Chinese translations 6th century

After countless lives perfecting generosity, morality, and wisdom since Dipamkara's prophecy, Siddhartha Gautama is born in Kapilavastu in the present Xian kalpa. He renounces palace life, practices austerities, then sits beneath the Bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, defeats Mara, and attains anuttara samyak sambodhi, becoming the fourth Buddha of the present eon (after Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, Kasyapa). He turns the wheel of Dharma at Sarnath, teaches for 45 years, and enters parinirvana at Kushinagar. As present Buddha, he is the temporal center of the triad — the only one whose historical biography is treated as recent memory rather than prophecy. Defines the 'present' in Sanshi Fo; his Dharma is the one currently in decline, necessitating Maitreya's future mission; his relics and images become the devotional focus that Dipamkara anticipates and Maitreya will renew.

Maitreya in Tusita and the Three Assemblies of the Dragon Flower Tree*Mile Shangsheng Jing* (T.452, trans. Kumarajiva), *Mile Xiasheng Jing* (T.453), *Mile Dachen Jing* (T.456)

3rd-6th century CE

After Shakyamuni's parinirvana, the bodhisattva Maitreya dwells in the inner court of Tusita heaven, where he teaches devas and awaits his time. The sutras describe his future descent: when human lifespan, now declining, rises again to 80,000 years and the world is governed by the cakravartin King Sankha in Ketumati, Maitreya will be born as a brahmin's son, renounce, awaken under a dragon-flower tree (longhua shu, naga-puspa), and hold three assemblies under that tree, liberating 282 koṭis of beings in total (92+94+96). Those who have kept precepts, given alms, or merely recited Maitreya's name in Shakyamuni's age will be reborn in time to attend. Until then, devotees pray for rebirth in Tusita to study under him. Creates the Maitreya cult of rebirth in Tusita (parallel to Amitabha's Sukhavati); motivates vegetarian societies and millenarian movements awaiting Maitreya; fixes Maitreya as future Buddha in Sanshi Fo; inspires iconography of Maitreya with stupa crown and later Budai form.

Accounts II
The Three Thousand Buddhas of the Three Eons*Sanqian Fo Ming Jing* (T.446 Past, T.447 Present, T.448 Future), *Beihua Jing* (Karunapundarika Sutra, T.157)

5th-8th century CE

To systematize time, Chinese Buddhism enumerates 1,000 Buddhas for each eon: 1,000 of the past Adorned Eon (ending with Vipasyin, Sikhin, Visvabhu), 1,000 of the present Auspicious Eon (beginning with Krakucchanda and including Shakyamuni as 4th), and 1,000 of the future Constellation Eon (beginning with Maitreya). The sutra lists their names for repentance rituals (li fo). The Sanshi Fo triad is thus a shorthand: one representative per thousand. Repentance rites (e.g., Liang Emperor Repentance) recite all 3,000 names, prostrating to past Buddhas for forgiveness, present Buddhas for guidance, future Buddhas for aspiration. Liturgical basis for Sanshi Fo halls where three images stand for 3,000; annual repentance ceremonies still performed in Chinese temples; explains why some temples have 3,000 small Buddha niches surrounding the central triad.

The Monk Qici Becomes Budai — Maitreya Walks Among Us*Song Gaoseng Zhuan* (Song Biographies of Eminent Monks, 988 CE) by Zanning, *Jingde Chuandeng Lu* (1004 CE)

10th century historical monk; legend codified 10th-12th century

In late Tang / Five Dynasties, an eccentric monk named Qici (契此) wandered the Jiangnan region, fat, bald, carrying a cloth sack (budai), begging, predicting weather, entertaining children, claiming no fixed temple. At his death near Fenghua (Zhejiang) in 917 CE, he left a verse: 'Maitreya, true Maitreya, reborn countless times, time and again manifested to people, yet people do not recognize him.' Monks took this as revelation that Qici was an incarnation (huashen) of Maitreya. From Song onward, images of the laughing, big-bellied Budai replaced the slender princely Maitreya in many Sanshi Fo triads and standalone shrines, especially at temple entrances (Tianwang Dian). The story collapses future into present: Maitreya is not only awaited but already among us in humble form. Iconographic transformation of future Buddha in East Asia; Budai becomes one of the most globally recognized 'Buddhas' though doctrinally still a bodhisattva; explains why many Western museums label Budai statues as 'Maitreya/Budai' and why Sanshi Fo triads often show a laughing figure on the right.

Huayan Interpenetration — All Three Times in One Thought*Huayan Jing* (Avatamsaka) 80-fascicle version (T.279), Fazang (643-712) *Huayan Yisheng Jiaoyi Fenqi Zhang*, *Tanxuan Ji*

7th-8th century Chinese commentarial systematization

Huayan patriarchs used the Sanshi Fo to illustrate 'time interpenetration' (shishi wuai). In Fazang's essay, past, present, and future are not linear but mutually containing: each thought-moment (yinian) contains the three eons. Vairocana (Pilu Zhanna Fo) as Dharmakaya embodies all three times simultaneously, while Dipamkara, Shakyamuni, and Maitreya are his temporal emanations (nirmana). A famous Huayan anecdote: Empress Wu Zetian asked Fazang to demonstrate interpenetration; he pointed to a golden lion statue and to the Sanshi Fo hall, saying as the lion's each hair contains the lion, each eon contains the others. This philosophical reading elevates the triad from historical sequence to metaphysical principle, influencing monumental triads where central Vairocana replaces Shakyamuni. Justifies variant triad Vairocana-Shakyamuni-Maitreya or Vairocana-Rocana-Shakyamuni; influences monumental bronze triads at Longmen and Todai-ji; provides doctrinal bridge between temporal Sanshi Fo and ontological Trikaya, source of later conflation.

Who is the Past Buddha? Dipamkara vs. Kasyapa vs. VairocanaPast Buddha is Dipamkara (Randeng Fo), the lamp-maker who prophesied Shakyamuni. This is the standard Han Chinese temple triad and matches Buddhavamsa lineage.Past Buddha is Kasyapa (or Vipasyin) as immediate predecessor in Seven Past Buddhas list; or Vairocana as timeless Dharmakaya representing past. Tibetan and Huayan temples use these alternatives.
Is Maitreya Already Here as Budai?Maitreya remains in Tusita, not yet a Buddha; Budai was a manifestation but not final Buddhahood. Future descent is still awaited when Dharma is forgotten.Budai was Maitreya incarnate, therefore Maitreya has already come and Sanshi Fo are all present. Some folk temples treat Budai as fully enlightened future Buddha already among us.
Temporal vs. Ontological Triad — Are Sanshi Fo and Trikaya the Same?Sanshi Fo (three times) and Sanshen Fo/Trikaya (three bodies) are distinct: one is chronological, one is ontological. Conflating them is a modern tourist error.Huayan synthesis holds they are ultimately non-dual: Vairocana (Dharmakaya) manifests as Dipamkara/Shakyamuni/Maitreya, so temporal triad is an expression of Trikaya.

The three Buddhas are the same as the 'Three Bodies of Buddha' (Trikaya) or represent 'past, present, future lives of Shakyamuni.' — Sanshi Fo are three different Buddhas who preside over three eons: Dipamkara (past), Shakyamuni (present), Maitreya (future). Trikaya is a different doctrine about three aspects of one Buddha (Dharmakaya, Sambhogakaya, Nirmanakaya). Shakyamuni's past lives are Jataka tales, not Dipamkara. The confusion arises because both use 'three' and both appear as three statues, and because Huayan philosophy later linked them.

The fat laughing Buddha is the present Buddha (Shakyamuni) or a separate deity called 'Laughing Buddha.' — The fat laughing figure is Budai, the 10th-century monk Qici identified as an incarnation of Maitreya — the future Buddha. He is not Shakyamuni (who is shown slender, in monastic robes, often touching the earth) and not a separate god. In Sanshi Fo triads he appears on the right as the future aspect, not the center.

Maitreya will come soon to save the world, and several modern leaders have been Maitreya. — Canonical sutras place Maitreya's descent in the distant future when human lifespan is 80,000 years and Shakyamuni's Dharma is forgotten — not imminent. Claims by rebel leaders (6th century to White Lotus) and modern cult leaders to be Maitreya are heterodox and have been repeatedly condemned by orthodox Buddhism and criminalized by Chinese law.

Dipamkara is a fictional Chinese invention with no Indian source. — Dipamkara is attested in Pali Buddhavamsa, Sanskrit Mahavastu and Divyavadana, and Gandharan art (2nd-3rd century) as the Buddha who prophesied Shakyamuni. Chinese Randeng is a faithful translation of Sanskrit Dipamkara ('lamp-maker'), not an invention, though his iconographic lamp attribute was elaborated in China.
