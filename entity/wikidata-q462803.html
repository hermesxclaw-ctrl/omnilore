Who It Is

When the sun died and the world went dark, the gods didn't pray. They threw a party. And the woman who saved the world wasn't a warrior or a sage — she was a dancer who stripped down, stomped on a bucket, and made the heavens laugh so hard that the sun herself had to come out and see what all the fuss was about. This is the story of Ame no Uzume, the goddess who partied the light back into existence.

Ame no Uzume is characterized by her boldness, wit, and uninhibited nature. She is the only deity in the Kojiki who dares to challenge the despair of the other gods with laughter and dance. She is a trickster figure, but her tricks are always in service of a greater good. She is also a mediator, using her performance to draw Amaterasu out of her cave and restore light to the world.

Powers
Divine Dance (Kagura)

Ame no Uzume's most famous power. Her dance is not merely entertainment; it is a ritual act capable of altering the emotional and spiritual state of the heavens. Her dance is said to be the origin of kagura, the sacred dance performed at Shinto shrines. It is a power of joy, laughter, and transformation.

Limit: The dance is most effective when performed in a state of genuine abandon and joy. A forced or insincere performance would lack its divine potency.

Divine Persuasion / Mediation

Ame no Uzume is a master of persuasion, able to influence even the most stubborn of deities. She uses her wit, charm, and performance to change the course of events. This is most clearly seen in the cave episode, where she convinces Amaterasu to emerge.

Limit: Her persuasion is indirect; she works through emotional and psychological means rather than direct confrontation or force.

Divine Voice / Chant

In the Nihon Shoki, Ame no Uzume is said to have chanted a ritual invocation as part of her dance. This suggests a power of spoken or sung words to influence the divine realm.

Limit: The exact nature of this power is not detailed in the text.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ame no Uzume is typically depicted as a beautiful, youthful goddess with flowing black hair, often wearing the traditional attire of a Shinto priestess (a white kimono with a red hakama). In artistic depictions of the cave scene, she is shown with a spear or a sakaki branch in hand, and sometimes with a fan. Her expression is often lively and mischievous, capturing her role as the divine entertainer.

Symbols — The overturned tub (or bucket) she stomped on, the sakaki tree branch, the spear, the fan, and the mirror (which was used to lure Amaterasu out). Her dance itself is a symbol of the power of joy and creativity to overcome sorrow and chaos.

Domains — Ame no Uzume is the goddess of dance, theater, entertainment, and the arts. She is also associated with divine mediation, joy, and the dispelling of darkness and despair. She is a patron of performing artists, especially dancers and actors.

Accounts I
The Dance Before the Heavenly CaveKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Mythic age (Kamiyo)

When the sun goddess Amaterasu, distraught over the violence of her brother Susanoo, hid herself in a cave, the world was plunged into darkness. The eight hundred myriad gods gathered at the dry bed of the Heavenly River to devise a plan. They brought a mirror, jewels, and a sacred sakaki tree. Ame no Uzume, the goddess of dance, then performed a wild and comedic dance. She stripped off her clothing, stomped on an overturned tub, and made the gods laugh uproariously. Curious, Amaterasu peeked out of the cave, saw her own radiant reflection in the mirror, and was drawn out, restoring light to the world. Light is restored to the world. Amaterasu returns to her rightful place in the heavens. Ame no Uzume is celebrated as the originator of sacred dance and theater.

Accounts II
The Descent of Ninigi (Ame no Uzume's role)Kojiki, Nihon Shoki

Mythic age (Kamiyo)

When the heavenly gods decided to send Ninigi, the grandson of Amaterasu, to rule the earthly realm, Ame no Uzume accompanied him as a guide and protector. She was sent ahead to clear the way and establish a path for the divine descent. Ame no Uzume's role as a guide and mediator between the heavenly and earthly realms is established. She is associated with the imperial lineage and the establishment of the Japanese state.

Ame no Uzume is a minor or unimportant goddess. — She is a central figure in the most famous myth in Shinto, the restoration of light to the world. She is also the patron of all performing arts in Japan.

Her dance was a simple 'striptease' or a joke. — It was a profound ritual act rooted in shamanic tradition. The nudity was a form of divine ecstasy, not mere entertainment. It was a powerful act of spiritual transformation.

She is a 'trickster' in the same vein as Loki or Coyote. — While she uses wit and deception, her tricks are always in service of the cosmic order and the greater good. She is not a chaotic or malicious figure.
