This page is intentionally blank and ready for source-backed lore, versions, images, and connections. Ame no Uzume is the goddess of dance, theater, entertainment, and the arts. She is also associated with divine mediation, joy, and the dispelling of darkness and despair. She is a patron of performing artists, especially dancers and actors.

Attested in Wikidata public knowledge graph — Q462803

Form

Ame no Uzume is typically depicted as a beautiful, youthful goddess with flowing black hair, often wearing the traditional attire of a Shinto priestess (a white kimono with a red hakama). In artistic depictions of the cave scene, she is shown with a spear or a sakaki branch in hand, and sometimes with a fan. Her expression is often lively and mischievous, capturing her role as the divine entertainer.

Powers

Divine Dance (Kagura) Ame no Uzume's most famous power. Her dance is not merely entertainment; it is a ritual act capable of altering the emotional and spiritual state of the heavens. Her dance is said to be the origin of kagura, the sacred dance performed at Shinto shrines. It is a power of joy, laughter, and transformation. Kojiki and Nihon Shoki (both) The dance is most effective when performed in a state of genuine abandon and joy. A forced or insincere performance would lack its divine potency. None

Divine Persuasion / Mediation Ame no Uzume is a master of persuasion, able to influence even the most stubborn of deities. She uses her wit, charm, and performance to change the course of events. This is most clearly seen in the cave episode, where she convinces Amaterasu to emerge. Kojiki and Nihon Shoki (both) Her persuasion is indirect; she works through emotional and psychological means rather than direct confrontation or force. None

Divine Voice / Chant In the Nihon Shoki, Ame no Uzume is said to have chanted a ritual invocation as part of her dance. This suggests a power of spoken or sung words to influence the divine realm. Nihon Shoki The exact nature of this power is not detailed in the text. None

Behavior & Habitat

Ame no Uzume is characterized by her boldness, wit, and uninhibited nature. She is the only deity in the Kojiki who dares to challenge the despair of the other gods with laughter and dance. She is a trickster figure, but her tricks are always in service of a greater good. She is also a mediator, using her performance to draw Amaterasu out of her cave and restore light to the world.

Accounts

When the sun goddess Amaterasu, distraught over the violence of her brother Susanoo, hid herself in a cave, the world was plunged into darkness. The eight hundred myriad gods gathered at the dry bed of the Heavenly River to devise a plan. They brought a mirror, jewels, and a sacred sakaki tree. Ame no Uzume, the goddess of dance, then performed a wild and comedic dance. She stripped off her clothing, stomped on an overturned tub, and made the gods laugh uproariously. Curious, Amaterasu peeked out of the cave, saw her own radiant reflection in the mirror, and was drawn out, restoring light to the world.

When the heavenly gods decided to send Ninigi, the grandson of Amaterasu, to rule the earthly realm, Ame no Uzume accompanied him as a guide and protector. She was sent ahead to clear the way and establish a path for the divine descent.

Modern Interpretations

Theater Kagura (sacred dance) Various Shinto traditions Ancient to present direct Ame no Uzume is considered the founder and patron of kagura, the ritual dance performed at Shinto shrines. Her dance is reenacted in various forms throughout Japan. Literature The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki Various 712, 720 CE direct The primary sources of her myth. Anime / Manga Various series, including 'Noragami', 'Okami-san and Her Seven Companions', 'Kamichama Karin' Various 2000s-present inspired Ame no Uzume appears as a character in several anime and manga, often as a cheerful, eccentric, and powerful female deity, echoing her mythological role. Video Game Okami Clover Studio / Capcom 2006 inspired The game's protagonist, Amaterasu, is a sun goddess, and the game's narrative is heavily inspired by the cave episode. Ame no Uzume is referenced as a character in the game's lore. Video Game Persona series Atlus 1996-present inspired Ame no Uzume appears as a persona (a summonable entity) in several Persona games, often associated with dance and support abilities. Internet Culture Memes and fan art Various 2010s-present reference Ame no Uzume is a popular subject of fan art and memes, particularly in contexts related to dance, comedy, and 'saving the world with a smile.'

Scholarly Debates
The nature of her dance
The Kojiki describes a wild, ecstatic, and ribald dance involving nudity. ⇌ The Nihon Shoki describes a more structured, theatrical performance with props.
Evidence for A: Kojiki text
Evidence for B: Nihon Shoki text
The purpose of her dance
It was a spontaneous expression of joy and divine inspiration. ⇌ It was a calculated, strategic performance designed to manipulate Amaterasu.
Evidence for A: The Kojiki's emphasis on the gods' laughter and the 'divine possession' she experiences.
Evidence for B: The Nihon Shoki's more detailed and deliberate description of the performance, as well as the pre-planned strategy of Om
What We Don't Know

The exact nature of the 'divine possession' mentioned in the Kojiki is not well understood. The connection between Ame no Uzume and specific historical shamanic practices is a subject of ongoing scholarly debate. A comprehensive study of her iconography in Japanese art has not been completed.
