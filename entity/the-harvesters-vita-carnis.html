Who It Is

The final, stationary stage of the Mimic life cycle. An Elder Mimic that has consumed enough prey will root itself into the ground and transform into a Harvester — a tree-like organism that produces Crawler eggs continuously. Harvesters are the reproductive engines of the Carnis ecosystem. They are immobile but not defenseless: their 'branches' are prehensile and can impale threats within a 15-meter radius. The documentary shows footage of a Harvester 'blooming' — releasing thousands of Crawlers into the wind in a pink mist of infant organisms.

Form

A massive, trunk-like organism, 5-15m tall, rooted in the ground. Its 'bark' is hardened gray flesh. Its 'branches' are elongated Mimic arms ending in grasping claws. Its 'crown' is a cluster of pulsating egg sacs that glow faintly pink. The Elder Mimic's frozen face is visible at the base of the trunk, eyes now hollow cavities through which Crawlers enter and exit.

Powers

Continuous Crawler production

Prehensile branch attack (15m radius)

Root-based nutrient absorption

Wind-dispersal of offspring

Beings of the same nature, imagined by other peoples.

The Harvester closes the Mimic life cycle loop — showing that the Carnis is not just a collection of monsters but a complete, self-sustaining ecosystem that is slowly replacing Earth's.
