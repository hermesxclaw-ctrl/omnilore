You're walking through your office building after hours.

Who It Is

You're walking through your office building after hours. You take a shortcut through a door you've never noticed before. The door closes behind you. You're in a hallway. Yellow wallpaper. Fluorescent lights that hum at the exact frequency of a migraine. The carpet is damp. The hallway goes left, right, and forward, each direction identical and infinite. You walk for hours. Days. The lights never turn off. There are no windows. No doors except the one you came through, and it's not there anymore. Then you hear it: footsteps. Not yours. Something is in here with you. Something that has been here a lot longer than you have. And it has stopped pretending to be human.

The Backrooms is a concept born from a single 4chan post in 2019, which presented a photograph of a disorienting yellow room with fluorescent lighting and the caption: 'If you're not careful and you noclip out of reality in the wrong areas, you'll end up in the Backrooms, where it's nothing but the stink of old moist carpet, the madness of mono-yellow, the endless background noise of fluorescent lights at maximum hum-buzz, and approximately six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms to be trapped in. God save you if you hear something wandering around nearby, because it sure as hell has heard you.' From this single post, an entire collaborative universe emerged: the Backrooms wiki, with thousands of user-generated 'levels' (floors of infinite, surreal liminal space), entities (things that live in the Backrooms), and communities of 'wanderers' trying to survive and find an exit.

The Backrooms represents liminal horror — the fear of transitional spaces, of the uncanny, of the mundane made monstrous by removal of context. The yellow rooms with their humming lights are not scary because they contain monsters but because they contain nothing, and the nothing goes on forever. The Backrooms is the horror of the infinite office building, the abandoned mall, the pool at midnight, the hotel corridor that should end but doesn't. It is the feeling you get in a place that should be full of people but is empty, amplified to cosmic scale. It is the worst possible version of being lost: lost in a space that has no exit, no geography, and no rules — except that you are not alone.

Emergence

The Backrooms was created by an anonymous 4chan user on May 12, 2019, on the /x/ (paranormal) board. The original post was responding to a thread asking for 'images that feel strangely familiar but unsettling.' The anonymous user posted the now-famous photo of a yellow-tinted room with fluorescent lights and peeling wallpaper (later traced to a 2018 renovation of a former furniture store) and wrote the original 'noclip' description. The concept exploded. By 2020, multiple Backrooms wikis had been established, the most prominent being the Backrooms Wikidot. The mythology expanded from a single infinite level (Level 0: The Lobby) to hundreds of levels, each with its own horror: Level 1 (Habitable Zone — an infinite warehouse), Level 2 (Pipe Dreams — an infinite network of hot, dark maintenance tunnels), Level 3 (Electrical Station), Level 4 (Abandoned Office), and so on. YouTube creators like Kane Pixels produced found-footage series set in the Backrooms, bringing the concept to mainstream audiences with millions of views.

Form

The Backrooms does not have a single appearance — each 'level' is different. The classic Level 0 is the most iconic: an infinite maze of rooms separated by partition walls, with mono-yellow wallpaper, fluorescent lights that hum constantly, beige carpeting that's slightly damp for no reason, and an ambient temperature of 74°F with no ventilation. Other levels include abandoned hospitals, empty swimming pools, endless libraries, fog-shrouded cities, and geometries that violate Euclidean space. The consistent elements are: fluorescent lighting (usually humming), a sense of being indoors despite the infinite scale, decay (peeling wallpaper, damp carpet, rust), and the absolute absence of other people — except for the entities. The Backrooms is not a place of dramatic horror but of profound, stomach-dropping wrongness. Everything looks like somewhere you've been, but no place has ever been this empty or this large.

Noclip Entry The method of entry into the Backrooms. It occurs when a person 'noclips' out of reality, a glitch in the simulation of reality, usually by falling through a floor or passing through a wall in an empty or mundane area. All versions Requires a 'glitch' in reality. It is not a deliberate or reliable method. Most who enter do so by accident. Some versions require a state of mind (e.g., extreme boredom or detachment) to trigger the noclip.

Non-Euclidean Geometry The space of the Backrooms is not bound by normal physics. Rooms are larger on the inside than the outside, hallways loop, and paths that should lead to a destination lead back to the start. It is impossible to map the space accurately, and it is believed to be procedurally generated, infinite, and constantly shifting. All versions The geometry is 'hostile' to navigation but not to life. It is a passive, inescapable maze. Some levels have a 'center' or a 'border,' while others are truly infinite. The Wikidot canon has levels with finite, but vast, boundaries.

Entity Manifestation The Backrooms is home to countless entities. These range from the passive and harmless to the predatory and hostile. The most iconic is 'The Hound,' a dog-like creature with a human-like laugh. Others include 'The Skin-Stealer,' a humanoid that wears the skin of its victims, and 'The Faceling,' a humanoid with no face. The presence of entities is often unpredictable, and they seem to be a part of the environment, not visitors to it. All versions Entities are not omnipotent. They can be avoided, outrun, or, in some cases, fought. Many are confined to specific levels. The nature of the entities is debated. Some are natural fauna of the Backrooms, while others are thought to be transformed humans or escaped experiments from other levels.

Psychological Manipulation The Backrooms environment is a psychological weapon. The monotony, isolation, and sensory deprivation are designed to erode sanity. It induces paranoia, hallucinations, and a state of 'losing one's mind.' The silence and the hum of the lights are used to create a constant, low-level stress that eventually breaks the will. All versions This is a passive effect, not an active attack. It takes time to work, and some individuals are more resistant than others. Some levels are designed to be 'safe' zones, like the Hub, which offers respite from the madness. The effect is not universal.

Behavior & Habitat

The Backrooms is a passive horror — it does not actively hunt you. It simply contains you. You are trapped, and the space itself is the antagonist: the endless sameness, the isolation, the sensory deprivation (or overload — the hum of fluorescent lights is a constant torture), the slow breakdown of sanity. Wanderers report that loneliness is the worst threat. The entities that live in the Backrooms are inhabitants, not guardians — they are beings that evolved here, that have adapted to this impossible ecosystem. They hunt wanderers because wanderers are prey, not because the Backrooms has directed them to. The Backrooms does not communicate, does not reward, does not punish. It is the ultimate indifferent universe — a literal one.

Weaknesses & Wards

The Backrooms has no known weaknesses — it is a dimension, not a creature. Escape is possible theoretically — certain levels have exits, and with luck, determination, and knowledge of the 'levels' (shared by wanderers), one might find a way out. Almond water (which appears in Level 4 and other levels) provides sustenance and has minor healing properties. Certain levels are 'safe' — habitable by wanderer communities that have established outposts. Knowing 'the rules' — don't trust the lights, don't follow the sounds, keep moving, never stay on one level too long — increases survival chances. The Backrooms itself cannot be destroyed; it can only be navigated and, with immense luck, escaped.

Accounts

The foundational text. A user posts an image of a mono-yellow, empty office space and invents the Backrooms. The post describes the space as a 'noclip' accident, a place of 'six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms.' It is a single paragraph that spawned an entire genre.

A massive, collaborative writing project that expands the Backrooms into a complex multiverse. It includes hundreds of levels, each with its own description, entities, and lore. It introduces factions like the M.E.G., a group of explorers who document and map the Backrooms, and a complex history of 'The First Contact.' The canon is known for its 'serious,' pseudo-scientific tone and its focus on exploration and survival rather than pure horror.

A parallel wiki that is more accessible and creature-focused. It includes many of the same levels as the Wikidot canon but with a greater emphasis on entities as monsters. It is often seen as less 'serious' and more open to fan fiction and gaming-inspired content. It is the canon most commonly referenced in YouTube videos and short-form content.

A critically acclaimed series of found-footage videos that reimagines the Backrooms as a government experiment. The story follows a group of scientists who create a portal to the Backrooms, only to have their research facility breached by its entities. The series is noted for its high production value, unsettling atmosphere, and unique take on the lore.

Field Notes

⁙ The original Backrooms photo was taken of a renovation project at a former HobbyTown USA store in Wisconsin — a perfectly mundane room made terrifying by context.

⁙ 'Noclipping' is a term from video games (particularly Source engine games) where players glitch through solid walls — the Backrooms applies this concept to reality itself.

⁙ The Backrooms Wikidot has catalogued over 1,000 levels, 300+ entities, and dozens of wanderer 'groups' — a fully realized alternate dimension built entirely by volunteers.

⁙ Almond water is the Backrooms' universal resource — it appears in abandoned rooms and has nutritional, healing, and minor reality-anchoring properties.

⁙ Kane Pixels' Backrooms series was so successful that A24 — the prestigious indie studio — optioned it for a feature-length film, making it possibly the first 4chan post-to-major-motion-picture pipeline.

Cultural Significance

The Backrooms is the defining internet horror mythos of the early 2020s, continuing the lineage from Slender Man (2009) and SCP (2007). It represents a new kind of horror: not monsters or jump scares but atmosphere, wrongness, and the uncanny. The Backrooms' collaborative nature — a wiki endlessly expanded by anonymous contributors — mirrors the SCP Foundation but with a focus on liminal spaces rather than anomalous objects. The concept has resonated deeply in the post-pandemic era, when lockdowns forced people to confront the emptiness of familiar spaces. The Backrooms has become a visual aesthetic (liminal space photography, the 'backrooms aesthetic') and a philosophical meditation on isolation and the nature of reality.

Modern Interpretations

The Backrooms has been adapted into multiple found-footage film series (Kane Pixels, Matt Studios, Async Research), video games ('The Backrooms Game,' 'Escape the Backrooms,' various Roblox and Unity experiences), a pilot for a television series by A24 (announced 2023, directed and created by Kane Pixels), and countless YouTube documentaries and analysis videos. The 'level' system has become a collaborative worldbuilding project comparable to the SCP Foundation. The concept of 'liminal spaces' — transitional, uncanny locations that feel both familiar and deeply wrong — has entered the cultural lexicon, with subreddits and Instagram pages dedicated to liminal photography. The Backrooms demonstrates how internet mythology continues to evolve: from single post to shared universe to transmedia franchise, all without corporate ownership.

What We Don't Know

The primary gap is the lack of a single, authoritative canon. The Backrooms is a collaborative, ever-evolving project, and any single page can only capture a snapshot of its current state. The research is also limited by the ephemeral nature of internet content; many early 4chan threads and forum posts have been lost. The exact number of levels and entities is constantly changing, and it is impossible to document them all.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of the Backrooms not as a place, but as a state of being. It is the feeling of being lost in a bureaucratic nightmare, the anxiety of a void that is both familiar and alien. It is the internet's version of the 'Twilight Zone' — a place where the rules of reality are suspended, and the only constant is the hum of the machines that keep the lights on.
