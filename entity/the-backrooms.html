Who It Is

Imagine stepping through a door in an empty office building and finding yourself in a world of endless, buzzing, yellow rooms — a place where the very geometry has given up on being logical, and the only sound is the hum of a light that never turns off. This is the Backrooms, a nightmare born not from the mind of a single author, but from the collective unconscious of the internet. It is a horror story for the digital age, a place where the boundaries between reality and simulation break down, and the only way out is to never have gone in.

The Backrooms itself is a passive, indifferent trap. It does not attack, but it does not let go. It is a space that is fundamentally 'wrong,' causing a deep, primal unease (liminality). It is a maze that resists mapping, with geometry that changes when unobserved. The primary 'behavior' is entrapment and psychological erosion. The environment is designed to induce a state of 'subliminal paranoia' and madness from sensory deprivation and monotony. Powers
Noclip Entry

The method of entry into the Backrooms. It occurs when a person 'noclips' out of reality, a glitch in the simulation of reality, usually by falling through a floor or passing through a wall in an empty or mundane area.

Limit: Requires a 'glitch' in reality. It is not a deliberate or reliable method. Most who enter do so by accident.

Non-Euclidean Geometry

The space of the Backrooms is not bound by normal physics. Rooms are larger on the inside than the outside, hallways loop, and paths that should lead to a destination lead back to the start. It is impossible to map the space accurately, and it is believed to be procedurally generated, infinite, and constantly shifting.

Limit: The geometry is 'hostile' to navigation but not to life. It is a passive, inescapable maze.

Entity Manifestation

The Backrooms is home to countless entities. These range from the passive and harmless to the predatory and hostile. The most iconic is 'The Hound,' a dog-like creature with a human-like laugh. Others include 'The Skin-Stealer,' a humanoid that wears the skin of its victims, and 'The Faceling,' a humanoid with no face. The presence of entities is often unpredictable, and they seem to be a part of the environment, not visitors to it.

Limit: Entities are not omnipotent. They can be avoided, outrun, or, in some cases, fought. Many are confined to specific levels.

Psychological Manipulation

The Backrooms environment is a psychological weapon. The monotony, isolation, and sensory deprivation are designed to erode sanity. It induces paranoia, hallucinations, and a state of 'losing one's mind.' The silence and the hum of the lights are used to create a constant, low-level stress that eventually breaks the will.

Limit: This is a passive effect, not an active attack. It takes time to work, and some individuals are more resistant than others.

Accounts I
The Original 4chan Post (2019)4chan /x/ board

2019

The foundational text. A user posts an image of a mono-yellow, empty office space and invents the Backrooms. The post describes the space as a 'noclip' accident, a place of 'six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms.' It is a single paragraph that spawned an entire genre. Created the core concept and aesthetic. The image and text were immediately shared and became a viral meme, leading to the creation of the Wikidot and Fandom wikis.

The Wikidot Canon (2019-present)backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com

2019-present

A massive, collaborative writing project that expands the Backrooms into a complex multiverse. It includes hundreds of levels, each with its own description, entities, and lore. It introduces factions like the M.E.G., a group of explorers who document and map the Backrooms, and a complex history of 'The First Contact.' The canon is known for its 'serious,' pseudo-scientific tone and its focus on exploration and survival rather than pure horror. Established the most detailed and 'official' version of the Backrooms mythos. It is the source for most in-depth lore discussions and is considered the 'true' canon by many fans.

Accounts II
The Fandom Canon (2019-present)backrooms.fandom.com

2019-present

A parallel wiki that is more accessible and creature-focused. It includes many of the same levels as the Wikidot canon but with a greater emphasis on entities as monsters. It is often seen as less 'serious' and more open to fan fiction and gaming-inspired content. It is the canon most commonly referenced in YouTube videos and short-form content. Created a more accessible and 'pop-culture' version of the Backrooms. It is often the first introduction to the concept for many people.

Kane Pixels' 'The Backrooms (Found Footage)' (2022)YouTube, Kane Pixels

2022

A critically acclaimed series of found-footage videos that reimagines the Backrooms as a government experiment. The story follows a group of scientists who create a portal to the Backrooms, only to have their research facility breached by its entities. The series is noted for its high production value, unsettling atmosphere, and unique take on the lore. Brought the Backrooms to a wider audience and legitimized it as a serious horror concept. The series is considered by many to be the best cinematic adaptation of the mythos.

The Backrooms is a 'real' place that people can 'noclip' into. — The Backrooms is a work of fiction, an internet meme. The 'noclip' concept is a reference to a video game glitch, not a real phenomenon.

The Backrooms is a single, endless room. — The Backrooms is a multiverse of 'levels,' each with its own distinct environment, entities, and hazards. Level 0 is the iconic 'mono-yellow' Lobby, but it is only the first of hundreds.

The Backrooms is 'just' a horror meme with no depth. — The Backrooms has evolved into a complex, collaborative fiction with a rich lore, including factions, histories, and philosophical themes about isolation, reality, and the nature of existence.

The Backrooms is a 'new' form of horror. — The Backrooms is a modern expression of the 'liminal space' horror genre, which has existed for decades. It is a direct descendant of the 'creepypasta' tradition and shares themes with works like the SCP Foundation and the works of H.P. Lovecraft.
