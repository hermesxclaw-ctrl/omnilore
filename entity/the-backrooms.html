Who It Is

You're walking through your office building after hours. You take a shortcut through a door you've never noticed before. The door closes behind you. You're in a hallway. Yellow wallpaper. Fluorescent lights that hum at the exact frequency of a migraine. The carpet is damp. The hallway goes left, right, and forward, each direction identical and infinite. You walk for hours. Days. The lights never turn off. There are no windows. No doors except the one you came through, and it's not there anymore. Then you hear it: footsteps. Not yours. Something is in here with you. Something that has been here a lot longer than you have. And it has stopped pretending to be human.

The Backrooms is a concept born from a single 4chan post in 2019, which presented a photograph of a disorienting yellow room with fluorescent lighting and the caption: 'If you're not careful and you noclip out of reality in the wrong areas, you'll end up in the Backrooms, where it's nothing but the stink of old moist carpet, the madness of mono-yellow, the endless background noise of fluorescent lights at maximum hum-buzz, and approximately six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms to be trapped in. God save you if you hear something wandering around nearby, because it sure as hell has heard you.' From this single post, an entire collaborative universe emerged: the Backrooms wiki, with thousands of user-generated 'levels' (floors of infinite, surreal liminal space), entities (things that live in the Backrooms), and communities of 'wanderers' trying to survive and find an exit.

The Backrooms represents liminal horror — the fear of transitional spaces, of the uncanny, of the mundane made monstrous by removal of context. The yellow rooms with their humming lights are not scary because they contain monsters but because they contain nothing, and the nothing goes on forever. The Backrooms is the horror of the infinite office building, the abandoned mall, the pool at midnight, the hotel corridor that should end but doesn't. It is the feeling you get in a place that should be full of people but is empty, amplified to cosmic scale. It is the worst possible version of being lost: lost in a space that has no exit, no geography, and no rules — except that you are not alone.

Emergence

The Backrooms was created by an anonymous 4chan user on May 12, 2019, on the /x/ (paranormal) board. The original post was responding to a thread asking for 'images that feel strangely familiar but unsettling.' The anonymous user posted the now-famous photo of a yellow-tinted room with fluorescent lights and peeling wallpaper (later traced to a 2018 renovation of a former furniture store) and wrote the original 'noclip' description. The concept exploded. By 2020, multiple Backrooms wikis had been established, the most prominent being the Backrooms Wikidot. The mythology expanded from a single infinite level (Level 0: The Lobby) to hundreds of levels, each with its own horror: Level 1 (Habitable Zone — an infinite warehouse), Level 2 (Pipe Dreams — an infinite network of hot, dark maintenance tunnels), Level 3 (Electrical Station), Level 4 (Abandoned Office), and so on. YouTube creators like Kane Pixels produced found-footage series set in the Backrooms, bringing the concept to mainstream audiences with millions of views.

Form

The Backrooms does not have a single appearance — each 'level' is different. The classic Level 0 is the most iconic: an infinite maze of rooms separated by partition walls, with mono-yellow wallpaper, fluorescent lights that hum constantly, beige carpeting that's slightly damp for no reason, and an ambient temperature of 74°F with no ventilation. Other levels include abandoned hospitals, empty swimming pools, endless libraries, fog-shrouded cities, and geometries that violate Euclidean space. The consistent elements are: fluorescent lighting (usually humming), a sense of being indoors despite the infinite scale, decay (peeling wallpaper, damp carpet, rust), and the absolute absence of other people — except for the entities. The Backrooms is not a place of dramatic horror but of profound, stomach-dropping wrongness. Everything looks like somewhere you've been, but no place has ever been this empty or this large.

Powers

The Backrooms itself is the entity — an infinite, self-contained dimension with its own shifting geography and rules. It can be accessed only through 'noclipping' — a videogame term for glitching through a solid surface — which in the Backrooms lore means falling through reality at certain liminal moments (walking through the wrong door, taking a wrong turn at twilight, 'phasing' between consciousness and sleep). It is larger than any measurable space — approximately 600 million square miles of rooms in Level 0 alone, and an unknown number of levels. It resists mapping — hallways rearrange, rooms shift, exits lead to the wrong places. It seems to generate spaces based on human architecture but without human logic — rooms that serve no purpose, hallways that don't lead anywhere, stairs that form impossible loops. Time behaves inconsistently. The Backrooms does not 'want' anything — it simply IS, and being in it is the horror.

Behavior & Habitat

The Backrooms is a passive horror — it does not actively hunt you. It simply contains you. You are trapped, and the space itself is the antagonist: the endless sameness, the isolation, the sensory deprivation (or overload — the hum of fluorescent lights is a constant torture), the slow breakdown of sanity. Wanderers report that loneliness is the worst threat. The entities that live in the Backrooms are inhabitants, not guardians — they are beings that evolved here, that have adapted to this impossible ecosystem. They hunt wanderers because wanderers are prey, not because the Backrooms has directed them to. The Backrooms does not communicate, does not reward, does not punish. It is the ultimate indifferent universe — a literal one.

Weaknesses & Wards

The Backrooms has no known weaknesses — it is a dimension, not a creature. Escape is possible theoretically — certain levels have exits, and with luck, determination, and knowledge of the 'levels' (shared by wanderers), one might find a way out. Almond water (which appears in Level 4 and other levels) provides sustenance and has minor healing properties. Certain levels are 'safe' — habitable by wanderer communities that have established outposts. Knowing 'the rules' — don't trust the lights, don't follow the sounds, keep moving, never stay on one level too long — increases survival chances. The Backrooms itself cannot be destroyed; it can only be navigated and, with immense luck, escaped.

Field Notes

⁙ The original Backrooms photo was taken of a renovation project at a former HobbyTown USA store in Wisconsin — a perfectly mundane room made terrifying by context.

⁙ 'Noclipping' is a term from video games (particularly Source engine games) where players glitch through solid walls — the Backrooms applies this concept to reality itself.

⁙ The Backrooms Wikidot has catalogued over 1,000 levels, 300+ entities, and dozens of wanderer 'groups' — a fully realized alternate dimension built entirely by volunteers.

⁙ Almond water is the Backrooms' universal resource — it appears in abandoned rooms and has nutritional, healing, and minor reality-anchoring properties.

⁙ Kane Pixels' Backrooms series was so successful that A24 — the prestigious indie studio — optioned it for a feature-length film, making it possibly the first 4chan post-to-major-motion-picture pipeline.

Cultural Significance

The Backrooms is the defining internet horror mythos of the early 2020s, continuing the lineage from Slender Man (2009) and SCP (2007). It represents a new kind of horror: not monsters or jump scares but atmosphere, wrongness, and the uncanny. The Backrooms' collaborative nature — a wiki endlessly expanded by anonymous contributors — mirrors the SCP Foundation but with a focus on liminal spaces rather than anomalous objects. The concept has resonated deeply in the post-pandemic era, when lockdowns forced people to confront the emptiness of familiar spaces. The Backrooms has become a visual aesthetic (liminal space photography, the 'backrooms aesthetic') and a philosophical meditation on isolation and the nature of reality.

The Approach

Imagine walking through your office building after hours. You take a shortcut through a door you've never noticed before. The door closes behind you. You're in a hallway. Yellow wallpaper. Fluorescent lights that hum at the exact frequency of a migraine. The carpet is damp. The hallway goes left, right, and straight, all identical. You turn around. The door is gone. You are now in the Backrooms, a place that exists not in any physical location, but in the collective unconscious of the internet—a digital myth born from a single, anonymous post on a forgotten message board.

Etymology

The name 'Backrooms' derives from the colloquial term for the hidden, liminal spaces behind the visible areas of commercial buildings—storage rooms, maintenance corridors, unused offices. The concept was popularized by a 2019 4chan post describing a person 'noclipping' out of reality into an endless maze of yellow-walled rooms.

Cultural Ownership

Internet-native, global, primarily English-speaking. Originated on 4chan's /x/ (paranormal) board. Subsequently developed by anonymous users on Reddit (r/backrooms), Twitter, TikTok, and YouTube. No single creator or copyright holder. The concept is public domain in practice, though some specific adaptations (e.g., Kane Pixels' YouTube series) are copyrighted.

Disputed Identifications

The Backrooms is often conflated with the broader 'liminal space' aesthetic (abandoned malls, empty parking lots, hallways). Some classify it as a creepypasta, others as a modern mythos, a digital folklore, or an ARG (Alternate Reality Game). There is no consensus on whether it is a 'story' or a 'setting.' The specific 'Levels' system (Level 0, Level 1, etc.) is a fan-created structure, not part of the original 4chan post.

Description

Appearance

The Backrooms are described as an infinite, monotonous maze of empty rooms and hallways. The defining features are: yellow, stained wallpaper; damp, musty carpet; fluorescent lighting that hums at a constant, irritating frequency; and a pervasive smell of old, wet carpet. The architecture is generic, non-descript, and repetitive—office-like, but with no purpose. The space is poorly lit, with flickering lights and shadows. In Kane Pixels' version, the aesthetic is specifically early-1990s, with beige walls, CRT monitors, and dated office furniture. Other adaptations vary: some depict darker, more decayed versions; others emphasize the 'liminal' quality of empty, non-places.

Symbols & Iconography

The Backrooms have no official symbols. In fan culture, the most recognizable icon is a simple, low-resolution image of a long, yellow hallway with a patterned carpet and a distant doorway. This image, often referred to as 'Level 0,' has become the visual shorthand for the entire concept. The 'noclip' glitch (a video game term for passing through walls) is the symbolic mechanism of entry. The 'Entity' or 'Monster' is often depicted as a tall, shadowy humanoid figure, though this is not universal.

Behaviour & Personality

The Backrooms themselves are not sentient in a traditional sense. They are a place, a location, a dimension. However, they exhibit a kind of passive, malevolent inertia: they trap, disorient, and drive people mad through sensory deprivation and monotony. The 'entities' that inhabit them (in later lore) are hostile, predatory, or simply indifferent. There is no known purpose or intelligence behind the Backrooms; they simply exist.

Domains & Habitats

The Backrooms are a single, infinite domain. Within it, fans have created a complex hierarchy of 'levels,' each with its own distinct environment, hazards, and entities. Level 0 is the original yellow hallway. Level 1 is a dark, warehouse-like space. Level 2 is a maze of pipes and mechanical rooms. Level 3 is a sewer. Some levels are safe, others are deadly. This 'level system' is entirely fan-created and not part of the original concept.

Version Differences

There is no single, canonical version. The original 4chan post describes only a single, endless space. The 'level system' emerged on Reddit and the Backrooms Wiki. Kane Pixels' version introduces a specific origin (the Async experiment) and a more defined aesthetic. Other popular versions include the 'Poolrooms' (a level of infinite, tiled pools) and the 'Fun' (a level of children's play areas). Each adaptation adds, removes, or reinterprets elements. The core concept—an infinite, liminal, trapping space—remains constant.

Powers & Abilities

Liminal Trapping

Source Version All versions

Description

The Backrooms' primary 'power' is its ability to trap individuals who 'noclip' out of reality. Once inside, there is no known way to leave. The space is infinite, disorienting, and non-Euclidean. Doors may lead to other rooms or back to the same room. Hallways may loop. Time may pass differently.

Limitations

Entry is accidental, not intentional. The mechanism ('noclipping') is poorly understood. Some versions suggest specific locations (abandoned buildings, liminal spaces) are more likely to cause entry.

Contradictions

Some fan versions claim there are ways to 'exit' the Backrooms (e.g., finding a specific door, reaching 'Level 9223372036854775807' or 'The End'). These are not part of the original concept and are disputed. Kane Pixels' series suggests a possible exit through the Async Research Institute's portal, but this is not confirmed within the narrative.

Psychological Torment

Source Version Original post, all versions

Description

The Backrooms induce psychological distress through monotony, sensory deprivation, and the constant hum of fluorescent lights. Victims report paranoia, hallucinations, and madness. The space itself seems to prey on the mind.

Limitations

The effect varies by individual. Some characters in fan stories remain lucid for extended periods. The mechanism is psychological, not supernatural.

Contradictions

None.

Entity Generation (Fan Lore)

Source Version Fan-created (post-2020)

Description

In later fan lore, the Backrooms generate hostile entities—monsters, humanoids, or abstract threats—that hunt or stalk trapped individuals. These entities are often described as 'faceless,' 'tall,' or 'shadowy.'

Limitations

Entities are not present in the original 4chan post. Their existence, behavior, and origin are entirely fan-created and vary widely. Kane Pixels' series features a single, ambiguous entity that seems to be a byproduct of the space, not a separate being.

Contradictions

The nature of entities is highly disputed. Some fans describe them as former humans, others as native lifeforms, others as manifestations of the space itself. There is no consensus.

Stories & Myths

The Original 4chan Post (2019)

Source 4chan, /x/ boardCulture Internet-native, anonymousPeriod 2019

Summary

An anonymous user describes a scenario where a person 'noclipping' out of reality enters an endless, yellow-walled, carpeted space with fluorescent lights. The post warns of an unnamed entity that can hear you. This is the foundational text of the Backrooms mythos.

Participants

Anonymous user (creator)

The narrator (implied victim)

The unnamed entity

Consequences

The post went viral, spawning a subreddit (r/backrooms), a wiki, and countless fan stories, videos, and artworks. It became one of the most recognizable internet creepypastas of the 2020s.

Variants

The original post has been reposted and modified countless times. Some versions add details (e.g., the entity is called 'The Hound'), but these are not original.

Kane Pixels' 'The Backrooms' (2022)

Source YouTube, Kane PixelsCulture Internet-native, English-speakingPeriod 2022

Summary

A series of found-footage videos set in 1991. A teenage boy, using a VHS camcorder, documents his exploration of the Backrooms after entering through a portal created by the Async Research Institute, a secret government project. The series features a distinct visual style (VHS grain, 90s aesthetics) and a narrative arc involving the discovery of the space, encounters with an entity, and a possible escape.

Participants

Kane Pixels (creator)

The protagonist (unnamed)

The Async Research Institute

The entity

Consequences

The series is widely regarded as the definitive visual adaptation of the Backrooms. It influenced the aesthetic of subsequent fan works and brought the concept to a wider audience. It also introduced a specific origin story (the Async experiment), which is not part of the original lore.

Variants

None. The series is a single, coherent narrative.

The Backrooms Wiki (Fandom/Wikidot)

Source Fandom.com, Wikidot.comCulture Internet-native, collaborativePeriod 2019-present

Summary

A collaborative, fan-created wiki that attempts to systematize the Backrooms lore. It includes a hierarchy of 'levels' (Level 0, Level 1, etc.), descriptions of entities (e.g., 'Partygoers,' 'Smilers'), survival guides, and theoretical explanations for the Backrooms' existence. The wiki is constantly evolving, with new levels and entities being added regularly.

Participants

Countless anonymous and pseudonymous contributors

Consequences

The wiki became the de facto 'canon' for many fans, despite having no official authority. It created a complex, internally consistent (though often contradictory) mythology. It also sparked debates about the nature of 'canon' in a collaborative, anonymous mythos.

Variants

There are two major wiki platforms: Fandom and Wikidot. They have different rules, different levels, and different 'canons.' They are not compatible.

Noclip (video game term)

Type inspirationSource Video game culture (Quake, Source engine games, etc.)

Description

The term 'noclip' is a video game cheat code that allows a player to pass through walls and objects. The Backrooms' origin story—'noclipping out of reality'—directly borrows this concept. It implies that reality is like a video game, with glitches and hidden areas.

Version Note

The term is used in the original 4chan post and has been adopted by all subsequent versions.

Conflicting Accounts

None.

Psychological Horror and Entrapment

Source Original 4chan post, all adaptations

Description

The core horror of the Backrooms is the idea of being trapped in an infinite, monotonous space with no hope of escape. This taps into primal fears of confinement, isolation, and madness. The constant hum of fluorescent lights and the smell of damp carpet are designed to be viscerally unpleasant.

Scholarly Context

The Backrooms can be analyzed through the lens of 'liminal horror,' 'existential horror,' and 'digital folklore.' Some scholars have compared it to the concept of 'hell' in religious traditions, or to the 'non-places' described by anthropologist Marc Augé.

Fan-Created Violence and Body Horror

Source Backrooms Wiki (Fandom/Wikidot), various fan stories

Description

Some fan-created levels and entities feature graphic violence, body horror, and disturbing imagery. For example, the 'Partygoers' are described as entities that force humans to 'join the party' by transforming them into more Partygoers. The 'Smilers' are entities with only a mouthful of teeth. These elements are not present in the original post and are controversial among fans.

Scholarly Context

This is a common pattern in collaborative horror fiction: the original concept is simple and suggestive, but fans expand it with increasingly graphic and detailed content. This can be seen as a form of 'folk horror' or 'creepypasta evolution.'

Modern Interpretations

The Backrooms has been adapted into multiple found-footage film series (Kane Pixels, Matt Studios, Async Research), video games ('The Backrooms Game,' 'Escape the Backrooms,' various Roblox and Unity experiences), a pilot for a television series by A24 (announced 2023, directed and created by Kane Pixels), and countless YouTube documentaries and analysis videos. The 'level' system has become a collaborative worldbuilding project comparable to the SCP Foundation. The concept of 'liminal spaces' — transitional, uncanny locations that feel both familiar and deeply wrong — has entered the cultural lexicon, with subreddits and Instagram pages dedicated to liminal photography. The Backrooms demonstrates how internet mythology continues to evolve: from single post to shared universe to transmedia franchise, all without corporate ownership.
