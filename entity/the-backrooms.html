The Backrooms

Pronunciation BAK-ro͞omz

Known As

BackroomsThe Backrooms Level 0The HubLevel 0The Lobby

Etymology

The name 'Backrooms' is a colloquialism referring to the liminal, forgotten spaces behind the public-facing areas of commercial buildings — the 'back rooms' of offices, malls, and warehouses. The term was popularized by the 2019 4chan post that framed the setting as an endless, procedurally-generated maze of such spaces.

Cultural Ownership

Internet-native, anonymous; originated on 4chan's /x/ (paranormal) board in May 2019. The core concept is a collaborative, open-source mythos, with no single owner, but the most popular and influential expansion is the 'Wikidot' canon.

Disputed Identifications

There is no official canon. The Backrooms is a 'creator-owned' mythos where every wiki, video, and game iteration is a distinct, non-canonical interpretation. The 'Fandom' and 'Wikidot' wikis are the two largest, with conflicting level lists, entities, and rules.

Archive Dossier — Approach

Imagine stepping through a door in an empty office building and finding yourself in a world of endless, buzzing, yellow rooms — a place where the very geometry has given up on being logical, and the only sound is the hum of a light that never turns off. This is the Backrooms, a nightmare born not from the mind of a single author, but from the collective unconscious of the internet. It is a horror story for the digital age, a place where the boundaries between reality and simulation break down, and the only way out is to never have gone in.

You're walking through your office building after hours.

Who It Is

You're walking through your office building after hours. You take a shortcut through a door you've never noticed before. The door closes behind you. You're in a hallway. Yellow wallpaper. Fluorescent lights that hum at the exact frequency of a migraine. The carpet is damp. The hallway goes left, right, and forward, each direction identical and infinite. You walk for hours. Days. The lights never turn off. There are no windows. No doors except the one you came through, and it's not there anymore. Then you hear it: footsteps. Not yours. Something is in here with you. Something that has been here a lot longer than you have. And it has stopped pretending to be human.

The Backrooms is a concept born from a single 4chan post in 2019, which presented a photograph of a disorienting yellow room with fluorescent lighting and the caption: 'If you're not careful and you noclip out of reality in the wrong areas, you'll end up in the Backrooms, where it's nothing but the stink of old moist carpet, the madness of mono-yellow, the endless background noise of fluorescent lights at maximum hum-buzz, and approximately six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms to be trapped in. God save you if you hear something wandering around nearby, because it sure as hell has heard you.' From this single post, an entire collaborative universe emerged: the Backrooms wiki, with thousands of user-generated 'levels' (floors of infinite, surreal liminal space), entities (things that live in the Backrooms), and communities of 'wanderers' trying to survive and find an exit.

The Backrooms represents liminal horror — the fear of transitional spaces, of the uncanny, of the mundane made monstrous by removal of context. The yellow rooms with their humming lights are not scary because they contain monsters but because they contain nothing, and the nothing goes on forever. The Backrooms is the horror of the infinite office building, the abandoned mall, the pool at midnight, the hotel corridor that should end but doesn't. It is the feeling you get in a place that should be full of people but is empty, amplified to cosmic scale. It is the worst possible version of being lost: lost in a space that has no exit, no geography, and no rules — except that you are not alone.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

The archetypal Backrooms (Level 0) is an infinite, non-Euclidean maze of office rooms. The walls are a sickly, uniform mono-yellow wallpaper with a damp, stained texture. The floor is covered in a damp, old, musty-smelling carpet that is 'sticky' to the touch. The ceiling is a drop-tile grid, from which hang buzzing fluorescent lights that cast a harsh, flickering, and unrelenting glow. There are no windows, no natural light, and the space is unnervingly silent except for the constant, low 'hum-buzz' of the lights. The layout is nonsensical: rooms are randomly segmented, doors lead to more rooms, and hallways loop back on themselves. Some rooms contain office furniture, such as desks, chairs, and filing cabinets, but these are often arranged in impossible configurations or are empty and broken.

Behavior

The Backrooms itself is a passive, indifferent trap. It does not attack, but it does not let go. It is a space that is fundamentally 'wrong,' causing a deep, primal unease (liminality). It is a maze that resists mapping, with geometry that changes when unobserved. The primary 'behavior' is entrapment and psychological erosion. The environment is designed to induce a state of 'subliminal paranoia' and madness from sensory deprivation and monotony. The longer one stays, the more the space seems to 'hunt' them, subtly altering its layout to keep them lost. The 'entities' within are the active threat, but the space itself is the ultimate predator.

Domains

The Backrooms is a dimension that exists 'outside' or 'behind' reality. It is entered by 'noclipping' through a glitch in reality, often in an empty, mundane space (a wall, a floor, a doorway). In the lore, it is a vast multiverse of levels, each with its own environment, hazards, and entities. Level 0 is the 'Lobby,' the first and most iconic level. Other levels range from abandoned malls, to pitch-black caverns, to city-sized mazes, to impossible nature preserves. The 'Hub' is a central, safe level that connects to many others. The 'The Frontrooms' is the name given to our normal reality.

Symbols

The primary symbol is the mono-yellow wallpaper and the buzzing fluorescent light. This image has become an iconic shorthand for liminal space and internet horror. The act of 'noclipping' (glitching through a wall) is the symbolic portal. The 'hum' of the lights is a sonic symbol of the setting's oppressive, monotonous dread. In the Wikidot canon, the 'M.E.G.' (Major Explorer Group) is a symbol of organized human resistance and documentation within the chaos.

Emergence

The Backrooms was created by an anonymous 4chan user on May 12, 2019, on the /x/ (paranormal) board. The original post was responding to a thread asking for 'images that feel strangely familiar but unsettling.' The anonymous user posted the now-famous photo of a yellow-tinted room with fluorescent lights and peeling wallpaper (later traced to a 2018 renovation of a former furniture store) and wrote the original 'noclip' description. The concept exploded. By 2020, multiple Backrooms wikis had been established, the most prominent being the Backrooms Wikidot. The mythology expanded from a single infinite level (Level 0: The Lobby) to hundreds of levels, each with its own horror: Level 1 (Habitable Zone — an infinite warehouse), Level 2 (Pipe Dreams — an infinite network of hot, dark maintenance tunnels), Level 3 (Electrical Station), Level 4 (Abandoned Office), and so on. YouTube creators like Kane Pixels produced found-footage series set in the Backrooms, bringing the concept to mainstream audiences with millions of views.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Noclip Entry

Source Version All versions

Description

The method of entry into the Backrooms. It occurs when a person 'noclips' out of reality, a glitch in the simulation of reality, usually by falling through a floor or passing through a wall in an empty or mundane area.

Limitations

Requires a 'glitch' in reality. It is not a deliberate or reliable method. Most who enter do so by accident.

Contradictions

Some versions require a state of mind (e.g., extreme boredom or detachment) to trigger the noclip.

Non-Euclidean Geometry

Source Version All versions

Description

The space of the Backrooms is not bound by normal physics. Rooms are larger on the inside than the outside, hallways loop, and paths that should lead to a destination lead back to the start. It is impossible to map the space accurately, and it is believed to be procedurally generated, infinite, and constantly shifting.

Limitations

The geometry is 'hostile' to navigation but not to life. It is a passive, inescapable maze.

Contradictions

Some levels have a 'center' or a 'border,' while others are truly infinite. The Wikidot canon has levels with finite, but vast, boundaries.

Entity Manifestation

Source Version All versions

Description

The Backrooms is home to countless entities. These range from the passive and harmless to the predatory and hostile. The most iconic is 'The Hound,' a dog-like creature with a human-like laugh. Others include 'The Skin-Stealer,' a humanoid that wears the skin of its victims, and 'The Faceling,' a humanoid with no face. The presence of entities is often unpredictable, and they seem to be a part of the environment, not visitors to it.

Limitations

Entities are not omnipotent. They can be avoided, outrun, or, in some cases, fought. Many are confined to specific levels.

Contradictions

The nature of the entities is debated. Some are natural fauna of the Backrooms, while others are thought to be transformed humans or escaped experiments from other levels.

Psychological Manipulation

Source Version All versions

Description

The Backrooms environment is a psychological weapon. The monotony, isolation, and sensory deprivation are designed to erode sanity. It induces paranoia, hallucinations, and a state of 'losing one's mind.' The silence and the hum of the lights are used to create a constant, low-level stress that eventually breaks the will.

Limitations

This is a passive effect, not an active attack. It takes time to work, and some individuals are more resistant than others.

Contradictions

Some levels are designed to be 'safe' zones, like the Hub, which offers respite from the madness. The effect is not universal.

Form

The Backrooms does not have a single appearance — each 'level' is different. The classic Level 0 is the most iconic: an infinite maze of rooms separated by partition walls, with mono-yellow wallpaper, fluorescent lights that hum constantly, beige carpeting that's slightly damp for no reason, and an ambient temperature of 74°F with no ventilation. Other levels include abandoned hospitals, empty swimming pools, endless libraries, fog-shrouded cities, and geometries that violate Euclidean space. The consistent elements are: fluorescent lighting (usually humming), a sense of being indoors despite the infinite scale, decay (peeling wallpaper, damp carpet, rust), and the absolute absence of other people — except for the entities. The Backrooms is not a place of dramatic horror but of profound, stomach-dropping wrongness. Everything looks like somewhere you've been, but no place has ever been this empty or this large.

Powers

The Backrooms itself is the entity — an infinite, self-contained dimension with its own shifting geography and rules. It can be accessed only through 'noclipping' — a videogame term for glitching through a solid surface — which in the Backrooms lore means falling through reality at certain liminal moments (walking through the wrong door, taking a wrong turn at twilight, 'phasing' between consciousness and sleep). It is larger than any measurable space — approximately 600 million square miles of rooms in Level 0 alone, and an unknown number of levels. It resists mapping — hallways rearrange, rooms shift, exits lead to the wrong places. It seems to generate spaces based on human architecture but without human logic — rooms that serve no purpose, hallways that don't lead anywhere, stairs that form impossible loops. Time behaves inconsistently. The Backrooms does not 'want' anything — it simply IS, and being in it is the horror.

Behavior & Habitat

The Backrooms is a passive horror — it does not actively hunt you. It simply contains you. You are trapped, and the space itself is the antagonist: the endless sameness, the isolation, the sensory deprivation (or overload — the hum of fluorescent lights is a constant torture), the slow breakdown of sanity. Wanderers report that loneliness is the worst threat. The entities that live in the Backrooms are inhabitants, not guardians — they are beings that evolved here, that have adapted to this impossible ecosystem. They hunt wanderers because wanderers are prey, not because the Backrooms has directed them to. The Backrooms does not communicate, does not reward, does not punish. It is the ultimate indifferent universe — a literal one.

Weaknesses & Wards

The Backrooms has no known weaknesses — it is a dimension, not a creature. Escape is possible theoretically — certain levels have exits, and with luck, determination, and knowledge of the 'levels' (shared by wanderers), one might find a way out. Almond water (which appears in Level 4 and other levels) provides sustenance and has minor healing properties. Certain levels are 'safe' — habitable by wanderer communities that have established outposts. Knowing 'the rules' — don't trust the lights, don't follow the sounds, keep moving, never stay on one level too long — increases survival chances. The Backrooms itself cannot be destroyed; it can only be navigated and, with immense luck, escaped.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

The Frontrooms (Reality)

Type creatorSource All versions

Description

The Backrooms is a 'behind' or 'outside' of our reality. It is entered by 'noclipping' out of the Frontrooms. The Frontrooms is the 'real' world, and the Backrooms is its forgotten, glitched-out shadow.

Version Note

The relationship is one of contrast: the Backrooms is the 'un-real' or 'sub-real.'

Conflicting Accounts

Some versions imply the Backrooms is a simulation within our reality, while others suggest it is a separate dimension that has always existed.

The M.E.G. (Major Explorer Group)

Type allySource Wikidot canon

Description

A major faction in the Wikidot canon. A group of explorers who have banded together to map the Backrooms, document its levels, and rescue lost wanderers. They are the closest thing to a 'protagonist' faction in the lore.

Version Note

The M.E.G. is largely absent from the Fandom canon.

Conflicting Accounts

The M.E.G.'s motivations and methods are debated. Some see them as benevolent saviors, while others see them as a totalitarian force trying to control the Backrooms.

The Hound (Entity 1)

Type hostileSource All versions

Description

The most iconic entity. A creature that resembles a large, hairless dog, but with a human-like laugh. It is a predator that hunts by sound and is known for its unsettling, high-pitched cackle. It is a symbol of the active, predatory threats within the Backrooms.

Version Note

The Hound is a central threat in the early levels of the Wikidot canon.

Conflicting Accounts

The Hound's origin is unknown. Some believe it is a corrupted human, while others believe it is a natural predator of the Backrooms.

The Skin-Stealer (Entity 2)

Type hostileSource Fandom canon

Description

A humanoid entity that wears the skin of its victims to disguise itself. It is a stalker that mimics the appearance of a lost wanderer, only to reveal its true, flayed form when it gets close.

Version Note

A prominent entity in the Fandom canon, often featured in fan art and gaming adaptations.

Conflicting Accounts

The Skin-Stealer is a classic 'body horror' monster, and its abilities are often exaggerated in fan content.

The Faceling (Entity 3)

Type neutralSource All versions

Description

A humanoid with no facial features. They are often found wandering the Backrooms, and their intentions are unclear. Some are passive, while others are aggressive. They are a symbol of the uncanny and the 'almost human' horror of the Backrooms.

Version Note

Facelings are a common sight in the early levels, and their behavior varies widely.

Conflicting Accounts

Some believe Facelings are lost humans who have been 'erased,' while others believe they are a separate species that mimics humans.

Field Notes

⁙ The original Backrooms photo was taken of a renovation project at a former HobbyTown USA store in Wisconsin — a perfectly mundane room made terrifying by context.

⁙ 'Noclipping' is a term from video games (particularly Source engine games) where players glitch through solid walls — the Backrooms applies this concept to reality itself.

⁙ The Backrooms Wikidot has catalogued over 1,000 levels, 300+ entities, and dozens of wanderer 'groups' — a fully realized alternate dimension built entirely by volunteers.

⁙ Almond water is the Backrooms' universal resource — it appears in abandoned rooms and has nutritional, healing, and minor reality-anchoring properties.

⁙ Kane Pixels' Backrooms series was so successful that A24 — the prestigious indie studio — optioned it for a feature-length film, making it possibly the first 4chan post-to-major-motion-picture pipeline.

Archive Dossier — Stories

The Original 4chan Post (2019)

Source 4chan /x/ boardCulture Internet-nativePeriod 2019

Summary

The foundational text. A user posts an image of a mono-yellow, empty office space and invents the Backrooms. The post describes the space as a 'noclip' accident, a place of 'six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms.' It is a single paragraph that spawned an entire genre.

Participants

Anonymous 4chan user

Consequences

Created the core concept and aesthetic. The image and text were immediately shared and became a viral meme, leading to the creation of the Wikidot and Fandom wikis.

Variants

The original post is the 'canon' origin, but it is often misattributed or embellished in later retellings.

The Wikidot Canon (2019-present)

Source backrooms-wiki.wikidot.comCulture Internet-nativePeriod 2019-present

Summary

A massive, collaborative writing project that expands the Backrooms into a complex multiverse. It includes hundreds of levels, each with its own description, entities, and lore. It introduces factions like the M.E.G., a group of explorers who document and map the Backrooms, and a complex history of 'The First Contact.' The canon is known for its 'serious,' pseudo-scientific tone and its focus on exploration and survival rather than pure horror.

Participants

Thousands of anonymous contributors

M.E.G. (in-universe)

Consequences

Established the most detailed and 'official' version of the Backrooms mythos. It is the source for most in-depth lore discussions and is considered the 'true' canon by many fans.

Variants

The Wikidot canon is itself split into several eras, with the 'Backrooms Hub' being the central hub for the main canon.

The Fandom Canon (2019-present)

Source backrooms.fandom.comCulture Internet-nativePeriod 2019-present

Summary

A parallel wiki that is more accessible and creature-focused. It includes many of the same levels as the Wikidot canon but with a greater emphasis on entities as monsters. It is often seen as less 'serious' and more open to fan fiction and gaming-inspired content. It is the canon most commonly referenced in YouTube videos and short-form content.

Participants

Thousands of anonymous contributors

Consequences

Created a more accessible and 'pop-culture' version of the Backrooms. It is often the first introduction to the concept for many people.

Variants

The Fandom canon is less structured and more prone to 'power creep' and contradictory entries.

Kane Pixels' 'The Backrooms (Found Footage)' (2022)

A critically acclaimed series of found-footage videos that reimagines the Backrooms as a government experiment. The story follows a group of scientists who create a portal to the Backrooms, only to have their research facility breached by its entities. The series is noted for its high production value, unsettling atmosphere, and unique take on the lore.

Participants

Kane Pixels (creator)

A-Sync Research (in-universe)

Variants

This is a distinct, non-canonical interpretation, but it has heavily influenced fan art and other adaptations.

Known Gaps

The primary gap is the lack of a single, authoritative canon. The Backrooms is a collaborative, ever-evolving project, and any single page can only capture a snapshot of its current state. The research is also limited by the ephemeral nature of internet content; many early 4chan threads and forum posts have been lost. The exact number of levels and entities is constantly changing, and it is impossible to document them all.

Archive Dossier — Contradictions

The Origin of the Backrooms

Position A

It is a glitch in reality, a 'noclip' out of our world (original 4chan post).

Position A — evidence

The original 4chan text describes it as an accident.

Position B

It is a government experiment, a created portal (Kane Pixels' series).

Position B — evidence

Kane Pixels' series shows a deliberate attempt to create a portal to the Backrooms.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus. The original is the 'mythic' origin, while Kane Pixels offers a more 'rational' origin. Most fans accept that there is no single canon.

The Nature of the Entities

Position A

They are natural fauna of the Backrooms, a part of its ecosystem.

Position A — evidence

Many entities are described as 'native' to their levels, with no connection to humans.

Position B

They are corrupted humans, transformed by the environment.

Position B — evidence

Entities like the Skin-Stealer are explicitly described as wearing human skin, implying a human origin. The Hound's laugh is a distorted human sound.

Scholarly Consensus

Debated. The Wikidot canon tends towards the 'natural fauna' view, while the Fandom canon often hints at a human origin.

The Size and Structure of the Backrooms

Position A

It is a single, infinite, procedurally generated space (original 4chan post).

Position A — evidence

The original post mentions 'six hundred million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms.'

Position B

It is a multiverse of distinct 'levels,' each with its own rules and boundaries (Wikidot/Fandom canon).

Position B — evidence

The wikis have hundreds of levels, from the 'Lobby' to the 'Hub' to the 'Endless City.'

Scholarly Consensus

The 'levels' model is the most popular and is the standard for most fan content. The original 'infinite' model is seen as a more 'pure' but less developed concept.

The 'Realness' of the Backrooms

Position A

It is a work of fiction, an internet meme.

Position A — evidence

No scientific evidence exists for the Backrooms.

Position B

It is a real, hidden dimension that some people have accidentally entered.

Position B — evidence

Viral 'found footage' videos and personal anecdotes claim to be real encounters.

Scholarly Consensus

The Backrooms is a work of fiction. The 'real' claims are part of the genre's aesthetic, a form of 'creepypasta' role-playing.

Cultural Significance

The Backrooms is the defining internet horror mythos of the early 2020s, continuing the lineage from Slender Man (2009) and SCP (2007). It represents a new kind of horror: not monsters or jump scares but atmosphere, wrongness, and the uncanny. The Backrooms' collaborative nature — a wiki endlessly expanded by anonymous contributors — mirrors the SCP Foundation but with a focus on liminal spaces rather than anomalous objects. The concept has resonated deeply in the post-pandemic era, when lockdowns forced people to confront the emptiness of familiar spaces. The Backrooms has become a visual aesthetic (liminal space photography, the 'backrooms aesthetic') and a philosophical meditation on isolation and the nature of reality.

Modern Interpretations

The Backrooms has been adapted into multiple found-footage film series (Kane Pixels, Matt Studios, Async Research), video games ('The Backrooms Game,' 'Escape the Backrooms,' various Roblox and Unity experiences), a pilot for a television series by A24 (announced 2023, directed and created by Kane Pixels), and countless YouTube documentaries and analysis videos. The 'level' system has become a collaborative worldbuilding project comparable to the SCP Foundation. The concept of 'liminal spaces' — transitional, uncanny locations that feel both familiar and deeply wrong — has entered the cultural lexicon, with subreddits and Instagram pages dedicated to liminal photography. The Backrooms demonstrates how internet mythology continues to evolve: from single post to shared universe to transmedia franchise, all without corporate ownership.
