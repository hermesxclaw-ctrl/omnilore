Who It Is

He is four feet of pure, waddling, toothy joy — a baby shark who learned to walk on land, steal Captain America's shield for a sled, swallow the Infinity Gauntlet whole, and become Marvel's most unexpectedly beloved rescue pet. Jeff the Land Shark didn't arrive with a prophecy or a tragic origin; he toddled in on four stubby legs, bit the furniture, and stole the entire West Coast Avengers.

Attested behavior across comics is consistently: intensely affectionate and loyal to his caregivers (especially Gwen and Kate), gluttonous and food-motivated, mischievous, curious, and non-verbal but highly expressive through body language. It's Jeff! is entirely wordless; personality is conveyed through pantomime — Jeff steals food (Thanksgiving turkey, Coney Island boardwalk snacks), hijacks objects (Captain America's shield as a sled, Infinity Gauntlet as a chew toy), crashes superhero pool parties, and seeks naps in sunbeams or laundry piles. Powers
Amphibious Physiology

As a bio-engineered landshark, Jeff breathes both in water and on land and swims with shark-like propulsion while also walking/running on four legs. He is shown swimming in pools and ocean and trotting, pouncing, and climbing with agility.

Limit: No evidence of deep-sea pressure resistance or indefinite submersion; shown tiring and needing rest. Cannot fly or breathe in vacuum.

Enhanced Bite and Digestive Durability

Powerful jaws capable of chewing through superhero artifacts for comedy: bites and swallows the Infinity Gauntlet, chews Captain America's shield (as sled, not destroyed), devours entire Thanksgiving turkey and boardwalk food. Implies enhanced tooth hardness and stomach capable of containing powerful objects without injury.

Limit: Played for slapstick; Gauntlet is not shown functioning while swallowed, and shield is not damaged — suggests toon-like durability rather than true power to destroy artifacts. No attested ability to bite through adamantium or similar in serious continuity.

Superhuman Durability and Toon Resilience

Survives being tossed, squashed, stretched, and caught in superhero brawls with no lasting injury. Recovers instantly from slapstick impacts that would injure a normal animal.

Limit: Not invulnerable in serious dramatic terms; shown needing rescue and showing pain/fear. Durability is comedic, not quantified as bulletproof.

Empathic Bonding and Human Social Intelligence

Forms rapid, strong attachments to caregivers and recognizes allies. Shows ability to understand human social cues, follow simple instructions, and coordinate in fights (assisting Kate, Gwen, America Chavez and Fuse against Zombiotes). Acts as emotional support animal for Gwen and Kate.

Limit: No telepathy or speech; comprehension is animal-level, not human-level reasoning. Cannot be commanded like a trained super-pet with complex tactics.

Symbiote Bonding (Temporary)

When bonded to a Symbiote (Venomized Jeff), gains symbiote tendrils, enhanced strength, and ability to generate symbiote constructs while retaining Jeff's personality. The pair cooperate to obtain food and fight Zombiotes at Coney Island.

Limit: Bond is temporary and voluntary; Jeff and symbiote separate amicably after Zombiotes defeated. No evidence of long-term symbiote retention or corruption as with Eddie Brock. Requires proximity to symbiote.

Gameplay Abilities (Marvel Rivals - Non-Canon Mechanics)

In Marvel Rivals, Jeff is a Strategist: Joyful Splash (healing stream), Aqua Burst (knockback), Hide and Seek (dive underground with healing bubble), and ultimate It's Jeff! (swallows allies and enemies to reposition/heal). These are game-balanced support mechanics.

Limit: Not canonical to Earth-616 comics. Cooldowns, healing numbers, and swallow mechanics exist only in game balance context.

Accounts I
The Adoption — West Coast Avengers (vol. 3) #7: 'Best Coast'West Coast Avengers (vol. 3) #7 (Marvel Comics, Jan 2019) — Kelly Thompson & Daniele di Nicuolo

2018-2019 Modern

After the West Coast Avengers defeat the West Coast Masters of Evil, Gwen Poole (Gwenpool), a former M.O.D.O.K. Superior employee who knows she is in a comic, discovers a lone baby landshark left behind from M.O.D.O.K. Superior's failed Santa Monica invasion (species introduced in #1). Motivated by her own shark motif and the loss of her earlier kitten also named Jeff, Gwen adopts the pup and names him Jeff. Kate Bishop reluctantly co-parents. The issue establishes Jeff as the team's mascot and Gwen's emotional anchor, cementing the running gag into a permanent character after overwhelming positive fan response online. Jeff becomes a recurring supporting character for the remainder of West Coast Avengers (through #10). Establishes his origin as bio-weapon stray turned rescue pet, and his dual ownership by Gwen and Kate — a relationship that persists through all later series.

Securing Jeff's Future — Gwenpool Strikes Back & Deadpool (2019-2020)Gwenpool Strikes Back #1-5 (2019, Leah Williams/David Baldeon) and Deadpool (vol. 8) #1-10 'King Deadpool' (2019-2020, Kelly Thompson/Chris Bachalo, Gerardo Sandoval)

2019-2020 Modern

Aware that her solo titles keep getting cancelled and that she may be erased from continuity, Gwen Poole decides to find Jeff a more stable home. She entrusts Jeff to Wade Wilson (Deadpool), reasoning his title is more secure — only for Deadpool's book to be promptly cancelled as well in a meta-joke. Jeff is then briefly left in the care of monster-hunter Elsa Bloodstone. These issues treat Jeff as a narrative hot potato, emphasizing that his cuteness makes him too valuable to lose even as his owners' books fold. Establishes Jeff as a shared Marvel pet whose custody moves with publishing realities; sets up his later status as communal mascot rather than tied to a single solo title. Reinforces Thompson's continued stewardship of the character even when writing Deadpool.

It's Jeff! — The Wordless Domestic Comedy (Seasons 1-4)It's Jeff! Infinity Comic Seasons 1-4 (Marvel Unlimited, Sept 2021-May 2025) — Kelly Thompson & Gurihiru; print collections It's Jeff! #1 (2023) and It's Jeff: The Jeff-Verse #1 (2023)

2021-2025 Modern

An ongoing series of dialogue-free vignettes following Jeff's daily life back with Gwen and Kate (Gwen absent Season 1, returns Season 2). Signature gags: Jeff is the darling of a superhero pool party, steals Captain America's shield to use as a sled with the Young Avengers, swallows the Infinity Gauntlet and waddles around with it, and steals a superhero Thanksgiving turkey. Other shorts show him napping in laundry, chasing drones, befriending other Marvel animals, and causing gentle chaos at Avengers gatherings. The format relies entirely on Gurihiru's expressive, animation-ready art. Elevated Jeff from supporting pet to headliner; proved a silent animal comic could sustain long-form readership. Led to Eisner nomination (2022 Best Digital Comic) and win (2024 Best Humor Publication), and established the visual template most fans recognize.

Accounts II
Venomized — Summer of Symbiotes / Venom WarExtreme Venomverse #5 'The Rhythm of the Night' (July 2023) and Venom War: It's Jeff #1 (Nov 2024) — Kelly Thompson & Gurihiru

2023-2024 Modern

As part of the Summer of Symbiotes, Jeff is bonded to a Symbiote, becoming Venom-Jeff with a glossy black body and white spider emblem. In the 2024 Venom War one-shot, Jeff and his Symbiote learn to cooperate, using their combined abilities to scavenge food from Coney Island boardwalk stands. When Zombiotes (zombie-symbiote hybrids) attack the boardwalk, Jeff teams with Kate Bishop, Gwen Poole, America Chavez and Fuse to fight them off. After the Zombiotes are defeated, Jeff and the Symbiote separate amicably — the Symbiote having enjoyed being a well-fed landshark. Provides Jeff's only super-powered transformation to date; demonstrates that symbiote bonding does not corrupt his gentle personality. Featured on standard cover by Leinil Francis Yu and variant by Peach Momoko, expanding Jeff's visibility in Venom lore.

The Weapon X Encounter — Gwenpool (2025) Limited SeriesGwenpool (2025) #1-5 — Cavan Scott & Stefano Nesi (guest stars Jeff and Kate Bishop)

2025 Modern

Jeff accompanies Kate Bishop and Gwen Poole as they join Peter Parker in confronting X-31, a reconstructed clone of Gwen Stacy originally created by New U Technologies and later modified by a branch of the Weapon X Project led by the Grand Architect. Jeff assists in fighting Weapon X soldiers while Spider-Man unmasks the Grand Architect — revealed to be a counterpart of Marvel Comics writer Cavan Scott in a meta-textual twist. Jeff's role is primarily as loyal companion and comic relief, but he actively participates in the skirmish. Reunites Jeff with his original West Coast Avengers family in a 2025 continuity, and ties his recent history to cloning/Weapon X lore. Shows Jeff can be integrated into more serious X-related plots without losing comedic tone.

Jeff the Land Shark (vol. 1) — First Solo Print Limited SeriesJeff the Land Shark (vol. 1) #1-5 (Marvel Comics, June 2025 onward) — Kelly Thompson & Tokitokoro

2025-2026 Modern

Headlining his first dedicated print miniseries, Jeff stars in more complex, longer-form stories than the gag strips. Thompson described the pitch as 'the slow burn success of Jeff in comics and the wildfire success of Jeff in Marvel Rivals can work together to bring us... more complex Jeff stories.' Tokitokoro's art gives Jeff slightly more detailed, manga-inflected expressiveness while retaining his chibi appeal. The series was nominated for Best Humor Publication at the 2026 Eisner Awards, and is slated to be followed by Jeff the Land Shark: Superstar (July 2026). Marks Jeff's transition from digital wordless star to print headliner capable of carrying narrative arcs; solidifies his status as a franchise-worthy Marvel animal hero alongside predecessors like Lockjaw and Tippy-Toe.

Cross-Company Playdates — Deadpool/Batman and It's Jeff/AquamanDeadpool/Batman #1 backup story 'Jeff & Krypto' (DC/Marvel crossover, Sept 2025, Kelly Thompson & Gurihiru) and It's Jeff/Aquaman #1 (digital one-shot, April 2026, Kelly Thompson & Andres Genolet)

2025-2026 Modern

In the historic DC/Marvel crossover Deadpool/Batman #1, Jeff co-stars with Superman's dog Krypto in a Thompson/Gurihiru backup — a dialogue-light romp contrasting Jeff's shark waddle with Krypto's Kryptonian flight. Between DC's Superman/Spider-Man #1 and Marvel's Spider-Man/Superman #1, the digital one-shot It's Jeff/Aquaman #1 pairs Jeff with Arthur Curry for an ocean-themed adventure, playing on Jeff's amphibious nature versus Aquaman's Atlantean kingship. First time Jeff appears in a DC-co-published book, underscoring his marketability as Marvel's ambassador animal character. Demonstrates publisher confidence in Jeff as a crossover draw comparable to more established icons.

Jeff is just a baby great white shark that lives in water. — Jeff is a bio-engineered landshark species — an amphibious, quadrupedal shark created by M.O.D.O.K. Superior to attack on land. He walks, runs, and lives primarily on land and in domestic spaces, though he can swim.

Jeff can talk or has human-level intelligence. — In comics Jeff is non-verbal and communicates through expressive body language, barks, and chirps; It's Jeff! is entirely wordless. His intelligence is that of a highly empathetic pet, not a talking super-pet. Even in Marvel Rivals he does not speak English.

Jeff was created for Marvel Rivals and then added to comics. — Jeff debuted in comics in 2018-2019 and headlined It's Jeff! since 2021, years before his December 2024 Marvel Rivals debut. The game massively amplified his popularity but did not create him.

Jeff can use the Infinity Gauntlet's powers after swallowing it. — In It's Jeff! he swallows/chews the Gauntlet as a gag and waddles around with it, but never activates Infinity Stones or exhibits cosmic power. The joke is that a pet treats a universe-ending weapon as a chew toy.

Venom-Jeff is Jeff's permanent new form. — Venomization was temporary for two event stories (Extreme Venomverse #5 and Venom War: It's Jeff #1). Jeff and the symbiote separate amicably after the Coney Island Zombiote fight and Jeff returns to normal.
