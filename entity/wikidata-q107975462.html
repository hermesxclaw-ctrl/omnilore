The Approach

She walks into Spiders Cafe in a blue miniskirt and a coat lined with thorns that haven't sprouted yet — Paula, the Baroque Works officer who can turn any inch of her skin into a needle, a stiletto, a sea urchin's nightmare. When Nami meets her on the stone stairs of Alubarna Palace, Miss Doublefinger doesn't just fight; she turns her own body into a weapon shop, and the question becomes whether a thief who fights with a trick baton can survive a woman who is the trick.

Content advisory. Fantasy violence and attempted murder; criminal organization and assassination themes; body-horror transformation (spikes protruding through skin/clothing); no sexual violence depicted for this character.
Identity

Miss Doublefinger

Pronunciation Japanese: Misu Daburu Fingā (ミス・ダブルフィンガー) / Pōra (ポーラ); English: miss DUB-əl-FING-gər / PAW-la (Paula) / ZAH-la (Zala variant)

Known As

Paula (Pōra / ポーラ)Zala (VIZ Media early translation and some international editions)Miss Double Finger (variant spacing)Mr. 1's partner

Etymology

Codename follows Baroque Works Officer Agent pattern: male agents are numbered (Mr. 1, Mr. 2...), female partners take a codename evoking a holiday or festive wordplay. 'Doublefinger' is Oda's pun on New Year's Day / holiday double-meaning and the two-finger 'peace' sign — a sly joke that the deadliest woman in the agency is named after a cheerful gesture. Her civilian name Paula (ポーラ) is a conventional feminine given name with no additional kanji meaning; VIZ initially rendered it as 'Zala' before standardizing to Paula to match Vivre Card. The fruit name Toge Toge no Mi derives from toge (棘, thorn/spike).

Cultural Ownership

Original fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda, owned and published by Shueisha (Weekly Shonen Jump) and licensed internationally by VIZ Media, Toei Animation, and Bandai Namco. No folklore or public-domain antecedent; entirely modern shōnen manga creation (1999 debut). Cultural stewardship lies with Oda/Shueisha franchise.

Disputed Identifications

Not to be confused with Miss Valentine (Mikita) or Miss Goldenweek (Marianne) — other Baroque Works female Officer Agents with similar codename structure. Early fan translations conflated Paula/Zala with an unrelated anime-only character; Vivre Card No. 0514 confirms Paula as canonical civilian name, resolving Zala vs Paula dispute. Not a Devil Fruit Logia or Zoan — consistently classified as Paramecia. No relation to real-world persons named Paula Zala.

Description

Appearance

Attested manga/anime: Tall (178cm), slim, long wavy dark indigo/black hair parted in middle, sharp facial features, heavy eye makeup. Pre-battle outfit: short blue coat with fur collar over blue mini-dress, yellow waist sash, high-heeled boots, often seen smoking. Post-transformation, spikes protrude through clothing without tearing it — stylistic choice by Oda. As Paula in Spiders Cafe, wears waitress uniform: white blouse, black vest, apron. Vivre Card notes age 28 at Alabasta, birthday March 12. Anime color palette shifts coat to more purple-blue in some episodes. No attested post-timeskip redesign; cover story shows her in prison stripes then casual reformed clothing.

Symbols & Iconography

Thorn/needle motif everywhere: coat lining, earrings, stiletto heels that become actual stilettos. Baroque Works tattoo/symbol not visibly tattooed on her (unlike Mr. 1); identification is via codename and partnership. Her spikes visually quote sea urchin (uni) and cactus — Oda's marginal notes compare her to a 'human sea urchin.' The double-finger peace sign recurs as ironic gesture in her introduction at Spiders Cafe. No religious iconography.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: Cool, professional, sardonic, and businesslike — the straight woman to Mr. 1's stoicism and Mr. 2's flamboyance. She treats assassination as clerical work; at Spiders Cafe she calmly serves tea while discussing murder. Shows dry humor and mild exasperation at Baroque Works' weirder members. In battle, she is sadistic and confident, mocking Nami as weak and enjoying the advantage of lethal spikes, but also pragmatic — she attempts to kill quickly without monologuing excessively. Cover story shows loyalty to colleagues and willingness to do manual labor to free them, suggesting camaraderie beneath mercenary exterior. No attested backstory explaining motivation beyond employment by Crocodile; personality inferred from dialogue, not exposition. Disputed: fanon often portrays her as dominatrix archetype due to spikes and heels; canon does not sexualize beyond design.

Domains & Habitats

Primary habitat: Baroque Works infrastructure in Alabasta — Spiders Cafe (oasis restaurant front on the desert edge, approx. 6km from Rainbase) where she works undercover as waitress Paula, and later the royal palace at Alubarna during Operation Utopia. No attested hometown or origin island; Vivre Card lists birthplace unknown. Post-defeat habitat: Alabasta prison (canon cover story) then New Spiders Cafe re-opened with former agents (cover story final panels). Not seen in New World post-timeskip; current whereabouts unknown in main storyline.

Version Differences

Manga vs Anime: Anime Episode 103 adds filler dialogue at Spiders Cafe and extends Nami vs Doublefinger fight (Episode 117-118) with additional spike attacks (Stinger Hedgehog, Stinger Step) and more prolonged Clima-Tact gags; manga fight is tighter (3 chapters). Anime censors some spike impalement impacts (less blood). VIZ English manga (early print) calls her Zala; later VIZ and Funimation standardize to Paula; Japanese raw always ポーラ. 4Kids dub (2004-2007) heavily cut Alabasta arc and renamed attacks; Funimation uncut restores original names. Vivre Card adds height/birthday/food preferences absent in manga. No live-action Netflix appearance as of Season 1 (2023, covers East Blue only); no confirmed casting for Season 2 Alabasta.

Powers & Abilities

Toge Toge no Mi (Spike-Spike Fruit / Thorn-Thorn Fruit)

Source VersionManga Chapters 191-192; Vivre Card confirms Paramecia classification; One Piece Yellow databook p. 214

Paramecia-type Devil Fruit that allows user to grow sharp spikes/spines from any part of the body at will. Spikes can be short like sea-urchin quills covering the whole body (defensive urchin form) or elongated into needles, spears, and stiletto blades from limbs, hair, or heels. Oda demonstrates both piercing and hardening functions — spikes can puncture stone walls and block sword-like attacks.

Limitations

Cannot generate spikes at range — must be attached to body. Standard Devil Fruit weaknesses apply: seawater/Seastone (kairoseki) nullifies power and user cannot swim; vulnerable to blunt force, fire, and electricity when not spiked. Nami exploits that spikes conduct electricity via Thunderbolt Tempo. Spikes do not grant superhuman durability beyond piercing defense; user can still be struck where spikes are not deployed. No attested Haki usage.

Contradictions

Early fan wikis mislabeled as Logia; official sources unanimously Paramecia. Anime filler shows spikes growing to exaggerated lengths not seen in manga — considered adaptation-specific.

Assassin Combat & Espionage Skills

Source VersionManga Chapters 160, 190-192; implied by Officer Agent rank

Trained Baroque Works Officer Agent: expert in undercover waitressing/espionage, close-quarters combat using spike-augmented martial arts (kicks, palm thrusts, hair needles), acrobatics, and use of high heels as stabbing weapons. Demonstrates professional coordination with Mr. 1 (Daz Bonez) — paired for high-value targets.

Limitations

No superhuman strength beyond fruit augmentation; relies on fruit for lethal offense. Defeated by Nami's intellect and Clima-Tact trickery, not raw power, suggesting vulnerability to deception and elemental counters.

Contradictions

None attested; power level consistently portrayed as mid-tier Officer Agent, below Mr. 1 and Mr. 2.

Stinger Techniques (Named Attacks)

Source VersionManga Chapter 192; Anime Episode 117 adds names

Attested named moves: Double Stinger (both arms into spike spears), Stinger Hedgehog (full-body spike defense), Sea Urchin Stinger (rolling spike ball), Stinger Step (heel spike stab). Anime adds Stinger Flail. All are applications of Toge Toge no Mi, not separate powers.

Limitations

Technique names are descriptive; no additional power beyond fruit. Anime-only names are non-canonical for manga purists.

Contradictions

VIZ vs Japanese name romanization: 'Stinger' vs 'Toge' — same move.

Stories & Myths

Recruitment and Life as Paula at Spiders Cafe

Source One Piece Chapter 160-161; Vivre Card No. 0514Culture One Piece / Baroque Works (Alabasta Saga)Period Manga publication 2000; in-story circa 1520 Age of the Sea (Alabasta arc, before Straw Hat arrival)

Summary

Paula operates undercover as a waitress at Spiders Cafe, the desert oasis front for Baroque Works Officer Agents. At 8 o'clock, Crocodile's summons via Banchi the turtle brings all Officer Agents together. Miss Doublefinger arrives with Mr. 1 (Daz Bonez), her assigned partner per Baroque Works' gender-paired system. She serves and listens as Mr. 0's plan Operation Utopia (to seize Alabasta Kingdom using civil war and Pluton) is relayed via Mr. 2. Her calm professionalism establishes her as the reliable, unsentimental core of the mid-tier officers.

Nami vs Miss Doublefinger — The Thorn and the Thunder

Source One Piece Chapters 191-193; Anime Episodes 117-118Culture One Piece / Alabasta Saga climaxPeriod 2001 publication

Summary

In the palace stairwell, Miss Doublefinger reveals Toge Toge no Mi, sprouting spikes from arms, legs, and hair. She overwhelms Nami physically, piercing walls and nearly impaling her, mocking Nami's Clima-Tact as a toy. Nami, using Usopp's imperfect weapon, initially fails. Remembering Vivi's courage and using tactical cunning, Nami creates a Mirage Tempo and then Thunder Tempo — exploiting that metal spikes conduct electricity. A summoned lightning bolt travels through Doublefinger's own spikes, electrocuting her. Doublefinger is defeated, unconscious, her spikes retracting. Nami stands over her, proving brains over brawn.

Consequences

Removes one Officer Agent from battlefield; gives Nami her first solo victory against a Devil Fruit user, crucial for crew credibility. Establishes Clima-Tact's combat viability. Doublefinger's defeat foreshadows Baroque Works collapse.

Variants

Manga: concise 2-chapter fight; Anime: extended with additional spike attacks and comedic beats. VIZ translation calls attack 'Spike Spike Fruit' vs 'Thorn-Thorn.' No contradictory outcome — defeat is canonical in all versions.

Source One Piece Cover Story, Chapters 359-413 (33 installments)Culture One Piece / Cover Story canonPeriod 2004-2005 publication; in-story shortly after Alabasta

Summary

Miss Goldenweek (Marianne), Miss Valentine (Mikita) and Mr. 5 escape Hina's capture and travel to free their comrades. They find Miss Doublefinger and Mr. 1 in a desert prison, still in striped uniforms. Using Goldenweek's Colors Trap art to manipulate guards and later to inspire the prisoners, they free everyone. Final installments show the reformed Baroque Works (including Doublefinger, now in casual clothes) opening a new Spiders Cafe — now a legitimate cafe/ spider-themed shop — choosing honest work over crime. Doublefinger is shown serving customers alongside former enemies, smiling genuinely for first time.

Miss Doublefinger has no active role in Impel Down, Marineford, Dressrosa, Wano, or Egghead arcs. Relationships
Miss Doublefinger→Crocodile / Mr. 0creator / employer

Boss of Baroque Works; recruited Paula as Officer Agent. She follows his orders without personal loyalty — professional mercenary relationship. Crocodile views all agents as expendable; he does not rescue her after defeat. No attested personal history before recruitment.

Miss Doublefinger→Daz Bonez / Mr. 1ally / partner

Assigned partner under Baroque Works' 'Unluckies' pairing system (numbered male + female). They operate as a duo, share Spiders Cafe assignment, and fight side-by-side at Alubarna (he vs Zoro, she vs Nami). Cover story shows them imprisoned together and freed together, suggesting mutual respect. He is stoic swordsman with Dice-Dice Fruit; she is spike assassin — complementary close-combat styles. No romantic relationship attested; partnership is professional.

Miss Doublefinger→Namihostile / victim (attempted)

Primary adversary. Miss Doublefinger attempts to kill Nami at Alubarna Palace, viewing her as weak thief. Nami defeats her using Thunder Tempo, exploiting spike conductivity. Their fight is thematic mirror: two women who use trickery and transformation (spikes vs Clima-Tact illusions). No prior acquaintance; no post-battle interaction.

Miss Doublefinger→Miss Goldenweek / Marianneally / colleague

Fellow Officer Agent (partner to Mr. 3). Initially junior to Doublefinger, but becomes her rescuer in cover story. Goldenweek's Colors Trap frees Doublefinger from prison, and Doublefinger later works at the reformed cafe alongside her. Shows Baroque Works found-family dynamic post-defeat.

Miss Doublefinger→Nefertari Vivi and Alabasta Kingdomvictim / target

As part of Operation Utopia, Miss Doublefinger participates in plan to overthrow Vivi's kingdom, incite civil war, and kill Vivi and King Cobra. She has no personal grudge against Vivi; Vivi is a job target. Her defeat indirectly saves Vivi's kingdom.

Miss Doublefinger→Mr. 2 Bon Kurei, Mr. 4, Miss Merry Christmas, Miss Valentineally / colleague

Fellow Officer Agents and Frontier Agents. Share Spiders Cafe meeting and Baroque Works hierarchy. Miss Doublefinger shows mild exasperation at Mr. 2's flamboyance and Mr. 4's slowness. Cover story shows collective reformation — they open new Spiders Cafe together, indicating enduring camaraderie.

Miss Doublefinger→Marines (Tashigi, Smoker, Hina)hostile / captor

Arresting authority post-Alabasta. Hina's fleet captures Baroque Works stragglers; Doublefinger is taken into custody. No personal enmity; law enforcement vs criminal. Hina later pursues Miss Goldenweek's rescue party but fails to recapture Doublefinger after escape.

Assassination and Criminal Conspiracy

Source Chapters 160-212; Vivre Card occupation: Baroque Works Officer Agent / Assassin

Description

Miss Doublefinger is a professional assassin employed by Baroque Works, a criminal syndicate that functions as a secret army. Her duties include murder of targets designated by Crocodile, espionage, and participation in Operation Utopia — a plot to cause drought, famine, and civil war in Alabasta that canonically kills hundreds of soldiers and civilians (Chapters 176-212). She personally attempts to murder Nami, a 18-year-old, by impalement.

Scholarly Context

Oda frames Baroque Works as critique of private military companies and colonial exploitation; Alabasta arc explicitly parallels real-world resource wars. No sanitization — manga shows corpses of rebel and royal soldiers.

Incitement of Civil War and Mass Harm

Source Chapters 155-180 (Crocodile's plan exposition)

Description

Operation Utopia involved Dance Powder (drought-inducing weapon), false-flag attacks, and manipulation of both rebel and royal armies. As Officer Agent, Doublefinger is complicit in war crimes even if not shown ordering massacres directly. The arc does not shy from showing child soldiers and starving citizens.

Scholarly Context

Scholarly commentary (e.g., One Piece and Politics analyses) notes Alabasta as allegory for Middle Eastern water conflicts; Doublefinger's role is mid-level perpetrator, not architect.

Contradictions & Disputes

Civilian Name: Paula vs Zala

Position A

Canonical name is Paula (ポーラ, Pōra)

Position A — evidence

Vivre Card No. 0514 (2018) explicitly lists ポーラ (Pōra) and is supervised by Oda; Shueisha's official English Vivre Card translation uses Paula; One Piece official website (one-piece.com) character list uses Paula.

Position B

Canonical name is Zala (ザラ)

Position B — evidence

Early VIZ Media translation (Vol. 18-21, 2004) and some European translations (French Glénat) used Zala, derived from early romaji guess; some wikis retained Zala for years.

Scholarly Consensus

Paula is definitive per latest official source; Zala is deprecated translation variant. OmniLore uses Paula with Zala noted as alias.

Devil Fruit Classification and Translation

Position A

Toge Toge no Mi is Paramecia, translated as Spike-Spike Fruit

Position A — evidence

One Piece Yellow p.214 and Vivre Card classify as Paramecia (超人系); VIZ uses Spike-Spike Fruit; literal toge = thorn/spike both valid

Position B

Fan sources called it Thorn-Thorn Fruit or mislabeled as Logia

Position B — evidence

Early scanlations used Thorn-Thorn; anime subtitles varied; Logia claim stems from misunderstanding that she 'turns into spikes' like Logia transformation

Scholarly Consensus

Paramecia consensus unanimous in official sources; Spike-Spike vs Thorn-Thorn is translation preference, Spike-Spike is VIZ standard.

Fate After Alabasta: Imprisoned vs Reformed

Position A

Remains imprisoned / sent to Impel Down

Position A — evidence

Anime-only viewers who missed cover stories; Chapter 212 shows arrest, no immediate follow-up in main chapters

Position B

Freed and reformed, runs new Spiders Cafe

Position B — evidence

Cover Story Chapters 359-413 (canon, by Oda) shows prison break and reformation; Vivre Card confirms post-Alabasta status as 'former Baroque Works, now cafe owner'

Scholarly Consensus

Cover stories are canon per Oda; reformed status is canonical. Impel Down claim is misconception.
