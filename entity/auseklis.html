Who It Is

Before the morning star fades, a young god rides a horse given by the Sun, his path crossing the sky in a celestial courtship that echoes in thousands of Latvian folk songs. Auseklis, the dawn star, is a figure of youthful vigor and cosmic drama, a deity whose name and symbol have outlived the faith that once sang of him.

Described as very young, playful, and too young to work with other deities. He is a groom in the celestial wedding drama.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From the root aus- ('dawn') plus the derivative suffix -eklis, related to Indo-European dawn deities.

Pronunciation — OW-sek-lis (approximate Latvian pronunciation)

Aliases — Auseklenis, Ausekliņš, Auseklīts, Auseklītis, Ausekliņis, Auseklius

Ownership — Latvian (Baltic); also related to Lithuanian Aušrinė, though Auseklis is specifically Latvian.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description is attested in the provided source. As a celestial deity, he is likely personified as a young male figure, but specifics are unknown.

Symbols — The eight-pointed star (isogonal octagram) is called Auseklis. It is also one of the crosses of Lietuvēns, a protective symbol. In the 1980s, it became a symbol of the third Latvian National Awakening.

Domains — Heavenly realm; associated with the morning star and dawn.

Accounts I
The Celestial WeddingLatvian dainas; analysis by Elza Kokare

Pre-Christian, recorded in 19th century

Auseklis is a suitor or groom of Saules meita (the Sun's daughter). He may court her, but in some versions Mēness interferes, and Auseklis is deprived of his bride. The wedding is a central myth of Baltic celestial deities. Establishes Auseklis as a figure in the celestial hierarchy and explains his relationship with other deities.

Accounts II
The Horse Given by the SunAnalysis by Marija Gimbutas

20th century interpretation

Auseklis is a 'little god' who appears with a horse that the Sun gave him. He falls in love with the Sun's daughter. Highlights his youth and dependence on the Sun.
