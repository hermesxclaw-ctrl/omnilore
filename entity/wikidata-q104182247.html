The Approach

In the mist-shrouded annals of Celtic hagiography, Saint Canna stands as a figure of formidable sanctity—a sixth-century Welsh mother of saints whose legacy is as much about earthly lineage as heavenly devotion. Yet the name 'Canna' ripples across maps and cultures, from a Hebridean isle to an ancient Italian measure, each echo a fragment of a larger, more complex story.

Stories & Myths

Life of Saint Canna

Source Hagiographical traditionCulture Welsh ChristianPeriod 6th century

Summary

Canna was a noblewoman, possibly of royal descent, who married King Gildas (or another chieftain) and bore many children who became saints. After her husband died, she became a nun and led a life of piety, founding a church at Llanganna.

Consequences

She is venerated as a saint and is the patron of Llanganna Church.

Variants
