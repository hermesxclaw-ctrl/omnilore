Who It Is

Hilda Bidan never wanted to be a Gundam mother. An Earth Federation mobile suit engineer who believed she could keep her family safe inside the sterile corridors of the colony at Green Noa, she is remembered not for a heroic battle but for a single, devastating moment in a hallway — when the war she helped build walked through her front door and her own son watched her die.

Attested in Episodes 1-5, 9: Hilda is portrayed as warm, patient, and emotionally supportive toward her son Kamille, in contrast to her husband Franklin Bidan, who is domineering, dismissive, and physically abusive toward Kamille. She attempts to mediate between father and son, encourages Kamille's engineering talent, and expresses worry about Franklin's work with the Titans. She is not politically radical; she appears to accept her role as a Federation civilian engineer and does not openly oppose the Titans until the violence reaches her home. Mobile Suit Engineering and Systems Knowledge

Hilda is identified in official materials and dialogue as an engineer/technician affiliated with the Earth Federation Forces, working on mobile suit development at Green Noa. She is not shown piloting, but possesses technical literacy sufficient to work on the Gundam Mk-II project alongside Franklin. Original prose: She understands mobile suit systems, colony infrastructure, and Federation R&D workflow.

Limit: Civilian engineer, not a test pilot or Newtype. No combat ability, no piloting feats, no authority over military command. Her expertise does not protect her from Titans' violence.

Maternal Mediation and Emotional Support

Narrative function rather than superpower: Hilda provides emotional stability for Kamille, defends him verbally against Franklin's abuse, and encourages his independence. This is her primary agency in the story.

Limit: Unable to prevent Franklin's abuse or the Titans' oppression; her mediation fails. Her kindness is not depicted as altering plot outcomes beyond motivating Kamille's grief.

Civilian Status and Non-Combatant Vulnerability

As a non-combatant, she has no weapons, armor, or evasion training. This is an anti-ability that defines her fate: when Titans soldiers storm the Bidan residence in Episode 9, she is defenseless.

Limit: Cannot resist armed soldiers; death is instantaneous and arbitrary, underscoring war's indiscriminate violence.

Accounts I
Life on Green Noa - The Bidan Family Before the Gryps WarMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episodes 1-4

UC 0087 (in-universe); 1985 (production)

Hilda lives with husband Franklin Bidan and 17-year-old son Kamille on Green Noa 1, a colony used by the Earth Federation and its elite Titans task force to develop the Gundam Mk-II. The family is already fractured: Franklin is absorbed in his work and openly contemptuous of Kamille, especially mocking his feminine-sounding name, while Hilda tries to keep peace. She is seen doing domestic work and discussing Kamille's future, worried that Franklin's ambition and the Titans' presence will endanger them. This establishes the normalcy that the war will shatter. Establishes Hilda as the emotional anchor for Kamille and foreshadows the family's destruction; positions the Bidans as civilian collaborators whose proximity to the Titans makes them targets.

The Theft of the Gundam Mk-II and Emma Sheen's DefectionMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 4 'Emma's Decision'

UC 0087; 1985

When Kamille, angered after being struck by Titans officer Jerid Messa for his name, helps AEUG pilot Quattro Bajeena (Char Aznable) steal the Gundam Mk-II, Green Noa falls into chaos. Hilda is shown in the colony during the security lockdown, separated from Kamille. She learns her son has become involved with the anti-Federation AEUG. Franklin aligns more openly with the Titans to protect his position, while Hilda expresses fear for Kamille rather than condemnation. The family is politically split: Franklin remains with the Titans, Kamille defects to the AEUG, and Hilda is caught in the middle. This split makes the Bidan home a site of suspicion for the Titans.

Father and Son - Escalating AbuseMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 5 'Father and Son'

UC 0087; 1985

Franklin Bidan's abuse toward Kamille intensifies, including physical strikes and verbal humiliation. Hilda intervenes, pleading with Franklin to be gentler and telling Kamille to be patient. A key scene shows her trying to comfort Kamille after Franklin hits him for his rebellious behavior. Her intervention is gentle but ineffective, highlighting her lack of power within the patriarchal family and the militarized colony. She does not leave Franklin, reflecting both personal and economic entrapment. Deepens audience sympathy for Kamille and explains his later rage and instability; cements Hilda as a sympathetic but powerless figure. Critics have noted this as Tomino's recurring theme of failed parenthood in UC Gundam.

Accounts II
The Raid on the Bidan Home - Death of Hilda BidanMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 9 'Kamille's Labyrinth' (Kamīru no Meikyū)

UC 0087; 1985

Titans military police, searching for AEUG collaborators and acting on the chaos after the Mk-II theft, storm civilian residences. In the Bidan apartment, Titans soldiers burst in. Hilda is caught in the doorway/hallway as gunfire erupts. In the TV version, she is shot and killed by Titans soldiers during the raid - not deliberately targeted as an individual, but killed as part of indiscriminate state violence against civilians suspected of harboring AEUG sympathies. Kamille is not present but learns of her death shortly after, and later sees her body. Her death is abrupt, without heroic last words, emphasizing the senselessness of the Titans' repression. Catalyzes Kamille's full radicalization and hatred of the Titans and of his father, whom he blames for bringing the Titans into their lives. Franklin's later death (Episode 11, killed when his untested mobile suit is destroyed) leaves Kamille an orphan. Hilda's death is cited by Tomino as the point where Kamille 'loses his labyrinth' - his childhood home and moral compass.

Aftermath - Kamille's Grief and Franklin's GuiltMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episodes 10-11

UC 0087; 1985

Following Hilda's death, Kamille is shown in shock and then fury, lashing out at Titans officers and at his father. Franklin, who had been absent during the raid, returns to find his wife dead. His reaction is portrayed as hollow and self-justifying - he mourns but also uses her death to justify his continued work for the Titans, claiming it will bring order. Kamille rejects him completely. Hilda is referenced in dialogue as the only parent who truly cared for Kamille, and her absence haunts his subsequent decisions, including his volatile relationship with Four Murasame and his eventual mental collapse. Hilda becomes a posthumous moral presence; her death is repeatedly invoked as the original trauma that explains Kamille's instability and his distrust of adults. In franchise retrospectives, she is listed among the 'mothers of UC' whose deaths illustrate Tomino's anti-war thesis.

Legacy in Expanded Continuity - Mention and FlashbackGundam Ace - Mobile Suit Zeta Gundam Define (manga) by Hiroyuki Kitazume and Yoshiyuki Tomino, 2008-2018; and Gundam Perfect File / Data Collections

2008-2018 (manga); 1990s-2020s (reference books)

In Zeta Gundam Define, which retells the Zeta story with expanded scenes, Hilda's role is faithfully reproduced with additional domestic scenes that emphasize her kindness and her awareness that Franklin's work is dangerous. Reference books such as the Gundam Perfect File and Entertainment Bible reproduce her character sheet and note her as 'deceased, killed during Titans raid on Green Noa.' She also appears in flashback panels when Kamille remembers his parents during later battles, particularly before his final confrontation with Paptimus Scirocco. Maintains her canonical status across all UC retellings; no resurrection or retcon has occurred. Her death remains fixed, unlike some Zeta characters whose fates differ between TV and film (e.g., Four Murasame).

Hilda Bidan was a Newtype or had psychic powers. — No primary source attributes Newtype abilities to Hilda. She is an ordinary human (Oldtype). Confusion arises because her son Kamille is one of the most powerful Newtypes in UC, leading fans to assume heritability, but Newtype is not strictly hereditary and neither parent is shown as Newtype.

Hilda piloted a mobile suit or fought the Titans. — Hilda never pilots a mobile suit or engages in combat in any canonical source. She is a civilian non-combatant. Any image of her in a pilot suit is fanart or confusion with Emma Sheen or Reccoa Londe.

Hilda abandoned Kamille or was a bad mother. — Primary episodes show Hilda as consistently supportive and protective, the only parent who defends Kamille. She does not abandon him; she is killed while he is away. The 'bad mother' reading confuses her powerlessness with neglect.

Hilda survived in the A New Translation films (where many characters' fates were changed). — Unlike Four Murasame and others whose fates were altered in A New Translation, Hilda still dies in Film I. Her death is retained in all official continuities.
