Who It Is

In the vast, echoing archive of Greek mythology, most gods have grand temples, epic poems, and screaming fans. Amyrus has a single, dusty footnote in a Byzantine lexicon — and even that is half-erased. He is the ultimate background character of the ancient world: a name that might be a god, might be a river, might be a town, and is almost certainly a ghost. His story is not one of heroic deeds, but of the sheer, stubborn persistence of a name that refused to be completely forgotten.
