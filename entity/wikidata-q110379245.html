Who It Is

Zeo smiles like any other ten-year-old who loves Beyblade — until his skin sparks, his memories glitch, and his father admits the boy he buried years ago never came home. In the world of Beyblade, Zeo Zagart is not a rival with a tragic past; he is the tragic past, rebuilt in metal and longing, and forced to decide whether a copy can earn the right to be a person.

Attested: Initially cheerful, polite, eager to befriend Tyson Granger and the Bladebreakers, especially eager to be accepted as a 'real' blader. Shows naivety about social norms, intense loyalty to his father Dr. Zagart, and anxiety about disappointing him. After learning he is an android built to replace Dr. Zagart's deceased biological son (also named Zeo), he exhibits identity crisis, withdrawal, and determination to prove his worth through Beyblade rather than through being a perfect copy. Powers
Beyblading - Cerberus (Balance Type)

Wields the Beyblade Cerberus, containing the Bit-Beast Cerberus. In-universe, Bit-Beasts are spirit entities that enhance spin, attack power, and can manifest as spectral beasts. Cerberus is depicted as a three-headed canine that can unleash multi-directional attacks and defensive guarding.

Limit: Requires intact Beyblade and strong blader spirit; Cerberus is not among the Four Sacred Bit-Beasts (Dragoon, Dranzer, Driger, Draciel) and is portrayed as slightly weaker. Power fluctuates with Zeo's emotional state; self-doubt weakens manifestation. Cannot function without Zeo's launch and control.

Android Physiology (Anime Canon)

As an android duplicate, Zeo possesses superhuman durability, resistance to fatigue, and internal mechanical systems. Ep. 43 shows him sustaining damage that would incapacitate a human child but continuing to function, with visible circuitry.

Limit: Not invulnerable; can be damaged, requires repair by Dr. Zagart. Emotional and cognitive functions are human-like and vulnerable to psychological distress. No weapons, flight, or transformation beyond Beyblade manifestation. Power source and maintenance needs never fully explained.

Bit-Beast Communication / Sensitivity

Due to his creation in proximity to Bit-Beast research, Zeo shows heightened sensitivity to Bit-Beast energy, able to sense the sacred rock and the presence of other Bit-Beasts.

Limit: Sensitivity is plot-driven, not a controllable power; causes distress rather than advantage. Lost or diminished after he chooses his own path separate from his father's research.

No Supernatural Powers Outside Beyblade Context

Zeo has no magic, shapeshifting, or mythic powers beyond the franchise's Beyblade system. He is not a deity, spirit, or folklore being.

Limit: All abilities are contingent on franchise technology/mysticism of Bit-Beasts. Cannot be transplanted to other universes.

Accounts I
Introduction - The Boy Who Wanted to BladeBeyblade: V-Force Ep. 31-35

2002 (in-universe contemporary)

Zeo is introduced as the cheerful son of the reclusive scientist Dr. Zagart, who invites Tyson and the Bladebreakers to his island lab. Zeo befriends Tyson, expressing admiration for Dragoon and a desperate desire to become a true blader rather than just a scientist's son. He is given the Beyblade Cerberus and begins training, showing natural talent but also a need for validation. Establishes Zeo as sympathetic rival/ally and sets up his dependence on paternal approval. Cerberus is introduced as his Bit-Beast.

The Zagart Tournament and Search for the Sacred Bit-BeastsBeyblade: V-Force Ep. 36-42

2002

Dr. Zagart reveals his research into the rock containing the four Sacred Bit-Beasts, seeking their power to bring his work to fruition. Zeo participates in battles to test Cerberus against other bladers, acting as his father's field agent while secretly longing to be seen as an equal competitor. He struggles when his father's obsession puts his new friends in danger. Positions Zeo between loyalty to his father and loyalty to his friends; foreshadows that his father's goal is not purely scientific.

Revelation - The Android SonBeyblade: V-Force Ep. 43

2002

In the central twist, it is revealed that the original human Zeo Zagart died as a young child due to illness/accident (details vary by translation). Unable to accept the loss, Dr. Zagart built an android replica and transferred his memories/identity onto it, raising it as his son. During a battle, Zeo is damaged and his mechanical interior is exposed. Dr. Zagart confesses, and Zeo experiences a profound identity crisis, questioning whether his feelings and friendships are real or programmed. Core emotional arc: Zeo must decide if he is a person or a copy. Dr. Zagart's grief and unethical use of technology are condemned. This episode is the most cited by fans and the source of content advisories about child death.

Accounts II
Choosing His Own Path - Zeo vs. TysonBeyblade: V-Force Ep. 44-48

2002

Rejecting being merely his father's tool, Zeo chooses to battle Tyson not for his father's plan but to prove his own existence. He unleashes Cerberus at full power, with the three heads manifesting independently. Tyson's Dragoon ultimately defeats him, but Tyson affirms Zeo as a real friend and real blader regardless of his origin. Zeo collapses but is not destroyed. Zeo's defeat is framed as moral victory; he gains self-acceptance. Dr. Zagart's plan begins to collapse as Zeo refuses to cooperate.

Redemption and DepartureBeyblade: V-Force Ep. 49-51

2002

After Dr. Zagart's ultimate plan to extract the Sacred Bit-Beasts fails and his lab is destroyed, Zeo forgives his father while also asserting independence. He is repaired and chooses to leave the island to travel and find his own purpose, promising Tyson they will meet again as equals. The ending leaves him alive and autonomous, not deactivated. Provides closure without death of the android Zeo; themes of chosen family and personhood. Zeo does not appear as a regular in Beyblade G-Revolution (season 3), making this his narrative conclusion.

Legacy Cameo - Guidebooks and GamesBeyblade Official Encyclopedia and video games (2002-2004)

2002-2004

Zeo appears as an unlockable character in Beyblade: V-Force Ultimate Blader Jam (GBA) and in Takara guidebooks that recap his story for new readers. These materials treat his android origin as canonical trivia and list his Beyblade stats, but add no new story. He is absent from later Beyblade Burst continuity, confirming his story is self-contained to the original generation. Establishes Zeo as a generation-specific character, not a recurring franchise mascot. No further canonical development after 2004.

Zeo is a Power Rangers character. — Power Rangers Zeo is a team/season title (Zeo Rangers, Zeo Crystal). There is no major individual character named Zeo in that series. The Beyblade character Zeo Zagart is unrelated and from a different franchise and country (Japan vs US adaptation).

Zeo is a human boy who just happens to be good at Beyblade. — In Beyblade V-Force canon, Zeo is revealed to be an android replica built after the death of Dr. Zagart's biological son. His human-like behavior is intentional to explore artificial personhood. Ignoring the reveal misses his core arc.

Cerberus is one of the Four Sacred Bit-Beasts. — The Four Sacred Bit-Beasts are Dragoon, Dranzer, Driger, and Draciel. Cerberus is Zeo's personal Bit-Beast, not sacred, and is portrayed as less powerful. Dr. Zagart seeks the sacred four; Zeo's Cerberus is separate.

Zeo appears throughout all Beyblade series. — Zeo appears only in the original generation's V-Force arc (2002) and related games/guidebooks. He does not appear in Beyblade: G-Revolution as a regular, nor in Beyblade: Metal Saga or Beyblade Burst, which are separate continuities.
