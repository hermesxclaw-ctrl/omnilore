Who It Is

A name that echoes through Japanese cinema, Suzume Iwato is the teenage protagonist of Makoto Shinkai's 2022 animated film 'Suzume no Tojimari' (Suzume's Locking Up), a journey that begins with a chance encounter and a mysterious door and unfolds into a nationwide quest to close the portals that unleash calamity upon Japan.

Suzume is portrayed as a brave, resourceful, and compassionate young woman. She is initially somewhat reserved and introverted, but her journey forces her to become proactive and decisive. She is deeply empathetic, often putting others' needs before her own, and she carries a quiet grief over the loss of her mother in the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake. She is also curious and somewhat impulsive, as seen when she first opens the mysterious door. Throughout the film, she grows in confidence and learns to confront her past trauma.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The given name 'Suzume' (すずめ) means 'sparrow' in Japanese, a bird often associated with resilience and return. The surname 'Iwato' (岩戸) literally means 'rock door' or 'stone door', evoking the mythical cave where the sun goddess Amaterasu hid, connecting to the film's theme of doors and sealing.

Pronunciation — soo-zoo-meh ee-wah-toh

Aliases — Suzume Iwato

Ownership — Japanese contemporary popular culture; created by Makoto Shinkai and produced by CoMix Wave Films and Toho.

Powers
Locking Abilities

Suzume is able to lock the supernatural doors that serve as portals to the 'Ever-After' realm, using a special key that she obtains from Souta. When she locks a door, she must focus on the memory of the place and utter a closing incantation, which seals the door and prevents the 'worm' (a giant, otherworldly being) from emerging and causing earthquakes.

Limit: She can only lock doors that are opened by the worm or its agents. The key is not originally hers; it was entrusted to her by Souta. She cannot lock a door without focusing on the memory of the place, which requires emotional connection.

Empathy and Connection

Suzume has a unique ability to connect with others and with places, which is central to her success. She can sense the memories and emotions tied to locations, and this empathy allows her to understand what needs to be locked away. This is not a supernatural power but a deep emotional intelligence.

Limit: This ability is emotionally taxing; she experiences the grief and trauma of the places she visits.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Suzume is a 17-year-old high school girl with shoulder-length dark brown hair, often worn loose or in a simple ponytail. She has large, expressive brown eyes and a fair complexion. In the film, she typically wears a school uniform (sailor suit) and later a casual outfit consisting of a white shirt, a red jacket, and a black skirt. She is often depicted with a determined expression, but her face reveals vulnerability in moments of reflection.

Symbols — The central symbol associated with Suzume is the key she uses to lock doors, which appears as a glowing, ornate key that transforms from a small object to a large, magical key. The doors themselves are often depicted as standing alone in various landscapes, with a surreal, otherworldly glow. Suzume's red jacket is a recurring visual motif, standing out against the muted tones of the environment, symbolizing her vitality and determination.

Domains — Suzume's primary habitat is modern-day Japan, specifically her hometown of Kyushu and the various locations she travels to across the country, including ruins, abandoned towns, and rural areas. She is a high school student, so her daily life involves school and home, but her adventure takes her to liminal spaces, such as abandoned amusement parks, flooded towns, and the ruins of her childhood home. The Journey to Close the DoorsSuzume no Tojimari (film, novel, manga)

2022 (set in modern day, with flashbacks to 2011)

Suzume Iwato, a 17-year-old girl living in a quiet town in Kyushu, encounters a mysterious young man named Souta Munakata who is searching for an abandoned door. Driven by curiosity, Suzume follows him and discovers a door standing alone in the ruins of an abandoned resort. When she opens it, she inadvertently releases a 'worm' that causes an earthquake. To stop the disaster, Suzume must travel across Japan with Souta, who has been transformed into a chair by a curse, to close these doors before more calamities strike. Along the way, she learns about the 'Ever-After' realm, the source of the worms, and confronts her own past trauma related to the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake, in which she lost her mother. Ultimately, she must make a personal sacrifice to save Souta and prevent a catastrophic earthquake from hitting Tokyo. Suzume successfully closes the doors and saves Japan from further disasters.
