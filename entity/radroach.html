Who It Is

A giant cockroach mutated by radiation following the Great War. Radroaches are one of the most common and weakest creatures in the wasteland. They have tough chitinous shells and can survive in extremely radioactive environments. Despite their weak individual threat, they often swarm in large numbers.

The Approach

In the post-apocalyptic wastelands of the Fallout universe, few creatures are as ubiquitous—or as underestimated—as the radroach. These irradiated cockroaches, mutated by centuries of nuclear fallout, scuttle through ruined buildings and irradiated ruins, serving as both a nuisance and a grim reminder of humanity's folly. But beyond their role as low-level pests, radroaches have burrowed deep into gaming culture, becoming emblematic of survival, resilience, and the absurdity of a world gone mad.

Content advisory. Mild science-fiction violence; cockroach-related phobias; post-apocalyptic themes.
Identity

Radroach

Pronunciation /ˈrædroʊtʃ/

Known As

Giant cockroachIrradiated cockroachWasteland roach

Etymology

A portmanteau of 'radiation' and 'cockroach', reflecting its origin as a cockroach mutated by nuclear fallout.

Cultural Ownership

The radroach is a creation of Interplay Entertainment and Bethesda Softworks, belonging to the Fallout intellectual property.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans have conflated radroaches with other mutated insects in Fallout, such as radscorpions or bloatflies, but they are distinct species. In earlier concept art, radroaches were sometimes depicted with more exaggerated features, leading to minor inconsistencies between games.

Description

Appearance

Radroaches are large, mutated cockroaches, typically ranging from the size of a cat to a small dog. They have a dark brown or black chitinous exoskeleton, long antennae, six spiny legs, and a segmented abdomen. Their eyes are compound and often glow with a faint red or green luminescence, suggesting radiation exposure. In Fallout 4, they are depicted with a slightly more detailed texture, showing cracked carapaces and a sickly sheen. Their mandibles are prominent and capable of delivering painful bites.

Symbols & Iconography

In the Fallout franchise, radroaches symbolize the resilience of life in a post-nuclear world. They are often used as a tutorial enemy, representing the mundane dangers of the wasteland. Their image has become iconic for the series, appearing in promotional art, merchandise, and fan art.

Behaviour & Personality

Radroaches are aggressive but relatively weak creatures. They attack by lunging at their prey, biting with their mandibles. They are often found in groups, emerging from dark corners, ruined buildings, and sewers. They are not particularly intelligent, but their swift movements and numbers can overwhelm an unprepared traveler. They are attracted to heat and food sources, making human settlements a common hunting ground.

Domains & Habitats

Radroaches thrive in urban ruins, underground tunnels, and any area with organic debris. They are common in the Capital Wasteland, the Commonwealth, and Appalachia. They are particularly abundant in Vaults, where they often infest the corridors and living quarters.

Version Differences

Across the Fallout series, radroaches have remained largely consistent in appearance and behavior. In Fallout (1997) and Fallout 2 (1998), they were depicted as top-down sprites, smaller and less detailed. In Fallout 3 (2008) and Fallout: New Vegas (2010), they were rendered in 3D, with a more menacing appearance and larger size. Fallout 4 (2015) and Fallout 76 (2018) retained this design but added more realistic textures and animations. In Fallout: Brotherhood of Steel (2004), they were depicted as even larger and more aggressive, likely due to the action-oriented gameplay.

Contradictions & Disputes

Size variation

Position A

Radroaches are consistently depicted as small, cat-sized creatures.

Position A — evidence

Fallout 3, Fallout: New Vegas, Fallout 4, Fallout 76 show them as relatively small.

Position B

In some games, such as Fallout: Brotherhood of Steel, they are larger and more threatening.

Position B — evidence

Fallout: Brotherhood of Steel features larger radroaches due to its action-oriented gameplay.

Scholarly Consensus

The size difference is attributed to gameplay and visual style differences, not lore contradictions.

Radiation damage

Position A

Radroaches are resistant to radiation and do not cause radiation damage.

Position A — evidence

In most games, radroach attacks deal only physical damage.

Position B

In some games, their bite may inflict a small amount of radiation damage.

Position B — evidence

In Fallout 4, some players report receiving radiation damage from radroach bites, though this may be a bug.

Scholarly Consensus

The official game guides do not list radiation damage for radroach bites, so it is likely a bug or rare occurrence.
