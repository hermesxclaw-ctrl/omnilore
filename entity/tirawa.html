Who It Is

Before the world was a world, there was only the vast, silent expanse of the sky, and in that sky dwelled a power so vast it had no name, only the will to create. This was Tirawa, the architect of the cosmos, a god who did not shape the world with his hands, but spoke it into being through the stars. He is the ultimate 'boss' of the Pawnee universe, a celestial CEO who delegated the day-to-day running of creation to his starry vice-presidents, the Morning Star and the Evening Star. He is not a god you can see or touch, but the order of the universe itself—the reason the sun rises, the corn grows, and the people have a path to walk.

Tirawa is a remote, powerful, and orderly creator. He delegates authority and creative tasks to the celestial bodies—the stars, sun, and moon—who act as his chiefs or agents. He is not a capricious or interventionist deity in daily human affairs, but he set the universe in motion and established its laws. He is a source of ultimate authority and moral order, to whom the Pawnee prayed for guidance, provision, and cosmic balance. He is also a deity who gave the Pawnee their rituals and ceremonies to maintain this balance.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name's precise linguistic roots in the Pawnee (Skiri and South Band) language are not fully recoverable from published sources. 'Atius' is often glossed as 'father' or 'our father,' making 'Atius Tirawa' roughly 'Father Tirawa' or 'Our Father Above.' The name itself is treated as a proper noun,

Powers
Cosmic Creation

Tirawa created the universe, the earth, and all living things. He did this by delegating creative power to the celestial beings, who worked under his direction. He created the first humans from clay and gave them the breath of life.

Limit: He works through intermediaries, the stars. He is not described as directly micromanaging the world after creation.

Supreme Authority and Lawgiver

Tirawa established the cosmic and moral order. He gave the Pawnee their ceremonies, rituals, and social structure. He is the ultimate source of knowledge and power, and all other spiritual beings are subordinate to him.

Limit: His authority is absolute but is exercised through the established cosmic order and the rituals he ordained.

Provision and Guidance

Tirawa is a provider. He is prayed to for bountiful harvests (through his agents, the stars), successful hunts, and the general well-being of the people. He guides the priests and shamans in their rituals and visions.

Limit: He responds through the established order and through the rituals given to the people. He is not a personal, intimate god who appears to individuals, but a distant, all-powerful father.

Bestower of Sacred Knowledge

Tirawa gave the Pawnee the sacred bundles and the knowledge of how to perform the ceremonies associated with them. This knowledge is essential for maintaining the balance of the world.

Limit: This knowledge is entrusted to specific individuals and societies within the tribe.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Tirawa is not described in anthropomorphic or zoomorphic terms in the primary sources. He is a formless, omnipresent power associated with the sky, the heavens, and the overarching cosmic order. He is less a 'being' with a body and more a pervasive, creative force. His 'appearance' is the sky itself and the ordered movement of the celestial bodies.

Symbols — The Sky itself is his primary symbol. The celestial bodies—particularly the Morning Star (the male principle, associated with the east, war, and the Skiri band) and the Evening Star (the female principle, associated with the west, agriculture, and fertility)—are his most prominent agents and children. Accounts I
The Creation of the UniverseGeorge A. Dorsey, 'The Pawnee: Mythology (Part I)' (collected from Skiri and South Band informants)

Pre-reservation era, recorded in the early 20th century

In the beginning, there was only Tirawa. He created the heavens and the earth. He placed the stars in the sky, giving each one power and a role. The Morning Star and the Evening Star were his most powerful helpers. He instructed the Morning Star to create the sun and the Evening Star to create the moon. Together, they were to rule the sky and the earth. Tirawa then created the first humans, the Pawnee, and placed them on the earth. He gave them the sacred bundles and taught them the ceremonies they would need to live in harmony with the cosmos. He told them to look to the stars for guidance and to pray to him through them. After establishing this order, Tirawa withdrew to the heavens, becoming a remote but ever-present authority. The creation of the ordered universe, the establishment of the Pawnee people, and the foundation of all Pawnee ritual and social life. The stars became the intermediaries between Tirawa and humanity.

Accounts II
The Origin of the Sacred BundlesGeorge A. Dorsey, 'The Pawnee: Mythology (Part I)'

Pre-reservation era, recorded in the early 20th century

The sacred bundles, the most important religious objects of the Pawnee, were given to the people by Tirawa through the stars. The bundles contained objects like sacred pipes, corn, and animal skins, each with a specific ritual meaning. The power and knowledge of how to use these bundles were revealed to the priests and shamans in visions. The bundles were not just tools; they were living embodiments of the cosmic order, containing the power of the stars themselves. They were kept in special lodges and were central to all major ceremonies. The establishment of the central religious institution of the Pawnee people. The bundles were the tangible link between the people and the celestial powers.

The Hako CeremonyAlice C. Fletcher, 'The Hako: A Pawnee Ceremony'

Pre-reservation era, recorded in the late 19th century

The Hako was a major, multi-day ceremony performed to ensure the well-being of the people, particularly the children, and to maintain the relationship between the human and spiritual worlds. It was a complex ritual involving songs, dances, and the use of a sacred pole. The ceremony was directed to Tirawa, but it was mediated by the stars and other spiritual beings. The Hako was a way of 'renewing the covenant' between the people and their creator, ensuring his continued favor and the fertility of the earth and the people. The Hako was a powerful communal ritual that reinforced social bonds and the people's place in the cosmos. It was a direct address to Tirawa for his blessing.

Tirawa is the 'Great Spirit' of all Native Americans. — Tirawa is a specific deity of the Pawnee people. The concept of a 'Great Spirit' is a generalized term often used by non-Natives to homogenize diverse Native American beliefs. Tirawa has a specific name, mythology, and ritual practice unique to the Pawnee.

Tirawa is an anthropomorphic god with a physical form. — Tirawa is not described in human or animal form. He is a formless, omnipresent power associated with the sky and the cosmic order. He is the sky and the heavens, not a being who lives in them.

The Morning Star sacrifice was a common, regular practice. — The Morning Star sacrifice was a rare and highly significant ritual, not a regular event. The last known instance was in 1838. It was a response to specific cosmic and social pressures and was not a routine part of Pawnee life.

Tirawa is a 'sky god' like Zeus or Odin. — While Tirawa is associated with the sky, he is not a personal, anthropomorphic sky god who lives on a mountain and intervenes in human affairs. He is a more abstract, transcendent creator who established the cosmic order and delegates authority to the stars. He is more akin to a cosmic principle than a personal god.
