Who It Is

In the heart of the Venezuelan forest, a girl with a voice like a bird's weaves through the emerald shadows, speaking a language that predates words. She is Rima, the Daughter of the Didi, a creature of myth and wilderness who has haunted the edges of civilization since W. H. Hudson first conjured her in 1904. Her story is one of loss, love, and the brutal collision between the wild and the modern—a tale that has been reshaped by film, comics, and cartoons, yet never fully tamed.

In the novel, Rima is shy, gentle, and speaks in a bird-like language, reflecting her closeness to nature. She is curious about Abel but fearful of other humans. She has a deep spiritual connection to the forest and its creatures. In the DC Comics version, she is more assertive and heroic, using her abilities to protect the jungle from threats. In First Wave, she is silent and mysterious, communicating only through whistles.

Powers
Communication with Birds

Rima can understand and speak the language of birds, and can communicate with them. She uses this ability to gather information and summon birds for assistance.

Limit: Limited to birds; in the novel, her speech is described as bird-like, not a universal animal language.

Animal Friendship

She can befriend and command animals, such as calling a bear to push down trees or summoning crocodiles to attack enemies.

Limit: Seems to require mutual trust; animals respond to her commands but not always instantaneously.

Agility and Speed

She is extremely agile and swift, able to move through the jungle with ease.

Limit: Not superhuman, but exceptional for a human.

Plucking Poison Darts from Air

She can catch poison darts mid-air, showcasing her reflexes and speed.

Limit: Requires quick reflexes; not a supernatural ability.

Reputation for Magic

Natives believe she has magical powers, which serves as a defense mechanism.

Limit: It is a reputation, not actual magic; her 'magic' is her natural abilities.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the novel, Rima is described as small (4'6"), demure, dark-haired, and about 17 years old. She is of mysterious origin, possibly of white ancestry, but raised in the jungle. In the 1959 film, Audrey Hepburn portrayed her with a more conventional Hollywood look, with dark hair and delicate features. In DC Comics' 1974 series, she is depicted as a grown woman with ash-blonde hair, wearing a leopard-skin bikini, and is more physically powerful. I

Symbols — Rima is associated with birds, particularly the 'bell bird' whose song she imitates. She is often depicted with a bird perched on her shoulder or hand. The name 'Rima' itself is linked to the Spanish word for rhyme, suggesting a poetic connection. In visual adaptations, she is often shown in a jungle setting with lush vegetation and wildlife.

Domains — Rima's domain is the tropical rainforest of South America, specifically the region that is now Venezuela. She lives in a secluded part of the forest, away from human settlements, and is deeply tied to the ecosystem. In adaptations, she is often shown as the protector of this environment.

Accounts I
Green MansionsW. H. Hudson's novel

1904

Abel, a political fugitive from Venezuela, flees into the jungle and encounters Rima, a mysterious girl who lives among the birds. They develop a deep bond, and Abel falls in love with her. Rima reveals that she is of unknown origin, possibly from a lost tribe. The local natives fear her, and when they discover her hiding place, they set the forest ablaze, killing Rima. Abel is devastated and leaves the jungle. Rima's death symbolizes the destruction of the wilderness. Abel's grief and departure highlight the theme of loss.

Rima the Jungle Girl (comic series)DC Comics

1974-1975

Rima is depicted as a blonde, powerful jungle heroine who protects her domain from poachers, criminals, and supernatural threats. She is aided by her ability to communicate with animals and her agility. The series ran for seven issues, featuring various adventures. The series was cancelled after seven issues, but Rima remained a cult favorite.

Accounts II
First WaveDC Comics

2010-2011

In this reimagining, Rima is a silent native woman with piercings and tattoos, communicating via bird whistles. She appears as a supporting character in a pulp-inspired universe. The series was well-received but limited to six issues.

The All-New Super Friends HourHanna-Barbera

1977-1980

Rima appears as a guest star in three episodes, teaming up with superheroes to fight environmental and criminal threats. In 'Fire', she helps Batman and Robin stop a forest fire and escaped prisoners. In 'River of Doom', she aids Wonder Woman in rescuing archaeologists from natives. In 'Return of Atlantis', she joins Wonder Woman to stop Queen Ocina's conquest. Rima became known to a wider audience through the cartoon.

Rima is a typical 'jungle girl' like Sheena, Queen of the Jungle. — Rima is a more ethereal and tragic figure, more connected to nature and less action-oriented. She is a literary creation with poetic speech, not a pulp adventure heroine.

Rima is based on a real person. — There is no evidence that Rima was based on a real individual. Hudson drew on legends of a lost white tribe, but Rima is fictional.

Rima is an indigenous person. — In the novel, Rima is of unknown origin, possibly of European descent, and is distinct from the local indigenous tribe.

Rima has supernatural powers. — Rima's abilities are natural, such as communication with birds and agility, not supernatural. Her reputation for magic is based on the natives' superstition.
