Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Magwayen' is derived from the Visayan word 'magway', meaning 'to ferry' or 'to transport', and the suffix '-en', which is a common diminutive form in Visayan languages.

Pronunciation — Mag-way-en

Aliases — Maguayan, Mahulayon

Ownership — Magwayen is a deity from the pre-colonial mythology of the Visayan people, specifically from the island of Panay in the Philippines.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Magwayen is often depicted as a goddess with a gentle and compassionate face, wearing a crown of shells and carrying a staff made of a giant clamshell.

Symbols — Magwayen is associated with the symbols of the sea, including shells, fish, and the moon.

Domains — Magwayen is said to reside in the underwater realm of the Visayan people, where she ferries the spirits of the dead to the afterlife.

Accounts I
The Creation of Madja-asPedro Monteclaro's book titled Maragtas

Pre-colonial

According to the story, Magwayen was created by Datu Sumakwel to exercise his authority over all the other datus of Panay. The creation of Magwayen led to the establishment of the confederacy of Madja-as, which united the datus of Panay under one rule.

Accounts II
Magwayen's Journey to the UnderworldMagwayen - Myth and Folklore Wiki - Fandom

Modern

According to the story, Magwayen embarked on a journey to the underworld to rescue the spirits of the dead, who were trapped in the treacherous waters of the underworld. Magwayen's journey to the underworld led to the release of the spirits of the dead, who were able to move on to the afterlife.
