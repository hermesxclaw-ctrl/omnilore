In the darkness between the villages, a sound like iron chains dragging over cobblestones — it is the Kludde, changing from dog to cat to man, hunting for those who walk alone at night.

Who It Is

De Kludde is a terrifying water-and-night spirit from Flemish folklore (Dutch-speaking Belgium), particularly prevalent in the triangle between Brussels, Ghent, and Antwerp. He is a shapeshifter who can appear in many forms: a large black dog with glowing red eyes, a giant cat with spikes on its back, a raven, a goat, or most characteristically, a tall, gaunt man in black who drags heavy chains behind him. The sound of chains clanking is the Kludde's signature warning. Despite his many forms, all share common features: two of his legs are human, and two are animal; his eyes burn like coals; and he is always accompanied by the sound of dragging chains. The Kludde lurks near water — canals, rivers, and ponds — and along lonely country roads at night. He attacks lone travelers, especially those returning home drunk or late. His preferred method is to leap upon the back of his victim and cling there, digging in with clawed feet, while growing heavier and heavier until the victim collapses from exhaustion. The Kludde may then drown the victim in the nearest body of water. Unlike many water spirits, the Kludde can leave the water and travel on land. He is said to be the spirit of a witch or a warlock who sold their soul to the devil and now roams the Flemish countryside as punishment. Protective measures against the Kludde include carrying a blessed candle, reciting the Lord's Prayer backward (a dangerous act in itself in folk belief), or throwing sand or salt behind one's back while running. The Kludde shares features with the Dutch Aufhocker, the English Black Shuck, and the Belgian Nekker.
