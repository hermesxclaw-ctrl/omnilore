Who It Is

Imagine walking home on a moonless night through the Flemish countryside. You hear a rustling behind you, and a small dog appears at your heels. It grows, its eyes glowing like embers, and before you can react, it leaps onto your back, digging claws into your shoulders. You are carrying the Kludde, and it will not let go until you cross a bridge. This is the terror that haunted Flemish travelers for centuries.

Kludde is a malicious and mischievous spirit. It lurks in rural areas, especially near water, bridges, and forests, and preys on travelers, particularly those who are alone or out late at night. It may jump on a person's back, causing them to carry it for miles, or it may chase them, laughing or cackling. It can also appear as a harmless animal to gain trust. It is not known to kill, but it terrorizes and exhausts its victims. It may be repelled by holy symbols, prayers, or by turning one's clothes inside out.

Powers
Shapeshifting

Kludde can transform into various animals, especially a black dog, cat, horse, pig, or calf. It may also take on a human appearance, often with a monstrous or distorted face. The transformation is used to deceive or frighten victims.

Limit: It cannot fully disguise its supernatural nature; it often retains glowing eyes or an unnatural smell. It may be forced to reveal its true form if addressed by name or if a holy object is used.

Riding on backs

Kludde may jump onto a traveler's back and refuse to get off, making the person carry it for a long distance. The weight is often described as immense, and the spirit may dig its claws into the victim's shoulders.

Limit: The victim can free themselves by reaching a bridge or crossing a stream, or by using a charm.

Glowing eyes and frightening aura

Kludde's eyes glow like coals, and it can emit a terrifying aura or sound, such as cackling laughter or a rattling chain, to induce fear.

Limit: Fear may be resisted by brave individuals or those with protective charms.

Water affinity

In some versions, Kludde is closely tied to water and may drag victims into ponds or rivers, drowning them.

Limit: Not present in all tales; in many, it stays on land.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kludde is most commonly described as a large, black, shaggy dog with fiery or glowing eyes. It may also appear as a black cat, a horse, a pig, a calf, or even a human with a distorted face. In some tales, it appears as a monstrous creature with a long tail and sharp claws. It is often said to have a foul smell and to leave a trail of slime or fire. Its shape-shifting ability is central to its nature, and it may change form to lure victims.

Symbols — Kludde is associated with darkness, night, and water. It is often depicted in folklore illustrations as a black dog with a chain or as a shadowy figure near bridges, ditches, or crossroads. It may be accompanied by a rattling chain or a sound like a heavy object being dragged.

Domains — Kludde inhabits the Flemish countryside: woods, fields, ditches, ponds, bridges, and crossroads. It is particularly associated with water, though it is not a purely aquatic spirit. It is nocturnal, active mainly at night, and may be encountered during storms or fog.

The Ride on Kludde's BackFlemish oral tradition, recorded in 19th-century collections

Unknown; recorded in 19th century

A farmer is returning home late at night when he sees a small dog following him. The dog grows larger and jumps onto his back, becoming extremely heavy. The farmer tries to shake it off but cannot, and the Kludde digs its claws into his shoulders, cackling. The farmer carries it for miles until he reaches a bridge, where the Kludde jumps off and disappears into the water. The farmer is exhausted and bears scars on his shoulders. He learns to avoid traveling alone at night.

Kludde is a unique, purely Flemish invention. — Kludde shares many traits with other European spirits, such as the Aufhocker and the Nekker, and may be a regional manifestation of broader folklore.

Kludde is a terrifying demon that kills people. — In most tales, Kludde is a trickster that frightens and harasses rather than kills. Drowning is rare and only in certain variants.

Kludde is the same as Lange Wapper. — They are distinct entities, though both are Flemish shapeshifters. Lange Wapper is more human-like and associated with Antwerp.
