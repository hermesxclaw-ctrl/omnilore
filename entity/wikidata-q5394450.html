Who It Is

He starts as the boy who walks to school late on purpose, hands in pockets, muttering sarcasm to hide the bruises at home — and ends as a husband and father who learns that the most heroic thing a Key protagonist can do is not save a magical girl, but show up, stay, and keep living after loss. Tomoya Okazaki is not a chosen one with powers, but a delinquent who becomes the emotional center of Clannad, the visual novel that redefined the nakige (crying game) genre.

Attested personality across all versions: sarcastic, blunt, outwardly cynical and lazy, using humor and feigned indifference as defense. He is intelligent but underachieving due to disengagement from school after his shoulder injury and family trauma. Internally, he is deeply empathetic, observant, and self-sacrificing - he compulsively helps others (Fuko, Kotomi, Tomoyo, Kyou/Ryou, Misae) even at cost to himself, because he understands loneliness. He is loyal to Sunohara despite constant teasing, and respectful to adults who earn it (Akio, Sanae). Character arc: from nihilistic delinquent

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese personal name. Family name Okazaki (岡崎) means 'hill cape' or 'hill promontory' (oka = hill, saki = cape/point). Given name Tomoya (朋也) is commonly written with kanji 朋 (tomo = friend, companion) + 也 (ya = to be, emphatic suffix). Key's official materials render it as 岡崎朋也. The reading Tomoy

Pronunciation — Japanese: [to.mo.ja o.ka.za.ki] — toh-moh-yah oh-kah-zah-kee, with flat pitch accent, stress on second mora of Tomoya. English dub: toh-MOY-uh oh-kuh-ZAH-kee.

Aliases — Okazaki Tomoya, 岡崎 朋也 (Okazaki Tomoya)

Ownership — Original character created by Key (brand of VisualArt's), scenario by Jun Maeda, Kai, Yūichi Suzumoto and Tōya Okano; character design by Itaru Hinoue. Owned by VisualArt's / Key.

Powers
Athleticism and Brawling (Former Basketball)

Former star of junior high basketball team; tall, fast, strong. After a fight with his father Naoyuki injured his right shoulder, he can no longer raise his arm fully or play competitively, but retains street-fighting ability and stamina.

Limit: Permanent shoulder injury prevents overhead sports; chronic pain hinted. Not superhuman; loses fights against larger groups and is often bruised.

Social Intelligence and Mediation

Exceptional ability to read lonely or troubled people, gain trust quickly, and orchestrate solutions (rebuilding drama club, helping Fuko's sister's wedding, reuniting Kotomi with her past, managing Tomoyo's student council campaign). Often acts as counselor/confidant.

Limit: Cannot solve his own family trauma without external help; his interventions sometimes backfire (e.g., initially worsening Kotomi's trauma by pushing too hard). Requires time and repeated effort.

Light Orb Affinity (Hikari no Tama)

Unconsciously collects orbs of light granted when he genuinely makes others happy (Fuko, Kotomi, etc.). In VN lore, these lights are manifestations of the town's happiness and can grant a miracle when enough are gathered. In the true ending, they reverse Nagisa and Ushio's deaths.

Limit: Tomoya does not control orbs consciously; they only activate when his intentions are selfless. Requires multiple completed happinesses. In anime, the miracle is presented more ambiguously as wish fulfillment.

Illusionary World Link (Junk Doll)

Psychic/spiritual connection to the Illusionary World where his 'other self' is a doll built by a lonely girl (Ushio's soul). He experiences dreams of snow, the girl, and the robot. This world is revealed to be a manifestation of Ushio's dying dream and the town's collective unconscious.

Limit: No active control; appears as dreams and visions, especially during emotional distress. Only fully understood in After Story's climax.

Manual Labor and Electrical Work

Post-graduation, trained as electrician under Yoshino Yusuke; competent with wiring, construction, and later office sales. Represents his transition to working-class adulthood.

Limit: Initially underpaid and overworked; struggles with commuting and fatigue. Not a supernatural power but a grounded skill.

Accounts I
On the Hill Where Cherry Blossoms Flutter - Meeting Nagisa FurukawaClannad visual novel Common Route; anime Episode 1 (2007)

2004 (VN release), set in 2003-2004 school year

Tomoya, a third-year delinquent who hates his town and arrives late every day, meets Nagisa Furukawa mumbling to herself at the foot of the cherry blossom hill: 'Anpan...' He impulsively shouts it with her, breaking her spell of anxiety. Learning she repeated a year due to chronic illness and wants to revive the drama club, he reluctantly agrees to help. Through recruiting members and confronting Nagisa's self-doubt and her parents Akio and Sanae, Tomoya rediscovers purpose. This arc establishes the central romance and the theme that small kindnesses accumulate as light. Tomoya falls in love with Nagisa; the drama club succeeds; Nagisa gains confidence to perform; Tomoya begins repairing his view of family. Unlocks Nagisa route and After Story.

The Illusionary World - The Girl and the Junk DollClannad VN Illusionary World interludes; anime cold opens throughout both seasons

2004-2008

Interspersed with the main story, a snow-covered wasteland where only a young Girl and a Doll made of junk exist. The Doll was built by the Girl to have company. The Girl is lonely and slowly losing warmth as winter deepens. The Doll tries to build a vehicle to escape with her. In the climax of After Story, it is revealed the Girl is Ushio's soul in a liminal world created by the town's lights, and the Doll is Tomoya's soul, experiencing their bond outside time. Their escape attempt parallels Tomoya's real-world journey to reconnect with Ushio. Provides metaphysical explanation for the miracle ending; reframes Tomoya's suffering as part of a larger cycle of wishes. Essential to understanding the light orb system.

After Story - Marriage, Birth, Loss, and ReturnClannad After Story visual novel arc; anime Episodes 10-22 (2008-2009)

2005-2009 (in-universe 2004-2009)

After graduation, Tomoya and Nagisa marry young and live in a small apartment. Tomoya works as an electrician; Nagisa works at the bakery while battling recurring illness. Nagisa becomes pregnant despite health risks. During childbirth, Nagisa dies after delivering daughter Ushio, echoing her own mother's difficult birth. Tomoya collapses into depression, leaves five-year-old Ushio in the care of Akio and Sanae, and lives a hollow, workaholic life, estranged from his daughter and drinking. A trip with Ushio to see his grandmother and later a journey together forces him to confront his father's sacrifice and his own abandonment. Ushio then falls ill with the same mysterious illness as Nagisa and dies in Tomoya's arms in the snow. In despair, Tomoya wishes to do over - the accumulated lights grant a miracle, rewinding time to Nagisa's delivery, where both survive. The emotional core of Clannad; establishes Tomoya as one of visual novel history's few protagonists to experience marriage and parenthood, loss, and adult depression. True ending requires prior collection of lights.

Accounts II
The Star That Watches - Kotomi Ichinose RouteClannad VN Kotomi Route; anime Episodes 10-12 (2007)

2004

Tomoya reconnects with Kotomi Ichinose, a genius but socially isolated girl who was his childhood friend before her parents died in a plane crash. He had forgotten her due to trauma. He helps her confront grief, restore her parents' garden, and play the violin piece her parents loved. Kotomi's parents were scientists who left her a suitcase and a teddy bear; Tomoya helps her understand their love was not abandonment. The route ends with Kotomi cutting her hair and choosing to go to college abroad, with Tomoya's encouragement. Grants a light orb; shows Tomoya's capacity to heal childhood wounds. Kotomi appears later in After Story as supportive friend.

The Blade That Protects - Tomoyo Sakagami RouteClannad VN Tomoyo Route; anime Episode 16 + OVA 'Another World: Tomoyo Chapter' (2008); sequel Tomoyo After

2004-2005

Tomoyo Sakagami, a famously strong transfer student who beats up delinquents, wants to become student council president to save the cherry blossom trees lining the town's road. Tomoya, initially her rival, becomes her campaign manager and boyfriend. Their relationship faces opposition from Tomoyo's family responsibilities (caring for brother Takafumi) and the student council's disapproval of their dating. In the VN's good end, Tomoyo achieves her goal and they stay together; in the anime OVA alternate timeline, Tomoya and Tomoyo remain a couple after Nagisa's route is set aside, ending with Tomoyo leaving town for new opportunities. Tomoyo After continues this timeline where they live together as adults raising Takafumi's child Tomo. Demonstrates Tomoya in a more equal, combative partnership; explores themes of ambition vs romance. Tomoyo After is considered one of Key's darkest sequels due to Tomo's trauma.

The Starfish Party - Fuko Ibuki RouteClannad VN Fuko Route; anime Episodes 2-9 (2007)

2004

Tomoya meets Fuko Ibuki, a first-year who carves starfish (hitode) sculptures and is obsessed with inviting people to her sister Kouko's wedding. Fuko is actually in a coma after an accident; the Fuko Tomoya sees is an astral projection sustained by her strong wish. Tomoya and Nagisa help distribute starfish and invite the whole school, allowing Fuko's sister to feel blessed. After the wedding, Fuko disappears from everyone's memory except Tomoya and Nagisa, until she awakens years later and attends Ushio's kindergarten in After Story. First major demonstration of light orb / town magic; establishes supernatural rules. Fuko's reappearance in After Story Episode 19 is a payoff for long-time viewers.

Tomoya is a typical harem protagonist who dates multiple girls. — In the VN, player can pursue multiple routes, but they are alternate timelines, not simultaneous dating (except one bad end). In canon anime, Tomoya is devoted to Nagisa after the common route and does not romantically pursue others. His relationships with other heroines become platonic help.

Clannad is just a sad school romance about a sick girl. — Only the first half is school romance; the second half (After Story) is about marriage, work, parenthood, and grief over 5+ years of adult life. Nagisa's illness is not the only tragedy; Tomoya's growth as husband and father is the core.

Tomoya's shoulder injury was caused by his father beating him severely. — It was a single shove during an argument where Tomoya fell into a window frame. Naoyuki was drunk and negligent, not a habitual violent abuser. The narrative emphasizes mutual estrangement and Naoyuki's depression after his wife's death, not chronic beating.

The ending erases all tragedy and makes everything meaningless. — The miracle is earned only after Tomoya fully experiences loss, abandonment, and reconciliation, and after helping many others (collecting lights). The VN frames it as reward for lived empathy, not cheap reset. The suffering remains meaningful as what taught him to value family.

Tomoya is voiced by the same actor in all versions. — Yuichi Nakamura voices him in the main VN (All Ages), anime, and drama CDs, but the original adult PC release credited Kentaro Ito (alias), and the Toei film uses Kenji Nojima. English dub is David Matranga.
