Who It Is

He laughs while his cannons turn a battlefield to ash, then whimpers for Stellar when the drugs run out - Auel Neider is not Gundam SEED Destiny's most powerful pilot, but its most pitiable weapon: a teenage boy rebuilt by the Earth Alliance into a living gun that needs permission to stay sane.

Attested personality in anime: impulsive, emotionally volatile, childishly cruel, and deeply dependent on his teammates for emotional regulation. He exhibits sadistic glee in combat, taunting opponents, but quickly collapses into panic, whining, and clinginess when denied drugs or when Stellar is threatened. He refers to Stellar as an older-sister figure and is possessive of her. He shows absolute obedience and childlike affection toward Neo Roanoke (Mu La Flaga), calling him 'Neo' and seeking praise. Powers
Extended Human Physiology (Second-Generation Biological CPU)

Artificially enhanced Natural human. Through drugs, surgical conditioning, and memory manipulation at Lodonia, Auel possesses reflexes, spatial awareness, and G-force tolerance exceeding baseline Naturals and approaching Coordinator levels, specifically tuned for mobile suit combat. Effects are temporary and require regular dosing.

Limit: Requires regular administration of performance-maintenance drugs (identified in supplementary materials as 'Gamma Glipheptal' and related compounds). Without dosing, suffers severe withdrawal: tremors, hallucinations, panic attacks, and eventual collapse. Conditioning includes a 'block word' failsafe to prevent psychotic break, but anime never vocalizes Auel's specific keyword (unlike Stellar's trigger). Lifespan is implied to be shortened; mental maturity arrested. Cannot maintain enhancement without logistical support.

Mobile Suit Piloting - Heavy Artillery Specialist

Pilots the GAT-X103AP Verde Buster Gundam, a rebuilt and recolored version of the original Buster Gundam. Specializes in long-range bombardment and mid-range combined fire. Demonstrated feats: destroying multiple ZAFT ZAKUs at Armory One theft, suppressing the Minerva's support fleet, and leveling structures around Lodonia.

Limit: Verde Buster is not equipped with Phase Shift variants for close combat; vulnerable in melee against high-mobility suits like Impulse. Auel's piloting style is reckless and relies on overwhelming firepower rather than tactics; easily lured into close combat where he is outmatched by Shinn Asuka and Kira Yamato. Suit destroyed in Episode 32.

Conditioned Aggression and Pain Suppression

Through conditioning, exhibits reduced fear response and heightened aggression in combat, allowing continued fighting despite injuries that would incapacitate normal pilots. Shown laughing while under fire and continuing to fire after his suit is damaged.

Limit: Aggression is uncontrolled; he breaks formation to chase targets, disobeys Neo's orders when enraged, and becomes incapacitated by emotional shock (Stellar's death). Pain suppression fails during drug withdrawal, leaving him helpless.

Accounts I
Theft of the Second Stage Gundams - Armory OneMobile Suit Gundam SEED Destiny Episodes 6-7 'The End of a Past' / 'The Land of Confusion'

CE 73, October - In-universe date: Armory One incident

Under orders from Phantom Pain commander Neo Roanoke, Auel infiltrates the ZAFT colony Armory One alongside Sting Oakley and Stellar Loussier. While ZAFT's new Second Stage Series Gundams are being unveiled, the trio hijacks three units: Sting takes Chaos (ZGMF-X24S), Stellar takes Gaia (ZGMF-X88S), and Auel seizes Verde Buster (GAT-X103AP). Auel uses Verde Buster's combined beam cannons to blast through hangar defenses and cover their escape, killing several ZAFT guards and technicians. The theft forces ZAFT to deploy Shinn Asuka in Impulse to pursue them. Establishes Phantom Pain as antagonists and provides Earth Alliance with advanced mobile suits. Triggers the deployment of the Minerva and the prolonged pursuit arc. First clear demonstration that Auel is an Extended, not a regular pilot.

Pursuit of the Minerva and Battles at Sea - Dardanelles and CreteMobile Suit Gundam SEED Destiny Episodes 11-13, 21

CE 73, November-December

Aboard the Girty Lue, Auel repeatedly engages the ZAFT battleship Minerva and later the Archangel. At the Dardanelles Strait and later near Crete, Auel in Verde Buster provides heavy fire support, attempting to destroy the Minerva's support ships and engaging Shinn's Impulse and Kira's Freedom. He shows particular cruelty, firing on disabled suits and laughing. Neo repeatedly has to recall him when he overextends. During one sortie, Auel is forced to retreat after his drug timer expires and he begins to shake, requiring Sting to drag him back. Demonstrates Phantom Pain's hit-and-run strategy and Auel's dependence on teammates. Builds rivalry with Shinn, who will later kill him. Shows Extended weakness in prolonged campaigns.

Reunion at Lodonia - Discovery of the Extended LaboratoryMobile Suit Gundam SEED Destiny Episodes 30-31 'The Cost of a Soul' / 'The Light of a Star'

CE 73, late December

After Stellar is captured by ZAFT and later returned to Phantom Pain, Auel and Sting return to the Lodonia facility to resupply drugs. ZAFT forces led by Shinn and Rey Za Burrel assault the lab and discover its horrors: rows of children being conditioned, drug vats, and records of failed Extended subjects. Auel is enraged to see his 'home' violated and attacks Shinn, screaming that ZAFT has no right to judge them. The battle reveals to ZAFT personnel (and the audience) the full extent of Earth Alliance human experimentation. Auel's dialogue confirms he has no memory of parents and considers Lodonia his birthplace. Provides canonical exposition for Extended program's dark history. Turns audience sympathy partially toward Auel while cementing Earth Alliance/LOGOS as war criminals. Sets up final confrontation.

Accounts II
Battle of Berlin - Loss of StellarMobile Suit Gundam SEED Destiny Episodes 32 'Stellar' (first half)

CE 73, Berlin, Germany

Phantom Pain deploys the massive mobile armor GFAS-X1 Destroy Gundam piloted by the mentally unstable Stellar. Auel and Sting are ordered to support her in Verde Buster and Chaos. When Kira Yamato in Freedom disables Destroy without initially killing Stellar, Auel rushes to protect her, begging Neo over radio to let them retreat. Stellar, disoriented and without her block word, dies when Shinn in Impulse destroys Destroy's cockpit (or when Kira's attack leads to crash, depending on edit). Auel witnesses her death from nearby and suffers a complete psychological break, screaming her name repeatedly as his drug withdrawal begins. Stellar's death removes Auel's primary emotional anchor and triggers his final berserk attack. Marks the moral turning point where both ZAFT and Archangel crews recognize Extended as victims.

Death in Combat - Auel vs Shinn AsukaMobile Suit Gundam SEED Destiny Episode 32 'Stellar' (second half)

CE 73, immediately after Berlin battle, outskirts of Lodonia/Berlin region

Enraged and in withdrawal, Auel ignores Neo's retreat order and charges Shinn's Impulse in Verde Buster, vowing to avenge Stellar. He fires wildly, destroying terrain but failing to hit the more agile Impulse. Shinn, also emotionally charged, closes distance, evades the combined beam cannon, and impales Verde Buster's cockpit with Impulse's beam saber/Excalibur. Auel is killed instantly; Verde Buster explodes. Sting, watching from Chaos, calls out but does not intervene in time and retreats. Neo later notes Auel's death with detached regret, highlighting Phantom Pain's expendability doctrine. Removes one of three Extended antagonists; leaves Sting as sole survivor of the trio until his own death later. Demonstrates that Shinn, despite being protagonist, is willing to kill a mentally compromised child soldier, complicating his hero status. Auel's death is not commemorated by Earth Alliance; his body is not recovered on screen.

Posthumous Legacy - Extended Program AftermathMobile Suit Gundam SEED Destiny Episodes 33-50, Gundam SEED Destiny Final Plus, and supplementary books

CE 73-74, retrospective

After Auel's death, his existence is referenced in ZAFT debriefings and by Gilbert Durandal as evidence of LOGOS's inhumanity. The data recovered at Lodonia, including Auel's conditioning records, is used by Durandal to justify his Destiny Plan. In later episodes, Sting mentions Auel when confronting Neo, saying 'Auel and Stellar are dead because of you.' No resurrection or clone of Auel appears; unlike Mu La Flaga/Neo and Rey Za Burrel, Auel is not a clone and is not revisited. His Verde Buster is not rebuilt in-series, though its design is reused in games and model kits as a playable unit. Cements Auel as a cautionary example of weaponized children. Informs Durandal's political narrative and Shinn's later guilt. In meta terms, Auel remains a minor but memorable antagonist whose death underscores Destiny's theme of exploited youth.

Auel Neider is a Coordinator like Kira and Athrun — Auel is a Natural who was artificially enhanced into an Extended. Coordinators are genetically engineered at birth; Extended are Naturals modified after birth through drugs and conditioning. He has no Coordinator genes and is explicitly on the Earth Alliance (Natural) side.

Auel pilots the Chaos or Gaia Gundam — Auel exclusively pilots the GAT-X103AP Verde Buster (green heavy artillery unit). Sting pilots Chaos (blue) and Stellar pilots Gaia (pink/red). The trio's suits are color-coded and never swapped in canon.

Auel survives Destiny and appears in Gundam SEED Freedom — Auel dies definitively in Episode 32 and does not appear in the 2024 film Gundam SEED Freedom, which is set in CE 75. No clone or flashback cameo of Auel is in Freedom; only Sting's and Stellar's archetypes are referenced via new Black Knight Squad members.

The Extended are adults who volunteered for enhancement — Extended are children abducted or acquired and conditioned from childhood with no consent, subjected to memory manipulation and drug dependency. Auel has no memory of family and considers Lodonia his home, indicating institutionalization from early age.
