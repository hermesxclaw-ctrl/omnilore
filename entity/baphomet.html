Who It Is

No ancient temple ever raised an altar to it, no priest ever censed an image of it in worship, and no archaeological dig has ever turned up an idol that answers to its name — yet Baphomet is arguably the most famous 'god' nobody actually worshipped. It begins not as a deity but as a word: in July 1098 a Crusader wrote home to translate the enemy's prophet as 'Bafomet', and centuries later, in 1307, that same name was dragged into a torture chamber and made the pretext for the destruction of the Knights Templar. It was only in 1856, in a Paris engraving drawn by an ex-seminarist haunted by his own failed priesthood, that Baphomet finally got a body — a winged, androgynous, goat-headed figure of impossible balance, half male and half female, one hand raised to mercy and the other to justice, a flame of intelligence burning between its horns. As a modern occult emblem, Baphomet is not a behaving creature but a pedagogic symbol of equilibrium, alchemical totality, and the reconciliation of opposites. In Lévi's own theory it is 'the absolute in symbolic form': neither good nor evil but the sign of the universal balance, the flame of intelligence tied to matter yet above it. Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name's origin is disputed. The dominant scholarly position (Oxford English Dictionary; modern medievalists) holds that Baphomet descends from a probable Old French corruption of 'Mahomet' (Muhammad), reflecting Latin Christian medieval belief that Muslims were idolaters who worshipped Muhammad a

There is no attested ancient pronunciation because the figure as such is a medieval and modern literary/cult construction rather than a continuously worshipped deity.

Powers
Synthesis of opposites / perfect equilibrium

Lévi's core claim: Baphomet is 'the absolute in symbolic form', the power to reconcile binary opposites — mercy and justice (white and black moons of Chesed/Geburah), male and female (the two hermaphrodite arms), spirit and matter (the torch-fillet 'flame of intelligence' above the animal head), good and evil. It functions as an emblem of the alchemical Summa and of occult totality rather than an operational supernatural power.

Limit: Symbolic, pedagogical; it has no independent volition in Lévi's system.

Light and illumination (the 'flame of intelligence')

The torch between the horns symbolizes enlightenment rising above brute matter—the soul 'elevated above matter' while still attached to it. In the occult-natural scheme it is linked to Lévi's 'Astral Light', a Mesmer-derived magnetic/spiritual fluid that skilled magicians could manipulate. The figure thereby represents access to hidden knowledge and magic.

Limit: For Lévi, the Astral Light was a natural force of the magus's imagination and will, not the devil's gift.

Union of spirit and matter; alchemical transmutation

The image's layered anatomy (scales=water, semicircle=atmosphere, feathers=volatile, phallic caduceus=eternal life/generation) encodes an alchemical chain of elements. Baphomet thus represents the philosopher's-work of transformation and the generative/magical 'child' — for Crowley the 'hieroglyph of arcane perfection' symbolizing the spiritual nature of the spermatozoon and the union of Chaos and Babalon in sex-magic.

Limit: Ritual/initiatory symbolism; the O.T.O. secret instruction is reserved to higher degrees.

The pentagram/magical signature (Sigil of Baphomet)

The inverted pentagram with a goat's head and Hebrew letters (Leviathan, plus originally Samael and Lilith) is the modern operational sigil of materiality and of the Church of Satan. The inversion encodes Lévi's invented doctrine that one-point-down = evil/materiality, one-point-up = good/spirit-ruling-matter.

Limit: Iconographic and organizational, not a functional magical power.

Fear and intimidation (the 'phantom of all terrors' register)

In Western demonology, folk-horror, and modern pop-culture fiction, Baphomet is frequently vested with the classic powers of a horned demon-lord: sovereignty over a cult or underworld hierarchy, the ability to receive worship, inspiring terror, and (in grimoire-derived media) command over legions. This is an ascribed, narrative power-set with no primary historical attestation as actual cult.

Limit: Confined to fiction and folklore; demonological registers are inconsistent (Baphomet is variously a name for the Devil, an independent demon, or a symbolic glyph).

Accounts I
The Tempest that never was: the Templar idols (1307-1314)Trials of the Knights Templar; arrest order of Philip IV, 13 October 1307; Inquisition records Paris 1307 / Poitiers 1308 / papal commissions 1310

1307-1314

King Philip IV of France, deeply in debt to the Templars and locked in a struggle with the papacy, ordered the simultaneous arrest of all Templars in France at dawn on Friday 13 October 1307. Under torture, dozens of knights confessed to renouncing Christ, spitting and urinating on the cross, exchanging 'obscene kisses' (on the spine, navel, and mouth), and worshipping an idol variously described as a cat, a long-bearded head, or a three-faced head — the 'Baphomet', which one Florentine Templar reportedly told initiates to adore as 'your god and your Mahomet'. No such idol was ever produced. Pope Clement V, pressured by Philip, dissolved the order in 1312 (Vox in excelso), and on 18 March 1314 Grand Master Jacques de Molay and Geoffroi de Charney, recanting their coerced confessions, were burned as relapsed heretics in Paris. The reconstructed Gnostic idol: Hammer-Purgstall and the faked Baphomets (1818)Joseph Freiherr von Hammer-Purgstall, Mysterium Baphometis revelatum (1818)

1818 (setting the 19th-century Templar-as-Gnostic narrative)

The Viennese Orientalist von Hammer-Purgstall argued that the Templars were secret Ophite/Gnostic serpent-worshippers and that Baphomet signified the 'Baptism of Metis' or Sophia Achamoth, a hermaphrodite wisdom-figure. He relied on 'Baphomet' statuettes (some clearly faked or pseudo-Egyptian curiosities) then held in Weimar, Vienna, and cabinets of antiquities. Contemporaries (F. J. M. Raynouard immediately rebutted it in the Journal des savants; Charles William King; later Peter Partner) demonstrated the objects were unfounded forgeries from 'Rosicrucian or alchemical quacks' and never tied to the Templars. Yet the essay gave lasting scholarly patronage to the idea of an esoteric Templar Baphomet. Installed a durable pseudohistorical link between the Templars, Gnosticism, and the Baphomet head-idol that occultists then built upon.

Éliphas Lévi authors the modern Baphomet: the Sabbatic Goat (1856)Éliphas Lévi (born Alphonse Louis Constant), Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie, frontispiece and caption

1854-1856

Lévi, a former Saint-Sulpice seminarist turned ceremonial magician and (per modern historian Julian Strube) lifelong romantic-socialist, published his own drawing of a winged androgynous goat-headed god called 'Baphomet'/'The Sabbatic Goat'. His caption decoded every element — the point-up pentagram (light), the two hands making the sign of occultism above and below invoking mercy and justice, the male and female arms of Khunrath's androgyne, the torch of intelligence between the horns, scales/water, semicircle/atmosphere, feathers/volatility, the rod of eternal life in place of genitals, and humanity's two breasts. Lévi insisted the Templar masters worshipped this figure not as the devil but as the god Pan of the Alexandrian and Gnostic schools; the image, he claimed, was the frontispiece of the 'ritual' reproducing 'the exact figure of the terrible emperor of night'. This single engraving created Baphomet as the world knows it. Every subsequent Baphomet — the Tarot Devil, the Sigil, the Satanic Temple statue — descends from Lévi's image.

Accounts II
Stanislas de Guaita and the inverted pentagram: the Sigil (1897)Stanislas de Guaita, La Clef de la Magie Noire (1897)

1897

De Guaita published the first goat's head set inside a downward-pointing pentagram, with five Hebrew letters at the points spelling Leviathan, and the names Samael and Lilith. Building on Lévi's doctrine of inversion (matter ruling over spirit = 'evil'; spirit over matter = 'good'), this became the formal sigil later adopted by Anton LaVey, who copied it from Maurice Bessy's A Pictorial History of Magic and the Supernatural after removing the Samael/Lilith text, renaming it the 'Sigil of Baphomet' (named in The Satanic Rituals, 1972). Created the iconic modern logo of Satanism — the goat-headed star — now used by the Church of Satan, The Satanic Temple, Joy of Satan, and countless subcultures.

The Taxil hoax and the 'Luciferian doctrine' (1891-1897)Léo Taxil (Marie Joseph Gabriel Antoine Jogand-Pagès) with 'Dr. Bataille' and 'Dr. Karl Hacks', Le Diable au XIXe siècle (1892); Waite, Devil Worship in France (1896)

1891-1897

The excommunicated free-thinking hoaxer Léo Taxil pretended to convert to Catholicism and published lurid 'exposés' claiming Freemasons were Satanic Palladists under demons' orders, inventing the high-priestess 'Diana Vaughan'. In 1897 he called a press conference and confessed the whole affair was a joke meant to mock both Freemasonry and the Catholic Church's gullibility. The famous 'Luciferian doctrine' quote — 'Lucifer is God... Adonay is also god' — is not in Taxil's own writings but was published by Abel Clarin de la Rive, sourced to Taxil's fictional creation Vaughan. It nevertheless circulated for decades as a genuine Freemasonic confession, and Christian evangelist Jack T. Chick recycled the Taxil material claiming Freemasons worship Baphomet. Codified the modern conspiracy link between Freemasonry, Baphomet-worship, and Satanism, and supplied a 'Luciferian' text still quoted today to allege Masonic devil-worship.

Baphomet as civic symbol: the Satanic Temple statue at the state capitols (2014-2024)Statue of Baphomet; The Satanic Temple (founded 2013, co-headed by Lucien Greaves/Doug Mesner)

2014-2024

In 2012 Oklahoma Rep. Mike Ritze privately placed a Ten Commandments monument at the state capitol. The Satanic Temple (which does not believe in a literal Satan and casts Baphomet as 'a literary figure, not a deity — he stands for rationality, skepticism', per Time) crowdfunded an 8.5 ft, 3,000-lb bronze Baphomet statue by Mark Porter, unveiling it in Detroit on 25 July 2015. The Oklahoma Supreme Court ordered the Ten Commandments monument removed as an unconstitutional religious display, so the TST statue never stood there; it was later displayed in Arkansas (2018) and Iowa, where Iowa the display was destroyed by a Christian candidate, Michael Cassidy, in December 2023 — triggering a hate-crime charge and a $75,000+ legal-defense crowdfund. A likeness of the statue also appears in Netflix's Chilling Adventures of Sabrina, which the Satanic Temple sued over (settled 2018). Transformed Baphomet from an occult glyph into a public emblem in the American church-state and religious-pluralism debate, and a flashpoint for Christian-nationalist vandalism.

Baphomet is an ancient god worshipped continuously for millennia. — Baphomet is unknown to any primary ancient source as a deity. The name first appears only in the 11th-14th century as a corruption of 'Mahomet' and as a Templar-trial idol-accusation; the goat-headed image is entirely a 19th-century creation of Éliphas Lévi.

Baphomet is 'the Goat of Mendes', an ancient Egyptian god of fertility. — Mendes' chief deity was the ram-headed Banebdjedet (a Ba of Osiris), not a goat-headed Baphomet. The equation is a 19th-century occultist conflation (via Lévi and Herodotus) with no Egyptological support.

The Freemasons secretly worship Baphomet / Lucifer. — No Masonic body venerates Baphomet. The claim originates in the 1890s Taxil hoax, which Taxil himself confessed as a joke; the 'Luciferian doctrine' text was fabricated and circulated as fact.

The inverted pentagram symbol has always meant 'evil' / Baphomet. — The specific meaning of pentagram inversion ('matter ruling over spirit' vs 'spirit over matter') is an 1890s doctrine invented by occultists, popularized by LaVey's Church of Satan. Historically the pentagram is a many-valued symbol (protection, magic, Pythagorean) whose meaning depends entirely on context.

Baphomet = Satan = the Christian Devil as a real being. — Lévi explicitly wrote Baphomet was not the devil but 'the god Pan' of the Gnostic schools; the nontheistic Church of Satan and the Satanic Temple explicitly do not believe in or worship it as a being. The equation with the biblical Devil is a polemical/conflationist reading.
