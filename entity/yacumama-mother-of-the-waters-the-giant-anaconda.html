Who It Is

Yacumama's enigmatic presence has captivated the imagination of people for centuries. What secrets lie beneath the surface of this mysterious entity, and how can we better understand its role in the Amazonian ecosystem?

Yacumama is considered a protective spirit, watching over the Amazon River and its inhabitants. It is said to be a fierce guardian, defending its domain against any threat. However, some accounts suggest that Yacumama can be benevolent, granting wisdom and fertility to those who treat it with respect.

Powers
Water Control

Yacumama is said to have the power to control the Amazon River's flow, creating powerful whirlpools or stilling the waters at will. It can also summon fish and other aquatic creatures to do its bidding.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Yacumama is described as a massive serpent, exceeding 30 meters in length, with a head reaching 2 meters in width. Its body is said to be as thick as a tree trunk, and its scales are as black as coal. According to some accounts, Yacumama's eyes glow like embers, while others describe them as piercing green.

Symbols — Yacumama is often depicted with a crown of water lilies or a necklace made of fish bones. Its presence is said to be marked by the sound of rushing water and the scent of blooming flowers.

Domains — Yacumama is said to inhabit the Amazon Rainforest, specifically the Amazon River and its tributaries. Its domain is believed to extend from the Andes Mountains to the Atlantic Ocean.

Accounts I
The Creation of YacumamaQuechua mythology

Pre-Columbian

According to Quechua mythology, Yacumama was created by the sun god, Inti, to protect the Amazon River and its inhabitants. Inti tasked Yacumama with watching over the river's flow and ensuring the fertility of the surrounding lands. The creation of Yacumama led to the establishment of a balance between the natural and spiritual worlds, ensuring the well-being of the Amazonian ecosystem.

Accounts II
Yacumama and the Lost CityAymara oral tradition

Colonial

According to Aymara oral tradition, Yacumama was said to have discovered a lost city deep within the Amazon Rainforest. The city was believed to hold ancient knowledge and treasures, which Yacumama guarded fiercely, allowing only a select few to access its secrets. The discovery of the lost city by Yacumama led to a significant increase in the Amazonian ecosystem's biodiversity, as the city's knowledge and treasures were shared with the surrounding communities.
