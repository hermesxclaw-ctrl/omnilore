Who It Is

Yacumama ('Yaku' = water, 'Mama' = mother) is the spirit of the aquatic realm in Amazonian cosmology — a giant anaconda (or primordial water-serpent) who embodies the power and life-force of the rivers, tributaries, lakes, and flooded forests of the Amazon basin. Depending on the ethnic tradition (found among the Shipibo-Conibo, Ashaninka, Matses, and many others), Yacumama is either a specific deity-spirit or a category of water-snake spirits. She is described as a colossal anaconda — far larger than any ordinary snake — whose body can span the width of a river or extend for the length of a tributary. Her movement creates the river's currents; her breathing produces the waves and whirlpools. When she surfaces, she is indistinguishable from a fallen tree or a submerged log — and she may capsize canoes or drag disrespectful people to her underwater realm. Yacumama is not inherently malevolent, but she is implacably protective of the waters and their life. Fishermen and river-travelers must petition her for safe passage, leaving offerings of tobacco, manioc, or beads at river mouths and confluences.
