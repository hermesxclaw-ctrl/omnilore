Who It Is

Nzambi a Mpungu (also Nzambi or Nzambi Mpungu) is the supreme creator deity of the Kongo people — the being who created the universe and then withdrew, leaving the management of earthly affairs to lesser spirits and ancestors. The name 'Nzambi a Mpungu' translates roughly to 'The High God Who Is Above All' or 'Almighty God.' When Portuguese missionaries arrived in the Kongo Kingdom in the late 15th century, they found a well-established belief in a supreme being and quickly seized upon 'Nzambi' as the translation for the Christian God. The Kongo king Nkuwu (later João I) was baptized in 1491, and the syncretism between Nzambi and the Christian God has persisted for over five centuries. However, indigenous Kongo theology maintains key differences: Nzambi is not continuously involved in human affairs; he is approached through the bisimbi (nature spirits) and the ancestors (bakulu). He is associated with the land of the dead (Kalunga), which is also the name of the boundary line between the living and the dead — analogous to a great river. Nzambi is neither male nor female in some traditions and is understood as a force rather than a personality. His name survives in diaspora religions: 'Zombi' in Haiti (the serpent deity), 'Nzambi' in Brazilian Quimbanda, and as the etymological root of the word 'zombie' — a profound distortion of an originally sacred concept.
