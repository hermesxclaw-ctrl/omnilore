The Approach

In the shadow of the great Earth Mother Tellus, there stands a figure barely whispered in the annals of Roman religion: Tellumo, the male counterpart of the Earth goddess. While Tellus was venerated in grand temples and public festivals, Tellumo lingers as a ghost in the margins of ancient texts, mentioned only by a handful of authors. Who was this obscure deity, and why did the Romans, who so often paired male and female forces, leave him so scantily recorded?

Content advisory. No dark material identified. This subject is extremely obscure; the dossier reflects the limitations of surviving evidence.
Identity

Tellumo

Pronunciation tel-LOO-mo (Classical Latin: /telˈluː.moː/)

Known As

TellurusTellumo (variant spelling)Tellurus (variant)

Etymology

The name Tellumo is derived from Tellus, the Roman earth goddess, with the masculine suffix -mo, parallel to other divine pairs such as Faunus/Fauna or Liber/Libera. The root is likely connected to the Proto-Indo-European *telh₂- meaning 'to bear, support', as suggested by Michiel de Vaan.

Cultural Ownership

Roman religion, specifically the Republican and Imperial periods. No evidence of worship outside Roman context.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have conflated Tellumo with Tellurus, but they may be distinct, albeit equally obscure, figures. Augustine of Hippo mentions Tellumo, while Martianus Capella names Tellurus. The relationship between the two is uncertain.

Description

Appearance

No ancient descriptions of Tellumo's appearance survive. Given the pattern of male counterparts, he may have been depicted as a male figure associated with the earth, perhaps holding a cornucopia or agricultural implements, but this is purely speculative.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols are attested. By analogy with Tellus, he might have been associated with the cornucopia, but there is no evidence.

Behaviour & Personality

No information survives. As a personification of the male aspect of the earth, he might have been conceived as a fertilizing or supporting force, but this is conjecture.

Domains & Habitats

The earth itself, but no specific cult sites or temples are recorded.

Version Differences

The only difference is the name: Tellumo vs Tellurus. Augustine uses Tellumo; Capella uses Tellurus. Whether they are the same deity is unclear.
