The Approach

In the shadows of Asian forests, a spider with a hero's name weaves its web—not for the gods, but for moths drawn to tree trunks. Spartaeus, named after a mythical prince, is a real arachnid whose peculiar gait and silken sheets challenge our perceptions of jumping spiders.

Identity

Spartaeus

Pronunciation SPAR-tay-us

Known As

Boethus (preoccupied name)

Etymology

Named after Spartaeus, son of the Rhodian nymph Himalia and Zeus in Greek mythology. The name was chosen to replace the preoccupied Boethus.

Cultural Ownership

Scientific taxonomy; not tied to a single culture, but the name derives from Greek mythology.

Disputed Identifications

The genus was originally described as Boethus, but that name was preoccupied, leading to the renaming in 1984.

Description

Appearance

Spartaeus are jumping spiders (family Salticidae) with typical salticid body structure: eight eyes, robust legs, and relatively large size for the family. They are often cryptically colored to match tree bark. Detailed morphological descriptions vary by species.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography; scientific illustrations and photographs serve as visual records.

Behaviour & Personality

They build large sheet webs on tree trunks to capture prey, mainly moths. They exhibit an unusual walking behavior, waving their palps and legs in a distinctive manner.

Domains & Habitats

Found in Asia, from Sri Lanka to Indonesia, including Japan, China, Laos, Vietnam, Taiwan, Philippines, Malaysia, and India. They inhabit forested areas, often on tree trunks.

Version Differences

No significant version differences; species-level variations exist in morphology and distribution.
