Who It Is

In the storm-dark heart of Shinto's oldest myths, Susanoo-no-Mikoto rages as the unruly god of sea and storm—a divine force of chaos whose wildness both devastates and renews. His tales are not tidy morality plays; they are violent, contradictory, and deeply human, showing a god who slays a monstrous serpent to save a maiden, yet also wreaks havoc in heaven and is cast out in disgrace. He is the tempest that destroys and the rain that brings life, a figure whose legacy ripples through Japanese culture from ancient chronicles to modern anime.

Susanoo is depicted as violent, impetuous, and unruly, but also heroic and protective. He is known for his destructive tantrums, which led to his banishment from heaven, but also for his courageous deeds, such as slaying Yamata-no-Orochi and saving Kushinada-hime. He is a complex figure, embodying both chaos and redemption. His behavior is often driven by grief, jealousy, or a desire for attention, as seen in his reaction to his mother's death and his rivalry with his sister Amaterasu.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Susanoo is of uncertain etymology. It may derive from 'susa' (violent, impetuous) or refer to a place called Susa in Izumo. The suffix '-no-o' or '-no-mikoto' is an honorific. Some scholars connect it to the verb 'susamu' (to be violent). The meaning is often interpreted as 'impetuous male'

Pronunciation — soo-sah-noh-oh noh mee-koh-toh (Japanese: スサノオノミコト)

Aliases — Takehaya Susanoo-no-Mikoto, Haya-susanoo-no-mikoto, Kami-susanoo-no-mikoto, Susanoo, Susanowo, Susa-no-o

Ownership — Susanoo-no-Mikoto is a major deity in Shinto, the indigenous religion of Japan. He is venerated at numerous shrines, most notably Izumo Taisha, and is a central figure in Japanese

Powers
Storm and Sea Control

Susanoo is the god of storms and the sea. He can summon winds, rain, and tempests, and is believed to control the ocean and its tides. This power is often seen as destructive, but also as necessary for agriculture, as storms bring rain.

Limit: His storms are often unpredictable and can cause devastation. In some myths, he is not fully in control of his powers, and his emotions can trigger storms.

Divine Strength and Combat

Susanoo is a formidable warrior, capable of slaying monsters and gods. He famously defeated the eight-headed serpent Yamata-no-Orochi using a clever plan and a magical sword.

Limit: His strength is not always enough on its own; he often relies on strategy and divine tools, such as the sword and the sake.

Sword of Kusanagi

Susanoo discovered the legendary sword Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi (Grass-Cutting Sword) in the tail of Yamata-no-Orochi. This sword became a symbol of imperial authority and is one of the Three Sacred Treasures of Japan.

Limit: The sword is not his primary weapon in most myths; he gives it to Amaterasu as a peace offering. Its powers are more prominently associated with later heroes, such as Yamato Takeru.

Transformation and Trickery

Susanoo is capable of transforming himself, as seen when he changed into a 'poor man' or a 'wandering deity' to approach the old man and his wife before slaying the serpent. He also uses trickery, such as getting the serpent drunk on sake.

Limit: His transformations are not extensively described, and he does not use trickery in other major myths.

Control over the Underworld

In some traditions, Susanoo is associated with the underworld (Yomi) or the realm of the dead. He is said to have visited Yomi and to have power over spirits and demons.

Limit: This aspect is not prominent in the Kojiki, and it is unclear if he rules the underworld or merely visits it.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Susanoo is described as a powerful, bearded male deity, often depicted with wild hair and a fierce expression. He is sometimes shown holding a sword or a staff. In later art, he is often depicted as a warrior or a storm god, with flowing robes and a stern countenance. In the Yamata-no-Orochi episode, he is described as having a 'fierce and terrible' appearance.

Symbols — Susanoo's symbols include the sword (especially the Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi, which he discovered in the tail of Yamata-no-Orochi), the storm, the sea, and the serpent (specifically Yamata-no-Orochi). He is also associated with the moon (in some traditions) and with agriculture, as he is credited with clearing the land of Izumo. In art, he is often shown with a sword and a serpent, or riding a dragon.

Domains — Susanoo is primarily associated with the sea and storms, but also with the land of Izumo (modern Shimane Prefecture), where he descended after his banishment. He is also connected to the underworld (Yomi) in some myths, as he is said to have visited it. His primary shrine is Izumo Taisha, and he is venerated throughout Japan.

Accounts I
Birth of SusanooKojiki; Nihon Shoki (multiple variants)

Mythic, Age of the Gods

After Izanagi escaped from the underworld, he bathed to purify himself. As he washed his nose, Susanoo was born. In some variants, he was born from a white copper mirror or from Izanagi's left eye. Susanoo was given dominion over the sea by his father, but he refused his role and wept, causing calamities. Izanagi, angered by Susanoo's defiance, banished him from the heavenly realm. Susanoo's subsequent actions led to further conflicts with his sister Amaterasu.

The Heavenly Tantrum and BanishmentKojiki; Nihon Shoki

Mythic, Age of the Gods

Before his exile, Susanoo went to the heavenly realm to see his sister Amaterasu. He proposed a test of divine power by creating new deities, but he became violent, destroying rice fields, defecating in the palace, and flaying the heavenly horse. He also killed the food goddess Ōgetsu-hime. The gods, horrified, fined him and expelled him from heaven. Susanoo was banished from heaven, and Amaterasu hid in a cave, plunging the world into darkness. Susanoo descended to Izumo.

Accounts II
The Slaying of Yamata-no-OrochiKojiki; Nihon Shoki

Mythic, Age of the Gods

After his exile, Susanoo descended to the land of Izumo. He encountered an old man and his wife weeping because their daughter, Kushinada-hime, was to be devoured by the eight-headed serpent Yamata-no-Orochi. Susanoo agreed to save her in exchange for her hand in marriage. He turned her into a comb and placed her in his hair. He set out eight jars of sake, and the serpent drank them, becoming intoxicated. Susanoo then slew the serpent, and in its tail found the sword Kusanagi. Susanoo married Kushinada-hime and built a palace in Izumo. He gave the sword Kusanagi to Amaterasu as a peace offering. He also became the ancestor of the Izumo clan.

Susanoo and ŌkuninushiKojiki; Nihon Shoki

Mythic, Age of the Gods

Susanoo's role as a father or foster father to Ōkuninushi (also known as Ōnamuchi) is significant. In the Kojiki, Ōkuninushi is a descendant of Susanoo, and he undergoes trials set by Susanoo to win the hand of Susanoo's daughter, Suseri-hime. In some versions, Susanoo helps Ōkuninushi to create the land of Izumo. Ōkuninushi became the ruler of Izumo and later the god of the land, and his descendants were the Izumo clan. Susanoo's relationship with Ōkuninushi is complex, involving both conflict and cooperation.

Susanoo and the Food GoddessKojiki; Nihon Shoki

Mythic, Age of the Gods

In a tale explaining the origin of food, Susanoo requested food from the goddess Ōgetsu-hime. She produced food from her nose, mouth, and rectum. Susanoo, disgusted, killed her. From her corpse, various crops and silkworms were born. This story explains the origin of grains and other foodstuffs. It also shows Susanoo's violent and impulsive nature.

Susanoo is purely an evil or destructive god. — Susanoo is a complex deity who is both destructive and heroic. He is responsible for chaos but also for saving Kushinada-hime and defeating Yamata-no-Orochi. He is a protector as well as a troublemaker.

Susanoo is the god of the underworld. — In the primary myths, the underworld (Yomi) is ruled by Izanami. Susanoo's association with the underworld is a later interpretation, not found in the Kojiki or Nihon Shoki.

Susanoo and Tsukuyomi are the same deity. — Susanoo and Tsukuyomi are distinct deities. Tsukuyomi is the moon god, while Susanoo is the storm/sea god. They are siblings, but not the same.

Susanoo is a demon or monster. — Susanoo is a kami (deity) in Shinto, not a demon. He is venerated at shrines and is an important mythological figure.
