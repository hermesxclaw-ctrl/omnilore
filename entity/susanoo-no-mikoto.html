Etymology

The name Susanoo is of uncertain etymology. It may derive from 'susa' (violent, impetuous) or refer to a place called Susa in Izumo. The suffix '-no-o' or '-no-mikoto' is an honorific. Some scholars connect it to the verb 'susamu' (to be violent). The meaning is often interpreted as 'impetuous male' or 'violent god'.

Cultural Ownership

Susanoo-no-Mikoto is a major deity in Shinto, the indigenous religion of Japan. He is venerated at numerous shrines, most notably Izumo Taisha, and is a central figure in Japanese mythology as recorded in the Kojiki (712 CE) and Nihon Shoki (720 CE).

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have linked Susanoo to other deities or figures, such as the Korean god or hero, or to the wind god. There is also debate about his original domain: he is often called the god of the sea and storms, but some myths suggest he was originally a chthonic or agricultural deity. His identification with the deity of the underworld (Yomi) in later texts is also debated. These are scholarly interpretations, not canonical facts.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the storm-dark heart of Shinto's oldest myths, Susanoo-no-Mikoto rages as the unruly god of sea and storm—a divine force of chaos whose wildness both devastates and renews. His tales are not tidy morality plays; they are violent, contradictory, and deeply human, showing a god who slays a monstrous serpent to save a maiden, yet also wreaks havoc in heaven and is cast out in disgrace. He is the tempest that destroys and the rain that brings life, a figure whose legacy ripples through Japanese culture from ancient chronicles to modern anime.

Susanoo-no-Mikoto is the Shinto kami of storms, seas, and the mortal world — the tempestuous brother of Amaterasu. He is born from Izanagi's nose when he purified himself after returning from Yomi (the underworld).

Who It Is

Susanoo-no-Mikoto is the Shinto kami of storms, seas, and the mortal world — the tempestuous brother of Amaterasu. He is born from Izanagi's nose when he purified himself after returning from Yomi (the underworld). Susanoo is a chaotic, violent, but ultimately heroic figure in Japanese mythology. His most destructive act was his rampage through Takamagahara, which caused Amaterasu to hide in the heavenly cave and plunge the world into darkness. For his crimes, he was banished from heaven by the council of kami. He descended to Izumo province on earth, where he performed his greatest deed: slaying the eight-headed, eight-tailed serpent Yamata-no-Orochi by getting it drunk on sake, then chopping off its heads and tails. From the serpent's tail, he found the sword Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi (Grass-Cutter), which he gave to Amaterasu as a peace offering. This sword became one of the three Imperial Regalia of Japan. Susanoo then married Kushinada-hime and became the ruler of Izumo. He is the ancestor of the Izumo clan and the father of Okuninushi. Susanoo represents the wild, creative-destructive power of nature — storms that bring both destruction and life-giving rain.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

In the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Susanoo is described as a powerful, bearded male deity, often depicted with wild hair and a fierce expression. He is sometimes shown holding a sword or a staff. In later art, he is often depicted as a warrior or a storm god, with flowing robes and a stern countenance. In the Yamata-no-Orochi episode, he is described as having a 'fierce and terrible' appearance.

Symbols & Iconography

Susanoo's symbols include the sword (especially the Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi, which he discovered in the tail of Yamata-no-Orochi), the storm, the sea, and the serpent (specifically Yamata-no-Orochi). He is also associated with the moon (in some traditions) and with agriculture, as he is credited with clearing the land of Izumo. In art, he is often shown with a sword and a serpent, or riding a dragon.

Behaviour & Personality

Susanoo is depicted as violent, impetuous, and unruly, but also heroic and protective. He is known for his destructive tantrums, which led to his banishment from heaven, but also for his courageous deeds, such as slaying Yamata-no-Orochi and saving Kushinada-hime. He is a complex figure, embodying both chaos and redemption. His behavior is often driven by grief, jealousy, or a desire for attention, as seen in his reaction to his mother's death and his rivalry with his sister Amaterasu.

Domains & Habitats

Susanoo is primarily associated with the sea and storms, but also with the land of Izumo (modern Shimane Prefecture), where he descended after his banishment. He is also connected to the underworld (Yomi) in some myths, as he is said to have visited it. His primary shrine is Izumo Taisha, and he is venerated throughout Japan.

Version Differences

The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki present different accounts of Susanoo's birth and actions. In the Kojiki, Susanoo is born from Izanagi's nose, while in the Nihon Shoki, he is born from a white copper mirror or from Izanagi's left eye (depending on the variant). The Kojiki emphasizes his violent nature and his exile from heaven, while the Nihon Shoki includes additional episodes, such as his encounter with a weeping old man and his marriage to Kushinada-hime. The Nihon Shoki also includes a variant where Susanoo is not exiled but descends to Izumo voluntarily. These differences reflect the compilation of various regional traditions.

Origins

Susanoo was born from Izanagi's nose. Given dominion over the sea, he wept and wailed, causing destruction. He went to Takamagahara to say goodbye to Amaterasu but caused such chaos that she hid. Banished, he descended to Izumo and killed the eight-headed serpent.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Storm and Sea Control

Source Version Both Kojiki and Nihon Shoki; also in later Shinto tradition.

Description

Susanoo is the god of storms and the sea. He can summon winds, rain, and tempests, and is believed to control the ocean and its tides. This power is often seen as destructive, but also as necessary for agriculture, as storms bring rain.

Limitations

His storms are often unpredictable and can cause devastation. In some myths, he is not fully in control of his powers, and his emotions can trigger storms.

Contradictions

Some sources associate him primarily with the sea, while others emphasize his storm aspect. There is also debate about whether he was originally a wind god or a sea god.

Divine Strength and Combat

Source Version Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Yamata-no-Orochi episode.

Description

Susanoo is a formidable warrior, capable of slaying monsters and gods. He famously defeated the eight-headed serpent Yamata-no-Orochi using a clever plan and a magical sword.

Limitations

His strength is not always enough on its own; he often relies on strategy and divine tools, such as the sword and the sake.

Contradictions

None noted.

Sword of Kusanagi

Source Version Kojiki and Nihon Shoki.

Description

Susanoo discovered the legendary sword Kusanagi-no-Tsurugi (Grass-Cutting Sword) in the tail of Yamata-no-Orochi. This sword became a symbol of imperial authority and is one of the Three Sacred Treasures of Japan.

Limitations

The sword is not his primary weapon in most myths; he gives it to Amaterasu as a peace offering. Its powers are more prominently associated with later heroes, such as Yamato Takeru.

Contradictions

Some versions of the myth place the sword in the tail of the serpent, while others do not mention it.

Transformation and Trickery

Source Version Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, Yamata-no-Orochi episode.

Description

Susanoo is capable of transforming himself, as seen when he changed into a 'poor man' or a 'wandering deity' to approach the old man and his wife before slaying the serpent. He also uses trickery, such as getting the serpent drunk on sake.

Limitations

His transformations are not extensively described, and he does not use trickery in other major myths.

Contradictions

None noted.

Control over the Underworld

Source Version Nihon Shoki and later theological interpretations.

Description

In some traditions, Susanoo is associated with the underworld (Yomi) or the realm of the dead. He is said to have visited Yomi and to have power over spirits and demons.

Limitations

This aspect is not prominent in the Kojiki, and it is unclear if he rules the underworld or merely visits it.

Contradictions

The Kojiki assigns the rulership of Yomi to Izanami, not Susanoo. Some scholars believe this association is a later development.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

Izanagi

Type fatherSource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Izanagi is Susanoo's father. He gave Susanoo dominion over the sea, but Susanoo's refusal to rule led to his banishment.

Version Note

In the Kojiki, Susanoo is born from Izanagi's nose. In the Nihon Shoki, he is born from a mirror or from Izanagi's left eye.

Conflicting Accounts

The circumstances of Susanoo's birth vary, but his relationship with Izanagi is consistently strained.

Amaterasu

Type siblingSource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Amaterasu is Susanoo's sister and the sun goddess. Their relationship is marked by rivalry and conflict, culminating in Susanoo's banishment from heaven. They later reconcile, with Susanoo giving Amaterasu the sword Kusanagi.

Version Note

In the Kojiki, Susanoo's tantrum in heaven is the cause of Amaterasu hiding in a cave. In the Nihon Shoki, there are multiple variants of their conflict.

Conflicting Accounts

The severity of Susanoo's actions varies between texts, and some versions depict him as more sympathetic.

Tsukuyomi

Type siblingSource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Tsukuyomi is the moon god and another sibling of Susanoo. They are not directly interacting in most myths, but both are children of Izanagi.

Version Note

In the Nihon Shoki, Tsukuyomi kills the food goddess Ōgetsu-hime, a deed attributed to Susanoo in the Kojiki.

Conflicting Accounts

The story of the food goddess is attributed to different siblings in different texts.

Kushinada-hime

Type spouseSource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Kushinada-hime is the daughter of the old man and woman whom Susanoo saved from Yamata-no-Orochi. He married her and they had many children.

Version Note

In the Kojiki, Susanoo turns her into a comb; in the Nihon Shoki, he uses a decoy.

Conflicting Accounts

The method of saving her differs, but the marriage is consistent.

Ōkuninushi

Type descendant/foster sonSource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Ōkuninushi is a descendant of Susanoo, and in some myths, Susanoo acts as a father figure, testing Ōkuninushi and helping him to become the ruler of Izumo.

Version Note

In the Kojiki, Ōkuninushi is a descendant and undergoes trials set by Susanoo. In the Nihon Shoki, he is also a descendant.

Conflicting Accounts

The exact nature of their relationship varies, with some accounts depicting Susanoo as a helpful ancestor and others as a stern father-in-law.

Yamata-no-Orochi

Type enemySource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Yamata-no-Orochi is the eight-headed serpent that Susanoo slew. It was terrorizing the land of Izumo and demanded human sacrifices.

Version Note

The story is consistent across texts, though details vary.

Conflicting Accounts

None noted.

Ōgetsu-hime

Type victimSource Kojiki; Nihon Shoki

Description

Ōgetsu-hime is the food goddess whom Susanoo killed in a fit of disgust, leading to the creation of crops from her body.

Version Note

In the Nihon Shoki, this deed is attributed to Tsukuyomi.

Conflicting Accounts

The identity of the killer differs between texts.
