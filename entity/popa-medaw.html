Who It Is

On the slopes of an extinct volcano, where wild orchids bloom and the air is thick with the scent of flowers, there lives a spirit queen whose love and loss shaped the fate of a kingdom. Popa Medaw, the Lady of Mount Popa, is no distant deity—she is a mother, a lover, and a guardian, her story woven into the very soil of Myanmar.

She is portrayed as loving and devoted, especially to her sons and consort. Her death from a broken heart suggests a deep emotional nature. As a nat, she is venerated for her protective and nurturing qualities.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Popa Medaw (Burmese: ပုပ္ပားမယ်တော်) translates to 'Queen-Mother of Popa' or 'Lady of Popa'. The name 'Me Wunna' (မယ်ဝဏ္ဏ) means 'Miss Gold' or 'Golden Lady'. The word 'Popa' is derived from the Pali word 'puppha', meaning 'flower'.

Pronunciation — Po-pa Me-daw (Burmese: ပုပ္ပားမယ်တော်)

Aliases — Me Wunna, Mei Wunna, Lady of Popa, Queen-Mother of Popa, Mother of Popa

Ownership — Burmese (Myanmar)

Form & Signs

Appearance — Popa Medaw is described as a beautiful woman, often depicted in traditional Burmese attire. As a flower-eating yakshini, she may have had a supernatural or monstrous aspect, but in nat iconography she is usually shown as elegant and regal.

Symbols — Flowers, particularly orchids and other blossoms found on Mount Popa, are associated with her. The mountain itself is her primary symbol and domain.

Domains — Mount Popa, an extinct volcano in central Myanmar, is her primary domain. She is the guardian of this mountain and its surrounding forests.

Accounts I
The Romance of Byatta and Mei WunnaBurmese folklore

Bagan period (circa 11th century)

King Anawrahta of Bagan ordered Byatta, a man of Indian descent with supernatural powers, to fetch fresh flowers from Mount Popa ten times daily. On one of these trips, Byatta encountered Mei Wunna, a flower-eating ogress. They fell in love, and she bore him two sons, Shwe Hpyin Naungdaw and Shwe Hpyin Nyidaw. After Byatta was killed, Mei Wunna died of a broken heart, and her sons were taken to the king's court. She became a nat, known as Popa Medaw. Mei Wunna's death elevated her to nat status. Her sons were later deified as the Shwe Hpyin brothers, and their mother was honored as the Queen-Mother of Popa.
