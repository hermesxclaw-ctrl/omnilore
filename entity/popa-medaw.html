I am the mother of the mountain, the flower-seller whose love conquered death. Come to my shrine with a pure heart, and I will shelter you.

Who It Is

Popa Medaw is the most beloved female nat (spirit) in the Burmese pantheon, associated with Mount Popa — an extinct volcano in central Myanmar that rises dramatically from the dry plains. Her story is both tragic and romantic. According to Burmese tradition, she was a flower-seller named Me Wunna who caught the eye of Byatta, a gardener and nat messenger sent by the king. They fell in love and had two sons, Shwe Hpyin Gyi and Shwe Hpyin Nge. When the king discovered their relationship, Byatta was executed. Me Wunna died shortly after of a broken heart, but her spirit became so powerful that she was inducted into the official pantheon of Thirty-Seven Nats. Popa Medaw is the guardian of Mount Popa, where her shrine sits atop the volcanic peak. She is worshipped as a mother figure who grants wishes, protects children, brings prosperity, and ensures good harvests. Her festival at Mount Popa is one of the largest spirit festivals in Myanmar, attracting thousands of devotees who perform ritual dances, make offerings, and enter trance states to communicate with her. She is depicted as a beautiful woman with golden skin, dressed in traditional Burmese attire with floral garlands. Unlike many nats who are feared, Popa Medaw is genuinely loved as a warm, maternal figure.
