Who It Is

Lakshmi (Lakṣmī), also known as Shri, is the Hindu goddess of wealth, fortune, prosperity, beauty, and auspiciousness—the divine consort of Vishnu and the most universally worshipped goddess in the Hindu household. Her origin myth from the Churning of the Ocean of Milk (Samudra Manthan) is one of the most celebrated episodes in Hindu mythology: when the devas and asuras churned the cosmic ocean to obtain the nectar of immortality (amrita), fourteen treasures emerged. Among them—and arguably the most precious—was Lakshmi herself, who rose from the frothing waters seated upon a fully bloomed lotus, radiant with beauty, holding a lotus flower, with elephants anointing her with sacred water. Her emergence from the primordial ocean establishes her as the embodiment of fortune, fertility, and the abundance of nature. Her iconography is instantly recognizable: a beautiful golden-skinned woman with four arms, seated or standing on a lotus, holding lotus flowers (representing spiritual purity amidst material abundance), with gold coins flowing from one hand (symbolizing material prosperity), and either the abhaya mudra (gesture of fearlessness) or varada mudra (gesture of giving). Two white elephants flank her, spraying water—symbols of royal authority and the rains that fertilize the earth. Lakshmi's central festival is Diwali (Deepavali), the festival of lights, when millions of Hindu households clean their homes, light clay lamps (diyas), draw elaborate rice flour designs (rangoli), and leave doors and windows open so that Lakshmi may enter and bless the household with prosperity for the coming year. In Vaishnava theology, Lakshmi is inseparable from Vishnu—she is his shakti, the active principle of his grace, and the mediator between the transcendent god and his devotees. Her eight primary manifestations (Ashta Lakshmi) govern different forms of wealth: Adi Lakshmi (primeval), Dhana Lakshmi (monetary wealth), Dhanya Lakshmi (agricultural wealth), Gaja Lakshmi (royal power), Santana Lakshmi (progeny), Veera Lakshmi (courage), Vidya Lakshmi (knowledge), and Vijaya Lakshmi (victory). Her worship extends throughout the Hindu world and into Buddhist traditions, where she appears as Vasudhara in Nepal and Tibet, and as Kisshoten in Japan.
