19th century to present (settlement period, exact eponym date not retrieved)

Charlos Cove is a small community on the eastern shore of Nova Scotia in Guysborough County, facing the Atlantic. Like many coves in the region, it was likely named for an early settler, fishing family, or landholder named Charlos/Charles, though the specific eponym biography is not detailed in sources accessed. The community persists as a dispersed fishing and residential hamlet, appearing on federal maps and census subdivisions. No founding legend, miracle, or mythic narrative is attached to the name in sources found. The Settlement of Charlos Heights, MontanaUSGS GNIS; Ravalli County histories; Bitterroot Valley settlement records

Late 19th - early 20th century (Bitterroot Valley homesteading era) to present

Charlos Heights is an unincorporated community on a bench east of the Bitterroot River, south of Hamilton, Montana. The name likely honors Chief Charlo (Charlos) of the Bitterroot Salish, or a settler family using that spelling — Bitterroot Valley features several Charlo/Charlos toponyms (Charlo, Montana; Charlos Heights). Federal records classify it as a populated place. No incorporated government; it functions as a residential area with orchard and rangeland history. No heroic legend narrative is attached in federal records. Toponym entered into GNIS; appears on USGS quadrangles, census maps, and local addressing; associated with the broader Charlo/Charlos naming cluster in western Montana.
