Who It Is

Tala is the Filipino goddess of Venus — the brightest false star in the sky, the morning and evening light that ancient sailors used to navigate before compasses existed. Daughter of the supreme creator Bathala, sister of the moon warrior Mayari and the dawn goddess Hanan, she sits at the exact junction where Hindu mythology, pre-colonial Philippine cosmology, and Spanish colonial syncretism collide.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Tala is the direct root of the Tagalog word tala, meaning 'bright star'. The name is derived from or convergent with the Sanskrit Tara (Sanskrit: तारा), the Hindu Buddhist goddess of the stars and the morning star, reflecting deep Indian cultural penetration into the pre-colonial Philippine

Stellar guidance and nocturnal navigatio

Stellar guidance and nocturnal navigation — Tala pilots light-orbs that ferry lost travelers to safety, a power rooted in the practical importance of Venus as a pre-dawn/post-sunset navigational marker for pre-colonial Filipino seafarers and farmers.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Tala is understood as a luminous female deity associated with the planet Venus — the brightest object in the sky after the Sun and Moon. As the morning and evening star, she embodies the transitional light of dawn and dusk.
