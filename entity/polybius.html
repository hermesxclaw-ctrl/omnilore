Who It Is

Polybius is a quintessential urban legend of the digital age -- a story about a mysterious arcade game that supposedly appeared in several Portland, Oregon arcades in 1981. According to the legend, Polybius was a vector-graphics game with an abstract, puzzle-shooter design reminiscent of Tempest. Players who engaged with the game experienced intense psychoactive and neurological effects: amnesia, night terrors, addiction, hallucinations, and even suicidal impulses. Men in black suits allegedly visited the arcades regularly, taking data from the machines and interrogating players, but never giving the game to the public -- it disappeared after about one month as mysteriously as it arrived. The legend claims it was a government mind-control experiment (MKUltra-style) or a German experiment in subliminal messaging. The first reference to Polybius appeared in 2000, when a user named 'Kurt Koller' wrote an entry on coinop.org, a coin-operated game database, describing the game from memory. The entry was clearly fictional in tone but contained enough dense, pseudo-documentary detail (specific Portland arcade names, dates, hardware specs) to feel plausible. The legend exploded via internet forums, where retro gamers embellished and 'remembered' playing the game -- an example of collective false memory creation. No physical cabinet, board, ROM, or contemporary newspaper article has ever been found. However, the Polybius legend has become a beloved piece of gaming and internet folklore, inspiring game designs, music, films, and episodes of TV shows like The Simpsons and Dimension 404. It represents a quintessentially modern urban legend: born online, impossible to prove or disprove, and endlessly generative.
