Who It Is

The word means 'raven' in ancient Greek — κόραξ — and it has never stopped circling. In one sky it is a glossy black bird that remembers faces and mimics wolves; in another it is a Sicilian teacher who taught men how to argue for their stolen land; in another it is a mythical king of Sicyon whose reign barely survives in a single paragraph; and in another it is a pale, winged superhuman who can turn invisible in shadow. All of them are called Corax, and none of them are the same creature.

1) Biological raven: highly intelligent, problem-solving, tool-using, food-caching, pair-bonded, playful (aerial acrobatics, sliding on snow), vocal mimicry, scavenger-predator; attested by ethological studies (Heinrich). 2) Corax of Syracuse (doxographical): characterized as shrewd, pragmatic teacher who systematized probability arguments to help citizens reclaim property after the fall of Thrasybulus (466 BCE); personality is reconstructed from anecdotes, not attested. 3) Mythical king Corax: no personality preserved; Pausanias gives only genealogical function. 4) Warhammer Corvus Corax:

Powers
Corvid intelligence and vocal mimicry (biological)

Wild Corvus corax demonstrates causal reasoning, planning for future, bartering, and mimicry of human speech, wolf calls, and mechanical sounds. Documented to drop nuts on roads for cars to crack, and to deceive conspecifics about cache locations.

Limit: No abstract language; mimicry is not semantic in human sense; cognition limited to ecological context; lifespan ~10-15 years wild, up to 40 captive.

Eikos / argument from probability (rhetorical technique)

Corax (with Tisias) credited with inventing rhetoric based on eikos — what seems likely to a jury. Example: if a weak man is accused of assaulting a strong man, he argues it is improbable he would attack; if a strong man is accused, he argues it is improbable he would do so knowing he would be suspected. Systematized for property litigation after democratic restoration in Syracuse.

Limit: No verbatim text survives; technique is reported second-hand and may be retrojection by later sophists; requires jury system and forensic context.

Transhuman Primarch physiology (Warhammer 40,000)

As one of 20 Primarchs engineered from the Emperor's genome, Corvus Corax possesses superhuman strength (able to crush ceramite), speed, endurance, rapid healing, eidetic memory, and strategic genius. Wields twin lightning claws and master-crafted wargear.

Limit: Still vulnerable to Primarch-level weapons, warp entities, and psychological trauma; not invulnerable; warp-shadowing has costs (corruption risk).

Wraith Slip / Shadow-walking (warp gift)

After Isstvan V and exposure to warp energies, Corax gains ability to become intangible shadow, turn invisible even to auspex, suppress his presence, and later manifest as a warp entity of living darkness with wings in the Eye of Terror (pursuing Word Bearers).

Limit: Requires concentration; tied to emotional state and warp proximity; later form is quasi-daemonic and exiles him from Imperium; not usable in all continuities.

Miracles and gospel-fabrication (literary)

In Park's novel, Corax performs ambiguous healings, exorcisms, and narrative miracles that may be sleight-of-hand, misinterpretation, or genuine; his chief power is storytelling — composing a counter-gospel that reframes Jesus' ministry.

Limit: Unreliable narrator; no supernatural confirmation within text; power is rhetorical and moral, not systematic.

Low-observable flight (technological)

BAE Systems Corax demonstrator: subsonic, jet-powered, reduced radar cross-section flying wing for autonomous ISR/strike research; intended to test stealth shaping, materials, and autonomous control.

Limit: Experimental demonstrator, not production aircraft; no weapons integrated publicly; program status largely undisclosed after 2006.

Accounts I
The Succession of Sicyon: Corax son of CorinthusPausanias, Description of Greece 2.5.7; also Eusebius, Chronicon (via Syncellus) variant king-list

Mythic Bronze Age as codified in 2nd c. CE

Pausanias, touring Sicyon, records its archaic king-list: after Epopeus died, Lamedon gave power to Sicyon, then to Polybus, then to Adrastus, then after Adrastus returned to Argos, to Ianiscus, then to... variant manuscripts diverge. In the transmitted text, Corax appears as a king of Sicyon who died childless, after which Epopeus (or Polybus in some reconstructions) succeeded. The passage is terse — no deeds, only genealogy — serving to link Sicyon to Argive and Corinthian lineages. No cult or ritual is attached. Establishes Sicyonian legitimacy; later chronographers used the list to synchronize Greek mythical chronology. No independent narrative cycle survives; Corax here is a genealogical placeholder.

The Invention of Rhetoric: Corax and Tisias reclaim SyracuseCicero Brutus 46-48; Quintilian 3.1.8; Diodorus Siculus 11.87 (context of Thrasybulus' expulsion); Byzantine Prolegomena to Hermogenes; Plato Phaedrus 273a-c (reference to Tisias)

Mid-5th century BCE (traditional date 466 BCE, after fall of Thrasybulus); sources are 4th c. BCE - 5th c. CE retrospectives

After the tyrant Thrasybulus was expelled and democracy restored, Syracusans faced mass property disputes with no legal expertise. Corax, perhaps a former advisor to the tyrant, systematized techniques of persuasive speech based on probability (eikos) to help citizens argue cases before large citizen juries. He taught Tisias, who taught Gorgias. The famous anecdote: Corax demanded payment from Tisias, who refused; they sued each other, each arguing that if Tisias won his first case he proved rhetoric worked and must pay, and if he lost he lost the case and must pay — a paradox later called the 'Corax vs. Tisias' or 'crocodile' paradox. The story illustrates the self-referential power of rhetoric. Traditional foundation myth of Greek rhetoric and forensic oratory; whether historical, it anchors the Sicilian origin theory vs. Athenian. The eikos method influenced Sophists, Antiphon, and later Roman rhetoric.

The Common Raven in Natural and Cultural HistoryLinnaeus Systema Naturae 1758; Buffon Histoire Naturelle des Oiseaux 1770-1783; Heinrich Ravens in Winter (1989) and Mind of the Raven (1999); folklore corpora (Greek, Norse, Indigenous North American)

1758 taxonomy to present; folklore layers from antiquity to present

Linnaeus fixed the raven as Corvus corax, the type of intelligence in birds. Natural history documents its Holarctic range, scavenging with wolves, caching, and play. Folklore layers: Greek korax as Apollo's bird turned black (Ovid Metamorphoses 2.531-632), Norse Huginn and Muninn as Odin's informants, Welsh/Celtic ravens of war (Badb), and Pacific Northwest Raven as trickster-creator (Tlingit, Haida). Modern ethology reframed the raven from omen to cognitive model, while conservation history tracks persecution and recovery. Raven becomes emblem of intelligence research, conservation, and persistent literary symbol (Poe 1845, etc.). Taxonomic stability anchors biodiversity data.

Accounts II
The Gospel of Corax: A Counter-Gospel in Roman JudaeaPaul Park, The Gospel of Corax (Soho Press 1996)

1996 novel set in c. 30-70 CE fictionalized Judaea

Corax is a charlatan-healer and former disciple of John the Baptist who attaches himself to Jesus' movement. After the crucifixion, he composes his own gospel — part memoir, part fabrication — presenting a Jesus who is human, conflicted, and politically entangled, while Corax himself performs dubious miracles to survive under Roman occupation. The novel is narrated as Corax's manuscript discovered and translated, questioning authorship, memory, and how gospels are made. Corax survives the destruction of the Temple, aging into a weary witness who doubts his own lies. Critically noted for its heterodox, humanist retelling; no franchise beyond the novel; studied as example of 1990s postmodern biblical fiction alongside works by Norman Mailer and José Saramago.

Corvus Corax: Deliverance, Isstvan V, and the Shadow WarGames Workshop: Horus Heresy series — Deliverance Lost (Thorpe 2011), Corax: Soulforge (Thorpe 2016), Shadows of the Past (short), Raven Guard Codex supplements (8th-10th ed.)

In-universe: c. 800.M30 - 014.M31; publication 1990-2024

Grown on the moon Lycaeus (renamed Deliverance) where he leads slave-miners in revolt against tech-guilds of Kiavahr, Corax is reunited with the Emperor and given command of the Raven Guard Legion (XIX). Known for hit-and-run, infiltration, and liberation ethos. At Isstvan V (Dropsite Massacre), his Legion is nearly annihilated by traitors; Corax escapes, returns to Deliverance, and attempts to rebuild using accelerated gene-tech (Raptor project) which is sabotaged by the Alpha Legion, producing mutated monsters he must euthanize. Guilt-ridden, he leaves the Imperium after the Heresy, enters the Eye of Terror to hunt the Word Bearers, mutating into a warp-infused shadow entity. Defines Raven Guard doctrine (stealth, freedom); explains Legion's small size in 40k era; Corax's disappearance creates 'lost Primarch' mystery; recent lore (2020s) hints at his return as warp-raven.

BAE Systems Corax: The Raven Takes FlightBAE Systems press release Jan 2004; Flight International 12 Jan 2004; Jane's Unmanned Aerial Vehicles and Targets 2004-2006

2004-2006 public disclosure; development late 1990s-2004

BAE Systems at Warton developed Corax (also called Raven) as a technology demonstrator for future unmanned combat air systems (later Taranis). The tailless flying wing was built to test low-observable shaping, composite structures, and autonomous flight control without public flight footage initially. First images released 2004 showed a dark, bat-like airframe. The program was part of UK's FOAS/FCAS studies and informed later BAE Taranis (2013 first flight). Details remain limited due to classification. Demonstrated UK stealth UAV capability; fed into Taranis and later FCAS/Tempest unmanned concepts; public name 'Corax' reinforced raven/stealth association.

Corax was a single, well-documented historical person with a surviving textbook on rhetoric — No work by Corax survives; everything is second-hand doxography centuries later, and his very existence is disputed. The 'textbook' is a reconstruction from later summaries.

Corvus corax means 'Corax the crow' as a separate genus — Corvus is the genus (crows/ravens), corax is the species epithet meaning 'raven' — the binomial is tautological ('raven raven'). The genus Corax does not exist for birds; fossil shark Corax is obsolete.

The Raven Guard Primarch Corvus Corax is based on the ancient Greek rhetorician or king — Games Workshop named him from Latin corvus/corax for thematic raven imagery (stealth, Deliverance's ravens); no in-universe link to Sicilian rhetoric or Sicyonian myth. Shared etymology only.

BAE Systems Corax is an operational combat drone — Corax was an experimental technology demonstrator (2004) for stealth and autonomy research, not a fielded UCAV; its successor Taranis was also a demonstrator. No evidence of operational deployment.

Ravens (Corvus corax) can talk like parrots and are bad omens that cause death — Ravens can mimic sounds including human words but do not acquire language; omen association is cultural, not causal. Ecologically they are scavengers and indicators, not harbingers.
