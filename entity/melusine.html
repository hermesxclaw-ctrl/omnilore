I am she who builds and she who destroys. Look upon me on the forbidden day, and you lose everything I have given you.

Who It Is

Mélusine is a fairy woman cursed to transform into a serpent (or fish-tailed mermaid) from the waist down every Saturday. She marries the nobleman Raymondin of Forez on the condition that he never sees her on Saturdays. She uses her supernatural powers to build castles, towns, and fortifications across the Poitou region — including the mighty fortress of Lusignan — and bears him ten sons, many of whom are marked by strange deformities reflecting her cursed nature. When Raymondin eventually breaks his oath and spies on her bathing, her secret is revealed. In grief, she transforms fully into a dragon-serpent and flies away wailing, her cries becoming the omen of death for the Lusignan line. Mélusine is bound to fountain and forest: an ambivalent figure who is both nurturing culture-bearer (builder of civilization) and untamable wild spirit. Her legend spawned a dynasty — the House of Lusignan claimed her as their ancestress, and she appears in the genealogies of royal houses across Europe including the Plantagenets. Her story has been retold by Goethe, Walter Scott, and in countless French folk songs. She represents the uneasy medieval relationship between Christianity and the older fairy faith, between domestic order and wild feminine power.
