Who It Is

Did you know that Mélusine, the fairy of the fountain, was said to have built the Castle of Lusignan in a single night? Her story is a tale of love, betrayal, and a curse that echoes through the centuries. But there's a darker twist: in some versions, she is not a fairy but a demon, and her serpent tail is a mark of her infernal pact. Which Mélusine will you believe?

Mélusine is portrayed as a benevolent and industrious fairy, who brings prosperity and good fortune to her mortal husband, Raymondin of Poitou. She is a skilled builder and architect, using her supernatural powers to construct castles and fortresses overnight. She is also a devoted mother, raising her many sons with care, though they often inherit her supernatural traits, such as unusual birthmarks or deformities. Powers
Shapeshifting

Mélusine can transform her lower body from human legs to a serpent's tail, typically on Saturdays. This transformation is a central element of her legend and is tied to her curse. She can also assume a fully human form at other times, allowing her to interact with mortals without revealing her supernatural nature.

Limit:

Supernatural Architecture

Mélusine is said to be able to construct castles, fortresses, and other structures overnight, using her magical powers. This ability is demonstrated in the building of the Castle of Lusignan, which she erects in a single night, impressing her husband and the local populace.

Limit:

Prophecy and Omens

Mélusine possesses the ability to foresee future events, particularly those concerning her descendants. She prophesies the rise and fall of the Lusignan dynasty, and her appearances as a specter are considered omens of death or misfortune for the family.

Limit:

Control over Water

As a water spirit, Mélusine has dominion over fresh water sources, including springs, rivers, and wells. She can calm or stir waters, and her presence is often associated with the health and fertility of the surrounding land.

Limit:

Cursing and Blessing

Mélusine can bestow blessings of prosperity and fertility upon her husband and children, but she can also curse those who betray her. Her departure from Lusignan is accompanied by a curse that the castle will fall, and she is said to return as a wailing spirit to herald the death of her descendants.

Accounts I
The Meeting at the Fountain of ThirstLe Roman de Mélusine by Jean d'Arras

Late 14th century

Raymondin, a young nobleman, accidentally kills his uncle in a hunting accident and flees in despair. He encounters Mélusine at the Fountain of Thirst in the forest of Colombiers. She offers him comfort and proposes marriage, but with a condition: he must never see her on Saturdays, when she will be in a transformed state. Raymondin agrees, and they marry. Mélusine uses her powers to build the Castle of Lusignan and to expand Raymondin's lands, bringing him wealth and power. They have many sons, each with a distinctive birthmark or deformity, such as a lion's claw or a single eye, but they grow to be strong and valiant knights. Raymondin and Mélusine live happily for many years, and their sons become heroes, but Raymondin's curiosity grows. Eventually, he is persuaded by his brother to spy on Mélusine, leading to the discovery of her serpent form and her subsequent departure.

Accounts II
The Discovery of the SecretLe Roman de Mélusine by Jean d'Arras

Late 14th century

After years of marriage, Raymondin's brother, the Count of Poitou, taunts him about Mélusine's mysterious absence on Saturdays, suggesting she is unfaithful. Raymondin, driven by jealousy and curiosity, peeks through a keyhole in the door of her chamber. He sees Mélusine bathing in a tub, her lower body transformed into a serpent's tail. He is horrified but does not reveal his discovery. Later, during an argument, he calls her a 'serpent' in front of their court, breaking his vow. Mélusine, heartbroken, transforms into a winged serpent and flies out the window, crying a lament. She circles the castle three times before disappearing, leaving behind a prophecy that the Lusignan line will fall and that she will return as a specter to warn of their deaths. Mélusine departs, and the castle of Lusignan is left without its protective spirit. Raymondin dies of grief, and the Lusignan dynasty eventually declines, fulfilling the prophecy. Mélusine is said to haunt the castle, appearing on the battlements before the death of a Lusignan heir.
