Who It Is

She is called day's eye — the flower that opens with the sun and the heroine who never quite belongs to the story named for her. Whether she is F. Scott Fitzgerald's golden girl on Long Island, Nintendo's tomboy princess of Sarasaland, or Disney's sharp-tongued duck in a bow, every Daisy is asked to be innocence and artifice at once, and every Daisy pushes back.

No single personality; archetype splits. Literary Daisy Buchanan: charming, careless, bored, performative, self-protective; Nick Carraway calls her voice 'an absolute smirk' and notes her 'carelessness.' She is sympathetic and complicit, not purely villainous. Princess Daisy: extroverted, competitive, tomboyish, loud and cheerful (Mario Strikers, Mario Tennis voice lines: 'Hi, I'm Daisy!'), courageous ruler who actively participates in sports and party games rather than awaiting rescue after her debut. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Old English dæges ēage 'day's eye', referring to Bellis perennis opening at dawn and closing at dusk. First attested c. 1000 as dæges eage. By Middle English dayeseye (Chaucer). Given name Daisy is a 19th-century flower-name coinage, popularized in Victorian England as part of the flower-name vogue

Pronunciation — /ˈdeɪzi/ DAY-zee; French Marguerite /maʁɡəʁit/; German Gänseblümchen /ˈɡɛnzəˌblyːmçən/ for common daisy

Aliases — Daisies, DAISY, Princess Daisy, Daisy Duck, Daisy Buchanan, Daisy Fay Buchanan

Ownership — Botanical term and flower folklore belongs to pan-European tradition, especially English. As a given name and character name, it is anglophone in origin but globally diffused via A

Powers
Social and Aesthetic Capital (Daisy Buchanan)

Wealth, beauty, and voice as instruments of influence. Nick notes her voice 'holds' men; she navigates upper-class codes to secure protection. No supernatural power; power is socio-economic.

Limit: Entirely dependent on male wealth (Tom, then Gatsby); cannot act independently without social cost; collapses under pressure (fails to leave Tom).

Athletic Prowess and Flower Power (Princess Daisy)

In Mario sports/party games, Daisy exhibits superhuman athleticism: Power Shot techniques (Flower Ball, Wonder Flower), super jumps, ground pounds. In Super Mario Land, she is damsel; in later games she is playable powerhouse with highest technique/power stats in Mario Tennis Aces, Mario Strikers Charged.

Limit: No canonical magic in mainline platformers; powers are game-mechanic specific and non-diegetic outside sports spin-offs. Cannot use powers in Super Mario Land narrative.

Anthropomorphic Durability and Comic Invention (Daisy Duck)

Toon physics: survives slapstick, quick costume changes, professional competence (reporter, etc. in comics). In DuckTales 2017 reboot, she is articulate and managerial.

Limit: No superpowers; bound by comedic universe rules; strength is social and verbal.

Divination and Healing Folklore (Flower Daisy)

Folk belief: daisy petal oracle 'he loves me, he loves me not' (French effeuiller la marguerite); herbal use: Bellis perennis in folk medicine for wounds, bruises (Culpeper's Herbal 1653: 'wound herb'). Daisy chain making as children's protective charm.

Limit: No efficacy beyond placebo; divination is game, not prophecy; herbal claims not clinically verified.

Resilience / Phototropism Metaphor

Botanical trait: daisy opens with sun, closes at night; metaphorically extended to fictional Daisies as resilient, cheerful survivors who 'bounce back' after trauma (Buchanan's social survival, Princess Daisy's return after kidnapping).

Limit: Metaphorical only; not a diegetic ability.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Botanical Daisy (Bellis perennis): low rosette, spoon-shaped crenate leaves, solitary capitulum 15-30mm diameter, white ray florets (often pink-tinged) surrounding yellow disc florets. Fictional Daisies deliberately echo this palette. Daisy Buchanan is described by Fitzgerald as blonde, small, white-and-gold associated ('her white flower' motif, 'golden girl'), dressed in white in Chapter 1, with a voice 'full of money' and 'a stirring, thrilling

Symbols — Flower symbolism: innocence, purity, new beginnings, loyal love, childhood (Victorian language of flowers: 'innocence' - Seaton 1995). Chaucer's daisy/marguerite is fidelity and Alceste's emblem. 'Fresh as a daisy' idiom (attested 1840s). Daisy chain = interconnectedness, childhood craft, also slang for linked sequence (daisy-chain). In Fitzgerald, white flower / golden flower duality = corrupted innocence and wealth

Domains — Bellis perennis: lawns, meadows, temperate Europe, naturalized worldwide; blooms March-September. Literary Daisy Buchanan: East Egg (old money mansion), Louisville childhood, Chicago, later unspecified East Coast. Princess Daisy: Sarasaland (desert, Birabuto Kingdom; Muda, Easton, Chai kingdoms), later Mushroom Kingdom guest; sports arenas, courts. Daisy Duck: Duckburg (Calisota), shared universe with Donald, Scrooge McDuck. Daisy the Diesel Rail

Accounts I
Daisy Fay Buchanan - The Great GatsbyF. Scott Fitzgerald, The Great Gatsby (Scribner, 1925)

1925 (novel set 1922)

Daisy Fay, Louisville belle, loves young officer Jay Gatsby before war, marries wealthy brute Tom Buchanan while Gatsby is abroad. In summer 1922, Gatsby, now rich via bootlegging, buys mansion across water from Daisy's East Egg home to win her back via cousin Nick Carraway. Daisy reunites with Gatsby, briefly considers leaving Tom, but retreats to the safety of old money after Gatsby confronts Tom at the Plaza Hotel. Driving Gatsby's yellow car, Daisy hits and kills Tom's mistress Myrtle Wilson and flees the scene. Gatsby takes blame and is murdered by Myrtle's husband George. Daisy and Tom leave town without attending Gatsby's funeral. Establishes Daisy as American archetype of careless wealth and corrupted American Dream; ends with Nick's judgment that Tom and Daisy are 'careless people' who 'smashed up things.'

Princess Daisy of Sarasaland - Super Mario LandNintendo R&D1, Super Mario Land (Game Boy, 1989), manual and game

1989 - present (ongoing franchise)

The alien Tatanga invades Sarasaland, hypnotizes its people, and kidnaps Princess Daisy, ruler of the four kingdoms (Birabuto, Muda, Easton, Chai). Mario traverses 12 levels across desert, sea, Easter Island-like ruins, and sky to defeat Tatanga in the Chai Kingdom and free Daisy. In the ending, Daisy kisses Mario and they depart together. Unlike Peach's Mushroom Kingdom, Sarasaland is desert-themed, establishing Daisy as exotic counterpart. Introduces Daisy as second princess in Mario canon; after long hiatus (1989-2000), she is revived as playable in Mario Tennis (N64, 2000) and becomes staple of spin-offs, redefined from damsel to tomboy athlete.

Daisy Duck - From Donna to DaisyWalt Disney Productions: Mr. Duck Steps Out (animated short, 1940-06-07); Walt Disney's Comics and Stories #280 onward; Carl Barks comics

1937 (Donna Duck debut) / 1940 (Daisy codified) - present

Donna Duck appears in Don Donald (1937) as Donald's early girlfriend. Redesigned as Daisy Duck in Mr. Duck Steps Out (1940), she is courted by Donald and rival Huey, Dewey, Louie. In comics, Daisy is Donald's on-off girlfriend, often exasperated by his laziness and temper, pursuing her own career and social life. Barks and later Don Rosa give her agency: she works as reporter, has nieces April, May, June, and forms friendship/rivalry with Donald. In House of Mouse and DuckTales 2017, she is fashion-forward and managerial. Establishes female counterpart to Donald who is not merely prize but foil and moral superior; archetype for assertive cartoon heroine.

Accounts II
Daisy (2006 film) - Hye-young the ArtistAndrew Lau, film Daisy (2006), screenplay by Kwak Jae-yong

2006

Hye-young (Jeon Ji-hyun), an artist who paints daisies, is loved from afar by Interpol detective Jeong Woo (Lee Sung-jae) who sends her daisies daily, and by hitman Park Yi (Jung Woo-sung) who is hired to kill Woo. Misunderstandings over who sends the flowers lead Hye-young to believe Yi is her secret admirer. When Yi is ordered to kill Woo, the triangle ends in tragedy: Woo dies, Yi is mortally wounded, Hye-young loses her voice after being shot, and later learns truth. Daisies symbolize innocent love and miscommunication. Popularized daisy as symbol of pure, unspoken love in East Asian pop culture; title's flower motif drives marketing and soundtrack.

Daisy the Diesel Railcar - Thomas & FriendsRev. W. Awdry, Branch Line Engines (1961) - Daisy; Britt Allcroft, Thomas & Friends TV series S2 (1986)

1961 (book) / 1986 (TV)

Daisy is a green diesel railcar who arrives on Thomas's branch line to help. She is lazy, fussy, and believes she is too good to pull trucks, claiming her fumes make her ill. After being shown up and eventually proving useful pulling a passenger coach, she learns humility but retains sassy personality. She represents modernization vs steam. Introduces female-coded diesel character who is flawed and comic, not idealized; remains recurring character in books and CGI series.

Daisy - Joyce Carol Oates Short StoryJoyce Carol Oates, 'Daisy' in Night-Side and other collections (1977)

1977

A young woman named Daisy, working-class, navigates sexual exploitation and violence in a bleak small town. Oates uses Daisy flower imagery ironically: the name's innocence contrasts with the protagonist's brutalization and attempt at self-assertion. The story is noted for its unflinching depiction of male predation. Example of Daisy name used to interrogate innocence myth; cited in feminist literary criticism.

Daisy is just a sweet, innocent flower name with no dark meaning. — While Victorian language gave daisy 'innocence,' literary Daisies (Buchanan, Oates) weaponize or ironize that innocence to critique wealth, carelessness, and gendered violence. The flower's 'innocence' is cultural construction, not inherent.

Princess Daisy is just 'another Princess Peach' with no difference. — After 2000, Nintendo deliberately differentiated Daisy as tomboy, athletic, loud, and competitive vs Peach's graceful, kind, magical. Gameplay stats, voice acting (Deanna Mustard), and lore (Sarasaland vs Mushroom Kingdom) are distinct.

The Daisy political ad aired many times and was an official nuclear threat. — It aired only once (Sept 7, 1964) and was pulled; it never explicitly mentioned Goldwater but implied his nuclear stance; its fame comes from news replay and analysis, not heavy rotation.

'He loves me, he loves me not' daisy divination is ancient Celtic druid ritual. — Documented from 19th-century European children's folklore (French effeuiller la marguerite); no ancient Celtic source attests it. Medieval daisy lore is herbal and courtly love, not druidic.

Daisy Buchanan is based on a single real woman and Fitzgerald intended her as purely villainous. — Fitzgerald drew on wife Zelda Sayre, socialite Ginevra King, and composite Jazz Age figures; his letters show ambivalence, calling Daisy both 'beautiful little fool' and product of her world. Novel invites sympathy and critique.
