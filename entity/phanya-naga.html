Who It Is

The Phanya Naga is the great serpent king of the Mekong River, one of the most important supernatural beings in the shared mythology of Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia. Nagas are serpentine beings that inhabit the watery depths beneath the Mekong and are believed to rule over all aquatic life. Phanya Naga is the chief of all nagas — a vast, multi-headed cobra-like deity whose body coils beneath the river. He is associated with fertility, rainfall, and the life-giving power of the river. The most famous annual phenomenon associated with the Phanya Naga is the Naga Fireballs of Nong Khai — thousands of glowing red orbs that rise from the Mekong River during the full moon of the 11th lunar month (October). Locals believe these are the fireballs emitted by the Phanya Naga to celebrate the end of Buddhist lent (Ok Phansa). The fires are a major tourist attraction and have been studied scientifically (with explanations ranging from swamp gas to geological phenomena, though locals remain convinced of their supernatural origin). Nagas are also central to the origin myths of the Lao kingdom of Lan Xang, whose kings claimed descent from the union of a Khmer prince and a naga princess. In Cambodia, the naga is central to the foundation myth of the Khmer people.
