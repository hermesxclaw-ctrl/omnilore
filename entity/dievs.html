Who It Is

Before the thunder of Perkūnas, before the weaving of Laima, there was Dievs—the sky itself, the shining dome above the Baltic world. He was not a distant tyrant but a quiet presence, a god who walked among mortals as a beggar to test their hearts. His name echoes through Indo-European languages, from the Vedic devas to the Latin deus, yet his story is uniquely Baltic, told in the whispers of folk songs and the silence of sacred groves.

Dievas is portrayed as modest, fair, chaste, and delicate. He tests humans' goodness and generosity, rewarding the virtuous and punishing the wicked. He is a creator and ruler but often non-interventionist (deus otiosus).

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Proto-Baltic *Deivas, from Proto-Indo-European *deywós, derived from *dyēus, the sky-father deity. Root *dei- means 'to shine'.

Pronunciation — Latvian: /diɛvs/; Lithuanian: /ˈdiəvɐs/

Aliases — Dievas, Debestēvs, Dīvs, Diews, Deivas, Praamžius

Ownership — Baltic peoples: Latvians, Lithuanians, Latgalians, Old Prussians, Yotvingians

Powers
Creation

Dievas created the universe, often in collaboration or conflict with Velns (the devil/trickster).

Limit: Sometimes needs help or is tricked by Velns.

Omnipotence

As supreme god, he is all-powerful, but he rarely uses his power directly.

Limit: Often depicted as passive, allowing other deities to act.

Omniscience

He knows all and tests humans to reveal their true nature.

Limit: Not explicitly stated, but implied in testing narratives.

Shapeshifting

He can manifest in various forms, including human, animal, bird, or plant.

Limit: Mostly appears as an old man in recorded tales.

Sky and Light Control

He is the god of the sky and light, controlling celestial phenomena.

Limit: Not fully detailed in surviving myths.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Described in later Christian texts as appearing in human form, often as an old sage or beggar. Earlier sources may have envisioned him as the sky itself.

Symbols — Sky, light, oak tree (possibly), the god-tree Diemedis (southernwood). No specific iconography is attested from pre-Christian times.

Domains — Sky, heavens, the universe. He is associated with light, prosperity, wealth, and time (as Praamžius).

Accounts I
Creation of the UniverseBaltic mythology

Pre-Christian

Dievas and his twin brother Velns fought on a rock in the ocean, which became the central point of the universe. Their struggle shaped the world. The world was created, and the two became adversaries.

Accounts II
The Testing BeggarLithuanian folk tales

Unknown (recorded 19th-20th century)

Dievas descends from heaven in the form of an old beggar to test humanity's goodness and generosity. Those who help him are rewarded; those who turn him away are punished. Moral lessons about hospitality and kindness.

The Heavenly TwinsBaltic mythology

Pre-Christian

Dievas has two sons, the Dievo sūneliai (Lithuanian) or Dieva dēli (Latvian), known as the Heavenly Twins. They are associated with the morning and evening stars or other celestial phenomena. The twins play roles in various myths and are sometimes linked to the sun and moon.
