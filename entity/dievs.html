Who It Is

Dievs is the supreme sky god of Baltic (especially Latvian) mythology, the ruler and father of the pantheon. His name shares a root with the Indo-European *Dyēus (cf. Zeus, Jupiter, Dyaus Pita), making Dievs one of the oldest continuously worshipped sky-father deities in Europe. Unlike his Greek or Roman counterparts, Dievs is not an anthropomorphic warlord in the clouds — he is a benevolent but distant farmer-king, living on a celestial farmstead (Dieva sēta) in the Milky Way, which the Latvians called the 'Belt of Dievs.' He owns silver-studded horses, golden cattle, and a sword that never misses. Dievs rides his gray stallion across the sky each day, maintaining cosmic order. He dispenses justice and ensures the harvest, but he rarely intervenes directly, letting Laima weave fate and Perkūns wield thunder. In the Latvian dainas (a vast corpus of folk verses), Dievs appears as a father figure who helps orphans, punishes the arrogant, and welcomes the righteous dead to his celestial farm. His relationship with humanity is one of respectful distance — he is the ultimate guarantor of morality, not a capricious meddler.

Attested in Latvian dainas (folk songs); Lithuanian oral traditionEra Pre-Christian (Bronze Age through medieval Baltic paganism)
