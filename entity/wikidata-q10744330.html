Who It Is

In the heart of Vietnam, a divine eye watches over a faith that seeks to unite the world's religions into one cosmic symphony. Cao Đài, the 'Highest Power,' emerged in the 1920s as a syncretic revelation, promising a path to universal salvation through the convergence of Buddhism, Christianity, Taoism, and Confucianism. But who is this deity, and how did a small, colonial-era movement grow into a global faith with millions of followers?

Cao Đài is described as a loving, merciful, and just deity who communicates with humanity through spirit mediums. He is believed to have revealed the Cao Đài doctrine to establish a new era of universal salvation, emphasizing moral living, vegetarianism, and the unity of all religions.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Cao Đài' literally means 'high tower' or 'high palace' in Vietnamese, often interpreted as 'Highest Power' or 'Supreme Being.' It is also an epithet for God in the Cao Đài faith.

Pronunciation — kow dai (approximately 'kow' as in 'cow' with a rising tone, 'dai' as in 'dye' with a falling-rising tone)

Aliases — Đức Cao Đài, Ngọc Hoàng Thượng Đế, Cao Đài Tiên Ông, Đại Đạo Tam Kỳ Phổ Độ

Form & Signs

Appearance — Cao Đài is often depicted as a large Divine Eye, representing God's omnipresence and omniscience. The eye is typically shown within a triangle, symbolizing the three teachings (Buddhism, Taoism, Confucianism) or the three realms (heaven, earth, and hell).

Symbols — The Divine Eye is the central symbol of Cao Đài. It appears on altars, temples, and religious regalia. The color scheme often includes blue (representing the universe), red (representing humanity), and yellow (representing the divine). The left eye is used because the left is considered the side of the heart and spirit.

Domains — Cao Đài is believed to reside in the highest heaven, beyond all other deities. The Holy See in Tây Ninh is considered His earthly sanctuary, where His presence is especially strong.

Accounts I
The Foundation of Cao ĐàiCao Đài oral tradition and religious texts

1920s

In 1920, a group of Vietnamese civil servants began holding seances in Saigon. They received messages from a spirit identifying itself as Cao Đài, who revealed the new religion's doctrine. The first official revelation was received on Christmas Eve 1925, and the religion was formally established in 1926. The Cao Đài religion was founded, growing rapidly in southern Vietnam, and later became a significant political and social force.

Accounts II
The Divine Eye RevelationCao Đài religious texts and oral tradition

1920s-1930s

Cao Đài revealed Himself to the mediums as a Divine Eye, symbolizing His all-seeing nature. This symbol became the central icon of the faith, used in temples and personal worship. The Divine Eye became the universal symbol of Cao Đài, unifying the faithful.

The Holy See ConstructionCao Đài history and temple records

1933-1955

The Holy See in Tây Ninh was built as the global center of Cao Đài. Its architecture blends Eastern and Western styles, with intricate carvings and a grand prayer hall. The construction was a collective effort of the faithful. The Holy See became a pilgrimage site and the administrative headquarters of the Cao Đài Church.
