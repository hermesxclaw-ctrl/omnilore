It was not a lion. It was not a leopard. It was a cat, but bigger than any cat that walks the earth. Gray as storm clouds, with a short tail, and it killed not for hunger but for pleasure.

Who It Is

The Mngwa ('strange one' in Swahili) is a cryptid giant cat reported from the coastal regions and interior of Tanzania. Unlike the Marozi, which is a spotted lion, the Mngwa is described as a cat of unprecedented size -- larger than a lion, with gray or brindled fur, a short tail, and a terrifying, almost supernatural ferocity. Swahili oral tradition speaks of the Mngwa as a specifically man-eating predator that hunts humans not for food but out of malevolent intent. During the 1920s and 1930s, a series of attacks were attributed to the Mngwa in villages along the Tanzanian coast, with colonial police conducting searches and finding tracks of a cat larger than any known species. The most famous account comes from British administrator W. Hichens, who documented a string of killings in the village of Mchinga in 1922. Footprints found at the scene measured larger than a lion's and showed a different pad pattern. A police expedition failed to find the creature. The Mngwa is sometimes equated with the Nunda or 'giant cat' of Tanzanian folklore. Cryptozoologists have proposed the Mngwa could be: a giant golden cat (Profelis aurata) of abnormal size (the golden cat is a forest-dwelling feline found in East Africa but normally weighs under 40 pounds); a descendant of the extinct saber-toothed cats; or a mutated lion or leopard. The Mngwa's description -- giant cat, brindled/gray coat, specific man-eating behavior -- does not cleanly match any known African felid, making it one of the more perplexing cryptid cats.
