Who It Is

Imagine you're walking home on a moonless night in the Visayan islands. The air is thick with the scent of rain and earth. From the darkness behind you, a voice—your own name, clear as a bell—cuts through the silence. You know you shouldn't answer. You've been told since you were a child. But the voice is so familiar, so insistent. That is the call of the Amalanhig, the risen one, and once you answer, it will never stop chasing you. It is not a ghost, and it is not a zombie. It is a corpse that has stood up from its grave with a purpose, and it will not rest until its business is done.

The Amalanhig is a nocturnal predator that primarily hunts humans and animals. It is known for its distinctive hunting method: it will call out a person's name. If the person responds, the Amalanhig will chase them. It is said to be incredibly fast despite its stiff-legged gait, and it can only be stopped if the victim runs in a zigzag pattern or hides in a place the Amalanhig cannot reach, such as a body of water or a high platform. It is also repelled by garlic, salt, and the sound of a rooster's crow.

Powers
Name Calling

The Amalanhig can call out the name of its intended victim. This is not a simple sound; it is a supernatural lure that compels the victim to respond. Once a person responds, the Amalanhig is able to track them and is compelled to hunt them down.

Limit: The victim must respond for the power to take full effect. Ignoring the call or not answering is a known defense.

Supernatural Speed and Strength

Despite its rigid, knee-less gait, the Amalanhig is said to be able to outrun a fleeing human. It also possesses great strength, able to break down doors and crush its prey.

Limit: It cannot bend its knees, so it cannot climb stairs or jump. It is also unable to cross water.

Undead Resilience

As an undead creature, the Amalanhig is already dead and cannot be killed by conventional means. It feels no pain and will continue to pursue its prey even after sustaining massive injuries.

Limit: It can be destroyed by decapitation, burning the body, or by exposing it to sunlight until it turns to ash. Holy water and religious icons are also said to be effective.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Amalanhig is described as a corpse that has risen from its grave. It is often depicted as a decrepit, rotting figure, sometimes with a elongated neck or a missing head, though the core description is of a shambling, undead humanoid. Its most notable feature is its inability to bend its knees, forcing it to walk with a stiff, unnatural gait. It is said to emit a foul odor of decay.

Symbols — The Amalanhig symbolizes the fear of the dead returning, the violation of the sacred boundary of the grave, and the consequences of improper burial rites. It represents the unresolved dead, those who are not at peace and who return to prey on the living.

Domains — Cemeteries, burial grounds, and the boundaries between the living world and the spirit world. It is a creature of the night and is most active during the hours of darkness.

Accounts I
The Unfinished Business of the DeadVisayan Oral Tradition

Pre-colonial to present

The most common narrative is that the Amalanhig is created when a person dies with unfinished business, such as a vendetta, a promise unfulfilled, or a great wrong done to them in life. Their spirit is unable to find peace and instead animates their corpse to seek resolution or revenge. They rise from their grave and terrorize the living, particularly those connected to their unfinished business. The community must identify the source of the unrest, perform proper rituals to appease the spirit, or in extreme cases, exhume and burn the corpse to destroy the Amalanhig.

Accounts II
The Stiff-Legged ChaseVisayan Oral Tradition

Pre-colonial to present

A common anecdote involves a person walking home at night who hears their name called from behind. They turn and see the Amalanhig emerging from the shadows. The chase begins. The victim, remembering the old stories, runs in a zigzag pattern, or makes for a river or the sea. The Amalanhig, unable to bend its knees or cross water, is forced to give up the chase as the sun begins to rise. The victim survives by using their knowledge of the creature's weaknesses.

The Amalanhig is the same as a zombie. — While both are undead, the Amalanhig is not a mindless, plague-spreading creature like the modern pop-culture zombie. It retains its intelligence, has a specific purpose (often revenge or unfinished business), and possesses specific supernatural abilities like the name-calling lure. It is more akin to a vengeful spirit inhabiting a corpse.

The Amalanhig can be killed by shooting it. — Traditional folklore says that the Amalanhig is already dead and cannot be killed by conventional means. It can only be destroyed by decapitation, burning, or exposure to sunlight.

The Amalanhig is a vampire. — The Amalanhig is not a vampire. It does not exclusively drink blood. It is a flesh-eating monster that preys on humans and animals. While it shares some traits with vampires (undead, nocturnal, repelled by garlic), it is a distinct creature in Philippine folklore.
