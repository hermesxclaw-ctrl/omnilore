Who It Is

Tsagaan Übgen ('White Old Man') is a beloved Mongolian nature and longevity deity, depicted as a jolly, white-bearded old man with a long white robe and a staff (often topped with a dragon's head or a Buddhist symbol). He is the master of all wild animals and the guardian of the mountains, forests, and waters. Tsagaan Übgen embodies health, long life, prosperity, and harmony with nature. He can be found in both shamanic and Buddhist contexts — he is sometimes considered a local manifestation of the Buddhist deity Jambhala or a spirit converted and blessed by Buddha. Unlike many Mongolian spirits who are fearsome or austere, Tsagaan Übgen is warm, humorous, and generous. He rewards those who respect nature with long life and abundant herds, but those who hunt recklessly or pollute the land find their animals lost and their families struck with illness. Tsagaan Übgen is also the patron of the elderly and the guardian of family lineages. In art, he is shown surrounded by deer, birds, and other wild creatures, often seated at the base of a pine tree. During the Tsagaan Sar (White Moon / Mongolian Lunar New Year), offerings are made to Tsagaan Übgen for a prosperous year ahead. His legends share similarities with the Japanese Jurojin, the Chinese Shou Lao, and the Tibetan Mi-tshe-ring.

Attested in Mongolian oral tradition, shamanic belief, later syncretized with Tibetan BuddhismEra Pre-colonial shamanic origins, integrated into Buddhist practice by the 17th-18th century
