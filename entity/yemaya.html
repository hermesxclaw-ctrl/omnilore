Who It Is

Yemaya is a complex and multifaceted deity who is worthy of further study and exploration.

Yemaya is said to be motherly and protective, caring deeply for all her children and comforting them in times of sorrow. She is also said to be able to cure infertility in women, and is associated with the power to bring life and fertility.

Etymology — Derived from the Yoruba language, with 'Yem' meaning 'mother' and 'aya' meaning 'water'

Form & Signs

Appearance — Yemaya is often depicted as a motherly figure, with a gentle and nurturing appearance. She is associated with the ocean and rivers, and is often shown with a crown or other regalia that reflects her status as a powerful deity.

Symbols — Yemaya is associated with cowrie shells, which represent her wealth and abundance. She is also associated with the color blue, which reflects her connection to the ocean.

Domains — Yemaya is associated with the ocean and rivers, and is said to have dominion over the waters. She is also associated with the land and the fertility of the earth.

Accounts I
The Story of Yemaya and the Ogun RiverYoruba oral tradition

Pre-colonial

Yemaya is said to have created the Ogun River, which is a major river in Nigeria. She is associated with the river and is said to have the power to control its waters. The creation of the Ogun River had a significant impact on the Yoruba people and their way of life.
