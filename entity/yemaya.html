Who It Is

Yemaya (Yemoja, Yemanjá, Iemanjá) is the Yoruba orisha of the ocean, motherhood, fertility, and the primordial waters from which all life emerged. Her name derives from 'Yeye omo eja' — 'Mother whose children are fish' — reflecting her status as the great maternal force who nurtures all living beings. She is one of the most ancient and universally beloved orishas, embodying the ocean's dual nature: nurturing and life-giving, yet vast, mysterious, and capable of devastating power.

Physically, Yemaya is depicted as a majestic, full-figured mother goddess, often dressed in blue and white — her sacred colors — with seven skirts representing the seven seas. She wears cowrie shells and pearls, and her imagery frequently includes fish, waves, the crescent moon, and the star. In Brazilian Candomblé, Iemanjá is portrayed as a beautiful mermaid-like figure emerging from the sea; in Cuban Santería, Yemayá is the supreme mother whose image is often represented in Catholic churches as Our Lady of Regla, the patroness of Havana's harbor. Her physical form conveys abundance, protection, and the weight of maternal love.

Yemaya's origin myth places her at the very beginning of creation. In Yoruba cosmology, she is the daughter of Olokun (the god of the deep ocean) and, in some traditions, the mother of many other orishas — including Ogun, Shango, Oya, and Oshun. Her waters broke to birth the sun, the moon, and the stars, and the rivers of the world flow from her body. She is the archetypal mother: nurturing, protective, and fiercely devoted to her children. Yet when her children are threatened, the gentle mother becomes the raging ocean — her fury manifesting as storms, tidal waves, and shipwrecks.

In the Afro-Caribbean diaspora, Yemaya's worship is especially strong in Brazil, where the Festa de Iemanjá on February 2nd draws millions to the beaches. Devotees cast flowers, perfume, mirrors, and jewelry into the sea as offerings. Similar festivals occur in Cuba, where Yemayá is celebrated on September 7th. She is the patroness of fishermen, sailors, pregnant women, and anyone who seeks the comfort of a mother's embrace. Her offerings include watermelon, molasses, whole fish, blue candles, and all things of the sea. Her sacred number is seven, and her children are said to have an emotional, nurturing nature and a deep love of the ocean. In Santería initiation ceremonies, she is one of the most commonly received orishas, and her presence brings healing, abundance, and emotional stability.
