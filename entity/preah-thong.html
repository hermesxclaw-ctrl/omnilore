Archive Dossier — Identity

Preah Thong

Pronunciation PRAY-uh TONG (approximate; Khmer: ព្រះថោង)

Known As

Preah ThongPreah ThaongKaundinyaKaudinyaHun-tien

Etymology

Preah Thong (ព្រះថោង) is often interpreted as 'the great lord' or 'the noble one'; 'Thong' may relate to 'gold' or 'shining' in some interpretations, though this is uncertain. The name is sometimes linked to the Indian sage Kaundinya, whose legend parallels the Khmer myth.

Cultural Ownership

Cambodian (Khmer) culture; also shared with Mon-Khmer and Southeast Asian traditions, particularly the legend of the founding of the Funan kingdom.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars identify Preah Thong with the Indian Brahmin Kaundinya who, according to Chinese records, founded Funan in the 1st century CE. Others argue he is a purely local legendary figure, and the identification may be a later synthesis.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the mist-shrouded dawn of Khmer legend, a foreign prince named Preah Thong stepped ashore on a land that would become Cambodia—and there he met a serpent princess whose love would bind a kingdom to the waters. Their union, half-human and half-naga, is the mythic foundation of a people, a story of love, betrayal, and the eternal dance between land and river.

The bow of destiny brought me across the sea to a land ruled by the serpent. I did not conquer it — I married it, and Cambodia was born.

Who It Is

Preah Thong (known as Kaundinya in Indian sources and Chinese records) is the legendary Brahmin who founded the first Cambodian kingdom by marrying the naga princess Neang Kakey. According to the myth preserved in Khmer chronicles and corroborated by Chinese envoys who visited Funan in the 3rd century CE, Preah Thong was an Indian Brahmin who dreamed of a divine bow that would lead him to his destiny. He sailed east and encountered the Naga Princess on the coast of what is now Cambodia. The Naga King, her father, tested Preah Thong by swallowing the land and flooding the sea — when Preah Thong refused to flee, proving his courage, the Naga King withdrew the waters, creating the fertile Cambodian floodplains, and gave his daughter in marriage. Preah Thong is credited with introducing Indian concepts of kingship, writing, law, and statecraft to the Khmer people. The city he founded became the nucleus of the Funan kingdom, the first Indianized state in Southeast Asia. Preah Thong's story embodies the synthesis of Indian and indigenous traditions that defined Khmer civilization. The legend is memorialized in the bas-reliefs of Angkor Wat and is still taught to Cambodian schoolchildren as the origin of their nation.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Preah Thong is typically depicted as a handsome young prince, often in royal attire, sometimes with a spear or sword. In some artistic representations, he is shown with the naga princess Neang Neak, with her in serpent form or as a beautiful woman.

Symbols & Iconography

The naga (serpent) is central; the spear he thrusts into the ground to claim the land is a symbol of his authority. The white cloth he spreads over the naga princess's tail is sometimes interpreted as a symbol of civilization or domestication of the wild.

Behaviour & Personality

He is portrayed as adventurous, brave, and respectful of the supernatural. He accepts the naga princess's hand and agrees to her father's conditions, showing diplomacy and adaptability.

Domains & Habitats

The story is set on the coast of Cambodia, near the sea and rivers; the naga kingdom is underwater. The land he claims becomes the kingdom of Funan, later associated with the Khmer Empire.

Version Differences

There are multiple versions: in some, Preah Thong is a prince from India; in others, he is a local hero. Some versions emphasize the naga father's demand that Preah Thong build a city; others focus on the marriage and the birth of a lineage. The Chinese records of Kaundinya and Liuye are a parallel but distinct account.

Attested in Cambodian oral tradition, Khmer chronicles, Chinese historical records (Kang Tai, 3rd century CE)Era c. 1st-5th century CE (legendary, associated with the founding of Funan)

Archive Dossier — Powers

Divine favor and legitimacy

Source Version Common to all versions

Description

As the founder of a royal lineage, Preah Thong is endowed with the favor of the naga deities, granting him the right to rule. His union with the naga princess gives him supernatural protection and connection to the waters.

Limitations

This is a mythical attribute, not a personal power; it legitimizes his descendants.

Contradictions

Some versions do not emphasize any special powers, merely his role as a culture hero.

Skill in battle and statecraft

Source Version General heroic attributes

Description

He is often depicted as a capable leader who can defeat enemies and establish a kingdom.

Limitations

Not elaborated in most versions.

Contradictions

None noted.

Type spouseSource Khmer legend

Description

Naga princess, daughter of the naga king. Their marriage is the central event of the myth, uniting human and naga worlds.
