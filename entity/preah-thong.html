Who It Is

In the mist-shrouded dawn of Khmer legend, a foreign prince named Preah Thong stepped ashore on a land that would become Cambodia—and there he met a serpent princess whose love would bind a kingdom to the waters. Their union, half-human and half-naga, is the mythic foundation of a people, a story of love, betrayal, and the eternal dance between land and river.

He is portrayed as adventurous, brave, and respectful of the supernatural. He accepts the naga princess's hand and agrees to her father's conditions, showing diplomacy and adaptability.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Preah Thong (ព្រះថោង) is often interpreted as 'the great lord' or 'the noble one'; 'Thong' may relate to 'gold' or 'shining' in some interpretations, though this is uncertain. The name is sometimes linked to the Indian sage Kaundinya, whose legend parallels the Khmer myth.

Pronunciation — PRAY-uh TONG (approximate; Khmer: ព្រះថោង)

Aliases — Preah Thong, Preah Thaong, Kaundinya, Kaudinya, Hun-tien

Ownership — Cambodian (Khmer) culture; also shared with Mon-Khmer and Southeast Asian traditions, particularly the legend of the founding of the Funan kingdom.

Powers
Divine favor and legitimacy

As the founder of a royal lineage, Preah Thong is endowed with the favor of the naga deities, granting him the right to rule. His union with the naga princess gives him supernatural protection and connection to the waters.

Limit: This is a mythical attribute, not a personal power; it legitimizes his descendants.

Skill in battle and statecraft

He is often depicted as a capable leader who can defeat enemies and establish a kingdom.

Limit: Not elaborated in most versions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Preah Thong is typically depicted as a handsome young prince, often in royal attire, sometimes with a spear or sword. In some artistic representations, he is shown with the naga princess Neang Neak, with her in serpent form or as a beautiful woman.

Symbols — The naga (serpent) is central; the spear he thrusts into the ground to claim the land is a symbol of his authority. The white cloth he spreads over the naga princess's tail is sometimes interpreted as a symbol of civilization or domestication of the wild.

Domains — The story is set on the coast of Cambodia, near the sea and rivers; the naga kingdom is underwater. The land he claims becomes the kingdom of Funan, later associated with the Khmer Empire.

Accounts I
The Meeting of Preah Thong and Neang NeakKhmer oral tradition and royal chronicles

Legendary; possibly 1st century CE or earlier

Preah Thong, a prince from a foreign land (often said to be India), arrives on the coast of Cambodia. He encounters Neang Neak, the daughter of the naga king, who is bathing in the sea. They fall in love and marry. Her father, the naga king, gives his blessing and drinks the water of the land to transform it into a habitable country. He also demands that Preah Thong build a city, which becomes the capital of Funan. The couple's descendants become the Khmer kings. The founding of the kingdom of Funan, the establishment of the royal lineage, and the origin of the Cambodian people's connection to the naga.

Accounts II
The Parallel Legend of Kaundinya and LiuyeChinese historical records (e.g., Book of Liang)

1st century CE (as recorded later)

A foreigner named Kaundinya (Hun-tien) receives a divine arrow in a dream and sails to Funan. He meets a local queen named Liuye (Willow Leaf), who initially attacks him but then submits and marries him. Kaundinya becomes king and establishes the kingdom. The founding of Funan according to Chinese sources.
