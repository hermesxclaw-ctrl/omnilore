The Approach

In the quiet corners of Afro-Brazilian temples, where the smoke of incense curls around candles and the murmur of prayers rises with the tide, there appears an old Black man leaning on a cane. He is called Preto Velho — the Old Black One — and he carries the weight of centuries of slavery, yet his eyes hold a gentle wisdom and his voice a patient forgiveness. He does not come to judge, but to counsel, to heal, and to remind that even the most broken past can give rise to profound spiritual strength.

Content advisory. This entry discusses the legacy of slavery, racial oppression, and the historical trauma of the African diaspora in Brazil. The figure of Preto Velho emerges from this painful history, and the text handles the subject with respect and historical context. Some readers may find the references to slavery and its aftermath emotionally challenging.
Identity

Preto Velho

Pronunciation PREH-too VEL-yoo (Brazilian Portuguese)

Known As

Pai JoãoPai JoaquimVovôVovô CongoPai BeneditoPai FranciscoPai Tomás

Etymology

Portuguese for 'Old Black Man' or 'Old Black One'. 'Preto' is an older Portuguese term for a Black person, now often considered dated or offensive in Brazil; 'Velho' means old. The name reflects the archetype's appearance as an elderly enslaved African.

Cultural Ownership

Afro-Brazilian religions, particularly Umbanda, with roots in Candomblé and the broader African diaspora in Brazil. Preto Velho is a spiritual entity venerated primarily in Umbanda, and also appears in some other Afro-Brazilian traditions.

Disputed Identifications

Preto Velho is sometimes confused with other spirits, such as 'Pai João' or 'Vovô', and in popular culture may be conflated with the figure of 'Pai Tomás' (Uncle Tom) from Harriet Beecher Stowe's novel, though this is a later literary association and not part of the original Umbanda tradition. Some scholars debate whether Preto Velho is a distinct archetype or a subcategory of the broader 'Old Black' spirits, and there are variations in how different Umbanda houses interpret and work with these entities.

Description

Appearance

Preto Velho is typically described as an elderly Black man, often with white or gray hair, a beard, and a hunched posture. He is usually depicted wearing simple, worn clothing, reminiscent of enslaved Africans: often a white shirt and trousers, sometimes with a straw hat. He may lean on a wooden cane or staff, and his eyes are said to be kind and wise. In some representations, he carries a pipe or a small pouch of herbs.

Symbols & Iconography

Common symbols associated with Preto Velho include the cane (representing age and support), the straw hat, a pipe (associated with wisdom and contemplation), and sometimes a small fire or candle, symbolizing the hearth and spiritual light. In Umbanda altars, his image may be a small statue or a drawing, often placed alongside other entities. His colors are typically white, sometimes with touches of black or gold.

Behaviour & Personality

Preto Velho is known for his humility, patience, kindness, and wisdom. He speaks slowly and gently, often using proverbs and simple language. He is considered a counselor and healer, offering guidance on personal and spiritual matters. He is also associated with forgiveness and the idea of transcending past suffering. In rituals, mediums incorporating Preto Velho may adopt a hunched posture, speak with a slow, elderly voice, and use a cane. He is often seen smoking a pipe, which is part of his ritual persona.

Domains & Habitats

Preto Velho is believed to dwell in a spiritual realm, often associated with the 'Aruanda' — a mythical African homeland or spiritual paradise in Umbanda cosmology. He is also connected to the earth, forests, and ancestral spirits. He is invoked during Umbanda ceremonies, particularly in the 'linha' (line) of the 'Pretos Velhos', which is part of the 'linha de Umbanda'. He works in the realm of healing, wisdom, and ancestral guidance.

Version Differences

There are variations in how Preto Velho is understood across different Umbanda houses and regions. Some traditions emphasize his connection to African ancestors, while others see him as a generic spirit of the enslaved. Some houses may have specific named Preto Velhos, such as 'Pai João de Angola' or 'Pai Joaquim de Aruanda', each with their own stories and attributes. In some Candomblé contexts, similar figures may appear, but they are not identical to the Umbanda Preto Velho. The representation and role can vary significantly, and some groups may downplay or reinterpret the figure in modern contexts.

Summary

A story tells of a cruel slave master who, after death, sought help from the spirits. Pai João, a Preto Velho, appeared and, instead of seeking revenge, offered forgiveness and guidance, teaching the master about compassion and humility. This story illustrates the Preto Velho's role as a spirit of forgiveness and reconciliation.

Relationships

Other Umbanda entities (e.g., Caboclos, Exus, Orixás)

Type spiritual peersSource Umbanda cosmology

Description

Preto Velho works alongside other spirits in the Umbanda pantheon. He is often seen as a complementary figure to the Caboclos (indigenous spirits) and the Orixás (deities). While Orixás are powerful forces of nature, Preto Velho is a human spirit who has evolved through suffering.

Version Note

The hierarchy and relationships vary among Umbanda houses.

Conflicting Accounts

Some traditions place Preto Velho under the authority of a specific Orixá, such as Oxalá or Xangô, while others see him as independent.

Legacy of slavery

Source Historical records and academic scholarship

Description

Preto Velho's origin is rooted in the brutal history of the transatlantic slave trade and the enslavement of Africans in Brazil. Millions of Africans were forcibly brought to Brazil, subjected to horrific conditions, and denied their humanity. The figure of Preto Velho embodies the memory of this suffering and the resilience of those who endured it.

Scholarly Context

Historians such as Laurentino Gomes and others have documented the scale and brutality of slavery in Brazil. This history is essential for understanding the cultural significance of Preto Velho.

Racial stereotyping and cultural appropriation

Source Cultural criticism and academic analysis

Description

In some popular representations, Preto Velho has been reduced to a stereotype of the passive, submissive enslaved person, reinforcing harmful racial tropes. Additionally, elements of Umbanda have been appropriated by non-Afro-Brazilian groups without proper understanding or respect.

Scholarly Context

Scholars and activists have criticized the romanticization of slavery and the commodification of Afro-Brazilian religious practices. This is an ongoing issue.

Violence and oppression

Source Historical accounts and testimonies

Description

The historical context of Preto Velho includes the physical and psychological violence of slavery, including beatings, family separation, and dehumanization. While Preto Velho himself is a spirit of peace, his backstory is inseparable from this violence.

Scholarly Context

These accounts are well-documented in Brazilian history and are part of the national trauma.

Contradictions & Disputes

Origin of Preto Velho

Position A

Preto Velho is a direct representation of enslaved African ancestors.

Position A — evidence

Oral traditions and cultural memory link Preto Velho to the enslaved Africans.

Position B

Preto Velho is a symbolic archetype created within Umbanda, not tied to specific historical individuals.

Position B — evidence

Scholars note that Umbanda was formalized in the early 20th century, and Preto Velho may be a composite figure.

Scholarly Consensus

There is no consensus; many scholars see Preto Velho as a complex symbol that draws on historical memory but is not a literal ancestor.

Role in hierarchy

Position A

Preto Velho is subordinate to the Orixás.

Position A — evidence

Some Umbanda houses place Preto Velho under the guidance of an Orixá.

Position B

Preto Velho is an independent spirit with his own authority.

Position B — evidence

Other houses treat Preto Velho as a high-level spirit who can work independently.

Scholarly Consensus

Practices vary, and there is no universal hierarchy.

Gender and specific identities

Position A

Preto Velho is exclusively male.

Position A — evidence

The name 'Preto Velho' is masculine.

Position B

There are also female spirits called Pretas Velhas, but they are distinct.

Position B — evidence

In practice, female equivalents exist, such as 'Preta Velha' or 'Vovó'.

Scholarly Consensus

The tradition includes both male and female elder spirits, but the male figure is more prominent in popular imagination.
