Who They Are

Tezcatlipoca is one of the four creator gods of Aztec cosmology and arguably the most complex, powerful, and feared deity in the Mexica pantheon. His name means 'Smoking Mirror,' referring to his primary attribute — a black obsidian mirror that he wears either in place of a foot (which he lost in his primordial battle with the Earth Monster Cipactli) or on his chest. This mirror is his instrument of omniscience: it reflects the entire world, showing him all deeds, thoughts, and the darkest truths of every soul. Tezcatlipoca is the god of the night sky, conflict, sorcery, fate, temptation, and — paradoxically — the bringer of both wealth and destruction. He is the 'Enemy of Both Sides,' the cosmic force who destroys to create and tempts to reveal.

Physically, Tezcatlipoca is depicted in Aztec codices as a powerful, often dark-skinned figure with yellow and black facial stripes (his war paint). His missing right foot is replaced by an obsidian mirror or a serpent; in some representations, a bone protrudes from the stump. He wears a jaguar pelt (the jaguar being his nagual, or animal spirit), and his headdress features heron feathers and stars. He carries a shield with circular mirrors and an atlatl (spear-thrower). His general appearance communicates dark majesty, danger, and irresistible allure.

Tezcatlipoca's role in Aztec creation mythology is central. In the Aztec conception of cosmic history, the world has passed through five suns (eras), each ending in catastrophe. Tezcatlipoca ruled the First Sun — the Sun of the Earth (Nahui Ocelotl) — which ended when his rival Quetzalcoatl struck him down from the sky, causing the world to be devoured by jaguars. This rivalry between Tezcatlipoca (darkness, night, conflict) and Quetzalcoatl (light, wisdom, civilization) is the fundamental mythic axis of Aztec religion — a dialectical relationship in which neither can permanently triumph because both are necessary for cosmic balance.

Tezcatlipoca's powers are vast and terrifying. He can appear anywhere, in any form — a jaguar stalking through the night, a stranger at a crossroads, a terrifying apparition, or a seductive tempter. He tests human virtue by offering wealth, power, and pleasure, and then judges the response. In the Florentine Codex, he is described as a being who 'gave men poverty and misery, riches and prosperity' — the capricious god of fortune and misfortune. Kings prayed to him for success in war, and his favor was essential for political legitimacy. Slaves destined for sacrifice were sometimes called his 'beloved sons.'

Despite his dark reputation, Tezcatlipoca is not simply evil. He is the god of truth revealed through confrontation with shadow. The smoking mirror shows people as they truly are, stripped of illusion. He is the god of the yaoyotl (sacred war, 'Flowery War'), which the Aztecs understood not simply as combat but as a cosmic ritual maintaining the balance of the universe. Destruction through Tezcatlipoca is always creative destruction — the clearing away of what is false or stagnant to make way for renewal.
