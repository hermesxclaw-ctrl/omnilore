The Approach

In the lethal chess game of Kira's world, where names are written in a notebook to end lives, two successors to the great detective L emerge from the shadows of Wammy's House. One, Near, is a calm, methodical prodigy. The other, Mello, is a volatile, chocolate-obsessed renegade willing to burn his own bridges—and his own face—to catch the world's most wanted killer. His path is paved with abductions, underworld alliances, and a final, devastating gambit that changes the fate of the Kira investigation forever.

Content advisory. Contains themes of violence, kidnapping, organized crime, self-harm (burning), and death. Suitable for older teens and adults.

Etymology

The name 'Mello' is a pseudonym adopted by the character, derived from his real name 'Mihael Keehl'. The exact origin of the alias is not stated in the source material, but it is a common practice among the Wammy's House orphans to adopt aliases starting with a letter (L, M, N). 'Mello' may be a play on 'mellow' or simply a chosen name.

Cultural Ownership

Mello is a fictional character owned by Tsugumi Ohba, Takeshi Obata, and their publishers (Shueisha, Viz Media, etc.). He is part of the Death Note franchise.

Disputed Identifications

None. Mello is a distinct character with a clear identity in the Death Note canon. However, there is some debate among fans about his motivations and whether his actions were justified.

Description

Appearance

Mello is a young man with long, blond hair that he often keeps tied back. He has blue eyes and a scar on his left cheek, which he acquired from a self-inflicted burn. He typically wears dark, stylish clothing, including a leather jacket, and is often seen with a cross necklace. His appearance is distinctive and memorable.

Symbols & Iconography

Mello is often associated with chocolate bars, particularly a brand resembling Hershey's, which he is frequently seen eating. This has become a defining trait and a symbol of his character. His cross necklace is also a notable accessory, though its significance is not deeply explored.

Behaviour & Personality

Mello is impulsive, hot-headed, and highly competitive. He is driven by a strong desire to surpass his rival Near and to prove himself as L's true successor. Unlike Near's calm and analytical approach, Mello relies on bold, risky strategies and is willing to get his hands dirty. He is intelligent but often acts on emotion, leading to both successes and failures. He shows a capacity for loyalty and sacrifice, as seen in his final confrontation with Kira.

Domains & Habitats

Mello operates primarily in the criminal underworld, having allied with the mafia in Los Angeles. He is often shown in urban settings, safehouses, and criminal hideouts. He also has connections to Wammy's House, the orphanage for gifted children, but he left it to pursue his own path.

Version Differences

In the manga, Mello's story is more detailed, including his time with the mafia and his final sacrifice. The anime condenses some of these events but stays largely faithful. In the live-action films, Mello's role is significantly reduced or altered. In the 2006 films, he is not present; in the 2016 film, he appears as a different character with a different backstory.

Contradictions & Disputes

Mello's motivation

Position A

Mello is driven by a desire to surpass Near and prove himself as L's successor.

Position A — evidence

His rivalry with Near is a central theme; he often acts impulsively to gain an advantage over Near.

Position B

Mello is driven by a genuine desire for justice and to catch Kira.

Position B — evidence

His final sacrifice suggests a selfless commitment to stopping Kira.

Scholarly Consensus

Most fans and critics agree that Mello's motivations are a mix of both, with his rivalry being a primary driver but his sense of justice also playing a role.

Effectiveness of Mello's methods

Position A

Mello's aggressive and reckless methods are ultimately ineffective and lead to his death.

Position A — evidence

Mello's mafia alliance is destroyed, and he is killed without directly catching Kira.

Position B

Mello's methods are essential to the success of the investigation, as they force Kira to act and reveal himself.

Position B — evidence

Mello's actions, particularly the kidnapping of Takada, provide Near with the evidence needed to expose Kira.

Scholarly Consensus

The narrative suggests that both are true: Mello's methods are risky and lead to his demise, but they are also instrumental in the final victory.
