Who It Is

Ntxwj (also called Ntsuj) is the household guardian spirit in Hmong religion, residing at the threshold or central pillar of the home. Every Hmong household maintains a relationship with its Ntxwj, who protects the family from harmful spirits and ensures harmony within the home. Neglecting the Ntxwj invites misfortune.

Attested in Oral Tradition / Household RitualsEra Ancient

Field Notes

۩ The Ntxwj is typically offered a small portion of every meal before anyone eats, poured onto the floor or placed on a special altar.

۩ When a Hmong family moves to a new house, a shaman performs a 'Ntxwj calling' ceremony to ensure the protective spirit follows them.

۩ Different Hmong clans may have different traditions regarding the Ntxwj, including variations in where the spirit resides in the home and what offerings are preferred.

Cultural Significance

The Ntxwj embodies the Hmong concept of sacred domestic space. Every aspect of the home is spiritually significant, and the Ntxwj maintains the boundary between the safe interior world of the family and the dangerous exterior world of spirits. The New Year festival (Noj Peb Caug) includes special offerings to the Ntxwj, and weddings involve rituals introducing new spouses to the household spirit. In the Hmong diaspora, maintaining the Ntxwj tradition has become an important way of preserving cultural identity and continuity across generations.
