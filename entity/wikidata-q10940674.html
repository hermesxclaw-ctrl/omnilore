Who It Is

Imagine being handed the keys to the kingdom, a divine mandate to conquer the world below, only to be so utterly distracted by a pretty face that you forget the entire mission for eight years. That is the story of Ame no Wakahiko, the 'Heavenly Young Prince' of Japanese myth, a deity whose tragic flaw was not malice, but a fatal case of wanderlust and romance. His tale is a cosmic 'what not to do' lesson, where a single, impulsive shot from a bow seals your own doom.

Ame no Wakahiko is portrayed as a deity who is initially obedient to the heavenly mandate but is ultimately swayed by earthly temptations, specifically the affections of a beautiful earthly woman. He is characterized by a fatal combination of ambition and gullibility, leading to his downfall.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is composed of 'Ame' (heaven) and 'Wakahiko' (young man or young prince). It translates literally to 'Heavenly Young Prince' or 'Young Man of Heaven.' The 'Waka' prefix is an honorific indicating youth and vigor, common in early Japanese naming conventions for deities and heroes.

Pronunciation — Ah-meh noh Wah-kah-hee-koh

Aliases — Ame-no-Wakahiko, Amano-Wakahiko, Wakahiko, Ame no Wakahiko no Kami, Ame-waka-hiko, Heavenly Young Prince

Ownership — Japanese (Shinto mythology, as recorded in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki)

Powers
Divine Messenger Authority

As a chosen envoy of the heavenly deities, he possessed the authority and divine mandate to travel between the realms of heaven and earth.

Limit: His authority was contingent on his obedience. Failure to fulfill his mission revoked his divine protection.

Archery

He is depicted as a skilled archer, capable of shooting a pheasant with a single arrow.

Limit: His skill is only demonstrated against a bird, not in combat against another deity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The texts do not provide a physical description. As a heavenly deity, he is implied to be radiant and noble, but specifics are absent. In later artistic renditions, he is typically depicted as a youthful courtier or warrior in ancient Japanese dress, often holding a bow, which reflects his role as a hunter.

Symbols — His primary symbol is the bow and arrow, specifically the 'heavenly deer-arrow' (ame no kaguyama no maki) he carries. The pheasant, which is sent to check on him and which he kills, is also a key symbolic animal in his narrative.

Domains — He is associated with the heavenly realm (Takamagahara) as his point of origin, and the earthly realm (Ashihara no Nakatsukuni) as the place of his mission and demise. His specific domain, if any, is hunting, implied by his weapon.

Accounts I
The Failed MessengerKojiki and Nihon Shoki

Mythological age, as recorded in the early 8th century

The heavenly deities, led by Amaterasu, decide to pacify the earthly realm of Ashihara no Nakatsukuni. They dispatch Ame no Wakahiko as a messenger to subdue the unruly earthly deities. However, upon his arrival, Ame no Wakahiko forgets his divine mission. He marries Shitateru-hime, the daughter of the earthly deity Okuninushi, and does not report back to heaven for eight years. The heavenly deities, wondering at his long absence, send the pheasant Nakime to inquire about his progress. In a fit of anger or perhaps guilt, Ame no Wakahiko shoots the pheasant with his heavenly deer-arrow. The arrow, after killing the bird, flies all the way back to heaven and lands at the feet of the heavenly deities. Recognizing the arrow, the gods hurl it back down to earth, striking and killing Ame no Wakahiko. His funeral is held on earth, and his grieving wife's cries reach heaven, prompting his old friends and heavenly companions, Ame no Koyane and Ame no Futodama, to descend to earth to mourn him. Accounts II
The Return Arrow and the Death of Ame no WakahikoKojiki; Nihon Shoki; Philippi; Aston; Chamberlain.

Mythic age of the kami; recorded in the eighth-century chronicles.

Frustrated by Ame no Wakahiko's failure to return from his mission to the earth, the heavenly deities dispatched a pheasant, Nakime, to ask why he had not come home. When Ame no Wakahiko shot down the pheasant with a single arrow, the arrow, stained with the bird's blood, fell back up into the heavens and landed before Amaterasu and Takamimusubi. Recognizing the arrow as that which they had given to Ame no Wakahiko upon his departure, the two heavenly deities pronounced a curse and returned the shaft through the arrow-hole of the heavenly rock-house. The arrow plunged down and struck Ame no Wakahiko dead upon his bed, punishing his betrayal of the divine mission. The death of Ame no Wakahiko by his own returned arrow becomes a powerful warning about the fate of those who defy the heavenly mandate. It also accounts for the earthly kami leadership soon passing to Takemikazuchi and the completion of the pacification that Wakahiko had failed to carry out.

Ame no Wakahiko was a demon or evil god. — He was a heavenly deity who failed in his mission due to temptation and poor judgment. He is not depicted as inherently evil, but rather as weak and disobedient.

His story is a simple fable about obedience. — While obedience is a key theme, the story also explores the tension between the heavenly and earthly realms, the power of romantic attachment, and the tragic consequences of a single act of folly.
