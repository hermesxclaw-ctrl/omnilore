Form

Amatsuhikone is not described in detail in any surviving primary text. As a celestial deity (amatsukami), he would typically be depicted in later artistic traditions as a young male figure in Heian-period court dress, possibly holding a sword or a jewel. However, no canonical iconographic description exists in the Kojiki or Nihon Shoki.

Powers

Divine lineage As a son of Amaterasu, the sun goddess, and nephew of Susanoo, the storm god, Amatsuhikone possesses inherent divine authority and status within the Shinto pantheon. His lineage makes him a figure of considerable mythological importance. Kojiki and Nihon Shoki The texts do not describe any specific powers or abilities beyond his divine nature and lineage. None recorded in primary sources.

Ancestral authority Amatsuhikone is the ancestor of several important earthly lineages, giving him a form of spiritual authority over those who claim descent from him. This is primarily a genealogical power rather than a personal one. Kojiki and later genealogical records This power is only meaningful in the context of his descendants and their political/religious claims. The exact list of his descendants varies between sources.

Accounts

When Susanoo, the storm god, was banished from Takamagahara for his destructive behavior, he demanded to see his sister Amaterasu one last time. To prove his sincerity, he proposed a test: he would give her his Totsuka no Tsurugi (ten-span sword), and she would break it into three pieces, chew them, and blow them out as mist. From this mist, three goddesses were born. In return, Amaterasu gave Susanoo her jewel beads, which he likewise chewed and blew out, producing five male deities. Amatsuhikone was the second of these five divine sons, born from the jewel beads.

While the primary texts do not explicitly narrate Amatsuhikone's descent to Izumo, they strongly imply that his descendants came to rule the region. This connection is reinforced by the Izumo no Kuni no Miyatsuko no Kan'yogoto, a ritual text that traces the lineage of the Izumo rulers back to the celestial deities.

Scholarly Debates
Identity with Ame no Hohi
Amatsuhikone and Ame no Hohi are distinct deities, as listed separately in the Kojiki. ⇌ Some scholars suggest they may be variant names for the same deity, given their similar roles and associations with Izumo.
Evidence for A: The Kojiki lists them as separate individuals.
Evidence for B: Later genealogical records sometimes conflate them.
Birth order and method
Amatsuhikone was born from the jewel beads of Susanoo. ⇌ Some versions of the myth suggest the deities were born from the sword itself.
Evidence for A: The Kojiki clearly states the jewels were the source.
Evidence for B: The Nihon Shoki has some variations that could be interpreted differently.
What We Don't Know

The primary texts do not provide any detailed information about Amatsuhikone's appearance, personality, or individual deeds. His role is almost entirely genealogical. Further research into local shrine traditions and regional folklore in the Chugoku region might reveal more specific traditions associated with him. Access to Japanese-language academic sources would also be beneficial for a more complete picture.
