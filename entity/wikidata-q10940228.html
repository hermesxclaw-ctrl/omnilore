Who It Is

In the celestial court of Takamagahara, where the sun goddess Amaterasu held sway, a divine birth occurred that would echo through the ages. Five young princes emerged from the mist of a storm god's jewels, and among them was Amatsuhikone, the 'Heavenly Young Prince.' Yet, unlike his brother Ame no Oshihomimi, whose descendants would become the emperors of Japan, Amatsuhikone's story is one of quiet significance, a thread in the tapestry of divine lineage that connects heaven to the earthly realm of Izumo.

The primary texts do not attribute any specific personality traits to Amatsuhikone. He appears primarily as a genealogical figure, important for establishing divine lineages rather than for his own deeds. This suggests he may have been a more significant local deity whose individual mythology was not fully preserved in the central Yamato court records.

Powers
Divine lineage

As a son of Amaterasu, the sun goddess, and nephew of Susanoo, the storm god, Amatsuhikone possesses inherent divine authority and status within the Shinto pantheon. His lineage makes him a figure of considerable mythological importance.

Limit: The texts do not describe any specific powers or abilities beyond his divine nature and lineage.

Ancestral authority

Amatsuhikone is the ancestor of several important earthly lineages, giving him a form of spiritual authority over those who claim descent from him. This is primarily a genealogical power rather than a personal one.

Limit: This power is only meaningful in the context of his descendants and their political/religious claims.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Amatsuhikone is not described in detail in any surviving primary text. As a celestial deity (amatsukami), he would typically be depicted in later artistic traditions as a young male figure in Heian-period court dress, possibly holding a sword or a jewel. However, no canonical iconographic description exists in the Kojiki or Nihon Shoki.

Symbols — The sword (specifically the Totsuka no Tsurugi of Susanoo) is the instrument of his birth. As a son of Amaterasu, he is associated with the sun and celestial realm. Some local traditions associate him with agricultural fertility, particularly rice cultivation.

Domains — The celestial realm (Takamagahara) is his primary domain as a child of Amaterasu. However, his mythological role is tied to the earthly realm of Izumo, where his descendants were said to have ruled. He represents a bridge between the heavenly and earthly divine realms.

Accounts I
The Birth from the SwordKojiki, Book 1; Nihon Shoki, Book 1

Age of the Gods (mythological time)

When Susanoo, the storm god, was banished from Takamagahara for his destructive behavior, he demanded to see his sister Amaterasu one last time. To prove his sincerity, he proposed a test: he would give her his Totsuka no Tsurugi (ten-span sword), and she would break it into three pieces, chew them, and blow them out as mist. From this mist, three goddesses were born. In return, Amaterasu gave Susanoo her jewel beads, which he likewise chewed and blew out, producing five male deities. Amatsuhikone was the second of these five divine sons, born from the jewel beads. This divine birth established Amatsuhikone as a legitimate celestial deity and set the stage for the later descent of divine beings to the earthly realm. His lineage would come to be associated with the Izumo region.

Accounts II
The Descent to Izumo (Implied)Kojiki and local Izumo traditions

Age of the Gods (mythological time)

While the primary texts do not explicitly narrate Amatsuhikone's descent to Izumo, they strongly imply that his descendants came to rule the region. This connection is reinforced by the Izumo no Kuni no Miyatsuko no Kan'yogoto, a ritual text that traces the lineage of the Izumo rulers back to the celestial deities. This genealogical connection established the divine right of the Izumo rulers and linked the region's religious traditions to the central Yamato mythology.
