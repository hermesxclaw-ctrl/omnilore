Who It Is

Cautes is one of the two dadophoroi ('torch-bearers') who flank the central tauroctony scene in every Mithraeum. He stands with his torch raised upward, symbolizing the ascending light of the sun — day, spring, birth, and the diurnal cycle. Cautes is iconographically paired with Cautopates (the lowered torch), and together they represent the dualities of the cosmos: light/dark, life/death, sunrise/sunset, spring/winter. They are often depicted with crossed legs — a 'dancing' or 'running' posture that may derive from the Orientalizing style of the Anatolian Cybele cult, suggesting a Phrygian origin. The Cautes-Cautopates pair also encodes the equinoxes (the spring equinox = torch raised, the autumn equinox = torch lowered) and the path of the sun through the zodiac.
