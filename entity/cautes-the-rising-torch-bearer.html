Form & Signs

Appearance — Cautes is typically depicted as a youthful, beardless man, often with a radiant aura and an upraised torch.

Symbols — The upraised torch is a symbol of light, new life, and spiritual growth, while the torch-bearer's role as a divine attendant emphasizes their connection to the divine.

Domains — Cautes is primarily associated with the realm of light, which encompasses the sun, stars, and other celestial bodies.

Accounts I
The Birth of MithrasMithraic texts and art

Roman Empire

Cautes is often depicted in the birth scenes of Mithras, emphasizing the divine connection between the two figures. The birth of Mithras is seen as a symbol of the cyclical nature of life and death, with Cautes representing the light of new life.

Accounts II
The Bull-Slaying SceneMithraic art and texts

Roman Empire

Cautes and Cautopates are often depicted flanking Mithras in the bull-slaying scene, emphasizing their role as divine attendants. The bull-slaying scene is seen as a symbol of the struggle between good and evil, with Cautes representing the light of hope and renewal.
