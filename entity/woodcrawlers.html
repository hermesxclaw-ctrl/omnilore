Woodcrawlers

The Forest Crawlers, The Mimicking Predators

If you encounter a person in the woods who is standing perfectly still... who does not blink... and who asks you, in a voice that sounds like a recording, to follow them... do not follow them. Run.

Who It Is

Woodcrawlers are one of the primary Earth-based threats in Gemini Home Entertainment, introduced through a wildlife educational tape titled 'Wilderness Survival Guide.' They are described as creatures of unknown origin that have begun appearing in forested areas across North America. Woodcrawlers are remarkable — and terrifying — for their ability to mimic not just sounds but physical forms. They can rearrange their bodies to approximate human shapes, though imperfectly: limbs are too long, joints bend the wrong way, and faces are always slightly off — blank or featureless, or frozen in expressions that don't change. A tape shows a Woodcrawler attempting to mimic a park ranger: it has the uniform, the hat, and roughly the right shape, but it stands unnaturally still, and its 'voice' is just a recording of the ranger's voice played back in the wrong order. Woodcrawlers hunt by luring humans — they might imitate a lost child crying for help or a friendly hiker offering directions. When the victim gets close, the Woodcrawler attacks with incredible speed and strength, its body unfolding into a mass of limbs and teeth. The creatures are associated with the Iris and appear to be one of its means of extending influence on Earth. Their biology defies classification — they don't appear to be natural organisms so much as animated constructs, biological machines built for infiltration and predation. The name 'Woodcrawler' comes from their preferred habitat and their distinctive movement: they crawl along the ground with a motion that is insect-like but scaled to human size. Their design draws on body horror traditions and the universal fear of things that look human but aren't. In the Gemini Home Entertainment mythos, Woodcrawlers represent the front line of the Iris's invasion — the first sign that the wilderness is no longer safe, and perhaps never truly was.
