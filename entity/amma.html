Who It Is

Amma is the supreme creator god of the Dogon people of Mali, one of the most cosmologically elaborate religious systems in Africa. Dogon cosmology — famously documented by French anthropologist Marcel Griaule in 'Conversations with Ogotemmêli' (1948) and 'The Pale Fox' — presents a staggeringly complex vision of creation that has drawn comparisons to modern astrophysics due to its detailed knowledge of celestial bodies, including the Sirius star system. Amma sits at the apex of this system as the prime mover who created the universe through a combination of thought, speech, and craft.

In Dogon creation narrative, Amma existed alone in the void. His first creative act was to fashion a tiny seed — the po pilu — which contained within it the entire potential of the universe. This seed, when opened, released the spiral of creation, establishing the fundamental structure of space, time, and matter. Amma then created the Earth, conceived as a woman lying on her back, with an anthill as her clitoris and a termite mound as her womb. When Amma attempted to copulate with Earth, the termite mound blocked him; he destroyed it, and from this violent union came the jackal (Yurugu), the Pale Fox — an incomplete, rebellious being who introduced disorder and incompleteness into creation.

Amma's subsequent creation was more deliberate. Having learned from the failure with Earth, he created the Nommo — divine twin beings of water, speech, and order — who became his agents in completing and perfecting creation. The Nommo are central to Dogon cosmology: they taught humanity agriculture, metalworking, language, and weaving, and they sacrificed themselves so that their bodies could purify the world. Amma himself is rarely depicted directly in Dogon art, which focuses instead on the Nommo and on cosmological symbols (spirals, the kanaga mask representing the connection between sky and earth, etc.). He is understood as a transcendent creator who established the cosmic order and then delegated its maintenance to the Nommo and to humanity through ritual.

Amma's creation is fundamentally dualistic and spiraling. The universe is understood as a system of correspondences between the macrocosm (stars and planets) and the microcosm (the human body, the Dogon village, the granary). Every element of creation — from the arrangement of Dogon villages to the patterns of woven cloth — mirrors the cosmic order established by Amma. His name is invoked in all important Dogon rituals, particularly the Sigui ceremony, a once-every-sixty-years ritual that marks the renewal of the world and the Sirius cycle.

Dogon cosmology gained worldwide attention in the 20th century because of its apparent knowledge of Sirius B, a white dwarf star invisible to the naked eye, which the Dogon described in detail to Griaule — including its 50-year orbit, its extreme density, and its companion Sirius A. Whether this knowledge came from ancient observation, contact with ancient Egyptians or Sumerians, or more recent transmission remains debated, but it has cemented Amma's creation story as one of the most fascinating in world mythology.
