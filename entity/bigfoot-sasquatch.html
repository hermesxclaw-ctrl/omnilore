Who It Is

The legend of Bigfoot has captivated the imagination of people around the world, and continues to be a topic of interest and debate.

According to folklore, Bigfoot is a shy, solitary creature that avoids human contact. It is said to be a skilled hunter and gatherer, and is often associated with the forest and the wilderness.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Bigfoot' is derived from the creature's large size, while 'Sasquatch' is derived from the Salish language, specifically from the word 'Sasq' meaning 'wild' and 'Ts' meaning 'hairy' or 'bearded'.

Pronunciation — /ˈbɪɡˌfuːt/ (BIG-foo-t) or /ˈsæskwɒtʃ/ (SAS-kwotch)

Aliases — Sasquatch, Wild Man of the Woods, Big Fella, Hairy Man

Ownership — Bigfoot is a figure from modern Western folklore, primarily associated with Native American cultures, particularly the Salish and Algonquin peoples.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Bigfoot is typically described as a large, hairy, bipedal creature, ranging in height from 6 to 10 feet (1.8 to 3 meters) and weighing an estimated 500 to 800 pounds (227 to 363 kilograms). Its fur is often depicted as being dark brown or black, with a flat face, prominent forehead, and a strong, unpleasant odor.

Symbols — Bigfoot is often associated with symbols such as the footprint, which is said to be enormous and human-like. The creature is also often depicted in art and literature as a mysterious, elusive figure.

Domains — Bigfoot is said to inhabit the forests of North America, particularly in the Pacific Northwest region of the United States and Canada.
