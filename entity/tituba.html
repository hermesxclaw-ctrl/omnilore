Who It Is

An enslaved Indigenous American woman (c. 1692-1693) who was one of the first three people accused of witchcraft during the Salem witch trials. Enslaved by Reverend Samuel Parris of Salem Village, Tituba became a pivotal figure because she confessed to witchcraft when examined by authorities — a confession that gave credence to the entire witch-hunt. She claimed to have been approached by the Devil, to have signed his book, and implicated others. Unlike many who confessed and were executed, Tituba survived — she recanted, was imprisoned, and was later released after the hysteria subsided. Her testimony, combining elements of Caribbean folklore (she likely came from Barbados), Puritan demonology, and coerced confession, was instrumental in legitimizing the Salem trials in the eyes of the community.
