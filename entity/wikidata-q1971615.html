Who It Is

In the frozen vastness of the Arctic, where a single seal can mean the difference between life and death, there lurks a giantess of the deep whose mood dictates the fate of entire villages. She is Arnapkapfaaluk, the 'Big Woman' of the sea — a spirit not of gentle providence, but of terrifying caprice. While many cultures have a sea goddess who provides, Arnapkapfaaluk is the one who takes away, a chilling reminder that the ocean's bounty is never guaranteed.

Unlike the more nurturing Sedna, Arnapkapfaaluk is often portrayed as a dangerous and capricious spirit. She is said to control the sea mammals, particularly seals, and can withhold them from hunters, causing famine. She is feared rather than worshipped, and shamans would need to appease her or travel to her underwater dwelling to intercede on behalf of the community.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is from the Inuktitut language, roughly translating to 'Big Woman' or 'Great Woman'. It is a compound of 'arna' (woman) and a suffix indicating largeness or greatness.

Pronunciation — Ar-nak-kap-fa-luk (approximate; Inuktitut pronunciation varies by dialect)

Aliases — Arnaqkapfaaluk, Arnarkapfaaluk, The Big Woman, The Great Woman, The Sea Woman

Ownership — Inuit (Indigenous peoples of the Arctic regions of Canada, Greenland, and Alaska)

Powers
Control over Sea Mammals

Arnapkapfaaluk is believed to have dominion over the animals of the sea, particularly seals. She can release them to hunters or withhold them, leading to feast or famine.

Limit: Her power is tied to the actions and respect of humans; she can be appeased or confronted by powerful shamans (angakkuq).

Dwelling at the Bottom of the Sea

She is said to reside in a grand house at the bottom of the ocean, where she keeps the sea mammals in a pot or vessel, releasing them only when she chooses.

Limit: She is generally confined to her underwater realm, though her influence extends to the surface world through the availability of game.

Causing Storms and Misfortune

When angered, she can stir up the sea, create storms, and cause general misfortune for hunters and their families.

Limit: Her wrath is typically a response to human transgression or disrespect.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Arnapkapfaaluk is typically described as a giant woman, reflecting her name. Some accounts describe her as having only one eye, a common trait among powerful or fearsome beings in Inuit mythology. She is often associated with the sea and is said to dwell at the bottom of the ocean.

Symbols — The sea, seals, the ocean floor, a single eye, immense size.

Domains — The sea, marine animals (especially seals), hunting, famine, the underworld beneath the sea.

Accounts I
The Shaman's Journey to ArnapkapfaalukInuit oral tradition (various recorded versions)

Pre-contact to early 20th century

When the sea mammals fail to appear and the people face starvation, a shaman (angakkuq) may embark on a perilous spiritual journey to the bottom of the sea. There, he must confront Arnapkapfaaluk (or Sedna) in her underwater home. He must comb her hair, which is tangled and full of filth (a sign of human transgressions), and appease her anger. If successful, she releases the sea mammals, and the hunt can resume. Success restores the balance between humans and the sea, ending the famine. Failure could mean the shaman's death and continued suffering for the people.
