The lake is deep -- deeper than anyone knows. And in those depths, something ancient still swims. The Mapuche knew it. The settlers saw it. We call it Nahuelito.

Who It Is

Nahuelito is Argentina's most famous lake monster, reportedly inhabiting the deep, glacier-carved waters of Nahuel Huapi Lake in northern Patagonia. The lake, spanning over 200 square miles with depths exceeding 1,500 feet, is one of Argentina's largest and deepest lakes. The Mapuche indigenous people long told stories of a great creature living in the lake they called 'Cuero' (leather), which they associated with a malevolent water spirit. Modern sightings began in 1910, when a man named George Garrett reported seeing a creature with a long neck and humped back surface near the shore. In 1922, Dr. Clementi Onelli, director of the Buenos Aires Zoo, organized an expedition to search for the creature -- an event that captured international headlines. In 1960, the Argentine Navy allegedly tracked an unidentified submerged object in the lake for 18 days using sonar. Photographs from 1988 taken by an Argentine tourist appear to show a dark object with humps rising from the lake, reminiscent of classic lake monster imagery. Subsequent investigations have produced occasional photographs and sonar readings, though nothing conclusive. The creature is generally described as a plesiosaur-like animal 15 to 30 feet long with a swan-like neck, a bulky body, and humps. Skeptics suggest floating logs, wave phenomena, or large fish. Nahuelito has become a beloved part of Patagonian folklore and tourism.
