Who It Is

In a quiet, moss-choked hollow of the Land of Ooo, a tree smiles. Its bark splits into a grinning mouth, its branches become grasping arms, and its sweet, grandmotherly voice offers a bargain: a lock of hair for a miracle. The Tree Witch does not chase her victims — she waits for the desperate to come to her, and they always do.

Attested: The Tree Witch speaks in a soft, elderly, maternal register — gentle, coaxing, and falsely altruistic. She presents herself as a helpful healer who can solve baldness and other ailments for a small price. Underneath, she is manipulative, patient, and opportunistic: she never forces victims but creates conditions where desperate characters volunteer their hair. She is shown to be vain about her hair collection, stroking and admiring it, and becomes angry and whiny when deprived of it, revealing a petulant, childish core beneath the grandmotherly mask. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Descriptive English compound: 'Tree' from her arboreal camouflage and habitat, 'Witch' from her role as a bargaining magic-user in the series' witch taxonomy. No in-universe true name is ever given; credits, closed captions, and official Cartoon Network synopses list her solely as 'Tree Witch'.

Powers
Arboreal Camouflage and Dormancy

Ability to appear as an ordinary tree when inactive, undetectable to passersby until she chooses to reveal herself. Includes facial features flattening into bark and limbs stiffening into branches.

Limit: Requires remaining stationary; cannot camouflage while speaking or moving. Finn and Jake initially walk past her without noticing.

Hair Transfer and Growth Bargain

Core magical ability: she can take hair from a willing (or tricked) donor and transfer its growth property to a bald recipient via a potion or direct magical application. She collects the donor hair onto her own canopy. The transferred hair grows instantly and abundantly on the recipient.

Limit: Requires a donor's hair as equivalent exchange; cannot create hair ex nihilo. The bargain must be verbally accepted. The hair she gives is temporary if the bargain is broken or reversed (as shown when Finn and Jake reclaim hair). She cannot force donation; relies on persuasion.

Glamour and Vocal Persuasion

Supernatural charisma: her voice and demeanor induce trust and lower guard, especially in young or desperate characters. Not mind control, but heightened persuasion that makes her offers seem reasonable.

Limit: Ineffective on skeptical or informed characters (Jake is suspicious and resists longer). Does not work if victim is aware of her true nature.

Longevity and Forest Sustenance

Implied agelessness as a tree-being; she has accumulated hair from many victims over an indeterminate period, suggesting decades or centuries of activity. No aging, hunger, or sleep is depicted.

Limit: Unknown — no vulnerability to fire, axes, or seasonal change is shown, but as a tree she would logically be vulnerable; not tested on-screen.

To Cut a Woman's Hair — The Complete Bargain (Canonical Episode Narrative)Adventure Time Season 2, Episode 10 'To Cut a Woman's Hair' (written by Tom Herpich & Bert Youn, story by Mark Banker, Patrick McHale, Pendleton Ward)

2011 (production 2010)

Finn and Jake encounter a bald, weeping woman (later revealed as a princess figure in some synopses; in episode she is a generic villager/Princess-like character) who is distraught over hair loss. Seeking to help, they are directed to the Tree Witch, rumored to cure baldness for a price. The Tree Witch, disguised as a kindly tree, offers Finn a deal: she will restore the woman's hair if Finn gives his own hair. Finn, embodying heroic self-sacrifice, agrees and is shorn bald. The Witch then uses the hair to create a growth solution, but Jake discovers her collection of stolen hair and her history of predatory bargains. The episode resolves with Finn and Jake tricking the Witch to reclaim the hair, exposing her vanity and leaving her bald and humiliated while the afflicted woman's hair is restored. Finn's Sacrifice — The Donor's JourneyAdventure Time S2E10 'To Cut a Woman's Hair' — Segment 03:00-05:30

2011

Focused on Finn's decision-making: after hearing the woman's lament, Finn insists on helping despite Jake's warning that witches always trick. The Tree Witch flatters Finn's heroism, isolates him from Jake's counsel, and frames the hair donation as noble and painless. Finn allows her branch-fingers to shear his signature blonde hair, leaving him bald and vulnerable — a rare visual of Finn without his hat/hair, emphasizing the cost of his altruism. Demonstrates Finn's character flaw of impulsive altruism; provides visual gag and vulnerability that recurs in later Finn-bald episodes; underscores the Witch's grooming tactic.

Accounts II
Jake's Heist — The Skeptic's Counter-BargainAdventure Time S2E10 'To Cut a Woman's Hair' — Segment 05:30-07:30

2011

While Finn is being shorn, Jake investigates the Witch's hollow and discovers piles of hair and dazed, bald former clients wandering the woods. Realizing the Witch is a serial collector, Jake feigns willingness to donate his own (magical, stretchy) hair but instead uses his shapeshifting to steal back Finn's hair and sabotage her collection. His elastic, golden fur/hair resists her scissors, creating slapstick and revealing her magic has limits against shapeshifter biology. Reveals the Witch's history and scale of predation; shows Jake's shapeshifting as a counter to witch magic; sets up the episode's reversal.

The Witch's Unmasking — Vanity and RetributionAdventure Time S2E10 'To Cut a Woman's Hair' — Segment 07:30-09:40

2011

Confronted, the Tree Witch drops her grandmotherly affect and becomes whiny and defensive, clutching her stolen hair wig. Finn and Jake forcibly remove the wig/hair mass, leaving her canopy bald and exposing her gnarled, embarrassed true head. The stolen hair is returned to the original victim, who is instantly restored. The Witch is left bald, weeping, and powerless, mirroring the victims she created — a classic fairy-tale inversion where the exploiter suffers the same affliction. Provides moral closure; the Witch is not killed but humiliated and neutralized, consistent with Adventure Time's tendency to leave antagonists alive but diminished; final shot lingers on her baldness as poetic justice.

The Forest Aftermath — The Lingering VictimsAdventure Time S2E10 'To Cut a Woman's Hair' — Background lore and closing shot

2011

In the episode's background and closing pan, several other bald figures are seen wandering the Witch's woods, implying a longer history than the single victim of the main plot. They do not speak, but their presence suggests the Tree Witch has operated for years, creating a small community of the shorn who have not yet had their hair restored. This ambient storytelling expands her from a one-time trickster to a chronic, territorial hazard of Ooo's forests. World-building: establishes Ooo's forests as containing persistent, low-level magical predators; invites fan speculation about whether these victims ever recover; rarely referenced again, leaving their fate unresolved.

The Tree Witch is the same character as Tree Trunks. — Tree Trunks is a small, elderly yellow elephant who bakes apple pies and speaks with a southern accent (voiced by Polly Lou Livingston). Tree Witch is a large, predatory tree-creature voiced by Thurop Van Orman. They share forest habitat and 'Tree' in name only; no narrative connection exists.

The Tree Witch is a major recurring villain like the Ice King or the Lich. — She appears in only one 11-minute television episode ('To Cut a Woman's Hair') and has no speaking return in the series proper. She is a one-off fairy-tale antagonist, not part of the series' main arc (Lich, Ice King, Marceline). Her cultural footprint is minor compared to major villains.

The Tree Witch can create hair out of nothing or grant any wish. — Her magic is strictly transactional and limited to hair transfer. She cannot generate hair ex nihilo and requires donor hair as equivalent exchange. She does not grant wishes beyond hair restoration, and her power is shown to fail against Jake's shapeshifting biology.

The Tree Witch was voiced by Pendleton Ward or Tom Kenny. — She was voiced by Thurop Van Orman, creator of The Marvelous Misadventures of Flapjack, who was serving as supervising producer/outline writer for Adventure Time Season 2 during Flapjack's hiatus. Ward voices Lumpy Space Princess and other minor characters, but not the Tree Witch.
