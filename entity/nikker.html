Who It Is

De Nikker (variant of Nekker, Nix) is a malevolent water spirit from Dutch and Flemish folklore, particularly prevalent in the polder landscape of the Netherlands and Flanders. The Nikker is the Germanic water spirit of the Low Countries, adapted to the specific environment of canals, drainage ditches, peat bogs, and shallow lakes that characterize the region. He is described as a small, grayish-green humanoid with a slimy body, webbed hands and feet, bulbous eyes, and a mouth like a fish. His back is sometimes described as hollowed out like a trough, and his skin gives off a faint phosphorescent glow in the dark. The Nikker lives just beneath the surface of the water and grabs at anything that approaches too close — reaching up through the reeds to snatch children, sheep, or unwary fishermen. Unlike the more seductive Nixe or the artistic Fossegrim, the Nikker is purely predatory and mindless in his hunger. In the polder landscape, where drainage ditches and canals form a constant danger for children, the Nikker was the primary water bogeyman used to keep children away from the water's edge. The name 'Nikker' derives from the Proto-Germanic *nikwus (water spirit) and is related to the Old English 'nicor' (water monster), the Scandinavian 'Näcken,' and the German 'Nix.' The word 'nikker' was adopted in Dutch as a racial slur in the colonial period, derived from the folkloric figure. The Nikker can be repelled by iron, the name of Jesus, or by a silver coin thrown into the water. If a Nikker can be tricked into revealing his name, his power is broken. The creature is also associated with will-o'-the-wisps (dwaallichten), which the Nikker creates to lure travelers into waterlogged ground.
