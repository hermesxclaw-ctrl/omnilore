Who It Is

In the mist-shrouded mountains of Asturias and Cantabria, there lurks a creature of immense age and insatiable greed: the Cuélebre, a serpent-dragon whose scales grow harder with each passing century and whose wings unfurl only when it is ready to abandon the world it has terrorized. To the villagers below, it is a living nightmare that devours cattle and maidens alike, yet on the magical night of San Juan, the brave may challenge it—and if they succeed, they win the hand of a captive xana and a treasure beyond imagining.

The Cuélebre is typically sedentary, guarding its treasure and the xanas it holds captive. It emerges primarily to feed on livestock and occasionally humans. It is cunning and powerful, but vulnerable to specific tricks (e.g., red-hot stones, bread with pins). On San Juan's night, its magical powers wane, making it more susceptible to defeat.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name derives from the Latin 'colubra' (female snake), which evolved into the Asturian 'cuélebre' and Cantabrian 'culebre'.

Pronunciation — Asturian: /ˈkwe.le.βɾe/; Cantabrian: /kuˈle.βɾe/

Aliases — Culebre, Cuélebre (Asturian), Culebre (Cantabrian)

Ownership — Asturias and Cantabria, northern Spain

Powers
Impenetrable scales

As the Cuélebre ages, its scales become harder, eventually becoming impenetrable to most weapons.

Limit: Vulnerable to internal attacks (e.g., hot stones or pins) and on San Juan's night.

Flight

Older Cuélebres grow bat-like wings and can fly, especially when migrating to Mar Cuajada.

Limit: Not all accounts mention wings; flight may be reserved for the oldest.

Magical spit

Its saliva is said to turn into a magical stone with healing properties for various diseases.

Limit: The stone must be obtained safely; the creature is dangerous.

Spell-casting

The Cuélebre can cast spells, but these lose power on San Juan's night.

Limit: Ineffective on Midsummer night.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Cuélebre is a colossal winged serpent, often described with a dragon-like head, a long sinuous body covered in thick scales, and bat-like wings that emerge as it ages. Its scales become increasingly hard and impenetrable over time, making it nearly invulnerable. Some accounts depict it with a crest or horns, but these are not universal.

Symbols — In modern iconography, the Cuélebre is often depicted as a classic European dragon, but traditional descriptions emphasize its serpentine nature. It is associated with caves, treasure, and the supernatural guardian role.

Domains — Caves and underground lairs in the mountains of Asturias and Cantabria. It is also said to eventually fly to the mythical paradise of Mar Cuajada beyond the sea.

Accounts I
The Cuélebre and the XanaAsturian folklore

Traditional

A Cuélebre guards a xana (a fairy-like maiden) and a treasure in a cave. On San Juan's night, a brave young man must defeat the creature to free the xana and win her hand, along with the treasure. The hero often uses a red-hot stone or a loaf of bread filled with pins to kill the beast. The hero marries the xana and gains the treasure; the Cuélebre dies.

Accounts II
The Cantabrian Culebre's WrathCantabrian folklore

Traditional

In Cantabria, the Culebre is said to become more powerful on Saint Bartholomew's Night, unleashing its wrath against humans as vengeance for past wrongs. This is a time of danger when people stay indoors. The Culebre causes destruction and terror; no specific hero emerges.
