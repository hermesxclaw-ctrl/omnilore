Archive Dossier — Identity

Mazapegul

Pronunciation MAH-tsah-peh-GOOL (with stress on the last syllable)

Known As

MazapegoloMazapègulMazapegulMazapègol

Etymology

The etymology is uncertain. Some scholars suggest a connection to the dialectal 'mazza' (club or mace), possibly referencing a phallic symbol, combined with 'pegul' or 'pego' (pitch or tar), which might allude to a dark or sticky nature. Another theory links it to 'mazapècul' (a type of sweet), but this is speculative. The name is distinctly Romagnolo and does not appear in other Italian dialects.

Cultural Ownership

Italian, specifically the Romagna region (historical region in northern Italy, now part of Emilia-Romagna). It is a local folkloric figure, not widely known outside the area.

Disputed Identifications

Some folklorists have conflated the Mazapegul with the more general Italian 'incubo' or 'folletto' (goblin), but it is a distinct entity. It is sometimes compared to the Germanic Alp or the Slavic Kikimora, but these are separate traditions. There is no evidence that it is a version of the Befana, despite the 'related' metadata; that may be a spurious association.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the moonlit orchards of Romagna, where the Adriatic wind stirs the leaves, there lurks a creature of restless desire—the Mazapegul, a night spirit that slips into dreams and beds alike, leaving behind tangled hair and tangled hearts. Neither wholly demon nor mere folk tale, this enigmatic being embodies the anxieties and allure of the night, a figure that has haunted Italian folklore for centuries, yet remains as elusive as the dreams it disturbs.

In the dead of night, when the moon is hidden, the Mazapegul slips through the keyhole and settles upon the chest of the sleeping girl, stealing her breath and twisting her hair into knots.

Who It Is

The Mazapegul (also spelled Mazapégol) is a malevolent nocturnal spirit from the folklore of Romagna, in northern Italy's Emilia-Romagna region. Described as a small, shadowy creature with a sharp face, pointed ears, and long, spindly limbs, the Mazapegul is a type of incubus that preys upon sleeping victims — particularly young women and beautiful girls. It enters bedrooms at night and sits upon the sleeper's chest, causing the characteristic sensation of sleep paralysis (the 'mazapégol' experience). In addition to crushing its victims, the Mazapegul tangles their hair into impossible knots, steals their breath, and in some versions, sexually assaults them. The creature is also known to haunt stables, where it braids horses' manes and tails into elaborate patterns — the so-called 'goblin knots' (nodi del Mazapegul). Repelling a Mazapegul requires specific countermeasures: a pair of scissors placed under the pillow, a broom laid across the bedroom doorway, salt scattered on the windowsill, or the victim turning their shoes upside down before sleeping. The creature's name may derive from Romagnol dialect 'mazza' (club or to kill) and 'pegul' (a kind of pitch or tar, or perhaps 'little' in a pejorative sense). The Mazapegul shares strong similarities with the German Alp, the English mara, and the Swedish mara, representing a pan-European belief in nightmare spirits that attack sleepers.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

The Mazapegul is typically described as a small, impish creature, often with a hairy or furry body, pointed ears, and a mischievous grin. Some accounts describe it as having a tail and horns, while others say it appears as a normal man but with abnormal features. It is said to be able to change shape, sometimes appearing as a handsome young man to seduce women, or as a terrifying monster to frighten men. Its most distinctive feature is its tendency to leave tangled hair or knots in the hair of its victims, a sign of its nocturnal visits.

Symbols & Iconography

There is no known iconography, as it is a purely oral tradition. In local folklore, it is associated with the night, the moon, and orchards or fields. It may be linked to the symbol of the knot, representing the tangling of hair and the binding of dreams.

Behaviour & Personality

The Mazapegul is a mischievous and lecherous spirit. It is said to visit sleeping women at night, lying on them and causing erotic dreams, sometimes even engaging in sexual intercourse. It also torments men, giving them nightmares and tangling their hair. It is not inherently evil, but rather a nuisance or a trickster, though its sexual advances are non-consensual and thus harmful. It is said to be active mostly in the summer months, particularly in the countryside, and it may be warded off by various charms or by placing a broom upside down near the bed.

Domains & Habitats

The Mazapegul is a rural spirit, dwelling in fields, orchards, and barns. It is also said to inhabit houses, particularly during the night. It is associated with the countryside of Romagna, and is rarely reported in urban areas.

Version Differences

There are regional variations in the name and slight differences in appearance and behavior. Some versions emphasize its erotic nature, while others focus on its trickster aspect. In some accounts, it is male; in others, it can appear as female. The exact nature of its interactions with humans varies, but the core theme of nocturnal disturbance remains consistent.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

Alp

Type Related spiritSource Comparative folklore studies

Description

The Mazapegul shares many characteristics with the Germanic Alp, a nightmare spirit that sits on sleepers' chests and has sexual encounters with them. Both are nocturnal entities that cause sleep paralysis and erotic dreams.

Version Note

The Alp is more widespread in Germanic and Slavic regions; the Mazapegul is specifically Romagnolo.

Conflicting Accounts

Some scholars see them as variants of the same archetype, while others emphasize regional differences.
