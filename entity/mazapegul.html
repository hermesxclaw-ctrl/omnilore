Who It Is

In the moonlit orchards of Romagna, where the Adriatic wind stirs the leaves, there lurks a creature of restless desire—the Mazapegul, a night spirit that slips into dreams and beds alike, leaving behind tangled hair and tangled hearts. Neither wholly demon nor mere folk tale, this enigmatic being embodies the anxieties and allure of the night, a figure that has haunted Italian folklore for centuries, yet remains as elusive as the dreams it disturbs.

The Mazapegul is a mischievous and lecherous spirit. It is said to visit sleeping women at night, lying on them and causing erotic dreams, sometimes even engaging in sexual intercourse. It also torments men, giving them nightmares and tangling their hair. It is not inherently evil, but rather a nuisance or a trickster, though its sexual advances are non-consensual and thus harmful. It is said to be active mostly in the summer months, particularly in the countryside, and it may be warded off by various charms or by placing a broom upside down near the bed.

Powers
Shape-shifting

The Mazapegul can change its form, appearing as a handsome man, a hairy imp, or other shapes to suit its purposes.

Limit: Not specified; it may be limited to certain times (night) or places.

Nightmare inducement

It can cause nightmares and disturb sleep, particularly by sitting on the chest of the sleeper, causing a feeling of suffocation.

Limit: Only during sleep.

Hair tangling

It tangles the hair of its victims, creating knots that are difficult to undo, as a signature of its visits.

Limit: Only on sleeping victims.

Erotic dream projection

It induces erotic dreams in women, sometimes to the point of physical interaction, blurring the line between dream and reality.

Limit: Only on sleeping women; may be repelled by protective charms.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Mazapegul is typically described as a small, impish creature, often with a hairy or furry body, pointed ears, and a mischievous grin. Some accounts describe it as having a tail and horns, while others say it appears as a normal man but with abnormal features. Symbols — There is no known iconography, as it is a purely oral tradition. In local folklore, it is associated with the night, the moon, and orchards or fields. It may be linked to the symbol of the knot, representing the tangling of hair and the binding of dreams.

Domains — The Mazapegul is a rural spirit, dwelling in fields, orchards, and barns. It is also said to inhabit houses, particularly during the night. It is associated with the countryside of Romagna, and is rarely reported in urban areas.

Accounts I
The Night VisitorOral tradition, Romagna

Pre-modern to early 20th century

A young woman in a rural village begins to experience vivid, erotic dreams at night. She wakes up exhausted and with her hair tangled. An elderly woman recognizes the signs of the Mazapegul and advises her to place a broom upside down behind the door. The dreams cease, and the woman is relieved. The story serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of the night and the importance of protective measures. The woman is freed from the spirit's attentions.

Accounts II
The Jealous HusbandOral tradition, Romagna

19th century

A husband suspects his wife of infidelity because she talks in her sleep about a lover. He stays awake one night and sees a small, hairy creature enter the room and approach his wife. He tries to grab it, but it slips away, laughing. The next morning, the wife's hair is tangled, and the husband realizes it was the Mazapegul, not a human lover. He seeks the help of a local healer to ward off the spirit. The healer gives the husband a charm (an iron nail or a sprig of rue) to place under the pillow, and the Mazapegul never returns.
