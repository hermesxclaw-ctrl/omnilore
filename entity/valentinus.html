Who It Is

The most prominent and influential early Christian Gnostic theologian (c. 100 - c. 165 CE). Valentinus founded his school in Rome and was, according to Tertullian, a candidate for bishop of Rome before forming his own movement after another was chosen. His system is the most sophisticated and philosophically elaborate of all Gnostic cosmologies: the divine Pleroma of 30 Aeons, the fall and redemption of Sophia, the creation of the Demiurge and the material world, and the threefold classification of humanity into hylics (material, doomed), psychics (ensouled, capable of faith), and pneumatics (spiritual, possessing gnosis by nature). Only fragments of his writings survive, largely in the rebuttals of his orthodox opponents, including his 'Gospel of Truth' (found at Nag Hammadi). Valentinian Christianity was the most successful Gnostic movement, with a lasting network of churches into the 4th century.
