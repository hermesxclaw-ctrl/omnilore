Who It Is

In the golden age of mystery fiction, there are detectives who solve crimes with logic and deduction. Battler Ushiromiya is not one of them. He is a young man who returns to his family's island estate for a reunion that becomes a nightmare, where he must face the impossible: a locked-room murder, a witch who claims responsibility, and a game of wits that challenges the very nature of truth and reality.

Battler is initially brash, confident, and somewhat arrogant, often making jokes and acting impulsively. He is fiercely loyal to his family, especially his cousins and his younger sister, Maria. As the story progresses, he becomes more introspective and burdened by the weight of his responsibilities. He is determined to solve the mysteries using logic and refuses to accept supernatural explanations, embodying the 'anti-fantasy' side of the narrative.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Battler' is a given name chosen by the author, likely meant to evoke 'one who battles' or 'fighter'. 'Ushiromiya' is the family name, a Japanese surname that can be broken into 'ushiro' (behind) and 'miya' (shrine), but its meaning is not explicitly defined in the canon.

Pronunciation — BAH-tuh-ler OO-shee-roh-MEE-yah

Powers
Logic and Deduction

Battler is a skilled reasoner who can identify contradictions in supernatural explanations. He uses the 'Battler's Logic' to deconstruct the witch's claims, often by pointing out the lack of evidence or the impossibility of certain events.

Limit: His logic is only effective within the framework of the game board; he cannot defeat the witch if she introduces meta-physical rules that bypass conventional logic.

Meta-World Presence

Battler can enter the meta-world, a higher plane of existence where witches and game masters interact. Here, he can engage in debates with Beatrice and other supernatural beings, using rhetoric and reasoning as weapons.

Limit: His presence in the meta-world is tied to his connection to the game board; if he loses a game, he may be erased or trapped.

Resurrection

In the meta-world, Battler can be 'resurrected' if the witch allows it, as part of the game's rules. This is not a physical power but a narrative mechanic.

Limit: Resurrection is limited by the game's rules set by the witch or game master.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Battler is a tall, athletic young man with short, spiky brown hair and sharp eyes. He typically wears a white shirt, dark trousers, and a jacket, often with a tie. In later episodes, he appears in more casual or formal attire depending on the scene.

Symbols — Battler is often associated with the motif of a lion or a knight, symbolizing his role as a challenger to the witch. His catchphrase 'It's a closed circle!' references his focus on locked-room mysteries. He is also linked to the Ushiromiya family crest and the gold of the legend.

Domains — Battler primarily exists on Rokkenjima, a fictional island in the Izu archipelago, where the Ushiromiya family holds their annual conference. The island is depicted as having a mansion, a chapel, and various outbuildings. Battler also appears in the meta-world, a conceptual space where the witch Beatrice and other supernatural beings reside.

Accounts I
The Legend of the Golden WitchUmineko When They Cry, Episode 1

1986, October 4-5

Battler returns to Rokkenjima after six years to attend the Ushiromiya family conference. A typhoon traps the family on the island, and a series of murders begin, seemingly following the legend of the Golden Witch Beatrice. Battler, along with his cousins, attempts to solve the murders, but everyone is killed by the end. In the meta-world, Battler is challenged by Beatrice to solve the mystery. Battler is trapped in the meta-world, forced to play a game with Beatrice. He must prove that the murders were committed by humans, not by a witch.

Turn of the Golden WitchUmineko When They Cry, Episode 2

1986, October 4-5

Battler is given a second chance to solve the mystery. He becomes more proactive, questioning the family and investigating the island. He learns about Beatrice's past and the legend of the gold. Despite his efforts, the murders repeat, and he is unable to prevent them. He is confronted by Beatrice, who uses 'red truth' to assert that she is a witch. Battler's confidence is shaken, but he refuses to give up. He begins to understand that the game has deeper layers.

Banquet of the Golden WitchUmineko When They Cry, Episode 3

1986, October 4-5

Battler, now more determined, faces Beatrice in a new game. He is joined by a new ally, a witch-in-training named Bernkastel. Battler uses a new tactic: he creates his own 'anti-fantasy' arguments, and for the first time, he manages to corner Beatrice. However, he is defeated when Beatrice reveals a truth that shatters his worldview. Battler is forced to question his own identity and the nature of his existence.

Alliance of the Golden WitchUmineko When They Cry, Episode 4

1986, October 4-5

Battler's past is explored, including his mother's death and his relationship with his father. He learns about the 'Ushiromiya family's dark secrets'. He also meets a mysterious girl named Ange, who is from the future. Battler attempts to save his family but fails. In the meta-world, he discovers that his memories have been tampered with. Battler realizes that he may not be who he thinks he is. This sets up the answer arcs.

Accounts II
End of the Golden WitchUmineko no Naku Koro ni Chiru, Episode 5

1986, but with meta-world alterations

Battler is now a 'game master' and creates his own game board to challenge the witch. He designs a mystery that he believes is unsolvable by supernatural means. However, a new character, Erika Furudo, a self-proclaimed 'detective', interferes, and the game spins out of control. Battler is forced to confront the limits of his logic. Battler's game is destroyed, and he is killed in the meta-world. His role as the protagonist is taken over by others in later episodes.

Dawn of the Golden WitchUmineko no Naku Koro ni Chiru, Episode 6

1986, but with meta-world alterations

Battler's spirit remains in the meta-world, and he is revived by Beatrice. He must solve the mystery of his own death and the true nature of the game. He learns about the 'concept of the witch' and the role of the 'observer'. Battler gains a deeper understanding of the game's rules and his own purpose.

Requiem of the Golden WitchUmineko no Naku Koro ni Chiru, Episode 7

1986, but with meta-world alterations

This episode focuses on the past of Beatrice and the Ushiromiya family. Battler appears as a minor character, observing the tragedy that led to the legend. He learns the truth about his connection to Beatrice. Battler's identity is revealed: he is actually the son of Kinzo Ushiromiya and Beatrice, but his birth was hidden. He is the 'golden witch's child'.

Twilight of the Golden WitchUmineko no Naku Koro ni Chiru, Episode 8

1986, but with meta-world alterations

Battler, now fully aware of his origins, must decide the fate of the game board. He chooses to create a world where the tragedy can be avoided, even if it means sacrificing his own existence. He reconciles with Beatrice and brings the story to a close. The game ends, and the meta-world is dissolved. Battler's fate is left ambiguous, but he is implied to find peace.
