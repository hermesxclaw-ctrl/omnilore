Who It Is

Yeomna (Yeom-na), known in Sanskrit as Yama, is the king and chief judge of the underworld in Korean Buddhist and folk religious traditions. He presides over Myeongbu (the Hall of the Dead), where the souls of the deceased are brought for judgment after death. In Korean Buddhist iconography, Yeomna is typically depicted as a stern, imposing figure wearing the robes of a Chinese-style magistrate or king, seated behind a desk, holding a scepter or a brush, with the Mirror of Past Deeds (eopgyeongdae) before him, in which the soul's entire life is reflected without omission. The judgment process in the Korean Buddhist underworld involves ten kings (Siwang), each ruling over a different court, but Yeomna presides as the supreme magistrate. Souls are judged according to their deeds in life, and the bureaucracy of the underworld—staffed by demonic bailiffs, scribes, and torturers—administers appropriate punishments for the wicked and rewards for the virtuous. This vision of the afterlife is depicted vividly in the Siwangdo (Paintings of the Ten Kings), which show the souls being judged, punished, and in some cases guided toward better rebirths by bodhisattvas. In the shamanic tradition, Yeomna is also invoked during the jinogwigut (ritual for the dead), where shamans negotiate with the underworld bureaucracy to secure a favorable judgment for the deceased. Some shamanic narratives portray Yeomna as a deity who can be bargained with or even tricked, demonstrating a more folkloric and less strictly doctrinal relationship with the king of the dead.

Attested in Buddhist texts + Korean folk syncretismEra Buddhism (from 4th century CE in Korea); fully established in Goryeo/Joseon
