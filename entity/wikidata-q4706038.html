Accounts

The Alans, an Iranian nomadic people, migrated from the steppes north of the Caspian Sea. They raided into the Roman Empire, served as allies and mercenaries, and eventually settled in Gaul, Hispania, and North Africa. A group of Alans settled in the Caucasus, becoming the ancestors of the modern Ossetians.

The Nart sagas are a cycle of heroic epics about a race of superhuman heroes, the Narts. They are generally considered to preserve elements of Alan culture, including their customs, social structure, and worldview, though filtered through centuries of oral transmission and later Christian and Islamic influences.

A legendary account of a Breton saint named Alan, possibly a bishop or abbot, who performed miracles. The historical basis is unclear, and the legend may have been created to promote a local cult.

The river Alun in Ceredigion is mentioned in Welsh literature, often as a geographical marker or a minor setting. There is no evidence of a river deity named Alun in Welsh mythology.
