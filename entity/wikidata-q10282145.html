The Approach

In the chaotic arena of Super Smash Bros. Melee, where legendary heroes and villains clash, there exists a silent, faceless menace—the Fighting Wire Frames. These metallic automatons, born from the depths of Master Hand's twisted imagination, serve as relentless training dummies and formidable foes, their existence a testament to the game's hidden depths and the eerie possibilities of a world without individuality.

Identity

Fighting Wire Frames

Pronunciation FIGHT-ing WIRE FRAYMS

Known As

Wire FramesFighting Wire FrameWireframe characters

Etymology

The name 'Fighting Wire Frames' likely derives from their appearance resembling wireframe models, a common 3D graphics representation. 'Fighting' reflects their role as combatants.

Cultural Ownership

Owned by Nintendo and created for the Super Smash Bros. series. They are original characters not based on any specific pre-existing franchise.

Disputed Identifications

They are sometimes confused with 'Master Hand' or 'Crazy Hand' due to their similar origin, but they are distinct entities. Some fans speculate they are proto-versions of playable characters, but this is unconfirmed.

Description

Appearance

Fighting Wire Frames are humanoid figures composed of a metallic, wireframe-like mesh. They have a silver/gray color and a somewhat skeletal appearance, with visible joints and no facial features. Their bodies are slender and androgynous, and they move with a stiff, mechanical fluidity.

Symbols & Iconography

They are associated with the Master Hand, as they appear to be his creations. They lack any unique symbols or iconography, reinforcing their role as generic combat units.

Behaviour & Personality

They are mindless automatons, exhibiting no personality or emotions. They attack the player on sight and fight in a simple, aggressive manner, using basic punches, kicks, and grapples. They do not communicate or show any signs of intelligence.

Domains & Habitats

They appear exclusively in Super Smash Bros. Melee's single-player modes, particularly in the 'Adventure Mode' (on the 'Brinstar' and 'F-Zero' stages) and in various Event Matches. They are also fought as enemies in the 'Classic Mode' and 'All-Star Mode' as part of the 'Target Test' or 'Home-Run Contest'? Actually, they appear as enemies in the 'Adventure Mode' and in some Event Matches. They do not have a habitat per se, as they are constructs of Master Hand.

Version Differences

They are unique to Super Smash Bros. Melee. They do not appear in any other Super Smash Bros. game, though similar wireframe-like characters appear in other games (e.g., in the 'Wireframe' trophy in Melee). They have no known variants.
