Who It Is

Before the earth had shape and the sky had color, there was only Sidaba Mapu — the Immortal Master whose thought became light, whose breath became the sky, and whose children would become the gods, kings, and clans of Kangleipak.

Portrayed as omniscient, benevolent but distant creator-father. He creates through thought/will (Leisemlon: 'He thought and it became'), delegates worldly rule to his sons, and acts as arbiter in their rivalry. He is not a trickster or wrathful destroyer; anger is shown only as cosmic correction (e.g., testing sons). He is described as 'Mapu' — grandfatherly, protective, immortal, beyond birth and death, yet intimately concerned with dharma of Kangleipak. Powers
Cosmogony and Creation Ex Nihilo/Ex Se

Creates the universe (Taibang), sky, earth, time, gods and humans from his own body, breath and thought. Leisemlon describes him expanding himself into space, light and matter.

Limit: Creation is not instantaneous magic but emanation; he requires Leimarel Sidabi as earth-matrix to complete world-making in some recensions. Cannot be seen or fully known by lesser gods.

Immortality and Self-Existence (Sidaba)

Literally 'immortal' — not born, not dying, not decaying. Exists before and after creation, beyond cycles of birth-death that bind other deities and humans.

Limit: Immortality does not mean interventionist omnipotence on earth; he delegates earthly governance.

Sky Sovereignty and Celestial Control

Lord of Atiya/Soraren; controls sun, moon, stars, wind and rain as extensions of his domain. Numit (sun) is sometimes called his eye or son.

Limit: Day-to-day weather and territorial control delegated to Umang Lais (e.g., Nongshaba, Thangjing, Marjing, Wangbren, Koubru).

Progenitor of Gods and Clans

Father/emanator of Leimarel Sidabi (in some versions), Lainingthou Sanamahi, Nongda Lairen Pakhangba, and through Pakhangba the seven Yek Salais (Mangang, Luwang, Khuman, Angom, Moirang, Khaba-Nganba, Sarang Leishangthem) and humanity.

Limit: Genealogy varies: Leimarel Sidabi is wife, sister, or emanation depending on text.

Divine Arbitration and Dharma-Giving

Establishes cosmic law (dharma), tests and judges his sons (circumambulation trial), assigns Sanamahi to household/hearth and Pakhangba to kingship/cosmos.

Limit: Judgment is final but sons' rivalry continues in human history (kings vs priestly line).

Omniscience and Formlessness

Knows all, pervades all as light and space; can be formless and simultaneously take any form. Philosophical passages identify him with consciousness itself.

Limit: In narrative, he still speaks and acts anthropomorphically to interact with other gods.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In canonical Puya philosophy Sidaba Mapu is originally formless — described as self-existent light (mangal), breath and mind without decay. When anthropomorphized for narrative and ritual, he appears as an aged, luminous patriarch enthroned in the sky (Soraren/Atiya), white or golden in radiance, often invisible to mortal eyes. Lai Haraoba oral performance rarely gives him a fixed icon; unlike his sons Sanamahi (anthropomorphic household guardian

Symbols — Primary symbols: Atiya (sky/ether) itself; light/sun (Numit as his eye or manifestation); empty space/void (Atingkok aspect); the number 7 (seven Salais/clans emanated from him). No fixed vahana or weapon. In Lai Haraoba cosmology, the four cardinal directions and the vertical axis (sky-earth-underworld) emanate from him. The Meetei Mayek cosmology and the Kanglei house architecture (Yumjao, Sanamahi Kachin) symbolically map his sky domain above

Domains — Domain: Universe (Taibang), Sky (Atiya/Soraren), Time and creation itself. Habitat: Soraren, the highest sky-realm above the earth (Leibak), above Korou (heaven) and above the abodes of lesser Umang Lais (forest gods) and Lam Lais (territorial deities). He does not dwell on earth; earth is created by and for Leimarel Sidabi and his sons. In ritual geography, his place is the sky above the Sanamahi shrine and above the hearth fire.

Accounts I
The Creation from Thought — Leithak Leikharol and Leisemlon CosmogonyLeithak Leikharol; Leisemlon Ahanpa

Ancient Puya core, manuscript tradition pre-18th century

In the beginning there was only Sidaba Mapu (as Salailen) alone in void darkness. He became aware of himself and thought 'I shall expand.' From his thought came light (mangal), from light came sky (Atiya), from sky came wind, fire, water and earth. He then created Leimarel Sidabi to be the mother-earth who would give form to his creation. Together they emanate the gods, the directions, and the materials of the universe. The text presents creation not as making from external matter but as self-differentiation — the universe is Salailen's own body extended. Establishes Meitei cosmology of self-emanation, explains why all beings are kin (all are Salai/emanations), grounds the seven-clan system and the sacredness of sky and earth.

The Birth of Sanamahi and Pakhangba and the Contest for the ThroneSanamahi Laihui; summarized in Hodson 1908 and Parratt 2005

Puya narrative, orally performed in Lai Haraoba

Sidaba Mapu fathers two sons by Leimarel Sidabi: Lainingthou Sanamahi (eldest) and Nongda Lairen Pakhangba (younger, born as a serpent/dragon-like being). To decide who shall rule the earth, Sidaba commands them to circumambulate the world seven times; whoever returns first shall be king. Sanamahi, confident as elder, sets out. Pakhangba, still an infant unable to walk, weeps. His mother Leimarel advises him to circumambulate his father's throne (which equals the universe) seven times. Sidaba, pleased by Pakhangba's wisdom, declares Pakhangba king of the earth and Sanamahi lord of the household and hearth. Sanamahi is angered but accepts, becoming the ever-present household deity. Explains dual kingship: Sanamahi as Yumjao (house god) worshipped in every Meitei home, Pakhangba as Ningthouja (royal) ancestor and state deity. Ritual division persists: Sanamahi shrine in southwestern corner of house, Pakhangba in royal regalia.

Creation of the Seven Salais and HumanityLeisemlon Ariba; Wakoklon Heelel; Khumanlon, etc.

Puya genealogical lore

After enthroning Pakhangba, Sidaba Mapu causes Pakhangba to take human and serpent forms and to father the progenitors of the seven Yek Salais (clans) that constitute the Meitei nation: Mangang (Ningthouja), Luwang, Khuman, Angom, Moirang, Khaba-Nganba, and Sarang Leishangthem. In sometellings Sidaba himself breathes life into the first humans (Mioba/Mi) from earth and sky essence. The Salais are thus literally 'Salai' — emanations/extensions — of Salailen. Legitimizes clan exogamy, territorial divisions of Kangleipak, and kingship of Ningthouja clan as descendants of Pakhangba/Sidaba. Basis for Yek Salai system still referenced in Meitei identity.

Accounts II
Sidaba Mapu and the Ordering of the Umang LaisLai Haraoba hymns; Umang Lai Haraoba tradition; Parratt fieldwork

Oral/ritual tradition, codified in 19th-20th century transcriptions

Having ordered the center (hearth and palace), Sidaba Mapu emanates and assigns the Umang Lais — forest and territorial deities — to guard the directions and hills around the valley: Koubru (north), Marjing (northeast), Wangbren (south), Thangjing (southwest) and others. Each is a son or emanation of Sidaba/Leimarel or of Pakhangba, tasked to protect boundaries and receive worship at their sacred groves (Umang Lai Laipham). Creates sacred geography of Manipur Valley; every Umang Lai shrine reenacts Sidaba's original distribution. Lai Haraoba festival reenacts this creation annually.

The Burning of the Puyas and the Eclipse of Sidaba Mapu (Puya Meithaba)Cheitharol Kumbaba; oral history of Puya Meithaba; British colonial records

1729-1732 CE, reign of King Pamheiba (Garibniwaz), under influence of Santidas Goswami

King Pamheiba, converted to Gaudiya Vaishnavism, orders the collection and burning of Meitei Puyas at Kangla. Dozens of sacred manuscripts containing the lore of Sidaba Mapu, Sanamahi and Pakhangba are destroyed by fire. Sanamahism is proscribed; worship of Sidaba Mapu as Supreme God is suppressed in favor of Hindu deities (Vishnu/Krishna). Only a few Puyas are hidden and saved by Maichous (scholars). Sidaba Mapu worship retreats to household hearths and Lai Haraoba groves. Rupture in textual transmission; many creation details lost or reconstructed from memory. Explains gaps and contradictions in surviving Puyas. Spurs 20th-century Sanamahi revival movement seeking to restore Sidaba Mapu to supremacy.

The Lai Haraoba Reenactment — Sidaba Mapu Awakens the WorldLai Haraoba ritual texts (Khunung Eshei, Lairemma Eshei); Parratt 2005 ethnography

Living tradition, annual festival, texts codified 19th-20th century from older oral core

Each year at Umang Lai shrines, Maibas and Maibis reenact creation: Sidaba Mapu's thought, the emergence of Leimarel Sidabi, the birth of Sanamahi and Pakhangba, and the ordering of the world. Through song (Eshei), dance (Khamba-Thoibi dance, Lai Haraoba Jagoi) and mime, the community becomes participants in Sidaba's original creation, renewing the world and reaffirming that the valley and its people remain under the Immortal Master's protection. Keeps Sidaba Mapu theology alive despite textual loss; UNESCO-recognized intangible heritage; central to contemporary Sanamahi identity politics and cultural revival.

Sidaba Mapu is the same as Hindu Brahma, or a form of Vishnu/Shiva. — Sidaba Mapu is the indigenous Meitei Supreme God of Sanamahism, with distinct cosmogony (self-emanation, Salai system) predating Vaishnavism in Manipur. 18th-century Vaishnavite texts equated him with Brahma/Vishnu to facilitate conversion, but Puyas and living Sanamahism maintain he is distinct and supreme within his own theology.

Sanamahi and Sidaba Mapu are the same god. — Sanamahi (Lainingthou Sanamahi) is the eldest son of Sidaba Mapu and Leimarel Sidabi, lord of the household. Sidaba Mapu is his father, the sky-creator. Confusion arises because Sanamahi is the most visibly worshipped (daily hearth offerings) while Sidaba is more abstract and distant.

Meitei religion is just animism with many unrelated spirits and no Supreme God. — Sanamahism has a coherent Supreme Being theology (Sidaba Mapu/Salailen/Atingkok) with emanated deities (Sanamahi, Pakhangba, Umang Lais) understood as his extensions, not independent 'animist' spirits. The system is monotheistic/monistic with polytheistic expression.

The Puyas are mythological forgeries with no historical value. — While Puyas contain myth, they are also indigenous historical, genealogical and philosophical texts. Their destruction in Puya Meithaba and later reconstruction means they have layers, but they remain primary sources for Meitei intellectual history; dismissing them entirely erases indigenous archive.
