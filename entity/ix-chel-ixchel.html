Who They Are

Ix Chel is the Maya moon goddess, the goddess of fertility, childbirth, medicine, weaving, and the moon. She is both a nurturing mother and a destructive force — she can bring healing rains or devastating floods. Her sanctuary on the island of Cozumel was one of the most important pilgrimage destinations in the Maya world, where women traveled to pray for fertility and safe childbirth.

Cultural Significance

Ix Chel's sanctuary on Cozumel was a uniquely important religious center — one of the few Maya pilgrimage destinations that attracted visitors from as far as Central Mexico and Honduras. The island was discovered by the Spanish explorer Juan de Grijalva in 1518, who reported seeing the remains of numerous sacrifices at the goddess's temple. Ix Chel remains an important figure in modern Maya spirituality and has been adopted as a symbol of indigenous women's power and healing traditions. Her association with the moon, weaving, and medicine connects her to the essential domestic and healing arts that Maya women have practiced for millennia.
