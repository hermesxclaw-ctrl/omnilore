Who It Is

In the humid twilight of the Maya lowlands, an aged woman with jaguar ears and claws instead of hands presides over birth and healing, her serpent headdress entwined with the mysteries of earth and rain. She is Ixchel, the grandmother midwife, a fearsome warrior and a nurturing healer, whose sanctuary on the island of Cozumel drew pilgrims seeking fertility and whose image still echoes in modern games and stories.

Nurturing yet fearsome; patroness of midwives and healers, but also a warrior and bringer of rain/floods.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Ixchel' likely derives from Yucatec Maya: 'Ix' (feminine prefix) and 'chel' possibly meaning 'rainbow'. Alternative interpretations exist, and the Classic-era name is unknown.

Pronunciation — ish-CHEL

Aliases — Ix Chel, Chak Chel, Goddess O, Ix Chekel Yax, Ixhunie, Ixhunieta

Ownership — Maya civilization, particularly Yucatec, Poqom, and Tzʼutujil cultures; also relevant to broader Mesoamerican traditions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Aged woman with jaguar ears and sometimes claws; often shown with an entwined serpent headdress, crossed bones on skirt, and an inverted water jar. In Classic art, she may appear as a fearsome warrior with shield and spear.

Symbols — Jaguar attributes, serpent headdress, crossed bones, water jar, weaving implements (in headdress on Birth Vase), crescent (when misidentified as moon goddess).

Domains — Midwifery, medicine, sweatbath, earth, rain, possibly war; associated with islands (Cozumel, Isla Mujeres) and coastal regions.

Accounts I
The Thirteen SonsLas Casas, Apologética Historia Sumaria

Colonial (16th century)

Ixchel and her spouse Itzamna had thirteen sons, two of whom created heaven and earth and all that belongs to it. Establishes Ixchel as a creator figure in some traditions.

Accounts II
Sweatbath mythsEthnographic accounts (Tzʼutujil, Cakchiquel, Qʼeqchiʼ)

Modern ethnographic

Aged goddesses, such as Bʼatzbʼal, become patrons of the sweatbath after being imprisoned there; parallels with Oaxacan myths. Explains the ritual importance of the sweatbath.
