Who It Is

Scylla is a legendary man-eating monster who dwells on one side of a narrow channel opposite the whirlpool Charybdis, terrorizing the Strait of Messina between Italy and Sicily. Homer describes her as having twelve legs, six long necks with grisly heads each containing three rows of razor-sharp teeth, and a bark like a puppy. She lurks in a cave high on a cliff, snatching sailors from passing ships and devouring them alive. In the Odyssey, Odysseus loses six men to Scylla as he navigates the strait, choosing to sacrifice a few rather than risk losing his entire ship to Charybdis. Later authors gave Scylla an origin story: Ovid's Metamorphoses recounts that she was once a beautiful nymph transformed by the jealous Circe (who was in love with the sea-god Glaucus, whom Scylla had rejected). Circe poisoned Scylla's bathing pool, turning her lower half into a ring of barking dogs' heads fused to her body. Scylla is one of the enduring symbols of impossible choices — being 'between Scylla and Charybdis' means choosing between two equally terrible fates.

Origins

In Homer, Scylla is simply a monster with no parentage given (her mother is 'Crataeis' — possibly a name for Hecate). Ovid provides the most famous backstory: Scylla was a nymph, beloved by the sea-god Glaucus. When Glaucus asked Circe for a love potion, Circe fell in love with him instead. Spurned by Glaucus, Circe poisoned Scylla's bath, transforming her lower body into a ring of snarling dogs.
