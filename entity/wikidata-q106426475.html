The Approach

In the bustling town of Sakurashin, where humans and yōkai coexist under a fragile peace, there exists an immortal woman who remembers a time when her own humanity was a distant memory. Mariabelle, once a mortal girl from a plague-stricken village, now serves as the eternal assistant to a youthful land god, her past shrouded in the secrets of a legendary creator and a forbidden resurrection. Her story weaves together the threads of science, magic, and undying loyalty, making her one of the most enigmatic figures in the Yozakura Quartet universe.

Content advisory. The character's backstory involves themes of death, resurrection, plague, persecution, and the ethical implications of scientific creation. While the series is generally lighthearted, these elements are treated with a degree of seriousness and may be sensitive to some readers.

Etymology

The name 'Mariabelle' is a combination of 'Maria' and 'belle' (French for 'beautiful'), suggesting a beautiful, beloved figure. It may also be a reference to the Frankenstein myth, as Victor Frankenstein's fiancée is named Elizabeth, but the name Mariabelle itself is unique to this series.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese popular culture, specifically the Yozakura Quartet franchise created by Suzuhito Yasuda.

Disputed Identifications

There is no dispute over her identity. She is a unique character in the series, though her backstory draws clear inspiration from Mary Shelley's 'Frankenstein' novel, with Victor Frankenstein as her father. This has led to fan speculation about her being a direct adaptation, but the series presents her as an original character within its own continuity.

Description

Appearance

Mariabelle appears as a young woman with long, light-colored hair, often styled in a way that complements her various cosplay outfits. She has a gentle, composed expression and is typically seen in outfits chosen by Yuhi, ranging from maid costumes to other playful ensembles. Her true age is not reflected in her appearance, which remains youthful due to her immortality.

Symbols & Iconography

Her association with Victor Frankenstein and her resurrection link her to themes of life, death, and creation. In the series, she is often depicted with items related to her duties as an assistant, such as documents or tea sets. Her cosplay outfits serve as a visual symbol of her dedication to Yuhi's whims and her hidden enjoyment of them.

Behaviour & Personality

Mariabelle is diligent, loyal, and deeply appreciative of Yuhi's protection. She maintains a professional demeanor as his assistant, but she secretly enjoys the cosplay activities he requests. She has strong preferences, particularly for Japanese breakfasts, indicating a desire for normalcy and tradition. Her past as a plague victim and her resurrection have given her a mature, grateful outlook on life, though she rarely speaks of her past.

Domains & Habitats

She resides in Sakurashin, the town protected by the Seven Pillars, where humans and yōkai coexist. She works in the District Mayor's office under Yuhi, and is often seen in the office or accompanying Yuhi on official duties. Her home is presumably within the town, though not specifically detailed.

Version Differences

In the manga, her backstory is explored in more depth, including her relationship with Victor Frankenstein and the circumstances of her resurrection. The anime adaptations, particularly 'Hana no Uta', include her character but may condense or omit some of the detailed lore. The OVA 'Tsuki ni Naku' focuses on other characters, so her role may be minimal. Overall, her core characterization remains consistent across versions.

Stories & Myths

The Resurrection of Mariabelle

Source Yozakura Quartet manga and animeCulture Japanese popular culturePeriod Approximately 200 years before the main storyline (around the 19th century)

Summary

Mariabelle was a young girl living in a village afflicted by a deadly plague. Her father, Victor Frankenstein, a scientist, sought to create a cure but was unable to save her. In desperation, he used his scientific knowledge to resurrect her, granting her immortality. The process was successful, but it made her a half-yōkai, as her resurrection involved supernatural elements. After her resurrection, they faced persecution from other half-yōkai and humans who feared them. They eventually sought refuge in Sakurashin, where they hoped to be 'tuned' (a process that could sever their connection to the supernatural world). However, Yuhi, the land god, intervened and offered his protection instead of tuning them. Grateful, Mariabelle became Yuhi's permanent assistant.

Mariabelle gained immortality and a new life in Sakurashin. She became Yuhi's assistant, forming a close bond with him. Her father's whereabouts are not detailed, but it is implied he may have left or passed away.

Death and Resurrection

Source Manga and anime

Description

Mariabelle died from a plague and was resurrected by her father. This act of resurrection is a central part of her identity, but it also raises ethical questions about tampering with life and death.

Scholarly Context

The story draws on the Frankenstein myth, which explores the consequences of playing god. In this case, the resurrection is successful but grants immortality, which could be seen as a curse or a blessing.

Persecution

Source Manga and anime

Description

After her resurrection, Mariabelle and her father faced persecution from other half-yōkai and possibly humans who feared them. This forced them to seek refuge in Sakurashin.

Scholarly Context

This reflects themes of discrimination and the struggle for acceptance, common in stories about half-breeds and outsiders.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nature of her immortality

Position A

She is a half-yōkai due to her resurrection, making her immortal.

Position A — evidence

The series describes her as a half-yōkai after the resurrection, and she sought tuning to become fully human.

Position B

She is a human who was granted immortality through scientific means, not necessarily a yōkai.

Position B — evidence

Her father was a human scientist, and the resurrection process may have been purely scientific, but the series implies supernatural elements.

Scholarly Consensus

The series clearly states she became a half-yōkai, so the consensus is that she is a half-yōkai.
