Who It Is

In the beginning, before the sun goddess took her throne and before the islands of Japan were wrung from the salty deep, there were five. Five deities who simply appeared, with no parents, no story, no drama — just presence. Four of them are famous: the Central Master, the High Producer, the Divine Producer, and the Reed-Shoot Prince. The fifth is Ame-no-Naemasu — the heavenly one who straightens and makes abundant — a name that appears in exactly one creation list and then vanishes into the silence of history. If the cosmos has a filing cabinet, this deity is the folder labeled 'Important — Do Not Open.'

The deity's behavior is entirely unattested. The name suggests functions of straightening, directing, and making abundant, which may imply a role in cosmic order and agricultural fertility, but no narrative or ritual text describes specific actions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No textual or artistic description of Ame-no-Naemasu's appearance survives. As a heavenly deity (Amatsukami), it would likely be depicted in the classical Japanese style—robed in flowing garments, bearing a staff or sword—but this is pure reconstruction. No iconographic tradition exists for this figure.

Symbols — No symbols are attested. If the name's meaning is taken as a guide, a straight staff or measuring implement might be associated, but this is entirely speculative.

Domains — Based on the name's components and the deity's classification as a 'separate heavenly deity,' Ame-no-Naemasu likely presided over cosmic order, the straightening of divine will, and the abundance of natural resources. The deity appears connected to the foundational act of creation, though without specific domain attribution.

Accounts I
The Dawn ListingKogo Shūi

c. 807 CE

In the beginning, when heaven and earth first parted, five deities emerged separately from the primordial chaos. Ame-no-Naemasu is counted among this first generation of 'separate heavenly deities' (Kotoamatsukami). Unlike the later kami who were born from Izanagi and Izanami, these five emerged spontaneously, with no parentage and no narrative. They simply were, as the first expressions of cosmic order. The listing establishes Ame-no-Naemasu as one of the most ancient beings in the Shinto cosmos, predating the creation of the Japanese islands and the birth of the sun goddess Amaterasu. The deity's presence in this list signals its foundational importance, even though no stories about it survive.

Ame-no-Naemasu is a major deity with extensive mythology. — Ame-no-Naemasu appears only in genealogical lists and ritual registers. No myths, stories, or narratives about this deity survive. It is among the most obscure figures in the entire Shinto pantheon.

Ame-no-Naemasu is the same deity as Ame-no-Minakanushi. — These are distinct deities listed separately in the Kogo Shūi. While both are 'separate heavenly deities,' they are never identified with each other in any source.

The deity has a shrine or dedicated worship tradition. — No dedicated shrine to Ame-no-Naemasu is attested. The deity appears only in court ritual contexts and clan genealogies, not in popular worship.
