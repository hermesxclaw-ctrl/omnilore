Who It Is

Xwm Kab are protective guardian spirits in Hmong religion that watch over villages, sacred sites, and natural boundaries. They are the divine sentinels who maintain the spiritual integrity of community spaces and protect against invading malevolent forces.

Attested in Oral Tradition / Village RitualsEra Ancient

Field Notes

۩ Xwm Kab are often associated with specific trees — particularly banyan or fig trees — which must never be cut down.

۩ In some Hmong traditions, a small shrine or cloth banner marks the dwelling place of a Xwm Kab, and passersby should offer a respectful greeting.

۩ The Xwm Kab festival is one of the few Hmong rituals led by village elders rather than shamans, emphasizing the community's direct relationship with the guardian.

Cultural Significance

The Xwm Kab represent the Hmong understanding of sacred geography and the importance of maintaining proper boundaries between civilized and wild spaces. The annual village Xwm Kab festival is one of the most important community rituals in traditional Hmong society, involving the sacrifice of a chicken or pig, shared feasting, and the reaffirmation of community bonds. These traditions have been carried into diaspora communities, where Hmong villages in the US sometimes designate local parks or community centers as Xwm Kab sites.
