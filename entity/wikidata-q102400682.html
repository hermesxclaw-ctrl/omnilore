Who It Is

The ton is a unit of measure so deeply woven into language and commerce that it spans centuries, oceans, and even the realm of slang—from the weight of a ship's cargo to a hundred miles per hour on a British motorway.

The ton behaves as a standard unit in trade, shipping, and engineering, but also as a colloquial intensifier in English.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Derived from the Old English 'tunne' and Old French 'tonne', meaning a large cask or barrel. The term originally referred to a cask of the largest capacity, which could hold between 175 and 213 imperial gallons, weighing around 2,000 pounds.

Pronunciation — /tʌn/ (same for ton and tonne in US and UK; in UK, 'tonne' may be pronounced /tɒn/ or /tʌn/)

Aliases — tonne, metric ton, long ton, short ton, freight ton, measurement ton

Ownership — Global, with specific definitions in the United Kingdom, United States, Canada, Australia, and other Commonwealth nations.

Accounts I
The Cask OriginHistorical usage

Medieval to early modern

The ton originated as the name for a large cask used for wine and other goods, with a capacity of 175-213 imperial gallons and a weight around 2,000 pounds. The term became a standard measure of cargo volume and weight.

Accounts II
The Hundredweight SystemLegal definitions

15th-20th century

The ton was defined as 20 hundredweight, but the hundredweight varied: 108 lb in pre-15th century England, 112 lb (long) in the UK, and 100 lb (short) in the US. Led to long ton and short ton distinctions.

The Metric TonneInternational standards

20th century

The tonne (1,000 kg) was adopted as an SI-compatible unit for trade and science. Now the most common form of ton in international trade.

A ton is always 2,000 pounds — In the US, a short ton is 2,000 lb, but a long ton is 2,240 lb and a metric tonne is 1,000 kg.

Ton and tonne are pronounced differently — In the US and UK, they are often pronounced the same; in the UK, 'tonne' may be pronounced /tɒn/.

A ton is a unit of weight only — It can also be a unit of volume (e.g., freight ton, displacement ton) and force.
