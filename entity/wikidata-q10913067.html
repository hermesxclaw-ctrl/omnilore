Who They Are

This page is intentionally blank and ready for source-backed lore, versions, images, and connections. The Three Lords collectively preside over the transition from primordial chaos to civilization. Their domains include: (1) the cosmic order (establishing the patterns that govern the world); (2) the arts of subsistence (fire, hunting, agriculture, herbal medicine); (3) the social order (marriage, kinship, governance); (4) the intellectual order (writing, divination, the trigrams). In later Daoist tradition, they also become associated with cosmic cycles and the transmission of esoteric knowledge.

Form

The Three Lords are rarely described as having a single unified appearance, since they are a collective rather than a single figure. Individual members have their own iconography: Fuxi is typically depicted with a human upper body and a serpent's lower body, sometimes holding a carpenter's square (symbolizing his role in establishing cosmic order); Nüwa similarly has a serpent tail and is often shown holding a compass; Shennong is depicted with a ruddy complexion, sometimes with horns or a leaf garment, holding herbs or a plow. When depicted as a triad, they are often shown as august elderly sages in the robes of ancient rulers, sometimes with cosmic symbols such as the sun, moon, and stars, or the eight trigrams.

Powers

Civilizational Innovation Each Lord introduces a foundational technology or practice that transforms human existence. Suiren discovers how to create fire by drilling wood (and sometimes is said to have been born from fire itself); Fuxi teaches hunting, fishing, animal domestication, and establishes the institution of marriage; Shennong introduces agriculture and discovers the medicinal properties of plants. Attested across multiple texts including the Huainanzi, the Fengsu Tongyi, and later encyclopedias These are not supernatural powers in the modern sense; they are practical innovations presented as world-historical events. The Lords are not depicted as magicians but as teachers and inventors. Different texts assign different innovations to different Lords. Some versions give writing to Fuxi (who creates the trigrams), while others give it to Cangjie, a minister of the Yellow Emperor. Some versions give the institution of marriage to Fuxi, while others attribute it to Nüwa.

Cosmic Longevity The Three Lords are said to have ruled for extraordinarily long periods. Some traditions give each Lord a reign of 18,000 years; other traditions multiply this. The Huainanzi and related texts describe them as inhabiting a primordial time when the normal rules of human life did not yet apply. Attested in apocryphal and Daoist texts; the '18,000 years' figure appears in the Huainanzi and later commentaries These are not personal powers but attributes of the primordial age they inhabit. In some rationalizing traditions, the long reigns are interpreted as the reigns of dynasties or lineages rather than of individual persons. Different texts give wildly different reign lengths. Some traditions give the Three Lords a combined reign of 36,000 years; others give each individual Lord a reign of 18,000 years; still others compress their reigns into more 'historical' spans of a few hundred years.

Cosmic Ordering In the more abstract versions of the tradition, the Three Lords (as Heavenly Lord, Earthly Lord, and Human Lord) are responsible for establishing the fundamental structure of the cosmos. The Heavenly Lord creates the heavens, the Earthly Lord creates the earth, and the Human Lord creates humanity and its institutions. Attested in apocryphal Han texts and later Daoist traditions This version is more mythological and less 'practical' than the culture-hero version. It is not the dominant tradition in popular culture, which tends to favor the Fuxi-Nüwa-Shennong triad. This cosmic version of the Three Lords is difficult to reconcile with the culture-hero version. Are they the same figures on different levels of reality, or are they different figures entirely? The texts do not resolve this.

Transmission of Esoteric Knowledge In Daoist tradition, the Three Lords are associated with the transmission of esoteric knowledge, including the eight trigrams, the calendar, and the principles of yin and yang. Fuxi in particular is credited with creating the eight trigrams, which form the basis of the I Ching (Book of Changes). Attested in Daoist texts including the Yunji Qiqian and later commentaries This is a later development of the tradition, reflecting Daoist interest in Fuxi's trigrams as a cosmic code. The attribution of the trigrams to Fuxi is contested. Some traditions attribute them to the mythical figure of the River Map (Hetu) and the Lo River Writing (Luoshu), which are revealed to Fuxi by a dragon-horse and a turtle respectively.

Behavior & Habitat

As a collective, the Three Lords are characterized as the first civilizers of humanity. They are not typically depicted as warlike or dramatic figures; their heroism is civilizational. They teach, innovate, and establish order. Each Lord brings a specific gift of civilization: Suiren brings fire, Fuxi brings hunting, fishing, and the eight trigrams (or, in some versions, marriage and writing), Shennong brings agriculture and herbal medicine. They are depicted as wise, benevolent, and patient teachers who guide humanity from a state of nature toward organized society.

Accounts

In the beginning, humanity lived like animals: naked, eating raw food, without marriage, without writing, without agriculture. The Three Lords appear at this primordial moment and each brings a gift. Suiren shows humanity how to drill wood to make fire, transforming the diet and providing warmth and protection. Fuxi teaches hunting, fishing, and the domestication of animals; he establishes the institution of marriage, preventing the chaos of unregulated unions. Shennong teaches agriculture, showing humanity how to cultivate the five grains, and tastes hundreds of herbs to discover their medicinal properties, establishing the foundations of Chinese medicine. Each innovation builds on the previous one, moving humanity from subsistence to society to civilization. The Three Lords are thus the bridge between the natural world and the human world.

In the most popular version of the tradition, Fuxi and Nüwa are depicted as siblings (and in some versions, spouses) with human upper bodies and serpent lower bodies. Their intertwined serpent tails appear in Han dynasty stone reliefs, often with Fuxi holding a carpenter's square and Nüwa holding a compass — symbols of cosmic order. In some versions of the flood myth, Fuxi and Nüwa are the only survivors of a great deluge, and they repopulate the earth. In other versions, Nüwa is the creator of humanity, fashioning the first people from yellow clay. The relationship between the two figures is complex and varies across texts: they are sometimes siblings, sometimes spouses, sometimes both. Their serpent bodies mark them as belonging to the primordial, pre-human world, even as they establish the institutions of human society.

Shennong, the Divine Farmer, is said to have tasted hundreds of herbs to discover their medicinal properties. In some versions, he has a transparent stomach, allowing him (and his assistants) to observe the effects of each plant as it is digested. This mythic account establishes the foundation of Chinese herbal medicine. The Shennong Bencao Jing, a Han dynasty text attributed to Shennong, catalogs 365 medicinal substances — one for each day of the year — organized into three categories: superior (non-toxic, life-prolonging), medium (tonic, some toxic), and inferior (strongly acting, often toxic). The text became the foundational pharmacopoeia of Chinese medicine.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of the Three Lords as the Chinese mythological equivalent of a startup founding team. Suiren is the guy who discovers fire — the first killer app. Fuxi is the operations guy who sets up hunting, fishing, and marriage — the first social infrastructure. Shennong is the R&D guy who tests the herbs — the first pharmaceutical company. And like any startup, the founding team keeps getting reshuffled in the retelling: sometimes Nüwa is on the team, sometimes she's not, sometimes Suiren gets replaced by a fire god. The board of directors can't agree on the org chart, but everyone knows the company changed the world.
