Who It Is

The headless horseman of Irish folklore, a terrifying harbinger of death. The dullahan rides a black horse (or drives a black coach) while carrying his own severed head under his arm, which glows with phosphorescent light and grins with a rictus smile. The head possesses supernatural sight, able to see across the countryside even in darkness. The dullahan calls out the name of the person about to die, at which point that person immediately perishes. All gates and doors fly open before him; no lock can bar his passage. He uses a human spine as a whip. The dullahan is said to be a vestige of the Celtic god Crom Dubh, to whom human sacrifices were made.
