Who It Is

In the summer of 1969, before half a million people at Hyde Park, a new band took the stage as support for the Rolling Stones and unleashed a sound that seemed to come from a different world—dark, intricate, and unyielding. That band was King Crimson, and their debut album would soon be hailed as an 'uncanny masterpiece' by Pete Townshend. For over five decades, through countless lineups and hiatuses, King Crimson has remained a singular force in music, a collective that defied easy categorization and influenced generations of artists across genres. This dossier explores the full history, music, and enduring legacy of one of progressive rock's most enigmatic and influential acts.

King Crimson is known for its intense, complex, and often improvisational music. The band has never been a conventional rock group; they are known for their rigorous musicianship, experimental approach, and Fripp's strict leadership. The band has also been known for its frequent lineup changes and periods of inactivity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — King Crimson's visual identity evolved over time. In the early 1970s, they were known for their elaborate stage lighting, designed by Peter Sinfield, and often wore dark, theatrical clothing. In the 1980s lineup, the band adopted a more contemporary look. The band's album covers often featured distinctive artwork, such as the iconic screaming face on 'In the Court of the Crimson King'.

Symbols — The band is often associated with the image from their debut album cover, a screaming face, which became a symbol of their intense and dramatic music. The name 'Crimson' suggests a deep red color, often used in their album art and stage lighting.

Domains — Primarily active in the UK and internationally, with significant tours in the US and Europe. The band's music often evokes vast, cosmic, and dark landscapes, reflecting a European sensibility blending antiquity and modernity.

Accounts I
Formation and debutWikipedia article

1968-1969

King Crimson formed in November 1968 from the remnants of Giles, Giles and Fripp, with the addition of Greg Lake and Peter Sinfield. Their debut album, 'In the Court of the Crimson King', was released in 1969 and became an instant success, establishing them as a leading force in progressive rock. The album's success led to a US tour, but creative tensions caused McDonald and Giles to leave at the end of the tour.

The 1972-1974 experimental eraWikipedia article

1972-1974

Fripp disbanded the previous lineup and formed a new one with Bill Bruford, John Wetton, David Cross, and Jamie Muir. This version of the band drew from European free improvisation and modernist classical music, producing albums like 'Larks' Tongues in Aspic' and 'Red'. This lineup disbanded in 1974, leading to a seven-year hiatus.

Accounts II
The 1980s Discipline eraWikipedia article

1981-1984

Fripp reformed the band with Bruford, Adrian Belew, and Tony Levin. This version incorporated African music, gamelan, post-punk, and New York minimalism, resulting in the albums 'Discipline', 'Beat', and 'Three of a Perfect Pair'. The band disbanded in 1984, leading to another decade-long hiatus.

The Double Trio and Double DuoWikipedia article

1994-2003

In 1994, Fripp reformed the band with Belew, Levin, and new members Pat Mastelotto and Trey Gunn, forming a sextet known as the 'Double Trio'. They released 'Thrak' in 1995. In 1999, they became a quartet (the 'Double Duo') and released 'The ConstruKction of Light' and 'The Power to Believe'. The band went on hiatus again in 2003.

The 2013-2021 septet/octetWikipedia article

2013-2021

After another hiatus, King Crimson reformed in 2013 as a septet with three drummers, and later expanded to an octet in 2017. This version toured extensively until 2021, when Fripp announced the band had 'moved from sound to silence'. The band's final show was in 2021, marking the end of King Crimson's active career.
