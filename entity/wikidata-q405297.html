Aillen is associated with the Otherworld (Tír na nÓg or the fairy realm), fire, destruction, and the liminal festival of Samhain. He embodies the threat of the Otherworld to the established order of the mortal world, particularly the sacred kingship of Tara.

Form

Aillen's physical form is not described in detail in the primary sources. He is presented as a supernatural being from the Otherworld, capable of taking on a human-like form to approach Tara. He is not described as a dragon, giant, or animal. The key features are his musical instrument (a harp or pipe) and his fire-breathing ability. He is often depicted in modern art as a handsome, otherworldly man with fiery motif, but this is artistic license.

Powers

Fire Breathing Aillen can breathe fire with devastating effect, capable of burning down the great hall of Tara. This is his primary destructive power. Consistent across all versions of the Fenian Cycle. He must be close enough to his target, and he seems to require the element of surprise (i.e., his victims being asleep) to use it effectively. None noted in the primary sources.

Enchanting Music Aillen plays a harp or pipe with such sweet and magical music that it puts all who hear it into a deep, unbreakable sleep, rendering them helpless against his fiery attack. Consistent across all versions. The music's power can be negated by a counter-magic, as Fionn does by pressing the sharp point of his spear against his own forehead. The pain of the spear-point keeps him awake. None noted.

Otherworldly Nature As a being from the Otherworld, Aillen is not subject to normal mortal limitations. He can appear and disappear at will, and his motives are inscrutable by mortal standards. Implied in the primary sources. His power seems to be tied to the festival of Samhain, when the boundaries between worlds are thinnest. None.

Behavior & Habitat

Aillen is a destructive and cunning figure. His modus operandi is to approach the royal hall of Tara during the festival of Samhain (the beginning of winter, Nov 1), lull the inhabitants into a deep sleep with his sweet music, and then breathe fire to burn the hall to the ground. He is persistent, returning year after year for nine years, and is only defeated by Fionn's clever use of his spear to ward off the music's magic.

Accounts

For nine consecutive Samhain festivals, Aillen mac Midgna emerges from the Otherworld and burns the royal hall of Tara to the ground. He does this by playing enchanting music that puts the entire court, including the king and the Fianna, to sleep. The High King, Cormac mac Airt, is powerless to stop him. A young Fionn, son of Cumhall (the former leader of the Fianna who was killed by Goll mac Morna), arrives at Tara seeking his rightful place. He is initially mocked for his youth. On the night of Samhain, Fionn defies the sleeping court and waits for Aillen. When Aillen plays his music, Fionn presses the point of his own spear, the magical 'Fiacha' or his father's spear, against his forehead, using the pain to stay awake. When Aillen breathes fire, Fionn uses his cloak to shield himself. As Aillen retreats back to the Otherworld, Fionn follows and, in a final confrontation, kills him with a blow from his spear. He then cuts off Aillen's head and brings it back to Tara, proving his deed and winning the leadership of the Fianna.

What We Don't Know

The primary gap is the lack of a detailed physical description of Aillen in the primary sources. His exact nature (his species, if any) is left ambiguous. The etymology of his name and his father's name (Midgna) is also uncertain. A deeper search into specific manuscript variants of Macgnímartha Finn could yield more detail. The reception of Aillen in modern Irish-language literature and drama is also a potential area for further research.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Aillen as a mythic saboteur, a supernatural being who uses the most beautiful art — music — as a weapon of mass destruction. He is the original 'sleeping gas' and 'flamethrower' combo, a one-two punch that made the mightiest court in Ireland helpless. He is the ultimate test for a hero: not just a monster to be fought, but a problem to be solved, a spell to be broken, and a fire to be quenched.
