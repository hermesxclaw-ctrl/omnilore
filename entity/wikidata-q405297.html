Who It Is

For nine long Samhain nights, the High King of Ireland and his fiercest warriors were helpless. A beautiful, otherworldly melody would drift across the Hill of Tara, and before the last note faded, every hero in the hall would be locked in an enchanted slumber — just in time for the fire to come. The burner of Tara was Aillen, a being of music and flame, who each year reduced the seat of Irish kingship to ash. And he would have kept his fiery reign forever, if not for a young, untested warrior with a sharp spear and the will to hurt himself to stay awake.

Aillen is a destructive and cunning figure. His modus operandi is to approach the royal hall of Tara during the festival of Samhain (the beginning of winter, Nov 1), lull the inhabitants into a deep sleep with his sweet music, and then breathe fire to burn the hall to the ground. He is persistent, returning year after year for nine years, and is only defeated by Fionn's clever use of his spear to ward off the music's magic.

It is most often linked to the Old Irish word 'aill' meaning 'rock' or 'cliff', though some scholars suggest a connection to 'áil' meaning 'desire' or 'wish'. Powers
Fire Breathing

Aillen can breathe fire with devastating effect, capable of burning down the great hall of Tara. This is his primary destructive power.

Limit: He must be close enough to his target, and he seems to require the element of surprise (i.e., his victims being asleep) to use it effectively.

Enchanting Music

Aillen plays a harp or pipe with such sweet and magical music that it puts all who hear it into a deep, unbreakable sleep, rendering them helpless against his fiery attack.

Limit: The music's power can be negated by a counter-magic, as Fionn does by pressing the sharp point of his spear against his own forehead. The pain of the spear-point keeps him awake.

Otherworldly Nature

As a being from the Otherworld, Aillen is not subject to normal mortal limitations. He can appear and disappear at will, and his motives are inscrutable by mortal standards.

Limit: His power seems to be tied to the festival of Samhain, when the boundaries between worlds are thinnest.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Aillen's physical form is not described in detail in the primary sources. He is presented as a supernatural being from the Otherworld, capable of taking on a human-like form to approach Tara. He is not described as a dragon, giant, or animal. The key features are his musical instrument (a harp or pipe) and his fire-breathing ability. He is often depicted in modern art as a handsome, otherworldly man with fiery motif, but this is artistic license.

Symbols — Fire, music, the harp or pipe, Samhain (the boundary between worlds).

Domains — Aillen is associated with the Otherworld (Tír na nÓg or the fairy realm), fire, destruction, and the liminal festival of Samhain. He embodies the threat of the Otherworld to the established order of the mortal world, particularly the sacred kingship of Tara.

Accounts I
The Burning of Tara and Fionn's VengeanceThe Boyhood Deeds of Fionn (Macgnímartha Finn)

Mythological (set in the 3rd century AD, first written down in the 12th century)

For nine consecutive Samhain festivals, Aillen mac Midgna emerges from the Otherworld and burns the royal hall of Tara to the ground. He does this by playing enchanting music that puts the entire court, including the king and the Fianna, to sleep. The High King, Cormac mac Airt, is powerless to stop him. A young Fionn, son of Cumhall (the former leader of the Fianna who was killed by Goll mac Morna), arrives at Tara seeking his rightful place. He is initially mocked for his youth. On the night of Samhain, Fionn defies the sleeping court and waits for Aillen. When Aillen plays his music, Fionn presses the point of his own spear, the magical 'Fiacha' or his father's spear, against his forehead, using the pain to stay awake. When Aillen breathes fire, Fionn uses his cloak to shield himself. As Aillen retreats back to the Otherworld, Fionn follows and, in a final confrontation, kills him with a blow from his spear.
