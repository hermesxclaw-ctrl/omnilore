The Approach

In the spring of 1999, a small bay colt with a white blaze charged down the homestretch of Tokyo Racecourse, overtaking the mighty T. M. Opera O and Narita Top Road to claim the Tokyo Yushun—a victory that would echo through Japanese racing history. But Admire Vega's story is not just one of triumph; it is a tale of fragility, sudden loss, and a legacy that transcended the turf, reaching into the realms of anime and folklore.

Content advisory. Contains references to death, injury, and survivor's guilt in a fictional adaptation.
Identity

Admire Vega

Pronunciation AD-mire VAY-guh (English); A-do-MAI-ya BE-ga (Japanese)

Known As

アドマイヤベガ (Adomaiya Bega)AdVe (アドベ)Ayabe (アヤベ) in Umamusume

Etymology

The name 'Admire' is a common prefix for horses owned by Riichi Kondo, while 'Vega' likely refers to the star Vega, continuing a celestial naming theme. The Japanese transliteration is アドマイヤベガ.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese Thoroughbred racing culture; also a character in the Japanese media franchise Umamusume: Pretty Derby.

Disputed Identifications

None known. The name is unique to this horse.

Description

Appearance

Bay horse with a white blaze and a white sock on his left hind leg. Smaller than average for a male Thoroughbred, with a maximum racing weight of 464 kg.

Symbols & Iconography

His racing silks were owned by Riichi Kondo; no specific symbols are documented. In Umamusume, his character design features a somber expression and twin-tails reminiscent of his twin sister motif.

Behaviour & Personality

As a racehorse, he was known for his determination and late-closing style. In Umamusume, he is depicted as somber, gloomy, and driven by survivor's guilt over his twin sister's death.

Domains & Habitats

Raced primarily on turf at Japanese racecourses: Kyoto, Hanshin, Nakayama, Tokyo. Stood at Shadai Stallion Station in Hokkaido.

Version Differences

The real horse was male; the Umamusume character is female. The character's name was changed from AdVe to Ayabe to avoid trademark issues with Adobe Inc. The character's personality is fictionalized.

Stories & Myths

1999 Tokyo Yushun Victory

Source Racing records, news reportsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1999

Summary

Admire Vega, ridden by Yutaka Take, won the 66th Tokyo Yushun (Japanese Derby) by a neck over Narita Top Road, with T. M. Opera O third. This was his greatest career achievement.

Premature Death

Source News reportsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2004

Summary

Admire Vega died suddenly on 29 October 2004 from an accidental stomach rupture. A funeral was held on 23 November, attended by 200 people including fans, trainer Hashida, and politician Seiko Hashimoto.
