The Approach

In the autumn of 1995, a 560-kilogram behemoth thundered down the Nakayama turf, carrying more weight than any horse had ever carried to victory in a Japanese Grade 1 race. This was Hishi Akebono—a sprinter of extraordinary power and heart, whose legacy as a champion was matched only by the affectionate memory of his insatiable appetite.

Identity

Hishi Akebono

Pronunciation HEE-shee AH-keh-boh-noh

Etymology

Hishi (ヒシ) is likely a stable prefix, while Akebono (アケボノ) means 'dawn' in Japanese.

Cultural Ownership

American-bred, Japanese-trained Thoroughbred racehorse; owned by Japanese interests and raced in Japan.

Disputed Identifications

None known.

Description

Appearance

Black stallion, noted for his massive frame and heavy weight, often exceeding 560 kg.

Symbols & Iconography

His weight and power became his defining symbols, earning him a reputation as a heavy-framed sprinter.

Behaviour & Personality

Known for a powerful closing kick and exceptional weight-carrying ability. In popular culture, depicted as cheerful and food-loving, with a strong sense of justice.

Domains & Habitats

Raced on turf in Japan, primarily at Tokyo, Chukyo, Nakayama, and other JRA tracks. Stood at stud in Chiba and Ibaraki prefectures.

Version Differences

Real horse versus anthropomorphized character in Umamusume: Pretty Derby, where he is portrayed as a tall (180 cm) girl who loves eating.
