Who It Is

Percival (Perceval, Peredur, Parzival) is the original Grail Knight — the hero of Chrétien de Troyes's unfinished Perceval, the Story of the Grail (c. 1180s), the earliest surviving Grail narrative. His story is a bildungsroman: the education of a naive, wild youth into a knight worthy of the Grail. Raised in the Welsh forest by his mother, who has hidden him from all knowledge of chivalry after his father and brothers were killed in knightly combat, Percival first sees knights as shining angels and sets off in comical ignorance to join them — wearing homespun clothes, riding a hunting horse, and misapplying every scrap of his mother's well-meant advice.

Percival's early adventures are defined by his literal-mindedness and innocence. Told by his mother to always ask a person's name, he interrogates everyone he meets. Told to accept gifts from ladies, he wrestles a maiden for her ring and kisses her against her will, understanding nothing of courtesy. His natural gifts — strength, courage, and a good heart — are undisciplined. His education comes through a series of mentors: the knight Gornemant de Goort, who teaches him chivalry and weapons; the lady Blanchefleur, who teaches him love; and the hermit-uncle, who teaches him spirituality.

The Grail episode is Percival's defining test — and his defining failure. Visiting the castle of the wounded Fisher King, he witnesses a mysterious procession: a bleeding lance, a Grail (a serving dish, in Chrétien; a stone, in Wolfram) that emits light but holds a single mass-wafer, and other strange objects. Percival, trained by Gornemant not to ask too many questions, remains silent — and in the morning, the castle is empty. He later learns that if he had asked 'Whom does the Grail serve?', he would have healed the wounded king and restored the wasteland. His silence was a sin of excessive obedience, a failure of compassion, a spiritual immaturity.

In Wolfram von Eschenbach's German masterpiece Parzival (c. 1210), the hero's journey is deepened with theological and alchemical symbolism. Parzival wanders for years, renouncing God for seeming to abandon him, until a hermit (Trevrizent) teaches him humility and the nature of the Grail — a stone that fell from Lucifer's crown during the war in heaven, guarded by a holy order of knights. Parzival returns to the Grail Castle, asks the healing question, cures the Fisher King (his uncle Anfortas), and becomes Grail King. Wolfram makes Parzival the father of Lohengrin, the Swan Knight — linking the Grail legend to the medieval German imperial mythology.

Percival represents a different path to the Grail than Galahad's. He is not born pure; he achieves purity through suffering, error, and repentance. His story is one of growth from ignorance to wisdom, from selfishness to compassion — a human journey rather than a saintly predestination.
