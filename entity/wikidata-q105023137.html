Who It Is

In the dense spiritual landscape of Afro-Brazilian religion, Oxóssi emerges as the lord of the forests, a hunter whose arrow pierces the veil between the mundane and the sacred. Yet the name 'Oxóssi Tupi da Pedra Preta' carries a peculiar weight, blending the Yoruba deity with indigenous Tupi identity and a mysterious black stone. This figure, largely undocumented in mainstream sources, invites us to explore the intersections of African and indigenous Brazilian spirituality, where the boundaries between traditions blur and new forms of devotion arise.

Oxóssi is known as a solitary, wise, and generous hunter. He is associated with the pursuit of knowledge and the provision of food. He is often seen as a protector of the forest and its creatures. The 'Tupi' epithet might suggest a closer connection to the land and indigenous hunting practices.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Oxóssi derives from Yoruba, often associated with the hunter deity Odé. 'Tupi' refers to the indigenous Tupi people of Brazil, suggesting a syncretic link. 'Pedra Preta' means 'black stone' in Portuguese, possibly referencing a sacred stone (otá) used in Candomblé to represent the Orixá.

Pronunciation — oh-SHAW-see TOO-pee dah PEH-drah PREH-tah

Aliases — Oxóssi, Odé, Ode, Oxotocan, Oxóssi de Pedra Preta

Ownership — Afro-Brazilian, specifically Candomblé and Umbanda, with possible indigenous Brazilian influences. The 'Tupi' element indicates a unique local manifestation, possibly from a terrei

Form & Signs

Appearance — Oxóssi is typically depicted as a handsome young hunter, often wearing a green or blue cloak, carrying a bow and arrow (ofwa). He may be associated with the color green and with natural elements. The 'Tupi da Pedra Preta' variant might incorporate indigenous Brazilian elements, such as feathered headdresses or body paint, but this is speculative.

Symbols — Symbols include the bow and arrow, the ofwa (a metal or wooden staff), and the color green. The 'Pedra Preta' might be a sacred stone (otá) used in rituals to represent the Orixá, possibly black in color. In Candomblé, otás are consecrated stones that house the Orixá's energy.

Domains — Forests, hunting grounds, and nature. In Candomblé, he is one of the most popular Orixás, especially in Brazil. The 'Pedra Preta' could refer to a specific sacred site, such as a rock formation or a stone altar.

Oxóssi is often associated with the myth of Odé, a hunter who was chosen to be king after providing food for his people. He is also linked to the story of Oxóssi's brother, Ogum, and their relationship. Oxóssi becomes a revered king and hunter, symbolizing the ideal leader who provides and protects.
