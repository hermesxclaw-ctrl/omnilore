Who It Is

Within the snowbound village of Resident Evil Village, House Beneviento stands apart—a decaying manor where the air thickens with dread and the line between the living and the crafted blurs. Here, Donna Beneviento, a reclusive noblewoman shrouded in grief, commands not through brute force but through the subtle, terrifying power of hallucination and a ventriloquist's doll named Angie. Her story is a tragic echo of loss, a century of mourning twisted by Mother Miranda's experiments, and a haunting reminder that in the world of Resident Evil, even the most broken soul can become a monster.

Donna is reclusive, shy, and deeply traumatized by the loss of her family. She speaks rarely, and when she does, it is often through Angie, who is brash, cruel, and mocking. This split personality suggests severe dissociative identity disorder, with Angie embodying her repressed anger and malice. She is fiercely loyal to Mother Miranda, whom she sees as a maternal figure, and is willing to kill to protect her. Her hallucinations and illusions are a manifestation of her grief and desire to control a world that has caused her pain.

Powers
Hallucination Inducement

Donna can induce terrifying hallucinations in her victims, making them see and experience things that are not real. This is used to disorient, frighten, and psychologically break her targets. The hallucinations are so potent that they can cause physical harm, as seen when Ethan is forced to saw off his own hand to escape a hallucinated trap.

Limit: The hallucinations appear to require close proximity or a specific trigger, such as inhaling a hallucinogenic agent. They are not permanent and can be resisted with strong will or outside intervention. The exact mechanism is not fully explained.

Puppetry and Ventriloquism

Donna controls her doll Angie as an extension of herself, speaking through her and moving her with lifelike precision. Angie acts as her proxy, delivering threats and taunts, and can even act independently to a degree. This allows Donna to interact with others while remaining hidden and protected.

Limit: Angie is a physical doll and can be destroyed, which would sever Donna's connection to that particular proxy. It is unclear if she can transfer her consciousness to other dolls.

Mimicry and Illusion

Donna is able to create illusions that mimic the appearance of other people or objects, as seen when she impersonates Mia to lure Ethan into a trap. This is a more advanced form of her hallucination power, creating a shared, seemingly solid illusion.

Limit: The illusion is not perfect and can be disrupted if the victim realizes the deception. It appears to require concentration and a direct line of sight.

Superhuman Durability and Regeneration

As a result of the Cadou parasite, Donna possesses enhanced physical resilience and regenerative abilities, allowing her to survive injuries that would be fatal to a normal human. This is common among the lords.

Limit: Her regeneration is not unlimited; she can be killed if her body is destroyed sufficiently, notably by decapitation or complete dismemberment.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Donna Beneviento is a slender woman of indeterminate age, typically seen wearing a black mourning veil that obscures her face, a high-collared black dress, and gloves. Her face, when glimpsed, appears pale and gaunt, with dark hollows around her eyes. She moves with a ghostly, almost floating gait. She is often accompanied by her doll, Angie, a porcelain-faced marionette with a cracked visage.

Symbols — Her primary symbol is the doll Angie, representing her fractured psyche and her role as a puppeteer. The House Beneviento itself, with its decaying Victorian decor and pervasive imagery of death and mourning, reflects her grief. The veil she wears signifies her withdrawal from the world and her hidden, monstrous nature.

Domains — She resides in House Beneviento, a large, dilapidated manor located in a secluded, misty area of the village. The house is filled with dolls, mannequins, and unsettling memorabilia, and is designed to disorient and terrify intruders. It is a place of psychological torment, reflecting her own fractured mind.

Accounts I
The Tragedy of House BenevientoIn-game documents, environmental storytelling

Early 20th century to 2021

Donna Beneviento was born into a wealthy family that owned a doll-making business. Her parents and relatives died in a tragic accident, leaving her alone and traumatized. She became reclusive, finding solace only in her dolls. Mother Miranda, recognizing her vulnerability and potential, implanted her with a Cadou parasite, which granted her powers but also twisted her mind. She became one of Miranda's lords, ruling over a section of the village, using her abilities to control and torment those who entered her domain. Donna's transformation into a lord cemented her loyalty to Miranda and her isolation from humanity. Her powers made her a formidable guardian of one of the flasks containing Rose's body parts. Her defeat at Ethan's hands results in her death and the recovery of the flask.

Accounts II
The Confrontation with Ethan WintersResident Evil Village (game)

February 9, 2021

Ethan Winters enters House Beneviento in search of the flask containing Rose's head. Donna, using her illusions, torments him with visions of Mia and a monstrous baby, forcing him to endure psychological horror. She attempts to kill him with a hallucination that makes him believe he is cutting off his own hand. Ethan eventually breaks free of the illusion, finds the real Donna, and kills her, retrieving the flask. Donna's death eliminates one of Miranda's lords and brings Ethan one step closer to restoring Rose. It also reveals the extent of Miranda's manipulation and the tragic nature of her followers.
