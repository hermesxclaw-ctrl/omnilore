The Approach

Before the first dynasty, before the first emperor, there was a king with twelve heads who ruled for eighteen thousand years—the Heavenly Sovereign, whose name is whispered at the very dawn of Chinese civilization.

Identity

Heavenly Sovereign

Pronunciation Tiānhuáng (tee-en hwahng)

Known As

TianhuangTianling

Etymology

Heavenly Sovereign (天皇; pinyin: Tiānhuáng) means 'Heavenly Emperor' or 'Heavenly Sovereign.' The name Tianling (天靈) means 'heavenly spirit.'

Cultural Ownership

Chinese mythology

Disputed Identifications

Sometimes conflated with other figures in the Three Sovereigns; the exact identity and number of heads vary by source.

Description

Appearance

Attested: Twelve heads (Sima Zhen) or thirteen heads (other sources). No other physical description is given.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are recorded.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: Unselfish, calm, and retiring; did not actively intervene but people's customs improved. A king of many achievements, ruling under the influence of the wood element.

Domains & Habitats

Ruled the world after the formation of Heaven and Earth. His remains said to be under Kunlun in Zhuzhou.

Version Differences

Number of heads varies: 12 (Sima Zhen) vs 13 (other sources). Some sources say he had 13 leaders or 13 people with the same family name. The Chunqiu Wei mentions Heavenly, Earthly, and Human Sovereigns with 9 brothers, dividing the world into Nine Provinces.

Stories & Myths

Suppression of Chaos and Division of the World

Source Sima Zhen's supplement to Records of the Grand HistorianCulture ChinesePeriod Legendary

Summary

After Heaven and Earth were formed, the Heavenly Sovereign ruled with twelve heads. He suppressed all chaos, divided the world for the many tribes, and chose the best people to rule them. He lived 18,000 years and his twelve children supported him.

Participants

Heavenly Sovereign

His twelve children

Tribes

Consequences

Established order and governance; his successor was the Earthly Sovereign.

Variants

Other sources describe different numbers of heads or leaders, and some attribute the division of the Nine Provinces to the Three Sovereigns collectively.
