Tawhirimatea

God of Storms and Weather; The Angry Wind; Father of the Four Winds

also known asTāwhirimāteaTawhiri
TypeStorm/Weather Deity
CultureMāori
Who It Is
Form

Tāwhirimātea is often depicted as a powerful and fierce god, associated with the elements of wind, thunder, and lightning. He is said to be the father of the four winds, and is often shown with a fierce expression and a powerful build.

Powers

Control over the elements Tāwhirimātea has the power to control the elements of wind, thunder, and lightning, and is said to be able to summon storms and whirlwinds with his anger.

Accounts

Tāwhirimātea and his brothers are said to have been born to the sky father Ranginui and the earth mother Papatūānuku. However, their brothers were jealous of Tāwhirimātea's power and sought to overthrow him. Tāwhirimātea, in turn, sought to destroy his brothers and their families, leading to a great battle between the two sides.

Tāwhirimātea is said to have had four sons, each of whom was associated with a different wind. The four winds were said to be the north wind, the south wind, the east wind, and the west wind. Tāwhirimātea was said to have control over the winds, and was able to summon them at will.

Scholarly Debates
Tāwhirimātea's role in the creation of the world
Tāwhirimātea is said to have played a key role in the creation of the world, separating the sky and the earth and creating the world as we know it today. ⇌ Tāwhirimātea is said to have had no role in the creation of the world, and is instead a god of the elements and the weather.
What We Don't Know

There is a lack of information on the role of Tāwhirimātea in Māori mythology, particularly in relation to his relationship with his brothers and his role in the creation of the world.

A way to hold it in your mind

Tāwhirimātea is a complex and multifaceted god, with a rich history and mythology. His story is one of conflict and creation, and is still celebrated today by the Māori people.
