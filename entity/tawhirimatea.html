Tawhirimatea

God of Storms and Weather; The Angry Wind; Father of the Four Winds

You separated my father from my mother, and now I will never let you forget what you did. Every storm, every wind — that is my grief, and it will never end.

Who It Is

Tāwhirimātea is the Māori god of storms, wind, thunder, lightning, and all weather phenomena — the furious son of Ranginui (Sky Father) and Papatūānuku (Earth Mother) who refused to accept his parents' separation. While all the other brothers agreed to or acquiesced to Tāne's plan to push their parents apart, Tāwhirimātea alone raged against it. After the separation, he ascended to the sky to join his father Ranginui, and from there he launched a devastating war against his brothers. He attacked Tāne Mahuta, shattering the great trees of the forest; he lashed Tangaroa, churning the oceans into devastating waves; he pursued Rongo and Haumia, who hid underground. Only Tūmatauenga stood his ground against the storm. To this day, Tāwhirimātea's anger has not fully abated — every storm, every hurricane, every gale is his continuing war against his brothers. He is the father of the four winds (north, south, east, west), and his children include rain, hail, and the thunderous clouds. In Māori tradition, Tāwhirimātea must be respected whenever venturing onto the sea or into the forest — his domain is everywhere above the earth, and his mood can change in an instant. He is not worshipped so much as appeased, and traditional karakia (prayers) were chanted before journeys to ensure his favor. Tāwhirimātea represents the Māori understanding that nature is not always benevolent — it contains fury, and that fury is not meaningless but is the expression of a primordial grief that the cosmos itself still carries: the pain of separation between heaven and earth.
