Who It Is

Apu Illapu (or simply the apus, 'lords') refers to the mountain-spirit cult within Inca religion — the belief that the high peaks of the Andes (the apus) were living deities who controlled the rain, clouds, and meltwater that sustained agriculture in the arid high valleys. Each community had its local apu (mountain god), and the most powerful — Ausangate, Salkantay, Machu Picchu (a sacred peak, not just the citadel), and others — were worshipped across the empire. Apu Illapu as a unified title means 'Lord Rain-Bringer,' and his cult involved the capacocha (a state-sponsored child sacrifice) in which perfectly-formed, unblemished children were taken to the highest peaks and buried alive as messengers to the mountain gods. The frozen mummies of these capacocha sacrifices — such as the famous 'Ice Maiden' (Juanita) on Ampato — have been among the most remarkable archaeological discoveries of the 20th century, preserving Inca textiles, ritual practice, and the physical evidence of their most solemn offering.
