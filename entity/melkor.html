Who It Is

Melkor, later named Morgoth ('Dark Enemy') by the Elves, is the original Dark Lord of Tolkien's legendarium — the most powerful of the Ainur who rebelled against Eru Ilúvatar in the primordial music of creation and spent the entire First Age waging war against the Valar, the Elves, and Men in his attempt to claim Arda as his own. His story is Tolkien's grand Miltonic tragedy: the greatest of all created beings, who possessed a share of all the gifts of his brethren (including Manwë's lordship, Aulë's craftsmanship, Ulmo's power over water), and who fell through pride into nihilistic hatred. In the Ainulindalë, Melkor introduced discord into the Great Music, seeking to weave themes of his own imagining rather than harmonizing with Eru's design. Eru revealed that even this rebellion was part of a greater plan, which enraged Melkor — now motivated not merely by creative pride but by a desire to unmake and destroy what he could not control. When the Ainur descended into Arda, Melkor claimed it as his own, and when the Valar shaped the world, Melkor marred everything they made. He raised mountains only to have the Valar level them; he filled valleys only to have them flooded. The Silmarillion describes this as a cosmic contest of creation and destruction, with Melkor pouring his own power into the very physical substance of Arda — a concept Tolkien called 'Morgoth's Ring,' meaning that all matter in Middle-earth is tainted with his corruptive essence. Melkor's most devastating act was the destruction of the Two Trees of Valinor (with the aid of the spider-monster Ungoliant), draining their light and plunging the world into darkness except for the last fruits that became the Sun and Moon. He stole the Silmarils, the three jewels crafted by Fëanor that contained the light of the Trees, setting them in his iron crown and precipitating the wars of the First Age. His servants included Sauron (his chief lieutenant), the Balrogs, and dragons including Ancalagon the Black. Unlike Sauron — whose evil was instrumental, seeking to order the world according to his own design — Melkor's evil became purely nihilistic: he descended from a creator who wanted his own world to rule into a destroyer who wanted to annihilate everything not his. At the end of the First Age, the Valar intervened directly (the War of Wrath), defeated Melkor, cut off his feet, and cast him through the Door of Night into the Timeless Void, where he awaits the prophesied Dagor Dagorath (the Final Battle), when he will return for the last war and be slain by Túrin Turambar. Melkor is Tolkien's Satan: the most powerful angel who fell furthest.
