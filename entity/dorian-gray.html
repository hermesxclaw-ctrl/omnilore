Who It Is
Form

Dorian Gray is described as a young and beautiful man, with a 'face like ivory' and 'lips like roses'. He has a slender and athletic build, with 'handsome' features and a 'youthful' appearance. However, as the story progresses, his portrait, which reflects the true state of his soul, becomes increasingly disfigured and twisted, revealing the corruption and decay that lies beneath his beautiful exterior. The contrast between his physical appearance and the state of his soul serves to highlight the tension between beauty and morality, and the dangers of prioritizing the former over the latter. Furthermore, Dorian's appearance is not just a physical description, but also a reflection of his inner character, with his beauty and charm serving as a mask for his darker impulses and desires.

Powers

Youth and Beauty Dorian Gray's most significant power is his ability to remain youthful and beautiful, despite the corrupting influence of his desires and the passage of time. This power is reflected in his portrait, which becomes increasingly disfigured and twisted as the story progresses, while his physical appearance remains unchanged.

Accounts

The story of Dorian Gray is a philosophical and aesthetic exploration of the human condition, beauty, morality, and the supernatural. It follows the life of a young and beautiful man, Dorian Gray, who becomes infatuated with his own image and makes a Faustian bargain to sell his soul in exchange for eternal youth and beauty. As the story progresses, Dorian's portrait, which reflects the true state of his soul, becomes increasingly disfigured and twisted, revealing the corruption and decay that lies beneath his beautiful exterior.

The preface to The Picture of Dorian Gray is a philosophical and aesthetic manifesto that outlines the author's views on art, beauty, and morality. It argues that art should be appreciated for its own sake, rather than for any moral or didactic purpose, and that the pursuit of beauty is a fundamental aspect of the human experience.

Scholarly Debates
Morality
Some scholars argue that The Picture of Dorian Gray is a moral tale, warning against the dangers of vanity and the pursuit of pleasure. ⇌ Others argue that the story is amoral, celebrating the beauty and individuality of Dorian Gray and rejecting the conventional moral norms of Victorian society.
What We Don't Know

One of the significant gaps in the research on Dorian Gray is the lack of attention to the character's psychological and emotional development. While the story provides a detailed account of Dorian's actions and behaviors, it does not offer a comprehensive understanding of his inner thoughts and feelings. Further research is needed to explore the psychological and emotional complexities of Dorian Gray and to provide a more nuanced understanding of his character.

A way to hold it in your mind

The story of Dorian Gray is a timeless and thought-provoking tale that continues to fascinate readers and scholars to this day. Its exploration of the human condition, beauty, morality, and the supernatural raises important questions about the nature of reality and the human experience. As a cultural icon, Dorian Gray represents the tension between the pursuit of beauty and the pursuit of virtue, highlighting the dangers of unchecked vanity and the corrupting influence of desire.
