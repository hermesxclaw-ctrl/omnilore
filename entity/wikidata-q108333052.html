Who It Is

Every culture has a name for the ones who did not go where they were supposed to go — the soul that missed its turn, lingers at the crossroads, or drifts gray and muttering at the edge of the living world. Called anima perdita in Latin liturgy, dusza pokutująca in Polish folklore, and a 'lost soul' in English since the 14th century, it is not a single monster with a fixed face but an archetype: the dead who are unmoored, unjudged, or unwilling to leave.

Theological: a lost soul is characterized by alienation from God, need for intercession, and suffering or restlessness until judgment or purification is complete. Folkloric: behavior is liminal and petitionary, not predatory - it knocks, sighs, appears on anniversaries (especially Zaduszki, All Souls' Day, Nov 2), asks for Masses, prayers, or completion of a vow, and may be grateful or vengeful depending on whether aid is given. Powers
Liminal Manifestation

Ability to appear briefly to the living, often as light, mist, or pale figure, typically at night or on anniversaries. Attested in Polish and Irish folklore; not omnipotent, limited to places/times of death or ritual invitation.

Limit: Cannot manifest without cause (unfinished business, lack of rites); warded by blessed objects, prayer, or daylight; often must be invited or acknowledged

Petition and Intercession Elicitation

Compels or begs the living to pray, have Mass said, or fulfill a vow on its behalf, which then allows it to depart. This is its primary 'power' in folk Catholicism - not coercion but supplication.

Limit: Requires a living intercessor; ineffective if ignored; Church doctrine says suffrages help souls in purgatory but does not confirm apparitions

Omen and Forewarning

Presence signals impending death, misfortune, or need for ritual correction. Hearing a lost soul's wail or seeing its light is an omen.

Limit: Not predictive in a controlled way; interpreted post hoc

Charge and Flame Suicide Attack (Doom variant)

Levitation, high-speed charge, and explosive self-detonation causing fire and impact damage; resistant to conventional ballistics, weak to plasma.

Limit: Low health, easily destroyed at range, can be shot down mid-charge; cannot open doors or use tools

Obsession Loop and Psychic Obscuration (Pixar variant)

Lost souls in Soul generate a dense, inky shroud that hides their true form and traps them in a repetitive thought loop, rendering them unresponsive and invisible to most humans; can be dispelled by genuine connection or 'spark' intervention.

Limit: Not supernatural power in-universe but psychological state made visible; can be rescued by Joe Gardner and 22 via empathy; no combat abilities

Accounts I
The Souls Who Would Not Choose - Inferno Canto IIIDante Alighieri, Inferno

1308-1321

Dante and Virgil reach the vestibule of Hell and encounter the ignavi - angels who were not rebellious nor faithful, and men who lived without infamy or praise. Their souls, neither in Hell nor Heaven, chase a blank banner while stung by wasps and worms, wailing. They are 'lost' because they refused moral choice, and even Hell rejects them. This establishes the literary archetype of the soul lost through neutrality. Codifies the idea that being spiritually lost can be worse than being damned; influences 700 years of art showing souls as a wailing, faceless mass. The passage is the most cited literary locus for 'lost souls' in Western canon.

The Penitent Soul at the Crossroads (Dusza pokutująca na rozstaju)Oskar Kolberg, Lud; Biegeleisen, U kolebki...; field accounts from Kraków region

Documented 19th century, oral tradition likely earlier

A man who died suddenly without confession appears as a pale light or sighing figure at the crossroads where he died. He cannot enter heaven or hell and begs passersby for three Masses or a pilgrimage to Częstochowa. If a living person promises and fulfills the request, the soul thanks them and vanishes in a brief brightness; if mocked, the soul brings misfortune or follows the mocker home until Zaduszki rites are performed. Reinforces Zaduszki (All Souls' Day) practices: leaving food, lighting candles, praying the Eternal Rest. Provides narrative justification for suffrages for the dead and warns against dying unprepared.

The Lost Souls of the Wild Hunt (Dziki Gon)Polish and German folklore compilations; Jacob Grimm, Deutsche Mythologie (1835) Vol.1 Ch.31; Kolberg

Medieval antecedents, recorded 19th century

On stormy autumn nights, a host of lost souls - those who died violent deaths, hunters who hunted on Sunday, or the unbaptized - are driven across the sky by a spectral huntsman. Hearing the Hunt is an omen; seeing it risks being taken. The souls wail and are not individually petitionary but part of a damned procession. Explains nocturnal noises and storm anxiety; encourages proper burial and observance of holy days. Later romanticized in literature and music (e.g., Mickiewicz's ballads).

Accounts II
Pixar's Soul - Joe Gardner and the Lost Souls in the ZonePixar Animation Studios, Soul, directed by Pete Docter, co-directed by Kemp Powers

2020

Jazz pianist Joe Gardner dies before his big break and finds himself in the Great Before. He discovers 'the zone' where living humans who are obsessively 'in the zone' or anxiously lost become 'lost souls' - hulking, dark, ink-like creatures looping their anxieties (a trader muttering numbers, a hedge-fund manager). Moonwind, a mystic who can enter the zone, rescues them by helping them reconnect to life. Joe himself briefly becomes a lost soul when his obsession blinds him. Reframes 'lost soul' from damnation to mental health metaphor; widely discussed in press as a depiction of depression and burnout. Won Academy Award for Best Animated Feature 2021 and re-popularized the phrase for younger audiences.

Doom 3: Resurrection of Evil - The Lost Soul Swarm in HellNerve Software / id Software, Doom 3: Resurrection of Evil

2005

After the UAC discovers the Artifact on Mars, portals to Hell reopen. The player, as Engineer Thomas Kelly (later possessed), encounters Lost Souls - flaming skulls that spawn from Soul Cubes and Hell portals in bulk. Lore codex says they are souls of the damned forged into demons. They attack in swarms in the Hell levels (Erebus, Hell) and are vulnerable to the Artifact's Hell Time power. Established Lost Soul as a recurring Doom bestiary staple; distinct from classic Doom (1993) Lost Soul by adding fire trail and charge behavior. Frequently cited in game wikis and Let's Plays, causing search confusion with folklore term.

Lost Souls (2000) and Lost Soul (2009) - Cinematic Possession NarrativesLost Souls (2000), dir. Janusz Kamiński, screenplay Pierce Gardner; Lost Soul (2009), dir. Simon Cox, starring Nick Mancuso

2000, 2009

In Lost Souls (2000), Maya Larkin (Winona Ryder), a former Catholic schoolgirl who survived a possession, believes a writer Peter Kelson is destined to become the Antichrist and his soul will be lost to Satan; she attempts exorcism. In Lost Soul (2009), a man deals with a soul trapped between worlds after a violent death. Both use 'lost soul' to mean a person whose soul is claimed by evil or stuck due to trauma. Critically panned but preserved the phrase in horror marketing; Lost Souls (2000) is notable for being Kamiński's directorial debut and for Winona Ryder's Razzie nomination. Illustrates modern conflation of theological and horror meanings.

A 'lost soul' is just another word for ghost that haunts to scare people. — In most folk sources, the lost/penitent soul is not a horror haunting but a suffering petitioner who wants help and is often pitiable. Malicious haunting is attributed to other categories (e.g., upiór, demon). Horror films inverted this for scare value.

The Catholic Church teaches that lost souls wander the earth as ghosts. — Official doctrine does not teach earthbound wandering. It teaches particular judgment, then heaven, purgatory (purification), or hell. Wandering soul belief is folk piety, tolerated but not doctrine, and often explained by theologians as not literal.

Pixar's Soul 'lost souls' are damned souls in Hell. — Filmmakers explicitly state they are living people dissociated by anxiety/obsession, not dead damned. They can be restored in life through mindfulness and connection. The film uses the term metaphorically for mental health.

Lost Soul Mountain is named after a specific legend of a damned prospector's ghost. — No primary USGS or historical record verifies such a legend. GNIS entry lists name without etymology. Any prospector story is local oral lore, unverified and likely post hoc.
