Who It Is

The Rakshasa is one of D&D's most sophisticated and insidious monsters — not a combat brute but a shape-shifting mastermind who weaves itself into the fabric of mortal society to corrupt, manipulate, and consume. In its true form, a Rakshasa is a humanoid creature with the head of a tiger or other great cat, backward-facing hands (palms outward), luxurious striped fur, and an impeccable bearing of aristocratic menace. It dresses in the finest silks, speaks with the eloquence of a diplomat, and moves with the grace of a predator who has nothing to fear. Unlike the animalistic rakshasas of Hindu mythology (from which they draw their name), D&D Rakshasas are subtle schemers who prefer a poisoned word to a clawed strike.

A Rakshasa's most terrifying ability is its near-immunity to magic. In most editions of D&D, Rakshasas are completely immune to spells of 6th level and lower — making them functionally immune to the vast majority of offensive magic that adventurers can bring to bear. Combined with their innate shape-changing ability (allowing them to perfectly impersonate any humanoid), their talent for illusion magic, and their ability to read thoughts and detect lies, Rakshasas are virtually undetectable infiltrators. A Rakshasa might spend decades — even centuries — as a trusted advisor to a king, a beloved merchant, or a respected cleric, slowly steering events toward catastrophe and feasting on the suffering it creates.

When forced into combat, a Rakshasa is deadly — its claws can curse those they wound with despair or disease, and its bite is as lethal as any great cat's. But a Rakshasa considers combat beneath its dignity. Its true weapons are blackmail, bribery, seduction, and the long game. A defeated Rakshasa does not die — its spirit reforms in the Nine Hells and begins plotting revenge, making it one of the few recurring villains in D&D that can hound a party across an entire campaign or even multiple campaigns.

The Rakshasa's D&D incarnation has diverged significantly from its Hindu mythological source, where rakshasas are shape-shifting demons who appear in the Ramayana as antagonists to Rama. While D&D retains the name and the shape-shifting nature, it has transformed them into uniquely D&D-style mastermind villains. Rakshasas appear in Baldur's Gate 3 (in a memorable side quest), the D&D cartoon series, and numerous published adventures where their presence guarantees layers of conspiracy and deception.
