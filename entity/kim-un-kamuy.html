Kim-un-kamuy

The Bear God, The Lord of the Mountains, The Most Honored Kamuy

I am the god who wears the fur coat of the mountain bear. When I visit your village, you honor me with wine and arrows, and I return to the mountains to tell the other gods: these humans are kind.

Who It Is

Kim-un-kamuy ('the kamuy who dwells in the mountains') is the bear god, the most powerful and most revered of all nature kamuy (spirits) in Ainu religion. The brown bear of Hokkaido is considered the earthly manifestation of Kim-un-kamuy — when a bear is seen, it is the god visiting the human world in his fur coat. The most important Ainu ritual, the Iomante (bear-sending ceremony), centered on Kim-un-kamuy. A bear cub would be captured and raised in the village with great care for a year or two, treated as an honored guest. At the ceremony, the bear was killed (usually by archery), its spirit was 'sent back' to the mountain world with prayers, gifts (headdresses, food, and wine), and instructions to tell the other kamuy how well it was treated. The Ainu believed that if the bear was treated with respect and properly sent off, Kim-un-kamuy would return again in another form, ensuring the cycle of life continued. After the ceremony, the bear's meat was distributed and eaten as sacred food. The Iomante was the most elaborate and expensive Ainu ritual, involving all members of the community and guests from neighboring villages. The ritual was suppressed by the Japanese government in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as part of the assimilation of the Ainu people. Today, the Iomante is sometimes performed in modified form as part of Ainu cultural revival. Kim-un-kamuy represents the Ainu understanding of the relationship between humans and nature: respectful taking of life as a sacred exchange between the human and divine worlds.

Attested in Ainu oral tradition (yukar), Ainu indigenous religionEra Pre-modern, central to Ainu ritual until the early 20th century
