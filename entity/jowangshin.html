Who It Is

Jowangshin (Jo-wang-sin) is the Korean kitchen deity, a guardian spirit of the domestic hearth who oversees the household's well-being, particularly the preparation and availability of food. While the Chinese counterpart (Zao Jun, the Kitchen God) is typically male, the Korean Jowangshin is almost exclusively female—a goddess embodying the nurturing, life-sustaining qualities of the hearth. She is believed to reside in a small clay jar or a bowl of water kept on a shelf above the kitchen stove or hearth, known as the jowangdok. This jar is periodically filled with fresh water as an offering, and in traditional households, a ritual (jowanggosa) was performed on the first full moon of the lunar year and on other auspicious occasions, offering rice cakes and praying for abundance and protection from fire. Jowangshin is invoked in Korean shamanic rituals (gut), particularly those concerned with household fortune and family health. In shamanic narrative songs, Jowangshin is sometimes portrayed as a woman who was transformed into a deity after a tragic life, or as a daughter of the Dragon King. Her worship reflects the deep reverence in Korean folk religion for the domestic sphere as a sacred space and for the hearth as the literal and symbolic center of the home, providing warmth, cooked food, and a gathering place for the family. With the modernization of Korean homes and the replacement of traditional hearths with electric and gas stoves, overt Jowangshin worship has declined dramatically, though the deity persists in folk memory and shamanic practice.

Attested in Korean folk religion; oral tradition and household ritualEra Joseon dynasty and earlier; worship practices into 20th century
