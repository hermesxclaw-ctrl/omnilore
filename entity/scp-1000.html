Archive Dossier — Identity

SCP-1000

Pronunciation S-C-P one-thousand; "Bigfoot" /ˈbɪɡ.fʊt/; "Homo nocturnus" /ˈhoʊ.moʊ nɒkˈtɜːr.nəs/.

Known As

BigfootChildren of the NightHomo nocturnusHomo sapiens noctisYeren

Etymology

The designation "SCP-1000" follows the SCP Foundation’s internal cataloging system for anomalous entities. The popular alias "Bigfoot" derives from North American cryptid folklore describing a large, hairy, bipedal ape-like creature. "Children of the Night" and the Latin binomials "Homo nocturnus" / "Homo sapiens noctis" are in-universe taxonomic and mythic names used in SCP-related narratives to emphasize their status as a nocturnal hominid species distinct from Homo sapiens.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-1000 is a fictional entity originating within the collaborative writing project known as the SCP Foundation. The core article "SCP-1000" is credited to the author thedeadlymoose and is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0. The concept draws heavily on North American Bigfoot/Sasquatch folklore and broader cryptid traditions, but its specific characterization, anomalous properties, and historical narrative are original to the SCP Foundation mythos.

Disputed Identifications

Within the SCP narrative, SCP-1000 is explicitly identified as a real, anomalous hominid species whose existence is systematically suppressed by the Foundation. This conflicts with real-world cryptozoological debates, where Bigfoot is generally regarded as unverified or hoax. In-universe, there are minor disputes over whether all regional "Bigfoot"-type sightings (e.g., Yeren in China, Himalayan yeti) are instances of SCP-1000 or separate species; some fan interpretations treat them as variants or related taxa, but this is not definitively resolved in the primary SCP-1000 article. Outside the SCP canon, the name "Bigfoot" is widely used for unrelated fictional and folkloric entities, creating homonymy but not true identification.

Archive Dossier — Approach

Imagine that every blurry photograph of Bigfoot, every campfire story about a shadow in the trees, is not just a joke or a hoax—but a fragment of a buried war crime. In the SCP Foundation’s universe, SCP-1000 is not a single monster but an entire species, a nocturnal cousin of humanity whose cities once covered the Earth. We did not merely outcompete them; we annihilated them with an impossible weapon that turned the act of seeing into a death sentence, then rewrote history so that their survivors would be dismissed as myths. To read SCP-1000 closely is to realize that the real horror is not the creature in the forest, but the civilization that decided it was better if no one ever knew what we did.

SCP-1000 were not always as they are now. They were once the dominant species on this planet. We took that from them. Remember that when you pull the trigger.

Who It Is

SCP-1000 is one of the most significant entries in SCP Series II -- a radical reimagining of the Bigfoot legend that transforms the cryptid into a tragic, world-building centerpiece of the SCP Foundation universe. The skip reveals that Bigfoot ('SCP-1000') are not primitive ape-men but the remnants of a technologically advanced, ancient hominid species (Homo nocturnus) that once dominated Earth. They were capable of manipulating reality through 'thaumic' or psychic resonance, building civilizations that rival or exceed modern human achievement. However, a catastrophic extinction-level event, initiated by humanity (or by the Foundation's precursors), reduced them to their current feral, reclusive state. The entity's containment involves not just managing a population of giant humanoids, but suppressing the knowledge of their true history. The iconic special containment procedure is chillingly simple: 'All instances of SCP-1000 are to be terminated on sight.' The article includes a crucial twist: SCP-1000 were not aggressive toward humans until humans began systematically destroying them. The entry is a masterwork of SCP storytelling -- it turns a familiar cryptid into a vehicle for exploring genocide, historical erasure, ecological grief, and the moral ambiguity of the Foundation itself. The phrase 'Daylight Hunters' (what SCP-1000 call humans) haunts the article. SCP-1000 is a fan-favorite and critically acclaimed Series II entry, widely praised for its emotional weight and its expansion of SCP Foundation lore into the deep past of alternate hominid evolution.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Attested (primary SCP article): SCP-1000 is described as a nocturnal, omnivorous ape classified within the Hominini branch alongside Pan and Homo. Adults range from 1.5 to 3 meters (5–10 feet) in height and weigh between 90 and 270 kilograms (200–600 pounds). They possess grey, brown, black, red, and occasionally white fur, large eyes adapted for good vision in low light, a pronounced brow ridge, and a sagittal crest on the forehead similar to that of gorillas but present in both sexes. Their overall morphology evokes a large, robust, bipedal ape, closely matching popular depictions of Bigfoot/Sasquatch. Reconstructed/interpretive: Fan art and adaptations often emphasize a hulking, muscular frame, elongated arms, and a somewhat humanoid facial structure, sometimes adding tribal adornments or scars to reflect their lost civilization.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: The SCP-1000 article itself does not describe specific symbols or material culture in detail, but it implies that SCP-1000 once possessed advanced technology and civilization, including architecture and tools, before their near-extinction. The article’s header image ("Still from unverified amateur footage") visually references typical Bigfoot imagery—grainy, distant footage of a large, hairy figure in a forest. Adaptation-specific: In fan works and extended SCP universes, SCP-1000 is sometimes associated with dark forests, nocturnal landscapes, and motifs of chains, ruins, and broken cities, symbolizing their fall from dominance and ongoing oppression. Some alternate reality narratives (e.g., Kaktusverse) depict murals or iconography showing "Children of the Night" as antediluvian beings under fae canopies, but these are specific to those storylines and not universally canonical.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-1000 is described as having intelligence on par with Pan troglodytes (common chimpanzee) in the present day, suggesting complex social behavior, tool use, and problem-solving but not human-level abstract reasoning. They are nocturnal and omnivorous, implying foraging and hunting behavior adapted to forest environments. The article notes that most surviving SCP-1000 individuals are feral and scattered, with their former civilization destroyed. Historical narrative within the article, however, states that prior to the extinction event, SCP-1000 possessed a highly advanced civilization surpassing that of contemporary humans, including anomalous technology and widespread infrastructure. Personality traits are largely inferred: they are portrayed as once-capable of cooperation, governance, and technological innovation, but now traumatized, wary, and hostile or fearful toward humans. Adaptation-specific: Some fan interpretations emphasize their bitterness, desire for revenge, or tragic resignation, but these are not explicitly stated in the core article.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-1000 currently inhabits remote forested regions, particularly in North America, aligning with Bigfoot folklore. The SCP article implies a global distribution in the past, with SCP-1000 existing "in large numbers" contemporaneously with proto-humans and humans until 10,000–15,000 years ago. Their nocturnal habits and forest dwelling make them difficult to observe, which synergizes with the lethal pseudo-disease SCP-1000-f1 that punishes observation. Fan and derivative sources extend their range to other cryptid-associated regions (e.g., Himalayas, South America), but the primary article focuses on North American forests and general wilderness. Cosmologically, SCP-1000 is situated firmly on Earth, with no extradimensional origin; their anomalous nature lies in their pseudo-disease and historical reality-bending technology rather than in being from another realm.

Version Differences

Attested vs adaptation: The core SCP-1000 article presents them as a scientifically classifiable hominid species with a specific anomalous effect (SCP-1000-f1) and a hidden history of dominance and genocide. Alternate reality narratives (e.g., Kaktusverse, From 120’s Archives) expand their origin, sometimes tying them to fae deities (Titania) or antediluvian mythic wars, framing them as "Children of the Night" created by supernatural beings. These versions introduce additional cosmological layers—fae, profane dark entities, wars with Daeva—that are not part of the original article’s minimal, quasi-scientific framing. Some adaptations emphasize SCP-1000 as primarily tragic victims; others portray them as ongoing existential threats. These differences must be kept distinct: the original SCP-1000 article is a relatively self-contained narrative about a suppressed cryptid species and human atrocity, while extended universes integrate them into a broader mythic meta-canon.

Archive Dossier — Powers

SCP-1000-f1 pseudo-disease (lethal observation effect)

Source Version Primary SCP-1000 article (SCP Foundation)

Description

Attested: SCP-1000-f1 is an anomalous pseudo-disease carried genetically by all present-day instances of SCP-1000. Any hominid (humans, chimpanzees, bonobos, and non-immune SCP-1000) that directly or indirectly observes an instance of SCP-1000 has a minimum 2% chance of being instantly killed via permanent cessation of brain function. This chance increases cumulatively with viewing time, at approximately +1% per 20 minutes of observation, and varies between individuals, with some SCP-1000 carrying a "death chance" as high as 90%. The effect applies even to dead individuals, though small fur samples do not exhibit it. This power effectively weaponizes mere perception, making SCP-1000 inherently dangerous to observe and providing a built-in mechanism for secrecy.

Limitations

Attested: The effect does not manifest in small fur samples, allowing limited study of their biology. Known means of preventing the effect are described as small-scale and redacted in the article, implying that the Foundation has developed partial countermeasures but cannot deploy them broadly. The majority of SCP-1000 individuals are born immune to the effect themselves; non-immune SCP-1000 die quickly. The article warns that, due to SCP-1000’s close relation to humanity, SCP-1000-f1 could potentially transfer to human carriers, but this remains hypothetical.

Contradictions

There is no explicit contradiction within the core article regarding the existence or mechanism of SCP-1000-f1, but fan discussions sometimes question its plausibility or propose alternative interpretations (e.g., that the effect is a reality-bending curse rather than a disease). Some extended universes reinterpret SCP-1000-f1 as a fae-derived curse or a side effect of Titania’s heart, but these are adaptation-specific and not part of the original scientific framing.

Advanced pre-extinction technology and civilization

Source Version Primary SCP-1000 article (historical narrative section)

Description

Attested (narrative within article): Prior to the extinction event, SCP-1000 possessed a civilization more advanced than that of contemporary humans, including anomalous technology capable of large-scale environmental manipulation and reality alteration. They are described as having cities, infrastructure, and tools that allowed them to dominate Earth. The article implies that humans acquired or repurposed some of this technology to overthrow SCP-1000, including the weapon that triggered the pseudo-disease and genocide.

Limitations

Attested: Almost all of SCP-1000’s advanced technology and infrastructure were destroyed or lost during the extinction event. Surviving SCP-1000 are feral and scattered, lacking access to their former capabilities. The Foundation actively suppresses and confiscates any remaining artifacts, further limiting SCP-1000’s ability to regain technological power.

Contradictions

Extended universes (e.g., Kaktusverse) attribute some of SCP-1000’s power to fae origins or external entities, while the original article presents their advancement as emergent from their own civilization. There is no clear canonical resolution; these are parallel interpretations.

Physical strength, endurance, and stealth

Source Version Primary SCP-1000 article; cryptid folklore context (Bigfoot)

Description

Attested/inferred: As large, robust hominids adapted to nocturnal forest life, SCP-1000 possess significant physical strength and endurance, enabling them to move through dense wilderness, climb, and potentially overpower humans. Their nocturnal habits and large eyes suggest enhanced low-light vision and stealth. The article does not quantify these abilities, but they are consistent with their morphology and with Bigfoot folklore, which often portrays such creatures as elusive and physically formidable.

Limitations

Unknown/implicit: The article does not specify particular weaknesses (e.g., to firearms, fire, or specific materials). Their feral state and reduced numbers limit their capacity for organized resistance or large-scale action.

Contradictions

Fan works sometimes exaggerate their strength to near-superhuman levels or grant them additional powers (e.g., invisibility, teleportation), but these are not supported by the core article.

Potential reality-bending or anomalous weaponry (historical)

Source Version Primary SCP-1000 article; derivative fan analyses

Description

Attested (indirect): The extinction event that nearly wiped out SCP-1000 is described as being triggered by an anomalous weapon or technology that induced SCP-1000-f1 and caused mass death. While the article focuses on the weapon as used by humans, it implies that SCP-1000’s civilization had access to similar or related anomalous technologies. In some interpretations, SCP-1000 themselves may have developed reality-bending devices or bio-anomalies, though this is not detailed.

Limitations

Attested: Whatever anomalous weaponry existed is now largely destroyed or controlled by the Foundation. SCP-1000 no longer wield such devices in the present narrative.

Contradictions

Extended universes sometimes attribute the weapon to external entities (e.g., fae, gods, or other SCPs), shifting agency away from humans or SCP-1000. These variations reflect different thematic emphases (human guilt vs cosmic war) rather than explicit contradictions in the original text.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

Humanity (Homo sapiens)

Type hostileSource Primary SCP-1000 article (historical narrative and containment procedures)

Description

Attested: The SCP-1000 article describes a historical relationship in which humans genocidally overthrew SCP-1000’s civilization using anomalous technology, triggering SCP-1000-f1 and reducing their population to a tiny remnant. In the present, humans (via the SCP Foundation) continue to suppress SCP-1000’s existence, confiscate evidence, and prevent their recovery. This relationship is characterized by fear, guilt, and ongoing oppression.

Version Note

Core SCP canon; scientific-horror framing.

Conflicting Accounts

Extended universes sometimes portray humans as more passive victims of cosmic forces, but the original article clearly assigns agency and culpability to humanity.

SCP Foundation

Type political/ownershipSource Primary SCP-1000 article (Special Containment Procedures)

Description

Attested: The SCP Foundation claims jurisdiction over SCP-1000, enforcing strict containment procedures, surveillance of media, and discrediting of investigators. The Foundation’s Mobile Task Force Zeta-1000 monitors alleged sightings, and Director Jones must approve any contact. The Foundation effectively controls SCP-1000’s fate, deciding whether to maintain suppression or consider restitution.

Version Note

In-universe organizational relationship.

Conflicting Accounts

Fan interpretations differ on whether the Foundation is morally justified or deeply corrupt in this case. Some stories depict internal dissent and ethical debates among Foundation staff.

Director Jones

Type political/administrativeSource Primary SCP-1000 article (Special Containment Procedures)

Description

Attested: Director Jones is named as the authority whose prior approval is required for any contact with SCP-1000 and to whom all interactions must be reported. This positions Jones as a key decision-maker in SCP-1000 containment and policy.

Version Note

Limited characterization; primarily a bureaucratic role.

Conflicting Accounts

No major contradictions; some fan works may expand Jones’ personality or stance, but these are speculative.

Bigfoot folklore (Sasquatch, Yeren, etc.)

Type cultural/interpretiveSource Primary SCP-1000 article; SCP Foundation Wikia summaries

Description

Attested/inferred: The SCP-1000 article explicitly connects SCP-1000 to Bigfoot-type cryptids, framing real-world folklore as distorted or suppressed evidence of SCP-1000’s existence. In-universe, sightings and stories about Bigfoot are treated as leaks of SCP-1000’s presence, which the Foundation works to discredit.

Version Note

Meta-relationship between fictional SCP canon and real-world folklore.

Conflicting Accounts

Real-world cryptozoology does not accept SCP-1000 as literal; the relationship is purely fictional and interpretive.

Titania (fae goddess)

Type creator (extended lore)Source SCP Foundation Wikia (Fandom) extended lore; references to SCP-4840 and related works

Description

Adaptation-specific: In Kaktusverse lore, Titania creates the Children of the Night (SCP-1000) as a counterpart to the Children of the Sun. She later becomes their goddess after granting them her heart. This positions Titania as both creator and patron, with a complex relationship involving betrayal and shifting loyalties.

Version Note

Not part of the original SCP-1000 article; belongs to a specific extended universe.

Conflicting Accounts

The original SCP-1000 article does not mention Titania or fae origins, instead presenting SCP-1000 as an evolved hominid species. These two origin stories are parallel, not reconciled.
