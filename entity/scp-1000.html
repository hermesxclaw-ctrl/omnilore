Who It Is

Imagine that every blurry photograph of Bigfoot, every campfire story about a shadow in the trees, is not just a joke or a hoax—but a fragment of a buried war crime. In the SCP Foundation’s universe, SCP-1000 is not a single monster but an entire species, a nocturnal cousin of humanity whose cities once covered the Earth. We did not merely outcompete them; we annihilated them with an impossible weapon that turned the act of seeing into a death sentence, then rewrote history so that their survivors would be dismissed as myths. To read SCP-1000 closely is to realize that the real horror is not the creature in the forest, but the civilization that decided it was better if no one ever knew what we did.

Attested: SCP-1000 is described as having intelligence on par with Pan troglodytes (common chimpanzee) in the present day, suggesting complex social behavior, tool use, and problem-solving but not human-level abstract reasoning. They are nocturnal and omnivorous, implying foraging and hunting behavior adapted to forest environments. The article notes that most surviving SCP-1000 individuals are feral and scattered, with their former civilization destroyed. Powers
SCP-1000-f1 pseudo-disease (lethal observation effect)

Attested: SCP-1000-f1 is an anomalous pseudo-disease carried genetically by all present-day instances of SCP-1000. Any hominid (humans, chimpanzees, bonobos, and non-immune SCP-1000) that directly or indirectly observes an instance of SCP-1000 has a minimum 2% chance of being instantly killed via permanent cessation of brain function. This chance increases cumulatively with viewing time, at approximately +1% per 20 minutes of observation, and varies between individuals, with some SCP-1000 carrying a "death chance" as high as 90%. The effect applies even to dead individuals, though small fur samples do not exhibit it. This power effectively weaponizes mere perception, making SCP-1000 inherently dangerous to observe and providing a built-in mechanism for secrecy.

Limit: Attested: The effect does not manifest in small fur samples, allowing limited study of their biology. Known means of preventing the effect are described as small-scale and redacted in the article, implying that the Foundation has developed partial countermeasures but cannot deploy them broadly. The majority of SCP-1000 individuals are born immune to the effect themselves; non-immune SCP-1000 die quickly. The article warns that, due to SCP-1000’s close relation to humanity, SCP-1000-f1 could potentially transfer to human carriers, but this remains hypothetical.

Advanced pre-extinction technology and civilization

Attested (narrative within article): Prior to the extinction event, SCP-1000 possessed a civilization more advanced than that of contemporary humans, including anomalous technology capable of large-scale environmental manipulation and reality alteration. They are described as having cities, infrastructure, and tools that allowed them to dominate Earth. The article implies that humans acquired or repurposed some of this technology to overthrow SCP-1000, including the weapon that triggered the pseudo-disease and genocide.

Limit: Attested: Almost all of SCP-1000’s advanced technology and infrastructure were destroyed or lost during the extinction event. Surviving SCP-1000 are feral and scattered, lacking access to their former capabilities. The Foundation actively suppresses and confiscates any remaining artifacts, further limiting SCP-1000’s ability to regain technological power.

Physical strength, endurance, and stealth

Attested/inferred: As large, robust hominids adapted to nocturnal forest life, SCP-1000 possess significant physical strength and endurance, enabling them to move through dense wilderness, climb, and potentially overpower humans. Their nocturnal habits and large eyes suggest enhanced low-light vision and stealth. The article does not quantify these abilities, but they are consistent with their morphology and with Bigfoot folklore, which often portrays such creatures as elusive and physically formidable.

Limit: Unknown/implicit: The article does not specify particular weaknesses (e.g., to firearms, fire, or specific materials). Their feral state and reduced numbers limit their capacity for organized resistance or large-scale action.

Potential reality-bending or anomalous weaponry (historical)

Attested (indirect): The extinction event that nearly wiped out SCP-1000 is described as being triggered by an anomalous weapon or technology that induced SCP-1000-f1 and caused mass death. While the article focuses on the weapon as used by humans, it implies that SCP-1000’s civilization had access to similar or related anomalous technologies. In some interpretations, SCP-1000 themselves may have developed reality-bending devices or bio-anomalies, though this is not detailed.

Limit: Attested: Whatever anomalous weaponry existed is now largely destroyed or controlled by the Foundation. SCP-1000 no longer wield such devices in the present narrative.

Accounts I
SCP-1000 (core containment narrative)SCP-1000 - SCP Foundation article

Early 2010s (Series II era of SCP Wiki)

The SCP-1000 article presents a dual-layer narrative. On the surface, it describes SCP-1000 as a dangerous cryptid species—Bigfoot—whose existence is suppressed by the Foundation due to a lethal observation-based pseudo-disease, SCP-1000-f1. The containment procedures emphasize surveillance of media reports, discrediting investigators, and strict prohibition of contact. In the deeper layer, revealed through internal notes and historical exposition, the article recounts a hidden history: SCP-1000 once dominated Earth with a civilization more advanced than that of humans. Approximately 10,000–15,000 years ago, humans acquired anomalous technology and genocidally overthrew SCP-1000, triggering SCP-1000-f1 and reducing their population to 1–5%. The Foundation’s current policy is to maintain the cover story that SCP-1000 is a myth, while secretly debating whether humanity’s actions were justified and whether SCP-1000 should be allowed to recover. Within the SCP universe, the story establishes humanity as perpetrators of a massive, anomalously assisted genocide against a sentient species.

Accounts II
Children of the Night (Kaktusverse and related extended lore)SCP Foundation Wikia (Fandom) entry on SCP-1000; cross-references to SCP-4840 and other Kaktusverse works

Mid–late 2010s (development of Kaktusverse meta-canon)

In the Kaktusverse and related extended lore, SCP-1000 is reinterpreted as the "Children of the Night," created by the fae goddess Titania as a counterpart to the "Children of the Sun." They are cast into darkness under fae tree canopies, where isolation and lack of dreaming turn them cruel. They wage war against the fae, enslave and torture their creators, and later invade Europ, dragging millions of humans in chains to their lands. A sorcerer named Noah eventually discovers that the Children feed on suffering and orchestrates the "Day of Flowers," a cataclysmic event that ends their reign. This mythic narrative integrates SCP-1000 into a broader cosmology involving fae, Daeva, and cosmic entities like YASH, the Profane Dark. This version reframes SCP-1000 as both oppressors and victims, highlighting cycles of creation, rebellion, and punishment. It expands their role from a hidden cryptid species to a central actor in cosmic wars, influencing the fate of fae and human civilizations. It introduces SCP-1000 as Bigfoot, explains the lethal observation effect, and dramatizes the hidden history of their near-extinction and human culpability.
