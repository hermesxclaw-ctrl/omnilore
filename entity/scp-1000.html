SCP-1000 were not always as they are now. They were once the dominant species on this planet. We took that from them. Remember that when you pull the trigger.

Who It Is

SCP-1000 is one of the most significant entries in SCP Series II -- a radical reimagining of the Bigfoot legend that transforms the cryptid into a tragic, world-building centerpiece of the SCP Foundation universe. The skip reveals that Bigfoot ('SCP-1000') are not primitive ape-men but the remnants of a technologically advanced, ancient hominid species (Homo nocturnus) that once dominated Earth. They were capable of manipulating reality through 'thaumic' or psychic resonance, building civilizations that rival or exceed modern human achievement. However, a catastrophic extinction-level event, initiated by humanity (or by the Foundation's precursors), reduced them to their current feral, reclusive state. The entity's containment involves not just managing a population of giant humanoids, but suppressing the knowledge of their true history. The iconic special containment procedure is chillingly simple: 'All instances of SCP-1000 are to be terminated on sight.' The article includes a crucial twist: SCP-1000 were not aggressive toward humans until humans began systematically destroying them. The entry is a masterwork of SCP storytelling -- it turns a familiar cryptid into a vehicle for exploring genocide, historical erasure, ecological grief, and the moral ambiguity of the Foundation itself. The phrase 'Daylight Hunters' (what SCP-1000 call humans) haunts the article. SCP-1000 is a fan-favorite and critically acclaimed Series II entry, widely praised for its emotional weight and its expansion of SCP Foundation lore into the deep past of alternate hominid evolution.

Form

Attested (primary SCP article): SCP-1000 is described as a nocturnal, omnivorous ape classified within the Hominini branch alongside Pan and Homo. Adults range from 1.5 to 3 meters (5–10 feet) in height and weigh between 90 and 270 kilograms (200–600 pounds). They possess grey, brown, black, red, and occasionally white fur, large eyes adapted for good vision in low light, a pronounced brow ridge, and a sagittal crest on the forehead similar to that of gorillas but present in both sexes. Their overall morphology evokes a large, robust, bipedal ape, closely matching popular depictions of Bigfoot/Sasquatch. Reconstructed/interpretive: Fan art and adaptations often emphasize a hulking, muscular frame, elongated arms, and a somewhat humanoid facial structure, sometimes adding tribal adornments or scars to reflect their lost civilization.

Powers

SCP-1000-f1 pseudo-disease (lethal observation effect) Attested: SCP-1000-f1 is an anomalous pseudo-disease carried genetically by all present-day instances of SCP-1000. Any hominid (humans, chimpanzees, bonobos, and non-immune SCP-1000) that directly or indirectly observes an instance of SCP-1000 has a minimum 2% chance of being instantly killed via permanent cessation of brain function. This chance increases cumulatively with viewing time, at approximately +1% per 20 minutes of observation, and varies between individuals, with some SCP-1000 carrying a "death chance" as high as 90%. The effect applies even to dead individuals, though small fur samples do not exhibit it. This power effectively weaponizes mere perception, making SCP-1000 inherently dangerous to observe and providing a built-in mechanism for secrecy. Primary SCP-1000 article (SCP Foundation) Attested: The effect does not manifest in small fur samples, allowing limited study of their biology. Known means of preventing the effect are described as small-scale and redacted in the article, implying that the Foundation has developed partial countermeasures but cannot deploy them broadly. The majority of SCP-1000 individuals are born immune to the effect themselves; non-immune SCP-1000 die quickly. The article warns that, due to SCP-1000’s close relation to humanity, SCP-1000-f1 could potentially transfer to human carriers, but this remains hypothetical. There is no explicit contradiction within the core article regarding the existence or mechanism of SCP-1000-f1, but fan discussions sometimes question its plausibility or propose alternative interpretations (e.g., that the effect is a reality-bending curse rather than a disease). Some extended universes reinterpret SCP-1000-f1 as a fae-derived curse or a side effect of Titania’s heart, but these are adaptation-specific and not part of the original scientific framing.

Advanced pre-extinction technology and civilization Attested (narrative within article): Prior to the extinction event, SCP-1000 possessed a civilization more advanced than that of contemporary humans, including anomalous technology capable of large-scale environmental manipulation and reality alteration. They are described as having cities, infrastructure, and tools that allowed them to dominate Earth. The article implies that humans acquired or repurposed some of this technology to overthrow SCP-1000, including the weapon that triggered the pseudo-disease and genocide. Primary SCP-1000 article (historical narrative section) Attested: Almost all of SCP-1000’s advanced technology and infrastructure were destroyed or lost during the extinction event. Surviving SCP-1000 are feral and scattered, lacking access to their former capabilities. The Foundation actively suppresses and confiscates any remaining artifacts, further limiting SCP-1000’s ability to regain technological power. Extended universes (e.g., Kaktusverse) attribute some of SCP-1000’s power to fae origins or external entities, while the original article presents their advancement as emergent from their own civilization. There is no clear canonical resolution; these are parallel interpretations.

Physical strength, endurance, and stealth Attested/inferred: As large, robust hominids adapted to nocturnal forest life, SCP-1000 possess significant physical strength and endurance, enabling them to move through dense wilderness, climb, and potentially overpower humans. Their nocturnal habits and large eyes suggest enhanced low-light vision and stealth. The article does not quantify these abilities, but they are consistent with their morphology and with Bigfoot folklore, which often portrays such creatures as elusive and physically formidable. Primary SCP-1000 article; cryptid folklore context (Bigfoot) Unknown/implicit: The article does not specify particular weaknesses (e.g., to firearms, fire, or specific materials). Their feral state and reduced numbers limit their capacity for organized resistance or large-scale action. Fan works sometimes exaggerate their strength to near-superhuman levels or grant them additional powers (e.g., invisibility, teleportation), but these are not supported by the core article.

Potential reality-bending or anomalous weaponry (historical) Attested (indirect): The extinction event that nearly wiped out SCP-1000 is described as being triggered by an anomalous weapon or technology that induced SCP-1000-f1 and caused mass death. While the article focuses on the weapon as used by humans, it implies that SCP-1000’s civilization had access to similar or related anomalous technologies. In some interpretations, SCP-1000 themselves may have developed reality-bending devices or bio-anomalies, though this is not detailed. Primary SCP-1000 article; derivative fan analyses Attested: Whatever anomalous weaponry existed is now largely destroyed or controlled by the Foundation. SCP-1000 no longer wield such devices in the present narrative. Extended universes sometimes attribute the weapon to external entities (e.g., fae, gods, or other SCPs), shifting agency away from humans or SCP-1000. These variations reflect different thematic emphases (human guilt vs cosmic war) rather than explicit contradictions in the original text.

Incident Log

The SCP-1000 article presents a dual-layer narrative. On the surface, it describes SCP-1000 as a dangerous cryptid species—Bigfoot—whose existence is suppressed by the Foundation due to a lethal observation-based pseudo-disease, SCP-1000-f1. The containment procedures emphasize surveillance of media reports, discrediting investigators, and strict prohibition of contact. In the deeper layer, revealed through internal notes and historical exposition, the article recounts a hidden history: SCP-1000 once dominated Earth with a civilization more advanced than that of humans. Approximately 10,000–15,000 years ago, humans acquired anomalous technology and genocidally overthrew SCP-1000, triggering SCP-1000-f1 and reducing their population to 1–5%. The Foundation’s current policy is to maintain the cover story that SCP-1000 is a myth, while secretly debating whether humanity’s actions were justified and whether SCP-1000 should be allowed to recover.

In the Kaktusverse and related extended lore, SCP-1000 is reinterpreted as the "Children of the Night," created by the fae goddess Titania as a counterpart to the "Children of the Sun." They are cast into darkness under fae tree canopies, where isolation and lack of dreaming turn them cruel. They wage war against the fae, enslave and torture their creators, and later invade Europ, dragging millions of humans in chains to their lands. A sorcerer named Noah eventually discovers that the Children feed on suffering and orchestrates the "Day of Flowers," a cataclysmic event that ends their reign. This mythic narrative integrates SCP-1000 into a broader cosmology involving fae, Daeva, and cosmic entities like YASH, the Profane Dark.

A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine that every blurry photograph of Bigfoot, every campfire story about a shadow in the trees, is not just a joke or a hoax—but a fragment of a buried war crime. In the SCP Foundation’s universe, SCP-1000 is not a single monster but an entire species, a nocturnal cousin of humanity whose cities once covered the Earth. We did not merely outcompete them; we annihilated them with an impossible weapon that turned the act of seeing into a death sentence, then rewrote history so that their survivors would be dismissed as myths. To read SCP-1000 closely is to realize that the real horror is not the creature in the forest, but the civilization that decided it was better if no one ever knew what we did.
