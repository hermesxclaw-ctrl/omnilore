Who It Is

Der Klaubauf is a fearsome companion figure from Austrian Alpine folklore, particularly in Tyrol and Carinthia, who accompanies Saint Nicholas during the Krampuslauf (Krampus run) and also appears during the Perchtenlauf. The Klaubauf is distinct from the more widely known Krampus in several key ways: whereas Krampus is a shaggy, goat-like demon, the Klaubauf is depicted as a tall, muscular humanoid with a hand-shaped head or, in some traditions, a single enormous claw or hand — the name 'Klaubauf' literally means 'grasp up' or 'pinch-open.' His most terrifying feature is that his entire head is a giant hand or his mouth is replaced by what looks like a grasping claw, used to snatch up naughty children. He wears heavy chains that he drags through the streets, creating a deafening clatter, and carries a large bag or basket for stuffing children into. In Tyrolean tradition, the Klaubauf mask (Larve) is carved from wood with exaggerated features: bulging eyes, a gaping mouth, and often a single horn. The Klaubauf tradition is part of the broader Perchten culture — pre-Christian Alpine winter spirits that were assimilated into the Christmas season. Unlike Krampus, who specifically appears on December 5th (Krampusnacht), Klaubaufs appear throughout the Advent season and into Epiphany. The Klaubauf represents the punitive aspect of winter, enforcing moral behavior through ritualized terror. Today, Klaubauf runs (Klaubaufläufe) are held in Tyrolean villages, where young men in elaborately carved masks and heavy fur costumes parade through the streets.
