Who It Is

An indestructible, immovable black cube, 10cm per side. Once every 10,000 years, it opens, releases something, and closes. The last opening was approximately 9,980 years ago. A deep-space object larger than Jupiter is on trajectory to intersect with the cube's location at the calculated opening date. It is accelerating.

Form

A perfect matte black cube, 10cm³. Absorbs all light and radiation. No detectable temperature. Cannot be scanned. It is simply there, waiting.

The Approach

An abandoned limestone mine in Indiana should be nothing more than a scar in the earth—dust, stone, and the memory of labor. Yet the tunnels of SCP-2951 do not end where the maps say they should. A collapse opens a passage no miner ever cut, and those who go in speak of pillars that hold up the world and fire that burns for "10,000 years." The deeper the Foundation pushes into the Lemon Quarry, the clearer it becomes that this is not just a failed industrial venture but a hidden architecture of punishment and support, a place where reality itself seems to rest on structures that were never meant for human eyes.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses fictional but intense material: industrial accidents, worker deaths, lethal anomalies, psychological trauma, infernal imagery, and possession-like loss of control. While SCP-2951 is a work of collaborative internet horror, its themes may be distressing to some readers.
Identity

SCP-2951 — 10,000 Years

Pronunciation S-C-P two-nine-five-one, "ten thousand years".

Known As

SCP-295110,000 YearsLemon Quarry anomaly

Etymology

The numerical designation "2951" follows the SCP Foundation’s internal cataloging system for anomalous objects and locations. The subtitle "10,000 Years" is attested in the original SCP article and related media, referring to extreme or seemingly immeasurable spans of time associated with the anomaly’s interior and the language of punishment and endurance used in the narrative.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-2951 is a collaboratively authored fictional anomaly within the SCP Foundation shared universe. The primary textual authority is the SCP Foundation wiki entry credited to the author known as "Djkaktus". As an internet-horror artifact, it belongs to the broader participatory culture of online creepypasta, collaborative fiction, and the SCP community rather than to a single traditional culture or nation.

Disputed Identifications

Within the SCP narrative, SCP-2951 is consistently identified as an anomalous limestone mine near Guthrie, Indiana. Interpretive materials and fan discussions sometimes frame it as a metaphorical or literal gateway to "Hell" or a fundamental cosmic structure ("pillars that support the world"), but these are interpretive positions rather than alternate canonical identifications. No major intra-franchise dispute exists over its basic identity as a spatial-temporal anomaly in a quarry; what is debated is the metaphysical nature of the entities and structures within it.

Description

Appearance

Attested: SCP-2951 is described as an abandoned limestone mine associated with the Lemon Quarry near Guthrie, Indiana. The exterior includes a collapsed primary entrance and a dilapidated storage building housing a secondary access shaft. Inside, the mine’s tunnels are irregular, with evidence of structural deterioration typical of disused industrial sites. As exploration proceeds, the interior diverges from expected mine architecture, presenting uncharted tunnels, vast caverns, and non-standard geological formations. Reconstructed/interpretive: Visual adaptations (short film, YouTube readings) emphasize darkness, confined spaces, and sudden transitions into cavernous chambers dominated by immense stone pillars and fiery or glowing depths, reinforcing the sense of a descent into a hostile, otherworldly environment.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: The most prominent symbolic elements are the "pillars that support the world" and references to fire, endurance, and punishment. The pillars function both as literal structures within the anomaly and as metaphors for cosmic supports whose failure would destabilize reality. The repeated phrase "10,000 years in the fire" (and similar formulations) evokes religious and mythological imagery of damnation and eternal torment. Adaptation-specific: In the short film and audio adaptations, visual and auditory emphasis is placed on the pillars, echoing voices, and the transformation of human speech into something alien, reinforcing themes of possession and prophetic utterance.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-2951 itself behaves as a location with irregular spatial and temporal properties. Exploratory teams report disorientation, non-Euclidean tunnel layouts, and time discrepancies. Unknown anomalous entities within the mine exhibit hostile or predatory behavior, leading to the deaths or disappearances of personnel. The entities’ "personality" is largely inferred from their effects: they appear indifferent to human life, focused on maintaining or enacting a long-duration punishment or cosmic function. Interpretive: The narrative voice and logs suggest a cold, bureaucratic horror—Foundation procedures colliding with an incomprehensible, ancient system of suffering. The anomaly’s "personality" can be read as that of an impersonal mechanism of torment rather than a single sentient being.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-2951’s domain is the subterranean environment of the Lemon Quarry, extending into anomalous spaces that may not correspond to normal geography. Its habitat includes limestone tunnels, collapsed shafts, and newly opened passages that were not cut by human mining operations. Within, the anomaly manifests vast caverns, pillars, and possibly a fiery or abyssal region below. Interpretive: Thematically, SCP-2951 occupies the domain of infernal or underworld spaces in horror fiction—an industrial site that becomes a gateway to something older and more metaphysical, blending modern mining infrastructure with mythic hellscapes.

Version Differences

Attested (SCP article): Focuses on containment procedures, the 1944 Lemon Quarry incident, and later Foundation explorations, with detailed logs of Mobile Task Force Trotter-5 entering the mine and encountering anomalies and entities. The tone is clinical but increasingly disturbed as the exploration log progresses. Adaptation-specific (YouTube readings, Exploring Series): These versions follow the SCP text closely but add narration, pacing, and sometimes minor interpretive emphasis, highlighting particular lines (e.g., "10,000 years in the fire") and the cosmic significance of the pillars. Adaptation-specific (2023 short film): The film centers on Commander Terrence as the sole survivor of a team exploring SCP-2951. It dramatizes interrogation scenes and the transformation of Terrence’s voice when he speaks about the pillars and what lies below, emphasizing possession and prophetic horror. Names, dialogue, and some details differ from the original article, but the core concept—an abandoned mine classified as SCP-2951, a disastrous exploration, and references to pillars and catastrophic consequences—remains consistent.

Powers & Abilities

Irregular spatial geometry

Source Version Primary SCP Foundation article; summarized in Exploring the SCP Foundation: SCP-2951 – 10,000 Years.

Description

Attested: SCP-2951’s interior exhibits non-standard spatial behavior. Tunnels appear and disappear, previously uncharted passages open following seismic events, and the mine’s layout does not match historical maps or mining records. Exploratory teams encounter new tunnels that were not cut by the mining group and are not charted, suggesting that the anomaly can generate or reveal additional space. This spatial irregularity contributes to disorientation and entrapment.

Limitations

The spatial anomalies appear confined to the mine and its immediate subterranean environment. There is no attested evidence that SCP-2951’s geometry affects the surrounding town or surface structures beyond the quarry. The Foundation’s containment procedures rely on restricting access rather than attempting to neutralize the spatial anomaly, implying practical limitations in understanding or controlling it.

Contradictions

Some interpretive materials frame the new tunnels as manifestations of a metaphysical underworld ("Hell"), while others treat them as purely anomalous spatial phenomena without explicit religious content. The SCP text itself remains ambiguous, allowing both readings.

Temporal anomalies and extreme duration

Source Version Primary SCP article; thematic emphasis in SCP Archives audio and Exploring Series video.

Description

Attested: The interior of SCP-2951 is subject to irregular temporal behavior. The title "10,000 Years" and repeated references to long spans of time in the narrative suggest that time within the anomaly may pass differently, or that the entities and structures inside are associated with vast durations of punishment or endurance. Exploration logs imply time distortions, though precise measurements are not fully detailed in publicly available summaries.

Limitations

The exact mechanics of time distortion are not fully specified. There is no clear, quantified description of time dilation or acceleration, and the Foundation’s containment procedures do not rely on exploiting temporal effects. As a result, much of the temporal anomaly remains interpretive rather than experimentally characterized.

Contradictions

Some readers interpret "10,000 years" as metaphorical, representing the subjective experience of torment rather than literal elapsed time. Others argue for a more literal reading, suggesting that the anomaly may connect to or embody an ancient, ongoing process of punishment spanning millennia. The text does not definitively resolve this tension.

Manifestation of anomalous entities

Source Version Primary SCP article; SCPOneCanonProject summary of events and participants.

Description

Attested: Exploratory teams report encounters with unknown anomalous entities within SCP-2951. These entities are likely connected to the 1944 incident and are implicated in the deaths or disappearances of miners and Foundation personnel. Their exact nature—physical, spectral, or conceptual—is not fully described in accessible summaries, but they are clearly hostile and capable of lethal force.

Limitations

The entities appear confined to SCP-2951’s interior. There is no attested evidence of them leaving the mine or affecting distant locations. Their abilities are known primarily through outcomes (injury, death, psychological trauma) rather than detailed observation, limiting precise characterization.

Contradictions

Some adaptations emphasize the entities as demonic or infernal beings, while others leave them more abstract, focusing on the environment itself as the primary antagonist. The SCP text maintains ambiguity, allowing multiple interpretations.

Psychological and vocal alteration (possession-like effect)

Source Version SCP 2951 – 10,000 Years (2023 short film, IMDb listing and quoted dialogue).

Description

Adaptation-specific: In the 2023 short film and quoted dialogue, Commander Terrence’s voice changes when he speaks about the pillars and what lies below, leading Agent Mann to exclaim that "That’s not his voice." Terrence delivers prophetic statements about the pillars cracking, the Foundation becoming loose, and the might below falling upon the meek. This suggests an ability of SCP-2951 or its entities to alter or inhabit human consciousness and speech, using survivors as conduits for its message.

Limitations

This possession-like effect is explicitly documented in the film adaptation, not in the original SCP article. It may represent a dramatization rather than a canonical power. Within the film, the effect appears triggered by Terrence’s exposure to SCP-2951 and manifests during interrogation, but its duration and full impact are not exhaustively explored.

Contradictions

Because this ability is adaptation-specific, it may conflict with more restrained portrayals in the SCP text, where survivors’ psychological states are described but not necessarily as overt possession. Scholars of SCP lore often treat such filmic embellishments as non-canonical or semi-canonical, depending on their fidelity to the original article.

Structural influence on reality ("pillars that support the world")

Source Version SCP 2951 – 10,000 Years (short film); thematic readings in Exploring the SCP Foundation video.

Description

Interpretive: The phrase "pillars that support the world" appears in adaptation dialogue and is thematically consistent with the SCP article’s emphasis on the mine’s interior as a place of fundamental, possibly cosmological significance. If taken literally, SCP-2951 houses structures whose failure would destabilize the world or reality itself. If taken metaphorically, the pillars represent the hidden systems of suffering and control underpinning the SCP universe.

Limitations

The original SCP article does not provide explicit, empirical evidence that the pillars physically support the world. The notion is primarily conveyed through dialogue and narrative emphasis. As such, this "power" is best understood as a narrative device rather than a rigorously defined ability.

Contradictions

Literalist readings (pillars as actual cosmic supports) conflict with more grounded interpretations (pillars as symbolic of systemic oppression or punishment). The SCP text’s ambiguity prevents a definitive resolution, and community discussions reflect this divide.

Stories & Myths

SCP-2951 – 10,000 Years (core SCP article)

Source SCP Foundation wiki entry for SCP-2951, authored by Djkaktus.Culture Internet-horror, SCP Foundation collaborative fiction community.Period Published October 5, 2016 (per SCPOneCanonProject metadata), set across events from 1937 to post–April 19, 2003.

Summary

The article presents SCP-2951 as an abandoned limestone mine near Guthrie, Indiana, exhibiting spatial and temporal anomalies and housing unknown entities. It recounts the 1944 Lemon Quarry collapse: seismic activity prompts an exploratory team to investigate, a critical access tunnel collapses, and subsequent efforts to clear debris lead to the discovery of a new, uncharted tunnel. The incident results in deaths and the mine’s abandonment. Decades later, the SCP Foundation classifies the site as SCP-2951, imposes containment procedures, and dispatches Mobile Task Force Trotter-5 ("Hell’s Heroes") to explore the anomaly. The exploration log details the team’s descent, encounters with irregular geometry and hostile entities, and the progressive breakdown of the mission, culminating in casualties and a reinforced prohibition on further exploration.

Consequences

The 1944 incident leads to the mine’s closure and sets the stage for later anomalous classification. The Foundation’s exploration results in significant loss of life and a deeper, though still incomplete, understanding of SCP-2951’s dangers. Containment procedures are tightened, exploratory missions are forbidden, and the site becomes a long-term, unresolved threat within the SCP universe. The narrative reinforces themes of institutional hubris and the limits of human control over cosmic or infernal structures.

Variants

The core article is the primary canonical version. Variants include narrated readings (SCP Archives), analytical summaries (Exploring the SCP Foundation), and fan discussions that emphasize different aspects of the story (e.g., theological implications, cosmic horror). These variants generally follow the same plot but differ in tone, emphasis, and minor interpretive details.

1944 Lemon Quarry collapse

Source Addendum 2951.1 in the SCP article; SCPOneCanonProject timeline summary.Culture Industrial United States, mid-20th century; reframed through SCP’s occult-horror lens.Period August 23, 1944.

Summary

Seismic activity beneath the Lemon Quarry prompts supervisors to send an exploratory team into the limestone mine to assess structural damage. The team reports a collapsed access tunnel. A larger crew is assembled to clear debris, but additional seismic activity triggers another collapse, trapping workers. Teams on both sides of the blockage work for hours to free the trapped crew. When the blockage is finally cleared, the crew reports that while the original access tunnel remains blocked, a new tunnel—one not cut by the mining group and not present on any charts—has opened. This event marks the first documented manifestation of SCP-2951’s anomalous interior and leads, eventually, to the mine’s abandonment and later SCP classification.

Participants

Quarry supervisors

B.G. Hoadley Mining Group workers

Jeremiah Howard Barnes (as later documenter)

Consequences

The collapse causes deaths and injuries, undermines confidence in the mine’s safety, and contributes to its closure. The discovery of the uncharted tunnel introduces the anomaly that will later be designated SCP-2951. The incident becomes a key historical anchor in the SCP narrative, linking mundane industrial disaster to occult phenomena.

Variants

The SCP article presents the incident through recovered documentation from J. Howard Barnes’s office. Fan summaries and analytical videos restate the event with varying levels of detail but do not significantly alter its core facts.

Trotter-5 exploration of SCP-2951

Source Exploration log within the SCP article; SCPOneCanonProject listing of characters and events.Culture SCP Foundation paramilitary culture; internet-horror audience.Period Late 20th to early 21st century (exact date varies in timeline summaries, with key events around June 1998 and April 19, 2003).

Summary

Mobile Task Force Trotter-5 ("Hell’s Heroes") is deployed to enter SCP-2951 via the secondary shaft and document its interior. Led by Dr. Armin Rosstetler and accompanied by agents such as Young, Davies, Jenna, Kevin, and Daniel, the team descends into the mine. As they progress, they encounter irregular geometry, uncharted tunnels, and signs of previous activity. The environment grows increasingly hostile, with anomalous entities attacking or otherwise causing casualties. Communication becomes strained, and the log records mounting fear, confusion, and fatal encounters. Ultimately, the mission fails to fully map or understand SCP-2951, and several team members die or are lost. The Foundation responds by forbidding further exploration and reinforcing containment.

Consequences

The exploration confirms SCP-2951’s status as a dangerous, poorly understood anomaly. The deaths and psychological trauma suffered by Trotter-5 members underscore the risks of direct engagement. The Foundation’s policy shifts toward strict containment and avoidance, leaving SCP-2951 as a lingering, unresolved threat. The log also deepens the mythos around the mine, hinting at deeper structures and entities that remain unexplored.

Variants

Narrated versions (SCP Archives, Exploring Series) dramatize the log, adding voice acting and pacing. These variants may highlight particular lines or emotional beats but generally adhere to the article’s structure.

SCP 2951 – 10,000 Years (2023 short film)

Source Independent short film listed on IMDb; inspired by the SCP article.Culture Contemporary independent horror cinema; SCP fandom.Period Released January 29, 2023 (United States).

Summary

The film follows Commander Terrence, the only survivor of a team that explored an abandoned mine now classified as SCP-2951. Through interrogation scenes, Terrence recounts what happened in the mine, while his interrogators—Dr. Rosstetler and Agent Mann—attempt to understand the anomaly. As Terrence speaks about the pillars that support the world and what lies below, his voice changes, suggesting possession or influence by SCP-2951’s entities. Agent Mann becomes visibly frightened, insisting that "That’s not his voice." Terrence delivers ominous prophecies about the pillars cracking, the Foundation becoming loose, and the might below falling upon the meek. The film condenses the broader SCP narrative into a character-focused horror story, emphasizing psychological and metaphysical dread.

Consequences

Within the film’s narrative, Terrence’s survival becomes a vector for SCP-2951’s message, suggesting that the anomaly’s influence extends beyond the mine through those who have encountered it. The film contributes to the broader reception of SCP-2951, introducing it to audiences who may not read the original article and reinforcing its themes of cosmic instability and institutional vulnerability.

Variants

As an adaptation, the film differs from the article in character focus, dialogue, and specific events. It is one of several media interpretations, alongside audio readings and analytical videos, that expand SCP-2951’s presence beyond text.

Within the fictional universe, the SCP Foundation is the organization that discovers, classifies, and contains SCP-2951. It imposes special containment procedures, restricts access to the Lemon Quarry, and deploys Mobile Task Force Trotter-5 to explore the anomaly. In meta terms, the Foundation wiki and its community are the creative context in which SCP-2951 was authored and disseminated.

The entities inhabiting SCP-2951 are hostile to human intruders, causing deaths and psychological harm. Their relationship to humans is predatory or punitive. Dark History
Content advisory. SCP-2951’s narrative includes industrial disaster, multiple deaths, implied prolonged torment, possible infernal or hell-like punishment, psychological trauma, and possession-like effects. Readers and viewers may encounter descriptions of trapped workers, lethal anomalies, and metaphors of enduring "10,000 years in the fire."

Industrial disaster and worker deaths

Source Addendum 2951.1 in SCP article; SCPOneCanonProject timeline.

Description

The 1944 Lemon Quarry collapse traps workers underground and results in fatalities. The event is described in clinical terms but implies significant suffering and fear among the trapped crew. The subsequent discovery of an uncharted tunnel links their deaths to the emergence of SCP-2951, blending real-world industrial risk with occult horror.

Scholarly Context

This element reflects longstanding horror traditions that connect industrial sites and mining disasters to underworld imagery and hauntings. It also resonates with historical concerns about worker safety and exploitation in mining industries.

Deaths and psychological trauma of Mobile Task Force Trotter-5

Source Exploration log in SCP article; SCPOneCanonProject character and event listings.

Description

The exploration of SCP-2951 by Trotter-5 leads to multiple deaths and severe psychological stress. Team members encounter hostile entities, disorienting environments, and the apparent breakdown of normal reality. The log records fear, confusion, and fatal encounters, emphasizing the human cost of the Foundation’s attempts to control anomalies.

Scholarly Context

This material aligns with the SCP franchise’s broader critique of militarized responses to the unknown, highlighting how institutional attempts to dominate anomalies often result in trauma and loss.

Infernal imagery and prolonged torment

Source SCP article thematic content; SCP Archives audio; Exploring the SCP Foundation video; dialogue from the 2023 film.

Description

The title "10,000 Years" and repeated references to fire and endurance evoke images of hellish punishment and prolonged suffering. While the article does not explicitly name the interior as "Hell," adaptations and fan interpretations often frame SCP-2951 as a site of eternal or near-eternal torment, where entities or structures enforce long-duration suffering.

Scholarly Context

This draws on religious and mythological traditions of hell as a place of fire and endless punishment, recontextualized in a modern industrial setting. Scholars of internet horror note how SCP entries frequently remix theological motifs into bureaucratic and scientific frameworks.

Possession and loss of bodily autonomy

Source SCP 2951 – 10,000 Years (short film, quoted dialogue).

Description

In the film adaptation, Commander Terrence’s voice changes as he speaks about SCP-2951, suggesting that the anomaly or its entities have taken partial control of his speech. Agent Mann’s reaction—"That’s not his voice"—underscores the horror of losing control over one’s body and identity. This possession-like effect is a form of psychological and spiritual violence.

Scholarly Context

Possession is a common motif in horror, often used to explore themes of agency, guilt, and the intrusion of the sacred or profane into the self. Here, it reinforces the idea that SCP-2951’s influence extends beyond physical space into human consciousness.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nature of SCP-2951’s interior (infernal vs purely anomalous)

Position A

SCP-2951’s interior is effectively a hell-like realm of fire and prolonged torment, possibly connected to or identical with a metaphysical underworld.

Position A — evidence

The title "10,000 Years" and repeated references to fire and endurance; dialogue in the film about pillars that support the world and catastrophic consequences if they crack; fan interpretations that explicitly label the interior as hellish.

Position B

SCP-2951 is a spatial-temporal anomaly with hostile entities and symbolic references to punishment, but it is not explicitly or literally "Hell."

Position B — evidence

The SCP article’s clinical tone and avoidance of explicit theological labels; the framing of SCP-2951 as a Euclid-class anomaly with containment procedures focused on physical access rather than spiritual exorcism.

Scholarly Consensus

Within SCP fandom and commentary, there is no single consensus. Many readers embrace the infernal reading as thematically powerful, while others prefer a more ambiguous, cosmic-horror interpretation that leaves the interior’s metaphysical status unresolved.

Literal vs metaphorical "10,000 years"

Position A

The phrase "10,000 years" refers to literal spans of time associated with SCP-2951’s interior, implying extreme temporal distortion or ancient, ongoing punishment.

Position A — evidence

The title and repeated emphasis on long duration; thematic parallels to religious texts that describe punishment in terms of thousands of years.

Position B

"10,000 years" is primarily metaphorical, expressing the subjective experience of endless suffering rather than a precise temporal measurement.

Position B — evidence

Lack of explicit, quantified temporal data in the SCP article; the tendency of horror narratives to use large numbers hyperbolically to convey emotional intensity.

Scholarly Consensus

Commentators generally treat the phrase as both literal and metaphorical, acknowledging that the text invites a sense of vast duration without committing to specific physics. The ambiguity is considered part of the story’s power.

Canonical status of film-specific elements (Commander Terrence, possession)

Position A

Film-specific elements such as Commander Terrence and overt possession are part of SCP-2951’s evolving canon, reflecting the franchise’s multi-author, multi-medium nature.

Position A — evidence

Position B

These elements are non-canonical embellishments that should be distinguished from the original article’s content when analyzing SCP-2951.

Position B — evidence

The SCP Foundation wiki remains the primary canonical source; film adaptations are not officially integrated into the site’s continuity and may alter details for dramatic effect.

Scholarly Consensus

Most lore-focused discussions treat the film as an inspired adaptation rather than strict canon. Scholars of internet fiction emphasize the importance of distinguishing primary texts from derivative works while acknowledging their mutual influence.
