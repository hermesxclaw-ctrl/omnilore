Who It Is

Before the first mountain rose in Manipur, before the first Meitei king sat on his throne, there was Atingkok — a sky-father who spoke the universe into being with a thought, then quietly stepped back to watch his creation unfold. He is the architect of cosmic order, the father of the household god Sanamahi, and the ultimate source of all divine authority in one of India's most fascinating but least-known indigenous religions. Imagine a creator god who doesn't micromanage, who sets the laws of nature and society and then trusts his children to run the show — that's Atingkok, the original 'hands-off' deity.

Atingkok is portrayed as a wise, benevolent, and somewhat distant creator god. He is the prime mover who sets the cosmic order in motion and then withdraws, allowing his children and other deities to manage the world. He is characterized by patience, foresight, and a sense of cosmic justice. In the myths, he is not a capricious or wrathful deity but rather a thoughtful architect of existence. He tests his creations, particularly the first humans, and intervenes only when cosmic balance is threatened.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Atingkok likely derives from the Meitei (Manipuri) root 'ating' meaning 'sky' or 'heavenly' combined with 'kok' meaning 'head' or 'origin'. Thus Atingkok can be read as 'the head/origin of the sky' or 'the heavenly source'. The epithet 'Maru Sidaba' translates roughly as 'the eternal lord'

Pronunciation — Ah-ting-kohk (approximate); in Meitei romanization: Atingkok; often rendered with a glottal stop between the second and third syllables.

Aliases — Atingkok Maru Sidaba, Atingkok Guru Sidaba, Atingkok Sithaba, Salailen Sidaba, Salailen, Soraren

Powers
Creation of the Universe

Atingkok is the sole creator of the universe. From the void, he brought forth the sky, the earth, the celestial bodies, and all living beings. He is often described as creating the world through his thought, word, or by using the primordial elements.

Limit: He does not create ex nihilo in all accounts; some versions suggest he shaped pre-existing primordial matter.

Omniscience and Omnipresence

As the supreme deity, Atingkok is all-knowing and present everywhere. He perceives all events, thoughts, and actions across time.

Limit: He chooses not to intervene in every matter, allowing free will and the natural order to unfold.

Granting of Boons and Curses

Atingkok can bestow blessings, powers, and prosperity upon mortals and deities alike. Conversely, he can impose curses or cosmic punishments for transgressions.

Limit: His boons are typically conditional on moral behavior or ritual purity.

Cosmic Order and Lawgiving

Atingkok established the moral and natural laws of the universe, including the cycles of day and night, seasons, birth, and death. He is the ultimate judge who maintains cosmic balance.

Limit: He delegates the daily enforcement of these laws to lesser deities and spirits.

Shape-shifting and Manifestation

In some myths, Atingkok is able to assume various forms or manifest himself in different aspects to interact with the world, often taking the form of a wise man or an animal.

Limit: Not a central aspect of his character; he usually acts from the celestial realm.

Domains — Atingkok is the lord of creation, the sky, and cosmic order. He presides over the beginning of time, the formation of the universe, and the establishment of natural and social laws. In the domestic sphere, he is honored in the Sanamahi cult, where he is invoked for household protection and prosperity.

Accounts I
The Creation of the UniverseLeithak Leikharol and other Puya

Ancient/Classical period

In the beginning, there was only a vast, formless void. Atingkok, the self-existent lord, willed the universe into being. He first created the sky and the earth, separating them from the primordial waters. He then created the sun, the moon, and the stars to light the world. He fashioned the mountains, rivers, and forests, and filled them with animals and plants. Finally, he created the first human beings, often said to be a man and a woman, and breathed life into them. He established the cycles of nature and the laws of society, then withdrew to the celestial realm, leaving his son Sanamahi to guard the household and the welfare of humans. The establishment of the cosmic order and the beginning of human history. The creation of the world sets the stage for all subsequent myths and the relationship between gods and humans.

Accounts II
The Birth of SanamahiWakoklon Heelel Thilel Salai Amailon Pukok and oral tradition

Ancient/Classical period

Atingkok and Leimarel Sidabi had a son, Sanamahi, who became the most important household deity in Meitei religion. Sanamahi was born from the divine union of the sky father and the earth mother. He was given the duty of protecting the home and the family, and he is honored in every Meitei household with a dedicated shrine in the southwest corner of the main room. The birth of Sanamahi establishes the divine family structure and the link between the cosmic creator and the domestic sphere. Sanamahi becomes the central domestic deity, and his worship is a key part of Meitei religious practice. The story establishes the importance of the family and the home in Meitei culture.

The Creation of the First HumansSakok Lamlen and other Puya

Ancient/Classical period

Atingkok created the first human beings, sometimes said to be a brother and sister or a couple, from clay or from wood. He breathed life into them and taught them the arts of civilization, including agriculture, weaving, and the use of fire. He also gave them the knowledge of the sacred rituals and the names of the deities, ensuring that they would remember their creator. In some versions, Atingkok created multiple pairs of humans in different regions, explaining the diversity of mankind. Humanity is established as the caretaker of the earth, with a direct connection to the divine. The gift of ritual knowledge forms the basis of Sanamahist worship.

Atingkok is a minor local deity with no wider significance. — Atingkok is the supreme creator god in the traditional Meitei religion of Manipur, a tradition with a rich cosmology and a complex pantheon. His significance is central to the cultural and religious identity of the Meitei people.

Sanamahism is a form of Hinduism or a sect of it. — Sanamahism is a distinct indigenous religion with its own cosmology, deities, and practices. While it has been influenced by Hinduism over the centuries, it retains its unique character and is recognized as a separate religious tradition by its adherents.

Atingkok is the same as the Hindu god Brahma. — While some syncretic interpretations equate Atingkok with Brahma, this is a later development. In the traditional Meitei understanding, Atingkok is a unique deity with his own mythology and attributes.

The Meitei creation myths are simple or primitive. — The Meitei creation myths are sophisticated narratives that address complex questions about the origin of the universe, the nature of humanity, and the relationship between the divine and the mortal. They are comparable in depth to creation myths from other world cultures.
