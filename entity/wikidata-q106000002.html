Who It Is

A flaxen chestnut with a star as bright as his future, Taiki Shuttle was a Japanese racing legend whose hooves thundered from Tokyo to Deauville, claiming the hearts of fans and the highest honors of his sport before his quiet retirement and peaceful passing in 2022.

On the track, he was a consistent and versatile performer, winning on both dirt and turf, and from sprint to mile distances. He showed resilience, coming back from injury and setbacks to dominate his divisions. In retirement, he was described as a pensioned stallion, and the incident of his mane being cut suggests he was a calm horse, as he was unharmed.

Mile-to-Sprint Versatility

Taiki Shuttle won graded stakes from 1600 meters (one mile) to 1200 meters (sprint), demonstrating exceptional speed and adaptability.

Limit: He lost his final race at the Sprinters Stakes (1200m), suggesting he may have been better at the mile distance.

International Success

Won the Prix Jacques Le Marois (1600m) in France, proving his ability to travel and compete at the highest level abroad.

Limit: Only one international start.

Consistency and Durability

Won 11 of 13 starts, finishing second and third in the other two. He overcame injuries and a delayed debut to achieve a near-flawless record.

Limit: He had leg issues that delayed his debut and may have contributed to his final defeat.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Flaxen chestnut horse with a large white star. Chestnut coat is a reddish-brown; flaxen mane and tail are lighter, almost blonde. The star is a white marking on the forehead.

Symbols — His racing silks, as owned by Taiki Farm, are not described in the provided source. In popular culture, his anthropomorphized form in Umamusume: Pretty Derby is depicted with a cheerful, energetic design.

Domains — Trained in Japan, specifically at Fujisawa Stable's Miura training center, then raced at various Japanese racecourses including Tokyo, Hanshin, and Kyoto. He also traveled to France for the Prix Jacques Le Marois. After retirement, he stood at Arrow Stud in Shinhidaka, Hokkaido, and later retired to Kimura Farm, then Northern Lake Farm.

Accounts I
Rise from Delayed Debut to ChampionJBIS, netkeiba, news reports

1997

Taiki Shuttle's career began with setbacks: leg injury and hoof suppuration delayed his entry into training and his debut. When he finally raced in April 1997, he won his first three starts, including the Shobu Stakes. After a second-place finish in the Bodaiju Stakes, he won the Unicorn Stakes, Swan Stakes, and Sprinters Stakes, earning the title of Best Sprinter or Miler of 1997. Established himself as a top sprinter/miler in Japan, setting the stage for his 1998 campaign.

Triumph in FranceRacing records, news reports

1998

After winning the Keio Hai Spring Cup and Yasuda Kinen, Taiki Shuttle was shipped to France for the Prix Jacques Le Marois. He won the race, becoming one of the few Japanese horses to win a Group 1 in France at the time. He returned to Japan to win his second Mile Championship. His international victory enhanced his reputation and contributed to his Horse of the Year title.

Accounts II
Final Defeat and RetirementRacing records, news reports

1998

In his final race, the Sprinters Stakes, Taiki Shuttle finished third behind Meiner Love, ending his winning streak. Despite the loss, he was named Horse of the Year. He retired with a record of 11 wins from 13 starts. Retired to stud, leaving a legacy as one of Japan's greatest milers.

The Mane-Cutting IncidentNews reports (2019-2020)

2019

While pensioned at Kimura Farm, Taiki Shuttle and fellow stallion Rose Kingdom had their manes mysteriously cut by an unknown person in September 2019. The horses were unharmed, but the farm filed a police report and suspended visitation. The suspect, Kazuyo Tanaka, was arrested the following year. The incident raised security concerns and led to a halt in public visits to the farm.
