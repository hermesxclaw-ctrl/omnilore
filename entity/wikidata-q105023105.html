The Approach

In the shadowed groves of Afro-Brazilian spiritual practice, there exists a figure whose very name speaks of the forest's raw power: Caboclo Rei dos Troncos, the King of the Trunks. He is not a king of thrones or palaces, but of the ancient, gnarled trunks that stand as silent witnesses to centuries of belief, resistance, and transformation. His presence in the spiritual landscape of Brazil is a testament to the enduring power of ancestral memory and the natural world, yet his story remains largely untold in the written records of the broader world.

Content advisory. This entry discusses Afro-Brazilian religious traditions, including elements of spirit possession and syncretism. It is presented with respect for the living traditions and should not be used to sensationalize or misinterpret these practices.
Identity

Caboclo Rei dos Troncos

Pronunciation ka-BOH-kloo RAY doos TROHN-koos

Etymology

The name is Portuguese: 'Caboclo' refers to a spirit of indigenous Brazilian origin in Afro-Brazilian religions, often associated with the forest and nature. 'Rei' means 'King', and 'Troncos' means 'trunks', likely referring to tree trunks. Thus, the name can be interpreted as 'King of the Tree Trunks' or 'King of the Forest Trunks'.

Cultural Ownership

Afro-Brazilian, specifically within Umbanda and related traditions that incorporate indigenous and African elements.

Disputed Identifications

There is no widely known dispute regarding this specific entity. However, the caboclo category itself encompasses many spirits, and individual names may vary by region and tradition. It is possible that 'Rei dos Troncos' is a specific manifestation or a title given to a particular caboclo in certain houses.

Description

Appearance

No verified description available. In general, caboclos are often depicted as indigenous Brazilian warriors or forest dwellers, wearing feathered headdresses and carrying bows or spears. As a 'King of Trunks', he might be envisioned as an elder figure connected to ancient trees, perhaps with a staff or surrounded by forest imagery.

Symbols & Iconography

Likely associated with tree trunks, forests, and possibly a royal staff or crown. In Umbanda, caboclos are often linked to the forest and its spirits.
