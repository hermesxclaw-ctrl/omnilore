Who It Is

Loki is a recurring high-level demon/Persona in the SMT/Persona franchise, representing chaos, trickery, and transformation. In the Persona series, Loki is most famously the initial Persona of Goro Akechi in Persona 5 — a manifestation of his deceitful 'detective prince' persona and his true, chaotic nature as the black-masked killer. Loki's design in P5 is striking: a black-and-white striped, almost zebra-patterned humanoid figure with a massive horned helmet, jagged sword-wings, and a chessboard-motif body that represents Akechi's life as a 'game piece' manipulated by his father. Loki is typically associated with Almighty, Curse, and Physical skills — befitting a god whose schemes destroy gods and mortals alike.

Origins & Fall

P5 Loki: Tall, slender humanoid with black-and-white striped body (like a zebra or optical illusion). Head is a massive, curved horned helmet that obscures his face, leaving only glowing red eyes visible. Wings are jagged, sword-like protrusions — more weapon than limb. Chessboard patterns on his body reference Akechi's 'game piece' self-perception. Older SMT designs: More traditional depictions showing a red-haired, mischievous figure with flame motifs, sometimes androgynous. SMT V: A dramatic redesign featuring a floating, multi-limbed figure with a mask-like face.

Attested in Video game series — Shin Megami Tensei / Persona (Atlus). Based on the Norse trickster god Loki — the shape-shifting, gender-fluid deity of mischief, chaos, and fire who fathers monsters and engineers the death of Baldr, ultimately leading to Ragnarök.Era 1995–present

Form

Almighty damage (Megidolaon) bypassing all resistances. Curse/Dark magic (Mudoon, Eigaon). Physical attacks emphasizing speed and precision. Debilitating spells (Debilitate, reducing all enemy stats). In Persona 5, Loki grants Akechi the ability to induce psychotic breakdowns — a unique power used to assassinate targets. Loki's versatile movepool makes it effective against nearly any enemy type.

Powers

Loki embodies chaos and rebellion. In P5, Akechi's Loki represents his rejection of the 'hero' persona (embodied by his second Persona, Robin Hood) and his embrace of his true self: angry, betrayed, and willing to destroy everything — including himself — to punish those who wronged him. The duality of Robin Hood (justice) and Loki (chaos) in Akechi's character is central to understanding his psychology. Across SMT, Loki is a Chaos-aligned demon who values freedom above all — including the freedom to destroy — and despises Law-aligned systems of control.

Almighty Attacks (Megidolaon) In the Persona series, Loki is one of the earliest personas to learn 'Megidolaon' or other powerful Almighty spells. This represents his mythological ability to bypass conventional rules, laws, and defenses—attacking the very essence of reality. Persona 2, 4, 5 High SP cost; cannot be blocked or resisted by typical means, but is vulnerable to magic reflection in some games. None in-game; this is a game-mechanic representation of his 'rule-breaking' nature.

Ice Magic (Bufudyne, Mabufudyne) Loki is consistently associated with Ice magic in the series. This is a subtle reference to his mythological children: the monstrous wolf Fenrir and the serpent Jörmungandr, both of whom are associated with the cold, destructive forces of winter and the end of the world (Ragnarök). All appearances Ineffective against enemies that absorb or nullify Ice. None.

Status Ailments (Charm, Confusion, Despair) Loki's skillset often includes spells that confuse, charm, or inflict despair on enemies. This reflects his mythological role as a master of lies, manipulation, and psychological warfare. Persona 3, 4, 5 (via skill inheritance) Some enemies are immune to these ailments. None.

Agility and Evasion His stats in the games are typically weighted heavily towards Agility and Luck, making him a fast and unpredictable combatant. This reflects his mythological ability to slip out of traps and transform to escape any situation. All appearances Low physical defense. None.

Shape-shifting (Mythological, not a direct game mechanic) While not a direct battle skill, the game's lore and design nod to his mythological shape-shifting. His costume and mask imply a fluid identity, and his fusion mechanics (combining two personas to create him) symbolize his nature as a composite, ever-changing being. Persona series (lore and design) Not a direct mechanic. None.

Behavior & Habitat

Light/Bless attacks are typically effective (Loki's chaos is weak to holy order). In Norse myth, he is bound with his son's entrails until Ragnarök — symbolically, he is weak to being trapped or outsmarted. In P5, Akechi's Loki is ultimately consumed by his own rage and outmaneuvered by the Phantom Thieves.

Weaknesses & Wards

Loki is one of the most narratively significant Personas in the series due to his role in Persona 5 — Akechi's dual Persona represents one of the most complex villain arcs in JRPG history. The chess/optical illusion design of P5's Loki is frequently praised as one of the best Persona designs. Loki's recurring presence across SMT as a Chaos representative makes him a through-line for the series' Law vs. Chaos thematic conflict.

Accounts

Loki, jealous of the beloved god Baldr, discovers that Baldr is immune to all things in the world because his mother Frigg has extracted an oath from everything to not harm him. Loki, in disguise as an old woman, tricks Frigg into revealing that the mistletoe was too young to swear the oath. Loki then fashions a dart from the mistletoe and tricks the blind god Höðr into throwing it at Baldr, killing him. This is the catalyst for Ragnarök. In the games, this story is not told, but Loki's archetype as a 'murderer of joy' and 'instigator of the end' is inherent in his chaotic alignment.

Thor's hammer is stolen by the giant Þrymr, who demands Freyja's hand in marriage as a ransom. Loki helps Thor by disguising him as Freyja (and himself as a handmaiden) to infiltrate the giant's hall. Loki's quick wit and sharp tongue save the ruse until Thor gets his hammer back and slaughters the giants. This story highlights Loki's role as a necessary, if unreliable, ally to the gods.

After Baldr's death, the gods capture Loki. They bind him to a rock with the entrails of his son Nari or Narfi (depending on the version), and a serpent is placed above him to drip venom onto his face. His faithful wife Sigyn holds a bowl to catch the venom, but when she empties it, Loki's screams of agony cause earthquakes. This is his 'imprisonment' until Ragnarök.

Loki, in the form of a mare, mates with the stallion Svaðilfari and gives birth to the eight-legged horse Sleipnir, who becomes Odin's steed. With the giantess Angrboða, he fathers the wolf Fenrir, the serpent Jörmungandr, and Hel, the ruler of the underworld. These children are prophesied to bring about the destruction of the gods.

Field Notes

⛧ You are the god who ruins everything. Not out of malice — or maybe entirely out of malice, you've forgotten which — but because order is boring and rules are made to be broken and the gods have been smug for far too long. So you become a horse and seduce a stallion. You carve a mistletoe dart and hand it to a blind man. You father a wolf that will eat the sun. And when they chain you up with your own son's entrails, you smile, because you know the chains won't hold. You are chaos. You are the fun parent. That's Loki: the god who looked at the universe and said, 'What if we shook things up a little?'
