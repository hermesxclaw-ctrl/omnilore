Who It Is

Before he was a Marvel anti-hero with a redemption arc, before he was a tragic boy in a video game, Loki was the Norse god who murdered joy itself, bound the forces of the apocalypse, and once gave birth to an eight-legged horse. In the sprawling, demon-packed universe of Shin Megami Tensei and Persona, Atlus took this chaotic, boundary-shattering figure and distilled him into a single, iconic image: a gaunt, white-faced jester in black and white, his long nose a razor-sharp contradiction to his elegant suit. He is the lie that reveals a deeper truth, the chaos that exposes the flaw in every order, and the whisper in your ear that says 'the rules are a suggestion.' Whether he is the persona of a detective fighting for justice or a demon demanding your soul in a negotiation, the Atlus Loki is a masterclass in turning a thousand-year-old myth into a modern, psychological weapon.

As a Persona, Loki is not a character with a personality but a manifestation of the user's psyche. He represents the 'rebellious intellect' and the 'shadow of reason'. When summoned, he acts as a tool, deploying powerful Almighty (non-elemental) and Ice-based attacks. As a demon in SMT, he is a cunning, talkative, and manipulative negotiator. He delights in riddles, trickery, and offering deals that seem beneficial but often have a hidden, chaotic cost. Powers
Almighty Attacks (Megidolaon)

In the Persona series, Loki is one of the earliest personas to learn 'Megidolaon' or other powerful Almighty spells. This represents his mythological ability to bypass conventional rules, laws, and defenses—attacking the very essence of reality.

Limit: High SP cost; cannot be blocked or resisted by typical means, but is vulnerable to magic reflection in some games.

Ice Magic (Bufudyne, Mabufudyne)

Loki is consistently associated with Ice magic in the series. This is a subtle reference to his mythological children: the monstrous wolf Fenrir and the serpent Jörmungandr, both of whom are associated with the cold, destructive forces of winter and the end of the world (Ragnarök).

Limit: Ineffective against enemies that absorb or nullify Ice.

Status Ailments (Charm, Confusion, Despair)

Loki's skillset often includes spells that confuse, charm, or inflict despair on enemies. This reflects his mythological role as a master of lies, manipulation, and psychological warfare.

Limit: Some enemies are immune to these ailments.

Agility and Evasion

His stats in the games are typically weighted heavily towards Agility and Luck, making him a fast and unpredictable combatant. This reflects his mythological ability to slip out of traps and transform to escape any situation.

Limit: Low physical defense.

Shape-shifting (Mythological, not a direct game mechanic)

While not a direct battle skill, the game's lore and design nod to his mythological shape-shifting. His costume and mask imply a fluid identity, and his fusion mechanics (combining two personas to create him) symbolize his nature as a composite, ever-changing being.

Limit: Not a direct mechanic.

Accounts I
The Slaying of BaldrProse Edda (Gylfaginning)

13th Century (recording of older tradition)

Loki, jealous of the beloved god Baldr, discovers that Baldr is immune to all things in the world because his mother Frigg has extracted an oath from everything to not harm him. Loki, in disguise as an old woman, tricks Frigg into revealing that the mistletoe was too young to swear the oath. Loki then fashions a dart from the mistletoe and tricks the blind god Höðr into throwing it at Baldr, killing him. This is the catalyst for Ragnarök. In the games, this story is not told, but Loki's archetype as a 'murderer of joy' and 'instigator of the end' is inherent in his chaotic alignment. Baldr's death leads to the binding of Loki, and ultimately to Ragnarök, where Loki fights against the gods and is killed by Heimdallr.

The Theft of Mjölnir (Þrymskviða)Poetic Edda

13th Century

Thor's hammer is stolen by the giant Þrymr, who demands Freyja's hand in marriage as a ransom. Loki helps Thor by disguising him as Freyja (and himself as a handmaiden) to infiltrate the giant's hall. Loki's quick wit and sharp tongue save the ruse until Thor gets his hammer back and slaughters the giants. This story highlights Loki's role as a necessary, if unreliable, ally to the gods. Thor retrieves his hammer; the giants are killed. Loki proves his value as a 'fixer' for the gods' problems.

Accounts II
The Binding of LokiProse Edda (Gylfaginning)

13th Century

After Baldr's death, the gods capture Loki. They bind him to a rock with the entrails of his son Nari or Narfi (depending on the version), and a serpent is placed above him to drip venom onto his face. His faithful wife Sigyn holds a bowl to catch the venom, but when she empties it, Loki's screams of agony cause earthquakes. This is his 'imprisonment' until Ragnarök. Loki's punishment cements his status as an enemy of the gods. In the games, this story explains his association with binding, knots, and venom (poison), which sometimes manifests as a skill or a design element.

The Birth of the MonstersProse Edda (Gylfaginning), Poetic Edda (Hyndluljóð)

13th Century

Loki, in the form of a mare, mates with the stallion Svaðilfari and gives birth to the eight-legged horse Sleipnir, who becomes Odin's steed. With the giantess Angrboða, he fathers the wolf Fenrir, the serpent Jörmungandr, and Hel, the ruler of the underworld. These children are prophesied to bring about the destruction of the gods. The gods raise the monsters themselves to control them, but ultimately, they fulfill their destructive destiny. Loki's role as a 'father of monsters' is a key part of his archetype in the games, where he is often a high-level 'demon' associated with the 'Tyrant' or 'Fallen' races.

The Atlus Loki is the same as the Marvel Loki. — They are completely distinct interpretations. The Atlus Loki is a demonic archetype of chaos, while the Marvel Loki is a tragic, complex anti-hero. They share only the name and the 'trickster' label.

Loki is 'the' devil or Satan. — While Loki is an agent of chaos and often opposes the gods, he is not a singular 'devil' figure. He is a trickster, a shape-shifter, and a boundary-walker. In the MegaTen series, he is distinct from Satan and Lucifer, who have their own separate roles and designs.

Loki is a god. — This is disputed. He is often referred to as a 'god' in popular culture, but in the Norse sources, he is more accurately a jötunn (giant) who lives among the gods. He is not worshipped and is often treated as an outsider.

Loki's 'jester' design is from Norse mythology. — The jester's cap and mask are a modern invention by Atlus, designed to visually represent his 'trickster' archetype. The Norse Loki had no such costume.
