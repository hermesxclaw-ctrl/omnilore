Who It Is

Sraosha (from Avestan sru- 'to hear') is the yazata of hearing — of obedient hearkening to the divine word and the ritual recitation of the Gathas. He is the intermediary par excellence between the human worshiper and the divine realm, carrying prayers upward and bringing blessings downward. Every Zoroastrian ritual is performed 'with Sraosha present' (the Sroš Baj invocation, a seven-line protective formula, is the first prayer many Zoroastrians learn). Sraosha's most solemn function is as the psychopomp who guides the soul for three days after bodily death, protecting it from the assault of daevas (demons) and the corpse-demon Nasu. On the fourth dawn, he leads the soul to the Chinvat Bridge for judgment, where he serves alongside Mithra and Rashnu. His nocturnal battle against the forces of evil is nightly: he 'never sleeps' and his mace is perpetually raised.
