Who It Is

In the grim darkness of the 41st millennium, the Orks wage an eternal, joyful war—and at the heart of their green-skinned savagery lurk two gods who are, by any rational measure, the same deity. Gork and Mork, the brutal and cunning, the cunning and brutal, embody the Orkish paradox: a race that fights for the sheer love of it, and whose belief literally shapes reality. They are the ultimate expression of Ork nature, and their presence is felt in every bellowed WAAAGH! and every clang of a crude choppa.

Gork and Mork embody the Ork psyche. Gork is the brutal and cunning, representing the raw, violent, and direct approach to war. Mork is the cunning and brutal, representing the sneaky, tactical, and underhanded approach. In Ork mythology, they are constantly fighting each other, and their battles shape the Ork race. They are also depicted as being intensely interested in the wars of their followers, and they grant blessings to Orks who fight well.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The names 'Gork' and 'Mork' are likely onomatopoeic or playful, evoking the sounds of violence ('gork' and 'mork' as percussive, guttural words). They may also be a play on 'gob' or 'goblin' and 'ork', but no official etymology has been given by Games Workshop.

Pronunciation — Gork: /ɡɔːrk/ (rhymes with 'fork'); Mork: /mɔːrk/ (rhymes with 'fork' or 'talk')

Aliases — The Ork Gods, The Twin Gods of the Orks, Gork and Mork (as a pair), Gork (the brutal and cunning), Mork (the cunning and brutal)

Ownership — Created by Games Workshop for the Warhammer 40,000 and Warhammer Fantasy settings. They are a fictional creation and are not based on any real-world mythology.

Powers
Warp Entity Existence

Gork and Mork are Warp entities, meaning they are manifestations of the psychic energies of the Ork race. They are sustained by Ork belief and are effectively immortal as long as Orks exist.

Limit: They cannot physically manifest in the material universe except through the actions of their followers or in the Warp.

Blessings and Curses

Gork and Mork are said to bless Orks who perform great acts of violence or cunning, granting them supernatural strength, luck, or resilience. Conversely, they may curse Orks who act cowardly or dishonorably (in the Ork sense).

Limit: The mechanics of these blessings are not detailed in the game rules; they are mainly background lore.

WAAAGH! Energy

The collective psychic energy of the Orks, known as the WAAAGH!, is channeled by Gork and Mork. This energy can manifest as weirdboyz' psychic powers, and in extreme cases, it can cause reality to bend to the Orks' belief, such as making red vehicles faster.

Limit: The WAAAGH! energy is a background element and is not fully explained in the rules.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Gork and Mork are rarely depicted directly. In Ork iconography, they are shown as massive, green-skinned Orks with exaggerated features, often wielding huge weapons. Gork is typically portrayed as more brutish and muscular, with a heavy brow and a snarling expression, while Mork is depicted as more cunning, with a sly grin and a slightly leaner build. Symbols — The most common symbols associated with Gork and Mork are the Ork glyphs for 'war' and 'destruction'. Ork banners often feature crude depictions of the twin gods, sometimes as two heads facing opposite directions, or as a single two-faced head. Ork vehicles and equipment are often adorned with icons of Gork and Mork, and Ork war cries often invoke their names.

Domains — Gork and Mork reside in the Warp, the psychic dimension of the Warhammer 40,000 universe. They are Warp entities that are created and sustained by the collective psychic belief of the Ork race. They are not tied to any physical location, but their influence is strongest where Orks are present.

Mythological, timeless

Gork and Mork are constantly fighting each other, and their battles are so violent that they shake the Warp. This eternal war is the template for all Ork behavior. The Ork race is perpetually warlike, and their gods' example is used to justify their aggression.

Accounts II
The Creation of the OrksVarious sources (e.g., Codex: Orks, Xenology)

Pre-history

The Old Ones engineered the Orks to be the ultimate warriors, but after the Old Ones vanished, the Orks evolved their own culture and religion, with Gork and Mork as their gods. The Orks spread across the galaxy, and their gods became a fundamental part of their psyche.

The Great WAAAGH! of GhazghkullCodex: Orks (various editions), novels, and White Dwarf articles

M41

Ghazghkull was killed and resurrected by the gods, who told him to unite the Ork race and conquer the galaxy. He now leads a massive WAAAGH! that threatens the Imperium. The WAAAGH! has become a major threat to the Imperium and has drawn the attention of other factions.
