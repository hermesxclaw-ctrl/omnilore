Who It Is

In the shadow of Lake Khövsgöl, a shamanic deity who once eloped with a Khan's daughter still guards the eastern shores—his stone image vanished, but his name echoes in incantations and a hidden cave where horsemen still leave their offerings.

As a fertility deity, he grants cattle and children. He is also a patron of shamanic initiation and a protector. Legends describe him as a trickster figure, eloping with Genghis Khan's wife or daughter, and as a betrayer of black shamans by siding with Buddhism.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Dayan' likely derives from Mongolian 'dayan' (meaning 'the second' or 'again'), possibly indicating a secondary or recurring nature; 'Deerh' or 'Degereki' may relate to 'upper' or 'heavenly' (cf. 'deger' meaning 'above'). Exact etymology is uncertain.

Pronunciation — Approximately /ˈda.jan ˈdeːrx/ in Mongolian; variations exist.

Aliases — Dayan Degereki, Dayan Dērx

Ownership — Mongolian (Khalkha and Buryat), particularly associated with the Darkhad Valley and the eastern shore of Lake Khövsgöl.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Depicted as an equestrian figure wearing Mongolian attire, including boots. He often has a triple shamanic feather crown with three mirrors, but statues show a warrior helmet. In a scroll painting, he wears a mirror around his neck to ward off evil spirits, and a bead necklace symbolizing the coexistence of Buddhism and shamanism.

Symbols — Triple shamanic feather crown, three mirrors, warrior helmet, horse, mirror necklace, bead necklace.

Domains — Associated with Lake Khövsgöl, particularly the eastern side; a cave near the Russia-Mongolia border; the Deerkhiin Khüree monastery; and a shrine in Tsagaannuur.

Accounts I
Elopement with Genghis Khan's wife or daughterBirtalan (2005, 2011) as cited in Wikipedia

Unknown (legendary)

Dayan Deerh, as a shaman for Genghis Khan, eloped with the Khan's wife or daughter. The Khan threatened him with death by fire and metal, but Dayan Deerh was invulnerable to those; he could only be killed by suffocation. In return for not being threatened with suffocation, he became a protector of the Khan and his people. Dayan Deerh becomes a protector of Genghis Khan and his people.

Accounts II
Betrayal of black shamansBirtalan (2005, 2011) as cited in Wikipedia

Unknown

Dayan Deerh is said to have betrayed black shamans by going over to the Buddhist side, which explains why some black shamans reject him. Division in shamanic practice; Dayan Deerh becomes associated with yellow shamanism.

Transformation into a stone statueBirtalan (2011) as cited in Wikipedia

Unknown

According to sacral texts, Dayan Deerh turned himself into a stone statue. Warriors of Genghis Khan tried unsuccessfully to destroy it. Statue venerated in Tsagaannuur; later disappeared.
