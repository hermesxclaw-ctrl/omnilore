Archive Dossier — Identity

Dayan Deerh

Pronunciation Approximately /ˈda.jan ˈdeːrx/ in Mongolian; variations exist.

Known As

Dayan DegerekiDayan Dērx

Etymology

The name 'Dayan' likely derives from Mongolian 'dayan' (meaning 'the second' or 'again'), possibly indicating a secondary or recurring nature; 'Deerh' or 'Degereki' may relate to 'upper' or 'heavenly' (cf. 'deger' meaning 'above'). Exact etymology is uncertain.

Cultural Ownership

Mongolian (Khalkha and Buryat), particularly associated with the Darkhad Valley and the eastern shore of Lake Khövsgöl.

Disputed Identifications

Some black shamans reject Dayan Deerh, viewing him as a traitor to their tradition for aligning with Buddhism. His identity as a former shaman of Genghis Khan is a legendary claim, not historically verified.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the shadow of Lake Khövsgöl, a shamanic deity who once eloped with a Khan's daughter still guards the eastern shores—his stone image vanished, but his name echoes in incantations and a hidden cave where horsemen still leave their offerings.

I crossed the sky on my spirit horse, I spoke with Ülgen and faced Erlik. Now I wait in the drum and the antler — call me, and I will come.

Who It Is

Dayan Deerh is a powerful spirit-deity in Mongolian shamanism, believed to be the deified spirit of an ancient, immensely powerful shaman who achieved near-divine status. The name 'Dayan Deerh' means 'the one who rises' or 'the one who can fly to the sky,' referencing his ability to ascend through the celestial layers that ordinary shamans cannot reach. In Mongol shamanic practice, Dayan Deerh is the patron of shamans (böö) — it is his spirit that possesses and empowers a shaman during ceremonies. He is invoked at the beginning of shamanic rituals to open the path to the spirit world. He is depicted as a powerful warrior-shaman wearing a helmet with antlers, carrying a drum and a sword. Dayan Deerh is also a psychopomp who guides the souls of the dead to their proper place in the afterlife. Unlike Ülgen or Erlik, who are cosmic deities, Dayan Deerh is closer to the human world — he was once a man who transcended mortality through shamanic power. He rides a spirit horse that can traverse all three worlds (upper, middle, lower). His cult is particularly strong among the Daur Mongols and the Buryats. Dayan Deerh's role demonstrates how Mongolian shamanism blurs the line between ancestor veneration and deity worship — powerful shamans become spirits who guide, protect, and empower their living successors.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Depicted as an equestrian figure wearing Mongolian attire, including boots. He often has a triple shamanic feather crown with three mirrors, but statues show a warrior helmet. In a scroll painting, he wears a mirror around his neck to ward off evil spirits, and a bead necklace symbolizing the coexistence of Buddhism and shamanism.

Symbols & Iconography

Triple shamanic feather crown, three mirrors, warrior helmet, horse, mirror necklace, bead necklace.

Behaviour & Personality

As a fertility deity, he grants cattle and children. He is also a patron of shamanic initiation and a protector. Legends describe him as a trickster figure, eloping with Genghis Khan's wife or daughter, and as a betrayer of black shamans by siding with Buddhism.

Domains & Habitats

Associated with Lake Khövsgöl, particularly the eastern side; a cave near the Russia-Mongolia border; the Deerkhiin Khüree monastery; and a shrine in Tsagaannuur.

Version Differences

In yellow shamanism, he is a protector and integrated into Buddhist practice; in black shamanism, he is often rejected. Some black shamans do not accept him. His role evolved from fertility god to shamanic spirit to protector.
