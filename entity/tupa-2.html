Who They Are

Tupã is the supreme creator god of the Guarani people, the lord of thunder and lightning, and the architect of the universe. In Guarani mythology, Tupã created the world from the primordial waters, fashioned the first humans from clay, and established the cosmic order. His voice is heard in the thunder, and his light is seen in the lightning. He is the most powerful and revered figure in the Guarani pantheon, and his name is the Guarani word for 'god.'

Cultural Significance

Tupã is the most important figure in Guarani religion, and his worship has persisted continuously from pre-Columbian times to the present. The Guarani name for God — Tupã — was adopted by Jesuit missionaries in the 17th and 18th centuries to translate the Christian God, which made the Guarani acceptance of Christianity relatively smooth but also meant that many Guarani syncretized Tupã with the Christian god. The concept of Yvy Marã Ey ('the Land Without Evil') — the earthly paradise where Tupã dwells — inspired several famous Guarani migrations to the east coast of Brazil in the 16th and 17th centuries, as shamans led followers in search of the promised land.
