The Approach

When Edward Elric shatters his automail arm in battle, he does not call a doctor or an alchemist — he calls Winry Rockbell. At fifteen, wrench in hand and grease on her cheek, she is the only person in Amestris who can rebuild a boy out of steel and keep him human enough to keep fighting. Winry never transmutes, never fights with alchemy, and never wants to — her power is quieter and rarer: to mend what war and ambition break, even when the person who broke it is the boy she loves.

Content advisory. Contains discussion of war-related parental death, child bereavement, graphic injury and amputation (automail surgery), gun violence and attempted revenge, state hostage-taking and coercion, and wartime medical ethics in the Ishvalan War. No sexual violence is attested for this character.

Etymology

Given name 'Winry' is an original coinage by Arakawa, rendered in katakana as Winrī, likely chosen for its bright, Western-sounding resonance rather than a traditional Japanese or European etymology; no official etymology is given in the manga or guidebooks. Surname 'Rockbell' combines English 'rock' and 'bell', evoking both the Resembool (hometown name) phonetics and the metallic, resonant imagery appropriate to a family of automail smiths. Arakawa has not provided an in-universe linguistic origin for the surname. The Resembool place name itself plays on 'resemble' and 'bell'.

Cultural Ownership

Created and owned by Hiromu Arakawa, serialized by Square Enix in Monthly Shōnen Gangan (2001-2010). Published in English by VIZ Media. Anime adaptations produced by Bones Inc. Character design and narrative authority rests with Arakawa; anime staff (Seiji Mizushima for 2003, Yasuhiro Irie for Brotherhood) adapted under supervision. No traditional folklore ownership; modern Japanese manga creation with global fandom stewardship.

Disputed Identifications

Not to be confused with Pinako Rockbell (grandmother) or Sara Rockbell (mother). Fan conflation of Winry's role in the 2003 anime (where her romantic arc is muted and her engineering mentorship differs) with manga/Brotherhood canon is common but inaccurate. No relation to similarly named characters in other franchises. The 2003 anime and Brotherhood are distinct continuities; Winry's actions in one do not canonically apply to the other without attribution.

Description

Appearance

Winry is depicted as a teenage girl, initially 15 and 16 by the series' end (born c. 1899 in the series' pseudo-early-20th-century timeline). She has long, light blond hair typically tied in a high ponytail with bangs, blue eyes (light in manga, vivid blue in anime), and a light complexion. Her work attire is iconic: a dark tube top or tank top under denim overalls/work coveralls, often with a bandana, gloves, and a tool belt, with grease smudges when working. Casual wear includes simple blouses, skirts, and later a more mature coat in the epilogue. She is slightly shorter than Edward after his late growth spurt — a deliberate visual gag in Ch. 85-86. Arakawa draws her with expressive, large eyes and hands that are always detailed when handling tools, emphasizing dexterity over sexualization. No automail herself; her body is entirely organic, contrasted deliberately with Edward's steel limbs.

Symbols & Iconography

Symbols associated with Winry are mechanical rather than mystical: crossed wrenches, automail schematics, the Rockbell family dog Rush Valley souvenir earrings (small hoop/stud), and most importantly, the screwdriver and wrench as extensions of care. Her earrings, made by Edward and Alphonse as children from scrap metal, function as a narrative symbol of childhood promise and later romantic commitment — Edward returns them polished and she wears them continuously. The Rockbell automail shop sign (a gear and wrench) and the Resembool hills/house represent home and non-alchemical labor. Unlike alchemists who bear transmutation circles, Winry's iconography is deliberately non-magical: oil, steel, and bandages. In official art, she is often posed with Paninya or Garfiel's baby, reinforcing healer/builder motifs.

Behaviour & Personality

Canonically kind, optimistic, sincere, and emotionally direct, but also famously hot-tempered and physically assertive — she regularly throws wrenches at Edward when he breaks his automail through recklessness. Arakawa describes her as the 'buffer' who allows readers to understand the Elrics' pain because she can voice what they cannot. She is empathetic and outspoken about the emotional cost of violence, telling Edward her hands are for building and saving, not killing. She is an 'Automail Otaku' — intensely passionate about machinery to the point of ignoring social cues when presented with a novel prosthesis. She shows early maturity: delivering a baby at 15 in Rush Valley, confronting Scar with a gun and then choosing not to shoot, and later choosing to aid Scar despite his murder of her parents, reflecting her parents' medical ethic. She is stubborn, refuses to leave the country when Edward tells her to flee for safety, and insists on supporting him on her own terms. Her romantic feelings for Edward develop gradually and are textually confirmed in manga/Brotherhood, though she is initially flustered and in denial.

Domains & Habitats

Primary habitat is Resembool, a rural town in the east of Amestris, in the Rockbell family house-workshop run with her grandmother Pinako. Secondary domain is Rush Valley, the automail engineering capital, where she apprentices under Garfiel (manga/Brotherhood) after being refused by Dominic. She also temporarily resides in Central City (at Maes Hughes's house, at the Elrics' dorm) and in the North (Briggs area) when servicing Edward's cold-weather automail. Her workshop is her cosmology — a space of non-alchemical creation that stands in deliberate contrast to the military, the laboratory, and the Gate. She operates outside the state alchemist/military hierarchy, representing civilian craft, domestic medicine, and post-war reconstruction.

Version Differences

Manga/Brotherhood (canon): Winry's arc is tightly integrated with the Ishvalan War legacy, Scar's redemption, and her apprenticeship under Garfiel; romance with Edward is explicit and culminates in marriage and children in Ch. 108 epilogue (photograph shows two children). Age progression is clear (15 → 16 → 18 in epilogue). 2003 anime: Rush Valley arc is altered — she apprentices more directly with Dominic and is less involved in the northern hostage plot; the Scar confrontation is similar but the resolution and her later involvement in the Homunculi climax differ significantly; the anime adds original material where she discovers the Elrics' bodies are linked to alchemy and aids in the underground city. No epilogue marriage is shown; instead the 2005 film Conqueror of Shamballa shows an alternate-world counterpart. Personality is consistent across versions, but 2003 Winry has slightly less agency in the final act and her engineering feats are less emphasized. Both English dubs use Caitlin Glass, but Japanese voice changes (Toyoguchi 2003 vs. Takamoto Brotherhood). Attested vs adaptation-specific is clearly marked in guidebooks.

Powers & Abilities

Automail Engineering - Design, Fabrication and Fitting

Source VersionManga Ch. 9, 42, 63; Brotherhood Ep. 10-12, 41; Profiles guidebook - attested

Master automail mechanic, described in-universe as a prodigy. Can design, forge, assemble, and surgically fit fully functional prosthetic limbs integrated with nerves and musculature, including Edward's highly advanced arm and leg. Capable of custom cold-weather models for Briggs and high-performance combat models. Skills include metallurgy, nerve-interface calibration, and maintenance under field conditions.

Limitations

Requires workshop tools, materials, and time; cannot perform automail surgery alone without a surgeon (Pinako handles surgical implantation, Winry assists and later performs independently as she matures). Automail requires regular maintenance and is vulnerable to damage, cold, and water. No alchemical ability to create automail instantly.

Contradictions

2003 anime implies she can perform more solo surgeries earlier than manga timeline suggests; manga shows gradual apprenticeship under Pinako then Garfiel.

Mechanical Diagnostics and Field Repair

Source VersionManga Ch. 11, 64; Brotherhood Ep. 12, 41 - attested

Can diagnose automail faults by sound, feel, and visual inspection, and perform emergency repairs with limited tools. Notable feat: identifying a missing screw she herself forgot after three sleepless nights (Ch. 11), and later rapid field replacement of Edward's arm in the North (Ch. 64) under Kimblee's surveillance.

Limitations

Fatigue impairs precision (the missing screw incident). Field repairs are temporary without proper calibration.

Contradictions

None significant; consistently portrayed.

Emergency Medicine and Midwifery

Source VersionManga Ch. 11-12 (Rush Valley birth), Ch. 65 (treating Scar); Brotherhood Ep. 12, 42 - attested

Trained informally by Pinako (a renowned automail surgeon) and by her late parents (both doctors). Successfully delivered Dominic's grandchild (a breech/baby in distress) at age 15 in Rush Valley, saving mother and infant when no doctor was available. Provides first aid, wound bandaging, and triage, including treating Scar's severe wounds despite personal trauma.

Limitations

Not a licensed physician; cannot perform major surgery alone and defers to Pinako for automail implantation surgery. Her medical knowledge is practical and inherited, not formally credentialed.

Contradictions

2003 anime omits the childbirth scene's full medical detail; some fan summaries exaggerate her as a full doctor, which is inaccurate.

Emotional Resilience and Moral Influence (Narrative Ability)

Source VersionManga Ch. 54-55, 65; Brotherhood Ep. 24-25, 42; Arakawa commentary in Chronicle - attested, interpretation labeled

No supernatural power, but functions as the series' moral anchor. Her refusal to kill Scar and her decision to treat him embodies the Rockbell medical ethic ('save lives regardless of who they are'). Her presence directly alters Edward's decisions — he pins her gun down rather than letting her become a killer, and her words ('your hands are for saving lives') recur as his ethical restraint. This is treated as a character power within the narrative structure.

Limitations

Cannot prevent violence or change military structures alone; her influence is interpersonal, not systemic. She is physically vulnerable and is used as a hostage precisely because she is non-combatant.

Contradictions

None; consistent across versions, though 2003 anime gives this theme less resolution.

Apprenticeship Learning and Technical Adaptation

Source VersionManga Ch. 11-12, 64, 108; Guidebooks - attested

Rapid learner who absorbs techniques from Dominic, Garfiel, and Atelier engineers in Rush Valley. Learns to work with new alloys and to design automail for different climates and fighting styles. By the epilogue she is running the Rockbell shop independently alongside Pinako and later with her own family.

Limitations

Initially refused by Dominic due to his fear of Pinako and his no-apprentice rule; requires sponsorship to enter the trade as a young woman in a male-dominated field.

Contradictions

2003 anime has her stay in Rush Valley longer and learn under Dominic more directly, contradicting manga where Garfiel is her direct master.

Stories & Myths

The Rockbell Legacy and the Orphaning in Ishval

Source Fullmetal Alchemist manga Ch. 9, 54-55; Fullmetal Alchemist Chronicle; Brotherhood Ep. 10, 24Culture Amestrian (fictional nation modeled on early 20th-century Europe) - manga creationPeriod Manga publication 2001-2010; in-universe c. 1901-1908 (Ishvalan War)

Summary

Winry is born in Resembool to renowned doctors Urey and Sara Rockbell, who run a clinic alongside automail surgeon Pinako. During the Ishvalan War of Extermination, her parents volunteer to treat the wounded on both sides without discrimination in Ishval. They are murdered by Scar, an Ishvalan monk whose brother's arm was taken for his own automail, in a moment of vengeful grief after Amestrian soldiers kill his brother. Winry, still a young child, is raised thereafter by her grandmother Pinako. The trauma is initially hidden from her; she learns the truth as a teenager when she confronts Scar. Her parents' ethic — to save anyone regardless of affiliation — becomes her own moral inheritance.

Building Edward's Automail - The Price of Human Transmutation

Source Manga Ch. 1, 9, 23; Brotherhood Ep. 3, 10; 2003 anime Ep. 3Culture Amestrian / mangaPeriod 2001-2003 publication; in-universe c. 1910-1911

Summary

After Edward and Alphonse attempt to resurrect their mother Trisha via human transmutation, Edward loses his left leg and right arm (the latter to bind Al's soul to armor) and Al loses his entire body. Pinako and Winry, their childhood neighbors and surrogate family after Trisha's death, rescue and rebuild Edward, surgically installing automail limbs that Winry helps design and maintain despite being only ~11 at initial installation. When Scar destroys Edward's arm in Central, the brothers return to Resembool and Winry, now 15, forges a new arm in days, working without sleep. Her pride is wounded when she forgets a single screw due to exhaustion, causing the arm to fail in a later fight — a humbling lesson in craft and care.

Rush Valley - The Automail Capital and the Birth

Source Manga Ch. 10-12; Brotherhood Ep. 11-12; 2003 anime Ep. 9-10Culture Amestrian / mangaPeriod 2003-2004 publication; in-universe 1914

Summary

En route to meet Izumi Curtis, Winry insists on detouring to Rush Valley, the mecca of automail engineers. There she befriends Paninya, a young pickpocket with automail legs who steals to survive after being crippled and abandoned. Winry convinces her to earn money honestly and becomes her friend. She also meets Dominic, a gruff master engineer, and his pregnant daughter-in-law. When the daughter-in-law goes into premature labor with no doctor available, Winry — at 15, with only Pinako's informal training — successfully delivers the baby, saving both lives. Dominic, impressed but bound by his refusal to take apprentices and his fear of Pinako (with whom he shares a contentious past), refers her to Garfiel, who takes her as an apprentice. She remains in Rush Valley to study while the Elrics continue their journey.

The Gun at Scar - Inheritance vs. Revenge

Source Manga Ch. 54-55; Brotherhood Ep. 24-25; 2003 anime Ep. 33Culture Amestrian / mangaPeriod 2006-2007 publication; in-universe 1914

Summary

Reunited with the Elrics in Central, Winry learns that Maes Hughes has been murdered, deepening her grief. When the Elrics track Scar for information about the Philosopher's Stone, Winry follows to help and discovers Scar is her parents' killer. In a warehouse confrontation, she seizes Edward's gun and points it at the wounded Scar, trembling with rage and grief. Edward physically intervenes, pinning the gun down and telling her that her hands are meant to save lives, not take them — echoing her parents' philosophy. Scar escapes with Alphonse in pursuit. Edward later tells her that the next time he makes her cry, they will be tears of joy, and Winry realizes she has been in love with him since childhood.

Hostage in the North - Cold-Weather Automail and the Choice to Save Scar

Source Manga Ch. 63-65, 71-72; Brotherhood Ep. 41-42, 45Culture Amestrian / mangaPeriod 2007-2008 publication; in-universe 1914-1915

Summary

After Edward and Alphonse discover that King Bradley is the Homunculus Wrath, the military uses Winry and Riza Hawkeye as hostages to ensure obedience. Winry is sent north to Briggs under escort by Solf J. Kimblee, ostensibly to service Edward's automail which is failing in the extreme cold. She replaces his arm with a northern model. Edward confesses he is being coerced to capture Scar. When the group captures the wounded Scar after his battle with Kimblee's chimeras, Winry — despite her trauma — bandages Scar's wounds, stating her parents would have done the same and that saving someone does not mean forgiving them. To free herself from being used against the Elrics and to help her friends escape military surveillance, she conspires to pretend to be Scar's hostage, allowing the group to flee. She is later smuggled via Paninya and others back toward Resembool.

The Promised Day and the Epilogue - Tears of Joy

Source Manga Ch. 95-108; Brotherhood Ep. 61-64Culture Amestrian / mangaPeriod 2009-2010 publication; in-universe 1915-1918

Summary

During the Promised Day, Winry is in Resembool when Father activates the Nationwide Transmutation Circle, which briefly kills many including Winry (her last word is 'Ed'). She is restored when Edward, Alphonse, and their allies defeat Father and reverse the transmutation. In the aftermath, she remains in Resembool, running the shop with Pinako. In the final chapter's epilogue (several years later, c. 1918), a photograph shows Edward and Winry married with two children — a son and daughter — and Edward still wearing his automail arm maintained by Winry. Edward is now taller than her, a quiet visual payoff. Winry's final narrative role is not as a fighter but as the home that endures after war, the craft that continues, and the promise that Edward's tears of joy have been fulfilled.

Murder of Urey and Sara Rockbell by Scar during Ishvalan War

Source Manga Ch. 54-55; Brotherhood Ep. 24-25; Fullmetal Alchemist Chronicle interview on Ishval as allegory for war crimes

Description

Winry's parents, serving as neutral battlefield doctors, were killed by Scar in Ishval after Amestrian forces killed Scar's brother. Scar, in grief and rage, murdered the two doctors who were treating Ishvalans. Winry was a young child at the time and was orphaned. The series presents this as a war crime within a genocidal campaign (the Ishvalan War of Extermination), not as a justified act. Scar later expresses remorse and is confronted by Winry.

Scholarly Context

Critics and scholars (e.g., analyses of Fullmetal Alchemist's Ishval arc as reflection of real-world ethnic cleansing and Japanese war memory) note Arakawa's deliberate framing of the Rockbells' neutral medical ethic versus the military's extermination orders. The murder is not sanitized; Winry's trauma and Scar's accountability are both preserved.

Winry's attempted revenge - gun confrontation with Scar

Source Manga Ch. 55; Brotherhood Ep. 25

Description

At age 15, upon learning Scar killed her parents, Winry seizes a firearm and aims it at the wounded Scar, trembling and unable to pull the trigger. Edward physically stops her, stating her hands are for saving lives. The scene depicts a minor handling a gun under extreme emotional distress and the moral intervention of a peer. No one is shot.

Scholarly Context

Widely cited as the series' ethical center: Arakawa explicitly rejects revenge killing and affirms the Rockbell medical ethic. The scene is used in media-studies discussions of shōnen heroines who are given moral agency without being forced to kill.

State hostage-taking and coercion of a minor

Source Manga Ch. 63-64; Brotherhood Ep. 41

After discovering Bradley is a Homunculus, the Amestrian military (via King Bradley and later Kimblee) uses Winry (age 15-16) and Riza Hawkeye as hostages to control Edward and Roy Mustang. Warning: Wartime medical neutrality and its violation — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Wartime medical neutrality and its violation

Source Manga Ch. 54-55, 65; Brotherhood Ep. 24, 42

Description

Urey and Sara Rockbell's decision to treat Ishvalan wounded alongside Amestrian soldiers violated military expectations and led to their being unprotected in a war zone. Their ethic — treat all wounded regardless of side — is presented as heroic but also as tragically vulnerable within a genocidal war. Winry later reenacts this ethic by treating Scar.

Scholarly Context

Parallels real-world principles of medical neutrality (Geneva Conventions) and their frequent violation in ethnic conflicts. Arakawa, whose family has medical/agricultural background, has spoken about labor and care ethics in interviews.

Mass death during Father's Nationwide Transmutation Circle (Promised Day)

Source Manga Ch. 104-107; Brotherhood Ep. 61-63

Description

Winry is among those whose souls are briefly taken when Father activates the circle using Amestris itself as a transmutation circle, killing thousands including Winry (shown collapsing, whispering 'Ed'). She is resurrected when the transmutation is reversed. The event depicts mass, indiscriminate death of civilians including minors.

Scholarly Context

The Promised Day is analyzed as an allegory for state-sponsored human sacrifice and the dehumanization inherent in alchemical/military hubris. Winry's death is brief and non-graphic, emphasizing vulnerability rather than spectacle.

Contradictions & Disputes

Is Winry and Edward's romance canon?

Position A

Manga/Brotherhood canon: romance is explicit and concludes with marriage and children (Ch. 108 epilogue photograph, Arakawa's commentary). Edward's promise about 'tears of joy' is fulfilled.

Position A — evidence

Manga Ch. 55 (Winry realizes love), Ch. 64 (Edward taller), Ch. 108 (wedding photo with two children), Brotherhood Ep. 64 final montage, Arakawa's epilogue notes in Vol. 27.

Position B

2003 anime canon: romance is only hinted, never confirmed; no marriage or children are shown; Conqueror of Shamballa leaves Edward in another world, making the pairing impossible in that continuity.

Position B — evidence

2003 anime Ep. 51 and film Conqueror of Shamballa (2005) — Edward remains in 1923 Germany, Winry remains in Amestris; no epilogue marriage. Official guidebooks distinguish continuities.

Scholarly Consensus

Scholarly and official consensus: both are true within their continuities; manga/Brotherhood is primary canon per Arakawa. Fandom confusion arises from conflating continuities; reputable sources now label them separately.

Who was Winry's automail master in Rush Valley?

Position A

Manga/Brotherhood: Dominic refuses to take her, refers her to Garfiel, who becomes her direct master and teaches her advanced techniques.

Position A — evidence

Manga Ch. 11-12 dialogue: Dominic says 'I don't take apprentices... I'm scared of Pinako' and introduces Garfiel; Brotherhood Ep. 12 follows this.

Position B

2003 anime: Dominic directly mentors Winry for an extended period in Rush Valley, with Garfiel's role minimized or absent.

Position B — evidence

2003 anime Ep. 9-10 — Winry stays with Dominic's family longer and learns directly from him; Garfiel does not appear as her master.

Scholarly Consensus

Manga/Brotherhood version is primary; 2003 anime diverged due to needing original material while manga was ongoing. Guidebooks list Garfiel as her master in canon.

Did Winry forgive Scar?

Position A

Popular fan summary: Winry forgives Scar and they become allies/friends.

Position A — evidence

Fan wikis and casual summaries often use 'forgives' shorthand.

Position B

Textual evidence: Winry explicitly says saving Scar does not mean forgiving him; she treats him because her parents would have, but does not absolve him.

Position B — evidence

Manga Ch. 65: Winry says 'I... I don't forgive you... but... my parents...' and later 'To save someone doesn't mean you forgive them.' Brotherhood Ep. 42 subtitles match. Arakawa's script emphasizes distinction.

Scholarly Consensus

Position B is textually accurate; scholarship on Fullmetal Alchemist's ethics emphasizes this nuance as intentional — mercy without absolution, breaking the revenge cycle without erasing accountability.

Winry's age and professional status

Position A

Winry is 15 during main story, 16 by Promised Day, 18+ in epilogue, and is a prodigy but still an apprentice under Pinako/Garfiel.

Position A — evidence

Profiles guidebook: Age 15 (first appearance), 16 (later), Blood type O, Height 158cm; manga shows her as student/apprentice, not licensed doctor.

Position B

Some early English promotional material and fanon describe her as a fully licensed adult engineer/doctor in her teens.

Position B — evidence

Misreading of her delivering a baby at 15 as proof of medical license; confusion with Pinako's credentials.

Scholarly Consensus

Position A is official; Winry's feat is presented as extraordinary precisely because she is not yet a licensed professional, highlighting prodigy status and the emergency context.
