Who It Is

When Edward Elric shatters his automail arm in battle, he does not call a doctor or an alchemist — he calls Winry Rockbell. At fifteen, wrench in hand and grease on her cheek, she is the only person in Amestris who can rebuild a boy out of steel and keep him human enough to keep fighting. Winry never transmutes, never fights with alchemy, and never wants to — her power is quieter and rarer: to mend what war and ambition break, even when the person who broke it is the boy she loves.

Canonically kind, optimistic, sincere, and emotionally direct, but also famously hot-tempered and physically assertive — she regularly throws wrenches at Edward when he breaks his automail through recklessness. Arakawa describes her as the 'buffer' who allows readers to understand the Elrics' pain because she can voice what they cannot. She is empathetic and outspoken about the emotional cost of violence, telling Edward her hands are for building and saving, not killing. Powers
Automail Engineering - Design, Fabrication and Fitting

Master automail mechanic, described in-universe as a prodigy. Can design, forge, assemble, and surgically fit fully functional prosthetic limbs integrated with nerves and musculature, including Edward's highly advanced arm and leg. Capable of custom cold-weather models for Briggs and high-performance combat models. Skills include metallurgy, nerve-interface calibration, and maintenance under field conditions.

Limit: Requires workshop tools, materials, and time; cannot perform automail surgery alone without a surgeon (Pinako handles surgical implantation, Winry assists and later performs independently as she matures). Automail requires regular maintenance and is vulnerable to damage, cold, and water. No alchemical ability to create automail instantly.

Mechanical Diagnostics and Field Repair

Can diagnose automail faults by sound, feel, and visual inspection, and perform emergency repairs with limited tools. Notable feat: identifying a missing screw she herself forgot after three sleepless nights (Ch. 11), and later rapid field replacement of Edward's arm in the North (Ch. 64) under Kimblee's surveillance.

Limit: Fatigue impairs precision (the missing screw incident). Field repairs are temporary without proper calibration.

Emergency Medicine and Midwifery

Trained informally by Pinako (a renowned automail surgeon) and by her late parents (both doctors). Successfully delivered Dominic's grandchild (a breech/baby in distress) at age 15 in Rush Valley, saving mother and infant when no doctor was available. Provides first aid, wound bandaging, and triage, including treating Scar's severe wounds despite personal trauma.

Limit: Not a licensed physician; cannot perform major surgery alone and defers to Pinako for automail implantation surgery. Her medical knowledge is practical and inherited, not formally credentialed.

Emotional Resilience and Moral Influence (Narrative Ability)

No supernatural power, but functions as the series' moral anchor. Her refusal to kill Scar and her decision to treat him embodies the Rockbell medical ethic ('save lives regardless of who they are'). Her presence directly alters Edward's decisions — he pins her gun down rather than letting her become a killer, and her words ('your hands are for saving lives') recur as his ethical restraint. This is treated as a character power within the narrative structure.

Limit: Cannot prevent violence or change military structures alone; her influence is interpersonal, not systemic. She is physically vulnerable and is used as a hostage precisely because she is non-combatant.

Apprenticeship Learning and Technical Adaptation

Rapid learner who absorbs techniques from Dominic, Garfiel, and Atelier engineers in Rush Valley. Learns to work with new alloys and to design automail for different climates and fighting styles. By the epilogue she is running the Rockbell shop independently alongside Pinako and later with her own family.

Limit: Initially refused by Dominic due to his fear of Pinako and his no-apprentice rule; requires sponsorship to enter the trade as a young woman in a male-dominated field.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Winry is depicted as a teenage girl, initially 15 and 16 by the series' end (born c. 1899 in the series' pseudo-early-20th-century timeline). She has long, light blond hair typically tied in a high ponytail with bangs, blue eyes (light in manga, vivid blue in anime), and a light complexion. Her work attire is iconic: a dark tube top or tank top under denim overalls/work coveralls, often with a bandana, gloves, and a tool belt, with grease smudges

Symbols — Symbols associated with Winry are mechanical rather than mystical: crossed wrenches, automail schematics, the Rockbell family dog Rush Valley souvenir earrings (small hoop/stud), and most importantly, the screwdriver and wrench as extensions of care. Domains — Primary habitat is Resembool, a rural town in the east of Amestris, in the Rockbell family house-workshop run with her grandmother Pinako. Secondary domain is Rush Valley, the automail engineering capital, where she apprentices under Garfiel (manga/Brotherhood) after being refused by Dominic. Accounts I
The Rockbell Legacy and the Orphaning in IshvalFullmetal Alchemist manga Ch. 9, 54-55; Fullmetal Alchemist Chronicle; Brotherhood Ep. 10, 24

Manga publication 2001-2010; in-universe c. 1901-1908 (Ishvalan War)

Winry is born in Resembool to renowned doctors Urey and Sara Rockbell, who run a clinic alongside automail surgeon Pinako. During the Ishvalan War of Extermination, her parents volunteer to treat the wounded on both sides without discrimination in Ishval. They are murdered by Scar, an Ishvalan monk whose brother's arm was taken for his own automail, in a moment of vengeful grief after Amestrian soldiers kill his brother. Winry, still a young child, is raised thereafter by her grandmother Pinako. The trauma is initially hidden from her; she learns the truth as a teenager when she confronts Scar. Her parents' ethic — to save anyone regardless of affiliation — becomes her own moral inheritance. Establishes Winry's core motivation and moral code; explains her medical instinct and her later capacity to forgive without forgetting; drives her confrontation with Scar and her decision to treat him; grounds the series' critique of war and military medicine.

Building Edward's Automail - The Price of Human TransmutationManga Ch. 1, 9, 23; Brotherhood Ep. 3, 10; 2003 anime Ep. 3

2001-2003 publication; in-universe c. 1910-1911

After Edward and Alphonse attempt to resurrect their mother Trisha via human transmutation, Edward loses his left leg and right arm (the latter to bind Al's soul to armor) and Al loses his entire body. Pinako and Winry, their childhood neighbors and surrogate family after Trisha's death, rescue and rebuild Edward, surgically installing automail limbs that Winry helps design and maintain despite being only ~11 at initial installation. When Scar destroys Edward's arm in Central, the brothers return to Resembool and Winry, now 15, forges a new arm in days, working without sleep. Her pride is wounded when she forgets a single screw due to exhaustion, causing the arm to fail in a later fight — a humbling lesson in craft and care. Defines Winry's role as Edward's literal maker and keeper; establishes the physical cost of alchemy and the non-alchemical labor that sustains it; begins the recurring motif of Edward breaking what Winry builds through recklessness, and her wrench-throwing discipline.

Rush Valley - The Automail Capital and the BirthManga Ch. 10-12; Brotherhood Ep. 11-12; 2003 anime Ep. 9-10

2003-2004 publication; in-universe 1914

En route to meet Izumi Curtis, Winry insists on detouring to Rush Valley, the mecca of automail engineers. There she befriends Paninya, a young pickpocket with automail legs who steals to survive after being crippled and abandoned. Winry convinces her to earn money honestly and becomes her friend. She also meets Dominic, a gruff master engineer, and his pregnant daughter-in-law. When the daughter-in-law goes into premature labor with no doctor available, Winry — at 15, with only Pinako's informal training — successfully delivers the baby, saving both lives. Dominic, impressed but bound by his refusal to take apprentices and his fear of Pinako (with whom he shares a contentious past), refers her to Garfiel, who takes her as an apprentice. She remains in Rush Valley to study while the Elrics continue their journey. Marks Winry's transition from prodigy to professional; demonstrates her medical skill and compassion independent of the Elrics; establishes her own found family and career path; Paninya becomes a recurring ally who later helps smuggle Winry.

Accounts II
The Gun at Scar - Inheritance vs. RevengeManga Ch. 54-55; Brotherhood Ep. 24-25; 2003 anime Ep. 33

2006-2007 publication; in-universe 1914

Reunited with the Elrics in Central, Winry learns that Maes Hughes has been murdered, deepening her grief. When the Elrics track Scar for information about the Philosopher's Stone, Winry follows to help and discovers Scar is her parents' killer. In a warehouse confrontation, she seizes Edward's gun and points it at the wounded Scar, trembling with rage and grief. Edward physically intervenes, pinning the gun down and telling her that her hands are meant to save lives, not take them — echoing her parents' philosophy. Scar escapes with Alphonse in pursuit. Edward later tells her that the next time he makes her cry, they will be tears of joy, and Winry realizes she has been in love with him since childhood. Central ethical climax for Winry; she chooses not to kill, preserving her identity as a healer; cements her romantic realization; sets up her later decision to treat Scar's wounds, proving forgiveness is not forgetting but continuing to save.

Hostage in the North - Cold-Weather Automail and the Choice to Save ScarManga Ch. 63-65, 71-72; Brotherhood Ep. 41-42, 45

2007-2008 publication; in-universe 1914-1915

After Edward and Alphonse discover that King Bradley is the Homunculus Wrath, the military uses Winry and Riza Hawkeye as hostages to ensure obedience. Winry is sent north to Briggs under escort by Solf J. Kimblee, ostensibly to service Edward's automail which is failing in the extreme cold. She replaces his arm with a northern model. Edward confesses he is being coerced to capture Scar. When the group captures the wounded Scar after his battle with Kimblee's chimeras, Winry — despite her trauma — bandages Scar's wounds, stating her parents would have done the same and that saving someone does not mean forgiving them. To free herself from being used against the Elrics and to help her friends escape military surveillance, she conspires to pretend to be Scar's hostage, allowing the group to flee. She is later smuggled via Paninya and others back toward Resembool. The Promised Day and the Epilogue - Tears of JoyManga Ch. 95-108; Brotherhood Ep. 61-64

2009-2010 publication; in-universe 1915-1918

During the Promised Day, Winry is in Resembool when Father activates the Nationwide Transmutation Circle, which briefly kills many including Winry (her last word is 'Ed'). She is restored when Edward, Alphonse, and their allies defeat Father and reverse the transmutation. In the aftermath, she remains in Resembool, running the shop with Pinako. In the final chapter's epilogue (several years later, c. 1918), a photograph shows Edward and Winry married with two children — a son and daughter — and Edward still wearing his automail arm maintained by Winry. Edward is now taller than her, a quiet visual payoff. Winry's final narrative role is not as a fighter but as the home that endures after war, the craft that continues, and the promise that Edward's tears of joy have been fulfilled. Provides canonical romantic closure rare in shōnen; confirms Winry's future as both engineer and mother, continuing the Rockbell line; thematically resolves the series' tension between alchemical ambition and humble, sustaining labor.

The Hughes Household Night and Central City InterludeManga Ch. 12-13; Brotherhood Ep. 13; 2003 anime Ep. 10

2003 publication; in-universe 1914

After fixing Edward's arm in Central, Winry spends the night at the Hughes household, experiencing the warm domesticity of Maes, Gracia, and baby Elicia. This brief, tender interlude contrasts sharply with the later news of Hughes's murder, which devastates Winry and the Elrics. It also shows her integration into the Elrics' extended chosen family beyond Resembool. Deepens the impact of Hughes's death on Winry; establishes her as part of the series' network of civilian homes that the military's conspiracy violates.

Winry's age and professional statusWinry is 15 during main story, 16 by Promised Day, 18+ in epilogue, and is a prodigy but still an apprentice under Pinako/Garfiel.Some early English promotional material and fanon describe her as a fully licensed adult engineer/doctor in her teens.

Winry is an alchemist or uses alchemy to make automail. — Winry has no alchemical ability whatsoever and never performs transmutation. Automail is purely mechanical engineering and surgery, deliberately contrasted with alchemy. This is central to her thematic role as non-magical craft.

Winry is just a love interest / damsel in distress who waits at home. — While she is Edward's love interest, her narrative function is far broader: master engineer, field medic, moral arbiter, and active resistance participant (hostage ruse, treating Scar, smuggling). She has her own career arc in Rush Valley independent of the Elrics and is praised by critics for avoiding damsel tropes.

Winry's parents were automail engineers like Pinako. — Urey and Sara Rockbell were doctors/surgeons, not automail engineers. Pinako is the automail surgeon/engineer. The Rockbell family combines both medical and mechanical traditions, but Winry inherits engineering from Pinako and medical ethics from her parents.

Winry and Edward were already a couple for most of the series. — Their romantic relationship is slow-burn and largely unspoken until late in the series. Winry realizes her love in Ch. 55, Edward is oblivious/flustered until Ch. 64-85, and they do not become a couple until the epilogue. Much of the series they are bickering childhood friends, not dating.

Winry appears and fights in the final battle against Father. — Winry does not fight in the Promised Day battle; she is in Resembool and is briefly killed by Father's nationwide circle, then restored. Her role in the climax is as the home front and the reason Edward fights to return, not as a combatant. She provides no combat support in Central.
