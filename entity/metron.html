The Approach

Perched atop his Mobius Chair, a being of pure intellect observes the multiverse as a living equation. He is Metron, the New God of knowledge, a cosmic scholar who trades in secrets and seeks the ultimate truth of existence. Neither hero nor villain, his allegiance lies only to the pursuit of understanding, making him one of the most enigmatic and powerful figures in the DC Universe.

Content advisory. Contains themes of cosmic-scale conflict, manipulation, and moral ambiguity. Some storylines involve dark revelations about the nature of the universe and the New Gods.

Etymology

The name 'Metron' is derived from the Greek word 'μέτρον' (metron), meaning 'measure' or 'standard'. This reflects his role as one who measures and quantifies the universe, seeking to understand its fundamental laws and secrets.

Cultural Ownership

Owned by DC Comics, a subsidiary of Warner Bros. Discovery. Created by Jack Kirby, first appearing in New Gods #1 (1971).

Disputed Identifications

Metron is sometimes confused with other cosmic beings like the Watchers (Marvel) or the Time Lords (Doctor Who), but he is a distinct character with a unique role in DC's cosmology. Within DC, he is sometimes conflated with the Source or the Presence, but he is a separate entity who seeks to understand them.

Description

Appearance

Metron is a tall, slender humanoid with blue skin and a bald head. He often wears a high-tech suit and a distinctive helmet with a visor that covers his eyes. He is almost always depicted seated on his Mobius Chair, a floating, high-tech throne that hovers in the air. His design is sleek and futuristic, reflecting his association with advanced technology and cosmic knowledge.

Symbols & Iconography

The Mobius Chair is his primary symbol, representing his mastery over time and space. His helmet and visor symbolize his role as an observer and seeker of truth. The Alpha and Omega symbols are sometimes associated with him, representing his quest to understand the beginning and end of all things.

Behaviour & Personality

Metron is coldly logical, detached, and intensely curious. He is driven by an insatiable thirst for knowledge and understanding, often prioritizing his quest for truth over emotional connections or moral considerations. He is not malevolent, but he can be manipulative and amoral, using others as pawns in his investigations. He rarely shows emotion and speaks in a calm, measured tone. He is, however, not entirely without a sense of justice, occasionally aiding heroes if it serves his purposes.

Domains & Habitats

Metron resides in the realm of New Genesis, but he is rarely there. He spends most of his time traveling the multiverse in his Mobius Chair, observing events and gathering knowledge. He is also known to visit Apokolips and other cosmic locales. He is particularly interested in the Source, the cosmic energy that powers the New Gods.

Version Differences

In the original Jack Kirby comics, Metron is a New God of New Genesis who seeks knowledge for its own sake. In later interpretations, particularly in the 2000s, he is portrayed as more morally ambiguous, sometimes aiding villains to gain information. In The New 52, an alternate version of Metron appears on Earth 2, with a different origin and role. In the DC Rebirth era, Metron's history is somewhat streamlined, but his core characterization remains consistent.

Contradictions & Disputes

Metron's morality

Position A

Metron is a neutral observer who values knowledge above all, often aiding heroes when it suits him.

Position A — evidence

In many stories, he helps the heroes without expecting anything in return.

Position B

Metron is amoral and manipulative, willing to sacrifice anyone for his quest.

Position B — evidence

In Cosmic Odyssey, he knowingly sends heroes to their deaths.

Scholarly Consensus

Metron is morally ambiguous, but generally not evil. His actions are driven by curiosity, not malice.

Metron's origin

Position A

Metron was born on New Genesis and is a true New God of New Genesis.

Position A — evidence

Some sources state he is a New God of New Genesis.

Position B

Metron was born on Apokolips and later defected to New Genesis.

Position B — evidence

In New Gods #7, it is implied he was born on Apokolips.

Scholarly Consensus

The Apokolips origin is more widely accepted, but it is not definitively stated in all continuities.

Mobius Chair's capabilities

Position A

The Mobius Chair can answer any question in the multiverse.

Position A — evidence

In some stories, it is said to hold all knowledge.

Position B

The Mobius Chair has limitations and cannot answer certain questions.

Position B — evidence

In others, it is shown to be fallible or restricted by the Source.

Scholarly Consensus

The chair is extremely powerful but not omniscient.
