Root systems connect trees across entire continents. They communicate. They share resources. And now — they are learning. They are learning about us.

Who It Is

Deep Root Disease is presented in Gemini Home Entertainment as a devastating ecological phenomenon, documented through a series of tapes titled 'The Deep Root Disease' that are styled as educational films about forest pathology. It is described as a disease affecting trees, but it is clearly much more than that. The disease causes trees to grow at impossible rates, their roots spreading and intertwining underground into vast, living networks. The trees become sensitive — reacting to sound, light, and proximity. In some cases, they move. People who enter forests affected by Deep Root Disease report feeling watched, hearing breathing from underground, and seeing roots that pulse like veins. The disease can jump to animals and humans, causing organic tissue to begin transforming into wood-like growth. Animals infected with Deep Root Disease become aggressive, their bodies sprouting bark and branches. Humans who contract it become part of the forest, their consciousness absorbed into the root network, where they remain aware but powerless. The disease is explicitly connected to the Iris — it is the planet-sized entity's method of terraforming Earth, converting the biosphere into something compatible with its own biology. Animated segments in the series illustrate the root systems spreading like veins through cross-sections of the continental United States. The horror of Deep Root Disease is ecological and slow — this is not a sudden apocalypse but a gradual, silent takeover, one forest at a time. By the time anyone notices, half the continent's trees may already be part of a single, thinking organism. The series masterfully uses the format of nature documentaries to present this horror, with calm, professional narration describing the end of the natural world as we know it.
