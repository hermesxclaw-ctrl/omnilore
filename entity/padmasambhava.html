Who It Is

Padmasambhava ('The Lotus-Born'), universally revered in Tibet as Guru Rinpoche ('Precious Master'), is the tantric master credited with establishing Buddhism in Tibet during the 8th century. According to traditional Tibetan hagiography, he was miraculously born as an eight-year-old child from a lotus blossom on Lake Danakosha in the kingdom of Uddiyana (identified with the Swat Valley in modern Pakistan). He was adopted by King Indrabodhi and raised as a prince, but his extraordinary nature manifested early. After accidentally causing a death, he was exiled and became a wandering ascetic, studying under countless masters throughout India, Nepal, and Central Asia, mastering all tantric traditions, and performing countless miracles. The Tibetan king Trisong Detsen, seeking to establish Buddhism in Tibet, invited the Indian abbot Shantarakshita to teach. When construction of Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet, was repeatedly disrupted by local spirits and demons, Shantarakshita recommended summoning Padmasambhava. Guru Rinpoche arrived in Tibet, subdued the hostile spirits and bound them by oath as protectors of the dharma, transforming them from obstacles into guardians. He then oversaw the completion of Samye, ordained the first Tibetan monks, translated numerous tantric texts, and concealed thousands of terma (hidden treasure teachings) throughout Tibet, to be discovered by future tertons (treasure revealers) at appropriate times. He also taught his primary consort Yeshe Tsogyal and 25 principal disciples. Before departing Tibet, he promised to return and protect his followers, and he is believed to currently reside at the Copper-Colored Mountain (Zangdok Palri), a pure land accessible to devoted practitioners. Padmasambhava is often considered the 'Second Buddha' by the Nyingma school, the oldest tradition of Tibetan Buddhism, and his mantra ('Om Ah Hum Vajra Guru Padma Siddhi Hum') is among the most recited in the Tibetan world. His iconography is distinctive: he holds a vajra, a skull-cup, and a khatvanga (trident staff), wearing elaborate robes and his signature lotus hat.
