Who It Is

Ajitanātha is the 2nd Tīrthaṅkara of Jainism, the second Ford-Maker of the current descending time cycle (avasarpinī). He was born to King Jitaśatru and Queen Vijayā (or Vijayādevī) in Ayodhya. His name means 'The Invincible' or 'The Unconquered One,' reflecting the fact that he could not be defeated by any enemy — internal or external. His emblem is the elephant, symbolizing strength, wisdom, and royal power. Before his renunciation, he was a king who ruled with justice and compassion. When he observed the transience of worldly pleasures, he renounced his kingdom and became a monk. After intense ascetic practice, he attained kevala (omniscience) under a mango tree. He preached the Jain path, established the fourfold order of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen, and guided countless souls to liberation. Ajitanātha attained nirvana (final liberation) on Mount Sammeta, where 20 of the 24 Tīrthaṅkaras achieved their final emancipation. His yakshini is Rohiṇī, and his yaksha is Mahāyaksha (also associated with him). Along with Rishabhanatha and Vāsupūjya, he is one of the Tīrthaṅkaras with particular importance in the Digambara tradition.

Origins

Ajitanātha was born in Ayodhya to King Jitaśatru. He ruled as a king before renouncing. After attaining omniscience under a mango tree, he preached the Jain path and attained nirvana on Mount Sammeta.
