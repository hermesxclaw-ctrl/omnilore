Who It Is

In the mist-wrapped hills of ancient Ireland, where the boundary between the mortal and the Otherworld thins, there lived a woman named Sadhbh—a name that whispers of sweetness and wisdom. Her story, though scant in surviving texts, weaves through the tapestry of Celtic legend as a figure of enchantment, transformation, and poignant loss, leaving scholars to piece together her identity from fragments of myth and folklore.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Sadhbh (pronounced 'sive' or 'sighv') is derived from Old Irish, meaning 'sweet' or 'goodly'. It is related to the word 'sáith' meaning 'satiety' or 'fullness', and is often interpreted as 'sweetness' or 'goodness'.

Pronunciation — The most common pronunciation is 'sive' (rhyming with 'dive'), though regional variations include 'sighv' and 'sowv'.

Aliases — Sadhb, Sive, Saoirse (variant)

Ownership — Irish and Celtic mythology. The name is associated with the Tuatha Dé Danann, the supernatural race in Irish legend.

Accounts I
The Birth of OisínFenian Cycle

Medieval

Sadhbh, a woman of the Tuatha Dé Danann, is transformed into a deer by a jealous druid. She is hunted by Fionn mac Cumhaill, who spares her. When she returns to human form, she becomes Fionn's wife and gives birth to Oisín. She is later turned back into a deer and disappears. Oisín grows up to be a great poet and warrior. Sadhbh's disappearance is a tragic loss for Fionn.
