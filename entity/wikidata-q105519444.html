The Approach

In the mist-wrapped hills of ancient Ireland, where the boundary between the mortal and the Otherworld thins, there lived a woman named Sadhbh—a name that whispers of sweetness and wisdom. Her story, though scant in surviving texts, weaves through the tapestry of Celtic legend as a figure of enchantment, transformation, and poignant loss, leaving scholars to piece together her identity from fragments of myth and folklore.

Content advisory. This page contains references to transformation, enchantment, and potential themes of coercion or loss common in Celtic mythology. Reader discretion is advised for younger audiences.

Etymology

The name Sadhbh (pronounced 'sive' or 'sighv') is derived from Old Irish, meaning 'sweet' or 'goodly'. It is related to the word 'sáith' meaning 'satiety' or 'fullness', and is often interpreted as 'sweetness' or 'goodness'.

Cultural Ownership

Irish and Celtic mythology. The name is associated with the Tuatha Dé Danann, the supernatural race in Irish legend.

Disputed Identifications

There is no clear consensus on which Sadhbh this entry refers to. Several figures in Irish mythology bear this name, including the mother of Oisín, a woman transformed into a deer, and other minor characters. The metadata provides no distinguishing details, so the identification is uncertain.

Stories & Myths

The Birth of Oisín

Source Fenian CycleCulture IrishPeriod Medieval

Summary

Sadhbh, a woman of the Tuatha Dé Danann, is transformed into a deer by a jealous druid. She is hunted by Fionn mac Cumhaill, who spares her. When she returns to human form, she becomes Fionn's wife and gives birth to Oisín. She is later turned back into a deer and disappears.

Participants

Sadhbh

Fionn mac Cumhaill

Oisín

The druid (Fear Doirche)

Consequences

Oisín grows up to be a great poet and warrior. Sadhbh's disappearance is a tragic loss for Fionn.

Variants

Some versions have Sadhbh as a mortal woman, others as a fairy. The transformation may be permanent or temporary.
