Who It Is
Form

Moloch is often depicted as a bronze statue with the body of a lion or a man, and a head that resembles a lion's head. The statue is said to have a large mouth and a furnace or oven within its body, where children were allegedly sacrificed. The appearance of Moloch varies across different cultures and artistic representations.

Powers

Child Sacrifice Moloch is said to demand the sacrifice of children in exchange for power and fertility. The entity is often associated with the practice of child sacrifice, where children are boiled alive in the furnace within the bronze statue.

Accounts

The story of Moloch and the children of Israel is a well-known narrative in the Hebrew Bible. According to the story, the children of Israel were instructed by God to sacrifice their children to Moloch, but they refused, leading to a period of famine and suffering.

The story of Moloch and the Phoenicians is a lesser-known narrative in the writings of Philo of Alexandria. According to the story, the Phoenicians worshipped Moloch as a deity, and offered sacrifices to the entity in exchange for power and fertility.
