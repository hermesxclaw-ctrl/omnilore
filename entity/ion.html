Who It Is

In the vast cosmic tapestry of the DC Universe, where will itself becomes a tangible force, there exists a being known as Ion—not merely a character, but a living embodiment of the very essence of willpower. This entity is not a single hero or villain, but a sentient manifestation of the emotional spectrum's green light, a primordial force that has chosen champions across the ages to wield its incredible power. To understand Ion is to understand the fundamental nature of will itself, a concept that has been explored, corrupted, and reborn through countless hosts and cosmic events.

Ion is a sentient entity that embodies the concept of willpower. It is typically portrayed as calm, wise, and resolute, but it can also be assertive and forceful when necessary. It chooses hosts who demonstrate exceptional willpower and often acts as a guide or mentor to them. However, it has also been corrupted or controlled by villains, leading to more aggressive or destructive behavior.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Ion' is derived from the Greek word 'ἰόν' (ion), meaning 'going' or 'the going one.' In the context of DC Comics, it symbolizes the active, forward-moving nature of willpower, aligning with the Green Lantern Corps' philosophy of overcoming fear through determination.

Pronunciation — EYE-on

Aliases — Ion

Ownership — DC Comics (Warner Bros. Discovery). Created by Ron Marz and Darryl Banks, first appearing in Green Lantern (vol. 3) #48 (1994).

Powers
Willpower Manifestation

Ion grants its host immense willpower, allowing them to create hard-light constructs limited only by their imagination and will. The host can create weapons, shields, and complex structures.

Limit: The constructs are limited by the host's willpower; if the host's will wavers, the constructs can fail. The host is also vulnerable to the yellow light of fear, as it is the opposite of willpower.

Energy Projection

Ion can project powerful beams of green energy that can be used for offense or defense. The energy can be shaped into various forms.

Limit: The power output is dependent on the host's willpower and emotional state.

Flight

Ion grants the ability to fly at high speeds, often faster than light.

Limit: None noted.

Enhanced Physical Attributes

The host gains superhuman strength, durability, and reflexes, allowing them to withstand extreme conditions and engage in physical combat.

Limit: The enhancements are not as high as some other cosmic entities, but they are significant.

Cosmic Awareness

Ion grants a degree of cosmic awareness, allowing the host to perceive events across the universe and sense disturbances in the emotional spectrum.

Limit: The awareness is not absolute and can be blocked or distorted.

Possession and Bonding

Ion can bond with a sentient being, granting them its powers. The bonding is usually voluntary but can be forced.

Limit: The host must have a strong will to maintain the bond; otherwise, they can be overwhelmed or corrupted.

Power Transfer

Ion can transfer its power to another being, either temporarily or permanently.

Limit: The transfer may weaken Ion or leave it dormant for a period.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ion typically manifests as a large, humanoid figure composed of green energy, often with a glowing, translucent appearance. It can also take the form of a green flame or a sphere of light. When bonded with a host, the host gains a green aura and a costume that incorporates green and white elements. The entity's appearance can vary depending on the artist and the era.

Symbols — Ion is associated with the Green Lantern Corps symbol, which is a stylized lantern. The entity itself is often depicted with a lantern-like emblem on its chest or as a pure embodiment of green light. The color green represents willpower in the emotional spectrum.

Domains — Ion resides in the Green Lantern Central Power Battery on Oa, but it can travel across the universe. It is intrinsically linked to the emotional spectrum, specifically the green light of willpower. It can exist in the physical realm when bonded with a host or manifest independently.

Accounts I
Emergence of IonGreen Lantern (vol. 3) #48-50 (1994)

1990s

During the 'Emerald Twilight' storyline, Hal Jordan goes mad and destroys the Green Lantern Corps, absorbing the power of the Central Power Battery. However, the entity Ion is revealed to have survived, and it later bonds with Kyle Rayner, making him the last Green Lantern. Ion chooses Kyle as its host, granting him immense power to become the new protector of the universe. Kyle Rayner becomes the sole Green Lantern, and Ion becomes a major force in the DC Universe. This sets the stage for Kyle's role as a cosmic hero.

The Final NightThe Final Night #1-4 (1996)

1990s

When the Sun-Eater threatens to consume Earth's sun, Kyle Rayner, empowered by Ion, plays a crucial role in the plan to reignite it. Ion's power is essential in the final effort, showcasing the entity's significance in cosmic events. The Sun-Eater is defeated, and Earth is saved. Hal Jordan sacrifices himself to reignite the sun, setting up his later resurrection.

Ion: The Guardian of the UniverseIon: The Guardian of the Universe #1-12 (2003-2004)

2000s

Kyle Rayner embarks on a solo series as Ion, exploring his role as a guardian of the universe. He faces various cosmic threats and learns more about the nature of Ion. The series delves into the responsibilities and challenges of wielding such immense power. Kyle grows as a hero and gains a deeper understanding of the emotional spectrum. The series establishes Ion as a key element in the Green Lantern mythos.

Accounts II
The Sinestro Corps WarGreen Lantern (vol. 4) #21-23, 25 (2007)

2000s

During the Sinestro Corps War, Ion is temporarily bonded to Sodam Yat, a Daxamite, making him one of the most powerful beings in the universe. Sodam Yat uses Ion's power to fight the Sinestro Corps, but the entity is later extracted by the Guardians. Sodam Yat becomes a major player in the war, and Ion's power is shown to be crucial in the battle against fear. However, Ion is eventually removed from Sodam Yat, leaving him without the entity's power.

Green Lantern: The Animated Series - The LossGreen Lantern: The Animated Series (2012)

2010s

In the animated series, Ion is depicted as a separate entity that is freed from the Central Power Battery. It briefly bonds with Hal Jordan, granting him immense power, but it is later released. The episode explores the nature of Ion as a living will. Ion's power is used to defeat a powerful enemy, but the entity is ultimately freed, emphasizing that it cannot be controlled.

The New 52 and RebirthGreen Lantern (vol. 5) #20 (2013), Green Lanterns (vol. 1) #1- (2016)

2010s-2020s

In The New 52, Ion is reimagined as a more primal force, and its role is somewhat diminished. In DC Rebirth, the emotional spectrum entities are reintroduced, and Ion is depicted as a powerful entity that is sought after by various factions. The nature of Ion is explored in the context of the broader emotional spectrum, and it remains a key element in the Green Lantern mythos.
