Who It Is

Ion is the living embodiment of willpower — the entity that represents the green light of the Emotional Spectrum and the collective will of all sentient beings in the DC Universe. It is the entity that dwells within the Central Power Battery on Oa, the source of energy for the Green Lantern Corps. As the manifestation of willpower, Ion possesses immense cosmic abilities: it can manipulate reality on a universal scale, create matter and energy from nothing, teleport across the cosmos, resurrect the dead, and enforce its will upon the fabric of existence. Ion has bonded with several hosts throughout DC history, most notably Kyle Rayner, who became Ion after absorbing the power of all remaining Green Lantern rings and the Central Power Battery itself. As Ion, Kyle Rayner achieved near-omnipotent status, re-creating the Guardians of the Universe and the Green Lantern Corps after they were destroyed. The entity later bonded briefly with Sodam Yat, a Daxamite Green Lantern, creating one of the most powerful beings in the universe. Ion represents the purest expression of the Green Lantern ethos: that willpower — the simple, stubborn refusal to give up — is one of the most powerful forces in existence. Unlike the other Emotional Entities that embody passion or fear, Ion is the entity of quiet, steady determination. It is the light that refuses to go out, no matter how dark the universe becomes.

The Approach

In the vast cosmic tapestry of the DC Universe, where will itself becomes a tangible force, there exists a being known as Ion—not merely a character, but a living embodiment of the very essence of willpower. This entity is not a single hero or villain, but a sentient manifestation of the emotional spectrum's green light, a primordial force that has chosen champions across the ages to wield its incredible power. To understand Ion is to understand the fundamental nature of will itself, a concept that has been explored, corrupted, and reborn through countless hosts and cosmic events.

Content advisory. Contains themes of cosmic power, mental possession, corruption, and existential struggle. Some storylines involve psychological manipulation and the burden of immense responsibility. No explicit graphic violence or sexual content.
Identity

Ion

Pronunciation EYE-on

Etymology

The name 'Ion' is derived from the Greek word 'ἰόν' (ion), meaning 'going' or 'the going one.' In the context of DC Comics, it symbolizes the active, forward-moving nature of willpower, aligning with the Green Lantern Corps' philosophy of overcoming fear through determination.

Cultural Ownership

DC Comics (Warner Bros. Discovery). Created by Ron Marz and Darryl Banks, first appearing in Green Lantern (vol. 3) #48 (1994).

Disputed Identifications

Ion is sometimes confused with the Green Lantern Corps' central power battery or the entity that empowers the Green Lanterns. However, Ion is a distinct sentient entity, one of the seven emotional spectrum entities. There is also a character named Ion in Greek mythology, but they are unrelated.

Description

Appearance

Ion typically manifests as a large, humanoid figure composed of green energy, often with a glowing, translucent appearance. It can also take the form of a green flame or a sphere of light. When bonded with a host, the host gains a green aura and a costume that incorporates green and white elements. The entity's appearance can vary depending on the artist and the era.

Symbols & Iconography

Ion is associated with the Green Lantern Corps symbol, which is a stylized lantern. The entity itself is often depicted with a lantern-like emblem on its chest or as a pure embodiment of green light. The color green represents willpower in the emotional spectrum.

Behaviour & Personality

Ion is a sentient entity that embodies the concept of willpower. It is typically portrayed as calm, wise, and resolute, but it can also be assertive and forceful when necessary. It chooses hosts who demonstrate exceptional willpower and often acts as a guide or mentor to them. However, it has also been corrupted or controlled by villains, leading to more aggressive or destructive behavior.

Domains & Habitats

Ion resides in the Green Lantern Central Power Battery on Oa, but it can travel across the universe. It is intrinsically linked to the emotional spectrum, specifically the green light of willpower. It can exist in the physical realm when bonded with a host or manifest independently.

Version Differences

In the pre-New 52 continuity, Ion was a distinct entity that bonded with hosts like Kyle Rayner and Sodam Yat. In The New 52 (2011-2016), Ion was reimagined as a more primal force, and its role was somewhat altered. In the DC Rebirth era, the emotional spectrum entities were reintroduced with some changes. The animated series 'Green Lantern: The Animated Series' also depicted Ion as a separate entity, but with a slightly different design and personality.

Ion grants its host immense willpower, allowing them to create hard-light constructs limited only by their imagination and will. The host can create weapons, shields, and complex structures.

The constructs are limited by the host's willpower; if the host's will wavers, the constructs can fail. The host is also vulnerable to the yellow light of fear, as it is the opposite of willpower.

In some stories, Ion's power is nearly limitless, while in others it is shown to be finite and can be overwhelmed by other emotional spectrum entities.

Emergence of Ion

Source Green Lantern (vol. 3) #48-50 (1994)Culture DC ComicsPeriod 1990s

Summary

During the 'Emerald Twilight' storyline, Hal Jordan goes mad and destroys the Green Lantern Corps, absorbing the power of the Central Power Battery. However, the entity Ion is revealed to have survived, and it later bonds with Kyle Rayner, making him the last Green Lantern. Ion chooses Kyle as its host, granting him immense power to become the new protector of the universe.

Participants

Kyle Rayner

Consequences

Kyle Rayner becomes the sole Green Lantern, and Ion becomes a major force in the DC Universe. This sets the stage for Kyle's role as a cosmic hero.

Variants

The story was later retconned in 'Green Lantern: Rebirth' (2004-2005), where it was revealed that Parallax, not Hal Jordan, was responsible for the destruction of the Corps, and Ion was the entity that had been trapped within the Central Power Battery.

Contradictions & Disputes

Origin of Ion

Position A

Ion was originally a separate entity that existed before the Green Lantern Corps, and was trapped within the Central Power Battery.

Position A — evidence

In 'Green Lantern: Rebirth', it is revealed that Ion was discovered by the Guardians and used to power the Central Power Battery.

Position B

Ion was created by the Guardians of the Universe as a tool to power the Green Lantern Corps.

Position B — evidence

In earlier stories, Ion was depicted as a unique entity that chose Kyle Rayner as its host, suggesting it was not created by the Guardians.

Scholarly Consensus

The current consensus is that Ion is an ancient entity that predates the Green Lantern Corps, and the Guardians harnessed its power. However, the exact nature of its creation is still ambiguous.

Ion's Power Level

Position A

Ion is one of the most powerful entities in the DC Universe, capable of feats like reigniting a star.

Position A — evidence

In 'The Final Night', Ion's power is essential to reigniting the Sun.

Position B

Ion's power is limited and can be overwhelmed by other emotional spectrum entities.

Position B — evidence

In 'The Sinestro Corps War', Ion is defeated by Parallax, suggesting it is not omnipotent.

Scholarly Consensus

Ion is extremely powerful, but not limitless. Its power is tied to the strength of the host's willpower.

Ion's Relationship with Kyle Rayner

Position A

Ion is a separate entity that can leave Kyle Rayner at any time.

Position A — evidence

In 'Green Lantern: Rebirth', Ion is separated from Kyle and later bonded to Sodam Yat.

Position B

Ion is permanently bonded to Kyle Rayner, making him a permanent host.

Position B — evidence

In 'Ion: The Guardian of the Universe', Kyle is referred to as 'Ion' and the entity seems to be a part of him.

Scholarly Consensus

Ion can be transferred between hosts, but Kyle Rayner has a special connection to it. The bond is not permanent, but it is significant.
