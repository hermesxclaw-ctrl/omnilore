Þorbjörg lítilvölva

Thorbjorg the Little Völva, The Seeress, Wearer of the Blue Cloak

Bring me the woman who knows the varðlokur. The spirits will not come unless the song is sung, and the song must be sung by one who remembers. I will sit on the high seat and tell you when this hunger ends — but first, the song.

Who It Is

Þorbjörg lítilvölva (Thorbjorg the Little Völva) provides the most detailed description of a seiðr ritual (Norse seeress-magic) in the entire saga corpus. She appears in Eiríks saga rauða when the Greenland settlement is suffering from famine, and the chieftain Þorkell invites her to perform a seiðr (prophecy séance) to determine when the hard times will end. Her description is ethnographic in its detail: she wears a blue cloak fastened with gemstones, a black lambskin hood lined with white catskin, calfskin shoes with long laces ending in brass buttons, and gloves of catskin with the fur inward. She carries a brass-bound staff (seiðstafr) and a pouch containing her ritual tools. She is given a high seat (seiðhjallr) on a platform, and a special meal of the hearts of all available animals. At night, the women of the household form a circle around her and sing the varðlokur (warding songs / spirit-summoning songs), which call the spirits to her. Only one young Christian woman, Guðríðr Þorbjarnardóttir, knows the songs (learned from her foster-mother in Iceland), and she sings them reluctantly (being Christian). Þorbjörg then enters trance. Her prophecy reveals that the famine will end in the spring, that Guðríðr will have a remarkable destiny (she later becomes the first European woman to give birth in North America and ends her life as a nun in Rome), and other specific details that come true. This passage is invaluable for understanding the material culture, gender roles, and syncretic tension (Christian/pagan) of late Viking Age seiðr practice. Þorbjörg is treated with awe and fear: she is given the best of everything, and the community organizes entirely around her ritual at a time of crisis.
