Who It Is

Mithras is a fascinating and complex deity, with a rich mythology and symbolism. This dossier provides an in-depth analysis of the deity's role in the world, exploring the cultural and historical context of the mythology.

Mithras is often depicted as a benevolent and just deity, who brings light and justice to the world. He is also associated with the concept of oaths and contracts, emphasizing the importance of keeping one's promises.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Mithras is often depicted as a beardless, muscular man with a radiant aura, wearing a Phrygian cap and a cloak. He is sometimes shown with a sword or a torch, symbolizing his role as a bringer of light and justice.

Symbols — The bull, often depicted as a symbol of Mithras, represents the struggle between good and evil. The Phrygian cap, a symbol of Mithras, represents his connection to the Persian deity Mithra. The torch, often carried by Mithras, represents his role as a bringer of light and justice.

Domains — Mithras is often associated with the underworld, where he is said to have killed the bull. He is also associated with the heavens, where he is said to have ascended to the realm of the gods.

Accounts I
The TauroctonyMithraism - Wikipedia

Roman Empire

The Tauroctony is a central myth in Roman Mithraism, depicting Mithras as a young man who kills a bull in a cave. The myth is often seen as a symbol of the struggle between good and evil, with Mithras representing the forces of good and the bull representing the forces of evil. The killing of the bull leads to the creation of the world and the establishment of the cycles of nature.

The Banquet SceneMithraism - Wikipedia

The Banquet Scene is a common depiction in Mithraic art, showing Mithras and Sol Invictus banqueting on the hide of the slaughtered bull. The scene is often seen as a symbol of the union between Mithras and the sun god. The banquet scene represents the union between Mithras and the sun god, emphasizing the importance of light and justice in the world.
