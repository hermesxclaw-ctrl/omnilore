Mithras (the Lord of the Tauroctony)

also known asthe Lord of the Tauroctony
Typegod (central deity of Roman Mithraism)
Who It Is
Form

Mithras is often depicted as a beardless, muscular man with a radiant aura, wearing a Phrygian cap and a cloak. He is sometimes shown with a sword or a torch, symbolizing his role as a bringer of light and justice.

Powers

Bringer of Light Mithras is often associated with the concept of light, which he brings to the world through his role as a bringer of justice and truth.

Judge of the Dead Mithras is often depicted as a judge of the dead, who weighs the souls of the departed and determines their fate in the afterlife.

Protector of Oaths Mithras is often associated with the concept of oaths and contracts, emphasizing the importance of keeping one's promises and honoring one's commitments.

Accounts

The Tauroctony is a central myth in Roman Mithraism, depicting Mithras as a young man who kills a bull in a cave. The myth is often seen as a symbol of the struggle between good and evil, with Mithras representing the forces of good and the bull representing the forces of evil.

The Banquet Scene is a common depiction in Mithraic art, showing Mithras and Sol Invictus banqueting on the hide of the slaughtered bull. The scene is often seen as a symbol of the union between Mithras and the sun god.

Scholarly Debates
etymology
The etymology of Mithras is unclear, and may be related to the Proto-Indo-European root *mei- 'to measure' or 'to contract'. ⇌ Some scholars argue that the etymology of Mithras is more closely related to the Persian deity Mithra, and may be a syncretic creation of the Roman Empire.
cultural ownership
Mithras is often seen as a Roman deity, created by the Roman Empire as a syncretic figure. ⇌ Some scholars argue that Mithras has Persian origins, and may have been brought to the Roman Empire through cultural exchange and syncretism.
What We Don't Know

There is a lack of understanding about the etymology and cultural ownership of Mithras, with some scholars arguing for a Persian origin and others seeing him as a Roman creation.
