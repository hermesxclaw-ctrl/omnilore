Who It Is

The Nixe (masculine: Nix) is a water-dwelling spirit of German folklore, often compared to the Greek naiad or Slavic rusalka. Female nixies appear as extraordinarily beautiful maidens — sometimes fully human above the waist and fish-tailed below, mirroring the mermaid — with long golden or green hair they comb while singing on riverbanks. Their music is hypnotically beautiful and can lure men to watery deaths, but nixies are not uniformly malevolent; many stories tell of nixies marrying human men, attending village dances, and warning communities of floods. However, their otherworldly nature always asserts itself — a nixie wife will inevitably return to the water if her true nature is revealed or if she is insulted. The male Nix is more consistently dangerous, sometimes appearing as a handsome young man to drown maidens, or as an old, green-toothed creature demanding human sacrifice to calm the waters. Nixies appear in the Nibelungenlied: the Rhine maidens who guard the cursed gold warn Hagen of the treasure's doom. Richard Wagner drew heavily on Nixe lore for his Rhine Maidens in the Ring Cycle, blending the Germanic water spirits with operatic drama. The Nixe represents the ancient Germanic reverence for — and terror of — water as a life-giving and death-dealing force, the liminal zone between the human world and the otherworld beneath the surface.
