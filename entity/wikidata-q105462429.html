The Approach

A boy who was never meant to be a leader, Kozuki Momonosuke was torn from his mother's arms at the age of eight and flung twenty years into the future, landing in a world where his father is a legend and his family's name is a death sentence. Yet from the moment he emerges from a dragon's egg in the body of a child, Momonosuke carries the weight of a nation's hope—and a destiny that will test whether a frightened boy can become the shogun his people need.

Content advisory. Contains themes of parental death, political oppression, child endangerment, and psychological trauma. Depictions of violence and coercion are present in the source material. No explicit sexual content.

Kozuki Momonosuke

Etymology

The name 'Kozuki' (光月) means 'light moon' in Japanese, fitting the family's connection to the moon and the Land of Wano. 'Momonosuke' (桃之助) combines 'momo' (peach) and 'nosuke' (a common male given name suffix), possibly alluding to the Momotaro legend, a Japanese folk hero born from a peach, which parallels Momonosuke's role as a heroic child destined to lead.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Eiichiro Oda for the manga One Piece, published by Shueisha in Weekly Shonen Jump. The character is part of a globally recognized franchise, but his cultural roots are distinctly Japanese, drawing on Japanese history, folklore, and the samurai genre.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans initially speculated that Momonosuke might be the son of Kaido due to his ability to transform into a dragon, a power associated with Kaido. However, the manga confirms that Momonosuke's dragon form is due to a Devil Fruit (the Artificial Zoan fruit, the Hebi Hebi no Mi, Model: Dragon) created by Vegapunk, not due to a biological lineage. No other disputed identifications are known.

Description

Appearance

Momonosuke appears as a small child of about 8 years old (physically), with long, flowing black hair that reaches his waist, often tied in a topknot. He has large, expressive eyes and typically wears a purple kimono with a green obi, accompanied by a small sword at his side. In his dragon form, he is a large, serpentine dragon with blue scales, white underbelly, and four legs, similar to Kaido's dragon form but smaller. He also has a brief adult form after consuming a copy of the Maturity Fruit (Juku Juku no Mi), appearing as a tall, strong young man with the same facial features but older.

Symbols & Iconography

Momonosuke is associated with the Kozuki clan's crest, which features a crescent moon with a stylized 'M' shape. He also carries his father's sword, Enma, though he does not use it effectively. His dragon form symbolizes his connection to the Kozuki lineage and the mythical power of the dragon, echoing the legend of the Kozuki clan's ability to hear the voice of all things.

Behaviour & Personality

Initially, Momonosuke is presented as a whiny, cowardly child who is prone to crying and relies on others for protection. He is deeply traumatized by the death of his parents and the upheaval of his life. However, as the Wano arc progresses, he shows remarkable courage and determination, especially when he decides to face Kaido and protect his people. He is fiercely loyal to his retainers and the Straw Hat crew, and he matures significantly, eventually accepting his role as shogun. He also has a playful side, often seen riding on the back of his dragon form or playing with the Straw Hat crew.

Domains & Habitats

Momonosuke's domain is the Land of Wano, specifically the Kuri region where his father ruled. He is also seen in the Flower Capital and Onigashima. He was initially raised in the isolated island of Zou, where he lived with the Mink tribe for several months after his time travel. His natural habitat is the samurai culture of Wano, with its castles, tea houses, and cherry blossom-covered landscapes.

Version Differences

In the manga, Momonosuke's dragon form is first shown in Chapter 967, but it is not clearly depicted until later. The anime slightly alters the pacing of his transformation scenes. In the anime, his adult form appears in Episode 1016, whereas in the manga it is Chapter 1024. There are no major differences in his characterization between the two. The Vivre Card reveals that his height is 110 cm (3'7") as a child, and 182 cm (6'0") as an adult, which is not explicitly stated in the manga.

Powers & Abilities

Artificial Zoan Devil Fruit (Hebi Hebi no Mi, Model: Dragon)

Source Version Manga Chapter 1047, where he first successfully transforms; also referenced in Chapter 999

Description

Momonosuke ate an artificial copy of Kaido's Devil Fruit, the Uo Uo no Mi, Model: Seiryu, created by Vegapunk. This fruit allows him to transform into a dragon, though the transformation is imperfect. He can partially transform, such as turning his hand into a claw, and fully transform into a blue dragon. In his dragon form, he can fly, breathe fire (though he has trouble controlling it initially), and has immense physical strength.

Limitations

The artificial fruit is inferior to the original, making his dragon form weaker than Kaido's. He initially cannot control his flames properly and has difficulty switching between forms. He also lacks the ability to use advanced Haki in this form.

Contradictions

Some fans speculated that he might be able to use Conqueror's Haki, but this is not confirmed. The anime shows him using a flame attack that he cannot control, while the manga implies he is learning to control it. No direct contradictions exist in canon.

Voice of All Things

Source Version Manga Chapter 1015, where he commands Zunesha

Description

Like his father Oden and the Roger Pirates, Momonosuke possesses the rare ability to hear the 'Voice of All Things,' allowing him to communicate with animals and perceive certain supernatural phenomena. He uses this to command Zunesha, the giant elephant, to attack the Beast Pirates.

Limitations

The ability is not fully understood; he cannot hear the voice of Sea Kings as clearly as Luffy, and he may not be able to use it at will.

Contradictions

It is unclear if this ability is hereditary or if it can be trained. The series does not explain its full scope.

Swordsmanship (basic)

Source Version Manga Chapter 955, where he tries to pick up Enma

Description

Momonosuke has basic training in swordsmanship, as shown when he attempts to wield his father's sword Enma. However, he is not skilled enough to use it effectively and often relies on others.

Limitations

He is too weak to control Enma, which drains the user's Haki.

Contradictions

None.

Maturity Fruit (Juku Juku no Mi) temporary effect

Source Version Anime filler episodes 744-746

Description

In a non-canonical filler arc (the 'Maturity' arc in the anime), Momonosuke eats a fruit that ages him into an adult temporarily. This is not part of the manga canon.

Limitations

The effect is temporary and not present in the manga.

Contradictions

This is a filler-only ability and is not considered canon.

Stories & Myths

The Fall of Oden and the Kozuki Clan

Source One Piece manga, Chapters 960-969 (flashback) and Chapter 920Culture Japanese mangaPeriod Fictional timeline: 20 years before the current story

Summary

Momonosuke is born as the son of Kozuki Oden, the daimyo of Kuri, and his wife Toki. When Oden is executed by Kaido and Orochi, Toki sends Momonosuke and four retainers (Kin'emon, Kanjuro, Raizo, and later Kiku) 20 years into the future to escape. Momonosuke witnesses his father's death and is separated from his mother, who stays behind and is killed. He arrives in the future alone, traumatized, and is found by the Mink tribe on Zou.

Consequences

Momonosuke becomes the heir to the Kozuki clan and the rightful shogun of Wano, but he is too young and weak to reclaim his position. His journey to gather allies and defeat Kaido becomes the central plot of the Wano arc.

Variants

The anime expands the flashback with additional scenes, but the core events are consistent.

The Journey to Wano

Source One Piece manga, Chapters 819-920Culture Japanese mangaPeriod Fictional timeline: present

Summary

Along the way, Momonosuke bonds with the crew, especially Luffy, and learns to trust others. He also encounters his father's former retainers and begins to understand his legacy.

The Raid on Onigashima

Source One Piece manga, Chapters 1000-1050Culture Japanese mangaPeriod Fictional timeline: present

Summary

Momonosuke participates in the final battle against Kaido. Initially, he is terrified and hides, but after seeing Luffy's determination, he finds the courage to fight. He transforms into a dragon and carries Luffy to the rooftop, where he faces Kaido. He uses his dragon form to bite Kaido and later commands Zunesha to attack. After Kaido's defeat, Momonosuke is declared the new shogun of Wano.

Witnessing father's execution

Source Manga Chapter 969

Description

Momonosuke, as a young child, witnesses his father Oden being boiled alive in a pot of oil by Kaido and Orochi. This traumatic event is depicted graphically and shapes his deep fear of Kaido.

Scholarly Context

The scene is drawn from historical Japanese punishments, but it is a fictional depiction. The trauma is a key motivator for Momonosuke's character development.

Forced separation from mother

Source Manga Chapter 920

Description

Toki sends Momonosuke into the future, knowing she will die. This is a form of child abandonment, albeit for survival. Momonosuke later learns of his mother's death, adding to his grief.

Scholarly Context

The theme of a parent sacrificing themselves for a child's future is common in literature, but the psychological impact on the child is explored realistically.

Child soldier in a war

Source Manga Chapters 1047-1050

Description

Momonosuke, despite being a child, participates in the raid on Onigashima, a violent battle with numerous casualties. He is placed in danger and even fights, which raises ethical questions about child soldiers.

Scholarly Context

The series does not explicitly address this, but it is a point of discussion among fans. The character's age is ambiguous due to time travel, but his physical age is that of a child.

Contradictions & Disputes

Momonosuke's age

Position A

He is physically 8 years old, but chronologically 28 years old due to time travel.

Position A — evidence

Manga Chapter 920 states he was sent 20 years into the future when he was 8.

Position B

Some fans argue that his mental age is closer to 8, as he did not experience the intervening years.

Position B — evidence

His behavior often reflects that of a child, but he also shows moments of maturity beyond his years.

Scholarly Consensus

The series treats him as a child, but the ambiguity is noted by fans. No official statement resolves this.

His ability to command Zunesha

Position A

Momonosuke can command Zunesha because he possesses the Voice of All Things.

Position A — evidence

Momonosuke explicitly says 'Zunesha, you're free!' and Zunesha responds, indicating a direct command.

Position B

Some speculate that Zunesha only obeyed because of Luffy's presence or because of a specific condition.

Position B — evidence

Zunesha's obedience is not fully explained; it may require the user to be the 'Joy Boy' or have a certain will.

Scholarly Consensus

The manga indicates that Momonosuke's command is valid, but the mechanics are left vague.

The nature of his Devil Fruit

Position A

His fruit is an artificial copy of Kaido's fruit, making it weaker.

Position A — evidence

Manga Chapter 999 states Vegapunk created it as a copy.

Position B

Some fans theorize that his fruit might be the original, and Kaido's is the copy, due to the color difference.

Position B — evidence

The blue color of Momonosuke's dragon is different from Kaido's blue, but this is likely due to the artificial nature.

Scholarly Consensus

The manga explicitly states it is a copy, so there is no real contradiction.
