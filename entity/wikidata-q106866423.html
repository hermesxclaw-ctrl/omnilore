The Approach

In the sprawling, tragic genealogy of Troy, most sons of King Priam are remembered only as names on a list—warriors who fell in a war that consumed an entire civilization. Lysithous is one such shadow, a Trojan prince whose entire existence rests on a single mention in a later mythological compendium, leaving us with more questions than answers about his life, his deeds, and his place in the epic cycle.

Identity

Lysithous

Pronunciation lī-SITH-oh-əs (English approximation); in Ancient Greek: Λυσίθοος (Lysíthoos).

Etymology

The name Lysithous (Λυσίθοος) is of Greek origin. It can be analyzed as λυσι- (from λύσις, 'loosing, releasing') and -θοος (from θοός, 'swift, quick'). Thus, it may mean 'swift to release' or 'releasing swiftly,' though such interpretations are speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically the Trojan cycle as transmitted through Hellenistic and Roman-era mythographic texts.

Disputed Identifications

No direct disputes are known, but the name is rare and could be confused with similar-sounding names. There is no evidence to identify him with any other character.

Contradictions & Disputes

Existence of Lysithous

Position A

Lysithous was a real figure in Greek mythology, as recorded by Apollodorus.

Position A — evidence

The manuscript tradition of the Bibliotheca includes the name.

Position B

Lysithous may be a textual corruption or a minor invention by Apollodorus or his source.

Position B — evidence

No other ancient source mentions him, suggesting he may be a minor or spurious addition.

Scholarly Consensus
