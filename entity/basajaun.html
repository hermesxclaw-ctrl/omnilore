Who It Is

The Basajaun (literally 'Lord of the Woods') is a hairy, powerfully-built wild man of Basque mythology who inhabits the deepest forests of the Pyrenees. Unlike the predatory wild man of other European traditions, the Basajaun is a protector and teacher of humanity. Standing well over two meters tall, covered in long hair, and possessing immense strength, he is the guardian of the forest and its animals. Basque legends credit Basajaun with teaching humanity the fundamental arts of civilization: agriculture (he showed humans how to sow wheat and harvest it), metallurgy (the secret of forging iron), and milling (how to construct water mills). He is also the protector of flocks — his distant whistle or cry warns shepherds of approaching storms or wolves. If a shepherd falls asleep, Basajaun may guard the sheep until he wakes. However, the Basajaun has a terrifying counterpart: the Basandere (Lady of the Woods), his female companion who is more dangerous and unpredictable. The Basajaun legend survived the Christianization of the Basque country remarkably intact, a testament to its deep roots. Ethnographers debate whether the Basajaun represents a cultural memory of Neanderthal-human contact (the Basque region was one of the last refuges of Neanderthals) or a more archetypal 'wild man' figure. The Basajaun festivals (with costumed 'wild men' running through villages) continue in towns like Ituren and Zubieta.
