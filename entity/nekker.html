Who It Is

De Nekker (also spelled Nikker, from Germanic *nikwus) is a malevolent water spirit from Flemish and Dutch folklore, closely related to the German Nix and the Scandinavian Näck. The Nekker is described as a monstrous, humanoid creature with a dark, slimy body, long stringy hair like waterweed, webbed hands and feet, and eyes that glow green or red beneath the water's surface. He lurks in ponds, canals, slow-moving rivers, and drainage ditches throughout the Low Countries. The Nekker's primary method of attack is drowning: he reaches up from the water with long, bony arms to grab the ankles of children playing near the water's edge, fishermen leaning too far over their boats, or anyone who falls into his domain. Once he has a victim, he drags them down to his underwater lair, where their souls are trapped forever. In some accounts, the Nekker masquerades as a floating log, a patch of reeds, or a water lily to lure victims close. He is particularly active at dusk and during the winter months, when the boundary between the water and the land seems less certain. Parents across Flanders traditionally warn children: 'Pas op voor de Nekker' (Watch out for the Nekker) to keep them away from dangerous waters. The Nekker can be repelled by iron, by calling upon St. Nicholas, or by striking the water three times with a wooden stick. In Christianized versions, the Nekker is a fallen angel condemned to live in water until Judgment Day. The name Nekker lives on in the Dutch word 'nikker,' which became a racial slur in the colonial period, as well as in the word 'nix' (nothing) in the sense of 'the Nekker takes everything.'
