It is not a wolf. It is something else. It has a stripe of black fur running down its back, and it is larger than any wolf the hunters have ever seen. It has killed sixty souls. The King himself has taken notice.

Who It Is

The Beast of Gevaudan is one of the most thoroughly documented cryptid cases in history -- a man-eating creature that terrorized the rural province of Gevaudan in south-central France between 1764 and 1767, killing an estimated 60 to over 100 people, mostly women and children. The beast was described by survivors as a large, wolf-like animal the size of a calf or donkey, with reddish-brown fur, a long tail, a broad chest, a large head with a pronounced snout, and distinctive black stripes along its back. Its attacks were notable for their precision -- the beast typically decapitated its victims or tore out their throats, often evading armed pursuers with apparent intelligence. The attacks became a national crisis, drawing the attention of King Louis XV, who dispatched professional wolf hunters, dragoons, and enormous sums of money to kill the creature. Several large wolves were killed and displayed as the beast, but the attacks continued after each killing. The final beast was supposedly shot by a local hunter named Jean Chastel in June 1767 using a silver bullet -- a detail that may have been added later and that influenced modern werewolf folklore. The true identity of the Beast remains debated: theories include an unusually large wolf or wolf-dog hybrid, a striped hyena escaped from a menagerie (striped hyenas match the description of the black dorsal stripes and long forelegs), a lion or young lion, a human serial killer using a trained animal, or a combination of multiple predators during a period of wolf population resurgence. The historical documentation -- including parish records, official reports, engraving illustrations, and newspaper coverage -- makes the Beast of Gevaudan one of the most credible and terrifying 'monster' episodes in European history.
