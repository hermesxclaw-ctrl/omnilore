Name & Tongue

Etymology — The etymology of the name 'Kon' is unclear, but it may be derived from the Quechua word 'kunka', meaning 'to blow' or 'to blow wind'.

Pronunciation — The pronunciation of 'Kon' is unknown.

Aliases — Kón, Con, The Boneless God, The Wingless Flyer

Ownership — Kon is a deity from Pre-Incan mythology, specifically from the Paracas Civilization, and was later adopted into Nazca and Incan mythology.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kon is often depicted as a wingless being, sometimes with a human-like body and other times as a more abstract, wind-like entity.

Symbols — Kon is associated with the wind and rain, and is often depicted with symbols such as the windmill, the anemone, or the raindrop.

Domains — Kon is said to inhabit the skies and the winds, and is often associated with the mountains and the deserts.

The Story of Kon and the WindWikipedia

Ancient

According to legend, Kon was a powerful deity who controlled the winds and rain. He was said to have the power to summon storms and whirlwinds, and was often called upon by the people to bring fertility and prosperity to their lands. The story of Kon and the wind is a testament to the importance of the natural world in Pre-Incan mythology.
