Who It Is

Kon (also Con, Kón) is one of the more enigmatic Andean deities — a god of wind and rain uniquely described as having no bones, no joints, and the ability to fly without wings. His cult was primarily coastal, predating the Inca Empire and associated with the Paracas and Nazca cultures. According to the chronicler José de Acosta (1590), Kon was a creator god who descended from the north. He moved through the air with incredible speed, flattening mountains and carving valleys with nothing more than his word and his breath. He created the first race of humans and gave them abundant crops, but when they offended him, he punished them by taking away the rain, turning the fertile coast into the desert it largely remains today — a powerful mythological explanation for the extreme aridity of the Peruvian coastal strip. He then left them with only the rivers as a source of water. In some versions, Kon was eventually displaced by another god, Pachacamac (who is often conflated with Viracocha), who turned Kon's people into monkeys and created a new race. The description of Kon as a boneless, wingless flying being is one of the most unusual divine physiologies in world mythology. Some scholars have interpreted this as a very early, perhaps even pre-ceramic, conception of divinity — a being of pure will and movement, unconstrained by the limitations of physical form. Others see in Kon a mythological memory of devastating El Niño events that transformed the once-fertile coast into desert. Kon's cult was absorbed into the Inca religious system (the Inca were masters of incorporating conquered peoples' gods into their pantheon), but his distinctly coastal nature means he never achieved the pan-Andean status of Inti or Pachamama.
