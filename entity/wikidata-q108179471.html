Who It Is

On the third night of the lunar month the moon is too thin to light a road — a sharp, bright sickle hanging low. The Japanese call it mikazuki, the three-day moon, and for more than a thousand years they have given that name to whatever is beautiful, fleeting, and edged: a sword whose steel ripples like moonlight on water, a destroyer that vanished in the dark of the Solomons, a boy soldier who never learned to read but learned to kill from inside a Gundam.

Attested sword has no personality; folklore gives it none beyond beauty. Touken Ranbu's Mikazuki Munechika is characterized as grand, teasing, and otherworldly — an old sword who calls the master 'master' affectionately, laughs 'hahaha,' feigns senility to hide sharp perception, and speaks as a Heian aristocrat. He is the game's mascot and most popular character, coded as beautiful and untouchable. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese mikazuki from mi- (three) + ka (day) + tsuki (moon), contracted from mikka no tsuki. Attested in Heian literature (e.g., Kokin Wakashu, c.905) for the waxing crescent visible on the third day of the lunisolar calendar. The poetic term implies slenderness, transience, and elegance. Powers
Tenka-Goken Craftsmanship (Sword)

As one of the Five Swords Under Heaven, Mikazuki Munechika represents the peak of Heian tachi forging: exceptional sharpness, resilience, and aesthetic hamon. Historically attributed to Sanjo Munechika, founder of the Sanjo school.

Limit: No supernatural powers attested in historical records; sharpness is metallurgical, not magical. Cannot cut without wielder. Later folklore adds no magical feats unlike Dojigiri (demon-slaying).

Alaya-Vijnana System and Barbatos Piloting (Gundam)

Mikazuki Augus has Alaya-Vijnana (Arayashiki) spinal implants (three surgeries, unusually high compatibility) allowing direct neural link to Gundam Barbatos. Grants superhuman spatial awareness, reaction speed, and ability to control the mobile suit as limbs, including via pseudo-brain via Barbatos' systems.

Limit: System causes progressive nerve damage, loss of sensation and motor function in limbs (Mikazuki loses use of right arm, then legs). Requires surgery; high rejection risk. Overuse causes hemorrhaging and death. Barbatos itself requires maintenance and is outmatched by later Gundams without upgrades (Lupus, Lupus Rex).

Superhuman Close Combat (Gundam)

Even outside the Gundam, Mikazuki is depicted as an exceptional hand-to-hand fighter and marksman, able to defeat adult soldiers despite his youth, due to brutal upbringing as Human Debris.

Limit: Human durability; wounded by gunfire, requires medical care. Not superhuman in supernatural sense — skills are learned survival, not powers.

Tsukumogami Manifestation (Touken Ranbu)

As a tsukumogami, the sword's spirit can take human form, fight time-retrograde forces, and use sword techniques. In-game stats: high leadership, balanced offense, special ability to boost team.

Limit: Game mechanics only; no lore claim of historical magic. Power scales with player's leveling (tokudai, kiwame).

Mikazuki (Danzan-ryu Technique)

Danzan-ryu jujitsu technique Mikazuki (crescent) — a sweeping throw or leg reap, classified in the Yodan (fourth degree) list, applying crescent-shaped foot motion to off-balance uke.

Limit: Requires compliant or off-balanced opponent; not a 'power' but a trained skill. Effectiveness depends on practitioner skill.

The Fox Helper and the Forging of Mikazuki MunechikaOral legend recorded in Edo-period sword compendia (e.g., Kyōhō Meibutsu Chō, 1735; Hon'ami family records) and modern Tokyo National Museum guide

Legend set c. 987 (Eien era), recorded Edo period

Swordsmith Sanjo Munechika, commissioned by Emperor Ichijo to forge a tachi, despaired at his lack of skill. He prayed at Inari Shrine in Kyoto. The deity Inari, or a fox messenger (kitsune), appeared as a boy or assistant and helped him hammer the steel, enabling him to complete a blade of unparalleled beauty. The resulting hamon's crescents were said to be the fox's blessing. The sword was named Mikazuki for those moons. Established Mikazuki as archetypal 'helped by kami' sword; cemented Sanjo school prestige. The fox legend is retold in every museum label and Touken Ranbu characterization (Mikazuki's fox-like laugh and Inari association). No contemporary Heian document confirms the story.

Through the Shoguns: Mikazuki as Heirloom of PowerHon'ami Koetsu appraisal records; Tokugawa Jikki; Tokyo National Museum provenance

14th century–1868

Mikazuki Munechika passed as a prestige object among rulers. Owned by Ashikaga shoguns, then Toyotomi Hideyoshi (who prized it among his Three Treasures), then Tokugawa Hidetada (second Tokugawa shogun) who received it from Hideyoshi's son Hideyori. It remained in the Tokugawa family through the Edo period as a symbol of legitimacy, rarely used in battle, kept as a treasure sword (meibutsu). After the Meiji Restoration it entered the Imperial collection and was designated National Treasure. Preservation in pristine condition; never lost or destroyed unlike many swords. Provenance makes it one of the best-documented Japanese swords. Its status as Tenka-Goken was codified in this era.

Destroyer Mikazuki: From China Coast to the SolomonsIJN records; Senshi Sosho; CombinedFleet.com Tabular Record of Movement (Allyn Nevitt)

1927–1943 (Showa)

Mutsuki-class destroyer Mikazuki (ex-No.25) commissioned 1927. Served in Second Sino-Japanese War patrols off China, then WWII escort and transport duties. As an older destroyer, relegated to Tokyo Express runs in the Solomons. On 27–28 July 1943, while landing troops at Cape Gloucester, New Britain, she ran aground on a reef at night, was unable to refloat, and was bombed and destroyed by US Army B-25s the next day. Crew abandoned ship; hull later destroyed by US forces. One of 12 Mutsuki-class lost; example of attrition of Japan's older destroyers. Wreck remains off New Britain. Name not reused postwar for JMSDF.

2015 (broadcast), in-universe P.D. 323

On terraformed Mars, orphan child soldier Mikazuki Augus, raised as Human Debris by Chryse Guard Security (CGS), is the only one who can pilot the ancient Gundam Barbatos via Alaya-Vijnana. When CGS is attacked by Gjallarhorn and betrays its own orphans, Mikazuki and his brother-figure Orga Itsuka lead a mutiny, renaming the company Tekkadan (Iron-Blooded Orphans). Mikazuki becomes Tekkadan's unstoppable ace, protecting Martian princess Kudelia Aina Bernstein on her Earth voyage, killing Gjallarhorn ace Crank Zent and many others without hesitation. His bond with Orga is absolute: 'If Orga tells me to, I'll do anything.' Tekkadan rises from exploited orphans to major Martian power; Mikazuki's legend as Barbatos' devil spreads. Establishes themes of child exploitation and found family. Ends with Tekkadan reaching Earth but losing comrades Biscuit and others.

The Most Beautiful Sword Becomes a Boy: Touken Ranbu's Mikazuki MunechikaTouken Ranbu ONLINE game lines, Touken Ranbu: Hanamaru (2016) and Katsugeki (2017) anime, stage musical Touken Ranbu

2015–present

In 2015, Nitroplus anthropomorphized Mikazuki Munechika as a serene, moon-beautiful youth who is the game's de facto mascot. His in-game story presents him as the eldest and most treasured sword, aware of his National Treasure status, gently teasing the player (Saniwa) while hiding deep knowledge of history. In Hanamaru, he is the calm elder who guides younger swords; in Katsugeki, he is a powerful fighter who can single-handedly defeat time-retrograde armies. His popularity drove a real-world pilgrimage boom to Tokyo National Museum, where visitors queue to see the actual blade. Revived public interest in Japanese swords (touken boom); Mikazuki Munechika became most cosplayed and merchandised Touken Ranbu character; museum reported 30%+ increase in visitors to sword gallery after 2015. Demonstrates how fictional character layer revitalizes heritage object.

Mikazuki Munechika was used to slay demons or in famous duels like Dojigiri — Unlike Dojigiri Yasutsuna (Shuten-doji) or Onimaru (demon queller), Mikazuki has no demon-slaying legend; its fame is for beauty, not battle feats. It was a treasure sword, rarely if ever used in combat after Heian.

The destroyer Mikazuki was named after the sword Mikazuki Munechika — IJN destroyers were named after natural phenomena (wind, moon, tide). Mikazuki was named for the crescent moon itself, not the sword, per IJN naming convention (Mutsuki-class = moons/months).

Mikazuki Augus is named after the sword and therefore is its reincarnation — No in-universe connection; Sunrise named him for the moon motif (fragile crescent) to contrast with brutal life; any sword link is fan speculation. Official materials never mention the sword.

Ayaka/Sayuri songs 'Mikazuki' are about the sword or Gundam character — Both songs use the poetic word for crescent moon as metaphor for love/distance; neither references sword, ship, or anime (Sayuri's is tied to Rampo Kitan, not Gundam).
