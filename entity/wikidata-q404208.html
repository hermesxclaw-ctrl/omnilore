He is associated with the sea (due to his name's connection to the Aegean), with raw, untamed physical strength, and with the primordial forces of nature that predate the Olympian order. He is also connected to the depths — both the depths of the sea and the depths of Tartarus, where he serves as a jailer.

Form

Aegaeon/Briareos is depicted as a colossal being with one hundred arms and fifty heads. Each of his hundred hands is enormous, and his fifty heads are described as fierce and terrifying. He is often portrayed as a towering giant whose mere presence shakes the earth. In artistic representations, he is sometimes shown with a wild, unruly beard and a fearsome, monstrous appearance, befitting his role as a primordial force of nature.

Powers

Superhuman Strength As one of the Hecatoncheires, Aegaeon possesses strength so great that it surpasses even that of the Titans and the Olympian gods. Hesiod states that he is 'unconquerable' and that his strength is 'irresistible'. All versions His strength is brute force, not magical or strategic. He is a blunt instrument, not a cunning warrior. None significant; his strength is universally acknowledged.

Hundred Arms and Fifty Heads His most defining physical trait, granting him the ability to fight or act on multiple fronts simultaneously. In the Titanomachy, he is said to have used his hundred hands to hurl enormous boulders at the Titans. All versions The sheer size and number of limbs could be a liability in confined spaces, though this is never explored in the myths. None.

Guardianship of Tartarus After the Titanomachy, Zeus stationed Briareos (along with his brothers) to guard the defeated Titans within Tartarus. This implies a role of immense responsibility and trust, as well as the physical capability to contain the most powerful beings in the cosmos. Hesiod's Theogony This is a duty, not a power. He is bound to this role by Zeus's command. Homer does not mention this role, focusing instead on his defense of Zeus.

Behavior & Habitat

In the myths, Aegaeon/Briareos is a powerful but largely passive ally of Zeus. He is depicted as a loyal enforcer of divine order, deploying his immense strength only when called upon. His behavior is characterized by immense physical power combined with a somewhat simple, obedient nature — he does not scheme or plot, but acts as a blunt instrument of cosmic authority.

Accounts

Aegaeon/Briareos is born to Uranus (Sky) and Gaia (Earth), along with his brothers Cottus and Gyges. They are three of the most powerful beings ever born, but their father Uranus, disgusted by their monstrous forms, hates them and confines them within the depths of Gaia's womb (or in Tartarus).

During a crisis on Olympus, the gods Hera, Poseidon, and Athena plot to bind Zeus in chains. Thetis, the sea-nymph, brings the hundred-handed giant Briareos (whom the gods call Aegaeon) to Olympus. The mere sight of him is so terrifying that the rebellious gods abandon their plan immediately.

During the ten-year war between the Olympians and the Titans, Zeus frees the Hecatoncheires from their prison in Tartarus. Grateful, they fight on Zeus's side. Briareos and his brothers use their hundred hands to hurl massive rocks at the Titans, overwhelming them and securing victory for the Olympians.

After the war, Zeus appoints Briareos and his brothers as the permanent guards of the Titans within Tartarus. They sit beside the brazen gates, ensuring the Titans never escape.

What We Don't Know

A significant gap exists in the archaeological record. While Aegaeon/Briareos is well-attested in literature, there are no definitive, unambiguous artistic depictions of him from the Archaic or Classical periods of Greece. The Arcadian sanctuary mentioned by Pausanias has not been definitively identified. Further research into obscure scholia and Byzantine encyclopedias (like the Suda) could yield additional traditions. The full extent of his role in local cult practice remains unknown.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Aegaeon/Briareos as the mythological equivalent of a sealed vault door or a supermax prison warden. He is not a leader or a schemer; he is the ultimate, unbreakable security system. If the universe were a corporation, he would be the head of security who never sleeps, has a hundred hands to handle every threat at once, and is personally loyal to the CEO (Zeus). He is the answer to the question: 'What do you do when even the gods get out of line?'
