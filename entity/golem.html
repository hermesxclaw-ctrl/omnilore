Who It Is
Form

The golem is typically depicted as a large, imposing figure made of clay or mud. It is often shown as a humanoid creature with a rough, unfinished appearance, lacking the finer features of a human being. The golem's appearance is meant to convey its status as a created being, rather than a naturally occurring one. The golem's size and strength are often exaggerated, with some accounts describing it as a giant or a monster. The golem's appearance has been influenced by various cultural and artistic traditions, with depictions ranging from the grotesque to the sublime. The golem's appearance is also symbolic, representing the idea of creation and the power of the creator.

Powers

Superhuman strength The golem is often depicted as a creature of immense physical strength, capable of lifting and moving heavy objects. This power is reflective of its status as a created being, with the ability to perform tasks beyond the capabilities of human beings.

Accounts

The story of the golem of Prague tells the tale of Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel, who creates a golem to protect the Jewish community from persecution. The golem is brought to life through the use of sacred language and is tasked with defending the community from harm.

The story of the golem's revenge tells the tale of a golem that is created to serve as a protector of the Jewish community, but ultimately turns against its creators. The golem's actions are motivated by a desire for revenge against those who have wronged it.
