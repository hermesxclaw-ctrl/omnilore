The Approach

In the pantheon of ancient Egyptian deities, Ipy stands as a formidable yet nurturing figure—a hippopotamus goddess whose protective embrace and fierce maternal instincts guarded the thresholds of birth and the perilous journey into the afterlife. Though her name may echo in the acronyms of modern science, her ancient presence endures in the shadows of temples and the hearts of myth.

Identity

Ipy

Pronunciation ee-pee

Known As

IpetApetOpet

Etymology

The name Ipy (also spelled Ipet, Apet, or Opet) is of uncertain meaning, possibly related to the Egyptian word for 'nurse' or 'to be pregnant'.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Egyptian

Disputed Identifications

Ipy is sometimes conflated with the goddess Taweret, another hippopotamus deity. Some scholars suggest Ipy may be a local or variant form of Taweret, but they are generally treated as distinct.

Description

Appearance

Ipy is depicted as a hippopotamus, often standing upright, with the paws of a lion, the back and tail of a crocodile, and sometimes human breasts to emphasize her nurturing aspect. She may also appear as a hippopotamus-headed woman.

Symbols & Iconography

Ipy is associated with the hippopotamus, a creature of the Nile, and often carries the ankh (life) and sa (protection) symbols. She may be shown with a papyrus scepter, and her presence in birth scenes is common.

Behaviour & Personality

Ipy is a protective and nurturing deity, particularly associated with childbirth and the protection of women and children. She also guards the deceased, warding off evil spirits in the afterlife.

Domains & Habitats

Ipy is primarily associated with the Nile and its marshes, where hippopotami dwell. She is also linked to the underworld, where she assists in the rebirth of the sun and the deceased.

Version Differences

Ipy is sometimes conflated with Taweret, another hippopotamus goddess. Some texts may use the names interchangeably, but Ipy is often considered a distinct deity, possibly a local form or a specific aspect of Taweret. The exact relationship remains debated.

Powers & Abilities

Protection

Source Version Attested in Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, and Book of the Dead.

Description

Ipy is a protective deity, particularly for women during childbirth and for children. She is also invoked to protect the deceased in the afterlife, warding off malevolent forces.

Limitations

Her protection is typically invoked through ritual or amulets; she does not appear to have unlimited power over fate.

Contradictions

None recorded.

Nurturing

Source Version Attested in temple reliefs and texts.

Description

Ipy is associated with nurturing and breastfeeding, as indicated by her human breasts and her role in royal birth scenes. She is sometimes called 'Nurse of the King'.

Limitations

Her nurturing role is specific to royal and divine contexts, not universal.

Contradictions

Some scholars conflate her with Taweret, whose nurturing role is more prominent.

Assisting in Rebirth

Source Version Attested in funerary texts.

Description

Ipy helps in the rebirth of the sun god and the deceased, guiding them through the underworld.

Limitations

She is not the primary deity in this role; she assists other gods like Ra or Osiris.

Contradictions

None recorded.

Stories & Myths

Birth of the Royal Child

Source Temple reliefs at Deir el-BahriCulture Ancient EgyptianPeriod 18th Dynasty (c. 1479-1458 BCE)

Summary

In the mortuary temple of Hatshepsut, reliefs depict the divine birth of the queen. Ipy appears among the goddesses who assist in the birth, providing protection and nourishment to the newborn.

Participants

Ipy

Hatshepsut

Amun

other goddesses

Consequences

The scene legitimizes Hatshepsut's rule by showing her divine origin and the support of the gods.

Variants

Similar scenes appear in other royal birth cycles, such as at Luxor temple, though Ipy is not always named.

Protection of the Deceased

Source Book of the DeadCulture Ancient EgyptianPeriod New Kingdom onward (c. 1550 BCE - Roman period)

Summary

Spells in the Book of the Dead invoke Ipy to protect the deceased in the afterlife, ensuring their safe passage and rebirth. She is often depicted on funerary amulets and papyri.

Participants

Ipy

the deceased

Consequences

The deceased is assured of protection and a successful afterlife, free from harm.

Variants

Different versions of the spells may substitute Taweret or other hippopotamus goddesses.

Ipy is closely associated with Taweret, another hippopotamus goddess. They are sometimes conflated or considered aspects of the same deity, though they may have distinct origins and cult centers.

There is no consensus on their exact relationship.
