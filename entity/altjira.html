Who It Is

Altjira is the supreme sky deity of the Arrernte (Aranda) people of Central Australia, and his name is linguistically the root of the English word 'Alcheringa' (Dreamtime) — a term popularized globally through anthropological literature. Altjira is described as a being with emu feet (a detail that emphasizes his deep connection to the Central Australian landscape, where emus are a central totemic species) and skin of the sky. He lives in the sky and is the ultimate source of the Dreaming Law (Altyerr) that governs all Arrernte life. Unlike many creator figures who actively shape the landscape, Altjira is more remote — he did not travel the land creating features but instead established the moral and spiritual order from his celestial home. His sons, the eternal sky people, carry out his will. The Arrernte concept of Altjira is philosophically complex: his name is etymologically connected to 'Altyerre' (Dreaming) and 'Altjirarenha' (eternal, uncreated), suggesting a being who exists outside of creation, in a state of eternal, unoriginated being — a concept that resonates with sophisticated theological notions of the uncreated creator found in world philosophy. The missionary and ethnographer Carl Strehlow, who worked among the Arrernte in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, recorded extensive details about Altjira, and his son T.G.H. Strehlow became one of the foremost authorities on Arrernte culture and language. Altjira is not directly worshipped through prayer in the Christian sense; instead, his presence and law are maintained through the performance of ceremonies (corroboree), the observance of totemic obligations, and the correct care of sacred sites. The word 'Alcheringa' (originally 'Altyerrenge') — made famous through the works of anthropologists Baldwin Spencer and F.J. Gillen — entered world literature as a synonym for the Aboriginal concept of the Dreamtime, though its original Arrernte meaning is more specific: the time of Altjira, the eternal, the time that is always.
