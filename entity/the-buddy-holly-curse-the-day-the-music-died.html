The Buddy Holly Curse ('The Day the Music Died')

also known as'The Day the Music Died'
Typemusic_mythology
CultureFine Art
Who It Is
Form

The Buddy Holly Curse is a cultural phenomenon associated with the tragic plane crash that killed American rock and roll musicians Buddy Holly, Ritchie Valens, and "The Big Bopper" J. P. Richardson on February 3, 1959. The event is often referred to as "the day the music died." The curse is said to have originated before the plane crash and is believed to be a harbinger of doom for musicians.

Powers

Harbinger of Doom The Buddy Holly Curse is said to be a harbinger of doom for musicians, often signaling the end of a musician's career or life.

Accounts

On February 3, 1959, American rock and roll musicians Buddy Holly, Ritchie Valens, and "The Big Bopper" J. P. Richardson were killed in a plane crash near Clear Lake, Iowa, together with pilot Roger Peterson.

The Buddy Holly Curse is said to have originated before the plane crash and is believed to be a harbinger of doom for musicians.
