Phra Ram

The Divine King, The Virtuous Prince, Incarnation of Narai (Vishnu)

also known asPhra RamaRamaRamRamachandraPhra RamathibodiPhra Narai (as an avatar of Vishnu)
TypeDivine Hero / Avatar / King

Who It Is
Form

In Thai art, Phra Ram is typically depicted with a green face and body, wearing a golden crown (chada) and elaborate royal attire. He often holds a bow and arrow. His green complexion distinguishes him from other characters, and his regal bearing reflects his status as a divine king. In khon performances, his costume is ornate, with a specific mask that is green with a golden crown.

Powers

Divine Archery Phra Ram is an unmatched archer, capable of shooting arrows with incredible accuracy and power. He can even use arrows imbued with magical mantras to create powerful effects, such as summoning weapons or destroying enemies. Ramakien His arrows are sometimes countered by powerful demons or magical shields. In some versions, his archery is more emphasized; in others, his use of divine weapons is limited.

Superhuman Strength and Combat Skills As a divine avatar, Phra Ram possesses immense physical strength, enabling him to fight powerful demons and perform feats beyond human capability. Ramakien He can be overpowered by the most powerful demons, such as Thotsakan (Ravana), requiring assistance from Hanuman and his allies. Some versions depict him as invincible, while others show him struggling in battle.

Divine Knowledge and Wisdom Phra Ram is wise and knowledgeable in statecraft, dharma, and military strategy. He receives guidance from sages and gods. Ramakien His wisdom is sometimes clouded by emotions, leading to personal dilemmas. None significant.

Ability to Command and Inspire Phra Ram is a charismatic leader who inspires loyalty in his allies, including the monkey army led by Hanuman. He is able to unite diverse forces against evil. Ramakien He sometimes faces dissent or challenges to his authority, but generally maintains loyalty. In some versions, his leadership is questioned, but in others it is unquestioned.

Accounts

The god Phra Narai (Vishnu) incarnates as Phra Ram, son of King Thotsarot of Ayutthaya and Queen Kaosuriya. He is born to defeat the demon king Thotsakan, who has become invincible to gods and demons. Phra Ram and his brothers, Phra Lak, Phra Phrot, and Phra Satrut, are raised as princes. Phra Ram is trained in martial arts and archery by sages, and he wins the hand of Nang Sida by stringing the divine bow of Phra Isuan (Shiva).

Due to the machinations of Queen Kao Keesi, Phra Ram is exiled for 14 years, accompanied by Sida and his loyal brother Phra Lak. They live in the forest, encountering sages and demons. The demon king Thotsakan, attracted to Sida, uses a golden deer to lure Phra Ram away and then abducts Sida.

Phra Ram, with the help of Hanuman and the monkey army, builds a causeway to Lanka. After a long war with many battles, Phra Ram defeats Thotsakan and rescues Sida. He then questions her purity, and Sida proves her faithfulness through a trial by fire (or in some versions, by other means). They return to Ayutthaya, where Phra Ram is crowned king.

Content Notes

The Ramakien is filled with battles, deaths, and destruction. Phra Ram and his allies kill many demons, and the war against Lanka is brutal. The story does not shy away from the harsh realities of combat.

The violence is often symbolic of the struggle between good and evil, and is depicted in Thai art with stylized aesthetics.

Ramakien

Sida is abducted and held captive by Thotsakan. After her rescue, Phra Ram doubts her purity, leading to her trial by fire and eventual exile. This reflects patriarchal attitudes and the burden of female purity.

Scholars have analyzed this as a reflection of ancient and traditional gender norms, and some modern adaptations have reinterpreted Sida's role.

Scholarly Debates
Phra Ram's divinity
Phra Ram is an avatar of Vishnu and thus divine. ⇌ In the Ramakien, Phra Ram is more humanized, with more emphasis on his royal and human qualities.
Evidence for A: The Ramakien states that Phra Narai incarnates as Phra Ram.
Evidence for B: The Thai version often shows Phra Ram experiencing human emotions and dilemmas.
Sida's purity
Sida is pure and faithful. ⇌ There are doubts about her purity due to her time in Lanka.
Evidence for A: Sida proves her purity through trials.
Evidence for B: Phra Ram and the public still have doubts, leading to her exile.
Phra Ram's role in Sida's exile
Phra Ram exiled Sida due to public pressure. ⇌ Phra Ram exiled Sida because he himself doubted her.
Evidence for A: In some versions, he is forced by the people's gossip.
Evidence for B: In other versions, he acts on his own suspicions.

A way to hold it in your mind

In the golden light of Thai temple murals, a green-skinned prince draws his bow, his eyes fixed on a demon king across the sea. This is Phra Ram, Thailand's version of the Hindu hero Rama, a figure whose story has been retold for centuries in the Ramakien, the Thai national epic. More than a mere translation, Phra Ram embodies Thai ideals of kingship, righteousness, and cosmic order, his adventures resonating from royal courts to shadow-puppet theaters. But who is this divine hero, and how does his Thai incarnation differ from his Indian original?
