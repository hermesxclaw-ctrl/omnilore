Who It Is

Before there was light, there was an embrace so tight it smothered the world. The Sky Father and the Earth Mother lay locked together, their children trapped in the crushing dark between them. In the South Island of Aotearoa, the Māori tell of a being who helped tear them apart — not with brute force alone, but with the power of the heavens themselves. Her name was Amai-te-rangi, the one who brings forth the sky, and her story is a reminder that creation is rarely gentle. It is a violent, sorrowful, necessary act — and sometimes, it takes a mother of the heavens to do what must be done.

Amai-te-rangi is primarily a creative and nurturing figure, involved in the cosmic processes that shaped the world. She is not depicted as a warrior or a figure of vengeance, but rather as a being connected to the generative forces of the universe.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is composed of Māori elements: 'amai' (a term often associated with nurturing, bearing, or bringing forth) and 'rangi' (sky, heavens, or celestial realm). The full name can be understood as 'the one who brings forth the sky' or 'the heavenly bearer'.

Pronunciation — Ah-MY-eh teh RAH-ngi (roughly; Māori vowels are pure: a = 'ah', i = 'ee', e = 'eh', the 'ng' is a velar nasal as in 'sing')

Aliases — Amai Te Rangi, Amai-te-Rangi, Amai te Rangi

Ownership — Māori (Ngāi Tahu and other South Island iwi traditions, particularly associated with Te Wai Pounamu / the South Island of Aotearoa New Zealand)

Cosmic Creation

Amai-te-rangi is involved in the creation of the world, specifically in the separation of the sky and the earth and the shaping of the South Island.

Limit: Not clearly defined; the extent of her creative power is not elaborated in surviving sources.

Celestial Authority

As a being connected to the sky, she holds authority over celestial realms and phenomena.

Limit: Not directly described in sources; this is a reasonable inference based on her name and role.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description survives in the sources. As a celestial being associated with the sky and creation, Amai-te-rangi is typically imagined in abstract terms — as a vast, nurturing presence connected to the heavens. In artistic representations, she might be depicted as a female figure intertwined with celestial motifs, stars, or the sky itself.

Symbols — The sky, stars, celestial vault, the act of separation, the South Island landscape (as her creation)

Domains — Sky, heavens, creation, cosmic order, the separation of Rangi and Papa (Sky Father and Earth Mother), the formation of the South Island of New Zealand

Accounts I
The Separation of Rangi and Papa (South Island Version)Ngāi Tahu oral tradition; recorded by Edward Shortland and others

Pre-contact; recorded in the 19th century

In the beginning, Rangi (the Sky Father) and Papa (the Earth Mother) lay in a close embrace, their children trapped in the darkness between them. In this South Island version of the tradition, Amai-te-rangi is one of the beings who assists in the great work of separating the parents. Her role is associated with the heavens and with the act of pushing Rangi upward, allowing light to flood into the world. The separation is a violent and sorrowful act, as Rangi and Papa are torn apart, but it is also necessary for life to flourish. As part of this process, the South Island of New Zealand is formed and shaped, becoming a distinct land. The world is filled with light and life. The South Island is established as a distinct landmass. The separation establishes the cosmic order that continues to this day.

Amai-te-rangi is a major deity in the pan-Māori pantheon. — Amai-te-rangi is a figure specific to South Island (Ngāi Tahu) traditions. She does not feature prominently in the creation narratives of other iwi.

Amai-te-rangi is the same as the goddess Hine-nui-te-pō. — Hine-nui-te-pō is the goddess of death and the underworld, a major figure in Māori mythology. Amai-te-rangi is a sky-associated creation figure. They are not the same being.
