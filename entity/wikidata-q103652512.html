Who It Is

In the pantheon of Chinese folklore, few figures embody the tempestuous spirit of rebellion and redemption as vividly as Nataku Taishi, the Third Lotus Prince—a boy-god who defied the heavens, battled dragons, and ultimately reshaped his own destiny through sacrifice and rebirth.

Nezha is characterized by a fiery, impulsive, and rebellious nature. He is fiercely independent, often clashing with authority, including his own father. Despite his defiance, he is also deeply loyal to his master and to justice, often fighting against evil forces. His actions are driven by a strong sense of right and wrong, though his methods are often violent and rash. Over time, he matures into a more disciplined deity, but his core temperament remains passionate.

Powers
Universal Ring (Qiankun Quan)

A golden ring that can grow to immense size, strike enemies with great force, and return to Nezha's hand. It is a symbol of his cosmic authority.

Limit: Requires mastery; in some versions, its power is limited by Nezha's spiritual cultivation.

Red Armillary Sash (Hun Tian Ling)

A red sash that can stretch to bind enemies, create barriers, or allow flight. It is often depicted as a ribbon that can trap foes.

Limit: May be countered by stronger magic or by cutting the sash in some tales.

Fire-Tipped Spear (Huo Jian Qiang)

A spear that can summon fire and is used in close combat. It is one of Nezha's primary weapons.

Limit: Requires skill; fire can be extinguished by water-based magic.

Wind-Fire Wheels (Feng Huo Lun)

A pair of wheels that allow Nezha to travel at great speed, often depicted as spinning flames beneath his feet. They can also be used as weapons.

Limit: May be affected by wind or water magic; requires balance and control.

Three Heads and Six Arms

A transformation that grants Nezha three heads and six arms, each wielding a different weapon, greatly enhancing his combat ability.

Limit: Requires spiritual energy; may be temporary in some versions.

Rebirth from Lotus

After his death, Nezha is resurrected by his master Taiyi Zhenren using a lotus flower, giving him a new body that is immune to many forms of harm.

Limit: The new body may be vulnerable to certain spiritual attacks; in some versions, it is less human.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Nezha is typically depicted as a handsome youth, often with a topknot or child-like hairstyle, and wearing a red belly cloth or armor. He is commonly shown with three heads and six arms, each holding a different magical weapon, though in some depictions he has two arms. Symbols — Key symbols include the Universe Ring (Qiankun Quan), the Red Armillary Sash (Hun Tian Ling), the Fire-Tipped Spear (Huo Jian Qiang), and the Wind-Fire Wheels (Feng Huo Lun). These represent his cosmic power, control over fire and wind, and his ability to traverse the heavens. In art, he is often shown in a dynamic fighting pose, symbolizing his martial prowess and defiance.

Domains — As a deity, Nezha is associated with youth, protection, and martial valor. He is often invoked as a protector of children and a guardian against evil spirits. In mythology, he resides in the celestial realm, but he frequently descends to the mortal world. He is also associated with the element of fire and with lightning, and his wind-fire wheels allow him to traverse the earth and sky.

Accounts I
Birth and Early ExploitsFengshen Yanyi

Ming dynasty (16th century)

Nezha is born as the third son of Li Jing, a military officer, after his mother carries him for three years and six months. He emerges as a ball of flesh, which his father cuts open with a sword, revealing a boy with supernatural abilities. Nezha quickly demonstrates his power by killing the Dragon King's son, Ao Bing, and later battles the Dragon King himself to protect his family. Nezha's actions provoke the Dragon King's wrath, leading to a confrontation that culminates in Nezha's suicide to save his family. His soul is later revived by his master.

Rebellion Against the Dragon KingFolk tradition and Fengshen Yanyi

Various

In a popular tale, Nezha kills the Dragon King's son and then, when the Dragon King threatens to flood the mortal world, Nezha confronts him. The conflict escalates until Nezha offers his own body as a sacrifice, cutting off his flesh and returning it to his mother and his bones to his father to sever his filial ties. This act of self-sacrifice allows Nezha to be reborn with a divine body, free from familial obligations, and he later becomes a powerful general in the war against the Shang dynasty.

Accounts II
Battle with Sun WukongJourney to the West

Ming dynasty (16th century)

When Sun Wukong rebels against Heaven, Nezha is sent to subdue him. The two engage in a fierce battle, with Nezha using his transformations and weapons, but he is ultimately defeated by the Monkey King's cunning and strength. Nezha's failure leads to the Jade Emperor seeking other means to control Sun Wukong, eventually resorting to the Buddha.

Service in the Investiture of the GodsFengshen Yanyi

Ming dynasty (16th century)

After his rebirth, Nezha serves as a general under the Zhou dynasty in their war against the Shang. He uses his powers to defeat numerous enemies, including powerful sorcerers and demons, and plays a crucial role in the final victory. Nezha is eventually deified as a marshal in the celestial hierarchy, and his father is also deified.
