Who It Is

One short syllable — Sham — holds a geography, a fraud, a racehorse that almost beat immortality, an equation that runs the modern material world, and a forgotten mutant in a future New York. Say it in Damascus and you mean home. Say it at a racetrack and you mean the chestnut colt who chased Secretariat. Say it in a lab and you mean the mathematics that lets us see electrons.

Arabic toponym: geographic-cultural entity, not a person; characterized in literature as fertile, urban, holy (al-Ard al-Muqaddasa), and contested. English sham: behavioral concept denotes deception, pretense, or experimental control without efficacy; sham surgery patients exhibit placebo responses; sham peer review denotes fraudulent evaluation. Racehorse Sham: described by jockey Laffit Pincay Jr. and trainer Frank Martin as intensely competitive, front-running, headstrong, requiring strong restraint; noted for willingness to duel. Toponymic and Cultural Authority (Al-Sham)

As a geographic-historical designation, al-Sham confers religious prestige (Qur'anic al-Ard al-Muqaddasa, hadith on Sham as blessed), administrative legitimacy for caliphates, and modern nationalist mobilization (Greater Syria ideology). Its 'power' is symbolic-political, not supernatural.

Limit: Boundaries never fixed; competing definitions produce political disputes; cannot be invoked as sovereign legal entity today.

Racing Prowess (Sham horse)

Exceptional early speed and stamina: won Santa Anita Derby (1973) by 2.5 lengths in 1:47 flat, Wood Memorial, Santa Catalina; set fractions in Kentucky Derby ( :23 3/5, :46 3/5) while dueling Secretariat, finishing second in 1:59 4/5 (then second-fastest Derby ever) and second in Preakness.

Limit: Vulnerable to right foreleg injury; fractured cannon bone after Belmont Stakes prep, retired July 1973; as sire, limited success (no major stakes winners of comparable class).

Kohn-Sham Effective Single-Particle Formalism

Maps interacting many-electron problem onto non-interacting electrons in effective potential V_eff = V_ext + V_Hartree + V_xc, enabling practical density functional calculations of ground-state densities, energies, band structures. Powers modern computational chemistry, materials design, catalysis.

Limit: Exact exchange-correlation functional V_xc unknown; approximations (LDA, GGA, hybrid) fail for strongly correlated systems, van der Waals, excited states; self-interaction error.

Social Mobilization (Sham 69 and Sam the Sham)

Punk band Sham 69 could incite sing-along mass chant ('If the Kids Are United,' 'Borstal Breakout') and football-terrace unity; Sam the Sham & the Pharaohs' 'Wooly Bully' (1965) achieved Billboard #2, cultural ubiquity and garage-rock influence.

Limit: Band's association with National Front infiltration of punk audiences led to gig violence and commercial decline; musical influence limited outside UK street-punk.

Accounts I
The Province of Bilad al-Sham — From Conquest to AjnādAl-Baladhuri Futuh al-Buldan, al-Tabari Ta'rikh, Yaqut Mu'jam, modern synthesis in Bosworth EI2

634-750 (Rashidun-Umayyad), codified 9th-13th century

Following the battles of Ajnadayn (634) and Yarmouk (636), Arab armies take Damascus, Jerusalem and Antioch. Caliph Umar organizes the Levant as Bilad al-Sham, divided into four (later five) ajnad: Dimashq, Hims, al-Urdunn, Filastin (and later Qinnasrin). Damascus becomes Umayyad capital (661-750). Abbasids retain the name but shift capital to Baghdad. Geographers describe Sham as fertile, urban, and holy, with distinct dialects and products. The term persists through Ayyubid, Mamluk and Ottoman periods as Sham-i Sharif. Creates enduring Levantine identity; Damascus-Jerusalem axis as religious and administrative core; later basis for Greater Syria nationalism and modern border disputes.

Sham vs Secretariat — The Duel of 1973Daily Racing Form charts; William Nack 'Secretariat: The Making of a Champion' (1975); Sports Illustrated 1973-05-14

1972-1973

Foaled 1970 at Claiborne Farm, Sham wins 5 of 13 starts including Santa Anita Derby and Wood Memorial, entering Kentucky Derby as second favorite. In Derby, Sham and Secretariat duel through historically fast fractions (:23 3/5, :46 3/5, 1:09 4/5). Secretariat pulls clear late to win in record 1:59 2/5; Sham finishes second in 1:59 4/5, far ahead of the rest. Two weeks later Sham again finishes second in Preakness. He suffers hairline fracture after Belmont and retires. His Derby time would have won every prior Derby. Cements Sham as 'greatest horse never to win Triple Crown race'; inspires Sham Stakes at Santa Anita (est. 2001); veterinary case study in dueling tactics and injury.

1993-1996

In dystopian Nueva York 2099, Xi'an Chi Xan gathers outcast mutants as X-Men 2099. Among Downtown mutants and victims of the Theatre of Pain — a body-horror cult that mutates subjects for entertainment — a character identified as Sham appears in crowd scenes and handbook indices as a minor affiliate/victim. No origin issue is dedicated to him. Illustrates 2099's theme of disposable mutants; character remains obscure, rarely referenced after series cancellation; contributes to Q107689503 Wikidata entry as 'fictional character'.

Sham 69 — Hersham to Top of the PopsDave Laing 'One Chord Wonders' (1985), Jon Savage 'England's Dreaming' (1991), band interviews

1975-1980

Schoolboys Jimmy Pursey and Dave Parsons form in Hersham, Surrey, punning Hersham as Sham 69 (graffiti 'Hersham 69' misread). Championed by John Peel, they sign to Polydor, score hits 'Borstal Breakout' (1978), 'If the Kids Are United' (1978 UK #9), 'Hurry Up Harry' (1978). Their football-chant punk draws skinhead following; National Front attempts to co-opt gigs lead to violence (Rainbow Theatre 1979). Band splits 1980, reforms later with Pursey. Pioneers Oi!/street punk; 'If the Kids Are United' becomes anti-racist anthem despite co-option; influence on later punk and football culture.

Sham (1920/1921) — The Lost Society MelodramaAFI Catalog #12286; Wid's Daily 1921-09-18; Photoplay synopsis Oct 1921

1920-1921

Frank G. Tompkins' one-act play Sham is expanded into a Broadway play and then a silent film starring Ethel Clayton as Katherine Van Riper, a society woman whose marriage is revealed as a sham to cover debts. The film, directed by Thomas N. Heffron for Oliver Morosco Photoplay Co., follows Katherine's moral dilemma when her husband's fraud is exposed; she chooses independence. Contemporary reviews praised Clayton but called plot 'thin.' No print is known to survive; only stills and reviews remain. Example of post-WWI 'society drama' interrogating sham marriages; loss illustrates silent film preservation crisis (~75% lost).

Sham el-Nessim — The Sniffing of the BreezeEgyptian Ministry of Antiquities; S. Ikram cultural histories; Al-Ahram archives

Ancient (linked to Shomu harvest) to present (modern holiday formalized 19th century)

Sham el-Nessim (شم النسيم, 'smelling the breeze') is celebrated on Coptic Easter Monday. Folk tradition links it to ancient Egyptian spring rite Shemu, marking creation and renewal with salted fish (fesikh), colored eggs, and outings to green fields. Under Muhammad Ali's era it became national holiday for Muslims and Copts. Families picnic along Nile, eat fesikh and onions, believed to ward off evil. Living Pharaonic survival claim; UNESCO intangible heritage candidate; annual public health warnings about fesikh safety.

The Kohn-Sham Equations — Making the Invisible ComputableKohn & Sham Phys. Rev. 140 A1133 (1965); P. Hohenberg & W. Kohn Phys. Rev. 136 B864 (1964)

1964-1965, Nobel Prize 1998

Hohenberg-Kohn prove ground-state density determines all properties. Kohn and Sham, at UC San Diego, propose fictitious non-interacting system with same density, yielding tractable equations. Initially met with skepticism, the method becomes dominant by 1990s with better functionals (Perdew, Becke). Kohn receives 1998 Nobel Chemistry Prize. Today >70% of materials modeling uses Kohn-Sham DFT. Enables design of drugs, batteries, catalysts, semiconductors without costly experiments; Lu Jeu Sham shares no Nobel but is eponymously immortalized.

Sham is just another name for the country Syria. — Arabic al-Sham historically denotes Greater Syria (the Levant), not the modern Syrian Arab Republic (al-Jumhuriyya al-Arabiyya al-Suriyya, est. 1946). Equating them erases Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine/Israel and misreads medieval sources.

Sham (horse) broke down during the Kentucky Derby, which is why he lost. — Sham finished sound in Derby and Preakness, both seconds to Secretariat. His career-ending fracture of the right cannon bone occurred in training after the Preakness, before the Belmont Stakes, not during the Derby duel.

Sham surgery and placebo are the same as no treatment. — Sham controls are active interventions omitting only the hypothesized therapeutic element; they control for placebo effect, anesthesia, incision, and ritual, and carry real risks unlike 'no treatment.'

Kohn-Sham orbitals are real electron orbitals. — Kohn-Sham orbitals are mathematical constructs of a fictitious non-interacting system that reproduces the true density; only the density and total energy are formally exact, not orbital energies (except HOMO in exact DFT).

Sham 69 were a neo-Nazi band. — Band espoused working-class unity ('If the Kids Are United') and denied racism; their skinhead following was infiltrated by National Front activists against the band's wishes, leading to violence the band opposed.

Sham in X-Men 2099 is a major hero with defined superpowers. — Available primary evidence shows Sham as obscure, possibly background character with no defined power set or solo storyline; fan wikis provide minimal data and may conflate word with name.
