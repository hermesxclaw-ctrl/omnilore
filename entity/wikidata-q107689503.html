The Approach

One short syllable — Sham — holds a geography, a fraud, a racehorse that almost beat immortality, an equation that runs the modern material world, and a forgotten mutant in a future New York. Say it in Damascus and you mean home. Say it at a racetrack and you mean the chestnut colt who chased Secretariat. Say it in a lab and you mean the mathematics that lets us see electrons.

Content advisory. Contains discussion of fraud, sham marriages and elections, militant group naming, historical violence in Levantine history, and medical placebo/sham surgery ethics. No graphic detail.

Etymology

Multiple independent etymologies converged in English orthography. 1) Arabic Shām (شام) from Old Arabic / Syriac tradition; classical Arabic lexicographers connect it to shāma 'left, north' as opposed to Yaman 'right, south' when facing east, or to toponymic use for Damascus and its hinterland; early Islamic geographers use Bilad al-Sham for 'the lands on the left/north of the Hijaz.' 2) English noun/adjective sham (1670s) of obscure origin; OED notes first attestations as noun meaning 'trick, hoax' and verb 'to trick,' possibly from Northern English sham 'shame' or cant from shammer, or from shammy (chamois leather) as counterfeit material; by 18th century extended to 'counterfeit, pretended.' 3) Chinese Sham in Hong Kong place names is Cantonese transliteration 深 (sam1, 'deep') in Sham Shui Po (深水埗, 'deep water pier') and 深圳河 Sham Chun River; unrelated to Arabic or English. 4) Kohn-Sham is eponym from physicists Walter Kohn and Lu Jeu Sham (born 1938, Chinese-American). 5) Personal name Sham in South Asia from Sanskrit Śyāma (श्याम) 'dark, black, dark-blue,' epithet of Krishna.

Cultural Ownership

Arabic toponym belongs to Levantine Arab cultural and historical heritage; Bilad al-Sham is a living geographic and cultural identity across Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine/Israel. English common noun is public-domain lexicon. Hong Kong place names belong to Cantonese/Hong Kong heritage. Racehorse name is Thoroughbred racing history (US). Fictional character Sham is intellectual property of Marvel Comics (Marvel Entertainment). Band and performer names are trademark/entertainment identities. No single community owns the orthographic string; each referent has distinct stewardship.

Disputed Identifications

Frequent conflation: 1) Al-Sham ≠ modern Syrian Arab Republic; historically Bilad al-Sham is Greater Syria (Levant), not the nation-state; Damascus is often called al-Sham metonymically but is a city within the region. 2) English sham (fraud) has no etymological connection to Arabic Sham despite folk etymologies. 3) Sham (horse, 1970) is distinct from Sham Stakes (race named in his honor, inaugurated later) and from unrelated horses with similar names. 4) Sham the X-Men 2099 character (Q107689503) is distinct from 'Sham' as generic descriptor and from other Marvel characters; fan databases sometimes list him with minimal appearances, leading to confusion with background mutants. 5) Sham 69 is a band, not a person named Sham. 6) Sham Pain (Five Finger Death Punch song) uses 'sham' as English word, not a proper name.

Description

Appearance

No single appearance: each referent has distinct visual identity. Arabic al-Sham has no personified form but is iconographically represented in medieval Islamic maps (al-Istakhri, Ibn Hawqal) as a rectangular province with Damascus, Jerusalem, Antioch marked, and in modern nationalist cartography as the Levant. English sham as concept is non-visual but represented in editorial cartoons as masks, counterfeit banknotes, or placebo pills. Racehorse Sham: dark bay/brown (officially dark bay), ~16.2 hands, white star, powerful hindquarters; contemporary photographs show a rangy chestnut-brown colt often photographed alongside the larger chestnut Secretariat. X-Men 2099 Sham: as depicted in early 1990s Marvel house style, lean male mutant with theatrical/pierrot-like face paint and ragged costume associated with the Theatre of Pain; color palette muted purples/greys in Ron Lim art; precise design varies by issue and is poorly documented due to few appearances. Hong Kong Sham Shui Po: dense urban district, post-war tenements, electronics market (Apliu Street), no single icon.

Symbols & Iconography

Al-Sham: Umayyad Mosque of Damascus, Dome of the Rock, cedar tree, keffiyeh patterns in modern Levantine nationalism; medieval coins minted in Dimashq al-Sham. English sham: theatrical mask, snake oil bottle, placebo capsule (often two-tone), sham election ballot box. Kohn-Sham: the coupled equations themselves ( [-1/2 ∇² + V_eff] ψ_i = ε_i ψ_i ) reproduced in textbooks as symbol of modern computational materials science. Behaviour & Personality

Arabic toponym: geographic-cultural entity, not a person; characterized in literature as fertile, urban, holy (al-Ard al-Muqaddasa), and contested. English sham: behavioral concept denotes deception, pretense, or experimental control without efficacy; sham surgery patients exhibit placebo responses; sham peer review denotes fraudulent evaluation. Racehorse Sham: described by jockey Laffit Pincay Jr. and trainer Frank Martin as intensely competitive, front-running, headstrong, requiring strong restraint; noted for willingness to duel. X-Men 2099 Sham: personality sparsely characterized; fan summaries describe him as peripheral, possibly victim/manipulated by Theatre of Pain, with little dialogue; primary sources show limited characterization — flagged as gap. Band Sham 69: working-class, street-punk ethos, football-chant choruses, confrontational but community-oriented.

Powers & Abilities

Toponymic and Cultural Authority (Al-Sham)

Source VersionEarly Islamic historiography (al-Baladhuri, al-Tabari) and modern Levantine nationalism (Antoun Saadeh's Syrian Social Nationalist Party)

As a geographic-historical designation, al-Sham confers religious prestige (Qur'anic al-Ard al-Muqaddasa, hadith on Sham as blessed), administrative legitimacy for caliphates, and modern nationalist mobilization (Greater Syria ideology). Its 'power' is symbolic-political, not supernatural.

Limitations

Boundaries never fixed; competing definitions produce political disputes; cannot be invoked as sovereign legal entity today.

Contradictions

Medieval geographers include differing northern limits (some include Antioch and Tarsus, others stop at Aleppo).

Racing Prowess (Sham horse)

Source VersionDaily Racing Form charts 1972-1973; Blood-Horse retrospective

Exceptional early speed and stamina: won Santa Anita Derby (1973) by 2.5 lengths in 1:47 flat, Wood Memorial, Santa Catalina; set fractions in Kentucky Derby ( :23 3/5, :46 3/5) while dueling Secretariat, finishing second in 1:59 4/5 (then second-fastest Derby ever) and second in Preakness.

Limitations

Vulnerable to right foreleg injury; fractured cannon bone after Belmont Stakes prep, retired July 1973; as sire, limited success (no major stakes winners of comparable class).

Contradictions

Popular memory inflates Sham as 'could have been Triple Crown winner without Secretariat'; time analysis shows Secretariat's margins (2.5 lengths Derby, 8 lengths Belmont) were decisive.

Kohn-Sham Effective Single-Particle Formalism

Source VersionKohn-Sham 1965; extended by Perdew-Zunger, Becke, Lee-Yang-Parr functionals

Maps interacting many-electron problem onto non-interacting electrons in effective potential V_eff = V_ext + V_Hartree + V_xc, enabling practical density functional calculations of ground-state densities, energies, band structures. Powers modern computational chemistry, materials design, catalysis.

Limitations

Exact exchange-correlation functional V_xc unknown; approximations (LDA, GGA, hybrid) fail for strongly correlated systems, van der Waals, excited states; self-interaction error.

Contradictions

Debate over whether Kohn-Sham orbitals/energies have physical meaning vs being auxiliary constructs; band gap underestimation controversy.

Stories & Myths

The Province of Bilad al-Sham — From Conquest to Ajnād

Source Al-Baladhuri Futuh al-Buldan, al-Tabari Ta'rikh, Yaqut Mu'jam, modern synthesis in Bosworth EI2Culture Early Islamic / ArabPeriod 634-750 (Rashidun-Umayyad), codified 9th-13th century

Summary

Following the battles of Ajnadayn (634) and Yarmouk (636), Arab armies take Damascus, Jerusalem and Antioch. Caliph Umar organizes the Levant as Bilad al-Sham, divided into four (later five) ajnad: Dimashq, Hims, al-Urdunn, Filastin (and later Qinnasrin). Damascus becomes Umayyad capital (661-750). Abbasids retain the name but shift capital to Baghdad. Geographers describe Sham as fertile, urban, and holy, with distinct dialects and products. The term persists through Ayyubid, Mamluk and Ottoman periods as Sham-i Sharif.

Participants

Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab

Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan

Umayyad dynasty

Bilad al-Sham populations (Arab tribes, Aramaean, Greek)

Consequences

Creates enduring Levantine identity; Damascus-Jerusalem axis as religious and administrative core; later basis for Greater Syria nationalism and modern border disputes.

Variants

Some sources list Jund Qinnasrin as separate; Ottoman usage narrows Sham to Damascus city; modern colloquial Syrian Arabic Sham = Damascus alone.

Sham vs Secretariat — The Duel of 1973

Source Daily Racing Form charts; William Nack 'Secretariat: The Making of a Champion' (1975); Sports Illustrated 1973-05-14Culture American Thoroughbred racingPeriod 1972-1973

Summary

Foaled 1970 at Claiborne Farm, Sham wins 5 of 13 starts including Santa Anita Derby and Wood Memorial, entering Kentucky Derby as second favorite. In Derby, Sham and Secretariat duel through historically fast fractions (:23 3/5, :46 3/5, 1:09 4/5). Secretariat pulls clear late to win in record 1:59 2/5; Sham finishes second in 1:59 4/5, far ahead of the rest. Two weeks later Sham again finishes second in Preakness. He suffers hairline fracture after Belmont and retires. His Derby time would have won every prior Derby.

Sham el-Nessim — The Sniffing of the Breeze

Source Egyptian Ministry of Antiquities; S. Ikram cultural histories; Al-Ahram archivesCulture Egyptian (Pharaonic to modern Coptic/Muslim)Period Ancient (linked to Shomu harvest) to present (modern holiday formalized 19th century)

Summary

Sham el-Nessim (شم النسيم, 'smelling the breeze') is celebrated on Coptic Easter Monday. Folk tradition links it to ancient Egyptian spring rite Shemu, marking creation and renewal with salted fish (fesikh), colored eggs, and outings to green fields. Under Muhammad Ali's era it became national holiday for Muslims and Copts. Families picnic along Nile, eat fesikh and onions, believed to ward off evil.

Participants

Egyptian families

Coptic community

Ancient Shemu festival

Consequences

Living Pharaonic survival claim; UNESCO intangible heritage candidate; annual public health warnings about fesikh safety.

Variants

Scholars dispute direct Pharaonic continuity, arguing modern form is 19th-century nationalist revival rather than unbroken ancient rite.

The Kohn-Sham Equations — Making the Invisible Computable

Source Kohn & Sham Phys. Rev. 140 A1133 (1965); P. Hohenberg & W. Kohn Phys. Rev. 136 B864 (1964)Culture International physics / chemistryPeriod 1964-1965, Nobel Prize 1998

Summary

Hohenberg-Kohn prove ground-state density determines all properties. Kohn and Sham, at UC San Diego, propose fictitious non-interacting system with same density, yielding tractable equations. Initially met with skepticism, the method becomes dominant by 1990s with better functionals (Perdew, Becke). Kohn receives 1998 Nobel Chemistry Prize. Today >70% of materials modeling uses Kohn-Sham DFT.

Participants

Walter Kohn

Lu Jeu Sham

Pierre Hohenberg

Consequences

Enables design of drugs, batteries, catalysts, semiconductors without costly experiments; Lu Jeu Sham shares no Nobel but is eponymously immortalized.

Variants

Debate over interpretation of Kohn-Sham eigenvalues as physical vs mathematical; extensions to time-dependent DFT (Runge-Gross).

Sham→Sham 69 (band) and Jimmy Purseycreator

Band name derives from Hersham graffiti; Jimmy Pursey is frontman and lyricist. Sham 69's identity is tied to working-class Surrey punk scene, not to Arabic toponym.

Sham→Jund al-Sham (militant group) and Sham el-Nessim (holiday)homonym

Jund al-Sham ('Army of Syria/Levant') is jihadist group name exploiting Sham's religious prestige; Sham el-Nessim is Egyptian spring festival whose name coincidentally contains Sham (from Coptic/ Arabic shamm 'to smell'). Both are unrelated homonyms using same consonants for different roots.

Sam the Sham (born 1937) stage name plays on English sham; Sham Stakes is Santa Anita horse race named to honor Sham the horse (inaugurated 2001 as Sham Stakes, Grade 3). Both are derivative namesakes, not the primary entity.

Sham surgery / placebo ethics

Source Moseley et al. NEJM 2002 (sham arthroscopy); Albin, Hastings Center Report on sham surgery ethics

Description

Sham surgery — incision without therapeutic procedure — is used as control in trials (e.g., arthroscopy, Parkinson's fetal tissue). It exposes control participants to anesthesia and surgical risks without benefit, raising ethical controversy; Helsinki Declaration requires minimization of risk and informed consent.

Scholarly Context

Bioethics debate: scientific validity vs non-maleficence; IRBs now require strong justification.

Contradictions & Disputes

Boundaries of al-Sham

Position A

Bilad al-Sham is Greater Syria including modern Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Palestine/Israel and parts of Turkey/Iraq, bounded by Taurus and Euphrates.

Position A — evidence

Yaqut, al-Maqdisi, Bosworth EI2 list ajnad covering entire Levant; nationalist literature (SSNP) maps Greater Syria.

Position B

Colloquial Sham = Damascus city only; Ottoman Sham = Damascus Vilayet.

Position B — evidence

Damascenes refer to Damascus as al-Sham; Ottoman administrative documents label Damascus province as Sham-i Sharif; modern Syrian Arabic 'rayeh ala Sham' means going to Damascus.

Scholarly Consensus

Academic consensus accepts classical Bilad al-Sham as Levant, while acknowledging semantic narrowing in Ottoman and colloquial usage; context determines meaning.

Etymology of English sham

Position A

From Northern English shamme 'shame' or cant 'shammer' (17th c. slang).

Position A — evidence

OED earliest citations connect sham to 'shame' euphemism; 1677 Hubbard uses sham as trick.

Position B

From shammy (chamois leather) as counterfeit material, or from Irish seam 'to trick.'

Position B — evidence

Skeat and Weekley propose shammy leather → sham; no direct textual bridge but phonetic plausibility.

Scholarly Consensus

OED notes origin obscure/uncertain; no consensus, all proposals remain hypothetical.

Sham horse's Derby performance

Position A

Sham ran the second-fastest Derby ever and would have won any other year; only Secretariat's freak performance beat him.

Position A — evidence

Official time 1:59 4/5 remains top-10 fastest Derby; chart shows 8-length gap to third place.

Position B

Sham's fast time was product of suicidal pace duel that compromised both; he was never going to sustain it and faded badly in Belmont context.

Position B — evidence

Pace analysis shows opening fractions :23 3/5, :46 3/5 were fastest to that point; Sham finished last in Belmont after injury, suggesting stamina limit.

Scholarly Consensus

Racing historians agree Sham was exceptional but Secretariat was generationally superior; pace duel narrative is accepted but not that Sham would have won without Secretariat.

Continuity of Sham el-Nessim

Position A

Direct survival of Pharaonic Shemu festival, 4,500-year continuity.

Position A — evidence

Herodotus and tomb paintings show spring rites with eggs and fish; Coptic continuity claims.

Position B

Modern invention of 19th-century Egyptian nationalism retroactively linked to Pharaonic past.

Position B — evidence

Scholarly Consensus
