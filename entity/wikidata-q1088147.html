Who It Is

At the bottom of the Indian Ocean, where light never reaches, a golden lance waits to pierce the Dragon. Krishna Chrysaor does not shout or posture — he sits in lotus, eyes closed, his chakra points gleaming like stars, and invites his opponent to strike. In Masami Kurumada's Saint Seiya, he is the most serene and the most unsettling of Poseidon's seven Marina Generals: a warrior-monk whose invulnerability is not armor, but enlightenment weaponized.

Attested: Calm, detached, almost priestly. Unlike more flamboyant Marina Generals (e.g., Sorrento, Io), Krishna speaks softly about enlightenment, suffering, and the flow of cosmos. He pities Shiryu for relying on brute force and invites him to realize the futility of attacking. He is confident to the point of stillness, refusing to dodge, trusting his chakra defense. Shows no sadism; his cruelty is philosophical — telling Shiryu his Dragon's fury is meaningless before the universe. When his defense is broken, he shows shock and then dignified acceptance, not rage. Version differences:

Powers
Golden Lance (Konjikiso / Ougon no Yari)

A double-ended golden spear that can extend, contract and change trajectory via cosmos. Stated to 'never miss' and to pierce any defense. Can be wielded at close range or fired like a projectile and recalled. In lore, it embodies Chrysaor's golden sword.

Limit: Requires line-of-sight/cosmos lock; can be deflected if all chakra points are struck simultaneously, disrupting the wielder's cosmos flow. Lance itself is not invulnerable — Shiryu's Excalibur cuts it.

Kundalini / Chakra Defense (Kundarini)

By awakening kundalini, Krishna manifests seven chakra points along his body as cosmos nodes. As long as kundalini flows, any attack is repelled or its damage nullified; cosmos circulates through chakras making him impervious to conventional blows.

Limit: If all seven chakras are struck at the exact same instant, the kundalini flow is severed and defense collapses permanently. Requires concentration and lotus meditation; disruption of focus weakens defense. Shiryu exploits this with simultaneous multi-point strike.

Maha Roshini (Maha Roshini / Great Light)

Offensive cosmos technique where Krishna unleashes a burst of light/cosmos from chakra points, blinding and repelling opponent. Sometimes interpreted as kundalini energy discharge.

Limit: Short range, primarily defensive repulsion; not shown to be lethal alone. Requires kundalini active.

Marina General Cosmos & Scale Protection

As one of seven Marina Generals, Krishna commands cosmos on par with Gold Saints (7th sense). Scale provides superhuman durability, underwater breathing, and pressure resistance in undersea Sanctuary.

Limit: Scale less durable than Gold Cloth; destroyed along with pillar when Krishna falls. Vulnerable to Gold Saint-level techniques (Excalibur).

Accounts I
The Awakening of Poseidon and the Seven Marina GeneralsSaint Seiya manga vol.15 ch.135-139; anime ep.100-110

1986-1988

After the Sanctuary Arc, sea god Poseidon's spirit awakens in 16-year-old Julian Solo. His steward Thetis gathers seven youths chosen by the seven ocean Scales. Krishna is introduced in silhouette among the generals summoned to the Undersea Sanctuary to defend the Mamoth Pillars that support the seas. He accepts Poseidon's plan to flood the Earth to cleanse humanity, not from malice but from detached belief in divine judgment. Original prose: Kurumada presents the generals as Poseidon's equivalent to Athena's Gold Saints, each guarding one ocean pillar. Establishes Krishna's role as Indian Ocean guardian and sets up Bronze Saints' infiltration of Undersea Sanctuary. No individual backstory given for Krishna beyond selection.

Guardian of the Indian Ocean PillarManga ch.139-140; anime ep.111

1988

Athena's Bronze Saints descend to the Undersea Sanctuary to destroy the seven Mamoth Pillars and reach Poseidon. Dragon Shiryu, separated from Seiya and Hyoga, arrives at the Indian Ocean Pillar. Krishna sits in levitating lotus, eyes closed, lance floating beside him. He announces that none who enter his ocean domain leave alive, and that his lance has never failed to pierce its target. Shiryu's initial Rozan Sho Ryuu Ha (Rising Dragon) is effortlessly repelled. Introduces Krishna's invulnerability gimmick and philosophical demeanor; establishes Indian Ocean Pillar as second or third pillar challenged (order varies by adaptation).

The Kundalini Duel — The Unbreakable Chakra DefenseManga ch.141 'Kundalini! The Chakra Points'; anime ep.112

1988

Krishna explains his power: through yoga he has awakened kundalini, the serpent power coiled at the base of the spine, opening seven chakras. As long as kundalini flows, no attack can harm him — cosmos circulates and nullifies damage. He demonstrates by letting Shiryu strike freely; blows pass through or are repelled. He then counters with lance thrusts that pierce Shiryu's armor and body repeatedly, even as Shiryu cannot locate a weakness. Shiryu, sensing cosmos, realizes the chakras are the key but cannot hit all at once while blind and wounded. Defines Krishna's signature ability and its explicit weakness (simultaneous strike on all chakras). Elevates fight from brute force to insight puzzle, typical of Kurumada's technique-vs-technique structure.

Accounts II
Excalibur Unleashed — The Dragon's Final RoarManga ch.142 'Excalibur vs. Golden Lance'; anime ep.113

1988

Near death, Shiryu recalls his master Libra Dohko and the Gold Saint Capricorn Shura, whose spirit bequeathed the holy sword Excalibur to Shiryu's right arm. Realizing he cannot strike seven points with fists alone, Shiryu concentrates his cosmos to its peak, attaining 7th sense. He unleashes Rozan Sho Ryuu Ha to lift Krishna, then at point-blank range executes Excalibur's vacuum blade, slicing simultaneously through all seven chakra points and severing the Golden Lance. Kundalini collapses; Krishna, mortally wounded, acknowledges Shiryu's enlightenment before falling. Shiryu then destroys the Indian Ocean Pillar with his remaining cosmos. Death of Krishna Chrysaor and destruction of Indian Ocean Pillar, allowing Bronze Saints to advance toward Poseidon. Demonstrates Bronze Saints surpassing Gold-level defense through 7th sense. Krishna's Scale and lance are destroyed.

The Myth Behind the Scale — Chrysaor and Pegasus, Sons of PoseidonHesiod Theogony 270-288, 979-983; Kurumada's author notes in Jump Gold Selection 3 p.124; Saint Seiya Taizen p.79

c.700 BCE (myth) / 1988 (manga commentary)

Kurumada's commentary explicitly links Krishna's Scale to the myth where Chrysaor and Pegasus spring from Medusa's blood after Perseus beheads her — both fathered by Poseidon. This makes Chrysaor the mythological brother of Pegasus. In Saint Seiya lore, this brotherhood is mirrored in the duel: Pegasus Seiya's comrade Dragon Shiryu defeats Chrysaor, avenging/symbolically reuniting the brothers. The manga has Krishna remark on this kinship to Shiryu, noting that Pegasus and Chrysaor share Poseidon's blood. No separate Krishna myth is invoked — the Hindu name is decorative. Provides meta-narrative resonance for Krishna's fight and explains Scale naming logic for all Marina Generals. Fan scholarship often misreads this as Krishna being related to Hindu Krishna's mythology, which Kurumada never claims.

Afterlife in Franchise Memory — Cameos and RetellingsSaint Seiya anime recap films, Saint Seiya: Poseidon arc Memorial (2004 DVD), Saint Seiya Awakening: Knights of the Zodiac (2019 mobile game) story mode ch. Poseidon

2004-2023

Krishna has no canonical resurrection; he remains dead after Poseidon Arc. He reappears only in flashback montages, game story retellings, and databook profiles. In Saint Seiya Awakening and Saint Seiya: Soldiers' Soul, his fight is playable as a boss stage with identical kundalini mechanic (player must hit all chakra points). In the 2014-2015 Saint Seiya: Soul of Gold and Saintia Sho, Marina Generals are referenced but Krishna does not return, unlike Kanon. His death is treated as definitive, reinforcing Poseidon Arc's high casualty rate among Marina Generals. Cements Krishna as one-arc antagonist with cult following among fans of technique-heavy fights, but no ongoing narrative role. Keeps character from being retconned or redeemed.

Krishna Chrysaor is the Hindu god Krishna appearing in Saint Seiya. — He is an original fictional human named after the deity, wearing a Greek-myth Scale named Chrysaor. He has no divine status, no flute, no connection to Vishnu's avatar lore beyond name and yoga aesthetics. Kurumada borrowed the name for exotic flavor.

Chrysaor is a sea monster like Kraken or Scylla, so Krishna's Scale should be monstrous. — In Hesiod, Chrysaor is a handsome giant/warrior ('golden sword'), not a sea monster. Kurumada chose him precisely because he is humanoid and brother of Pegasus, fitting a human general. The monstrous Scales are Kraken, Scylla, etc.

Krishna's kundalini makes him completely invincible and he could only be defeated by Athena herself. — Manga explicitly states weakness: simultaneous strike on all seven chakras breaks kundalini. Shiryu, a Bronze Saint with 7th sense and Excalibur, exploits this. No divine intervention needed.

Krishna appears in multiple arcs and is resurrected like other Saints. — He dies in Poseidon Arc and never returns in canonical manga/anime. Cameos are flashbacks or games. Unlike Kanon or Saga, he has no redemption arc.
