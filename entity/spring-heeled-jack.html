Who It Is

Spring-heeled Jack is one of the most bizarre and well-documented figures in urban legend history -- a supernatural or gothic attacker who terrorized London and other English cities throughout the Victorian era. First reported in 1837, Jack was described as a tall, thin figure wearing a dark cloak and helmet, with glowing red eyes, clawed metallic hands, and the ability to leap extraordinary heights -- jumping over walls, carriage tops, and even buildings. His attacks typically targeted young women, whom he would approach in the dark, tear at their clothing with his claws, and then leap away before help could arrive. Unlike the later Jack the Ripper, Spring-heeled Jack was not (consistently) a killer; his MO was assault and terror. The panic began in September 1837, when a businessman named Mr. Scales reported seeing the figure leap over a cemetery wall, and escalated through multiple attacks reported in the London press. The most famous incident occurred in February 1838, when an 18-year-old woman named Jane Alsop was attacked at her home in London by a figure matching Jack's description; her sisters intervened, and Jack fled by leaping away. The Lord Mayor of London publicly denounced the figure as a hoax, but reports continued for decades. Theories about Jack's identity include: an aristocrat's cruel prank or dare, a fire-juggling performer (the 'fire-breathing' aspect), a marquess with mechanical spring-shoes, a collective urban legend amplified by mass media, or an extraterrestrial/cryptid entity. Spring-heeled Jack entered popular culture through penny dreadfuls, theatrical productions, and later in comics and films. He stands as a uniquely Victorian fusion of gothic horror, urban panic, and tabloid journalism.
