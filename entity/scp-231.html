Who It Is

SCP-231 is one of the most controversial and morally complex entries in the SCP canon. The article, heavily redacted in its public-facing version, describes a series of seven female children — designated SCP-231-1 through -7 — impregnated through anomalous means. Six of the seven have already given birth, and each birth resulted in catastrophic anomalous events (designated XK-Class end-of-the-world scenarios). SCP-231-7 is the last remaining subject, a girl whose pregnancy has been maintained indefinitely through a procedure known only as 'Procedure 110-Montauk.' The nature of Procedure 110-Montauk is heavily redacted in the file, but implications throughout the article suggest it is horrific — described by one source as 'what the Devil does to child molesters in Hell.' The procedure is overseen by the O5 Council and must be performed by D-Class personnel with specified characteristics. The entry is a study in the Foundation's darkest moral compromises: the O5s have decided that subjecting one child to an unspeakable procedure is preferable to the apocalyptic alternative. This has made SCP-231 a touchstone for discussions about the Foundation's ethics. Various tales and interpretations have been written by the community, some attempting to reveal what 110-Montauk truly entails, others deliberately subverting the implication. The most famous reinterpretation is the 'Fear Alone' canon, where 110-Montauk does not involve physical harm but psychological terror — the child must be made afraid, because if she ceases to fear, she will give birth. In this interpretation, the Foundation deceives even its own personnel about the procedure's true nature to ensure the terror appears genuine. SCP-231 is not popular because of a cool monster design — it is popular because it forces readers to confront the ethical boundaries of necessary evil, making it one of the most mature and disturbing entries in the Foundation catalog.
