Who It Is

SCP-1048 is a small teddy bear composed entirely of what appears to be human ear tissue and hair. It is animate, capable of independent movement, and displays behaviors consistent with a friendly, curious animal — it waves at personnel, gives 'hugs,' and makes soft teddy-bear-like movements. Initially classified as Safe, SCP-1048 was considered harmless. Then, Foundation personnel discovered that SCP-1048 was creating duplicates of itself — and those duplicates were horrifying. SCP-1048-A was constructed from human ears and human vocal cords; it followed a junior researcher for weeks, endlessly repeating the researcher's private conversations back to them in a distorted whisper before attacking. SCP-1048-B was built from a human fetus, its tiny limbs rearranged into a parody of a teddy bear shape, and it screamed continuously. SCP-1048-C was constructed entirely of scrap metal and razor blades; it attempted to 'hug' a D-Class personnel to death. The original SCP-1048 appears to be the source, and it apparently creates its duplicates out of whatever materials it can find — including materials harvested from humans. It is unclear whether SCP-1048 is malicious or simply doesn't understand that its 'children' cause harm. Its behavior toward Foundation staff remains friendly and non-aggressive even as its copies murder people. This disconnect — a cute, waving teddy bear that is indirectly responsible for multiple deaths — is the core of SCP-1048's horror. The revelation that its initial classification as Safe was catastrophically wrong prompted a Foundation-wide review of other 'cute' anomalies. SCP-1048 now carries a Keter classification, and all instances of its duplicates are to be incinerated on sight. The original SCP-1048 remains in reinforced containment, still waving at researchers who pass by.
