Who It Is

Manas is the legendary hero of the Epic of Manas, the great epic poem of the Uyghur and Kyrgyz peoples and one of the longest epic poems in world literature (approximately 500,000 lines in its fullest version — 20 times longer than Homer's Iliad and Odyssey combined). Manas is the epic founder of the Kyrgyz nation and the embodiment of Turkic martial valor. According to the epic, Manas was born with supernatural signs — his hand clutching a clot of blood (symbolizing his warrior destiny), his body covered in hair, and his voice booming like thunder. He united the forty Kyrgyz clans (the 'kyrk' of Kyrgyzstan), fought against the Oirat (Kalmyk) invaders from the east, and established a kingdom of justice and prosperity. The epic recounts Manas's birth, his heroic childhood, his courtship of Kanikey (his wise wife), his battles against the Chinese and Oirat forces, his death by poisoning, and the continuation of his legacy by his son Semetey. Manas is not simply a warrior — he is also a diplomat, a unifier, a builder of cities, and a patron of the arts. The epic was traditionally performed by manaschi (bard) in a trance-like state, accompanied by the komuz (three-stringed lute). The most famous manaschi, Sayakbay Karalaev (1894-1971), recited the entire epic from memory across many sessions. For Uyghurs, the Manas epic is part of their shared Turkic heritage, and the hero represents the ideal of unity against oppression. The epic has been a source of cultural resistance and identity for Turkic peoples throughout periods of Russian and Chinese domination.
