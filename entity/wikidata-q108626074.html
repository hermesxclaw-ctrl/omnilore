Who It Is

On Ard Skellig, where jarls settle arguments with axes and kings are made by shouting the loudest, the sharpest blade belongs to the woman who never draws one. Cerys an Craite is small, freckled, and underestimated — until she outwits a shapeshifting horror, exposes a murderous conspiracy, and steals a crown from under her brother's sword.

Attested in-game as fiercely intelligent, willful, and politically astute. Where Skellige culture venerates physical strength and glorious death, Cerys values cunning, empathy, and stewardship. She is described by Geralt's journal as 'small in stature but with a will of iron' and by NPCs as 'Crach's little storm.' She is compassionate (volunteering to help Jarl Udalryk despite personal danger), stubborn to the point of recklessness, and allergic to being patronized because of her gender or size. Powers
Tactical Intelligence and Cunning

Cerys's defining ability is not supernatural but intellectual. She solves problems through deduction, empathy, and lateral thinking where warriors use force. Most clearly demonstrated in 'Possession' where she deduces the Hym's nature and in 'King's Gambit' where she uncovers Birna Bran's mass murder plot by noticing inconsistencies in the bear attack.

Limit: Her plans rely on Geralt's execution and on others believing her; she is physically outmatched and politically vulnerable as a young woman in a patriarchal warrior culture. Her cunning does not grant combat superiority.

Leadership and Statecraft (Potential Queen)

If crowned, Cerys enacts reforms: ending Skellige's isolationist raiding economy, strengthening ties with the Continent, investing in agriculture and trade, and unifying the clans. NPC epilogues state Skellige prospers under her rule and avoids the bloody civil war that follows other outcomes.

Limit: This is a political outcome, not an innate power, and is entirely player-dependent. It requires Geralt's intervention; without it she remains a jarl's daughter with no formal authority. Her reforms face resistance from traditionalists like Lugos.

Courage and Willpower / Resistance to Fear

Cerys willingly enters the cursed house of Jarl Udalryk to confront the Hym, a specter that feeds on guilt and drives victims to suicide. She withstands psychological torment and volunteers to become bait, showing exceptional mental fortitude. Geralt notes this is rare.

Limit: She is not immune to the Hym; she requires Geralt's witcher methods (tricking the Hym to possess her via ritual) and Ermion's druidic knowledge to survive. She suffers trauma and exhaustion afterward.

Skilled Swordswoman (Adequate)

Trained as a Skellige noble, she carries and can use a short sword and is shown sparring and traveling in dangerous wilderness. Gwent flavor suggests martial competence.

Limit: Explicitly not on the level of Geralt, Hjalmar, or Crach. She avoids direct combat and prefers to outwit foes. No superhuman feats attested.

Investigation and Forensic Reasoning

During King's Gambit, Cerys conducts a murder investigation: examining bodies, questioning survivors, identifying that the bears that killed the other claimants were drugged with mead and that the mead was poisoned by Birna Bran's agents.

Limit: Requires Geralt's witcher senses to confirm physical evidence; she cannot do it alone.

Possession - The Hym of UdalrykThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Skellige secondary quest 'Possession' (Level 19)

1272 (in-game chronology, after events of The Witcher 2)

Jarl Udalryk an Brokvar of Spikeroog has been haunted for years by a Hym - a shadow wraith born from terrible guilt that whispers to its host until he kills himself. Cerys, seeking to prove herself worthy of consideration as heir, travels to Spikeroog against her father's wishes to help Udalryk, whom she has known since childhood. She finds the jarl in a catatonic state, his hall terrified. Geralt arrives, hired to investigate. Cerys insists on a daring plan devised with the druid Ermion: to trick the Hym into leaving Udalryk by making it believe Cerys is an even more guilt-ridden host. She deliberately takes blame for a childhood accident and subjects herself to a ritual where the Hym possesses her. Geralt must then enter a cursed, illusionary world inside the possessed house and defeat the Hym in combat while Cerys resists mentally. If successful, Udalryk is freed and Cerys earns immense respect for her selflessness and bravery. The Lord of Undvik - The Shadow of HjalmarThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Skellige secondary quest 'The Lord of Undvik'

1272

While this quest primarily follows Hjalmar an Craite's attempt to slay the ice giant Myrhyff (Undvik's lord) and reclaim the isle, Cerys features in its framing. At Kaer Trolde, Crach laments both his children have left to prove themselves - Hjalmar to Undvik, Cerys to Spikeroog. Cerys later comments on her brother's reckless glory-seeking versus her own approach. Geralt travels to Undvik, finds Hjalmar's expedition decimated, and ultimately kills the giant. The quest serves as narrative counterpoint: Hjalmar seeks glory through monster slaying; Cerys seeks it through healing and justice. Sets up the succession contest. If Geralt helps Hjalmar, Hjalmar returns as a hero, strengthening his claim. Cerys's absence from the physical quest underscores her different heroic model. Both siblings' quests must be completed before King's Gambit triggers.

The King is Dead - Long Live the King (The Wake)The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Main quest 'The King is Dead - Long Live the King'

1272

King Bran of Skellige dies. All clans gather at Kaer Trolde for his wake, which by Skellige custom must end with the naming of a new king. Crach an Craite is a contender, but his children Cerys and Hjalmar are also put forward as candidates, to Crach's surprise. The wake devolves into drunken boasting and clan posturing. Cerys is present but marginalized by older jarls who dismiss her as 'Crach's little girl.' She quietly tells Geralt she intends to prove that a ruler needs wits, not just a strong arm. The quest ends with Bran's funeral and the announcement that the next king will be chosen at a moot after the claimants perform heroic deeds. Formally establishes the Skellige succession crisis that drives Act 2 of the Skellige storyline. Introduces Cerys as an underdog candidate and Birna Bran as antagonist.

King's Gambit - The Massacre at Kaer TroldeThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Skellige secondary quest 'King's Gambit' (Level 22)

1272

During a feast at Kaer Trolde to celebrate the claimants' deeds, the hall is attacked by bears that slaughter several would-be kings and jarls' sons. The attack is initially believed to be a freak accident or druidic curse, but Cerys immediately suspects foul play. She asks Geralt to help her investigate while Hjalmar wants to brute-force the issue by attacking the bears' source. Cerys's investigation leads Geralt to examine the dead bears (finding they were drugged with mead laced with a soporific), the mead barrels (traced to Clan an Craite's stores), and the surviving conspirators. She deduces that Birna Bran, widow of King Bran, orchestrated the massacre to eliminate rivals so her son Svanrige could be crowned as a puppet king. She confronts Birna publicly, presenting evidence. Birna confesses and is arrested. Hjalmar's parallel investigation involves torturing a druid and leads to a dead end, proving Cerys's method superior. This is Cerys's masterstroke. Coronation - Queen of SkelligeThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - Skellige secondary quest 'Coronation'

1272

At the moot on Ard Skellig, the jarls vote for the next ruler. If Geralt completed Possession via Cerys's method and supported her in King's Gambit, the jarls elect Cerys an Craite as the first queen of Skellige in generations. She is crowned at Kaer Trolde, with Crach proudly placing the crown on her head. Her coronation speech vows to end needless raiding, unite the clans, and lead Skellige into prosperity through trade and wisdom rather than blood. Epilogue slides later reveal that under Queen Cerys, Skellige flourishes, avoids civil war, and becomes a stable ally. If Geralt did not support her, the coronation crowns Hjalmar or Svanrige instead, and Cerys remains as a disappointed but loyal daughter, vowing to continue serving her people. Determines the canonical fate of Skellige for the game's epilogue and for imported saves into potential sequels. Queen Cerys is considered by many fans and critics as the 'best' ending for Skellige due to its peaceful and progressive outcome. CD Projekt Red has not declared any one ending strictly canonical, leaving it player-dependent.

Childhood on Ard Skellig - The Sparrowhawk's Upbringing (Background Lore)The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt - In-game dialogue, journal entries, and ambient NPC gossip at Kaer Trolde

c. 1250-1272 (backstory)

Scattered dialogue reveals Cerys grew up as the younger child of Crach an Craite, jarl of Kaer Trolde, alongside her older brother Hjalmar. While Hjalmar was groomed as a warrior heir, Cerys was underestimated due to her size and gender but was doted on by Crach, who called her his 'little sparrowhawk.' She learned swordplay, sailing, and clan law, but also spent time with druids and commoners. NPCs recall her as a wild child who once stole a longship for a dare and who always asked 'why' when told 'because that's how it's done.' This upbringing explains her reformist outlook and her close bond with her father despite their political rivalry. Provides character motivation and explains why traditionalists underestimate her. No direct gameplay consequences but enriches her arc.

Cerys an Craite appears in Andrzej Sapkowski's Witcher books. — She does not. Only her father Crach an Craite appears in the books. Cerys was created by CD Projekt Red for The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt (2015) to give Crach a daughter and create a succession conflict.

Cerys is a romance option for Geralt in Witcher 3. — She is not romanceable. Geralt can flirt lightly, but she rebuffs or deflects, and no romance scene exists. The Skellige romance options are Yennefer (main) and Jutta an Dimun (minor).

Cerys is the eldest child and rightful heir by birth order. — Hjalmar is the elder sibling (explicitly stated by Crach and NPCs). Skellige does not practice primogeniture; rulers are elected at a moot by jarls based on deeds, not birth order. Cerys must earn her claim through heroic deeds like any claimant.

Cerys uses magic or is a druid. — She has no magical abilities. She consults druids (Ermion) and respects their knowledge, but she is not a magic user. Her success comes from deduction and empathy.

If you don't help Cerys, she dies. — Cerys never dies in any Witcher 3 outcome. If she loses the election, she remains alive as Crach's daughter, disappointed but safe. No quest results in her death, unlike many other Witcher 3 characters.
