Who It Is

While the jarls of Skellige measure worth in scars and monster heads, Crach an Craite's daughter Cerys wins her crown with a riddle, a baby's laugh, and a murder solved before the mead goes cold — the first ruler in generations to prove that cunning can outlast the axe.

Attested across Possession and King's Gambit: Cerys is sharply intelligent, observant, empathetic, and politically astute. She listens before acting, questions superstition without dismissing it, and prefers to solve problems by understanding their cause rather than by force. She displays courage without bravado – volunteering to enter Udalryk's cursed house alone, confronting the hym, and later challenging Birna Bran at a feast full of armed jarls. Powers
Skellige Statecraft and Rhetoric

Cerys's primary 'power' is political intelligence: ability to read motives, expose conspiracies, and persuade jarls. In King's Gambit she deduces Birna Bran's conspiracy (poisoned mead, hired berserkers, murder of her sons) by observing seating, timing, and motive, then traps Birna into confession before the moot.

Limit: Dependent on Geralt's support to gather evidence; without Geralt's investigation she cannot prove Birna's guilt. Her persuasion fails if player chooses not to help her or supports Hjalmar instead.

Ritual Knowledge and Courage Against Curses

Knowledge of Skellige rites and willingness to face supernatural threats without magic. In Possession she identifies the hym as a curse born of Udalryk's guilt over betraying his brother Aki, and undertakes the traditional method to defeat it: tricking the hym into possessing her by feigning a shameful act (abandoning a baby in the oven), then overcoming it through the baby's trust.

Limit: Not a sorceress; she cannot cast spells or see spirits without Geralt and Ermion's help. The hym could have killed her if the ritual failed; her success depends on the hym's lore-specific weakness (it feeds on guilt and can be starved by shamelessness).

Axe Combat and Survival

Competent with Skellige hand-axe and survival in harsh terrain. She traverses the haunted house, the labyrinth, and the isles on foot and by boat. In combat sequences (if player accompanies her), she fights alongside Geralt but is not a Witcher-tier combatant.

Limit: Explicitly less physically strong than Hjalmar or Crach; she avoids direct martial contests and would lose a duel of strength. Gameplay does not allow her as a playable fighter with Witcher signs.

Leadership and Economic Reform (Queen Path)

If crowned, Cerys enacts reforms described in epilogue slides and NPC dialogue: she unites the clans, ends the cycle of off-season raids that waste Skelligan lives, invests in shipbuilding and trade, and strengthens the Isles. The epilogue states Skellige 'prospered and grew rich' under her rule, with fewer sons dying abroad.

Limit: Conditional – only occurs if Cerys becomes queen. The game presents this as a narrated outcome, not simulated economy. Long-term success is not explored beyond the epilogue.

Investigation and Empathy

Ability to gain trust of vulnerable characters (Udalryk, the villagers of Arinbjorn) and elicit truth. Her empathy allows her to understand Udalryk's trauma where others see only madness.

Limit: Empathy does not grant supernatural insight; she still requires Geralt's Witcher senses to confirm the hym's presence.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested (game model): Young Skelligan woman, early 20s, athletic but slight build. Pale skin, light blue-grey eyes, freckles across nose and cheeks. Dark auburn/brown hair cut in a practical, uneven bob to jaw length, often wind-tousled, with a small braid on the right side secured by a metal ring. Attire is functional islander garb rather than ornamental: muted green wool tunic, brown leather vest with Clan an Craite boar sigil, fur-lined brace

Symbols — Primary symbol is the Clan an Craite boar – a bristled wild boar on a red field – visible on her vest, shields of her supporters, and Kaer Trolde banners. In quests, her iconography is contrasted with Hjalmar's bear/slaying imagery: where Hjalmar is associated with the sword, the giant, and the monster head, Cerys is associated with the hearth, the hym (curse), the baby, and the labyrinth. In Gwent, her card art frames her with shieldmaidens

Domains — Habitat is the Skellige Isles, specifically Kaer Trolde on Ard Skellig (Clan an Craite stronghold, great hall carved into the mountain overlooking the sea). She is also associated with the village of Arinbjorn and the haunted house of Udalryk an Brokvar on the isle's western coast, and with the labyrinth (ancient Skelligan burial maze) beneath Kaer Trolde used in her coming-of-age trial. Possession – The Hym and Udalryk an BrokvarThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Secondary Quest 'Possession' (Skellige, recommended level 23)

1272 (in-game chronology, Third Northern War)

Cerys an Craite asks Geralt to help Jarl Udalryk an Brokvar of Clan Brokvar, who has been haunted for years by a hym – a shadow-creature born from terrible guilt that feeds on its host, causing nightmares, voices, and physical decay. While her brother Hjalmar seeks glory by slaying the giant of Undvik, Cerys chooses a quieter, stranger task: curing Udalryk. She and Geralt discover that Udalryk's guilt stems from abandoning his younger brother Aki during a storm at sea to claim the jarldom. The hym cannot be killed by steel; Skellige lore says it must be tricked. Cerys volunteers to become its new host. She stages a shameful act – placing Udalryk's infant son (or a foundling, depending on translation) in a hot oven and walking away – to lure the hym, which craves guilt, into possessing her instead. Because she feels no true guilt (the baby is unharmed, the act is a ruse), the hym starves. Geralt, in the spirit world, defeats the weakened hym while Cerys endures its assault. The Lord of Undvik – The Other PathThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Secondary Quest 'The Lord of Undvik'

1272

Though this quest centers on Hjalmar an Craite's attempt to kill the ice giant Myrhyff on Undvik, Cerys's story is defined by her deliberate absence from it. At Kaer Trolde, Crach explains his children have each chosen a deed to prove their worth to be considered for the throne: Hjalmar sails to Undvik to slay the giant that drove Clan an Undvik from their isle, while Cerys remains to help Udalryk. The contrast is intentional – the game frames Hjalmar's quest as the traditional Skelligan heroic test (strength, monster-slaying) and Cerys's as the untraditional one (healing, cunning). If Geralt completes Undvik with Hjalmar, he witnesses Hjalmar's bravery but also his recklessness and reliance on Geralt to finish the giant. Cerys later references Undvik at the feast, noting that killing one monster does not feed a village through winter. The Undvik quest, while not starring Cerys, establishes the succession debate: the jarls initially favor Hjalmar's martial feat, but Geralt's report can emphasize either Hjalmar's valor or the cost of his expedition. King's Gambit – Murder at the Feast of Kaer TroldeThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Main Quest 'King's Gambit' (Skellige main storyline)

1272, during the wake for King Bran an Tuiseach

Following King Bran's death, the jarls gather at Kaer Trolde for a wake that will become an election moot. Crach hosts a feast. During the night, two of Birna Bran's sons are found murdered, and Arnvald, a servant, is accused. Cerys, who had been investigating Birna's recent enrichment and her husband Bran's suspicious death, asks Geralt to help her prove the truth before the jarls execute the wrong man. Together they find evidence: a poisoned mead horn, a letter from Birna to Nilfgaard, and testimony that Birna hired the berserkers of Clan an Brokvar to kill her own sons to frame Crach and eliminate rivals for her son Svanrige. At the feast, Cerys stages a public accusation, provoking Birna into revealing her contempt for Skellige traditions. With Geralt's evidence, the jarls see Birna's plot. Birna is arrested and later executed; Svanrige is spared due to youth and ignorance. Exposing Birna removes the main conspirator for a Nilfgaardian puppet regime and demonstrates Cerys's fitness to rule. It directly leads to the moot where Cerys can be elected queen. Coronation – The Queen Who StayedThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Main Quest 'Coronation' and Epilogue Slides

1272, immediately after King's Gambit

At the moot on the hill of Kaer Trolde, the jarls cast their votes. If Geralt supported Cerys (completed Possession with her and exposed Birna with her), the majority elect Cerys an Craite as Queen of Skellige – the first queen in living memory, breaking the de facto male preference. The coronation is modest: Ermion places a simple circlet on her head, Crach weeps with pride, and Hjalmar, though disappointed, kneels and pledges his axe to his sister. In epilogue narration (voiced by Dandelion), Cerys is described as ruling wisely: she curtails the wasteful raiding season, builds up fishing and trade, fortifies the isles against Nilfgaard without becoming its vassal, and keeps the clans united through law rather than fear. She is shown on the throne at Kaer Trolde, listening to petitioners. Skellige prospers in this ending; the epilogue explicitly states fewer Skelligers die abroad and the isles grow wealthy. Childhood on Ard Skellig – The Sparrow and the BearThe Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Ambient dialogue, Character Journal, and Crach's conversations at Kaer Trolde; supplemented by The World of The Witcher companion book

c. 1250-1272 (Cerys's youth, reconstructed)

Cerys and Hjalmar are raised in Kaer Trolde as children of the most powerful jarl in Skellige. Crach, a legendary warrior, dotes on both but openly favors Hjalmar's martial prowess, taking him on raids while Cerys is left to learn the management of the keep, the sagas, and the women's work of the clan – which she turns to advantage. Villagers and servants in Arinbjorn recall Cerys as a child who would hide in the rafters to listen to the elders' councils, who learned to read the sea and the ledgers, and who once solved a dispute over a stolen goat by tracking its droppings rather than by oath-combat. Her brother's nickname among warriors is 'Hjalmar the Brave'; hers, when mentioned, is simply 'Crach's clever daughter.' The labyrinth beneath Kaer Trolde – a coming-of-age maze where Skelligan youths face their fears – is referenced as a trial Cerys passed not by fighting but by navigating. This backstory, told in fragments, explains why the jarls underestimate her until the feast. The Labyrinth Beneath Kaer Trolde (Cut and Restored Lore)The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Cut content / Environmental lore; referenced in Possession dialogue and The World of The Witcher p. 124

Undated Skellige tradition, referenced in 1272

Skellige tradition holds that youths of the clans must traverse the ancient labyrinth beneath Kaer Trolde – a maze of stone, traps, and ancestral spirits – to be considered adults. While Hjalmar is said to have smashed through it, Cerys is said to have mapped it. In Possession, Cerys mentions the labyrinth as a place where fear takes shape, foreshadowing the hym's nature. The World of The Witcher describes the labyrinth as a pre-Conjunction elven or dwarven structure repurposed by Skelligans. Though no playable labyrinth quest exists for Cerys in the final game (early development reportedly planned a more extensive Cerys labyrinth sequence), the lore remains in dialogue and art book sketches, showing Cerys holding a torch and thread, evoking Ariadne. Reinforces Cerys's archetype as the clever navigator vs the strong breaker. The cut content explains why some early previews described a larger Cerys role. Its absence is noted in gaps.

Cerys an Craite appears in Andrzej Sapkowski's Witcher books. — She does not. Sapkowski's saga features Crach an Craite (introduced in Baptism of Fire) but never mentions children named Cerys or Hjalmar. They were created by CD Projekt RED for The Witcher 3 (2015) to dramatize Skellige succession. The confusion arises because Crach is a book character and the games borrow his clan.

Cerys is a sorceress or has magical powers because she defeats the hym. — She has no magical ability. She defeats the hym through ritual knowledge, empathy, and a psychological trick (feigning guiltlessness), not spells. Ermion and Geralt provide the supernatural support; Cerys provides the human insight. The journal explicitly notes she is not magically gifted.

Cerys automatically becomes queen if you complete her quests. — Becoming queen requires two specific conditions: (1) complete Possession by helping Cerys (not just starting it) and (2) correctly expose Birna Bran during King's Gambit by finding all evidence and accusing her. Helping Hjalmar instead, missing evidence, or accusing the wrong person results in Hjalmar or Svanrige becoming king. The game does not crown her by default.

Cerys's name and Skellige are purely Norse/Viking. — Skellige and Cerys blend Celtic (especially Welsh/Irish) and Norse elements. Cerys itself is Welsh, while many Skellige names (Crach, Bran, Eist) are Irish/Welsh-inspired, and the culture mixes Celtic clan law (moot/thing) with Norse ship and raid imagery. CDPR developers cited both Celtic and Scandinavian research for Skellige.
