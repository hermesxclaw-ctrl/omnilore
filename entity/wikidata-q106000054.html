The Approach

In the spring of 1995, Japanese horse racing held its breath as an unbeaten colt named Fuji Kiseki — a son of the legendary Sunday Silence — swept through his first three starts with devastating ease, only to vanish from the track as suddenly as he had arrived, leaving behind a legacy that would echo through the sport for decades.

Etymology

The name 'Fuji Kiseki' is Japanese; 'Fuji' may reference Mount Fuji or the Fuji family name, while 'Kiseki' means 'miracle' or 'miracle'.

Description

Appearance

Brown or black horse, 1.63 meters (16.1 hands) high, with a white star and a white sock on his right hind leg.

Symbols & Iconography

In Umamusume, she is depicted as a tall, elegant girl with long dark hair and a star-shaped hair accessory, reflecting his star marking.

Behaviour & Personality

On the track, he was known for his powerful strides and dominant front-running style. In Umamusume, she is portrayed as a responsible and caring senior student, often teasing younger students.

Domains & Habitats

Trained in Japan, primarily at Niigata, Hanshin, and Nakayama racecourses.

Version Differences

Real horse vs. Umamusume anthropomorphized version: the latter is a female character with a distinct personality, while the real horse was male.
