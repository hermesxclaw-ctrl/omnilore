Who It Is

In the quiet valleys and rolling hills of what is now Northern California, the Pomo people speak of a creator whose name echoes through the ages—Madumda, the one who shaped the world from a mere thought. Unlike the distant, wrathful gods of some traditions, Madumda walked among the first humans, teaching them the sacred ways of life, and his legacy persists in the songs and stories of a people who still honor his gifts.

Madumda is portrayed as benevolent and wise, but also distant. He creates the world and then withdraws, leaving humanity to manage with the help of culture heroes. He is not capricious or vengeful, but his absence after creation is notable.

Powers
Creation

Madumda created the world, including the earth, sky, and all living beings. He did so by thought or word, without physical tools.

Limit: None attested; creation is portrayed as effortless.

Omniscience

Madumda is all-knowing and aware of human actions, though he does not intervene directly.

Limit: He does not use this knowledge to punish or reward actively.

Immortality

Madumda is eternal and uncreated.

Limit: None.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Madumda is not described in human form in most accounts; he is a formless, omnipresent creator. Some narratives imply he has human-like attributes, but no specific physical description is attested.

Symbols — No known symbols or iconography. Madumda is not represented in material culture; his presence is felt in natural phenomena and through ritual.

Domains — Madumda is associated with the sky and the cosmos, but he is not confined to a specific realm. He is present in the natural world, especially in sacred places like Mount Konocti.

Accounts I
The Creation of the WorldBarrett, S.A. 'Pomo Myths.' 1933.

Pre-contact, recorded early 20th century

In the beginning, there was only water. Madumda, who existed before all things, created the earth by spreading mud from the depths. He then shaped the land, placed mountains, and created plants, animals, and finally humans. He taught the first people how to live, including how to make fire and tools. Humanity was established with the knowledge needed to survive.

Accounts II
The Departure of MadumdaPowers, Stephen. 'Tribes of California.' 1877.

Pre-contact, recorded late 19th century

After creating the world and teaching humans, Madumda withdrew to the sky, leaving humanity to govern itself. He is still present, watching over them, but he no longer communicates directly except through dreams and visions. Humans must rely on their own wisdom and the guidance of shamans.
