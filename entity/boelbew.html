Who It Is

In the misty fens of the Kempen, where the willows weep and the waters lie still, there lurks a shadow that has haunted Flemish children for centuries—the Boelbew, a shape-shifting bogeyman who drags the unwary into the murky depths.

The Boelbew is a malicious spirit that preys on humans, especially children, who wander alone near water. It is said to lure victims with deceptive sounds or appearances, then drag them into the water to drown. It is nocturnal and avoids direct confrontation, preferring stealth and deception.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Boelbew is often described as a dark, shadowy figure that lurks near water, particularly in swamps and marshes. It may appear as a tall, gaunt humanoid with glowing eyes, or as a large animal such as a black dog, bull, or bear. Some accounts suggest it can become a mist or a shadow, blending into the fog.

Symbols — Associated with water, fog, and the darkness of the marsh. It may be depicted in traditional Flemish folklore illustrations as a monstrous creature with sharp claws.

Domains — Swamps, marshes, ponds, and other bodies of water in the Kempen region. It is most active at night and during foggy conditions.

Accounts I
The Warning of the BoelbewOral tradition (reconstructed)

19th century or earlier

Parents in the Kempen warned their children not to play near the marshes after dark, lest the Boelbew rise from the water and snatch them. The creature was said to mimic the sound of a crying baby to attract curious children, then pull them into the deep. Children were kept away from dangerous water bodies; the story served as a cautionary tale.

Accounts II
The Fisherman's EncounterLocal legend (reconstructed)

Unknown

A fisherman returning home at night saw a strange mist over the pond. As he approached, a dark figure emerged and called his name. He fled, but heard the creature laughing behind him. He later fell ill and believed the Boelbew had cursed him. The fisherman avoided the pond ever after; the story reinforced the fear of the creature.
