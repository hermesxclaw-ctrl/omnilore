Who It Is

Den Boelbew (also Boelebew, Bullebak in Dutch) is a fearsome bogeyman figure from the folklore of the Kempen region in northeastern Belgium (Flanders). The name derives from 'boelen' (to bawl or howl) and 'bew' (boy or being). The Boelbew is a swamp-dwelling, child-eating monster that lurks in the peat bogs, fens, and heathlands of the Kempen — a vast, thinly populated region of sandy soil, peat bogs, and heather fields. He is described as a massive, shaggy humanoid with enormous claws, a gaping mouth full of sharp teeth, and eyes that glow like embers in the dark. The Boelbew emerges from his bog at night, especially during winter when the peat mist (nevel) lies thick across the land, and wanders the edges of villages looking for children who have strayed too far from home. His howl — a deep, mournful sound that echoes across the flat landscape — is both a warning and a terror tactic. Parents in the Kempen traditionally used the Boelbew to enforce discipline: 'If you don't come inside now, den Boelbew zal je pakken!' (the Boelbew will get you!). The Boelbew is also associated with the will-o'-the-wisp (dwaallicht), and in some accounts carries a lantern to lure travelers into the bog. Unlike the more generalized bogeyman figures of other regions, the Boelbew is specifically tied to the landscape and culture of the Kempen, where the vast, misty heathlands and treacherous peat bogs posed a genuine danger to children and travelers. The figure shares features with the Dutch Witte Wieven, the Belgian Kludde, and the English Jenny Greenteeth.

The Approach

In the misty fens of the Kempen, where the willows weep and the waters lie still, there lurks a shadow that has haunted Flemish children for centuries—the Boelbew, a shape-shifting bogeyman who drags the unwary into the murky depths.

Identity

Boelbew

Pronunciation BOOL-bayv (approximate)

Etymology

The name 'Boelbew' is likely derived from Middle Dutch or dialectal Flemish. 'Boel' may relate to 'boeman' (bogeyman) or 'bul' (bull), while 'bew' could be a variant of 'beer' (bear) or 'boe' (bogey). Alternatively, it might be a compound of 'boel' (lover, but also 'bundle' or 'heap') and 'bew' (possibly 'water' in old dialects). The exact etymology is uncertain and requires further linguistic analysis.

Cultural Ownership

Belgian (Flemish) — specifically associated with the Kempen region, a rural area in the provinces of Antwerp and Limburg.

Disputed Identifications

Sometimes confused with the 'Boeman' (generic bogeyman) or the 'Kludde' (a shape-shifting water spirit). The Boelbew may be a regional variant of the Kludde or a distinct entity; the relationship is not clearly documented.

Description

Appearance

The Boelbew is often described as a dark, shadowy figure that lurks near water, particularly in swamps and marshes. It may appear as a tall, gaunt humanoid with glowing eyes, or as a large animal such as a black dog, bull, or bear. Some accounts suggest it can become a mist or a shadow, blending into the fog.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with water, fog, and the darkness of the marsh. It may be depicted in traditional Flemish folklore illustrations as a monstrous creature with sharp claws.

Behaviour & Personality

The Boelbew is a malicious spirit that preys on humans, especially children, who wander alone near water. It is said to lure victims with deceptive sounds or appearances, then drag them into the water to drown. It is nocturnal and avoids direct confrontation, preferring stealth and deception.

Domains & Habitats

Swamps, marshes, ponds, and other bodies of water in the Kempen region. It is most active at night and during foggy conditions.

Version Differences

Variants may describe the Boelbew as a type of 'nekker' (water spirit) or a manifestation of the 'kludde'. Some stories portray it as a warning figure to keep children away from dangerous waters, while others treat it as a genuine supernatural predator.

The Warning of the Boelbew

Source Oral tradition (reconstructed)Culture Flemish (Kempen)Period 19th century or earlier

Summary

Parents in the Kempen warned their children not to play near the marshes after dark, lest the Boelbew rise from the water and snatch them. The creature was said to mimic the sound of a crying baby to attract curious children, then pull them into the deep.

Participants

Boelbew

Consequences

Children were kept away from dangerous water bodies; the story served as a cautionary tale.

Variants

In some versions, the Boelbew appears as a black dog that chases travelers into the water.

Summary
