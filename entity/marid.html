Marid

Who It Is
Form

The Marid is typically described as a colossal, towering figure, often reaching heights that dwarf mountains. In classical Islamic texts and folklore, they are depicted as having a humanoid form but with monstrous features: skin that may be dark blue or black, eyes that glow like fire, and a mouth filled with sharp, jagged teeth. Some accounts describe them as having wings, which they use to fly through the heavens or dive into the depths of the sea. Their bodies are often wreathed in smoke or flame, and their voices are said to be thunderous, capable of shaking the earth. In the 'One Thousand and One Nights', the Marid appears as a giant with a turban-like headdress, but this is a later literary embellishment. The physical form of a Marid is not fixed; they are shape-shifters and can assume human or animal forms, but their true appearance is often too terrible for mortal eyes to behold.

Powers

Wish Granting Marids are famously capable of granting wishes to those who command them, often through a magical object like a ring or lamp. However, the wishes are frequently granted in a malevolent or ironic manner, as the Marid seeks to fulfill the letter of the wish while subverting its intent. This power is limited by the Marid's own pride and the constraints of the binding magic, but it is a defining trait in folklore.

Shape-shifting Marids can assume various forms, including human, animal, or hybrid shapes. They can appear as beautiful humans to deceive, or as terrifying beasts to intimidate. This ability is used to interact with humans and to evade capture or detection.

Elemental Control As beings of the sea, Marids have dominion over water, able to summon storms, create whirlpools, and control tides. They also have some influence over air and fire, allowing them to fly and generate flames. In some accounts, they can cause earthquakes and other natural disasters.

Superhuman Strength and Size Marids are described as having immense physical power, capable of lifting mountains, crushing buildings, and causing widespread destruction. Their size is often colossal, making them formidable opponents even for other supernatural beings.

Invisibility and Teleportation Like all jinn, Marids are invisible to the human eye in their natural state, though they can become visible at will. They can also teleport across vast distances, moving between the earthly realm and the unseen world in an instant.

Accounts

A poor fisherman casts his net and catches a brass bottle sealed with the seal of Solomon. When he opens it, a massive Marid emerges, declaring that he will kill the fisherman as a reward for freeing him, because he had grown bitter after centuries of imprisonment. The fisherman, using his wits, tricks the Marid by asking how such a huge being could fit in a small bottle, and when the Marid demonstrates by shrinking back into the bottle, the fisherman seals it again. The Marid pleads for mercy, and the fisherman releases him after the Marid promises to reward him with a pond of rare fish, which leads to further adventures.

According to tradition, King Solomon (Sulayman) had authority over the jinn, including the Marids. He commanded them to build the Temple in Jerusalem and to perform other tasks. One Marid, named Sakhr, rebelled and stole Solomon's ring, taking his throne and impersonating him for forty days. Solomon eventually regained his ring and punished the Marid by imprisoning him in a bottle and throwing it into the sea. This story is referenced in the Qur'an (Surah Sad, 38:34-35) and elaborated in hadith and folklore.

The Qur'an mentions that the heavens are guarded by shooting stars, which are used to repel devils (shayatin) who attempt to eavesdrop on divine decrees. Marids, as a type of shaytan, are among those who try to climb to the lower heaven to steal secrets. They are struck by meteors and cast down. This event is described in Surah Al-Saffat (37:7-10) and Surah Al-Jinn (72:8-9). The Marids are portrayed as rebellious beings who defy divine boundaries.

Scholarly Debates
Marid vs. Ifrit
Marids are the most powerful class of jinn, superior to Ifrits (as stated in Myth and Folklore Wiki). ⇌ Some sources use 'Marid' and 'Ifrit' interchangeably, or consider Ifrits to be more powerful in certain contexts (as seen in the Wikipedia source, which notes the similarity).
Nature of Marids: Evil vs. Neutral
Marids are inherently malevolent, being a type of shaytan (devil) that rebels against God and harms humans (as per Islamic tradition). ⇌ In some folklore and modern adaptations, Marids can be benevolent or neutral, granting wishes without malicious intent (e.g., in Dungeons & Dragons, they are often good-aligned).
Elemental Association
Marids are primarily associated with the sea and water (as per common folklore and modern fantasy). ⇌ Some classical sources link Marids to fire or air, given their origin as jinn created from smokeless fire, and their ability to fly.
