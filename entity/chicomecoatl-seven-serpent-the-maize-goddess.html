Who It Is

Chicomecoatl ('Seven Serpent,' from the calendrical date 7-Serpent, an auspicious day for maize) is the primary Aztec goddess of subsistence — the personification of the edible maize upon which Mesoamerican civilization depended. She is shown holding two ears of green jade maize, symbolizing the dual stalks of the milpa, and her body is often painted red (the color of maize kernels) with a square face-mask design. As Xilonen (the 'young maize,' when the ear is still 'hairy' with silk), she represents the tender, vulnerable phase of the cycle; as Chicomecoatl proper, she is the mature, harvested corn stored in granaries for the nation's survival. Her connection to the number seven (chicome) aligns her with germination and the seven stages of the moon. Her festival involved elaborate offerings of tamales, atole (maize gruel), and the ritual decoration of granaries.
