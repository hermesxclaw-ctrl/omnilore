Who It Is

In the quiet desert town of Night Vale, where the sun is a rumor and the sky is a conspiracy, one voice cuts through the static—Cecil Palmer, the radio host who has become the town's official chronicler, its most trusted (and least reliable) source of truth. With a voice like honey and a mind like a conspiracy theory, Cecil navigates a world where the impossible is mundane and the mundane is impossible, all while keeping his listeners informed, entertained, and slightly terrified.

Cecil is characterized by his calm, soothing voice, which contrasts with the often bizarre and terrifying content of his broadcasts. He is optimistic, earnest, and deeply caring about his listeners and his town. He is also naive and trusting, often accepting the town's strangeness without question. He has a dry sense of humor and a tendency to gossip, especially about the town's various entities. He shows great loyalty to his friends and family, and is willing to bend rules for those he loves.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Cecil' is of Latin origin, meaning 'blind' or 'dim-sighted', which is ironic given Cecil's role as the town's observer. 'Palmer' is an English surname referring to a pilgrim who carried a palm branch, suggesting a journey or quest.

Pronunciation — SEE-sil PAH-mer

Powers
Radio Broadcasting

Cecil is a skilled radio host, able to convey information clearly and engagingly, even when the information is nonsensical or terrifying. He can maintain his composure and deliver lines with appropriate emphasis and emotion.

Limit: He is limited by the reach of his radio signal, though in Night Vale, the signal seems to reach everywhere, including alternate dimensions.

Unreliable Narration

Cecil's broadcasts are not always factually accurate, as he often misinterprets events or withholds information. This is not a superpower but a narrative device, allowing the listener to experience the town's strangeness through his flawed perception.

Limit: He is not always aware of his own unreliability, which can lead to confusion for the listener.

Resilience to the Supernatural

Cecil seems to be able to function in the presence of cosmic horrors and other supernatural phenomena without suffering permanent psychological damage. He often describes terrifying events with a calm demeanor, suggesting a high tolerance for the bizarre.

Limit: He is not immune to the effects of the supernatural, as seen in episodes where he is affected by memory loss or body transformations.

Memory Manipulation (Limited)

In some episodes, Cecil is shown to be able to forget traumatic events or have his memories altered, but this is usually done by external forces, not by his own will.

Limit: He cannot control when or how his memories are altered.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Cecil's physical appearance is never fully described in the podcast, as he is a voice-only character. However, in the official novel and in fan art, he is often depicted as a middle-aged white man with short dark hair, a neat mustache, and a penchant for cardigans and bow ties. He is usually shown wearing a microphone headset.

Symbols — Cecil is associated with the microphone, the radio station, and the desert landscape of Night Vale. His voice is his primary symbol, often represented by sound waves or a radio tower. In fan art, he is frequently depicted with a subtle glow or shadow, hinting at his connection to the town's supernatural nature.

Domains — Cecil's primary domain is the Night Vale community radio station, located in the fictional desert town of Night Vale, which is somewhere in the American Southwest. The town is characterized by its isolation, its strange weather, and its many supernatural phenomena. Cecil also occasionally visits other locations within Night Vale, such as the Desert Flower Bowling Alley and Arcade Fun Complex, the dog park, and the local library.

Accounts I
The PilotWelcome to Night Vale, Episode 1

2012

Cecil introduces the town of Night Vale, discussing its strange events, including the opening of a new dog park that is not a dog park, the existence of a glowing cloud, and the presence of a mysterious hooded figure. He also mentions the local government's secrecy and the importance of not looking directly at the sun. Establishes the tone and setting of the series, introducing key elements like the dog park and the town's attitude towards the unknown.

The Man in the Tan JacketWelcome to Night Vale, Episode 49 'Old Oak Doors'

2014

Cecil encounters a man in a tan jacket who seems to be following him and knows his name. The man is later revealed to be a doppelganger or a time traveler, and Cecil must confront the possibility of his own identity being unstable. Deepens the mystery of Cecil's past and introduces the idea of alternate versions of himself.

Accounts II
The Birthday of the WorldWelcome to Night Vale, Episode 75

2015

Night Vale celebrates its annual Birthday of the World, a festival that involves a parade, a ritual sacrifice (which is later revealed to be voluntary), and the appearance of the Sun, which is usually hidden. Cecil reports on the festivities while dealing with his own anxieties about the event. Reinforces the town's cyclical nature and its relationship with the celestial bodies.

What Happened to Cecil?Welcome to Night Vale, Episode 100

2016

Cecil is replaced by a doppelganger who claims to be the real Cecil. The real Cecil is trapped in a mirror dimension and must find a way back. The episode explores themes of identity, memory, and the nature of self. Cecil returns to his life, but the experience leaves him questioning his own reality.

Cecil is based on a real person. — Cecil is a fictional character created by Joseph Fink and Jeffrey Cranor. He is not based on any real individual, though actor Cecil Baldwin provides his voice and has influenced the character's portrayal.

Cecil is the only character in Welcome to Night Vale. — Cecil is the narrator, but the show features a large cast of recurring characters, including Carlos, Diane, and various supernatural beings.

Cecil is a reliable narrator. — Cecil is famously unreliable, often misinterpreting events or withholding information. The show's humor and plot often depend on his flawed perspective.

Night Vale is a real place. — Night Vale is a fictional town in the American Southwest. It does not exist in reality.
