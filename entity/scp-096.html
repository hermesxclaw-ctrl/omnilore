Who It Is

SCP-096 is a tall, emaciated humanoid creature standing approximately 2.38 meters tall with unnaturally long, thin arms and legs. Its skin is pale, almost albino white, and completely hairless. When in a docile state, SCP-096 sits in the corner of its containment cell, facing the wall, with its long arms wrapped around its bent knees, making soft, continuous whimpering or crying sounds. This behavior gave it the nickname 'The Shy Guy.' The entity's anomaly triggers when any sentient being views its face, whether directly, through a photograph, through video footage, or even through a four-pixel rendering. Once SCP-096's face has been viewed, the entity enters an extreme state of emotional distress, covering its face with its hands, screaming and crying uncontrollably for approximately one to two minutes. After this period, SCP-096 will begin running directly toward the viewer. No known barrier or distance has ever stopped it. The entity is capable of traversing any terrain, crossing oceans, and bypassing any physical obstacle with extreme speed and strength. Upon reaching the person who viewed its face, SCP-096 invariably kills them, then returns to its docile state. During its rage state, SCP-096 is virtually indestructible; its skeletal structure is impervious to all known weapons, and it can regenerate tissue damage. One documented incident (096-1-A) resulted in ██ casualties after an unknowing civilian discovered a photograph of SCP-096's face. Attempts to terminate SCP-096 have all failed, including exposure to extreme temperatures, corrosive substances, high explosives, and physical dismemberment. The entity was first discovered in the wild after an amateur mountaineer photographed it on an expedition near Mount ████████. The Foundation currently maintains strict SCRAMBLE gear protocols, requiring all visual data containing SCP-096's face to be intercepted and destroyed. SCP-096 is one of the most iconic and feared entities in the Foundation canon, a tragedy of involuntary violence driven by an entity that seems to genuinely suffer during its rampages.

The Approach

Imagine a creature that spends its days in silent, pale stillness—gaunt, long-armed, and almost pitiably thin—until the moment someone, somewhere, sees its face. That single glance, whether in person or through a photograph buried on a hard drive, flips a switch: the creature begins to scream and sob, then runs, faster than any human, across mountains and gunfire, ignoring bullets and explosions, until it reaches the viewer and tears them apart. When the killing is done, it sits down and cries again, as if ashamed of what it cannot help but do. This is SCP-096, "The Shy Guy," one of internet horror’s most chilling inventions: a monster built not around teeth or claws alone, but around the terrifying idea that knowledge—seeing, knowing, looking—can itself be fatal.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses fictional but graphic violence, dismemberment, and psychological horror associated with SCP-096. While SCP-096 is not depicted as engaging in sexual violence, slavery, or colonial exploitation, the broader SCP universe includes ethically fraught containment practices and experiments that may be disturbing. Readers sensitive to gore, intense fear, or institutional coercion should proceed with caution.

Etymology

The designation "SCP-096" follows the SCP Foundation’s internal cataloguing system, where "SCP" abbreviates the organization’s motto "Secure, Contain, Protect" and "096" is a numerical identifier assigned within the collaborative fiction project. The alias "The Shy Guy" derives from the entity’s extreme distress response to having its face seen, metaphorically aligning its behavior with human shyness while exaggerating it into lethal horror.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-096 is a fictional entity originating in the English-language SCP Foundation collaborative writing project, hosted on the SCP Wiki. It is primarily owned and curated by the SCP Foundation community under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 licensing. Its broader cultural presence belongs to internet horror culture, creepypasta traditions, and fan communities that adapt SCP material into games, videos, and derivative works.

Disputed Identifications

There is no serious dispute that SCP-096 is a fictional construct of the SCP Foundation project. However, its status within internet folklore sometimes blurs into urban-legend territory, with occasional claims or roleplay presentations treating it as if it were a real paranormal entity. Within the SCP canon itself, there are minor disputes over its sapience, gender, and exact capabilities between different tales, cross-tests, and fan wikis, but these are interpretive disagreements rather than competing identifications.

Description

Appearance

Attested: In the original SCP article, SCP-096 is described as a humanoid creature approximately 2.38 meters tall, with very little muscle mass and signs of mild malnutrition. Its arms are grossly disproportionate, each about 1.5 meters in length, giving it an elongated, unsettling silhouette. The skin is almost entirely devoid of pigmentation and body hair, producing a pale, corpse-like appearance. Its facial structure is broadly human, but the eyes lack pigmentation and the jaw can open roughly four times wider than that of a typical human, creating a grotesque, gaping maw when distressed. The entity is usually depicted naked, with no clothing or adornment. Fan art and adaptations often exaggerate its gauntness, limb length, and jaw size, sometimes adding bloodstains or scars, but these are adaptation-specific embellishments rather than canonical features.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested/adaptation-specific: SCP-096’s primary iconography in internet culture centers on its blurred or obscured face, reflecting the canonical rule that viewing its face triggers lethal aggression. Official SCP illustrations and site graphics often censor the face with pixelation or black bars, reinforcing the containment protocol. In fan art, SCP-096 is frequently shown crouched or hunched, hands over its face, symbolizing shame and fear, or mid-sprint with an open jaw, symbolizing unstoppable rage. The numeric designation "096" and the nickname "Shy Guy" function as textual symbols, often stylized in horror fonts. In games and videos, SCP-096 is iconographically associated with warning signs about visual contact, red alarms, and surveillance cameras, emphasizing the danger of looking.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: Canonically, SCP-096 is docile and largely unresponsive when its face is not viewed. It spends most of its time sitting or pacing in its containment cell, showing no signs of higher brain functions or complex social behavior. When any human sees its face—whether directly, via photograph, or via video—the entity enters an extreme emotional state: it begins crying, screaming, and covering its face, then rapidly transitions into a violent pursuit of the viewer. During this pursuit, SCP-096 runs at very high speed, ignoring obstacles and environmental hazards, and continues until it reaches the target, at which point it kills them with extreme brutality. After the target is dead, SCP-096 returns to a docile state, sitting and crying before eventually walking back to its containment location. Reconstructed/interpretive: Some secondary sources and fan discussions attribute a kind of "shyness" or shame to SCP-096, interpreting its distress as embarrassment at being seen. Others emphasize its lack of sapience, treating its behavior as an automatic, anomalous response rather than a psychological motive. These interpretations are not explicitly stated in the original article but are consistent with its described reactions.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-096’s primary habitat is an SCP Foundation containment site, specifically a 5 m x 5 m x 5 m airtight steel cube with no visual monitoring equipment. It does not demonstrate a preference for any natural environment and is only observed outside containment during breaches or retrieval operations. In Incident-096-1-A, SCP-096 traverses mountainous terrain, demonstrating that it can operate in harsh outdoor environments without apparent difficulty. Adaptation-specific: In games and fan works, SCP-096 appears in underground laboratories, abandoned facilities, and horror-themed complexes, but these settings are creative expansions rather than canonical domains.

Version Differences

Attested vs adaptation-specific: The original SCP article presents SCP-096 as non-sapient, with no evidence of complex cognition beyond its trigger-response behavior. Some fan wikis and derivative tales describe it as male, assigning it he/him pronouns and occasionally implying a human origin, but the canonical article does not specify gender or backstory. Canon states that viewing its face via any visual medium (photo, video, live feed) triggers its response; a few later discussions and adaptations experiment with different trigger conditions, such as partial views or stylized representations, but these are not universally accepted. In cross-tests (e.g., with SCP-682), SCP-096’s durability and rage are emphasized, sometimes pushing its capabilities beyond the original description. Games and videos often exaggerate its speed, strength, and gore for dramatic effect. Overall, the core traits—docility when unobserved, catastrophic aggression when seen, extreme durability, and relentless pursuit—remain stable across versions, while details of origin, psychology, and exact limits vary.

Powers & Abilities

Facial-viewing trigger

Source Version Original SCP-096 article; Incident-096-1-A; summarized in Grokipedia and WikiHow.

Description

Attested: SCP-096’s defining anomalous property is that any human who views its face—whether directly or via photographs, video recordings, or live surveillance—becomes the target of an unstoppable pursuit. The trigger appears to be the act of visual recognition of its facial features, not mere proximity. Once triggered, SCP-096 enters a distressed state, then begins tracking and pursuing the viewer regardless of distance or intervening obstacles.

Limitations

Attested: The trigger is limited to human viewers; non-human entities are not documented as targets in the main article. The effect requires some form of visual access to the face; containment procedures focus on eliminating all visual media. Unknown: Whether partial views, stylized drawings, or distorted images always trigger the response is not fully tested in canon.

Contradictions

Some fan discussions suggest that even low-resolution or partial images can trigger SCP-096, while others argue that only clear facial recognition counts. Canon implies broad sensitivity but does not specify thresholds, leaving room for interpretive disagreement.

Relentless pursuit

Source Version Incident-096-1-A log; original SCP-096 article; SCP Database Wiki summary.

Description

Attested: Once triggered, SCP-096 will pursue the viewer until the target is dead, ignoring all attempts at containment or distraction. It demonstrates extreme speed and endurance, crossing long distances and difficult terrain without rest. In Incident-096-1-A, it breaches containment, traverses mountainous regions, and reaches the target despite military intervention.

Limitations

Attested: The pursuit ends when the target is killed, after which SCP-096 returns to a docile state. It does not continue attacking other individuals unless they have also seen its face. Unknown: The maximum range of its pursuit is not defined; it appears capable of global reach but this is inferred rather than explicitly measured.

Contradictions

Some crossovers and fan works depict SCP-096 as capable of intercontinental or even interplanetary pursuit, while canonical material only shows terrestrial movement. These extrapolations are speculative and not strictly supported by primary evidence.

Extreme durability and resistance to damage

Source Version Incident-096-1-A log; Captain █████████’s testimonial; SCP Database Wiki quotations.

Description

Attested: During Incident-096-1-A, SCP-096 withstands heavy gunfire, explosives, and environmental hazards without apparent impairment. Captain █████████’s testimonial notes that the entity "must have lost all its organs, all its blood, but it didn't acknowledge any of it" and that its bone structure remained unharmed despite intense damage. This suggests extraordinary durability, particularly in its skeletal system.

Limitations

Attested: While SCP-096 appears nearly indestructible in the incident, the Foundation continues to explore termination methods, implying that its invulnerability may not be absolute. Unknown: The exact mechanisms of its durability (regeneration vs structural resilience) are not described.

Contradictions

Some cross-tests (especially with SCP-682) imply that SCP-096 can be significantly damaged or neutralized under extreme conditions, while other interpretations treat it as effectively indestructible. These differences reflect narrative experimentation rather than a settled canon.

Superhuman strength

Source Version Original SCP-096 article (containment breach description); Incident-096-1-A.

Description

Attested: SCP-096 demonstrates the ability to tear through steel containment walls, rip apart human bodies, and overcome reinforced barriers. Its physical strength far exceeds that of a human, particularly during its enraged state.

Limitations

Attested: Its strength is primarily observed during triggered episodes; in its docile state, it does not routinely exert such force. Unknown: The upper limit of its strength is not quantified.

Contradictions

Adaptations often exaggerate its strength to cinematic levels (e.g., smashing vehicles or large structures), while the original article focuses on localized destruction of containment materials and human targets.

Obstacle-negating movement

Source Version Incident-096-1-A log; summarized in WikiHow and Grokipedia.

Description

Attested: SCP-096 appears able to navigate or bypass obstacles that would normally impede movement, including rough terrain and structural barriers. In Incident-096-1-A, it traverses mountainous regions and breaches containment without slowing.

Limitations

Attested: It still physically interacts with matter (e.g., tearing walls), so it does not phase through solid objects. Unknown: Whether it can cross bodies of water, extreme environments, or vacuum is not addressed.

Contradictions

Some fan works depict SCP-096 as teleporting or phasing, but this is not supported by canonical text and should be treated as adaptation-specific.

Post-kill emotional reset

Source Version Original SCP-096 article; Incident-096-1-A.

Description

Attested: After killing its target, SCP-096 returns to a docile state, often sitting and crying before eventually walking back to its containment location or a neutral position. This suggests a cyclical behavioral pattern tied to the trigger and resolution of its rage.

Limitations

Attested: The duration of the crying phase and the exact emotional content are not analyzed; the Foundation treats it as a behavioral marker rather than a psychological insight.

Contradictions

Interpretations differ on whether this crying indicates remorse, residual distress, or a purely anomalous reflex. Canon does not assign motive, leaving the behavior open to psychological speculation.

SCP-096 main containment narrative

Source SCP-096 article, SCP Foundation WikiCulture Internet horror, SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod Late 2000s (first posted 2008, subsequently edited)

Summary

The core narrative presents SCP-096 as a Euclid-class humanoid anomaly contained in a steel cube at an undisclosed SCP Foundation site. The article outlines strict containment procedures: no visual recording devices are allowed in its cell, and all images or videos of its face are forbidden. The description details its physical appearance, docile baseline behavior, and catastrophic reaction when its face is viewed. The Foundation’s protocols emphasize preventing any visual exposure, including accidental sightings via surveillance or media. Addenda describe attempts to understand and possibly terminate SCP-096, as well as the risks posed by its trigger mechanism.

Incident-096-1-A (Retrieval Incident)

Source Addendum 096-1-A, SCP Foundation WikiCulture Internet horror, SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod Late 2000s–early 2010s

Summary

Incident-096-1-A is a detailed log of a containment breach and retrieval operation involving SCP-096. The narrative begins with SCP-096 in its steel cube, monitored via non-visual sensors. A sudden spike in readings precedes a violent breach as SCP-096 dents and tears through the steel wall. Emergency plates drop, and security teams engage the entity with live rounds and heavy weaponry, but it ignores the damage and escapes. The log then follows SCP-096’s pursuit of a target who had viewed its face via a photograph, tracking it across mountainous terrain. Military forces attempt to stop SCP-096, but it reaches the target and kills them in a gruesome manner. Captain █████████’s testimonial emphasizes the entity’s apparent loss of organs and blood without functional impairment, highlighting its extreme durability.

Dark History
Content advisory. SCP-096’s narratives contain graphic violence, dismemberment, implied torture, and psychological horror. Readers may encounter descriptions of bodies torn apart, organs and blood loss, and intense fear responses. While sexual violence, slavery, and colonial themes are not central to SCP-096’s lore, the broader SCP universe sometimes engages with ethically fraught experimentation and coercive containment practices.

Graphic violence and dismemberment

Source Incident-096-1-A log; SCP Database Wiki quotations; WikiHow summary.

Description

Incident-096-1-A and related accounts describe SCP-096 tearing apart human bodies with extreme force. Captain █████████ notes that the entity continued attacking his squad despite apparent massive bodily damage to itself, and that its victims were violently dismembered. These scenes are presented in clinical but vivid language, emphasizing the horror of unstoppable physical destruction.

Scholarly Context

Within horror studies, SCP-096 exemplifies the trope of the unstoppable killer whose violence is both inevitable and excessive. The clinical framing reflects SCP’s pseudo-scientific style, which juxtaposes bureaucratic language with extreme gore.

Psychological horror and coercive containment

Source Original SCP-096 article; cross-test tales; WikiHow discussion of containment.

Description

The SCP Foundation’s containment procedures for SCP-096 involve strict control over visual information, effectively coercing staff into a regime of surveillance without sight. The threat that any accidental viewing will result in death creates a pervasive atmosphere of fear and anxiety. The Foundation’s willingness to conduct cross-tests and termination attempts, potentially sacrificing personnel, raises ethical questions about coercion and expendability.

Scholarly Context

Critical readings of SCP fiction often highlight the Foundation as a metaphor for authoritarian institutions that prioritize control over individual welfare. SCP-096’s visual trigger dramatizes the dangers of knowledge and the moral cost of managing dangerous information.

Contradictions & Disputes

Sapience and psychology

Position A

SCP-096 is non-sapient, exhibiting no higher brain functions and acting purely on anomalous triggers.

Position A — evidence

The original SCP-096 article states that SCP-096 shows no signs of higher brain functions, and containment procedures treat it as a reactive anomaly rather than a thinking agent.

Position B

SCP-096 possesses some level of sentience or emotional life, as suggested by its crying and "shy" behavior.

Position B — evidence

Incident-096-1-A and various summaries describe SCP-096 crying, screaming, and covering its face, behaviors that resemble emotional distress. Fan wikis and articles often interpret these as signs of shame or fear.

Scholarly Consensus

Within the SCP community, there is no formal scholarly consensus, but many readers accept a hybrid view: canonically, SCP-096 is described as non-sapient, yet its behavior invites psychological interpretation. The tension between clinical description and emotional imagery is part of its horror appeal.

Gender and identity

Position A

SCP-096 is genderless or unspecified, treated as an object rather than a person.

Position A — evidence

The original article uses neutral language and does not assign a gender, consistent with SCP’s tendency to treat anomalies as objects.

Position B

SCP-096 is male, often referred to with he/him pronouns and characterized as a "he" in fan works.

Position B — evidence

SCP Database Wiki and many fan discussions refer to SCP-096 as male, and some adaptations give it a masculine voice or backstory.

Scholarly Consensus

Canon supports the genderless or unspecified position, while fan culture frequently genders SCP-096 for narrative convenience. This is generally understood as a fan-layer addition rather than a canonical fact.

Trigger conditions for facial viewing

Position A

Any visual representation of SCP-096’s face, including photographs and video, triggers its pursuit.

Position A — evidence

The original article explicitly forbids photos, video, or recordings of SCP-096’s likeness, implying that these media can trigger the anomaly. Incident-096-1-A involves a photograph as the trigger.

Position B

Only direct, clear views of its face trigger the response; partial or distorted images may not.

Position B — evidence

Some fan interpretations and game mechanics suggest that low-resolution or partial views may not trigger SCP-096, allowing for gameplay where players can glimpse it without immediate death.

Scholarly Consensus

Canonical evidence supports a broad trigger condition, but the exact thresholds are undefined. Adaptations adjust the rules for practical reasons, leading to a functional but non-canonical narrowing of the trigger.

Durability and possibility of termination

Position A

SCP-096 is effectively indestructible, surviving massive damage without functional impairment.

Position A — evidence

Incident-096-1-A describes SCP-096 losing organs and blood without acknowledging the damage, and its bone structure remains intact despite heavy fire.

Position B

SCP-096 can be significantly damaged or potentially terminated under extreme conditions.

Position B — evidence

Cross-test narratives with SCP-682 and speculative termination attempts depict scenarios where SCP-096 is harmed or neutralized, though outcomes vary.

Scholarly Consensus

The main article leans toward extreme durability but stops short of declaring absolute invulnerability. Cross-tests are often treated as experimental or non-canonical, so there is no definitive consensus on termination. The ambiguity supports ongoing narrative exploration.

Canonical vs non-canonical crossovers

Position A

Only the original SCP-096 article and its direct addenda are canonical; crossovers are optional or non-canon.

Position A — evidence

The SCP Wiki distinguishes between main-list articles and tales, and not all crossovers are endorsed as canon. SCP-096’s primary lore is contained in its main article and incident logs.

Position B

Crossovers and games form a broader, semi-canonical meta-lore where SCP-096’s behavior is defined by community practice.

Position B — evidence

Games like SCP – Containment Breach and SCP: Secret Laboratory, along with widely read cross-test tales, shape how most fans understand SCP-096, effectively functioning as de facto canon for many.

Scholarly Consensus

SCP’s collaborative nature resists a single canon. Many community members treat the main article as core canon and crossovers as optional expansions. This pluralistic approach is itself a defining feature of SCP lore.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

Several dimensions remain partially documented or uncertain. The exact posting date and revision history of Incident-096-1-A are not clearly indicated in accessible sources, limiting precise chronology. The internal decision-making processes of the SCP Foundation regarding SCP-096 (e.g., detailed termination proposals, ethical debates) are only hinted at in public articles and may exist in non-public or unwritten lore. Cross-test narratives, especially with SCP-682, vary widely and lack a single authoritative version; this dossier treats them as semi-canonical but cannot resolve their contradictions. The psychological interiority of SCP-096—whether its crying and distress reflect emotion or purely anomalous behavior—remains interpretive, as canon does not provide explicit analysis. Reception data for specific fan films, animations, and memes are broad and representative rather than exhaustively catalogued, due to the sheer volume and rapid turnover of internet content. Finally, non-English translations and regional receptions of SCP-096 are underexplored here, as this research primarily draws on English-language sources.

Research Scope

This research focuses on SCP-096 as presented in the original SCP Foundation article and its immediate addenda, supplemented by secondary summaries (Grokipedia, WikiHow, SCP Database Wiki) and major media adaptations (notably SCP – Containment Breach and SCP: Secret Laboratory). It treats the SCP Wiki as the primary canon while acknowledging the collaborative, multi-author nature of the project, which resists a single authoritative narrative. Cross-test tales and fan works are included as semi-canonical or adaptation-specific layers, clearly distinguished from core lore. The scope is limited to English-language sources accessible as of July 30, 2026, and does not attempt a full survey of non-English translations or every individual fan production. The goal is deep, citation-ready coverage of identity, description, abilities, narratives, relationships, dark themes, reception, and contradictions, while explicitly flagging uncertainties and interpretive zones.
