Who It Is

Unkulunkulu (uNkulunkulu) is the supreme creator deity and first ancestor in Zulu cosmology. His name literally means 'The Very Old One' or 'The Great-Great Ancestor.' According to Zulu tradition, he emerged from the reeds (Uhlanga) at the beginning of time and brought forth humans, cattle, the sun, the moon, and all things. He taught the first people to hunt, build homes, make fire, cultivate crops, and established the moral code by which the Zulu people live. Unique among many African supreme deities, Unkulunkulu is not actively worshipped through direct prayer or sacrifice but is approached through the ancestors (amadlozi), who serve as intermediaries. He represents the ultimate origin point — the first person from whom all humanity descends — and his presence is felt more in the natural order and ancestral continuity than in dramatic divine interventions. Some narratives frame him as the first human who later became divine, blending the concepts of creator deity and ancestral progenitor. During colonial encounters, missionaries equated him with the Christian God, though Zulu theology frames him within a distinct ancestral ontology.
