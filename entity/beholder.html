Who It Is

Imagine a creature that is the embodiment of paranoia, a floating sphere of flesh and teeth, its single, massive eye glaring at you while a ring of ten smaller eyes, each a different color, swivel to track your every move. It is the Beholder, the Eye Tyrant, a monster so iconic that it has become the face of Dungeons & Dragons itself. It is not just a creature; it is a nightmare given form, a mastermind that believes itself to be the center of the universe, and it will do anything to protect its twisted vision of reality.

Beholders are fiercely intelligent, paranoid, and supremely arrogant. They are solitary creatures that believe themselves to be the pinnacle of creation, viewing all other beings as inferior, threatening, or useful only as tools. They are obsessive hoarders of knowledge and treasure, but their primary motivation is survival and the elimination of any perceived threat. A Beholder's paranoia is so extreme that it often sees threats in its own dreams and reflections. Powers
Anti-Magic Cone

The Beholder's central eye projects a 150-foot cone of anti-magic. Within this cone, all magic, magical items, and magical effects are suppressed. This is its primary defensive and offensive tool, neutralizing enemy spellcasters and magical defenses.

Limit: The cone is only active when the Beholder's central eye is open (it can close it as a free action). The Beholder itself is not immune to magic, but its own eye rays are not affected by its own anti-magic cone.

Eye Rays

Each of the Beholder's ten eyestalks can fire a different magical ray. The specific effects have varied across editions, but classic examples include: Charm Person, Charm Monster, Sleep, Petrification, Fear, Slow, Disintegration, Death Ray, Telekinesis, and Flesh to Stone. Each ray is a targeted beam with a specific range (usually 150 feet).

Limit: The Beholder can only fire a limited number of rays per round (typically 1-3 in early editions, up to 3 in 5th edition). It cannot fire a ray from an eyestalk that is closed or damaged. The rays are subject to the target's saving throws.

Levitation / Flight

The Beholder is a natural levitator and can fly with perfect maneuverability at a speed of about 20 feet per round. It can hover, move in any direction, and rotate in place.

Limit: It cannot be knocked prone or grappled easily due to its flight, but it is still susceptible to being restrained by webs or other physical means.

Legendary Actions (5th Edition)

In 5th Edition, the Beholder can take special actions outside of its turn, allowing it to use one of its eye rays at the end of another creature's turn. This makes it a highly dynamic and threatening opponent.

Limit: It can only use a limited number of Legendary Actions per round (3) and cannot use the same ray twice in a row.

Paranoid Genius

Beholders are exceptionally intelligent and possess an almost supernatural level of paranoia. This grants them a high Wisdom score and proficiency in Perception and Insight. They are masters of strategy, trap-making, and psychological manipulation.

Limit: Their paranoia can be a weakness, making them predictable to clever heroes who can exploit their obsessive fears.

Dream Lair (Ecology)

Beholders are unique in that they do not reproduce sexually. Instead, a Beholder's dreams are so vivid and powerful that they can manifest into reality. A Beholder that has a particularly intense dream of another Beholder will, upon waking, find that its dream has spawned a new, younger Beholder. This new Beholder is often hostile to its 'parent' and will attempt to kill it to establish its own domain.

Limit: This is a purely lore-based concept, not a mechanical ability.

Appearance — The Beholder is a floating, roughly spherical creature, about the size of a large beach ball or small boulder (typically 4-8 feet in diameter). Its body is covered in tough, leathery skin, often with a mottled or chitinous texture, in shades of purple, magenta, green, or blue. Symbols — The Beholder's primary symbol is its own image: a central eye with a ring of smaller eyes. This symbol is often used in its own heraldry, on its minions' gear, and in the traps it sets. It is a symbol of absolute surveillance, tyranny, and arcane power. In the broader D&D mythos, the Beholder is a symbol of a classic, high-level 'boss monster' encounter.

Domains — Beholders are typically found in underground environments: deep caverns, ruined cities, and the Underdark. They prefer to establish lairs in places that are defensible and offer a clear view of all approaches. They are not native to the surface world, but can be found there if their lair is breached or if they seek to conquer a new domain. Accounts I
The Beholder as a Symbol of TyrannyVarious D&D modules and campaign settings (e.g., 'The Lost Caverns of Tsojcanth', 'I, Tyrant').

1975 - present

The Beholder is the ultimate paranoid tyrant. It is a solitary mastermind that builds a fortress of traps and enslaved minions to protect its hoard of treasure and knowledge. It is a classic 'boss monster' that represents the dangers of absolute power and unchecked paranoia. The Beholder's defeat is a major milestone for any adventuring party, often yielding great treasure and magical artifacts.

Accounts II
The Xanathar of WaterdeepWaterdeep: Dragon Heist (2018) and other Waterdeep-based adventures.

2018 - present

Xanathar is the most famous named Beholder in D&D lore. He is a crime lord who rules the underworld of the city of Waterdeep from his lair beneath Skullport. He is paranoid, capricious, and obsessed with his pet goldfish, Sylgar. His story is one of a powerful but unstable ruler whose paranoia makes him a constant threat to his own organization and the city above. Xanathar's actions shape the events of the 'Dragon Heist' adventure, making him a central antagonist even though he is not the final boss.

The Dream Birth of a BeholderThe Ecology of the Beholder (Dragon Magazine, various issues) and 'I, Tyrant' (1996).

1980s-1990s (lore expanded)

This is the foundational myth of Beholder reproduction. A Beholder's dreams are so powerful that they can spawn a new Beholder. This new creature is a 'clone' of the original, but with its own twisted personality. It immediately sees its parent as a rival and will attempt to usurp its territory. This cycle of dream-born violence ensures that Beholders are forever isolated and paranoid. This explains why Beholders are always alone: their own offspring are their greatest enemies.
