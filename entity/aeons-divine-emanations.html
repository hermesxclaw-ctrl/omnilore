Who It Is

In Gnostic systems, Aeons (Greek: αἰῶνες) are the emanations of the unknowable God (the Monad, Bythos, or Proarkhe), arranged in hierarchies or pairs (syzygies). They inhabit the Pleroma, the realm of divine fullness. The most elaborate system is the Valentinian, which describes 30 Aeons proceeding from the Primal Father (Bythos) and his consort Sige (Silence): Nous (Mind) and Aletheia (Truth), Logos (Word) and Zoe (Life), Anthropos (Humanity) and Ecclesia (Church), and so forth through successive generations, culminating in Sophia (Wisdom). Each Aeon is both a being and a dimension of divine consciousness — they are not merely created entities but aspects of the Godhead's self-knowledge. The goal of the gnostic is to re-collect the divine spark scattered through the Aeons and return to the primal unity.
