Who It Is

In the beginning, before time itself, the unknowable One did not create the world—it overflowed. This overflow, a cascade of divine beings called Aeons, filled the pleroma with light and wisdom, yet the tragedy of their fall would shape all of existence. These are the Aeons, the eternal emanations of the Gnostic God, whose stories of creation, error, and redemption challenge our very understanding of the divine.

The Aeons are perfect, harmonious, and contemplative beings. They exist in a state of eternal bliss, contemplating the Father. However, their story is marked by a tragic event: the last Aeon, Sophia (Wisdom), desires to know the Father directly and, in her passion, falls from the Pleroma, leading to the creation of the material world. This event reveals that even divine beings can err, and that the salvation of the world involves restoring the fallen Aeon.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Greek αἰών (aiṓn), meaning 'age', 'lifetime', 'eternity', 'vital force'. In Gnostic contexts, it denotes an eternal, divine being or emanation.

Pronunciation — AY-on (Greek: ah-ee-OHN)

Aliases — Divine Emanations, Aiones, Sonships (in Basilidian Gnosis), Numbers and Sounds (in Marcan Gnosis), Syzygies (in Valentinianism)

Ownership — Gnostic Christianity, primarily 2nd-3rd century CE, with roots in Hellenistic Jewish and Platonic thought.

Powers
Emanation

Aeons are produced through a process of emanation from the primal source (the One, Monad, Bythos). Each Aeon is a personification of a divine attribute, such as Mind (Nous), Truth (Aletheia), Word (Logos), Life (Zoe), etc.

Limit: Aeons are not creators of the material world in most systems; they are spiritual beings. Their power is limited by their obedience to the Father; error leads to fall.

Syzygy (Pairing)

In Valentinianism, Aeons are arranged in male-female pairs (syzygies). Each pair represents a complementary aspect of the divine, e.g., Depth and Silence, Mind and Truth, Word and Life, Man and Church.

Limit: The syzygy is essential for the Aeon's completeness; a separated Aeon (like Sophia without her partner) is prone to error.

Knowledge (Gnosis)

Aeons possess perfect knowledge of the divine, and their purpose is to contemplate the Father. This knowledge is the model for Gnostic salvation.

Limit: The highest Aeons have direct knowledge, but lower Aeons have limited knowledge, leading to the fall of Sophia.

Restoration (Apokatastasis)

The Aeons participate in the final restoration of all things to the Pleroma. This involves the reintegration of Sophia and the spiritual elements of humanity.

Limit: This is an eschatological event, not an individual power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Aeons are generally described as immaterial, spiritual beings of light. They are not depicted in physical form, but are often associated with light, fullness (pleroma), and pairs (syzygies). In some texts, they are personified as male-female pairs, each with a name and a role in the divine economy.

Symbols — The Aeons are symbolized by the Pleroma (fullness), often depicted as a sphere or a series of spheres. The cross is sometimes used as a symbol of the boundary (horos) that separates the Pleroma from the void. In Gnostic art, they are often represented as radiant beings or as abstract concepts like 'Mind', 'Truth', 'Life'.

Domains — The Aeons reside in the Pleroma, the spiritual fullness of the divine realm. This is a transcendent, non-material reality beyond the physical cosmos. The Pleroma is often contrasted with the kenoma (emptiness) or the material world created by the Demiurge.

Accounts I
The Fall of SophiaApocryphon of John, Irenaeus, Against Heresies 1.29-30

2nd century CE

Sophia, the last Aeon, desires to know the Father directly, without her partner. Her passion and ignorance lead to the birth of a defective being, Yaldabaoth (the Demiurge), who creates the material world. Sophia's error is the origin of the cosmos and the human predicament. The creation of the material world, the imprisonment of divine sparks in human bodies, and the need for salvation through gnosis.

Accounts II
The Creation of the PleromaTripartite Tractate, Valentinian Exposition

2nd century CE

The Father, the One, emanates a series of Aeons in pairs, forming the Pleroma. This is a harmonious, perfect world of light and knowledge, contrasted with the lower, flawed world. The establishment of the divine realm, the model for human salvation.

The Redemption of SophiaGospel of Truth, Exegesis on the Soul

2nd century CE

Christ is sent from the Pleroma to rescue Sophia and the divine sparks trapped in matter. Through his teaching, the spiritual elements are awakened and eventually restored to the Pleroma. The possibility of salvation and the eventual restoration of all things.

Aeons are the same as angels — Aeons are divine emanations, not created angels. They are part of the divine realm itself, whereas angels are typically considered created beings.

Gnosticism is a single unified religion — Gnosticism is a modern umbrella term for a diverse set of sects with varying beliefs. The concept of Aeons is not uniform across all Gnostic groups.

The Demiurge is Satan — In Gnosticism, the Demiurge is an ignorant, not evil, being. He is distinct from the devil of orthodox Christianity, who is a fallen angel.
