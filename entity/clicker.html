Who It Is

The Clicker is one of the most terrifying common enemy types in modern horror gaming — a human host whose body has been so thoroughly consumed by the Cordyceps Brain Infection (CBI) that the fungal growth has erupted through the skull, destroying the eyes and replacing the face with a bloom of hardened fungal plates. Clickers are blind, but their echolocation — a grotesque, rapid clicking sound produced by the fungus itself — allows them to 'see' with terrifying accuracy. The sound design is what makes Clickers iconic: the irregular, staccato clicking echoes through dark corridors, warning the player that one wrong step, one broken bottle, one accidental noise will draw an instant, fatal attack.

The Cordyceps infection in The Last of Us progresses through four stages: Runner (Stage 1, recently infected, still recognizably human, fast and aggressive), Stalker (Stage 2, fungus begins to distort the body, more stealthy and cunning), Clicker (Stage 3, fully blinded, armored fungal plates, lethal close-range attacks), and Bloater/Shambler (Stage 4, massive, heavily armored, throws spore bombs). Clickers represent the midpoint of the infection — enough fungus has grown to make them physically formidable (their fungal armor deflects melee attacks and requires shivs to counter), but they retain enough mobility to chase and grapple the player. A single grab from a Clicker is an instant kill on higher difficulties.

The Clicker's design was directly inspired by real-world Cordyceps fungi, which infect insects and alter their behavior before killing them — the 'zombie ant' fungus. Naughty Dog's artists studied fungal growth patterns, time-lapse photography of mushrooms, and medical imaging of facial deformities to create the Clicker's unique, organic-horror look. The result is a monster that feels biologically plausible, which makes it far more frightening than supernatural zombies. The Clicker is not possessed by a demon or resurrected by a virus — it is a human being whose body has been repurposed by a parasitic organism, the person inside still possibly aware but unable to control their actions.

The Clicker became an instant horror icon upon The Last of Us's release, appearing in marketing, merchandise, Halloween costumes, and fan art. Its distinctive clicking is one of gaming's most stressful sound cues, and encounters with multiple Clickers (requiring silent movement and strategic use of limited shivs) are among the game's most tense sequences. The HBO adaptation (2023) faithfully recreated the Clicker design, introducing it to an even wider audience and solidifying it as one of the defining horror creatures of the 2010s.
