Though his identity is less familiar than that of Guan Yu or Mazu, his role in the pantheon reflects the deep human need for order, protection, and the triumph of justice over chaos.

Pronunciation Approximate Mandarin pronunciation: /tʰjɛn tuː ɥɛn ʂwaɪ/ (T'ien Tu Yüan Shuai in Wade-Giles).

Etymology

The name is composed of Chinese characters: 'Tian' (天) meaning 'heaven' or 'celestial', 'Du' (都) often meaning 'capital' or 'metropolis', and 'Yuan Shuai' (元帥) meaning 'marshal' or 'supreme commander'. Thus, the name can be interpreted as 'Marshal of the Celestial Capital' or 'Heavenly Capital Marshal', suggesting a high-ranking military officer in the celestial bureaucracy.
