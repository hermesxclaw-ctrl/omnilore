Who It Is

SCP-087 is an unlit platform staircase that appears to descend infinitely. The entrance is located on a university campus, accessed through an unremarkable door that, when opened, reveals a concrete stairwell descending into complete darkness. The staircase is 2.3 meters wide with a central handrail, and each flight consists of 13 steps before turning at a half-landing. The walls are plain concrete, and the only auditory information available to explorers is the sound of their own footsteps echoing downward. No external light penetrates beyond the first flight; all illumination must be provided by the explorer. SCP-087's most notable feature is the presence of something within it — something that dwells somewhere deep in the darkness. During Foundation exploration logs, personnel at varying depths reported hearing a child crying somewhere below them. As explorers descend further, the crying becomes closer, and additional phenomena manifest: the sound of something moving in the darkness, the feeling of being watched, and eventually, encountering a pale, featureless face floating in the darkness — designated SCP-087-1 — which appears approximately 200 flights down. SCP-087-1 has no visible body, only a white face with no mouth, wide-set nostrils, and completely black, pupil-less eyes. When encountered, it rushes toward the observer at high speed, and all subsequent contact with the exploration team is lost. The Foundation has conducted multiple exploration attempts, each encountering the entity at progressively different depths, suggesting SCP-087-1 can reposition itself. The origin and purpose of the stairwell remain unknown. Theories range from a dimensional gateway to a manifestation of collective human fear of descending into darkness. SCP-087 was one of the earliest Foundation entries to achieve viral popularity, its simplicity and the primal fear of descending into the unknown resonating deeply with readers. It spawned numerous fan games, the most famous being the 2012 indie horror title 'SCP-087-B.' The Foundation currently maintains the entrance sealed with controlled access for scheduled exploration missions only.

Form

Attested: SCP-087 is described as an unlit platform staircase located on the campus of an unnamed university. The stairs descend at a 38-degree angle for 13 steps before reaching a semicircular landing approximately 3 meters in diameter. At each landing, the staircase reverses direction by 180 degrees, creating a zig-zag descent. There are no windows, doors, or lighting fixtures within the stairwell, and the walls, steps, and landings are described as ordinary building materials consistent with a modern institutional structure. The visual range of any subject is limited to roughly 1.5 flights of stairs, even when artificial light is used. Within this space, SCP-087-1 appears as a disembodied face, usually described as hovering in mid-air at or near a landing. The face has opaque white eyes, no visible pupils, no nostrils, and no mouth, giving it a mask-like, incomplete appearance. Its skin tone and other details are difficult to discern due to the pervasive darkness and the limited range of light. Reconstructed: Fan art and game adaptations often depict the stairwell with concrete or painted walls, metal railings, and a claustrophobic, industrial aesthetic, but these visual details are extrapolations from the sparse canonical description rather than explicitly attested features. Unknown: The exact materials, color schemes, and architectural style beyond basic dimensions are not specified in the original SCP article.

Powers

Non-Euclidean spatial extension Attested: SCP-087’s staircase extends far beyond the physical limits of the building and surrounding geology. Exploration IV records a depth of descent that is "far beyond both the possible structure of both the building and geological surroundings," and the article states that it is unknown whether SCP-087 has an endpoint. This suggests that the stairwell either folds space in a non-Euclidean manner or connects to an extradimensional region, effectively creating an anomalously long vertical passage. Canonical SCP Wiki article (Description; Exploration IV transcript) Unknown: The exact spatial mechanics—whether the stairwell loops, branches, or connects to another dimension—are not specified. The stairwell appears to maintain consistent geometry (13 steps per flight, 3-meter platforms, 180-degree turns), but the total number of landings and the ultimate depth remain undetermined. Some fan descriptions refer to SCP-087 as "infinite," while the canonical article only states that its depth is unknown and beyond normal structural limits. The term "infinite" is interpretive rather than attested.

Light absorption and visual limitation Attested: SCP-087 absorbs excess light from sources brighter than approximately 75 watts. The article notes that lighting sources brighter than 75 watts have proven ineffective, as SCP-087 "seems to absorb excess light." Regardless of the light source used, subjects are limited to a visual range of about 1.5 flights of stairs. This creates a persistent darkness and severely restricts situational awareness. Canonical SCP Wiki article (Description) Attested: Light sources below 75 watts still function but do not extend visibility beyond the 1.5-flight limit. The absorption effect appears localized to the stairwell interior and does not affect the exterior environment. Fan adaptations sometimes depict brighter lights temporarily revealing more of the stairwell or SCP-087-1, but this is not supported by the original article.

Persistent auditory lure (crying child) Attested: Subjects within SCP-087 consistently hear distressed vocalizations from what is presumed to be a child between unspecified ages. Audio recordings confirm these vocalizations. The source of the sound is estimated to be approximately 200 meters below the initial platform, but attempts to descend toward it never reduce the apparent distance or volume; the crying remains at a constant level, suggesting an anomalous auditory phenomenon rather than a physically located child. Canonical SCP Wiki article (Description; exploration transcripts) Attested: The sound is only reported within SCP-087 and does not appear to propagate outside the stairwell. The article does not confirm the existence of an actual child, and SCP-087-1 is explicitly stated not to be the source of the pleading. Some fan interpretations treat the crying child as a trapped victim or as an aspect of SCP-087-1, but the canonical text maintains uncertainty and distinguishes the two phenomena.

Psychological distress and paranoia induction Attested: Subjects exploring SCP-087 report intense feelings of paranoia and fear, especially after encountering SCP-087-1. The exploration transcripts document escalating anxiety, panic, and in some cases screaming and uncontrolled flight back up the stairs. The environment’s darkness, endless descent, and the presence of the crying child contribute to this distress, and SCP-087-1’s sudden appearance at close range produces extreme terror. Canonical SCP Wiki article (Description; Documents 087-I–IV) Unknown: The article does not explicitly classify SCP-087 or SCP-087-1 as cognitohazards, nor does it quantify the psychological effects beyond narrative description. It is unclear whether the distress is purely situational or enhanced by anomalous influence. Fan sources sometimes label SCP-087-1 as a "cognitohazard" or mind-affecting entity, but this terminology is not used in the original article.

SCP-087-1: fear induction and anomalous manifestation Attested: SCP-087-1 appears as a face with opaque white eyes, no visible pupils, nostrils, or mouth. It manifests suddenly, often in mid-air at or near a landing, and remains fixed while the subject reacts. Every recorded exploration encounters SCP-087-1 at least once. The face’s appearance reliably triggers intense fear and panic in subjects, leading to abrupt termination of explorations as they flee upward. The article states that the nature of SCP-087-1 is entirely unclear but confirms that it is not the source of the crying. Canonical SCP Wiki article (Description; Figure B; Documents 087-I–IV) Attested: SCP-087-1 does not physically attack subjects in the recorded explorations; its effect is psychological rather than directly physical. Its mobility, origin, and persistence are unknown. Some derivative works depict SCP-087-1 as capable of physical harm or pursuit, but these abilities are not attested in the canonical text.

Knocking phenomenon at the entrance Attested: Following Exploration IV, staff and students on the campus report knocking sounds coming from the interior of SCP-087, at a variable rate of 1–2 seconds per knock. The door is subsequently padded with 6 cm of industrial foam, and reports of knocking cease. This suggests an anomalous ability of SCP-087 or an associated entity to produce rhythmic impact sounds at the boundary between the stairwell and the external environment. Canonical SCP Wiki article (Addendum) Attested: The knocking is only reported for a two-week period after Exploration IV and stops after the door is padded. The source of the knocking is not identified. Fan interpretations sometimes link the knocking to SCP-087-1 or to the crying child, but the article does not specify any connection.

Incident Log

The SCP Foundation discovers and contains SCP-087, an anomalous stairwell located on a university campus. The doorway is disguised as a janitorial closet and secured with reinforced steel, an electro-release lock, and internal padding. The article describes the stairwell’s geometry, its lack of lighting, its absorption of excess light, and the persistent sound of a crying child from below. It notes that four explorations have been conducted by Class-D personnel and that each has encountered SCP-087-1, a disturbing face that appears within the stairwell. Following the final exploration and subsequent knocking phenomena, access to SCP-087 is prohibited.

A Class-D subject equipped with a handheld video camera and a light source descends SCP-087. The subject reports hearing a child crying from below and attempts to reach the source. As the subject continues downward, the crying remains at a constant volume, and the stairwell appears to extend far beyond expectations. At one landing, the subject encounters SCP-087-1, the disembodied face, which appears suddenly in the darkness. The subject screams, panics, and flees back up the stairs, ending the exploration.

A second Class-D subject descends SCP-087 with similar equipment. The subject again hears the crying child and attempts to reach it. At the 51st landing, a censored event occurs (marked [DATA EXPUNGED] in the transcript), suggesting a disturbing or classified incident. The subject continues downward but eventually encounters SCP-087-1 again, leading to panic and retreat. The exploration reinforces the stairwell’s anomalous depth and the recurring presence of SCP-087-1.

A third Class-D subject descends SCP-087, reaching at least the 89th landing. The subject’s psychological state deteriorates as the descent continues, with increasing fatigue, fear, and disorientation. The crying child remains at a constant volume, and the stairwell’s depth continues to defy expectations. SCP-087-1 appears again, causing further distress. The exploration ends with the subject returning to the surface, and the Foundation notes the cumulative psychological toll of repeated descents.

The fourth exploration is the longest and most extreme. A Class-D subject descends hundreds of landings (the transcript references at least the 469th landing), with the crying child still sounding as if it is approximately 200 meters below. The subject experiences severe psychological strain, and portions of the transcript are expunged, indicating events too disturbing or classified to be publicly documented. The exploration ultimately leads the Foundation to conclude that SCP-087’s depth is far beyond the building’s structure and the surrounding geology, and that the risks of further exploration are unacceptable.

Content Notes

The SCP Foundation uses Class-D personnel—typically convicted criminals or otherwise marginalized individuals—as expendable test subjects to explore SCP-087. They are sent into a dangerous, psychologically damaging environment with limited support and are expected to endure extreme fear and possible harm. The transcripts show their distress but do not address their consent or well-being beyond operational concerns.

Within the SCP universe, the use of Class-D personnel raises ethical questions about coercion, human experimentation, and institutional violence. Fan and scholarly analyses of SCP often highlight this dynamic as a critique of technocratic control and the dehumanization of expendable subjects.

SCP-087 article (Documents 087-I–IV; general SCP Foundation lore)

The persistent sound of a distressed child crying from below suggests ongoing suffering that cannot be reached or alleviated. The Foundation’s explorations fail to locate or rescue the child, and the article leaves the child’s status unresolved. This creates a haunting image of perpetual, unreachable distress.

Interpretive readings of SCP-087 often treat the crying child as a symbol of trauma, guilt, or neglected victims. The lack of resolution can be seen as a commentary on institutional failure to address underlying harm, or as a pure horror device emphasizing helplessness.

SCP-087 article (Description; exploration transcripts)

Documents 087-II and 087-IV contain sections marked [DATA EXPUNGED], indicating events too disturbing or classified to be publicly documented. While the article does not specify the nature of these events, the context suggests severe psychological or physical harm, possibly including injury or death of the exploring subjects.

The use of expunged data in SCP narratives functions as a horror technique, inviting readers to imagine worst-case scenarios. It also reflects the in-universe practice of censorship and information control by the Foundation, which can be read as a critique of secrecy and institutional opacity.

SCP-087 article (Documents 087-II and 087-IV)

Explorers of SCP-087 are subjected to an environment where their efforts to reach the crying child are futile. The stairwell’s apparent endlessness, the constant sound, and the sudden appearance of SCP-087-1 combine to create a scenario of psychological torture: fear, exhaustion, and the sense of being trapped in a meaningless, dangerous task.

Analyses of SCP-087 in horror studies often emphasize its use of spatial and auditory repetition to induce dread. The narrative can be read as an allegory of bureaucratic or existential descent, where individuals are pushed deeper into harmful systems without hope of resolution.

What We Don't Know

Several gaps remain in the dossier due to limitations of available sources and the nature of SCP-087’s narrative. The exact initial posting date and full revision history of the SCP-087 article are not fully reconstructed here, and the primary author attribution (often cited as Zaeyde) is based on secondary references rather than direct page metadata. The contents of expunged sections in Documents 087-II and 087-IV are deliberately withheld in-universe and cannot be known without fabrication; this dossier therefore treats them as unknown. The identity, existence, and fate of the crying child remain unresolved, as does the ultimate extent and endpoint of SCP-087’s stairwell. Detailed information about specific fan-made games (developer names, release dates) is incomplete, and this dossier avoids asserting particulars that could not be verified. Non-English adaptations, translations, and regional receptions of SCP-087 were not exhaustively surveyed, so the reception section focuses on English-language internet culture. Finally, because SCP-087 is a collaborative, evolving work, new interpretations or derivative appearances may exist beyond the scope of this research cutoff date.

A way to hold it in your mind

You stand at a janitor’s closet that isn’t a closet at all—behind the reinforced door, a stairwell drops into darkness, a child’s sobbing echoing from somewhere that never gets closer no matter how many landings you count. SCP-087 is horror stripped to its bare geometry: thirteen steps, a three-meter platform, a 180-degree turn, repeated until the building’s foundations and the planet’s crust should have run out long ago. Somewhere in that impossible shaft, a pale face with no mouth and opaque white eyes waits at the edge of your flashlight’s reach, not attacking, not speaking, just watching until you break and run. This dossier follows that descent, not to find a bottom—there may not be one—but to map how a single staircase became one of the internet’s most enduring architectures of fear.
