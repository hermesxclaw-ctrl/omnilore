Who It Is

Before there was bread, there was a goddess. In the ancient land of Sumer, where the Tigris and Euphrates rivers carved life from the desert, the people knew that their daily loaf and their beer were not just food and drink—they were divine gifts. The goddess Ashnan was the personification of that gift, the very essence of grain, and her story is a cosmic tale of agriculture, sibling rivalry, and the birth of human labor.

In the myth, Ashnan is portrayed as a productive, nurturing deity who works with her brother Lahar to provide for the gods. She is also depicted as a debater, engaging in a verbal contest to prove her worth, which reflects the Mesopotamian tradition of the 'debate poem'.

Powers
Grain Production

Ashnan's primary power is the creation and provision of grain. In the myth, she is created to supply the Anunnaki gods with bread and beer, the products of grain. She embodies the agricultural abundance of the land.

Limit: Her power is tied to the agricultural cycle; she cannot provide grain without the work of humans (or the pickaxe).

Personification of Abundance

As a personification, Ashnan's very existence is a source of abundance. Her presence ensures the prosperity of the land and its people.

Limit: Her power is not martial or cosmic; it is purely tied to agriculture and sustenance.

Debate and Persuasion

In the myth, Ashnan is a skilled debater, able to argue her own importance against her brother Lahar. This is a form of verbal power, demonstrating the value of her contribution to the divine and human world.

Limit: This is a rhetorical power, not a supernatural one. She wins the debate through logic and the inherent value of her domain.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ashnan's physical appearance is not described in surviving texts. She is a personification of grain, so she is likely depicted in art as a female figure holding stalks of barley or wheat, or possibly with grain sprouting from her shoulders. However, no definitive iconography has been identified. She is often symbolically represented by the grain stalk itself.

Symbols — The symbol of Ashnan is the grain stalk or a sheaf of barley. She is also associated with the threshing floor and the storehouse. In the myth, her creation is tied to the 'pickaxe' (the tool used to break the ground), thus connecting her to the agricultural tool itself.

Domains — Her domain is grain, specifically barley and wheat, which were the staple crops of ancient Mesopotamia. She is the goddess of the harvest, the grain stores, and the bread and beer that sustain civilization. Her domain is tied to the agricultural cycle and the fertility of the fields.

Accounts I
Lahar and Ashnan: The Creation of Grain and CattleSumerian myth

Old Babylonian period (c. 2000-1600 BCE)

In the beginning, the Anunnaki gods did not have bread to eat or clothes to wear. To remedy this, they created Lahar (the cattle god) and Ashnan (the grain goddess). Lahar produced cattle and sheep, providing milk, meat, and wool. Ashnan produced grain, providing bread and beer. But the Anunnaki could not make use of these products. So, the gods created humans to tend the flocks and fields and to process the raw materials. Lahar and Ashnan were placed in the care of the humans. However, a dispute arose between the two deities over who was more important. Lahar boasted of his animals, which provided clothing and meat. Ashnan countered that her grain was more essential, as it provided the bread and beer that were the foundation of civilization. The debate was brought before the god Enki and the goddess Enlil, who ruled in favor of Ashnan, declaring that grain was the more important of the two.
