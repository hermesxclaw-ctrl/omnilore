Who It Is

The Lombards (Longobardi, 'long-beards') were a Germanic people who ruled northern Italy from 568 to 774 CE. According to their origin myth recorded by Paul the Deacon in his 'Historia Langobardorum,' the Lombards were originally called the Winnili. Before a battle with the Vandals, the goddess Frea (Frigg) advised the Winnili women to tie their hair around their faces like beards and stand on the battlefield at dawn. When Odin saw them, he asked 'Who are these long-beards?' Frea declared that since he had named them, he must grant them victory — and so the Winnili became the Longobardi. Beyond this origin, Lombard dragon lore connects to the lakes of northern Italy: Lake Gerundo near Lodi was said to harbor a dragon called Tarantasio that terrorized the region until a hero slew it and drained the lake. The dragon bones were supposedly hung in a local church. The Lombard dragon tradition merges Germanic dragon-slaying motifs (Beowulf, Sigurd) with the Italian landscape, the lakes becoming dragon lairs, the marshes becoming its hunting grounds. These serpents are quasi-ancestral — linked to the Lombard kingship as protective but potentially destructive forces. The legend also connects to the broader European tradition of 'lake dragons' that must be appeased or slain for civilization (agriculture and town-building) to flourish.
