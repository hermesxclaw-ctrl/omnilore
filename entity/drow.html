Who It Is

The Drow are one of D&D's most infamous and complex creations — a subterranean race of dark-skinned elves whose society is built on slavery, demon-worship, ritual sacrifice, and inter-house warfare. Physically, drow are striking: they have jet-black or dark gray skin (an adaptation to their lightless Underdark environment), pure white or silver hair, and large, pupilless eyes that can be lavender, red, or pale blue. They are shorter and more slender than surface elves, standing around 5 feet tall, with an almost supernatural grace. Drow mature at the same rate as surface elves but have shorter average lifespans due to their violent society — though those who survive assassination attempts can live for centuries.

Drow society is a theocratic matriarchy dedicated to Lolth, the Spider Queen — a demon-goddess who encourages chaos, treachery, and ambition among her worshippers. Each drow city is ruled by noble houses, with the top eight forming a ruling council. Advancement comes through murder: a lower house can destroy a higher house and claim its position, provided no witnesses survive to report the attack. Men are subservient in drow society, barred from the priesthood and from the most prestigious positions of power. The only notable male drow institution is the warrior academy (Melee-Magthere), producing fighters who serve the matron mothers.

Drow possess innate magical abilities: the ability to conjure floating globes of darkness and to outline enemies in harmless faerie fire, making them masters of ambush. Their equipment, forged in the Underdark and imbued with faerzress (a magical radiation unique to the deep earth), is legendary for its beauty and deadliness — but it disintegrates in sunlight, preventing drow from easily waging war on the surface world. Their most feared warriors wield snake-headed whips and poisoned crossbow bolts that can paralyze or kill with a scratch.

The drow were heavily criticized in the 1980s and 1990s for potentially reinforcing racial stereotypes (a dark-skinned race that is 'inherently evil'), a criticism that Wizards of the Coast has addressed in recent years by emphasizing cultural variation, adding non-evil drow communities, and foregrounding characters like Drizzt Do'Urden. The creation of R.A. Salvatore's heroic drow ranger fundamentally changed how drow are perceived, and the 2024 revision of D&D has significantly reworked them to emphasize that drow culture, not biology, determines their moral alignment. Drow have appeared in virtually every D&D video game, from the Baldur's Gate and Neverwinter series to the MMORPG Neverwinter Online.
