Who It Is

In the grand assembly halls of Mahāyāna sūtras, the Buddha is often surrounded by more than human monks and nuns. Among the vast audience are eight classes of celestial and chthonic beings—gods, dragons, celestial musicians, and hungry spirits—who have gathered not as adversaries but as protectors of the Dharma. Known as the Eight Legions (Aṣṭasenā), this motley host bridges the divine and the demonic, the heavenly and the earthly, and their presence underscores the universality of the Buddhist path.

As protectors of the Dharma, they are generally benevolent towards Buddhism, though some (like Asuras) are known for their pride and warlike nature. They are present at Buddha's teachings, listen to the Dharma, and vow to protect it and its followers.

Powers
Dharma protection

The primary function of the Eight Legions is to protect the Buddhist Dharma and its practitioners. They are often depicted as guardians who ward off evil and obstacles.

Limit: They are not omnipotent; their power is subordinate to the Buddha and the Dharma.

Supernatural abilities

Each class possesses its own supernatural abilities: Devas have divine powers and longevity; Nāgas can control weather and shape-shift; Yakṣas have magical powers and can appear or disappear; Gandharvas can produce enchanting music; Asuras have great strength and combat skills; Garuḍas can fly at incredible speeds and are enemies of Nāgas; Kiṁnaras can sing beautifully; Mahoragas have serpentine powers.

Limit: These abilities are often limited by their karma and realm.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Eight Legions are a diverse group of beings. Deva (gods) are often depicted as radiant, celestial beings. Nāgas are serpentine, often half-human, half-snake. Yakṣas are nature spirits, sometimes fierce. Gandharvas are celestial musicians, often depicted as handsome. Asuras are titanic beings, often warlike. Garuḍas are eagle-like birds. Kiṁnaras are part-human, part-bird or part-horse. Mahoragas are great serpents or beings with serpentine lo

Symbols — In art, the Eight Legions are often depicted as a group surrounding the Buddha. Each class has its own iconographic attributes: Devas may hold lotus or fly whisk; Nāgas often have a serpent canopy; Garuḍas have wings and beak; Asuras may have multiple arms and weapons; Gandharvas play musical instruments; Kiṁnaras are often shown with musical instruments or as celestial musicians.

Domains — They inhabit various realms: Devas in the heavens (especially the Cāturmahārājakāyika heaven), Nāgas in oceans and underworlds, Yakṣas on earth and in forests, Gandharvas in the celestial realm of music, Asuras in their own realm (Asura-loka), Garuḍas in the sky, Kiṁnaras in the celestial realms, and Mahoragas in the earth or underworld.

Accounts I
The Assembly at Vulture PeakLotus Sutra

circa 1st century BCE – 2nd century CE

In the Lotus Sutra, the Buddha delivers a sermon at Vulture Peak, and the Eight Legions are among the vast assembly of beings who listen to the teaching. Their presence signifies the universality of the Dharma, reaching all realms of existence. The Eight Legions become witnesses to the Buddha's teachings and are inspired to protect the Dharma.

Accounts II
The Golden Light Sutra's ProtectionGolden Light Sutra

circa 2nd to 5th century CE

The Golden Light Sutra recounts the Buddha's teachings on the importance of protecting the state and the Dharma. The Eight Legions are among the deities who vow to protect the kings and countries that uphold this sutra. The Eight Legions become associated with protective rituals and state protection.
