Who It Is

A red blur tears across Central City, a crackle of yellow lightning trailing behind a cowl that barely contains the static. He is not one man but a lineage — Jay, Barry, Wally, Bart — each borrowing the same name, the same bolt, the same impossible promise: that a human can outrun death itself. The Flash is DC Comics' living thought experiment about speed, time, and legacy.

Version-dependent but attested traits: Jay Garrick — avuncular, elder statesman, Keystone City college student turned elder Justice Society member, steady and mentoring. Barry Allen — forensic scientist, methodical, earnest, often guilt-driven, famously late (ironic), optimistic and self-sacrificing; Rebirth era emphasizes forensic rigor and moral center of Justice League. Wally West — initially impulsive Kid Flash, later matured into humorous, emotionally open, family-oriented Flash; fan-favorite for relatability and fear of replacing Barry. Powers
Superhuman Speed / Speed Force Conduit

Ability to move, think, and react at superhuman velocities, ranging from supersonic to faster-than-light and, with Speed Force, to time travel and interdimensional travel. Includes accelerated healing, enhanced reflexes, and aura that protects from friction, heat, and impact.

Limit: Requires caloric intake (Wally era: must consume thousands of calories); friction aura can be overwhelmed; extreme speed can cause molecular destabilization, aging, or being trapped in Speed Force. Barry initially needed treadmill to control vibration. Speed cannot be sustained indefinitely without rest in early stories.

Phasing / Vibration

Vibrating molecules to pass through solid matter, become invisible/intangible, or disrupt objects. Also used to create sonic booms and to travel between Earths (as in Flash of Two Worlds, vibrating at specific frequency).

Limit: Requires precise control; failure can cause molecular dispersal or explosion. Cannot phase through certain energy fields. Novices (Bart) struggle with control.

Time Travel and Dimensional Travel

By running faster than light or on Cosmic Treadmill, Flash can travel through time and to alternate Earths/timelines. Central to stories like Flashpoint.

Limit: Time travel creates temporal ripples, Flashpoint paradoxes, and is forbidden by Speed Force; often causes memory loss or timeline collapse. Requires immense speed and often treadmill assistance.

Speed Mirage, Afterimages, and Speed Steal/Lend

Creating vortexes, whirlwinds, afterimage decoys, and (Wally era) lending or stealing kinetic energy from others.

Limit: Vortexes can backfire; stealing speed can kill target if overused; lending can deplete Flash.

Accelerated Cognition and Speed Reading / Speed Mind

Thinking at super-speed, reading entire libraries in seconds, processing forensic evidence instantly, and perceiving time in attoseconds.

Limit: Information overload, inability to retain all read material, and psychological isolation — time appears frozen, leading to loneliness. Bart initially could not retain knowledge due to accelerated metabolism.

Weaknesses and Counters

Not invulnerable: needs traction, can be tripped, affected by cold (Captain Cold's gun slows molecular motion), psychic attack, and Speed Force negation. Rogues' weapons specifically exploit these limits.

Limit: Cold field slows speed; frictionless surfaces; time paradoxes; and in CW TV series, nanite or meta-dampening tech.

Accounts I
Origin of Jay Garrick — The Hard Water VaporsFlash Comics #1 (1940)

1940

College student Jay Garrick inhales heavy water vapors after a lab accident during a hard-water experiment, gaining super-speed. He becomes the Flash, dons winged helmet, and fights crime in Keystone City, later joining the Justice Society of America. Story establishes wartime heroics and mentor role. Creates original Flash archetype; establishes legacy concept that will be revived 16 years later. Jay's origin later retconned to Speed Force.

Origin of Barry Allen — Lightning and ChemicalsShowcase #4 (1956)

1956

Police scientist Barry Allen is struck by lightning that shatters chemical shelves in his lab, bathing him in electrified chemicals. He discovers he can run at superhuman speed and becomes the Flash, inspired by his childhood comic hero Jay Garrick (later revealed as real). He protects Central City while working as a forensic analyst. Launches Silver Age of Comics; redefines Flash as science-hero; introduces Central City, Rogues, and Speed Force mythology. Barry's forensic job becomes central to CW TV series.

Flash of Two WorldsThe Flash #123 (1961)

1961

Barry Allen vibrates at a frequency that transports him to Earth-Two, where he meets his idol Jay Garrick, who exists as a comic-book character on Barry's Earth but is real on this parallel Earth. Together they defeat three reformed villains (Fiddler, Shade, Thinker). Story explicitly establishes DC Multiverse. Invents DC Multiverse concept (Earth-One/Earth-Two), enables annual Justice League/Justice Society crossovers, foundational for Crisis on Infinite Earths and all later multiverse stories.

Accounts II
The Death of Barry Allen — Crisis on Infinite EarthsCrisis on Infinite Earths #8 (1985)

1985-1986

During the Anti-Monitor's assault on the multiverse, Barry Allen escapes the Speed Force, learns the Anti-Monitor's cannon will destroy Earths with antimatter, and runs so fast he generates a speed vortex that destroys the cannon but disintegrates him. His death is witnessed by Wally West. Barry becomes legend within the Speed Force. Barry remains dead for 23 years (until Final Crisis/Flash: Rebirth 2008-2009); Wally becomes the main Flash for a generation, cementing legacy theme. Widely cited as one of comics' most impactful heroic sacrifices.

The Return of Barry Allen and Terminal Velocity / Speed Force IntroductionThe Flash vol.2 #74-79 (1993) and #95-100, #110 (1994-1996)

1993-1996

A supposed Barry Allen returns, revealed to be Professor Zoom (Eobard Thawne) manipulating Wally. Wally confronts his fear of replacing Barry and learns to surpass him. Mark Waid then introduces the Speed Force — an extradimensional energy field that is the source of all speedsters — and Wally must rescue his family from it during Terminal Velocity. Wally learns to lend/steal speed. Defines modern Flash mythology; makes Wally the fastest Flash; Speed Force becomes central cosmology for all later writers including Geoff Johns and CW series.

FlashpointFlashpoint #1-5 (2011)

2011

Barry Allen wakes in an altered timeline where his mother Nora never died, but the world is on brink of Atlantean/Amazon war led by Aquaman and Wonder Woman, Thomas Wayne is Batman, and no Flash exists. Barry learns Eobard Thawne prevented his mother's murder being undone, but Barry himself went back to save her, shattering time. To restore reality, Barry must let his mother die again, merging timelines into New 52 continuity. Reboots DC continuity into The New 52 (2011-2016); most famous modern Flash story; adapted into animated film Justice League: The Flashpoint Paradox (2013) and The Flash film (2023). Establishes Barry's trauma over mother's murder as central character engine.

Rebirth — Return and Legacy RestoredThe Flash: Rebirth #1-5 (2009) and DC Rebirth #1 / The Flash vol.5 (2016)

2009-2016

Barry Allen returns from Speed Force after Final Crisis, confronts that Eobard Thawne murdered his mother Nora and framed his father Henry. Later, DC Rebirth reveals Wally West was trapped in Speed Force for a decade (meta-commentary on New 52 erasure) and Barry pulls him out, restoring legacy and acknowledging multiple Flashes coexist. Restores Barry as living Flash while keeping Wally as co-lead; sets up dual-Flash era and Williamson's run; addresses fan demand for legacy restoration.

The Flash is a single character. — The Flash is a legacy mantle held by at least four primary DC characters — Jay Garrick (1940), Barry Allen (1956), Wally West (1986), Bart Allen (2006) — plus others like Avery Ho. Each has distinct origin, personality, and era as main title holder.

Flash's powers come from being struck by lightning alone, or from the TV particle accelerator. — Comic origin is lightning + chemicals (1956) and later Speed Force (1994). The S.T.A.R. Labs particle accelerator explosion is invention of the 2014 CW series, not comics canon. Jay's origin involved hard-water vapors, not lightning at all.

Flash can run at unlimited speed with no consequences. — Comics consistently show costs: massive caloric need, molecular destabilization, being trapped in Speed Force, aging, memory loss, and timeline damage (Flashpoint). Cold-based weapons and friction loss are established counters.

Queen's song 'Flash' is about DC's Flash. — Queen's 1980 single 'Flash' (with lyric 'Flash! Ah-ah, savior of the universe') was written for Mike Hodges' film Flash Gordon (1980), based on Alex Raymond's space hero, unrelated to DC Comics.

Adobe Flash / flash memory are named after the superhero. — Adobe Flash (formerly Macromedia Flash, FutureSplash) is named for 'flash' meaning quick display/animation; flash memory is named for its ability to be erased in a flash (Toshiba, 1984). No connection to DC character beyond common English word.
