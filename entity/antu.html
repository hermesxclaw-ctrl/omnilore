Who It Is

Antu (from the Mapuche word for 'sun') is the solar Pillan — the most powerful of the ancestral volcano spirits who illuminates the world and gives warmth to the land. As a Pillan, Antu is an ancestral being who has ascended to divine status, dwelling in the sun and governing the daylight. He is one of the most important figures in Mapuche spiritual practice: every morning, the Mapuche traditionally greet the rising sun with prayers, and Antu is invoked for health, vitality, and protection from evil spirits. In Mapuche cosmology, Antu is partnered with Kuyen (the moon) and Wanglen (the stars), and together they form a celestial family that regulates the cycles of nature. Antu is also associated with the color blue (kallfü), which is the most sacred color in Mapuche spiritual practice, representing the sky and spiritual power. The machi (shaman) invokes Antu during healing ceremonies, and the rewe (the carved wooden altar/pillar that is the center of Mapuche ceremonial life) faces east toward the rising sun. During the Arauco War — the nearly 350-year conflict between the Mapuche and the Spanish Empire (1536–1883) — Antu was invoked for protection and victory, and the Mapuche's successful resistance was attributed in part to his favor. Contemporary Mapuche spirituality, including the active practice of nguillatún ceremonies, continues to center Antu as a primary spiritual force. Like the sun deities of many indigenous cultures, Antu combines cosmic scale with intimate, daily presence — every dawn is a personal encounter with the divine.
