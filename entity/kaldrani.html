I am the cold that has a name. The lava field is my hunting ground, the glacier's breath is my breath. You will feel warm just before I touch you — that is the lie your body tells as I arrive.

Who It Is

The Kaldrani (literally 'cold snout' or 'cold trunk') is a terrifying monster of Icelandic folk belief that haunts the harsh lava fields and highland deserts. It is described as a shapeless, shaggy creature with a long trunk-like snout that is perpetually frozen — it breathes frost, and its touch causes instant frostbite. The Kaldrani attacks sheep, horses, and lost travellers crossing the highlands, freezing them solid and consuming them while they are still frozen. It is particularly active during the winter months and during the 'hundradagar' (dog days) of deep winter. The Kaldrani moves through the lava fields with an unsettling silence — its frozen body makes no sound against rock — and its presence is often only detected by a sudden, inexplicable drop in temperature and a cloud of visible breath where there should be none. In some traditions, the Kaldrani is the ghost of a man who froze to death in the highlands and whose spirit now inflicts on others the death it suffered. It can be warded off by fire (the Kaldrani fears even a candle flame), by iron, and by reciting specific protective verses (galdrar) in Old Norse. The Kaldrani represents the genuine lethal danger of the Icelandic interior — a volcanic desert where temperatures can kill within hours, where the weather changes without warning, and where 'the cold' itself becomes a personalized, malevolent entity. The creature is a folk personification of hypothermia: the sensation of unnatural warmth that precedes freezing, the silent approach of cold, and the body's ultimate surrender to the landscape.
