The Approach

When the Supreme Kai himself goes pale at a name, you know it is not just another muscle-bound tyrant. Dabura — the horned Demon King whose spit turns heroes to stone and whose sword is conjured from nothing — ruled the entire Demon Realm until a frail wizard whispered a spell and made a king kneel.

Content advisory. Contains fantasy violence, demonic possession/mind control, petrification and murder, including death of a child-coded character (Majin Buu kills Dabura), and depiction of afterlife punishment/reward. No sexual violence attested for this character.
Identity

Dabura

Pronunciation Japanese: /daː.bɯ.ɾa/ (DAH-boo-rah, three morae). English dubs: Funimation /ˈdɑːbrə/ DAH-bruh; also heard as DAH-bra. Japanese voice maintains long middle vowel: Dābura.

Known As

Dābura (ダーブラ)Dabra (official English manga/anime romanization)Maō Dabura (魔王ダーブラ, Demon King Dabura)King of the Demon Realm (Makai no Ō)Majin Dabura (when under Babidi's spell)

Etymology

Name pun on the incantation 'abracadabra' (abura-kadabura in Japanese). Toriyama's Majin-related characters follow magic-spell puns: Bibidi, Babidi, Majin Buu, and Dabura. Confirmed in Dragon Ball Daizenshuu 6 and Toriyama interview commentary on naming. The 'Dabura' spelling reflects Japanese phonology; Viz Media and Funimation use 'Dabra'.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Akira Toriyama (1955-2024) for Weekly Shōnen Jump / Shueisha (Bird Studio). First appearance 1994. Intellectual property held by Shueisha, Toei Animation, and Bird Studio/Shueisha rights holders. No traditional folklore ownership; entirely modern commercial fiction, but draws on Western demonology and Buddhist/Hindu Mara/Maō motifs filtered through Toriyama's design language.

Disputed Identifications

Not to be confused with: (1) Dabura as distinct from Akuma/Devilman-type demons in Dr. Slump crossover; (2) fan conflation with Dabura as 'Makyo' demon (Garlic Jr. continuity) — separate anime-only continuity; (3) misromanization 'Dabula' or 'Darbra' in early scanlations. Daima (2024) introduces new Demon Realm hierarchy (Gomah, Degesu, Dr. Arinsu) that recontextualizes but does not replace Dabura's title; some fan wikis prematurely listed him as deposed by Gomah — canonical status clarified below.

Description

Appearance

Tall, powerfully built humanoid demon, approximately 2.2m, with chalk-red skin, stark white hair swept back, prominent black-rimmed eyes with red irises, pointed ears, and two short ivory horns protruding from forehead. Wears a high-collared dark blue cape with gold clasp, gold bracers, dark tunic with Demon Realm crest, and ornate belt. In manga color pages and anime, his cape has red interior. Facial features are stern, aged but not elderly, with pronounced brow and goatee-like chin hair. Unlike many Dragon Ball villains, he lacks animalistic or insectoid traits; design emphasizes regal demon lord rather than monster. When under Babidi's Majin spell, gains black sclera with white Majin crest 'M' on forehead. Stone Spit manifests as white viscous saliva. Attested in manga ch.446, anime ep.220, Daizenshuu illustrations.

Symbols & Iconography

Symbols: (1) Majin 'M' on forehead when enslaved, signifying Babidi's magical ownership, not loyalty; (2) Demon Realm insignia (pointed ear/horn motif seen on Daima's Demon Realm portal); (3) Conjured weaponry — Darkness Sword / Darkness Materialization — represents demonic creation magic rather than ki blade. Iconography draws on Western Christian demon (horns, cape, red skin) blended with Japanese maō (demon king) archetype and Toriyama's sleek armor design lineage (similar to Cold family). Stone Spit iconography evokes Medusa/basilisk petrification, rare in Dragon Ball where most attacks are concussive.

Behaviour & Personality

Regal, coldly polite, and analytically cruel. Unlike Babidi's other minions (Pui Pui boastful, Yakon bestial), Dabura is composed, strategic, and speaks with courtly formality even while threatening. Supreme Kai describes him as 'the most terrible demon' who ruled the Demon Realm by fear, yet Dabura shows pragmatic deference to stronger power (Babidi, then Buu). He is proud of his status as king but willingly kneels to Babidi after being placed under Majin mind-control — indicating his will was subjugated, not volunteered. Displays sadistic enjoyment in petrifying Piccolo and Krillin, and in toying with Gohan, but also caution: he advises Babidi not to underestimate Saiyans and to awaken Buu only as last resort (manga ch.460). After death, his afterlife personality is notably repentant/peaceful — King Yemma sends him to Heaven rather than Hell because he was controlled, implying underlying honor. Attested: manga ch.446-462; anime ep.222-227.

Domains & Habitats

Primary domain: Makai (Demon Realm / Demon World), a separate dimension from the Living World and Afterlife, described by Supreme Kai as a place where Dabura was absolute ruler (manga ch.446). In Daima retcon, Demon Realm consists of three worlds; Dabura ruled as Dai Maō (Great Demon King) over the whole realm from the First Demon World palace. Secondary habitat: Babidi's spaceship (stages 1-3, located underground on Earth), where he serves as final guardian before Majin Buu's seal. Post-mortem: Other World — specifically Heaven/Paradise, per manga ch.474 and Daizenshuu, an unusual fate for a demon king. No attested Earth residence before Babidi.

Version Differences

Manga (canon baseline): leaner design, shorter fight with Gohan, killed quickly by Buu turned into cookie and eaten. Anime (Toei): extended Gohan vs Dabura fight (adds multiple ki blasts, sword clashes), additional dialogue where Dabura questions Babidi's strategy, slightly more brutal petrification scenes. Dragon Ball Kai trims filler but retains anime pacing. Video games exaggerate his magic arsenal (e.g., Evil Impulse, Hell Blade). Dragon Ball Daima (2024): reframes his kingship historically — he is dead before series starts, succeeded by Gomah; flashbacks show younger Dabura with same design but adds lore that his father was Abura and that Demon Realm kings are appointed, not hereditary. Daizenshuu vs Chōzenshuu: power level listed as 'unknown, comparable to Perfect Cell' in Daizenshuu, later guidebooks avoid numeric power levels. English dub (Funimation) gives him Transylvanian-tinged accent; Japanese voice (Ryūzaburō Ōtomo) is deep, aristocratic.

Powers & Abilities

Stone Spit (Sekika no Tsuba / Stone Spit)

Source VersionManga ch.460-461, anime ep.224, Daizenshuu 7 p.118

Expels saliva that petrifies any target it touches, turning flesh, clothing, and even weapons into stone. Effect is instantaneous on contact; victim remains conscious as statue until shattered. Can be weaponized by spitting onto weapon or directly. Dabura can reverse it at will, but only while alive.

Limitations

Requires saliva to make contact; can be dodged (Trunks dodges, Gohan deflects with armor). If Dabura dies, petrification becomes permanent unless killed with specific magic — in manga, Piccolo and Krillin remain stone after Dabura's death until Buu is killed and their statues are not shattered. Dabura must consciously generate spit; cannot petrify through energy barrier. No range beyond spitting distance.

Contradictions

Anime shows spit turning entire body even from small droplet on sword; manga implies more limited spread. Daima does not reference ability.

Darkness Materialization / Materialization Magic (Bushitsu Busshitsu-ka / Darkness Sword)

Source VersionManga ch.461, Daizenshuu 4 p.158, Xenoverse terminology 'Darkness Sword Attack'

Demonic magic that creates solid matter from nothing — most famously a long, flat-edged sword conjured from air, as well as spear and armor. Distinct from ki constructs; classified as magic (mahō) by Supreme Kai, not ki technique.

Limitations

Requires concentration; sword can be broken by superior ki (Gohan snaps it). Magic weaker than Supreme Kai's but versatile. Cannot create living beings. In Daima, similar magic is common among Demon Realm denizens, reducing its uniqueness.

Contradictions

Viz translation calls it 'Evil Blade'; games call it 'Darkness Sword' or 'Hell Blade' — same ability, different localization.

Evil Flame (Jaen) & Hell Blitz / Evil Impulse

Source VersionManga ch.461 (flame vs Gohan), anime ep.225, Budokai/Xenoverse move lists

Expels massive flame breath or fires multiple concentrated ki/magic blasts. Evil Impulse is a rapid-fire energy barrage used against Gohan. In anime, can generate dark energy waves from sword.

Limitations

Flame can be repelled by ki barrier (Gohan deflects). Energy output below Super Saiyan 2 tier; Supreme Kai notes Dabura's power is roughly equal to Perfect Cell, insufficient against enraged SSJ2 Gohan.

Contradictions

Manga shows single flame burst; games expand into multiple super attacks not attested in manga.

Majin Enhancement & Flight / Ki Manipulation

Source VersionManga ch.446-447, Daizenshuu 7

Under Babidi's Majin spell, latent power drawn out beyond natural limits, granting enhanced strength, speed, durability, flight via ki, and ability to sense ki poorly (relies on Babidi's tracking). Can fire standard ki blasts and survive in space briefly inside spaceship.

Limitations

Majin boost is finite; does not grant regeneration like Buu. Still vulnerable to significantly stronger fighters (Buu kills him effortlessly). Mind controlled — cannot disobey Babidi's direct orders despite tactical disagreement. Power roughly Cell-level, not Buu-level.

Contradictions

Some guidebooks claim Majin boost was minimal because Dabura was already evil; others claim substantial. Manga states Babidi 'drew out his power' (ch.446).

Afterlife Existence & Demon Physiology

Source VersionManga ch.474, Daima ep.1 lore, Chōzenshuu

As Demon Realm native, possesses longevity (hundreds of years), resistance to extreme environments, and retains consciousness after death in Other World. Horns and red skin are biological, not transformation.

Limitations

Not immortal; can be killed by physical trauma (Buu's attack). Demon Realm natives are still mortal and subject to Other World judgment. No regeneration or soul manipulation attested.

Contradictions

Daima suggests Demon Realm natives have pointed ears as racial trait; manga never explicitly states Dabura's age or lifespan.

Stories & Myths

King of the Demon Realm

Source Dragon Ball manga ch.446; Daizenshuu 7; Dragon Ball Daima ep.1-2Culture Japanese manga fiction (Toriyama)Period In-universe: centuries before Age 774; Publication 1994-1995 (manga), 2024 (Daima retcon)

Summary

Long before the Buu crisis, Dabura ruled the Makai (Demon Realm) as its absolute monarch (Dai Maō). Supreme Kai (Shin) describes him to Goku and others as the most powerful demon, whom even the Supreme Kais feared and could not defeat — 'the king who ruled the Demon Realm with terror.' He commanded countless demons from his palace in the Demon Realm's First World. Daima expands this: Dabura's father was King Abura, and Dabura inherited the throne, ruling until his departure to the mortal universe. His reign was characterized by order through fear rather than chaos.

Consequences

Establishes stakes for Babidi's invasion — if Babidi controls Dabura, he controls the Demon Realm's army. Explains why Shin is terrified upon sensing Dabura's ki on Babidi's spaceship. Retconned in Daima to set up succession crisis after his death.

Variants

Manga: brief exposition by Shin. Anime: adds Shin's flashback visual of Dabura's silhouette. Daima: adds political structure — three Demon Worlds, Dabura as Great Demon King, succeeded by Gomah after his death; implies Dabura left realm voluntarily to serve Babidi, leaving throne vacant.

Enslavement by Babidi

Source Dragon Ball manga ch.446-449Culture Japanese mangaPeriod Age 774, weeks before 25th Tenkaichi Budokai; Publication 1994

Summary

The wizard Babidi, son of Bibidi, seeks to revive Majin Buu and needs powerful minions to gather energy. He locates Dabura in the Demon Realm and places him under his Majin mind-control spell (the 'M' crest), unlocking his latent evil power. Unlike Vegeta later, Dabura was already thoroughly evil, so control is complete and willing in effect — he kneels and pledges loyalty. Babidi transports Dabura, Pui Pui, and Yakon to Earth aboard his spaceship, hiding underground to siphon energy from strong fighters at the Tenkaichi Budokai.

He then retreats, allowing Babidi to teleport Goku's group inside the ship for staged battles to collect energy.

Dabura vs. Gohan — The Demon King’s Last Stand as Babidi’s General

Source Dragon Ball manga ch.460-463; anime ep.224-227Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod Age 774, Babidi's spaceship Level 3

Summary

After Pui Pui and Yakon are killed by Vegeta and Goku, Dabura faces Gohan on Babidi's third level. Dabura conjures his Darkness Sword, breathes Evil Flame, and spits toward Gohan, who shatters the sword and deflects flame. The fight is even — Gohan, as Super Saiyan, holds advantage but is rusty from seven years of peace and underestimates Dabura. Supreme Kai notes Dabura is 'about as strong as Cell.' Dabura deliberately goads Gohan, and Babidi uses the damage to collect energy to revive Buu. When Vegeta's pride is wounded by Gohan's struggle, Babidi senses Vegeta's evil heart and abandons Dabura's fight to possess Vegeta instead. Dabura advises against reviving Buu, warning Buu is uncontrollable, but Babidi ignores him.

Death at the Hands of Majin Buu

Source Dragon Ball manga ch.474; anime ep.232-233Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod Age 774, shortly after Buu's revival

Summary

Majin Buu is revived and immediately disobeys Babidi, refusing to kill Supreme Kai and the Saiyans on command. Dabura, disgusted, calls Buu a failure and attempts to assert authority, even trying to kill Buu with a blast. Buu, playful and annoyed, dodges, then turns Dabura into a cookie (or candy in some translations) with his transmutation beam and eats him. In manga, Buu inhales and bites; anime shows cookie transformation explicitly. Babidi is shocked but quickly placated when Buu obeys next command to kill. Dabura's petrification victims remain stone after his death.

Afterlife Judgment — The Demon King Sent to Heaven

Source Dragon Ball manga ch.474 (King Yemma cameo), Daizenshuu 7, Dragon Ball Z anime ep.233 fillerCulture Japanese manga/animePeriod Immediately after death, Age 774

Summary

In Other World, King Yemma (Enma Daio) judges Dabura. Noting that Dabura was under Babidi's mind control and thus not fully responsible for his Earth crimes, and that his time as Demon King, while cruel, was within Demon Realm law, Yemma sends him to Heaven (Tengoku) rather than Hell (Jigoku) — an unexpectedly merciful fate. Anime filler shows Dabura in Heaven, now docile, picking flowers and repenting, to Goku's surprise. This gag underscores Toriyama's theme that even demon kings can be rehabilitated when freed from evil influence.

Relationships
Dabura→Babidicreator (master/slave)

Wizard who placed Dabura under Majin mind-control spell, branding him with 'M' and drawing out his power. Dabura serves as Babidi's general and strongest fighter, though he privately doubts Babidi's plan to revive Buu. Relationship is coercive magical enslavement; Dabura cannot disobey direct orders despite superior physical power. Ends when Buu kills Dabura, freeing Babidi from his counsel.

Dabura→Majin Buu (Innocent Buu)ally then victim (killer)

Dabura helped revive Buu by providing energy via fights, then was killed by Buu seconds after revival. Dabura viewed Buu as uncontrollable weapon and tried to assert dominance; Buu viewed Dabura as annoying toy, turned him into a cookie and ate him. No prior personal history; Bibidi created Buu long before Dabura's birth.

Dabura→Son Gohanhostile (opponent)

Primary combat opponent. Dabura fought Gohan on Babidi's ship, stalemating Super Saiyan Gohan and exploiting Gohan's rustiness. Dabura used Stone Spit, sword, and flame; Gohan broke his sword and survived. Fight provided energy to revive Buu. Gohan later witnesses Dabura's death indirectly.

Dabura→Supreme Kai (Shin)hostile (ancient enemy)

Supreme Kai feared Dabura for centuries as Demon King who even Kais could not defeat. Shin provides exposition on Dabura's terror and recognizes his ki instantly. Dabura, in turn, recognizes Shin as a Kai and mocks his fear. Their enmity represents cosmic order (Kai) vs Demon Realm (Maō).

Dabura→Piccolo and Krillinvictim

Both turned to stone by Dabura's Stone Spit during ambush at Babidi's spaceship. Piccolo (in anime) tears off petrified arm and regenerates; Krillin remains fully petrified until after Buu's defeat when Dabura's death does not reverse effect but Dragon Balls later restore them. Demonstrates Dabura's hax ability bypassing power levels.

Dabura→Gomah, Degesu, Dr. Arinsu (Daima Demon Realm successors)political successor/rival

Introduced in Dragon Ball Daima (2024) as new Demon Realm leadership after Dabura's death. Gomah succeeds Dabura as Great Demon King, Degesu is vice-king, Arinsu is Dabura's sister (retcon). Dabura never interacts with them on-screen (already dead), but his death creates power vacuum they fill. Arinsu seeks to use Dabura's legacy.

Dabura→King Yemma (Enma Daio)judge (afterlife authority)

Judge of the dead who sends Dabura to Heaven instead of Hell, reasoning Dabura's Earth crimes were under Babidi's control. This merciful judgment surprises even Goku's group and provides Dabura a peaceful afterlife, unlike Frieza or Cell.

Demonic Enslavement and Mind Control

Source Manga ch.446-447; Daizenshuu 7 p.84

Description

Babidi's Majin spell completely subjugates Dabura's will, branded with 'M' on forehead. While Dabura was already evil, the spell removes autonomy — he kneels, obeys orders to kill, and cannot resist even when he disagrees strategically. Represents magical slavery and loss of agency. Supreme Kai notes the spell draws out hidden evil power, implying consent is manufactured.

Scholarly Context

Critics note Toriyama uses Majin control as metaphor for radicalization; Dabura contrasts with Vegeta, whose pride allows him to retain partial control. No sexual coercion attested for Dabura.

Warning: Murder and Petrification — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Murder and Petrification

Source Manga ch.448-449, 460-461

Description

Dabura murders Kibito (Supreme Kai's attendant) with a ki blast through the chest, and petrifies Krillin and Piccolo, leaving them as living statues. He also attempts to kill Gohan, Trunks, and Supreme Kai. Petrification is depicted as body horror — victims frozen mid-scream, conscious but immobile. In anime, Piccolo shatters his own stone arm to survive, showing self-mutilation.

Scholarly Context

Petrification as Medusa-like punishment; Toriyama rarely uses status-effect horror elsewhere, making Dabura unique. Violence is not sexualized or gratuitous beyond shonen battle norms.

Warning: Tyrannical Rule of Demon Realm — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Tyrannical Rule of Demon Realm

Source Manga ch.446; Daima ep.1

Description

Exposition states Dabura 'ruled the Demon Realm with terror' for centuries, implying widespread oppression, though no specific atrocities are detailed on-panel. Supreme Kai's fear suggests Dabura's rule involved conquest and fear among both demons and Kais. Daima expands that his rule was absolute monarchy over three worlds.

Scholarly Context

No detailed depiction of his rule's cruelty; left to implication. Modern reception notes this as thin worldbuilding later fleshed out by Daima, avoiding glorification of authoritarianism.

Cannibalistic Death (Turned into Food)

Source Manga ch.474; anime ep.233

Description

Majin Buu turns Dabura into a cookie/candy and eats him, depicted with dark comedy. While not self-inflicted, it is a disturbing, dehumanizing death — a sentient being consumed as confection. Anime shows Dabura's face on cookie before being bitten.

Contradictions & Disputes

Power Level Relative to Cell and Gohan

Position A

Dabura is equal to Perfect Cell (guidebook/manga statement)

Position A — evidence

Supreme Kai says 'Dabura is about as strong as Cell' (manga ch.460); Goku agrees 'maybe a bit stronger than we thought but not that much'; Daizenshuu 7 lists 'strength comparable to Cell'. Gohan as SSJ1 stalemates him, suggesting Cell-level.

Position B

Dabura is stronger than Perfect Cell, near Super Perfect Cell / SSJ2 level (anime scaling)

Position B — evidence

Anime extends fight where Dabura pressures SSJ Gohan and forces Gohan to consider SSJ2; video games give him power level 3,000,000,000+; some fans argue Shin's fear implies Dabura > Cell since Shin feared Dabura but not Cell (though Shin never met Cell).

Scholarly Consensus

Most reliable sources (manga + Daizenshuu) support Perfect Cell equivalence. Anime inflation is filler. Kanzenshuu and Chōzenshuu treat Supreme Kai's statement as authoritative; power scaling communities remain split but guidebook consensus is Cell-level.

Nature of Demon Realm and Dabura's Kingship

Position A

Original manga: Demon Realm is vague other dimension where Dabura is sole king by power

Position A — evidence

Manga ch.446: Shin says 'Dabura was king of the Demon Realm' with no detail; Daizenshuu describes Makai as separate dimension with little structure.

Position B

Daima retcon: Demon Realm is structured three-world polity with hereditary/political succession, Dabura as Dai Maō succeeded by Gomah

Position B — evidence

Daima ep.1-2 (Toriyama story): Demon Realm has First, Second, Third Worlds, a palace, a succession system, and Dabura's father Abura; Gomah becomes king after Dabura's death via Degesu's manipulation.

Scholarly Consensus

Daima is Toriyama's final story and considered canonical by Shueisha/Toei, thus retcon supersedes vague original. Some fans treat Daima as additive rather than contradictory; academic fan discourse (Kanzenshuu) accepts Daima as fleshing out previously underdeveloped lore.

Can Stone Spit Be Reversed After Death?

Position A

Petrification is permanent after Dabura's death (manga evidence)

Position A — evidence

Manga ch.474 onward: Krillin and Piccolo remain stone long after Dabura is eaten; they are only restored after Buu is killed and later wishes — not automatically. Anime confirms statues remain.

Position B

Petrification reverses when Dabura dies (game/mechanics assumption)

Position B — evidence

Several games (Budokai, Xenoverse) code Stone Spit as temporary stun that wears off, implying reversal; some early English fan summaries claimed death breaks spell.

Scholarly Consensus

Canon manga/anime clearly show petrification persists after death; game mechanics are non-canonical simplification. Daizenshuu 7 notes Dabura can voluntarily reverse it while alive, but does not state death reverses it.
