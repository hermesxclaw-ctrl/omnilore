Who It Is

Grettir Ásmundarson is the protagonist of Grettis saga, one of the last and greatest of the Sagas of Icelanders, and the most famous outlaw in Icelandic literature. Grettir is the physically strongest man who has ever lived in Iceland — but his strength is also his curse. He is quick-tempered, arrogant, unlucky, and fundamentally unable to fit into the webs of kinship and law that structure Icelandic society. His saga is structured as a series of monster-slayings and misadventures: he fights and kills the undead shepherd Glámr, who curses him with a fear of darkness (myrkfælni) that will haunt him to his death; he defeats a bear in Norway; he kills the berserker Snækollr; he retrieves treasure from the barrow of the draugr Kárr inn gamli (a classic barrow-breaking episode). But each triumph is followed by misfortune. Grettir is outlawed for a killing committed under ambiguous circumstances and spends nineteen years as a hunted outlaw — the longest outlawry in saga literature. He takes refuge on Drangey, an isolated island of sheer cliffs off the north coast of Iceland, where he lives with his brother Illugi and a slave named Glaumr. His enemies, led by Þorbjörn Öngull, use sorcery to create a log that floats to Drangey and becomes the weapon that wounds Grettir fatally (his axe glances off the enchanted wood and cuts his own leg, causing an infection). He dies fighting on Drangey, and his brother Þorsteinn later avenges him in Constantinople. Grettir is the archetypal tragic hero of the sagas: immensely gifted but fated to destruction, unable to navigate the social world, whose strength isolates him rather than glorifying him. His struggles against monsters (Glámr, the bear, the barrow-dweller) parallel the Beowulf story, and scholars debate whether Grettis saga was influenced by the Old English epic or whether both drew on a common Scandinavian monster-slaying tradition.
