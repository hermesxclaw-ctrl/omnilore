Who It Is

Amaterasu is the supreme sun goddess of the Shinto pantheon, the most revered kami in Japanese religion, and the mythical ancestor of the Imperial House of Japan. She is the daughter of Izanagi, born from his left eye when he washed his face after returning from the underworld. She rules over Takamagahara (the High Plain of Heaven) and is the central figure in the Japanese creation myth. Her most famous story is her withdrawal into the Heavenly Rock Cave (Ama-no-Iwato) after a violent argument with her brother Susanoo-no-Mikoto. Her withdrawal plunged the world into darkness and chaos. The other kami lured her out with a mirror, sacred jewels, and the uproarious laughter of the goddess Ame-no-Uzume, who danced wildly. When Amaterasu peeked out to see what was happening, she saw her own reflection in the mirror (which she had never seen before) and was drawn forth, restoring light to the world. The sun goddess is enshrined at the Grand Shrine of Ise, the holiest site in Shinto, and her sacred mirror (Yata-no-Kagami) is one of the three Imperial Regalia of Japan. The Emperor of Japan is traditionally considered her direct descendant, establishing the divine legitimacy of the imperial line.

Origins

Amaterasu was born from Izanagi's left eye during his purification after visiting the underworld. She was given dominion over the heavens. Her brother Susanoo was given the sea but refused his domain, causing chaos on both earth and heaven, leading to the rock cave incident.
