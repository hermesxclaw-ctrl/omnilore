Who It Is

From the cave of heaven to the imperial throne of Japan, Amaterasu-Ōmikami, the sun goddess, is not merely a deity of light but the radiant heart of Shinto, whose withdrawal plunged the world into darkness and whose return restored order—a myth that has shaped an entire nation's identity for over a millennium.

In the myths, Amaterasu is portrayed as wise, benevolent, and sometimes reclusive. She is deeply hurt by her brother Susanoo's destructive behavior and retreats into a cave, causing cosmic darkness. She is also decisive, as when she uses a mirror to lure herself out. She takes a central role in the divine council and establishes the imperial lineage by sending her grandson Ninigi to earth.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Amaterasu' is commonly interpreted as 'shining in heaven' (from ama 'heaven' + terasu 'to shine'), but alternative etymologies include 'shining from the sky' or 'heavenly shining one'. The suffix '-Ōmikami' means 'great august deity'.

Pronunciation — ah-mah-teh-rah-soo oh-mee-kah-mee

Aliases — Amaterasu, Ōhirume-no-muchi, Amaterasu-Ōmikami, Amaterasu-ōmikami, Amaterasu-omikami, Amaterasu-no-Ōkami

Ownership — Japan; central deity of Shinto, ancestral goddess of the Imperial family.

Powers
Solar radiance

Amaterasu is the goddess of the sun; her presence brings light and warmth to the world. Her withdrawal causes total darkness and chaos.

Limit: She can be temporarily obscured (as in the cave myth) but not defeated.

Divine authority

As the ruler of Takamagahara, she commands the other kami and makes decisions that affect the cosmos and the earthly realm.

Limit: She can be defied by powerful kami like Susanoo, but ultimately her will prevails.

Weaving

In one myth, she is weaving in the sacred weaving hall when Susanoo causes disruption by throwing a flayed horse into the hall.

Limit: Not a supernatural power per se, but a divine activity.

Blessing of imperial lineage

She bestows the imperial regalia (mirror, jewel, sword) upon her grandson Ninigi, granting divine legitimacy to the imperial family.

Limit: Only through the imperial line; the regalia are physical objects.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Amaterasu is rarely described physically in the myths. She is associated with the sun and light; in art she is often depicted as a beautiful woman, sometimes emerging from the cave, radiating light. She may be shown with a mirror (Yata no Kagami) or jewel (Yasakani no Magatama). In imperial regalia, she is symbolized by the mirror.

Symbols — Sun disk, mirror (Yata no Kagami), jewel (Yasakani no Magatama), and the sacred sword (Kusanagi no Tsurugi) are associated with her. The mirror is enshrined at Ise as her sacred symbol. She is often depicted with a radiant halo or as a sun.

Domains — Amaterasu rules the Takamagahara (High Plain of Heaven). She is the goddess of the sun and light, and by extension, of the imperial family. Her primary earthly shrine is the Grand Shrine of Ise (Naikū), where she is enshrined as the supreme kami.

Accounts I
The Cave Hiding (Ama-no-Iwato)Kojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (mythical)

After Susanoo's violent rampage, including flaying a horse and throwing it into Amaterasu's weaving hall, she retreats into a cave (Ama-no-Iwato) in grief. The world falls into darkness and chaos. The other kami devise a plan to lure her out: they gather a cockerel, make a mirror, and have the goddess Ame-no-Uzume perform a lewd dance on an overturned tub. The kami laugh at the spectacle, and curious, Amaterasu peeks out. She sees her own reflection in the mirror and is dazzled; the kami pull her out and seal the cave, restoring light. Light returns to the world; Susanoo is punished (fined and banished). The mirror becomes a sacred object and is later enshrined at Ise.

Accounts II
The Susanoo ConflictKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods

Susanoo, the storm god, asks to visit his mother in the underworld. Before leaving, he goes to Takamagahara to see Amaterasu. She suspects his intentions and challenges him to a test of divine offspring. They chew and spit out objects, and from the resulting children, they judge who is truthful. Susanoo is proven to have a pure heart, but then he goes on a rampage, destroying rice fields, defecating in the palace, and flaying a horse. Amaterasu hides in the cave. Susanoo is banished from heaven and descends to Izumo, where he later slays the serpent Yamata-no-Orochi.

The Descent of NinigiKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods

Amaterasu decides to send her grandson Ninigi to rule the earthly realm (Ashihara no Nakatsukuni). She gives him the three sacred treasures: the mirror, the jewel, and the sword. She tells him that the mirror is her spirit and to worship it as herself. Ninigi descends to Mount Takachiho in Kyushu and establishes the imperial line. The imperial family of Japan claims direct descent from Amaterasu, legitimizing their rule. The regalia become symbols of imperial authority.

Amaterasu is the creator of the universe. — In Shinto, the universe already existed, and Amaterasu is a kami (deity) born from Izanagi, not a creator god in the monotheistic sense.

Amaterasu is the equivalent of the Greek goddess Athena. — While both are goddesses associated with wisdom and weaving, they belong to different cultural contexts and have distinct myths.

Amaterasu is always depicted as a woman. — In some artistic representations, Amaterasu may be depicted androgynously or as a sun disk; gender is not always emphasized.
