Who It Is

In the depths of the Slavic mythological landscape lies Veles, a divine entity embodying both the richness of life and the shadows of chaos. As the Serpent of the Roots, he coils around the foundations of existence, a figure whose duality pervades the cultural consciousness of Eastern Europe. His tales intertwine with the fates of herdsmen, merchants, and poets, revealing a god whose influence stretches from the pastoral to the perilous.

Veles is characterized as chaotic, mischievous, and cunning. He embodies the unpredictability of nature and is both a protector and a trickster, often testing the loyalty and faith of those who invoke him. His dual nature allows him to be a benefactor to herdsmen and a source of strife for those who would betray him.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Veles' is believed to derive from the Proto-Slavic *vьles, which relates to cattle and wealth, emphasizing his role as a god of abundance. The term is linked to the Indo-European root *wel-, meaning 'to be strong' or 'to thrive'.

Pronunciation — ['vɛ.lɛs']

Aliases — Volos, The Cattle God (Skotiy Bog), The Serpent of the Roots

Ownership — Veles is predominantly recognized within Slavic mythology, encompassing various regional interpretations and adaptations across Eastern Europe, particularly among the Poles, Russia

Powers
Shape-shifting

Veles can transform into various animals, particularly serpents and cattle, allowing him to interact with both the mortal and spiritual realms.

Limit: Shape-shifting is limited by the presence of oaths and the binding nature of promises made to Perun.

Control over livestock

As the god of cattle, Veles has the power to bless herds and influence their fertility and health.

Limit: His blessings may be withdrawn if transgressions against him occur, leading to disease or misfortune among livestock.

Magical abilities

Veles is associated with magic, particularly in the context of oaths and commerce, granting him the ability to influence trade and relationships.

Limit: His powers are often countered by Perun's authority, emphasizing a balance between chaos and order.

Protection of wealth

Veles is invoked for prosperity and financial success, particularly in trade and agriculture.

Limit: Invocation requires rituals and offerings; failure to adhere to these can lead to financial ruin instead of prosperity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Veles is often depicted as a large, serpentine figure, coiled among the roots of the World Tree, sometimes with features of a goat or a dragon. He is associated with dark waters and is said to possess an otherworldly beauty that captivates those who encounter him.

Symbols — Common symbols associated with Veles include cattle, serpents, and various representations of water, reflecting his dominion over livestock and the underworld. His imagery often contrasts with that of Perun, who is symbolized by the oak and thunder.

Domains — Veles is primarily associated with the watery underworld Nav and the roots of the World Tree, where he governs wealth, commerce, and the dead. His influence extends to herdsmen, merchants, and those who practice magic and music, positioning him as a guardian of the arts and the divine balance of nature.

Accounts I
The Duel with PerunPrimary Chronicle

12th century

Veles and Perun engage in a cosmic struggle, symbolizing the eternal conflict between order and chaos. This narrative illustrates their opposing natures and highlights the necessity of both deities in the Slavic pantheon. The outcome of their duel establishes the seasonal cycles and the balance of nature, reflecting the agricultural rhythms of Slavic societies.

The Cattle God and His HerdsmenFolk tales

19th century

Stories recount how Veles aids herdsmen in times of need, providing guidance and protection against predators. These tales reinforce the cultural importance of cattle in Slavic society and Veles's role as a guardian of wealth.

Accounts II
Boyan the BardTale of Igor's Campaign

12th century

Boyan, a bard, invokes Veles during his performances, emphasizing the god's connection to music and storytelling. This invokes Veles's favor, enhancing the bard's abilities and ensuring the success of his narratives.

The Oath of the RusHistorical records

12th century

Oaths during treaties invoked both Veles and Perun, showcasing their roles in societal law and order. The dual invocation established a framework for legal and social contracts among the Rus, symbolizing balance and mutual respect.
