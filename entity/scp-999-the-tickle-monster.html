Who It Is

A large, gelatinous, orange blob-like creature that is universally friendly. SCP-999 smells pleasant (reminiscent of chocolate, fresh laundry, and whatever the observer finds comforting), makes happy gurgling sounds, and loves to 'tickle' people, which induces an overwhelming feeling of happiness and well-being. Contact with SCP-999 has demonstrated the ability to cure depression, PTSD, and even temporarily calm SCP-682.

Form

A roughly 54-kilogram mass of orange, translucent slime that smells like chocolate. It has a consistency similar to peanut butter and can reshape itself into simple forms. Two large 'eyes' and a wide 'mouth' are visible on its anterior surface.

The Approach

In a universe built on nightmares—plague gods, immortal reptiles, and rituals too cruel to name—SCP-999 is a bright orange interruption. It slithers toward you like an overexcited puppy, wraps you in warm, squishy slime, and suddenly the weight you’ve been carrying for years feels lighter. Some stories say this playful "Tickle Monster" is the seventh child of a cosmic horror called the Scarlet King, destined to undo his reign not with fire or steel, but with laughter and kindness. Whether you treat that as prophecy or metaphor, SCP-999 sits at a strange crossroads in internet horror: a living antidepressant in a world designed to terrify.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses fictional but intense material, including graphic violence, mass casualties, ritual abuse, and cosmic horror themes. While SCP-999 itself is portrayed as gentle and benevolent, its extended lore connects to narratives involving Procedure 110-Montauk, the Scarlet King, and SCP-682, which feature coercion, suffering, and death. Readers sensitive to such topics should approach the Scarlet King and SCP-231 mythos with caution.

Etymology

"SCP" is the standard designation used by the SCP Foundation collaborative fiction project for anomalous entities and objects; "999" is its numerical item designation within that catalog. The nickname "The Tickle Monster" derives from its characteristic behavior of enveloping and tickling people, while "The Vibrant Slime" appears in later lore (e.g., SCP-6001) emphasizing its bright coloration and benevolent nature.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-999 is a creature from the SCP Foundation shared-universe internet fiction project. The SCP Foundation is a collaborative, primarily English-language, online community whose works are published under Creative Commons licenses. There is no single canonical author or owner; instead, SCP-999’s identity and lore emerge from multiple contributors on the SCP Foundation wiki and derivative fan wikis, articles, and tales.

Disputed Identifications

Within SCP lore, SCP-999’s fundamental nature and origin are disputed. Some tales identify it as the seventh child of the cosmic entity known as the Scarlet King and the offspring of SCP-231-7, positioning it as a prophesied counterforce to the King. Other materials treat its origin as unknown or deliberately obscured. Its containment class is also variably described as Safe or Thaumiel in different stories, reflecting competing interpretations of its role as a weapon or therapeutic entity rather than a simple anomaly.

Description

Appearance

Attested: SCP-999 is described as a large, amorphous, gelatinous mass of translucent orange slime weighing approximately 54 kg (120 lbs), with a consistency similar to peanut butter. When at rest, it typically forms a rounded, oblate dome roughly 2 meters wide and 1 meter high. Its outer surface is a thin, transparent, elastic membrane about 0.5 cm thick, capable of flattening portions of its body to around 2 cm in thickness. It has no visible eyes or conventional facial features, but forms pseudopods to interact with people. Sensory qualities include a soft, squishy texture and a pleasant warmth, accompanied by individualized pleasing odors (e.g., chocolate, fresh laundry, bacon, roses, Play-Doh). Reconstructed: Some fan art and derivative descriptions depict it with cartoonish eyes or exaggerated facial expressions, but these are artistic additions rather than canonical textual features.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: SCP-999’s primary iconography is its bright orange, amorphous slime body and playful, hugging pseudopods. It is often visually associated with hearts, smiles, and pastel or candy-themed motifs in fan art, emphasizing its benevolent nature. In broader SCP mythos, it is symbolically linked to hope, healing, and resistance to cosmic horror, especially in tales where it opposes the Scarlet King. Adaptation-specific: In some derivative works, SCP-999 is stylized as a mascot-like character, sometimes with simplified round eyes or a cartoon mouth, to emphasize its friendliness and accessibility.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-999’s temperament is consistently described as playful, affectionate, and dog-like. When approached, it reacts with overwhelming elation, slithering toward the nearest person, leaping onto them, and "hugging" them with pseudopods while nuzzling their face and emitting high-pitched gurgling and cooing noises. Its favorite activity is "tickle-wrestling," in which it envelops a person from the neck down and tickles them until asked to stop (though it may delay stopping, reflecting its exuberance). It shows remorse when it accidentally causes minor injuries, contracting into a quivering mound and gurgling like a whimpering dog. It is strongly drawn to individuals who are unhappy or traumatized, and repeated interactions have been reported to improve their outlook on life. Reconstructed: Later sources emphasize its protective instincts, noting that it has risked itself to shield personnel from harm and assists during containment breaches, suggesting a moral orientation toward care and rescue.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-999 is contained within an SCP Foundation facility, housed in a simple pen that must be kept clean and stocked with food (primarily candy and sweets such as M&M’s and Necco wafers). It is allowed to roam the facility freely during daytime, interacting with personnel during breaks, but must remain in its pen at night or during emergency lockdowns for safety. Its "domain" is thus the interior of Foundation sites, particularly common areas where staff can visit it. Reconstructed: In mythic interpretations, SCP-999’s domain extends metaphorically to the emotional states of those it touches, functioning as a kind of ambulatory sanctuary or therapeutic presence within a hostile, horror-filled universe.

Version Differences

Attested vs adaptation-specific: The core SCP Foundation article presents SCP-999 as a Safe-class entity with unknown origin, focused on emotional euphoria and therapeutic potential. Fan wikis and later lore (e.g., SCP Database, Grokipedia, WikiHow) incorporate additional details: its classification as Thaumiel in certain tales, its familial ties to the Scarlet King and SCP-231-7, and its role as a prophesied weapon against cosmic evil. Some sources assign it a male gender, while the original article does not specify sex or gender. These differences reflect the SCP universe’s non-canonical, multi-author nature rather than strict contradictions in a single authoritative text.

Powers & Abilities

Euphoria induction

Source Version Primary SCP Foundation article; summarized and elaborated in Grokipedia and WikiHow.

Description

Attested: Physical contact with SCP-999’s surface causes immediate mild euphoria in human subjects, which intensifies with prolonged exposure and persists long after separation. This effect is accompanied by individualized pleasant odors and a sense of comfort and safety. Repeated interactions have been reported to produce sustained improvements in mood and outlook, including in individuals with depression or PTSD.

Limitations

Attested: The euphoria is temporary and can be removed by ending contact; long-term therapeutic effects are reported but not quantified. It does not forcibly control behavior beyond mood elevation, and subjects retain agency. The Foundation’s attempts to develop antidepressants from SCP-999’s slime are experimental and not fully realized in the lore.

Contradictions

Some sources emphasize near-miraculous cures of mental trauma, while others present the effect as strong but not absolute. This reflects narrative variation rather than explicit disagreement in a single text.

Emotional healing and trauma mitigation

Source Version SCP Foundation article (reports of improved outlook); Grokipedia and WikiHow (emphasis on emotional healing).

Description

Reconstructed from attested reports: Individuals suffering from severe depression or PTSD have reported a far more positive outlook on life after multiple interactions with SCP-999. Its presence appears to reduce intrusive thoughts, anxiety, and despair, functioning as a kind of living, anomalous therapy.

Limitations

The healing is described qualitatively, not clinically; there is no standardized protocol or guarantee of permanent cure. Effects may depend on repeated contact and the subject’s willingness to engage.

Contradictions

No explicit contradictions, but the degree of healing varies across narratives, with some tales suggesting near-total recovery and others implying partial relief.

Shape-shifting and malleability

Source Version SCP Foundation article; Grokipedia; WikiHow.

Description

Attested: SCP-999 can change its size and shape at will, flattening portions of its body to as thin as 2 cm and forming pseudopods for locomotion and interaction. It can envelop individuals from the neck down, conforming to their body shape for tickling or hugging.

Limitations

Its mass remains roughly constant (around 54 kg), so it cannot expand indefinitely. It appears limited to non-destructive, soft forms and does not exhibit offensive morphologies like blades or rigid structures.

Contradictions

No major contradictions; all sources agree on its malleable, slime-like nature.

Pleasant odor generation

Source Version SCP Foundation article; Grokipedia; WikiHow.

Description

Attested: SCP-999’s surface emits a pleasing odor that differs with each individual, often matching their personal preferences (e.g., chocolate, roses, fresh laundry, bacon, Play-Doh). This odor accompanies its euphoria-inducing contact and may contribute to its emotional effects.

Limitations

The odor is non-harmful and appears limited to benign scents. There is no evidence of it producing toxic or coercive smells.

Contradictions

None noted; the individualized nature of the odor is consistently reported.

Protective behavior and risk-taking

Source Version Grokipedia (bullet interception and breach assistance); SCP Database (SCP-682 test narrative).

Description

Reconstructed from attested narratives: SCP-999 has demonstrated protective instincts, such as intercepting a bullet to shield personnel and assisting during containment breaches by guiding people to safety. It shows no fear even when interacting with highly dangerous entities like SCP-682, and continues to seek playful contact despite prior harm.

Limitations

Its physical resilience is not fully defined; while it can survive crushing and other impacts, it is not portrayed as invulnerable. Its protective actions are opportunistic rather than systematically coordinated.

Contradictions

Some sources focus primarily on its therapeutic role and do not mention combat or protection, but this is omission rather than contradiction.

Pacification of hostile entities

Source Version SCP Foundation cross-test log (referenced in fan wikis); SCP Database; Grokipedia; WikiHow.

Description

Attested in cross-test lore: During a test with SCP-682, SCP-999’s tickling and euphoria-inducing contact caused SCP-682—an extremely hostile, omnicidal entity—to laugh uncontrollably and temporarily fall asleep, effectively pacifying it. This suggests SCP-999’s abilities can affect even powerful, resistant beings.

Limitations

The pacification was temporary; once SCP-999 was removed, SCP-682 recovered, breached containment, and killed numerous personnel. SCP-999’s effect did not permanently alter SCP-682’s nature or eliminate its threat.

Contradictions

Some interpretations frame SCP-999 as the only thing that can truly defeat the Scarlet King or SCP-682, while the original test narrative emphasizes only temporary pacification. This tension reflects aspirational mythic readings versus textual evidence.

Dietary absorption and digestion

Source Version SCP Foundation article; Grokipedia.

Description

Attested: SCP-999 consumes candy and sweets, particularly M&M’s and Necco wafers, by absorbing them into its slime body in an amoeba-like manner. It can digest organic materials with ease, though it is not portrayed as predatory.

Limitations

Its diet is restricted to sweets by containment procedures; there is no evidence of it needing or preferring other foods. Its digestive capacity is not weaponized in canonical descriptions.

Contradictions

None significant; all sources agree on its candy-based diet.

Potential Thaumiel-class strategic role

Source Version SCP Foundation Wikia (Fandom) notes; SCP Database (Possible origins); Grokipedia (Origins and Theories); WikiHow (origin section).

Description

Reconstructed: In certain tales (e.g., "New Job" and related Scarlet King narratives), SCP-999 is classified as Thaumiel, a containment class reserved for anomalies that help contain or neutralize other anomalies. In this framing, SCP-999 is the prophesied seventh child of the Scarlet King, destined to defeat or counteract him through its kindness and emotional power.

Limitations

This role is narrative and prophetic rather than empirically demonstrated. SCP-999 has not, in existing stories, conclusively defeated the Scarlet King; its potential remains speculative.

Contradictions

The primary SCP article does not mention the Scarlet King or Thaumiel classification, leading to a divergence between "core" description and later mythic expansions.

Stories & Myths

Core containment narrative of SCP-999

Source SCP Foundation main article "SCP-999"Culture Internet collaborative fiction (SCP Foundation community)Period Late 2000s onward

Summary

The foundational narrative presents SCP-999 as a Safe-class anomalous entity: a large, orange, gelatinous mass that induces euphoria and emotional healing in those who touch it. The SCP Foundation contains it in a simple pen, allows it to roam the facility during daytime, and encourages personnel to interact with it. The article details its physical properties, behavior, diet, and anomalous effects, emphasizing its playful, dog-like temperament and its beneficial impact on traumatized individuals. Addenda describe experiments with its slime and its potential use in developing antidepressants.

Participants

SCP-999

SCP Foundation personnel (researchers, guards, staff)

Consequences

SCP-999 becomes one of the most beloved anomalies within the Foundation, serving as a morale booster and potential therapeutic resource. Its existence introduces a rare note of kindness and hope into the otherwise grim SCP universe.

Variants

Fan wikis and secondary sources condense this narrative into laconic summaries, sometimes adding details about its intelligence, protective actions, and interactions with other SCPs.

Cross-test with SCP-682

Source SCP Foundation cross-test log (referenced in SCP Database and Grokipedia)Culture Internet collaborative fiction (SCP Foundation community)Period 2010s

Summary

In a cross-test, SCP-999 is introduced into SCP-682’s containment cell to attempt to neutralize or pacify the notoriously hostile, regenerating reptilian entity. SCP-682 initially crushes SCP-999 underfoot, but SCP-999 responds by climbing onto SCP-682 and tickling it. SCP-682 begins laughing uncontrollably, eventually falling asleep. When SCP-999 is removed, SCP-682 awakens, emits a psychic wave that causes nearby personnel to laugh uncontrollably, breaches containment, and kills many people. During the chaos, SCP-999 rescues as many individuals as it can, guiding them to safety. Despite the violence, SCP-999 expresses a desire to play with SCP-682 again, showing no fear.

Participants

SCP-999

SCP-682

SCP Foundation researchers and security personnel

Consequences

The test demonstrates SCP-999’s ability to temporarily pacify even extremely hostile anomalies, but also reveals the limits of its power: SCP-682 remains dangerous and ultimately causes mass casualties. SCP-999’s protective actions reinforce its reputation as benevolent and courageous.

Variants

Different retellings emphasize either the comedic absurdity of SCP-682’s laughter or the tragic aftermath of the breach. Some fan interpretations overstate the test as proof that SCP-999 can permanently defeat SCP-682, which is not supported by the original narrative.

"New Job" and the Scarlet King prophecy

Source SCP Foundation tales (e.g., "New Job") referenced in SCP Foundation Wikia and SCP DatabaseCulture Internet collaborative fiction (SCP Foundation community)Period 2010s–2020s

Summary

In the tale "New Job" and related narratives, SCP-999 is recontextualized within the Scarlet King mythos. SCP-231-7, the seventh "bride" of the Scarlet King, is subjected to Procedure 110-Montauk to prevent the birth of a prophesied child. Daevite tablets are discovered predicting that the seventh child would defeat the King. The Foundation eventually allows the child—SCP-999—to be born, then amnesticizes the mother and compensates her financially. SCP-999 is taken into custody but, due to its benevolent nature, is allowed to roam freely. In this framework, SCP-999 is classified as Thaumiel and regarded as the only entity capable of truly defeating the Scarlet King through its kindness and emotional power.

Participants

SCP-999

The Scarlet King

SCP-231-7

SCP Foundation personnel

Consequences

These tales elevate SCP-999 from a local therapeutic anomaly to a cosmic savior figure, reframing its euphoria-inducing abilities as a metaphysical counterforce to the Scarlet King’s cruelty and despair. They also cast Procedure 110-Montauk as a disinformation tactic and highlight the Foundation’s morally fraught role in managing apocalyptic prophecies.

Variants

Different authors offer variations on the prophecy, the nature of SCP-999’s birth, and the ethical framing of the Foundation’s actions. Some versions emphasize hope and redemption; others foreground the horror of the rituals and the ambiguity of the Foundation’s motives.

Test-914-0196 (SCP-914 interaction)

Source SCP Database Wiki (Test-914-0196 section)Culture Internet collaborative fiction (SCP Foundation community)Period 2010s–2020s

Summary

In this narrative, Professor Snider collects samples of SCP-999’s slime and subjects them to SCP-914, a machine that refines objects in unpredictable ways. The test explores how SCP-999’s anomalous properties might change under different refinement settings, potentially producing variants with altered or enhanced effects. The details of the outcomes vary by author, but the story generally focuses on the risks and ethical questions of modifying a benevolent entity’s essence for research or weaponization.

Participants

SCP-999

SCP-914

Professor Snider

SCP Foundation researchers

Consequences

The test underscores the Foundation’s experimental approach to anomalies, even those that are harmless and beloved. It raises questions about consent, exploitation, and the potential unintended consequences of tampering with SCP-999’s slime.

Variants

Different versions of the test produce different refined samples, some benign, some dangerous, reflecting the SCP universe’s non-canonical structure.

Procedure 110-Montauk is initially used to prevent the birth, but later narratives reveal that the child—SCP-999—was allowed to be born and then taken into Foundation custody.

SCP Foundation personnel

Foundation staff—researchers, guards, and other personnel—interact with SCP-999 regularly, often visiting it during breaks for emotional relief. SCP-999 responds with affection, tickling, and comforting behavior, and has been reported to protect personnel during containment breaches.

Version Note

In-universe, these relationships are informal but widespread, making SCP-999 a kind of communal companion.

Procedure 110-Montauk

Type ritualSource Scarlet King tales; SCP Database (Possible origins).

In Scarlet King-related lore, Procedure 110-Montauk is a brutal ritual applied to SCP-231-7 to prevent the birth of the prophesied child. Dark History
Content advisory. SCP-999’s core description is gentle and uplifting, but its broader lore intersects with narratives involving extreme violence, ritual abuse, and mass casualties. Content advisories include: graphic violence and death (SCP-682 breach), psychological trauma, coercive rituals (Procedure 110-Montauk), and cosmic horror themes related to the Scarlet King.

Violence and mass casualties in SCP-682 cross-test

Source SCP Foundation cross-test log; SCP Database; Grokipedia.

Description

During the cross-test between SCP-999 and SCP-682, SCP-682 ultimately breaches containment and kills numerous Foundation personnel. While SCP-999 temporarily pacifies SCP-682 and rescues as many people as it can, the narrative includes scenes of widespread death and destruction. SCP-999’s involvement highlights the contrast between its kindness and the surrounding brutality.

Scholarly Context

Within SCP lore, this event is often cited as an example of the limits of benevolent anomalies in a fundamentally hostile universe. It underscores the ethical complexity of using SCP-999 as a tool against extreme threats.

Ritual abuse and Procedure 110-Montauk

Source Scarlet King mythos tales (e.g., "New Job"); SCP Database (Possible origins).

Description

In Scarlet King-related tales, Procedure 110-Montauk is a horrific ritual imposed on SCP-231-7 to prevent the birth of a prophesied child. The procedure is described in other SCP entries as involving severe physical and psychological abuse. Later narratives suggest that the child—SCP-999—was allowed to be born and that the ritual’s horror was used as disinformation against enemies. SCP-999’s origin is thus entangled with a history of coercion, suffering, and moral compromise.

Scholarly Context

These stories are part of a broader trend in SCP fiction that explores the ethics of containment, sacrifice, and utilitarian decision-making. SCP-999’s role as a hopeful counterforce does not erase the trauma associated with its prophesied birth.

Cosmic horror and the Scarlet King

Source Scarlet King-related tales; SCP Foundation Wikia notes; SCP Database; Grokipedia.

Description

The Scarlet King mythos portrays a cosmic entity embodying cruelty, despair, and apocalyptic horror. SCP-999 is framed as the King’s seventh child, destined to oppose him. This association situates SCP-999 within a narrative of cosmic-scale violence, oppression, and existential dread, even as it functions as a symbol of resistance and healing.

Scholarly Context

SCP-999’s connection to the Scarlet King is often interpreted as a deliberate inversion of cosmic horror tropes: a small, friendly slime as the key to defeating an incomprehensible evil. This juxtaposition invites reflection on hope, kindness, and care as counter-horror strategies.

Contradictions & Disputes

Origin and familial relationships

Position A

SCP-999 is the seventh child of the Scarlet King and the offspring of SCP-231-7, prophesied to defeat the King.

Position A — evidence

Scarlet King-related tales (e.g., "New Job"), SCP Database (Possible origins), SCP Foundation Wikia notes, Grokipedia’s origin section.

Position B

SCP-999’s origin is unknown or unspecified; it is simply an anomalous entity discovered and contained by the SCP Foundation.

Position B — evidence

Primary SCP Foundation article, which does not mention the Scarlet King, SCP-231-7, or any familial relationships.

Scholarly Consensus

Within the SCP community, there is no single canon; both positions coexist. Many fans embrace the Scarlet King origin as compelling mythic context, while others treat it as one of several possible interpretations. The safest scholarly stance is to distinguish the core article’s agnostic origin from later narrative expansions.

Containment class (Safe vs Thaumiel)

Position A

SCP-999 is a Safe-class entity, reflecting its cooperative, non-hostile nature and minimal containment requirements.

Position A — evidence

Primary SCP Foundation article, which lists Object Class: Safe.

Position B

SCP-999 is or should be classified as Thaumiel, given its potential to contain or neutralize other anomalies (e.g., SCP-682, the Scarlet King).

Position B — evidence

Tales such as "New Job" and fan discussions that explicitly refer to SCP-999 as Thaumiel; SCP Foundation Wikia notes; SCP Database origin section.

Scholarly Consensus

The official article maintains Safe classification, but narrative branches and fan interpretations argue for Thaumiel based on strategic potential. The discrepancy reflects differing emphases: operational containment vs mythic role.

Gender and sex

Position A

SCP-999 is male, with sources referring to it using male pronouns and listing its sex/gender as male.

Position A — evidence

SCP Database infobox listing sex and gender as male; some fan discussions and stories using "he" pronouns.

Position B

SCP-999’s sex and gender are unspecified; it is a unique, amorphous slime entity without conventional biological sex.

Position B — evidence

Primary SCP Foundation article, which uses neutral language and does not assign sex or gender; Grokipedia and WikiHow generally use neutral or "it" pronouns.

Scholarly Consensus

Gendered language appears primarily in fan infoboxes and derivative works. A cautious approach treats SCP-999 as gender-neutral in scholarly description, noting male pronoun usage as a cultural artifact of certain fan communities.

Extent of therapeutic effect

Position A

SCP-999 can effectively cure mental trauma, including severe depression and PTSD, leading to lasting positive outlooks.

Position A — evidence

SCP Foundation article’s reports of individuals with crippling depression or PTSD having far more positive outlooks after multiple interactions; WikiHow’s emphasis on curing mental trauma.

Position B

SCP-999 provides strong but not absolute emotional relief; its effects are significant but not guaranteed cures.

Position B — evidence

Lack of clinical detail or standardized outcomes; the narrative framing of SCP-999 as therapeutic but not omnipotent; Grokipedia’s more cautious language about emotional healing.

Scholarly Consensus

Most sources agree that SCP-999 has powerful therapeutic effects, but the term "cure" is used loosely. A nuanced reading treats its impact as substantial emotional support rather than a medically defined cure.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

1. Exact creation date and revision history of the original SCP-999 article are not fully specified in the accessible snippets; a detailed textual history would require direct access to the SCP Wiki’s revision logs.
2. The full text of cross-test logs (especially with SCP-682 and SCP-914) is not reproduced here; summaries rely on secondary descriptions from fan wikis and synthesized sources, which may omit nuances.
3. Scarlet King-related tales such as "New Job" are referenced but not fully quoted or exhaustively analyzed; their internal variations and authorial intentions remain partially unexplored.
4. Reception in games, videos, and other media beyond web articles and fan art is under-documented in this dossier due to limited direct evidence in the consulted sources; SCP-999 may appear or be referenced in SCP-themed games or videos, but without verifiable details, these are not included.
5. Clinical or scientific analysis of SCP-999’s therapeutic effects is absent; all descriptions are narrative and anecdotal, reflecting the fictional nature of the SCP universe.
6. Non-English adaptations, translations, or localized interpretations of SCP-999 were not systematically surveyed; this dossier focuses on English-language sources available as of late July 2026.
7. Because the SCP universe is explicitly non-canonical and multi-author, any attempt to construct a single, unified chronology or ontology for SCP-999 would be misleading; this dossier instead highlights major strands while acknowledging unresolved contradictions.

Research Scope

This research synthesizes English-language, web-accessible sources on SCP-999 as of 2026-07-30, focusing on the primary SCP Foundation article, major fan wikis (SCP Foundation Wikia, SCP Database), secondary syntheses (WikiHow, Grokipedia), and referenced tales within the Scarlet King mythos. It treats the SCP universe as explicitly non-canonical, distinguishing between core article content and later narrative expansions. No attempt is made to exhaust all minor fan works, non-English adaptations, or ephemeral social media posts; instead, the dossier prioritizes sources that provide structured, citable information about SCP-999’s identity, abilities, stories, relationships, and reception.
