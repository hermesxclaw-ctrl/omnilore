Who It Is

According to traditional accounts, in a universe built on nightmares—plague gods, immortal reptiles, and rituals too cruel to name—SCP-999 is a bright orange interruption. It slithers toward you like an overexcited puppy, wraps you in warm, squishy slime, and suddenly the weight you’ve been carrying for years feels lighter. Some stories say this playful "Tickle Monster" is the seventh child of a cosmic horror called the Scarlet King, destined to undo his reign not with fire or steel, but with laughter and kindness. Whether you treat that as prophecy or metaphor, SCP-999 sits at a strange crossroads in internet horror: a living antidepressant in a world designed to terrify.

Attested: SCP-999’s temperament is consistently described as playful, affectionate, and dog-like. When approached, it reacts with overwhelming elation, slithering toward the nearest person, leaping onto them, and "hugging" them with pseudopods while nuzzling their face and emitting high-pitched gurgling and cooing noises. Its favorite activity is "tickle-wrestling," in which it envelops a person from the neck down and tickles them until asked to stop (though it may delay stopping, reflecting its exuberance). Powers
Euphoria induction

Attested: Physical contact with SCP-999’s surface causes immediate mild euphoria in human subjects, which intensifies with prolonged exposure and persists long after separation. This effect is accompanied by individualized pleasant odors and a sense of comfort and safety. Repeated interactions have been reported to produce sustained improvements in mood and outlook, including in individuals with depression or PTSD.

Limit: Attested: The euphoria is temporary and can be removed by ending contact; long-term therapeutic effects are reported but not quantified. It does not forcibly control behavior beyond mood elevation, and subjects retain agency. The Foundation’s attempts to develop antidepressants from SCP-999’s slime are experimental and not fully realized in the lore.

Emotional healing and trauma mitigation

Reconstructed from attested reports: Individuals suffering from severe depression or PTSD have reported a far more positive outlook on life after multiple interactions with SCP-999. Its presence appears to reduce intrusive thoughts, anxiety, and despair, functioning as a kind of living, anomalous therapy.

Limit: The healing is described qualitatively, not clinically; there is no standardized protocol or guarantee of permanent cure. Effects may depend on repeated contact and the subject’s willingness to engage.

Shape-shifting and malleability

Attested: SCP-999 can change its size and shape at will, flattening portions of its body to as thin as 2 cm and forming pseudopods for locomotion and interaction. It can envelop individuals from the neck down, conforming to their body shape for tickling or hugging.

Limit: Its mass remains roughly constant (around 54 kg), so it cannot expand indefinitely. It appears limited to non-destructive, soft forms and does not exhibit offensive morphologies like blades or rigid structures.

Pleasant odor generation

Attested: SCP-999’s surface emits a pleasing odor that differs with each individual, often matching their personal preferences (e.g., chocolate, roses, fresh laundry, bacon, Play-Doh). This odor accompanies its euphoria-inducing contact and may contribute to its emotional effects.

Limit: The odor is non-harmful and appears limited to benign scents. There is no evidence of it producing toxic or coercive smells.

Protective behavior and risk-taking

Reconstructed from attested narratives: SCP-999 has demonstrated protective instincts, such as intercepting a bullet to shield personnel and assisting during containment breaches by guiding people to safety. It shows no fear even when interacting with highly dangerous entities like SCP-682, and continues to seek playful contact despite prior harm.

Limit: Its physical resilience is not fully defined; while it can survive crushing and other impacts, it is not portrayed as invulnerable. Its protective actions are opportunistic rather than systematically coordinated.

Pacification of hostile entities

Attested in cross-test lore: During a test with SCP-682, SCP-999’s tickling and euphoria-inducing contact caused SCP-682—an extremely hostile, omnicidal entity—to laugh uncontrollably and temporarily fall asleep, effectively pacifying it. This suggests SCP-999’s abilities can affect even powerful, resistant beings.

Limit: The pacification was temporary; once SCP-999 was removed, SCP-682 recovered, breached containment, and killed numerous personnel. SCP-999’s effect did not permanently alter SCP-682’s nature or eliminate its threat.

Dietary absorption and digestion

Attested: SCP-999 consumes candy and sweets, particularly M&M’s and Necco wafers, by absorbing them into its slime body in an amoeba-like manner. It can digest organic materials with ease, though it is not portrayed as predatory.

Limit: Its diet is restricted to sweets by containment procedures; there is no evidence of it needing or preferring other foods. Its digestive capacity is not weaponized in canonical descriptions.

Potential Thaumiel-class strategic role

Reconstructed: In certain tales (e.g., "New Job" and related Scarlet King narratives), SCP-999 is classified as Thaumiel, a containment class reserved for anomalies that help contain or neutralize other anomalies. In this framing, SCP-999 is the prophesied seventh child of the Scarlet King, destined to defeat or counteract him through its kindness and emotional power.

Limit: This role is narrative and prophetic rather than empirically demonstrated. SCP-999 has not, in existing stories, conclusively defeated the Scarlet King; its potential remains speculative.

Accounts I
Core containment narrative of SCP-999SCP Foundation main article "SCP-999"

Late 2000s onward

The foundational narrative presents SCP-999 as a Safe-class anomalous entity: a large, orange, gelatinous mass that induces euphoria and emotional healing in those who touch it. The SCP Foundation contains it in a simple pen, allows it to roam the facility during daytime, and encourages personnel to interact with it. The article details its physical properties, behavior, diet, and anomalous effects, emphasizing its playful, dog-like temperament and its beneficial impact on traumatized individuals. Addenda describe experiments with its slime and its potential use in developing antidepressants. SCP-999 becomes one of the most beloved anomalies within the Foundation, serving as a morale booster and potential therapeutic resource. Its existence introduces a rare note of kindness and hope into the otherwise grim SCP universe.

Cross-test with SCP-682SCP Foundation cross-test log (referenced in SCP Database and Grokipedia)

2010s

In a cross-test, SCP-999 is introduced into SCP-682’s containment cell to attempt to neutralize or pacify the notoriously hostile, regenerating reptilian entity. SCP-682 initially crushes SCP-999 underfoot, but SCP-999 responds by climbing onto SCP-682 and tickling it. SCP-682 begins laughing uncontrollably, eventually falling asleep. When SCP-999 is removed, SCP-682 awakens, emits a psychic wave that causes nearby personnel to laugh uncontrollably, breaches containment, and kills many people. During the chaos, SCP-999 rescues as many individuals as it can, guiding them to safety. Despite the violence, SCP-999 expresses a desire to play with SCP-682 again, showing no fear. The test demonstrates SCP-999’s ability to temporarily pacify even extremely hostile anomalies, but also reveals the limits of its power: SCP-682 remains dangerous and ultimately causes mass casualties. SCP-999’s protective actions reinforce its reputation as benevolent and courageous.

Accounts II
"New Job" and the Scarlet King prophecySCP Foundation tales (e.g., "New Job") referenced in SCP Foundation Wikia and SCP Database

2010s–2020s

In the tale "New Job" and related narratives, SCP-999 is recontextualized within the Scarlet King mythos. SCP-231-7, the seventh "bride" of the Scarlet King, is subjected to Procedure 110-Montauk to prevent the birth of a prophesied child. Daevite tablets are discovered predicting that the seventh child would defeat the King. The Foundation eventually allows the child—SCP-999—to be born, then amnesticizes the mother and compensates her financially. SCP-999 is taken into custody but, due to its benevolent nature, is allowed to roam freely. In this framework, SCP-999 is classified as Thaumiel and regarded as the only entity capable of truly defeating the Scarlet King through its kindness and emotional power. These tales elevate SCP-999 from a local therapeutic anomaly to a cosmic savior figure, reframing its euphoria-inducing abilities as a metaphysical counterforce to the Scarlet King’s cruelty and despair. They also cast Procedure 110-Montauk as a disinformation tactic and highlight the Foundation’s morally fraught role in managing apocalyptic prophecies.

Test-914-0196 (SCP-914 interaction)SCP Database Wiki (Test-914-0196 section)

2010s–2020s

In this narrative, Professor Snider collects samples of SCP-999’s slime and subjects them to SCP-914, a machine that refines objects in unpredictable ways. The test explores how SCP-999’s anomalous properties might change under different refinement settings, potentially producing variants with altered or enhanced effects. The details of the outcomes vary by author, but the story generally focuses on the risks and ethical questions of modifying a benevolent entity’s essence for research or weaponization. The test underscores the Foundation’s experimental approach to anomalies, even those that are harmless and beloved. It raises questions about consent, exploitation, and the potential unintended consequences of tampering with SCP-999’s slime.
