Who It Is

A large, gelatinous, orange blob-like creature that is universally friendly. SCP-999 smells pleasant (reminiscent of chocolate, fresh laundry, and whatever the observer finds comforting), makes happy gurgling sounds, and loves to 'tickle' people, which induces an overwhelming feeling of happiness and well-being. Contact with SCP-999 has demonstrated the ability to cure depression, PTSD, and even temporarily calm SCP-682.

Form

A roughly 54-kilogram mass of orange, translucent slime that smells like chocolate. It has a consistency similar to peanut butter and can reshape itself into simple forms. Two large 'eyes' and a wide 'mouth' are visible on its anterior surface.

Euphoria induction Attested: Physical contact with SCP-999’s surface causes immediate mild euphoria in human subjects, which intensifies with prolonged exposure and persists long after separation. This effect is accompanied by individualized pleasant odors and a sense of comfort and safety. Repeated interactions have been reported to produce sustained improvements in mood and outlook, including in individuals with depression or PTSD. Primary SCP Foundation article; summarized and elaborated in Grokipedia and WikiHow. Attested: The euphoria is temporary and can be removed by ending contact; long-term therapeutic effects are reported but not quantified. It does not forcibly control behavior beyond mood elevation, and subjects retain agency. The Foundation’s attempts to develop antidepressants from SCP-999’s slime are experimental and not fully realized in the lore. Some sources emphasize near-miraculous cures of mental trauma, while others present the effect as strong but not absolute. This reflects narrative variation rather than explicit disagreement in a single text.

Emotional healing and trauma mitigation Reconstructed from attested reports: Individuals suffering from severe depression or PTSD have reported a far more positive outlook on life after multiple interactions with SCP-999. Its presence appears to reduce intrusive thoughts, anxiety, and despair, functioning as a kind of living, anomalous therapy. SCP Foundation article (reports of improved outlook); Grokipedia and WikiHow (emphasis on emotional healing). The healing is described qualitatively, not clinically; there is no standardized protocol or guarantee of permanent cure. Effects may depend on repeated contact and the subject’s willingness to engage. No explicit contradictions, but the degree of healing varies across narratives, with some tales suggesting near-total recovery and others implying partial relief.

Shape-shifting and malleability Attested: SCP-999 can change its size and shape at will, flattening portions of its body to as thin as 2 cm and forming pseudopods for locomotion and interaction. It can envelop individuals from the neck down, conforming to their body shape for tickling or hugging. SCP Foundation article; Grokipedia; WikiHow. Its mass remains roughly constant (around 54 kg), so it cannot expand indefinitely. It appears limited to non-destructive, soft forms and does not exhibit offensive morphologies like blades or rigid structures. No major contradictions; all sources agree on its malleable, slime-like nature.

Pleasant odor generation Attested: SCP-999’s surface emits a pleasing odor that differs with each individual, often matching their personal preferences (e.g., chocolate, roses, fresh laundry, bacon, Play-Doh). This odor accompanies its euphoria-inducing contact and may contribute to its emotional effects. SCP Foundation article; Grokipedia; WikiHow. The odor is non-harmful and appears limited to benign scents. There is no evidence of it producing toxic or coercive smells. None noted; the individualized nature of the odor is consistently reported.

Protective behavior and risk-taking Reconstructed from attested narratives: SCP-999 has demonstrated protective instincts, such as intercepting a bullet to shield personnel and assisting during containment breaches by guiding people to safety. It shows no fear even when interacting with highly dangerous entities like SCP-682, and continues to seek playful contact despite prior harm. Grokipedia (bullet interception and breach assistance); SCP Database (SCP-682 test narrative). Its physical resilience is not fully defined; while it can survive crushing and other impacts, it is not portrayed as invulnerable. Its protective actions are opportunistic rather than systematically coordinated. Some sources focus primarily on its therapeutic role and do not mention combat or protection, but this is omission rather than contradiction.

Pacification of hostile entities Attested in cross-test lore: During a test with SCP-682, SCP-999’s tickling and euphoria-inducing contact caused SCP-682—an extremely hostile, omnicidal entity—to laugh uncontrollably and temporarily fall asleep, effectively pacifying it. This suggests SCP-999’s abilities can affect even powerful, resistant beings. SCP Foundation cross-test log (referenced in fan wikis); SCP Database; Grokipedia; WikiHow. The pacification was temporary; once SCP-999 was removed, SCP-682 recovered, breached containment, and killed numerous personnel. SCP-999’s effect did not permanently alter SCP-682’s nature or eliminate its threat. Some interpretations frame SCP-999 as the only thing that can truly defeat the Scarlet King or SCP-682, while the original test narrative emphasizes only temporary pacification. This tension reflects aspirational mythic readings versus textual evidence.

Dietary absorption and digestion Attested: SCP-999 consumes candy and sweets, particularly M&M’s and Necco wafers, by absorbing them into its slime body in an amoeba-like manner. It can digest organic materials with ease, though it is not portrayed as predatory. SCP Foundation article; Grokipedia. Its diet is restricted to sweets by containment procedures; there is no evidence of it needing or preferring other foods. Its digestive capacity is not weaponized in canonical descriptions. None significant; all sources agree on its candy-based diet.

Potential Thaumiel-class strategic role Reconstructed: In certain tales (e.g., "New Job" and related Scarlet King narratives), SCP-999 is classified as Thaumiel, a containment class reserved for anomalies that help contain or neutralize other anomalies. In this framing, SCP-999 is the prophesied seventh child of the Scarlet King, destined to defeat or counteract him through its kindness and emotional power. SCP Foundation Wikia (Fandom) notes; SCP Database (Possible origins); Grokipedia (Origins and Theories); WikiHow (origin section). This role is narrative and prophetic rather than empirically demonstrated. SCP-999 has not, in existing stories, conclusively defeated the Scarlet King; its potential remains speculative. The primary SCP article does not mention the Scarlet King or Thaumiel classification, leading to a divergence between "core" description and later mythic expansions.

Incident Log

The foundational narrative presents SCP-999 as a Safe-class anomalous entity: a large, orange, gelatinous mass that induces euphoria and emotional healing in those who touch it. The SCP Foundation contains it in a simple pen, allows it to roam the facility during daytime, and encourages personnel to interact with it. The article details its physical properties, behavior, diet, and anomalous effects, emphasizing its playful, dog-like temperament and its beneficial impact on traumatized individuals. Addenda describe experiments with its slime and its potential use in developing antidepressants.

In a cross-test, SCP-999 is introduced into SCP-682’s containment cell to attempt to neutralize or pacify the notoriously hostile, regenerating reptilian entity. SCP-682 initially crushes SCP-999 underfoot, but SCP-999 responds by climbing onto SCP-682 and tickling it. SCP-682 begins laughing uncontrollably, eventually falling asleep. When SCP-999 is removed, SCP-682 awakens, emits a psychic wave that causes nearby personnel to laugh uncontrollably, breaches containment, and kills many people. During the chaos, SCP-999 rescues as many individuals as it can, guiding them to safety. Despite the violence, SCP-999 expresses a desire to play with SCP-682 again, showing no fear.

In the tale "New Job" and related narratives, SCP-999 is recontextualized within the Scarlet King mythos. SCP-231-7, the seventh "bride" of the Scarlet King, is subjected to Procedure 110-Montauk to prevent the birth of a prophesied child. Daevite tablets are discovered predicting that the seventh child would defeat the King. The Foundation eventually allows the child—SCP-999—to be born, then amnesticizes the mother and compensates her financially. SCP-999 is taken into custody but, due to its benevolent nature, is allowed to roam freely. In this framework, SCP-999 is classified as Thaumiel and regarded as the only entity capable of truly defeating the Scarlet King through its kindness and emotional power.

In this narrative, Professor Snider collects samples of SCP-999’s slime and subjects them to SCP-914, a machine that refines objects in unpredictable ways. The test explores how SCP-999’s anomalous properties might change under different refinement settings, potentially producing variants with altered or enhanced effects. The details of the outcomes vary by author, but the story generally focuses on the risks and ethical questions of modifying a benevolent entity’s essence for research or weaponization.

Content Notes

During the cross-test between SCP-999 and SCP-682, SCP-682 ultimately breaches containment and kills numerous Foundation personnel. While SCP-999 temporarily pacifies SCP-682 and rescues as many people as it can, the narrative includes scenes of widespread death and destruction. SCP-999’s involvement highlights the contrast between its kindness and the surrounding brutality.

Within SCP lore, this event is often cited as an example of the limits of benevolent anomalies in a fundamentally hostile universe. It underscores the ethical complexity of using SCP-999 as a tool against extreme threats.

SCP Foundation cross-test log; SCP Database; Grokipedia.

In Scarlet King-related tales, Procedure 110-Montauk is a horrific ritual imposed on SCP-231-7 to prevent the birth of a prophesied child. The procedure is described in other SCP entries as involving severe physical and psychological abuse. Later narratives suggest that the child—SCP-999—was allowed to be born and that the ritual’s horror was used as disinformation against enemies. SCP-999’s origin is thus entangled with a history of coercion, suffering, and moral compromise.

These stories are part of a broader trend in SCP fiction that explores the ethics of containment, sacrifice, and utilitarian decision-making. SCP-999’s role as a hopeful counterforce does not erase the trauma associated with its prophesied birth.

Scarlet King mythos tales (e.g., "New Job"); SCP Database (Possible origins).

The Scarlet King mythos portrays a cosmic entity embodying cruelty, despair, and apocalyptic horror. SCP-999 is framed as the King’s seventh child, destined to oppose him. This association situates SCP-999 within a narrative of cosmic-scale violence, oppression, and existential dread, even as it functions as a symbol of resistance and healing.

SCP-999’s connection to the Scarlet King is often interpreted as a deliberate inversion of cosmic horror tropes: a small, friendly slime as the key to defeating an incomprehensible evil. This juxtaposition invites reflection on hope, kindness, and care as counter-horror strategies.

Scarlet King-related tales; SCP Foundation Wikia notes; SCP Database; Grokipedia.

A way to hold it in your mind

According to traditional accounts, in a universe built on nightmares—plague gods, immortal reptiles, and rituals too cruel to name—SCP-999 is a bright orange interruption. It slithers toward you like an overexcited puppy, wraps you in warm, squishy slime, and suddenly the weight you’ve been carrying for years feels lighter. Some stories say this playful "Tickle Monster" is the seventh child of a cosmic horror called the Scarlet King, destined to undo his reign not with fire or steel, but with laughter and kindness. Whether you treat that as prophecy or metaphor, SCP-999 sits at a strange crossroads in internet horror: a living antidepressant in a world designed to terrify.
