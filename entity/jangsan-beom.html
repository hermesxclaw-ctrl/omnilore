Etymology

Jangsan (장산) likely refers to a mountain name, possibly Jangsan in Busan or another location. Beom (범) means tiger. Thus, 'Tiger of Jangsan'.

Cultural Ownership

Korean folklore, particularly associated with mountainous regions of South Korea.

Disputed Identifications

Some consider it a type of tiger, others a supernatural entity or spirit. It is sometimes linked to the gumiho (nine-tailed fox) due to mimicry abilities, but it is a distinct creature.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the mountains of South Korea, a tiger that cannot be seen stalks the forests. It mimics the voices of humans, calling out from the darkness, luring the unwary deeper into the wild. This is the Jangsan-beom, a cryptid born from fear and folklore, a creature that blurs the line between predator and ghost.

The jangsan-beom is Korea's mimic: a huge, white-furred tiger-like beast said to haunt Jangsan Mountain above Busan — hairless-faced in some tellings, long-limbed and wrong-jointed, fast as a landslide — whose weapon is not claw or fang but sound. It imitates.

Who It Is

The jangsan-beom is Korea's mimic: a huge, white-furred tiger-like beast said to haunt Jangsan Mountain above Busan — hairless-faced in some tellings, long-limbed and wrong-jointed, fast as a landslide — whose weapon is not claw or fang but sound. It imitates. Running water where there is no stream, to draw hikers off the trail; a woman weeping; a child calling for its mother; and at its most terrible, the exact voice of the person walking beside you, calling your name from ten meters into the fog. Its mimicry is described with one persistent flaw — the voice is almost right: a beat slow, a tone hollow, the words repeated a little too evenly — and that almost is the legend's engine, because every survivor's tale turns on the moment of noticing. The jangsan-beom is technically a modern codification: it surged to national fame through Busan hiking forums, horror webtoons, and the 2017 film The Mimic, and honest sourcing marks it as thin in pre-modern attestation. But its bones are old: Korea's tiger-fear is ancient and earned (Joseon records log hundreds of fatal tiger attacks), white tigers were mountain-spirit omens, and the do-not-answer-voices-in-the-mountains rule appears in Korean folk warnings long before the internet. The jangsan-beom is what happens when a country that once posted tiger-warnings at village gates moves to apartment towers within sight of the old mountain — the tiger comes back, upgraded, speaking your language. Almost.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Typically described as a tiger, but with unusual features such as glowing eyes or an eerie silence. Some accounts say it is invisible or can blend into shadows. It may appear as a normal tiger but with a human-like ability to mimic voices.

Symbols & Iconography

The tiger is a powerful symbol in Korean culture, representing courage and protection, but also danger. The Jangsan-beom subverts this, representing the deceptive and malevolent side of the wild.

Behaviour & Personality

It is a cunning predator that uses the voices of humans, often children or loved ones, to lure victims. It is patient and intelligent, stalking its prey. It is also known to imitate the sound of crying or calling for help.

Domains & Habitats

Mountainous areas, forests, and other remote regions of South Korea. It is particularly associated with Jangsan, a mountain in Busan, but similar legends exist elsewhere.

Version Differences

Some versions depict it as a physical tiger with supernatural abilities; others as a spirit or ghost that takes the form of a tiger. In some stories, it can be killed by normal means; in others, it is invulnerable.

First Reports

Busan-area legend attached to Jangsan (a real mountain with restricted military zones and genuine disappearance stories), nationally codified through 2010s internet horror and film; folklorists class it as contemporary legend with deep tiger-lore roots — and this entry says so honestly.

Supernatural Strength and Speed

Source Version Implied in all versions

Description

As a tiger, it possesses strength and speed beyond ordinary animals, enabling it to overpower prey.

Limitations

None known.

Contradictions

None.

Form

A big cat gone wrong: white or pale grey, oversized, sometimes described with a disturbingly smooth face, moving with a gait that reads feline until it stands — glimpsed at fog-edge, never in the open.

Powers

Precision vocal mimicry (water, weeping, names, familiar voices), fog-hunting, trail-luring, and the patience of an ambush predator that has learned the prey answers to sound.

Weaknesses & Wards

The flaw in the voice — cadence a half-beat off — gives the alert listener their one warning; groups that keep visual contact and never split to investigate sounds are outside its method entirely.
