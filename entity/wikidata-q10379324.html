Who It Is

In the frosty Finnish forests, a fiddler’s bow weaves ancient runo-songs into the modern world. Tellu Turkka, a name whispered among folk music circles, has spent decades bridging the gap between the Kalevala’s epic poetry and contemporary soundscapes. Her journey from conservatory student to a key figure in the Nordic folk revival is a testament to the enduring power of tradition and innovation.

Known for her dedication to traditional Finnish folk music and her collaborative spirit, having worked with many ensembles.

Accounts I
Early musical educationBiographical summary

1978-1988

Tellu Turkka studied violin at the Jyväskylä Conservatory and Savonlinna Art College, laying the foundation for her musical career. Developed classical technique that later informed her folk playing.

Involvement with HedningarnaBiographical summary

1990-1996, 2001-2003

Tellu was a member of the influential folk band Hedningarna, which blended Nordic folk with electronic and rock elements. Her interest in runo-songs deepened during this period. Contributed to the band's success and her own artistic development.

Accounts II
Founding LoitumaBiographical summary

1989-1991

Tellu was a founding member of the ensemble Loituma, known for its vocal and instrumental folk music. Helped establish a group that later gained international recognition.

Kalevala: Dream of the Salmon MaidenBiographical summary

1999

Tellu participated in Ruth MacKenzie's musical theatre performance based on the Kalevala at the Guthrie Theatre in Minneapolis. Brought Finnish folk music to an American audience.
