Who It Is

In the mythic medicine of Ireland, there was a healer whose salves could mend the shattered, whose well could restore the wounded, and whose envy could destroy his own children. Dian Cecht, the swift-power of the Tuatha Dé Danann, stands as a figure of paradox: the god who healed the gods, yet whose jealousy cost his son his life and humanity the knowledge of every healing herb. His story is not merely a tale of ancient magic, but a meditation on the limits of healing, the cost of pride, and the fragility of knowledge.

Dian Cecht is depicted as a skilled and powerful healer, but also as envious and prone to destructive jealousy. He is proactive in protecting Ireland (killing the monstrous child), yet his envy leads him to kill his own son and scatter healing herbs. He is also a figure of authority in law, as the Bretha Déin Chécht is attributed to him.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is likely a compound of Old Irish 'dían' (swift) and 'cécht' (power), thus 'swift power' or 'swift might'. The Cóir Anmann glosses 'cécht' as 'power' (cumachtae). A Proto-Celtic reconstruction *Deino-kwekwto- 'swift concoction' has been proposed, suggesting 'swift potion' or 'He-who-is-Swif

Pronunciation — Old Irish: [dʲiːa̯n ˈkʲeːxt̪]; approximate Modern Irish: /dʲiən kʲeːxt̪/

Aliases — Dian Cécht, Dian Cecht, Cainte, Canta, Déin Chécht (genitive), Diancecht (anglicized)

Ownership — Irish mythology, specifically the Tuatha Dé Danann pantheon. He is a pre-Christian deity of healing.

Powers
Healing

Dian Cecht is the healer of the Tuatha Dé Danann. He can heal almost any wound or illness, boasting he could mend anyone except those who were decapitated or had severe brain/spinal cord damage.

Limit: Cannot heal decapitation or severe brain/spinal cord injuries.

Curative Well (Tipra Sláine)

He used a well called 'Slainge's Well' or 'well of healing' to minister to the injured by soaking them in it. The well was located at Achad Abla.

Limit: The well's healing properties are not explicitly limited, but it is used for battle injuries.

Crafting (Silver Arm)

He crafted a functional silver arm for King Nuada after Nuada lost his arm in battle.

Limit: The arm was silver, not flesh, and later replaced by Miach's healing.

Serpent Slaying

He killed a monstrous child (Meichi) who had three serpents in his heart, and then destroyed the serpents, burning them to ashes and throwing them into a river, causing it to boil.

Limit: None.

Herbal Knowledge

He had knowledge of healing herbs, and he ground them on Lusmag. However, he scattered the herbs that grew from his son's grave, so that no one knows all their properties.

Limit: His scattering of herbs led to a loss of complete herbal knowledge.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description is attested in the sources. He is a divine figure, likely anthropomorphic, but no specific attributes are recorded.

Symbols — No specific symbols are directly associated with him, but his domain of healing suggests associations with wells, herbs, and salves. The silver arm he crafted for Nuada might be a symbolic artifact.

Domains — He is associated with the Otherworldly realm of the Tuatha Dé Danann. His healing well, Tipra Sláine, was located at Achad Abla ('Field of the Apple Tree') northwest of Mag Tuired. He also ground herbs on Lusmag ('Herb-plain').

Accounts I
The Healing Well at Mag TuiredCath Maige Tuired

Mythic

During the Second Battle of Mag Tuired, Dian Cecht, along with his sons Miach and Octriuil and daughter Airmed, chanted spells over the well of Sláine, and the wounded were immersed in it, becoming whole again. He boasted he could heal all but decapitation. The Tuatha Dé Danann were able to recover from wounds during the battle.

The Boiling of the River BarrowMetrical Dindsenchas

Mythic

The Morrígan, wife of the Dagda, bore a son of terrible aspect. Dian Cecht, foreseeing danger, counselled that the child be destroyed. When the child was killed, Dian Cecht opened the infant's heart and found three serpents. He destroyed the serpents and burned them to ashes, then threw the ashes into the nearest river. The ashes were so venomous that the river boiled and killed all living creatures in it, giving it the name Barrow ('Boiling'). The river was named Barrow; Ireland was saved from potential depopulation.

The Silver Arm of NuadaCath Maige Tuired

Mythic

King Nuada lost his arm in battle. Dian Cecht made him a silver arm that moved and functioned like a real arm. Later, his son Miach healed Nuada fully, giving him a flesh-and-blood arm, which enraged Dian Cecht, and he killed Miach. Miach's death led to Airmed's mourning and the growth of healing herbs from his grave, which Dian Cecht scattered.

Accounts II
The Scattering of the HerbsCath Maige Tuired (implied), Dindsenchas

Mythic

After Miach's death, his sister Airmed mourned over his grave, and all the healing herbs of the world grew from it. She arranged and catalogued them, but Dian Cecht, in anger and jealousy, scattered the herbs, so that no human now knows all their healing properties. Loss of complete herbal knowledge for humanity.

Healing of MiderTochmarc Étaíne

Mythic

Dian Cecht healed Mider after Mider lost an eye when struck with a twig of hazel. Mider's eye was healed.

The Judgements of Dian CéchtBretha Déin Chécht

Early Christian Ireland (text)

A legal text on the law of illegal injury is attributed to Dian Cecht. A later preface to the Senchas Már states that his judgements were accepted by Saint Patrick because they did not contradict Christian teaching. His legal judgements became part of Irish law.
