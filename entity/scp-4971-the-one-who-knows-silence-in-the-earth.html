Who It Is

In the hollowed-out carcass of a dead shopping mall, the floor drops away into a forest that should not exist—a sun that resets itself, trees with no DNA, and somewhere between the trunks a god that has been taking hearts since before Rome crossed the sea. The SCP Foundation calls it SCP-4971-▽, the One Who Knows Silence in the Earth, and warns that if it ever finds the door out, it will rewrite the living world in the language of ritual.

Attested: SCP-4971-▽ is characterized as a hostile, cosmic deity of sacrifice and rituals, feared within occult circles and rarely worshipped. It is described as a god that demands offerings—historically human hearts—from druidic practitioners, and in the modern narrative it threatens to remake all life in its own image. The entity is portrayed as patient, inexorable, and largely indifferent to individual human suffering, focusing instead on large-scale transformation and ritual fulfillment. Powers
Space-time anomaly (SCP-4971)

SCP-4971 manifests as a non-Euclidean, space-time anomaly within the SouthWood Park Mall. Anyone leaving the main concourse inevitably enters the anomaly’s forest interior, regardless of the apparent physical path taken. The anomaly’s internal geography is vast and likely extends far beyond the physical dimensions of the mall. Temporal behavior is also anomalous, with a sun that sets after twelve hours and returns to its setting position after thirteen, suggesting temporal loops or discontinuities.

Limit: The anomaly appears anchored to the mall structure; containment procedures focus on sealing the mall and preventing entry or exit. The Foundation can establish observational sites and limited encampments within the anomaly, but full mapping is infeasible.

Hostile anomalous entities within SCP-4971

SCP-4971 contains multiple hostile anomalous entities, some of which can be neutralized or deterred with light weapons, while others pose significant danger and require extreme caution. These entities may exhibit cognitohazardous properties, with speech or presence potentially affecting human cognition in unknown ways.

Limit: Foundation personnel are instructed to engage only when absolutely necessary, and many entities remain poorly understood due to limited safe observation.

Higher-dimensional existence (SCP-4971-▽)

SCP-4971-▽ is described as existing in higher dimensions, with the ability to warp reality and manipulate space-time within and potentially beyond SCP-4971. Fan sources list "higher-dimensional existence" and "higher-dimensional manipulation" among its powers, indicating that it can operate across multiple planes of reality.

Limit: Direct interaction with SCP-4971-▽ is extremely limited; Foundation containment focuses on preventing the entity from reaching the entrance of SCP-4971. Its full range of higher-dimensional capabilities remains speculative.

Virtual warping and reality manipulation

SCP-4971-▽ is credited in fan compilations with "virtual warping" and reality manipulation, suggesting the ability to alter environments, perceptions, and possibly digital or conceptual spaces. Within SCP-4971, the forest and mall architecture appear to shift in ways that defy conventional physics, consistent with such powers.

Limit: Specific limits are not defined; the Foundation assumes worst-case capabilities and classifies the entity as capable of initiating a VK-Class "Salted Earth" scenario.

Biological manipulation

SCP-4971-▽ is described as capable of biological manipulation, potentially remaking living organisms in its own image. Its stated goal is to "remake all life in its own image", implying large-scale alteration of biological forms.

Limit: No detailed case studies of such transformations are provided in the snippets; the threat is largely projected rather than documented through completed events.

Telekinesis

Fan sources list telekinesis among SCP-4971-▽’s abilities, implying the capacity to move objects and possibly people without physical contact.

Limit: Specific feats (e.g., lifting structures, manipulating weapons) are not detailed in the available summaries.

Absolute invulnerability

SCP-4971-▽ is described as absolutely invulnerable, with an occultist stating "you can't kill it". This suggests that conventional weapons and possibly anomalous countermeasures are ineffective at destroying or permanently harming the entity.

Limit: While invulnerability is asserted, the Foundation still attempts to engage SCP-4971-▽ with "all available force" to prevent it from breaching SCP-4971’s entrance, indicating that containment rather than destruction is the operational goal.

Absolute immortality

SCP-4971-▽ is described as absolutely immortal, existing beyond normal temporal constraints and surviving across ancient druidic eras into the modern SCP narrative.

Limit: Immortality does not preclude containment or restriction of movement; the Foundation’s procedures aim to prevent the entity from accessing the wider world.

Cognitohazardous influence

The SCP-4971 article notes that many entities within the anomaly may have cognitohazardous effects, and that their speech could interact with human cognitive function in unknown ways. SCP-4971-▽, as the central deity, is likely to exert such influence, potentially affecting perception, belief, and behavior.

Limit: Due to the risk, direct study of these effects is limited; the Foundation errs on the side of caution.

Ritual-based protection and destruction

In-universe druidic texts describe SCP-4971-▽ as a nature god who grants protection from forest creatures in exchange for human heart sacrifices. In the modern narrative, the entity’s rituals threaten to destroy or transform human habitability on Earth, leading to its classification as capable of a VK-Class "Salted Earth" scenario.

Limit: The entity appears bound to ritual structures—sacrifices, gateways, symbols—suggesting that its actions follow specific patterns rather than arbitrary whims.

Accounts I
SCP-4971 — Rituals (Primary Containment Narrative)SCP-4971 article (SCP Wiki)

2010s–2019 (approximate creation and publication period)

The primary SCP-4971 narrative presents the SouthWood Park Mall in Havensbrook, Indiana, as the site of a space-time anomaly. After the mall’s decline and closure in 2006, the SCP Foundation discovers that anyone leaving the main concourse enters a vast forest landscape under an anomalous sun. The Foundation establishes Armored Command Battalion Saito-9 (ACB Sa-9) and Observational Site-81-3 to contain the anomaly. Within SCP-4971, multiple hostile entities are encountered, and the presence of SCP-4971-▽, a god of sacrifice and rituals, is confirmed. The Foundation’s Classification Committee and Ethics Committee classify SCP-4971 as CERNNUNOS and recognize the potential for a VK-Class "Salted Earth" end-of-human-habitability scenario if SCP-4971-▽ breaches the entrance. The narrative includes exploration logs, memos, and ethical debates about containment and engagement. The Foundation seals the mall, establishes long-term containment, and recognizes SCP-4971-▽ as a global existential threat. The Last Appeal of BiphiReferenced in Antagonists Wiki summary of SCP-4971-▽

Post-publication expansions (exact date unclear)

Fan sources describe "The Last Appeal of Biphi" as a narrative arc involving an individual or entity named Biphi who attempts to appeal to or negotiate with SCP-4971-▽. The story explores the futility of bargaining with a cosmic deity of sacrifice, highlighting the entity’s indifference to individual pleas and its adherence to ritual structures. Biphi’s appeal fails, reinforcing the theme that SCP-4971-▽ cannot be placated by anything less than its demanded sacrifices and that human attempts at moral reasoning are ineffective. The failure of Biphi’s appeal underscores the hopelessness of direct negotiation and contributes to the occult community’s fear of SCP-4971-▽. It also enriches the mythos around the deity’s unyielding nature.

Accounts II
Discovery of SCP-4971Addendum 4971.1 (Discovery) in SCP-4971 article; Spanish translation summary

2006 (in-universe discovery), 2010s (real-world publication)

The discovery narrative recounts the history of the SouthWood Park Mall, opened in 1985 and operating until 2006. As attendance declines, the last major anchor store, Eagle Pass Outfitting, liquidates its stock and closes. The mall is temporarily closed in June 2006. Subsequent anomalous events and reports lead the SCP Foundation to investigate, revealing that the mall’s interior has become a gateway to SCP-4971’s forest landscape. The Foundation moves to secure the site and initiate containment procedures. The mall is permanently closed, entrances barricaded, and the site repurposed as Observational Site-81-3. The discovery marks the beginning of formal containment and research into SCP-4971.

Encountering the GodAntagonists Wiki summary; implied exploration logs in SCP-4971 article

In-universe post-discovery period

Exploration teams within SCP-4971 eventually encounter SCP-4971-▽. The entity’s presence is accompanied by ritualistic phenomena, hostile environmental changes, and possibly direct communication or manifestations of its will. An occultist warns Foundation staff that the entity cannot be killed and that if it finds a gateway into the wider world, it will begin performing its own rituals on a planetary scale. The encounter solidifies the Foundation’s understanding of SCP-4971-▽ as a god of sacrifice and rituals and informs the VK-Class scenario classification. Containment protocols are intensified, with explicit orders to engage SCP-4971-▽ with all available force if it approaches the entrance. Ethical and strategic debates within the Foundation escalate.

Project IsorropíaAntagonists Wiki summary of SCP-4971-▽

Post-primary article expansions (exact date unclear)

Project Isorropía is referenced as a possible ending or countermeasure scenario involving SCP-4971-▽. The project’s name suggests "balance" (from Greek isorropía), implying an attempt to restore equilibrium or counteract the deity’s influence. Fan sources indicate that Project Isorropía explores strategies to prevent SCP-4971-▽ from remaking all life, possibly through sacrificial trade-offs, containment gambits, or large-scale ritual interventions. The project underscores the desperation and moral complexity of confronting a cosmic deity of sacrifice. Outcomes vary in fan interpretations; some suggest partial success, others imply catastrophic failure.
