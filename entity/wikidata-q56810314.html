Who It Is

He is small enough to be overlooked on a pitch full of super-powered middle-schoolers, until he runs. When Toramaru Utsunomiya strikes the ball, a translucent tiger seems to lunge with it — a shy, tiger-obsessed boy from Kansai trying to step out of the shadow of his hero and become Raimon's next ace striker.

Canonically shy, earnest, and hero-worshipping at introduction. Toramaru idolizes Hiroto Kiyama (Gran of Aliea Academy's The Genesis, later Raimon teammate) and initially lacks self-confidence, fearing he cannot fill the ace striker role after Aliea Academy's defeat. He is polite, uses Kansai-ben in Japanese (attested in anime dialogue), and is close to his mother Akane. Under pressure he freezes, then overcompensates by attempting overly flashy shots. Powers
Tiger Drive (タイガードライブ)

Signature shoot hissatsu. Toramaru kicks the ball with a somersault, summoning a roaring tiger aura that drives the ball toward the goal with spiraling orange energy. In-universe, treated as a superhuman football technique, not literal magic, but visually supernatural.

Limit: High stamina cost; can be stopped by strong keeper hissatsu (e.g., God Hand, Majin The Hand). Requires run-up and is less effective when Toramaru is mentally hesitant (plot-dependent). In game mechanics, costs ~40 TP and has limited use per match.

Tiger Storm (タイガーストーム) — Combination with Hiroto Kiyama

Joint shoot with Hiroto Kiyama (Gran). Both leap and kick simultaneously, creating two intertwining tiger/dragon-like energy beasts (Hiroto's Ryuusei Blade energy merges) that spiral together. Considered Toramaru's strongest early technique.

Limit: Cannot be used without Hiroto; timing-dependent. In game, both participants consume TP. In anime, fails if their coordination is off (ep. 77).

Gladius Arch (グラディウスアーチ)

Advanced solo shoot developed during FFI arc. Toramaru performs a sharp arching kick, the ball glows and curves like a gladius sword's arc, bypassing defenders with a sudden bend.

Limit: Requires high technique stat; less powerful than Tiger Drive in raw force but better for curved shots. Game: 50 TP, Wind attribute.

Evolution (エボリューション) / RC Tiger Drive (RCタイガードライブ)

In later games (Inazuma Eleven 3 and Strikers), Toramaru can learn evolved forms: Evolution is a team chain shoot, RC Tiger Drive is a remote-controlled variant where the tiger aura changes trajectory mid-flight.

Limit: Evolution requires chain participants; RC Tiger Drive costs more TP and has lower base power for tactical control. Non-canonical in anime continuity.

Physical & Team Abilities (Non-hissatsu)

Above-average speed and dribbling for a forward of his size; noted for quick acceleration and small turning radius. Strong admiration-driven work ethic; improves rapidly with coaching. Weakness: initial lack of composure under pressure, leading to missed shots; physically smaller than defenders, so easily knocked off ball without hissatsu aura.

Limit: No supernatural resistance; vulnerable to physical tackles and keeper hissatsu. No defensive hissatsu. Growth-dependent — early arc Toramaru is not ace-level.

Accounts I
Scouting the Tiger — Aliea Academy AftermathInazuma Eleven 2: Kyoui no Shinryakusha (game chapters 6-8) / Anime episodes 68-72

2009-2010 (in-universe: shortly after Aliea Academy's defeat, winter of Raimon's second year)

After Raimon defeats Aliea Academy, coach Hibiki Seigou seeks a new forward to replace the graduated/ injured strikers. He scouts Toramaru Utsunomiya, a Kansai elementary school striker famous for Tiger Drive, who idolizes Hiroto Kiyama. Toramaru's mother Akane initially opposes him moving to Tokyo, fearing for his schooling and the intensity of Raimon's alien-level training. After watching Raimon's practice and Hiroto's encouragement, Toramaru convinces his mother and joins Raimon, debuting against Gemini Storm remnants and struggling with self-doubt. Establishes Toramaru as Raimon's new ace candidate; sets up mentor-student dynamic with Hiroto; introduces Kansai outsider perspective to Tokyo-based team.

Tiger Storm — Learning to Play TogetherAnime episodes 75-77 / Game Inazuma Eleven 2 late Aliea/FFI prelude

2010 (in-universe: FFI preliminaries training camp)

Toramaru attempts to copy Hiroto's style and fails repeatedly, his Tiger Drive blocked. Coach Kudou (replacing Hibiki) and Hiroto teach him that imitation limits him. During a training match vs. Raimon's reserves and later vs. Fire Dragon's reserves, Toramaru and Hiroto develop the combined Tiger Storm, symbolizing Toramaru finding his own rhythm within partnership rather than mimicry. The hissatsu succeeds after Toramaru admits his fear of not being 'special' enough. Toramaru gains confidence and a reliable finishing move; team chemistry improves before FFI; thematic resolution of 'hero worship vs. self-identity'.

Football Frontier International — Preliminaries vs. Korea's Fire Dragon and BeyondAnime episodes 78-86 / Inazuma Eleven 2 & 3 game FFI arc

2010 (in-universe: FFI Asia preliminaries)

As part of Inazuma Japan (Japan's national youth team formed from Raimon and rivals), Toramaru competes in FFI Asia qualifiers. He scores against Fire Dragon (Korea, led by Cha Woon-myung) using Tiger Drive, but is challenged by stronger opponents like Australia's Big Waves and Qatar's Desert Lion. His role shifts from starter to rotational forward as coaches test formations with Gouenji Shuuya and Hiroto. A key moment is his assist to Gouenji against Desert Lion, showing growth from solo scorer to team player. Secures Japan's progression; Toramaru learns positional flexibility; sets up rivalry/friendship with Korean forwards.

Accounts II
Gladius Arch and the World Tournament — Knights of Queen and Little GigantAnime episodes 92-127 / Inazuma Eleven 3: Sekai e no Chousen!!

2010-2011 (in-universe: FFI World Tournament on Liocott Island)

In the world stage, Toramaru develops Gladius Arch to overcome European defenses, notably England's Knights of Queen (with Edgar Partinus). He scores the opening goal vs. Knights using the new shoot, proving his evolution beyond tiger imagery alone. In the final vs. Little Gigant (Côte d'Ivoire, led by Rococo Urupa), Toramaru is used as a second-half substitute; his pressure forces Rococo to reveal God Hand X, and his cross leads to Hiroto's equalizer. Though not the final scorer (Endo/Gouenji/Tatsunari finish), his contribution is acknowledged as vital. Inazuma Japan wins FFI; Toramaru cements status as national-level forward; his new hissatsu enters franchise canon.

Ogre Attack — Alternate Timeline FilmFilm: Gekijouban Inazuma Eleven: Saikyou Gundan Ogre Shuurai (劇場版イナズマイレブン 最強軍団オーガ襲来) — 2010

2010 (film release; in-universe alternate future where Ogre Academy attacks Raimon's past)

In this non-canonical film timeline, adult future characters (including a grown Toramaru cameo in crowd/future shots) observe the attack by Ogre Academy. Young Toramaru appears with Inazuma Japan fighting Ogre's forwards (Baddap Sleed, etc.). He uses Tiger Drive against Ogre's keeper, which is blocked by Mugen The Hand, demonstrating the power gap. The film's resolution via Kanon Endo's time travel restores the main timeline. Film is side-canon; does not affect main series continuity but expands Toramaru's filmography and shows his technique against future-level opponents.

Ten Years Later — Raimon Alumni in Inazuma Eleven GOAnime Inazuma Eleven GO episodes 18, 24, 47 (flashbacks/cameos) / Game Inazuma Eleven GO: Shine/Dark (2011-2012) / Chrono Stone (2012)

2011-2012 (in-universe: 10 years after original series, Toramaru age ~22)

Adult Toramaru appears as part of the Raimon OB (old boys) network supporting the new Raimon team led by Matsukaze Tenma. He is no longer a professional player in the main GO continuity but works in football-adjacent role (implied coaching/support staff; exact job not explicitly stated in anime dialogue, shown in reunion scenes). In Chrono Stone, his younger self can be recruited via time travel for the Chrono Storm team. His adult design is taller, hair shorter, wearing casual suit rather than kit, symbolizing transition from player to mentor. Shows long-term legacy of original Raimon generation; Toramaru's arc completes from shy child to supportive adult; allows player to use him in GO's post-game.

Toramaru is the main protagonist of Inazuma Eleven. — The protagonist is Mamoru Endo (Mark Evans) throughout original series; Toramaru is a supporting forward introduced mid-series (Aliea aftermath) to fill striker gap. He never becomes captain or central POV character.

Tiger Drive and Tiger Storm are the same technique. — Tiger Drive is solo; Tiger Storm is a distinct combination hissatsu requiring Hiroto Kiyama. They have different animations, TP costs, and story contexts.

Toramaru becomes a professional player in Italy in GO's future. — No primary anime, game, or fanbook states this. Adult Toramaru in GO appears as Raimon OB support, not as pro in Italy. The Italy claim appears to be fanon conflating him with other characters (e.g., Fideo Ardena).

His name is pronounced 'Tora-MARU' with stress on last syllable in Japanese. — Japanese pitch accent is flat/high after first mora, not English stress. Kansai-ben may add intonation but not English stress pattern.
