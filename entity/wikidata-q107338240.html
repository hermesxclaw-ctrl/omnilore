The Approach

In the late twentieth century, a philosopher trained in the analytic tradition mounted a sustained, often provocative assault on theistic arguments, using the very physics of the Big Bang as his weapon. Quentin Smith, a professor at Western Michigan University, became one of the most visible atheist philosophers in America, not through polemic alone but through rigorous engagement with cosmology, time, and language. His debates with William Lane Craig and Alvin Plantinga were not mere academic sparring; they shaped the contours of contemporary philosophy of religion.

Behaviour & Personality

Smith was known for his rigorous, analytical style and willingness to engage in debate. He was a prolific writer, publishing over 140 articles. His work often challenged mainstream theistic philosophy, and he was considered a leading defender of atheism. He was also an editor and sought to promote naturalistic perspectives in philosophy.

Domains & Habitats

Stories & Myths

Debates with William Lane Craig

Source Public debates, published books, and articlesCulture Analytic philosophy of religionPeriod 1980s-2000s

Summary

Smith and Craig engaged in a series of debates on the existence of God, particularly focusing on cosmological arguments and the implications of Big Bang cosmology. They co-authored 'Theism, Atheism and Big Bang Cosmology' (1993), a point-counterpoint book. Their debates were notable for their intellectual rigor and mutual respect.

Participants

Quentin Smith

William Lane Craig

Consequences

These debates helped popularize the philosophy of religion and brought attention to the intersection of science and theology.

Variants

Smith also debated Alvin Plantinga and others.

Independent scholar period

Source Biographical accountsCulture American academiaPeriod 1980s

Summary

After a brief stint at the University of Kentucky, Smith resigned to become an independent scholar, focusing on research and writing. He later returned to academia as a visiting professor at Antioch College before joining Western Michigan University in 1993.
