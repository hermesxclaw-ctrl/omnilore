Who It Is

In the shadowed forests and misty marshes of the Baltic lands, there lurks a being of cunning and chaos—Velnias, the devil who is not merely a demon but a fractured reflection of an ancient god. He is the trickster who bargains with farmers, the tempter who steals the sun, and the chthonic lord whose name echoes the Slavic Veles and the Vedic Varuna. To understand Velnias is to peer into the very soul of Baltic paganism, where good and evil are not simple opposites but intertwined forces in a cosmic dance.

Velnias is a cunning trickster, often trying to outwit humans or gods. He is greedy, lustful, and malicious, but also foolish and easily deceived. He is not entirely evil; he is a force of chaos and temptation, testing the morality of humans. He is also a guardian of the underworld and the dead.

Powers
Shapeshifting

Velnias can transform into various animals, objects, or even humans to deceive or tempt others.

Limit: He may be forced to reveal his true form if his name is spoken or if he is confronted with iron or holy symbols.

Control over wealth and treasure

Velnias is often associated with hidden treasures, gold, and riches. He may offer wealth in exchange for a soul or a service.

Limit: The wealth he gives often turns to leaves, stones, or dung after a short time, or comes with a curse.

Supernatural strength and speed

Velnias is physically powerful and can move quickly, often outrunning humans or gods.

Limit: He can be outwitted by cleverness or by the use of specific objects like a needle in a haystack.

Cursing and hexing

Velnias can place curses on people, animals, or crops, causing illness, misfortune, or death.

Limit: His curses can be broken by counter-spells, holy water, or the help of a wise person (zintnieks).

Underworld authority

Velnias rules over the dead and the underworld, judging souls or tormenting sinners.

Limit: He cannot keep a soul if a deity or a clever mortal intervenes.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Velnias is often depicted as a dark, hairy being with horns, hooves, and a tail, similar to the Christian devil. However, in older folklore, he may appear as a handsome man, a black dog, a serpent, or a shadowy figure. He can shapeshift into various animals, especially black cats, goats, or ravens.

Symbols — Velnias is associated with the underworld, darkness, fire, and the wilderness. He is often depicted with a whip or a club, and sometimes with a bag of coins. In Latvian folklore, he is connected to the sea and the forest, and is said to live in a castle under the earth or in a deep lake.

Domains — Velnias dwells in the underworld, called Velniava or Velns' realm, but he frequently comes to the surface to meddle in human affairs. He is associated with forests, marshes, crossroads, and bodies of water. He is also linked to storms and fire, and is the enemy of the thunder god Perkūnas.

The Devil's Bargain with the FarmerLatvian folk tale

Unknown (pre-Christian origin)

A farmer makes a deal with Velns to get a good harvest. Velns demands the soul of the farmer's child, but the farmer, with the help of a clever servant, tricks Velns into accepting a pile of stones instead. Velns is furious and leaves, but the farmer must still pay a price in the end. The farmer loses his soul or a portion of his wealth, but the trickery shows that Velns can be deceived.

Accounts II
Perkūnas and VelniasLithuanian mythology

Ancient

Perkūnas, the thunder god, pursues Velnias across the sky and earth. Velnias hides under rocks, in trees, or in water, but Perkūnas strikes him with lightning. This myth explains the origin of thunder and lightning, and why Velnias is associated with fire and the underworld. Velnias is wounded or killed, but he always returns, symbolizing the eternal struggle between order and chaos.

Velnias as a Culture HeroLatvian folk songs

Unknown

In some dainas, Velns is depicted as a being who taught humans how to forge iron, brew beer, or make fire. He is a trickster who brings knowledge but also mischief. Humans gain valuable skills but also suffer from Velns' unpredictable nature.

Velnias is the same as the Christian Satan. — Velnias has pre-Christian roots and was not originally a purely evil being. He is a complex figure with both benevolent and malevolent aspects.

Velnias was a god of evil and darkness in ancient Baltic religion. — He was likely a chthonic deity associated with the underworld, wealth, and magic, not evil as understood in Christian terms.

Velnias was a dragon or serpent. — This is a misinterpretation of some mythological motifs, but Velnias is generally depicted as a humanoid or shapeshifter, not a dragon.
