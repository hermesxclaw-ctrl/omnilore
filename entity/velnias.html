Who It Is

Velnias is the chthonic devil-trickster of Baltic mythology, a complex figure who is neither purely evil (like the Christian Satan) nor merely a demon. He is the lord of the underworld, cattle, and wealth — the god of the dark, wild, chthonic realm that contrasts with Dievs's orderly heavens. Velnias is frequently depicted as a horned, black man with cloven hooves, a tail, and one eye — or as a snake, a black dog, or a raven. In the dainas, Velnias steals the sun or the moon from the sky, and Perkūns (the thunder god) pursues him with lightning. Velnias hides in rocks, trees, and water; when lightning strikes a tree, it is Perkūns striking at the devil. Despite being a divine adversary, Velnias is often more foolish than evil in folk tales — he is constantly outwitted by humans. He is bound by strict rules: he cannot break a promise, and he loses his power if anyone speaks his name in daylight. In Prussian myth, Velnias (Patollu) was worshipped as a god of death and wealth. The Velnias-Perku¯nas conflict represents the fundamental Baltic cosmic dualism: order vs. chaos, sky vs. earth, culture vs. wilderness. Unlike the wholly evil Christian devil, Velnias is an integral part of the cosmic balance — without him, the world would have no contrast.
