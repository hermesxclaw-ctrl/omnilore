Accounts

Plato recounts how Solon learned from Egyptian priests of a powerful island civilization beyond the Pillars of Hercules (Gibraltar) that existed 9,000 years before Solon's time. Atlantis was ruled by a confederation of kings descended from Poseidon and the mortal Cleito. It was a wealthy, technologically advanced, and militarily powerful state that attempted to conquer the Mediterranean. In a single day and night, Atlantis was destroyed by earthquakes and floods, sinking beneath the sea. Plato's account is widely regarded by scholars as a philosophical allegory or literary invention, not a historical record.

The Kingdom of Atlantia is one of the SCA's twenty kingdoms, covering Maryland, Virginia, North Carolina, South Carolina, and the District of Columbia. It was founded in 1981 and holds annual events, tournaments, and arts competitions. The name evokes the legendary Atlantis, giving the kingdom a romantic, mythic identity within the SCA's medieval-revival framework.

A contemporary fantasy-drama series set in a fictional coastal town called Atlantia, where supernatural events and mysteries unfold. The series ran for one season (12 episodes) and was not renewed. It features themes of mystery, family secrets, and supernatural phenomena.

A young adult dystopian-fantasy novel set in a fictional underwater city called Atlantia, where humans live beneath the sea. The protagonist, Rio, must navigate political intrigue and her own hidden powers. The title evokes Atlantis but the city is not a deity.

Scholarly Debates
Is Atlantia an ancient deity?
Yes — some modern neopagan or esoteric sources claim Atlantia is a goddess of the sea, Atlantis, or the Atlantic. ⇌ No — no ancient, medieval, or early modern source attests any deity named 'Atlantia'.
Evidence for A: Modern internet sources, unverified claims in occult forums.
Evidence for B: Absence from all standard mythographical reference works (Smith, Pauly-Wissowa, etc.) and from primary ancient texts.
Is Atlantia the same as Atlantis?
Yes — the name is a variant or personification of the legendary island. ⇌ No — Atlantis is a place in Plato's dialogue; Atlantia is a modern derivative name used for unrelated fictional places and organizations.
Evidence for A: Etymological similarity; modern usage often evokes Atlantis.
Evidence for B: No ancient source uses 'Atlantia'; modern usages diverge widely (SCA kingdom, Italian TV town, underwater city).

A way to hold it in your mind

Atlantia is like a name shouted into a canyon: the echo sounds real, but the voice that made it is missing. It's the mythological equivalent of a '404 Not Found' — a placeholder for a deity that never was. If you want an actual ancient sea-goddess, look to Amphitrite, Tethys, or the Nereids. Atlantia is the name of a longing, not a being — a beautiful, empty vessel waiting for someone to fill it with meaning.
