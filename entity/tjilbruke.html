Who It Is

Tjilbruke (also Tjirbruki) is the principal ancestral hero and law-giver of the Kaurna people of the Adelaide Plains in South Australia, a figure of profound emotional and cultural significance. The Tjilbruke Dreaming story is one of the most celebrated and widely shared narratives in contemporary South Australian Aboriginal culture. According to Kaurna tradition, Tjilbruke was a powerful ancestral man and hunter who lived at a time when birds and animals were still people. When his nephew Kulultuwi was killed for killing a female emu in violation of the Law, Tjilbruke carried his nephew's body along the coast south of what is now Adelaide, from Warriparinga to Cape Jervis, weeping as he walked. At each place he rested and wept, his tears created freshwater springs along the coastline — the Tjilbruke Springs that still exist today, forming a physical trail of sacred sites. At the end of his journey, at Rapid Bay, Tjilbruke placed his nephew's body in a cave and, overcome with grief, transformed himself into a glossy ibis (a waterbird), renouncing his human existence. The Tjilbruke Dreaming track is now recognized as a significant cultural landscape, and the Kaurna people have actively revived this tradition as part of broader cultural reclamation in post-colonial South Australia. Tjilbruke embodies the Kaurna understanding of Law, family obligation, grief, and the transformation of emotional experience into permanent features of the landscape. His story also carries ecological teaching: the prohibition against killing a female emu reflects sustainable hunting practices and respect for reproductive cycles. The Kaurna people's cultural revival since the 1970s has placed Tjilbruke at the center of contemporary Aboriginal identity in the Adelaide region, and his story is now taught in South Australian schools, performed in public ceremonies, and memorialized in public art.
