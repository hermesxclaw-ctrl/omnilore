Who It Is

Tjilbruke is an important creation ancestor for the Kaurna people, and his story has been passed down through generations. However, there are still many gaps in our understanding of his role in the creation of the Tjilbruke Dreaming Trail and the significance of his tears.

Tjilbruke is known for his kindness, generosity, and respect towards neighbouring peoples, while also being a fierce protector of the Kaurna territory and its animals.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Tjilbruke is derived from the Kaurna word 'tjil', meaning 'to cry' or 'to weep', and 'bruke', meaning 'man' or 'person'.

Pronunciation — CHIL-broo-keh

Aliases — The Ancestral Man, The Law Giver, The Grieving Father

Ownership — The Kaurna people of the Adelaide Plains, South Australia, consider Tjilbruke an important creation ancestor and a symbol of their cultural heritage.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Tjilbruke is often depicted as a powerful and wise ancestral figure, with a strong connection to the land and its creatures.

Symbols — The Tjilbruke Dreaming Trail is a major Dreaming trail that connects sites from within metropolitan Adelaide southwards as far as Cape Jervis, some of which are Aboriginal sacred sites of great significance.

Domains — The Tjilbruke Dreaming Trail spans across various domains and habitats, including the Adelaide Plains, the coast, and the surrounding mountains.

Accounts I
The Creation of the Tjilbruke Dreaming TrailWikipedia

Pre-colonial era

Tjilbruke created the Tjilbruke Dreaming Trail, a major Dreaming trail that connects sites from within metropolitan Adelaide southwards as far as Cape Jervis, some of which are Aboriginal sacred sites of great significance. The creation of the Tjilbruke Dreaming Trail had a profound impact on the Kaurna people, shaping their culture, spirituality, and connection to the land.

Accounts II
Tjilbruke's Tears and the Formation of SpringsNotability

Pre-colonial era

Tjilbruke's tears formed springs along the coast, which became sacred sites for the Kaurna people. The formation of springs had a significant impact on the Kaurna people, providing them with a source of water and spiritual significance.
