The red moon hangs low, and the line between man and beast is blurred. And behind it all, something watches. Something that has been watching since the first Hunt, and will watch until the last.

Who It Is

The Moon Presence is Bloodborne's secret final boss and the hidden architect of the entire Hunt — a Great One who created the Hunter's Dream as a trap, using Hunters as its agents to slay other Great Ones and their children. It is encountered only if the player has consumed three 'One Third of Umbilical Cord' items throughout the game, unlocking the true ending. When the player defeats Gehrman the First Hunter (the Moon Presence's imprisoned proxy), the Moon Presence descends from the blood-red moon itself — a skeletal, emaciated creature with a body like exposed muscle and sinew, a long, flowing tail/tentacles, and a featureless, vaguely skull-like face. It embraces the player in what appears at first to be a gesture of welcome, before its eyes begin to glow and it attempts to enslave the player as the new host of the Hunter's Dream.

The Moon Presence's motivations are among Bloodborne's deepest mysteries. Unlike other Great Ones, who seem driven by instinct to reproduce (and inevitably lose their children), the Moon Presence appears more calculating. It established the Hunter's Dream to create an endless cycle: Hunters kill beasts (who were once humans transformed by the Old Blood), grow stronger by consuming Blood Echoes, and eventually kill Great Ones — all of which feeds power back to the Moon Presence. Gehrman, the first Hunter trapped in the Dream, is both its warden and its prisoner, eternally guiding new Hunters to perpetuate the cycle. The final boss fight is a direct confrontation with the source of Yharnam's endless nightmare.

Mechanically, the Moon Presence is a terrifying opponent. It can reduce the player's health to 1 HP with a single gaze, forcing frantic healing while it closes in. It summons blood-magic projectiles, unleashes area-of-effect screams, and moves with an alien, floating grace entirely unlike any beast or humanoid boss in the game. Its design is deliberately unnerving — a creature that looks like a flayed human if a flayed human were also a cosmic entity, its movements somewhere between a marionette and a deep-sea creature.

The Moon Presence represents Bloodborne's cosmic horror at its purest: humanity is not at the center of the universe; we are pawns in a game played by entities we cannot comprehend. The player's ultimate triumph — if they consume the Umbilical Cords and resist the Moon Presence — is to transcend the game entirely: defeating the Moon Presence transforms the player into a newborn Great One, an infant squid-creature cradled by the Doll in the Dream. It is an ending that is simultaneously triumphant and terrifying — you have become what you fought, and what that means for humanity is unknowable.
