Accounts I
Yamata no Orochi - Mythological Foundation for KOFKojiki and Nihon Shoki

8th century CE (text compilation); mythic time

The storm god Susanoo slays the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi and finds the sword Kusanagi no Tsurugi in its tail. The sword, together with Yata no Kagami (mirror) and Yasakani no Magatama (jewel), becomes the Sanshu no Jingi. SNK explicitly cites this legend as the basis for the Orochi saga and the naming of Kyo Kusanagi (sword), Chizuru Kagura/Yata (mirror), and Iori Yagami/Yasakani (jewel). This is franchise-level background, not a Jun Kagami-specific story, but explains the thematic space a character named Kagami would occupy. Establishes the Three Sacred Treasures and the Orochi as a sealed calamity; provides narrative justification for clan rivalries and sealing rituals that drive KOF '94 through '97 and recur in later arcs. Any character named Kagami would be read against this mirror-treasure context.

The King of Fighters '94 - The Inaugural TournamentThe King of Fighters '94 (SNK, arcade/Neo Geo)

1994 (release); in-universe 1994

Crime lord Rugal Bernstein invites teams of three to a new tournament. Japan Team (Kyo Kusanagi, Benimaru Nikaido, Goro Daimon) emerges as protagonists. This establishes the 3-on-3 team format kept from SNK's earlier side-scrolling prototype 'Survivor' and introduces the cross-over concept (Art of Fighting, Fatal Fury, Ikari Warriors, Psycho Soldier characters). No verified participation by Jun Kagami in this tournament was located. Launches the franchise and the Orochi Saga; establishes Kyo as 'snazzy' hero originally named Syo Kirishima, renamed to tie to Kusanagi legend.

The Orochi Saga Climax - KOF '95 to '97The King of Fighters '95, '96, '97 (SNK)

1995-1997 (releases); in-universe 1995-1997

Kyo's rivalry with Iori Yagami intensifies as the Hakkeshu and Orochi's revival unfold. Chizuru Kagura, heir to the Yata mirror, debuts in KOF '96 to enlist Kyo and Iori to reseal Orochi, culminating in Orochi's defeat in '97. The mirror's power is depicted as duplication/illusion and sealing. This saga defines the 'mirror' role in KOF. Jun Kagami's relation to this mirror lineage, if any, is unverified. Orochi sealed; establishes Sacred Treasures team dynamic; Chizuru becomes the definitive mirror-bearer in canon.

The NESTS Chronicles - KOF '99 to 2001The King of Fighters '99, 2000, 2001 (SNK, later Eolith/BrezzaSoft)

1999-2001 (releases); in-universe 1999-2001

New protagonist K', a 'dark hero' created to contrast Kyo, seeks to destroy the NESTS cartel that kidnapped him and erased his memories to make him a weapon, infused with Kyo's flame via Kyo's DNA. NESTS creates clones (Krizalid, K9999) and experiments. The saga ends with NESTS' destruction. Introduces concepts of manufactured fighters and stolen powers. No verified appearance or mention of Jun Kagami in NESTS lab logs or team rosters was located. Shifts focus from mythology to bio-engineering conspiracy; explains SNK's intent to temporarily remove Kyo/Iori (scrapped) and add middle-aged characters to balance teenage cast.

Tales of Ash and Those From the Past - KOF 2003 to XIIIThe King of Fighters 2003, XI, XII, XIII (SNK Playmore)

2003-2010 (releases); in-universe 2003-2010s

Mysterious fighter Ash Crimson with green flames steals the powers of the Sacred Treasures clans (including Chizuru's mirror power in KOF 2003) for unknown reasons, while the group Those From the Past seeks Orochi's power for their master (later revealed as Saiki). Ash's arc ends with his self-sacrifice/time paradox. This saga directly involves theft and transfer of the mirror power, the thematic domain of any Kagami character. Mirror power is shown as stealable/transferable; creates time-travel paradox that later connects to Shun'ei (possessing unknown supernatural powers due to Ash's paradox).

The Shun'ei Saga and Verse - KOF XIV to XVThe King of Fighters XIV (2016), XV (2022) (SNK)

2016-2022 (releases); in-universe post-Ash

New protagonist Shun'ei, a young Chinese fighter with hydrokinetic-like supernatural powers linked to Ash's paradox, headlines a new arc alongside Verse, an entity containing souls including Orochi. Art director Nobuyuki Kuroki and planner Eisuke Ogura distanced newcomers Shun'ei and Sylvie from veterans; Kyo and Iori received major redesigns. Antonov, a new boss, was modeled by Yasuyuki Oda mixing traits of SNK founder Chairman Kawasaki and an executive. No verified team entry for Jun Kagami in XIV or XV default rosters was located; potential appearance in DLC, spin-off, or background lore remains unverified. Establishes new supernatural lineage not tied to Orochi; demonstrates SNK's current design philosophy of 'Kyo-ify' (Oda's term for making fighters as stylish as Kyo) and balancing appeal across generations.
