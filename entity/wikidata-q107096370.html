Who It Is

On the steep crags of Mount Hua, where the clouds tear against granite, there reigns a god who commands the ranks of immortals and ghosts, whose white dragon descends with the authority of the Jade Emperor himself. Xiyue Dadi, the Great Emperor of the Western Summit, is not merely a mountain spirit; he is a cosmic administrator, a judge of souls, and a father whose divine family's scandals echo through Chinese legend. His story spans dynasties, from Shang oracle bones to Tang imperial edicts, and his temple still stands as a silent witness to centuries of devotion.

As a cosmic administrator, he is just and authoritative, overseeing rewards and punishments. In folklore, he is depicted as stern and sometimes morally flawed, preying on mortal women.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Xiyue (西岳) literally means 'Western Summit' or 'Western Peak', referring to Mount Hua (华山), the westernmost of China's Five Sacred Mountains. Dadi (大帝) means 'Great Emperor'.

Pronunciation — Shee-yueh Dah-dee

Aliases — Emperor Xiyue, Great Emperor of the Western Summit, God of Mount Hua, King of the Metal Heavens, Jintian Yuansheng Emperor of Mount Hua, Suyuan Yaopo Daming Zhenjun

Ownership — Chinese, particularly Taoist and folk religion; venerated across China but especially in Shaanxi province near Mount Hua.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Wears a white robe emblazoned with the divine eye of Mount Hua; wears a crown symbolizing the nine schools of the Tai Chu era; carries the seal of Heavenly Communication of Truth; rides a white dragon.

Symbols — White color, dragon, divine eye, seal, mountain imagery.

Domains — Mount Hua (Western Sacred Mountain), his temple nearby; also associated with the west, metal element, and autumn.

Accounts I
Origin as Ancestor of DayuTradition traced to Shang dynasty

Shang dynasty (c. 1600-1046 BCE)

Worship of Xiyue Dadi is said to date to the Shang dynasty, when he was revered as the ancestor of Dayu, the legendary founder of the Xia dynasty. Establishes ancient pedigree for the deity.

Imperial Recognition under Han and TangHistorical records

Han and Tang dynasties

Emperor Wu of Han built a large-scale sacrificial temple. Emperor Xuanzong of Tang designated Mount Hua as the sacred mountain of the Tang royal family and bestowed the title 'King of the Metal Heavens'. Imperial worship followed rituals for the Three Great Gods. Elevated status of the deity and Mount Hua.

Accounts II
The Divine Family and the Legend of ChenxiangLocal folklore; later literary adaptations

Unknown; popular in later centuries

The god of Mount Hua had a divine family, but he preyed on mortal women, and his wives had affairs with mortals. His third daughter, Huayue Sanniang, married a mortal, Liu Xiang, against his wishes. She was imprisoned beneath Mount Hua for violating heavenly rules. Her son, Chenxiang, split the mountain with an axe to rescue her. Led to the legends of 'Chenxiang' and 'The Magic Lotus Lantern'.

Deification in Fengshen YanyiFengshen Yanyi (Investiture of the Gods)

Ming dynasty (16th century)

In the novel, Jiang Xiong, a general, is killed by Zhang Kui. After death, Jiang Ziya deifies him as the Jintian Yuansheng Emperor of Mount Hua, one of the Emperors of the Five Sacred Mountains. Gives a specific mortal identity to Xiyue Dadi.

Incarnation as GaoyaoRomance of Immortals of Past Dynasties

Ming dynasty

Gaoyao underwent a transformation or incarnation by Mount Hua and was bestowed the title 'Suyuan Yaopo Daming Zhenjun', responsible for overseeing the world's treasures and hardware, including pottery casting pits, and the management of feathers and birds. Provides an alternative origin and role.
