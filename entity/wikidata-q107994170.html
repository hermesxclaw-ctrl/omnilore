Who It Is

Ran Mitake doesn't want to change — and that's exactly why she's afraid everything already has. The cool, sharp-tongued vocalist of Afterglow has sung beside the same four childhood friends since they were small enough to get lost together on the way home, but as high school pulls each of them toward different futures, Ran's greatest fear isn't failing on stage. It's that the sunset glow her band is named for will fade, and she'll be left singing alone.

Canonically cool, blunt, and outwardly aloof — described in official profiles as 'cool and a little difficult to approach' — but intensely loyal to her four childhood friends. Ran values constancy and fears change; her central internal conflict is wanting everything to stay the same while knowing her friends are growing and diverging. She is stubborn, has difficulty expressing vulnerable feelings directly, and often covers worry with sharp remarks or silence, a classic tsundere pattern noted in both game and anime. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Family name Mitake (美竹) combines mi (美, beautiful) + take (竹, bamboo). Given name Ran (蘭) means orchid, a flower associated with elegance and refinement in Japanese hanakotoba. The floral meaning mirrors Afterglow's recurring flower and sunset imagery and contrasts with Ran's cool, prickly exterior.

Pronunciation — Japanese: Mitake Ran [mi.ta.ke ɾaɴ] ; English approximation: mee-tah-keh RAHN. Pitch accent on MI-take. Voice actress Ayane Sakura pronounces with crisp, low-toned delivery consistent with character's cool image.

Aliases — Ran, Ran-chan, Mitake

Lead Vocals - Cool Rock Timbre

Ran is Afterglow's lead vocalist, characterized by a powerful, slightly husky alto with strong chest voice and controlled vibrato. In-universe she is considered one of the stronger vocalists among Haneoka/Hanasakigawa bands, often compared directly to Yukina Minato. Repertoire spans original Afterglow rock songs and numerous cover songs in Girls Band Party!.

Limit: Not classically trained; struggles with high sustained notes compared to Yukina, leading to insecurity. Performance anxiety when emotional distress affects breath control (depicted in Band Story 2).

Rhythm Guitar

Plays rhythm guitar while singing, providing chordal foundation while Moca Aoba handles lead guitar. In lore she has played since childhood alongside Moca, with competent but not virtuosic technique — Afterglow's strength is cohesion, not technical flash.

Limit: In live-action reality, voice actress Ayane Sakura does not play guitar live; Afterglow live performances are vocal-only with backing tracks. In-story, Ran is not a lead soloist and defers complex solos to Moca.

Songwriting and Lyrical Voice

Afterglow songs are credited in-universe to the band collectively, but Ran's perspective dominates lyrics about constancy, sunset, and not wanting to change. Elements Garden's Asuka Oda writes actual lyrics to reflect Ran's viewpoint. Ran is not depicted as primary composer like Yukina or Chiyu.

Limit: Rarely initiates songwriting alone; relies on band input and Oda's external writing. Creative block tied to emotional state.

Emotional Anchor and Childhood Cohesion

Ran's longest-standing ability is social: she is the emotional center that kept five neighborhood friends playing together for nearly a decade. Her determination to keep Afterglow 'the same as always' sustains the band through periods when members consider quitting or drifting.

Limit: This same attachment becomes a weakness — fear of change causes her to lash out, withdraw, or reject help, temporarily destabilizing the band.

Accounts I
Childhood Promise - The Formation of AfterglowBanG Dream! Girls Band Party! Afterglow Band Story 0: 'Afterglow, The Same As Always'

2017 (depicts elementary school circa 6-7 years before main story)

Five neighborhood children — Ran, Moca, Himari, Tomoe and Tsugumi — bond over playing together after school. What begins as hanging out at Hazawa Coffee Shop and making noise with toy instruments becomes a real band when they decide to keep playing together 'as always.' Ran, already cool and a little bossy, becomes vocalist almost by default because she is the most willing to sing loudly. The story establishes the core promise that defines Ran's character: that the five will stay together and remain unchanged, a promise that will later haunt her when adolescence forces change. Establishes Afterglow as a pre-existing childhood band, unlike the school-formed Poppin'Party and Roselia, and sets up Ran's central fear of separation.

That Is How I Roll! - Ran's Insecurity and the Rivalry with YukinaBanG Dream! Girls Band Party! Afterglow Band Story 1: 'That Is How I Roll!' and Single 'That Is How I Roll!' (2017)

2017-2018 (first year high school)

After entering Haneoka, Afterglow confronts that other bands are improving rapidly. Ran becomes fixated on Roselia's vocalist Yukina Minato, whose powerful, disciplined vocals make Ran feel inadequate. She pushes herself to practice alone, refusing to burden friends, and snaps at Moca when Moca tries to lighten the mood. The band argues about whether they need to change to get better. Through a joint practice and a live-house show, Ran learns that Afterglow's strength is not becoming Roselia but trusting their own natural, long-nurtured chemistry. First major articulation of Ran's competitive drive and inferiority complex; cements Yukina as respectful rival rather than enemy; leads to Afterglow's debut single thematically.

Scarlet Sky and the Father's ShadowBanG Dream! Girls Band Party! Event Story 'Scarlet Sky' (2018) and Afterglow Band Story 2: 'One of Us'

2018 (in-game event, second year)

Ran's father, a respected professional in traditional Japanese flower arrangement (ikebana), disapproves of Ran's band activities and wants her to follow a more formal, traditional path. Ran, who admires but also resents her father's strictness, has been distant since middle school. When her father attends an Afterglow live without telling her, Ran freezes on stage and later argues with him about whether music is a frivolous hobby. The band supports her without forcing a resolution, and Ran eventually performs 'Scarlet Sky,' a song whose lyrics about a red sky at dusk mirror her tangled feelings about family expectations and wanting to be seen for who she is. Deepens Ran's backstory beyond 'cool girl' archetype; explains her aversion to being seen as childish or dependent; song 'Scarlet Sky' becomes signature emotional piece for the band.

Accounts II
One of Us - The Fear That Friendship Will EndBanG Dream! Girls Band Party! Afterglow Band Story 3: 'One of Us' (2019)

2019 (second to third year transition)

As graduation and future paths loom, Himari considers a different college, Tomoe talks about work, and Moca jokes about going separate ways — all normal growth that terrifies Ran. She becomes withdrawn, insisting 'Afterglow is the same as always' even as she senses it isn't. During a joint camp/training arc, Ran finally admits she is scared that 'same as always' is a lie and that she will be left behind if everyone changes. The other four reassure her not by promising nothing will change, but by promising they will keep choosing to play together even as they change, reframing 'same as always' from stasis to commitment. Resolves Ran's core internal contradiction; redefines Afterglow's band philosophy for later stories; directly quoted in later anniversary dialogue.

Girls Band Challenge and Stay Gold - Choosing What Afterglow Plays ForBanG Dream! Anime 3rd Season (2020) Episodes 2-5; Girls Band Party! Event 'Stay Gold' and Album 'Stay Gold' (2022)

2020-2022 (third year, Girls Band Challenge arc)

All Hanasakigawa/Haneoka bands are invited to the Girls Band Challenge, a competition that forces bands to articulate why they play. Afterglow initially struggles because they have no grand ambition like Roselia's aim for the top or Poppin'Party's Star Beat — they just want to play together. Ran, now more mature after reconciling with her father and friends, leads the band to embrace that as a valid reason. They perform without pretending to be something else, earning respect even without winning the grand prize. The 'Stay Gold' era cements this as Afterglow's mature identity: not chasing gold for its shine, but staying true. Provides narrative closure to Ran's arc from fear of change to acceptance; positions Afterglow as thematic foil to ambition-driven bands; album 'Stay Gold' becomes retrospective of their journey.

Cover Culture - Afterglow as the Cover BandBanG Dream! Girls Band Party! Cover Songs Collection Vol.1-8 (2017-2024) and in-game Cover Events

2017-2024 ongoing

Out-of-universe but canonically acknowledged: Afterglow is designated in-game as one of the primary cover bands, performing rock-leaning covers of anime and J-pop hits such as 'Hey-day Capriccio,' 'True Color,' and Vocaloid songs. Ran as vocalist carries these covers, showcasing her versatility beyond originals. Events like 'Summerly Tone!' and 'Sparkling Everyday!' frame covers as Afterglow's way of sharing songs they loved as children. This meta-story explains why Afterglow has the largest cover discography among early bands and why Ran's vocal is the most heard by casual players. Establishes Ran's voice as franchise workhorse for covers; drives streaming and player engagement; influences real-world perception of Ran as 'reliable rock vocalist.'

Ran is cold and hates her friends or is a bully — Ran is blunt and struggles to express vulnerability, but is deeply loyal and protective. Her sharp remarks cover anxiety about losing friends, not dislike. She consistently sacrifices for the band and is most hurt when she thinks she has pushed friends away.

Ran and Yukina are enemies who hate each other — They are respectful rivals. Ran admires Yukina's vocal discipline and uses her as a benchmark; Yukina acknowledges Ran's emotional power. They perform together at joint lives and offer blunt but constructive feedback. No canon enmity.

Afterglow is less serious because they are a 'cover band' and don't write originals — Afterglow has a full original discography ('That Is How I Roll!', 'Scarlet Sky,' 'Y.O.L.O!!!!!', 'Stay Gold' etc.) with lyrics by Asuka Oda reflecting Ran's perspective. Their designation as in-game cover band is a gameplay role to provide popular covers, not a lore statement that they lack originals.

Ayane Sakura plays guitar live as Ran — Afterglow is a vocals-only live unit. While Poppin'Party, Roselia, RAISE A SUILEN, Morfonica, MyGO!!!!! and Ave Mujica voice actresses play instruments live, Afterglow, Pastel Palettes and Hello, Happy World! perform vocals with backing tracks. Ayane Sakura sings but does not play guitar on stage.
