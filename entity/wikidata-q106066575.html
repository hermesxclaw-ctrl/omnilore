The Approach

In the shadow of a legendary king's ambition, a prince's death would echo across centuries, becoming the very foundation upon which the game of chess was said to be built. Talhand, a figure known almost entirely through the lore surrounding his brother's grief, stands as a poignant symbol of fraternal tragedy and the transformative power of loss.

Content advisory. Depictions of death in battle and themes of fratricidal conflict, though the exact historical veracity of these accounts is unverified and likely legendary.

Stories & Myths

The Death of Talhand and the Invention of Chess

Source Persian legendary tradition, possibly as retold in medieval Persian textsCulture Persian (possibly with Indian roots)Period Unknown; likely pre-Islamic or early Islamic era

Summary

Two brothers, Talhand and Gav, fought a war to determine succession to their father's kingdom. Talhand was killed in the battle. The grieving mother (or brother) demanded a representation of the battlefield, leading to the invention of chess as a symbolic recreation of the conflict.

Consequences

The invention of chess, which served as a memorial to Talhand and a means to explain the tragedy to his mother.

Variants

In some versions, the game is invented by Gav to comfort his mother; in others, it is invented by a sage. The number of pieces and their movements may also vary.
