Who It Is

Esege Malan ('Father Baldy' or 'Bald-Headed Father') is the supreme sky deity and creator god of the Buryat Mongols, the largest indigenous Siberian ethnic group related to the Mongols. He is an aged, wise god whose bald head symbolizes his ancient, pre-existent nature — he existed before all other gods. Esege Malan dwells in the western sky and created the universe by dividing the primordial waters from the sky. He established the order of the cosmos, assigning each god their domain. Unlike the more abstract Tengri, Esege Malan is a personal, anthropomorphic god — he is depicted as an old man with a long white beard, dressed in white furs, seated on a throne of clouds. He is the father of many deities, including the 99 Tenger (sky spirits) who govern specific aspects of the natural world. Esege Malan is associated with justice, law (especially Buryat customary law), and seasonal cycles. Buryat shamans offered him white horses and dairy products. The cult of Esege Malan was particularly strong among the western Buryats living around Lake Baikal, who maintained their shamanic traditions longer than many other Mongolic groups. With the Soviet suppression of religion in the 20th century, the Esege Malan cult went underground but has seen a revival since the 1990s as part of Buryat cultural reclamation.

The Approach

In the boundless dark before time, when the universe was silent and empty, there existed only one being: Esege Malan, the Highest God of the Buryat people. From a handful of earth, he shaped the sun and the moon, and from his will sprang all living things. Yet even a creator may face trials, for when Mother Earth asked for the celestial lights, Esege Malan found that giving them away was no simple task. This is the story of the Sky Father who rules the western horizon and convenes the sky-spirits in the Pleiades.

Etymology

The name 'Esege' likely derives from Mongolian 'eseg' meaning 'father' or 'ancestor', and 'Malan' may relate to 'bald' or 'smooth', possibly a descriptive epithet. In Buryat, 'Esege' is simply 'father', indicating a paternal creator figure.

Cultural Ownership

Buryat and Mongolian mythology, particularly the Buryat people of Siberia and Mongolia. He is a sky god and creator deity within the broader Tengrist tradition.

Disputed Identifications

There is scholarly debate about the relationship between Esege Malan and the Turkic deity Ülgen or the Mongolian Tengri. Some sources equate him with Tengri, the supreme sky god, while others consider him a distinct Buryat manifestation. The identification is not universally accepted.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with the western horizon, the Pleiades, and the moon. He is a sky deity, so celestial symbols are relevant. No specific iconography is documented.

Behaviour & Personality

As the highest god, he is the creator and ruler of the sky-spirits. He calls meetings of the sky-spirits in the Pleiades and on the moon. He is depicted as a paternal figure who creates the world but also faces challenges, as seen in the story of Mother Earth requesting the sun and moon.

Domains & Habitats

Resides in the sky (Tenger), ruling over the western horizon. He is the chief of the sky-spirits (Tengri).
