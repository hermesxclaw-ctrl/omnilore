Who It Is

Deep within the forgotten corners of speculative biology, there exists a concept so mutable it defies simple classification: the mutant plant. Not a single species, but a testament to nature's relentless capacity for change, this entity embodies the terrifying and wondrous potential of genetic deviation. From the irradiated wastelands of post-apocalyptic fiction to the laboratory vats of genetic engineering, the mutant plant represents our deepest fears and hopes about the natural world—and ourselves.

Behavior ranges from passive, mindless growth to intelligent, malevolent cunning. Some are portrayed as predatory, trapping and consuming animals and humans, while others are tragic figures, victims of mutation seeking to regain their former selves.

Powers
Enhanced Growth

Mutant plants can grow at accelerated rates, quickly overrunning environments and structures.

Limit: Often requires specific conditions like radiation or nutrient-rich soil.

Mobility

Some mutant plants can move, either by uprooting and walking or by extending tendrils to drag themselves.

Limit: Movement is often slow and cumbersome.

Toxic Spores or Venom

Many mutant plants produce poisonous spores, stinging tendrils, or venomous sap.

Limit: Often requires direct contact or close proximity.

Carnivory

A subset of mutant plants are carnivorous, trapping and digesting animals or humans.

Limit: Typically requires a food source and may be limited in size.

Sentience and Intelligence

Some mutant plants possess human-level or superhuman intelligence, capable of communication and complex planning.

Limit: Intelligence is often tied to specific mutations or alien origins.

Regeneration

Mutant plants can often regrow damaged parts or even regenerate from small fragments.

Limit: May be limited by environmental factors like fire or specific chemicals.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Mutant plants vary widely, but common traits include exaggerated size, abnormal coloration (often vivid greens, purples, or bioluminescent hues), thorny or tentacle-like appendages, and the ability to move or attack. Some are depicted as humanoid, while others are formless masses of vegetation.

Symbols — Mutant plants often symbolize nature's revenge, the consequences of unchecked scientific progress, or the horror of uncontrolled growth. They are frequently depicted in post-apocalyptic settings as survivors of human folly.

Domains — Mutant plants are commonly found in radioactive wastelands, overgrown forests, secret laboratories, and alien worlds. They thrive in environments altered by catastrophe or human intervention.

Accounts I
The Day of the TriffidsJohn Wyndham's novel (1951)

Post-World War II

A meteor shower blinds most of humanity, and the triffids—carnivorous, mobile plants with stinging tentacles—rise up to dominate the world. The protagonist, Bill Masen, navigates the ruins of society with a small group of survivors. Humanity is reduced to scattered communities, and the triffids become the new dominant species.

Little Shop of HorrorsRoger Corman's film (1960) and musical (1982)

1960s/1980s

A hapless florist's assistant, Seymour, discovers a strange plant that feeds on human blood. As the plant grows, it demands more, leading to murder and chaos. Audrey II grows to enormous size and attempts to take over the world, but is ultimately destroyed.

Accounts II
Swamp ThingDC Comics (1971)

1970s to present

Scientist Alec Holland is transformed into a plant-elemental creature after an explosion in his lab. He battles supernatural threats and struggles with his humanity. Swamp Thing becomes a guardian of the Green, the elemental force of plant life.

The RuinsScott Smith's novel (2006) and film (2008)

21st century

A group of tourists become trapped on a Mayan ruin overgrown with a sentient, carnivorous vine that mimics sounds and infects wounds. The group is slowly killed by the plant, which uses their bodies as fertilizer.

Nausicaä of the Valley of the WindHayao Miyazaki's manga (1982-1994) and film (1984)

1980s

In a post-apocalyptic world covered by a toxic jungle filled with mutated insects and plants, Princess Nausicaä seeks to understand the ecosystem and find a way for humans and nature to coexist. Nausicaä discovers that the jungle is actually purifying the Earth, and humans must adapt.
