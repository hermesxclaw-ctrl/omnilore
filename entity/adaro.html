Who It Is

In the sun-drenched waters of Makira, the largest island of the Solomon Islands' San Cristobal group, there lurks a spirit of the sea that rides the sun's path from dawn to dusk. The Adaro, a being of both awe and dread, is said to possess a gill-like fin atop its head and to travel along sunbeams, descending to the ocean depths at nightfall. But these are not mere fish-people; they are tricksters and malevolent forces, capable of stealing the breath of the unwary and bringing sickness and death. To understand the Adaro is to glimpse the rich and complex spiritual world of the Solomon Islanders, where the sea is not just a source of life but a realm of powerful, often dangerous, entities.

Adaro are generally considered malevolent or mischievous. They are known to shoot at people with invisible arrows or darts, causing sickness and death. They are also said to steal the breath or life-force of humans, leading to illness. They are tricksters, but their tricks are often harmful. They are not worshipped but are feared and placated through charms and rituals.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'adaro' is from the languages of Makira Island. The exact linguistic root is not fully documented in available sources, but it is used to denote a class of sea spirits. Some sources suggest a connection to the concept of 'spirit' or 'ghost', but this is speculative.

Pronunciation — AH-dah-roh

Aliases — Sea Sprite of Makira, The Sun-Path Rider

Ownership — Solomon Islands, specifically the island of Makira (San Cristobal) and surrounding areas. The Adaro is part of the indigenous belief systems of the Melanesian peoples of the region

Powers
Sunbeam Travel

The Adaro can travel along sunbeams, using them as pathways from the sea to the land and back. This allows them to move quickly and invisibly.

Limit: This ability is tied to the presence of sunlight; at night, the Adaro are confined to the ocean.

Invisible Arrow Shooting

The Adaro are said to shoot invisible arrows or darts at people, causing sickness and sometimes death. These arrows are not physical but spiritual in nature.

Limit: The effects can be countered by charms and protective rituals.

Breath Stealing

The Adaro are believed to steal the breath or life-force of humans, leading to illness and wasting away.

Limit: Not specified; presumably can be prevented by protective measures.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Adaro is described as having a human-like form but with distinct aquatic features. Most notably, it has a fin or gill-like appendage on the top of its head, resembling a cock's comb. It is said to be able to travel along sunbeams, indicating a possible radiant or translucent quality, though this is not explicitly stated. Symbols — The Adaro is associated with the sun and the sea. Its ability to ride sunbeams makes it a liminal figure between the celestial and aquatic realms. In local art and ritual, it may be represented through carvings or symbols related to the sea and sun, but specific iconography is not well-documented.

Domains — The Adaro dwell in the sea, particularly around the coasts of Makira. They ascend to the surface and travel along sunbeams, which they use as pathways. They are most active during the day when the sun is out, and they descend back into the ocean at night.

Early 20th century (recorded), but beliefs are likely ancient

The Adaro are sea spirits that live in the ocean around Makira. During the day, they rise to the surface and travel along sunbeams, which they use as roads to move across the land. They are dangerous to humans, as they can shoot invisible arrows that cause sickness. They are particularly active during the day, and people are advised to be cautious, especially near the coast. People who are shot by Adaro arrows fall ill and may die unless treated by a shaman or with protective charms.
