Who It Is

Ma'at is the Egyptian goddess of truth, justice, balance, and cosmic order — the fundamental principle that governed the universe, nature, society, and the individual. She is depicted as a woman wearing an ostrich feather on her head, the same feather against which the heart of the deceased was weighed in the Hall of Two Truths. Unlike most deities, Ma'at is both a goddess and an abstract concept: 'ma'at' meant order, truth, and justice in every context. The pharaoh's primary duty was to 'live by Ma'at' — to maintain cosmic and social order against the forces of chaos (isfet). Ma'at's feather is the standard against which every soul is measured in the afterlife: a heart lighter than the feather indicates a righteous life worthy of eternity. Her cult was universal — every temple incorporated Ma'at, and priests performed daily Ma'at-offering rituals. She was the daughter of Ra, married to Thoth, and represented the ethical core of Egyptian civilization for over 3,000 years.

Origins

Ma'at is the daughter of Ra, created at the beginning of time as the organizing principle of the universe. She existed before creation and will exist after it. She is both a goddess (with temples and priests) and an abstract principle that permeates all of existence.
