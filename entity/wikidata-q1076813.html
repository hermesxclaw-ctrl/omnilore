The Approach

When you look up at night in Faerûn, someone is looking back — silver-haired, star-crowned, weeping tears that became floating islands. Selûne is not a distant moon goddess who merely lights the way; she is the Moon itself, a primordial being older than the gods of Toril, locked in an eternal, fratricidal war with her twin sister Shar that has shattered worlds, birthed magic, and cursed and blessed the were-beasts who howl beneath her light.

Content advisory. Contains fantasy religious violence, cosmic horror themes, depictions of lycanthropy and body transformation, and references to betrayal and divine abuse within lore. No graphic sexual violence attested in primary sources.
Identity

Selûne

Pronunciation seh-LOON (/sɛˈluːn/) is the most common at-table pronunciation per Faiths and Pantheons (2002) audio guide; seh-LOO-nay (/sɛˈluːneɪ/) is also attested in 2E-era TSR pronunciation guides and used in Baldur's Gate 3 voice acting. Both are considered correct; the former dominates in 5E.

Etymology

Out-of-universe name coined by Ed Greenwood, creator of the Forgotten Realms, derived from Selene (Ancient Greek Σελήνη), the Greek personification of the moon. Greenwood has stated he deliberately echoed classical lunar theonyms to signal familiarity while creating a distinct Faerûnian deity. In-universe, the name is primordial, predating human language, given at her formation from the primordial essence of Realmspace by Ao.

Cultural Ownership

Intellectual property of Wizards of the Coast LLC (Hasbro), as part of the Forgotten Realms setting originally created by Ed Greenwood (first published 1987). Greenwood retains creator credit but not ownership. The deity is specific to the Dungeons & Dragons multiverse and is not drawn from a living real-world religion; distinct from the Greek Selene despite etymological borrowing.

Disputed Identifications

Frequently conflated with: 1) Selene (Greek mythology) - inspiration but separate entity; 2) Sehanine Moonbow - elven goddess of the moon, an allied but distinct power in the Seldarine, sometimes incorrectly merged by fans; 3) Elah/Lucha - regional aspects of Selûne herself, not separate goddesses, though some in-universe scholars treat them as syncretic identities; 4) Leira and Shar - Shar is Selûne's twin and antithesis, Leira is a separate goddess of illusion. In 4E, Selûne was merged into a combined pantheon as a greater god, leading to confusion with her 3E/5E status.

Description

Appearance

Attested form: Selûne most often manifests as a strikingly beautiful human woman of apparently middle years but timeless, with long hair of shining silver or white that seems to capture moonlight, eyes of pale silver or grey that reflect stars, and skin with a faint pearlescent luminescence. She is typically robed in shimmering white or silver, often diaphanous and drifting as if underwater, sometimes armored in argent plate when depicted as a warrior against Shar. Her avatar in Faiths & Avatars is described as 7 feet tall, levitating slightly, surrounded by a nimbus of moonlight. In more symbolic art (Faiths and Pantheons, 5E PHB), she is represented only by her holy symbol. She can also appear as a column of silvery light, a disembodied pair of eyes, or a plain, kindly older woman to test mortals. Unlike Shar, she is never depicted as dark or hidden; light emanates from her. Age/appearance is mutable - she chooses to appear as maiden, mother, or crone, but maiden/mother is canonical.

Symbols & Iconography

Primary holy symbol: a pair of female eyes surrounded by seven silver stars on a dark field - the Eyes of the Moonmaiden. Attested in every edition since 1987. The seven stars represent the seven Tears of Selûne (see Stories). Secondary symbols: a silver disk/moon (full moon argent), a silver mirror, or a staff topped with a moon. Colors: silver and white, with blue accents. Sacred animal: none exclusively, but wolves (especially werewolves seeking cure), cats, and birds associated with navigation. Sacred weapon: the mace (heavy mace) called the Moon's Hand; clerics favor maces and quarterstaves. Iconography in temples: open-air designs with moonwells, silvered mirrors to catch moonlight, central oculi aligned to lunar events, pools of still water for scrying. Her symbol is deliberately the inverse of Shar's black disk with purple border.

Behaviour & Personality

Alignment attested as Chaotic Good in 1E-3E and 5E (4E listed her as Good unaligned due to alignment simplification). Personality consistently described as benevolent, nurturing, wandering, and fiercely protective, yet mercurial and prone to sorrow and rash action - a reflection of the moon's phases. She is the goddess of guidance, compassion, and hope against darkness, encouraging her followers to fight evil, aid the lost, and seek redemption. She is notably forgiving toward lycanthropes and those cursed, offering control or cure where other gods offer destruction. She is also vengeful toward Shar and her followers, and can be wrathful when her faithful are harmed. She values personal freedom, star-gazing, navigation, and revelry under moonlight. Unlike more distant greater gods, she is described as unusually interventionist and personal, often sending omens (shooting stars, moonbeams), dreams, and direct aid. Faiths & Avatars notes she is "ever-changing yet ever-faithful" and suffers from loneliness stemming from her ancient betrayal by Shar.

Domains & Habitats

Portfolio (domains of concern): The Moon (and by extension moons, moonlight), Stars, Navigation, Wanderers, Questers, Seekers, Good-aligned lycanthropes, Divination, Dreams. In 3E/3.5E domains: Chaos, Good, Moon, Protection, Travel. In 5E suggested domains: Knowledge, Life (SCAG), Trickery variant for some interpretations. She dwells on the plane of Gates of the Moon (also called Argentil), a realm of silver and perpetual twilight she shares - uneasily - with other good-aligned powers including Sune and Desna-like figures; in the Great Wheel cosmology she resides in Brightwater/ Gates of the Moon on Ysgard/Arborea border. On Toril, her sacred places include the House of the Moon in Waterdeep, the shrine at Silverymoon (city named for her), and moonwells across Faerûn. Her influence waxes with the moon's phases; full moons are holy days. She is intimately tied to the Tears of Selûne - an asteroid field orbiting Toril that is literally her tears.

Version Differences

1E/2E (1987-1999): Greater goddess, Chaotic Good, highly detailed in Faiths & Avatars with avatar stats (AC -5, 300+ hp), active in Time of Troubles. 3E/3.5E (2000-2007): Greater deity (Divine Rank 19), stat block in Faiths and Pantheons, alignment Chaotic Good, portfolio refined, gains Moon domain. 4E (2008-2014): Downgraded cosmology - listed as Greater God, alignment Good (alignment system collapsed), racial pantheons dissolved, she absorbs some aspects of Sehanine Moonbow for the core Dawn War pantheon, described as exarch-free. 5E (2014-present): Restored as Greater Goddess, Chaotic Good, no official stat block (deities unstatted by design), domains Life and Knowledge per PHB/SCAG, lore largely reverts to 3E with emphasis on Shar conflict and lycanthrope redemption. Visual depiction shifts from armored warrior-maiden (1990s Larry Elmore art) to more ethereal, diverse representations in 5E. Baldur's Gate 3 (2023) popularized a more personal, trauma-informed depiction via Shadowheart's arc, emphasizing Selûne as healer of Shar's abuse.

Powers & Abilities

Lunar Dominion and Light Generation

Source VersionFaiths & Avatars (1996), Faiths and Pantheons (2002) - Divine Abilities: Alter Reality, Divine Radiance

As embodiment of Toril's moon, Selûne controls moonlight itself - she can create light equivalent to daylight, dispel magical darkness (including Shar's darkness), and illuminate truth. Her light reveals hidden things, dispels illusions, and harms undead and creatures of shadow. In avatar form she radiates moonfire.

Limitations

Her power wanes during new moons and in areas of profound shadow or Shar's influence; she cannot fully overcome Shar's Shadow Weave in its own demiplane. Light is not sun-like - it does not provide warmth or sustain plants.

Contradictions

4E sources describe her light as less cosmically unique (shared with Sehanine); 5E emphasizes her light as specifically anti-Shar, not generic illumination.

Divination, Navigation and Portal Lore

Source VersionFaiths & Avatars p.111, SCAG p.31

Selûne grants perfect navigation by stars and moon, the ability to find one's way even when lost, and prophetic dreams. She knows the location of portals and gates, especially moon-related gates and the Gates of the Moon. Clerics receive augury, divination, and find the path as domain spells.

Limitations

Cannot see into areas shielded by Shar or into the Plane of Shadow directly; her prophecies are often cryptic and tied to lunar cycles.

Contradictions

None major; consistent across editions.

Creation of Mystryl/Mystra and Weave Influence

Source VersionThe Grand History of the Realms (2007), Faiths and Pantheons, Ed Greenwood's lore

During the primordial battle with Shar, Selûne tore a portion of her own essence and hurled it at Shar, where it combined with Shar's essence to create Mystryl, the first goddess of magic. Thus Selûne is indirectly the progenitor of all Weave magic. She retains a connection to raw magic and can grant limited spellcasting even when Mystra is dead (as during the Time of Troubles).

Limitations

She is not the goddess of magic herself and cannot control the Weave; her influence is ancestral, not active.

Contradictions

Some early sources credit Ao directly; later canon solidifies Selûne/Shar as parents of Mystryl. 4E's Spellplague lore complicates lineage.

Lycanthrope Influence - Blessing and Curse Mitigation

Source VersionFaiths & Avatars, Faiths and Pantheons, SCAG p.37

Selûne has power over lycanthropy - both to afflict and to control. She can grant good-aligned lycanthropes (werebears, werewolves who resist evil) control over their transformations, suppress involuntary change during full moons, and offer cures. Her clerics can cast cure lycanthropy. Conversely, she can curse evil lycanthropes with loss of control.

Limitations

Cannot cure all lycanthropes against their will; Malar and Shar contest this portfolio. Her blessing requires willing acceptance and often a quest.

Contradictions

2E presented lycanthropy as almost entirely evil; 3E onward emphasizes redemption arc, reflecting Selûne's growing mercy aspect.

Tears of Selûne - Asteroid Creation and Celestial Manipulation

Source VersionRealmspace (1989), Spelljammer lore, Faiths & Avatars

Selûne's tears of sorrow and pain literally became the Tears of Selûne, a cluster of asteroids trailing Toril's moon. She can call down moon-motes, cause moonrock to fall, and manipulate tides. In extremis she can move celestial bodies.

Limitations

Creation of Tears was a one-time cataclysmic event during battle with Shar; she does not routinely create new asteroids. Tidal manipulation is subtle, not catastrophic.

Contradictions

Spelljammer vs. Realmspace descriptions of Tears vary in number and size.

Avatar and Divine Intervention

Source VersionDeities & Demigods (3E), Faiths and Pantheons stat block

As a greater deity (Divine Rank 18-19 in 3E), Selûne can create avatars, grant spells up to 9th level, answer prayers, and manifest omens. Her avatars wield the Rod of Four Moons and cast as 20th+ level clerics/wizards. She can possess willing Selûnites and send silver fire.

Limitations

Bound by Ao's decree post-Time of Troubles not to manifest directly without consequence; avatars are vulnerable if slain (though true form is not). In 5E she no longer receives official stats, reflecting design philosophy that gods are beyond combat.

Contradictions

1E/2E gave killable stats; 3E introduced divine rank immunity; 5E removes stats entirely.

Stories & Myths

The Birth of Selûne and Shar and the Creation of the World

Source Faiths & Avatars (1996), Faiths and Pantheons (2002), The Grand History of the Realms (2007) summarizing Ed Greenwood's cosmologyCulture Forgotten Realms (Faerûnian) - primordial mythPeriod Pre-history, before -35,000 DR (in-universe); published 1987-2007

Summary

In the beginning, Ao the Overgod created Realmspace from phlogiston, but it was empty. From the primordial void he created twin goddesses of light and darkness: Selûne, of silver light and creation, and Shar, of darkness and nothingness. Together they were to fill the universe. Selûne reached out and ignited a sphere of fire, creating Chauntea (the world, Toril) and other heavenly bodies, breathing life and warmth. Shar, jealous and desiring the void, sought to smother it. Their first war began - a cosmic struggle between preservation and oblivion that defines the Realms' dualism.

Participants

Ao

Selûne

Shar

Chauntea (created)

Consequences

Established the fundamental duality of the Realms (Selûne vs. Shar), created the physical world, and set the stage for all subsequent divine conflicts. This myth explains why Selûne and Shar are sisters and eternal enemies.

Variants

Early Dragon magazine versions (1980s) had a simpler creation with Selûne creating the moon alone; later canon (Grand History) elaborates the joint creation and makes Chauntea a separate creation from Selûne's fire. Some Calishite variants name Selûne as Elah and downplay Shar's role.

The Sundering and the Birth of Mystryl, Goddess of Magic

Source Faiths & Avatars p.110, The Grand History of the Realms p.5, Ed Greenwood's 'The Making of Mystryl' loreCulture Forgotten RealmsPeriod c. -35,000 DR; published 1996-2007

Summary

As their battle raged, Selûne and Shar fought across the heavens, tearing at each other. Exhausted, Selûne tore a fragment of her own divine essence - pure light and creation - and hurled it at Shar. Shar tore a fragment of void and hurled it back. Where the two essences collided and mingled, they coalesced into a new being: Mystryl (later Mystra), the goddess of magic and the Weave. Mystryl immediately intervened, separating the warring sisters and bringing balance, allowing life to flourish. Selûne thus became, in a sense, mother to magic itself.

Participants

Selûne

Shar

Mystryl (created)

Consequences

Created the Weave and the possibility of arcane magic on Toril. Established Selûne as progenitor of Mystra, explaining her occasional influence over magic and her protective attitude toward Mystra's chosen (including Midnight and Elminster). Shar never forgave Selûne for this creation, as Mystryl favored balance over nothingness.

Variants

4E Spellplague lore retcons Mystryl's death and rebirth as Mystra/Midnight, but retains Selûne's maternal role. Some scholarly in-universe texts dispute whether Mystryl was intentionally created or an accidental byproduct.

The Tears of Selûne

Source Realmspace by Dale Henson (1989), Spelljammer: Cloak of the Crystal Shadow, Faiths & AvatarsCulture Forgotten Realms / SpelljammerPeriod c. -30,000 DR; published 1989

Summary

During one of their most violent battles, Shar struck Selûne with a spear of pure darkness, wounding her grievously. Selûne's blood and tears of pain scattered across Realmspace. Her tears did not fade but crystallized into a cluster of small asteroids and moonlets that now trail Toril's moon, Selûne (the celestial body shares her name). These are the Tears of Selûne, numbering in the hundreds, some large enough to harbor hidden temples, spelljammer ports, and Sharran ambushes. Selûne's pain literally reshaped the heavens.

Participants

Selûne

Shar

Consequences

Created a permanent celestial feature used for navigation, spelljamming, and adventure sites. The Tears are considered holy to Selûnites and desecrated by Sharrans. Mechanically, they provide an asteroid field for Spelljammer campaigns. Theologically, they symbolize Selûne's suffering and endurance.

Variants

Realmspace counts 7 major tears and hundreds of minor; later 3E sources say 'hundreds' without fixed number. Some folk tales say the tears were of joy at Mystryl's birth, not pain - a sanitized version told to children.

The Dark Moon Heresy and the War of Silver and Shadow

Source Faiths & Avatars (1996), Powers & Pantheons (1997), Champions of Valor, The Grand History of the RealmsCulture Forgotten RealmsPeriod Historical, with peak heresy c. 1000-1300 DR; published 1996-1997

Summary

Shar's clergy promulgated the Dark Moon heresy: that Selûne and Shar are actually two faces of the same goddess, the Dark Moon, and that Selûne is merely Shar's light aspect that will eventually be reabsorbed into darkness. Selûnite clergy violently rejected this. The heresy led to centuries of shadow wars, assassinations of Selûnite priestesses, and the corruption of Selûne's temples from within by Sharran infiltrators posing as Selûnites. Selûne herself is said to have wept and sent visions to her faithful to expose the lie, culminating in open battles in Waterdeep and Silverymoon where Selûnite lycanthropes and Harpers fought Sharran shadow adepts.

Participants

Selûne

Shar

Selûnite clergy (including High Priestesses of Silverymoon)

Sharran clergy

Harpers

Malar's lycanthropes

Consequences

Solidified the theological distinction between Selûne and Shar for players, provided a major church-conflict adventure hook, and explained why Selûne's church is paranoid about Shar's infiltration. The heresy is now considered debunked in canon but persists as an in-universe conspiracy theory.

Variants

Some 2E modules present the heresy as partially true from Shar's perspective; 5E SCAG definitively states they are distinct sisters, not aspects.

The Time of Troubles - Selûne Walks Among Mortals

Source Avatar Trilogy (novels by Scott Ciencin, Troy Denning 1989), Faiths & Avatars, Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2ECulture Forgotten RealmsPeriod 1358 DR (in-universe); published 1989-1996

Summary

When Ao cast all gods down to Toril as mortals for stealing the Tablets of Fate, Selûne was incarnated as a mortal woman near Silverymoon. Unlike many gods who scrambled for power, she wandered, healing the sick, guiding lost travelers by moonlight, and protecting her faithful from Shar's mortal avatar, who hunted her. She allied with Mystra (as Midnight) and other good gods to survive. She regained divinity after the Tablets were restored, but her mortal experience deepened her compassion for mortals and lycanthropes. Her avatar's actions during this year are recorded as miracles: turning a battlefield's night into day to save Silverymoon's army.

Participants

Selûne (mortal avatar)

Shar (mortal avatar)

Midnight/Mystra

Mystra's Chosen

Citizens of Silverymoon and Waterdeep

Consequences

Demonstrated Selûne's willingness to suffer with mortals, increased her popularity post-Time of Troubles, and established her mortal avatar's appearance (silver-haired wanderer) that persists in later lore. Led to Ao's new stricture that gods must act through faithful rather than direct intervention.

Variants

Novel vs. sourcebook accounts differ on exact deeds; the Avatar Trilogy focuses on Mystra/Midnight, with Selûne as secondary figure, while Faiths & Avatars gives her a more central role in protecting the North.

The Silver Lady and the Lycanthropes - The Curse and the Cure

Source Faiths & Avatars p.112, Faiths and Pantheons p.25, Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide p.37, Monster Manual (5E) lycanthrope loreCulture Forgotten RealmsPeriod Ongoing since c. -1000 DR; codified 1996-present

Summary

Selûne's most personal ongoing myth concerns lycanthropes. Originally, all lycanthropes were seen as cursed by Malar and Selûne was their enemy. Over centuries, Selûne's doctrine evolved: she offered those afflicted with lycanthropy who were not evil a chance at control and redemption. Legend tells of a werewolf chieftain who prayed to Selûne under a full moon and, instead of transforming into a ravening beast, retained his mind and protected his village from Sharrans. Selûne blessed him, creating the first good-aligned lycanthropes (Order of the Silver Fang). Since then, Selûnite rituals on full moons can suppress transformation or cure willing lycanthropes, while Sharran and Malarite rites seek to make the curse permanent and savage.

Participants

Selûne

Malar (Beastlord)

Shar

Afflicted lycanthropes

Selûnite clergy

Consequences

Established Selûne as patron of good lycanthropes and those seeking cure, a unique niche among Faerûnian gods. Provides mechanical support for player characters with lycanthropy seeking redemption. Contrasts sharply with her earlier, more militant anti-lycanthrope stance in 1E, showing character development.

Variants

1E Monster Manual presents lycanthropy as purely monstrous; 2E Faiths & Avatars introduces redemption; 5E SCAG fully embraces 'Selûne redeems lycanthropes' as canon. Some regional folklore still depicts Selûne as hunting werewolves, reflecting older, harsher interpretation.

Relationships
Selûne→Sharhostile

Twin sister and eternal antithesis. Created simultaneously by Ao from primordial light and darkness, Shar embodies void, loss, and nihilism while Selûne embodies light, creation, and hope. Their war has defined Realmspace history, from the creation of Mystryl to the Tears of Selûne. Shar seeks to extinguish Selûne's light and prove that all light is eventually swallowed; Selûne seeks to protect creation from Shar's oblivion. Their clergy are in constant shadow war, with Sharrans infiltrating Selûnite temples. No reconciliation is possible in canon.

Selûne→Mystryl / Mystra / Midnightfamily

Daughter/creation. When Selûne and Shar's essences collided, they formed Mystryl, the first goddess of magic. Mystryl died and was reborn as Mystra, who died during Time of Troubles and was succeeded by the mortal Midnight who took the name Mystra. Selûne is thus mystical mother/grandmother to all Mystra incarnations. She is protective of Mystra and her Chosen (Elminster, the Seven Sisters), and Mystra's death during the Spellplague caused Selûne great grief. Clerics of both goddesses often cooperate.

Selûne→Chaunteaally

Co-creator and steadfast ally. Chauntea (Earthmother) was created when Selûne's fire warmed the void, forming Toril. Chauntea embodies the world itself while Selûne embodies its moon. They are considered sisters in a broader sense and allies against Shar. Their faiths often cooperate in agricultural communities where moon phases guide planting. In some myths, Chauntea sheltered Selûne when she was wounded by Shar.

Selûne→Sehanine Moonbowally

Elven goddess of the moon, dreams, and death. Sehanine and Selûne share the moon portfolio but are distinct - Sehanine is elven, Selûne is human/ universal. They are close allies, often depicted together in the Gates of the Moon, and their priesthoods cooperate, especially in Silverymoon and Evereska. Some scholars argue Sehanine is an elven aspect of Selûne, but both churches deny this. In 4E they were briefly conflated in the core pantheon.

Selûne→Sune and Lathanderally

Fellow good-aligned greater powers with whom Selûne shares the plane of Gates of the Moon/Brightwater. Sune (love/beauty) and Lathander (dawn/renewal) are her closest divine friends among the human pantheon. They frequently act together against Shar, Malar, and Bane. Selûne, Sune, and Lathander are sometimes called the 'Triad of Light' in popular Faerûnian devotion, though not a formal triad like Tyr/Ilmater/Torm. Their temples often stand near each other in Waterdeep.

Selûne→Eilistraeeally

Drow goddess of moonlight, dance, and redemption. Eilistraee, daughter of Lolth, is a patron of drow seeking escape from the Underdark, and her moonlit dances mirror Selûne's light. Selûne shelters Eilistraee's faithful and the two goddesses are allies against Lolth and Shar. Selûne's moonlight is said to be the only light that reaches the Underdark to guide Eilistraee's followers to the surface. Their alliance is particularly important in 5E lore where Eilistraee returned after being thought dead.

Selûne→Malar and the Lycanthropeshostile

Malar the Beastlord is Selûne's rival for dominion over lycanthropes. Malar embraces lycanthropy as a savage blessing and seeks to make all were-beasts bloodthirsty predators. Selûne seeks to redeem and control the curse. Their conflict plays out through afflicted lycanthropes - Malarite werewolves hunt Selûnite werebears. Selûne's church actively hunts Malarite lycanthropes while protecting good-aligned ones. This duality defines her portfolio as goddess of both the moon that triggers transformation and the mercy that controls it.

Primordial Mutilation and Cosmic Abuse

Source Faiths & Avatars p.109-110, Grand History of the Realms

Description

The core myth of Selûne and Shar is one of intimate violence between sisters. Shar's wounding of Selûne - spearing her with darkness and causing the Tears - is described in visceral terms in Faiths & Avatars as a betrayal by a twin. The text presents Shar's jealousy and desire to annihilate Selûne's creations as an abusive dynamic that has lasted eons, with Selûne repeatedly attempting reconciliation and Shar repeatedly attempting murder. This is not sanitized; the lore explicitly calls Shar 'the dark twin who hates with the intimacy of a sister.'

Scholarly Context

Scholars of fantasy religion (e.g., Appelcline) note this mirrors real-world dualistic theologies (Ahura Mazda/Angra Mainyu) but with a distinctly familial, gendered violence that is unusual for D&D's often impersonal pantheons.

Lycanthropy, Stigma, and Forced Transformation

Source Faiths & Avatars, Monster Manual (2E/5E), SCAG

Description

Selûne's portfolio includes lycanthropy, a condition depicted as both curse and disease involving loss of bodily autonomy, involuntary transformation, and potential for violence against loved ones. Older editions (1E-2E) presented Selûne's faithful as hunting and killing lycanthropes indiscriminately. Later lore retcons this into a redemption narrative, but the underlying horror - being trapped in a body that becomes a monster under her own moon - remains. Selûnite rituals to cure lycanthropy are described as painful and requiring the afflicted to be bound with silver chains under moonlight.

Scholarly Context

Modern readings highlight parallels to real-world stigma around chronic illness and the 'cure vs. acceptance' debate; Wizards has shifted from 'kill all werewolves' to 'offer control/cure' to address this.

Religious Persecution and Infiltration - The Dark Moon Heresy

Source Faiths & Avatars, Powers & Pantheons

Description

The Dark Moon heresy involved Sharran priests infiltrating Selûnite temples, posing as priestesses for years, then murdering Selûnites from within, desecrating moonwells with blood, and kidnapping initiates for torture in Shar's name. Faiths & Avatars describes Sharran 'shadow seductions' where Selûnites were gaslit into believing their goddess was merely Shar's mask. This reflects real-world histories of religious persecution and cult infiltration.

Scholarly Context

Presented without glamorization; sourcebooks frame Sharrans as villains and Selûnites as victims, with content warnings in later editions about gaslighting and cult abuse themes, especially relevant to Baldur's Gate 3's Shadowheart arc.

Contradictions & Disputes

Alignment and Nature

Position A

Selûne is Chaotic Good - mercurial, benevolent, freedom-loving (2E, 3E, 5E canon)

Position A — evidence

Faiths & Avatars (CG), Faiths and Pantheons (CG), PHB 5E (CG), SCAG (CG) - all explicitly list Chaotic Good with emphasis on wandering and change

Position B

Selûne is simply Good / Neutral Good - more lawful and stable (4E simplification, some 1E interpretations)

Position B — evidence

4E Player's Handbook (2008) and Deities & Demigods 4E list alignment as 'Good' (unaligned system); early Dragon #58 (1982) listed her as Chaotic Good but with Neutral tendencies

Scholarly Consensus

5E has reaffirmed Chaotic Good as correct; 4E's 'Good' is considered an edition artifact, not a lore change. The chaotic aspect reflects moon's phases and is now canonical.

Relationship to Sehanine Moonbow

Position A

Selûne and Sehanine Moonbow are distinct goddesses and allies (orthodox 2E/3E/5E view)

Position A — evidence

Demihuman Deities (1998) and Mordenkainen's Tome of Foes (2018) list them separately with different portfolios and planes; Faiths and Pantheons treats Selûne as human pantheon, Sehanine as elven

Position B

Sehanine is an elven aspect or name for Selûne (folk syncretism, 4E core pantheon merger)

Position B — evidence

4E Player's Handbook merges them into one 'Sehanine/Selûne' entry for the core pantheon; in-universe folk in Silverymoon sometimes pray to 'the Moonmaiden' without distinguishing

Scholarly Consensus

Official Wizards stance since 5E is distinct beings; merger was a 4E gameplay simplification and is now retconned. Fan confusion persists.

Origin of the Tears of Selûne

Position A

Tears are Selûne's literal tears/blood from Shar's wound (Realmspace, Faiths & Avatars)

Position A — evidence

Realmspace (1989) p. 12: 'Selûne's tears... crystallized'; Faiths & Avatars p.110: 'her tears became the Tears'

Position B

Tears are a natural asteroid cluster with mythic overlay, or Selûne's tears of joy at Mystryl's birth

Position B — evidence

Children's tales in Silverymoon (Volo's Guide) and some 5E flavor text describe Tears as tears of joy; Spelljammer 5E (2022) describes them naturalistically as 'asteroids trailing the moon'

Scholarly Consensus

Both can be true in mythic logic - divine tears becoming physical objects. Primary canon favors wound-tears; joy-tears is considered a bowdlerized folk variant.
