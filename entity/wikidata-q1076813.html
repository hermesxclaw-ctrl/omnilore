Who It Is

When you look up at night in Faerûn, someone is looking back — silver-haired, star-crowned, weeping tears that became floating islands. Selûne is not a distant moon goddess who merely lights the way; she is the Moon itself, a primordial being older than the gods of Toril, locked in an eternal, fratricidal war with her twin sister Shar that has shattered worlds, birthed magic, and cursed and blessed the were-beasts who howl beneath her light.

Alignment attested as Chaotic Good in 1E-3E and 5E (4E listed her as Good unaligned due to alignment simplification). Personality consistently described as benevolent, nurturing, wandering, and fiercely protective, yet mercurial and prone to sorrow and rash action - a reflection of the moon's phases. She is the goddess of guidance, compassion, and hope against darkness, encouraging her followers to fight evil, aid the lost, and seek redemption. Powers
Lunar Dominion and Light Generation

As embodiment of Toril's moon, Selûne controls moonlight itself - she can create light equivalent to daylight, dispel magical darkness (including Shar's darkness), and illuminate truth. Her light reveals hidden things, dispels illusions, and harms undead and creatures of shadow. In avatar form she radiates moonfire.

Limit: Her power wanes during new moons and in areas of profound shadow or Shar's influence; she cannot fully overcome Shar's Shadow Weave in its own demiplane. Light is not sun-like - it does not provide warmth or sustain plants.

Divination, Navigation and Portal Lore

Selûne grants perfect navigation by stars and moon, the ability to find one's way even when lost, and prophetic dreams. She knows the location of portals and gates, especially moon-related gates and the Gates of the Moon. Clerics receive augury, divination, and find the path as domain spells.

Limit: Cannot see into areas shielded by Shar or into the Plane of Shadow directly; her prophecies are often cryptic and tied to lunar cycles.

Creation of Mystryl/Mystra and Weave Influence

During the primordial battle with Shar, Selûne tore a portion of her own essence and hurled it at Shar, where it combined with Shar's essence to create Mystryl, the first goddess of magic. Thus Selûne is indirectly the progenitor of all Weave magic. She retains a connection to raw magic and can grant limited spellcasting even when Mystra is dead (as during the Time of Troubles).

Limit: She is not the goddess of magic herself and cannot control the Weave; her influence is ancestral, not active.

Lycanthrope Influence - Blessing and Curse Mitigation

Selûne has power over lycanthropy - both to afflict and to control. She can grant good-aligned lycanthropes (werebears, werewolves who resist evil) control over their transformations, suppress involuntary change during full moons, and offer cures. Her clerics can cast cure lycanthropy. Conversely, she can curse evil lycanthropes with loss of control.

Limit: Cannot cure all lycanthropes against their will; Malar and Shar contest this portfolio. Her blessing requires willing acceptance and often a quest.

Tears of Selûne - Asteroid Creation and Celestial Manipulation

Selûne's tears of sorrow and pain literally became the Tears of Selûne, a cluster of asteroids trailing Toril's moon. She can call down moon-motes, cause moonrock to fall, and manipulate tides. In extremis she can move celestial bodies.

Limit: Creation of Tears was a one-time cataclysmic event during battle with Shar; she does not routinely create new asteroids. Tidal manipulation is subtle, not catastrophic.

Avatar and Divine Intervention

As a greater deity (Divine Rank 18-19 in 3E), Selûne can create avatars, grant spells up to 9th level, answer prayers, and manifest omens. Her avatars wield the Rod of Four Moons and cast as 20th+ level clerics/wizards. She can possess willing Selûnites and send silver fire.

Limit: Bound by Ao's decree post-Time of Troubles not to manifest directly without consequence; avatars are vulnerable if slain (though true form is not). In 5E she no longer receives official stats, reflecting design philosophy that gods are beyond combat.

Accounts I
The Birth of Selûne and Shar and the Creation of the WorldFaiths & Avatars (1996), Faiths and Pantheons (2002), The Grand History of the Realms (2007) summarizing Ed Greenwood's cosmology

Pre-history, before -35,000 DR (in-universe); published 1987-2007

In the beginning, Ao the Overgod created Realmspace from phlogiston, but it was empty. From the primordial void he created twin goddesses of light and darkness: Selûne, of silver light and creation, and Shar, of darkness and nothingness. Together they were to fill the universe. Selûne reached out and ignited a sphere of fire, creating Chauntea (the world, Toril) and other heavenly bodies, breathing life and warmth. Shar, jealous and desiring the void, sought to smother it. Their first war began - a cosmic struggle between preservation and oblivion that defines the Realms' dualism. Established the fundamental duality of the Realms (Selûne vs. Shar), created the physical world, and set the stage for all subsequent divine conflicts. This myth explains why Selûne and Shar are sisters and eternal enemies.

The Sundering and the Birth of Mystryl, Goddess of MagicFaiths & Avatars p.110, The Grand History of the Realms p.5, Ed Greenwood's 'The Making of Mystryl' lore

c. -35,000 DR; published 1996-2007

As their battle raged, Selûne and Shar fought across the heavens, tearing at each other. Exhausted, Selûne tore a fragment of her own divine essence - pure light and creation - and hurled it at Shar. Shar tore a fragment of void and hurled it back. Where the two essences collided and mingled, they coalesced into a new being: Mystryl (later Mystra), the goddess of magic and the Weave. Mystryl immediately intervened, separating the warring sisters and bringing balance, allowing life to flourish. Selûne thus became, in a sense, mother to magic itself. Created the Weave and the possibility of arcane magic on Toril. Established Selûne as progenitor of Mystra, explaining her occasional influence over magic and her protective attitude toward Mystra's chosen (including Midnight and Elminster). Shar never forgave Selûne for this creation, as Mystryl favored balance over nothingness.

The Tears of SelûneRealmspace by Dale Henson (1989), Spelljammer: Cloak of the Crystal Shadow, Faiths & Avatars

c. -30,000 DR; published 1989

During one of their most violent battles, Shar struck Selûne with a spear of pure darkness, wounding her grievously. Selûne's blood and tears of pain scattered across Realmspace. Her tears did not fade but crystallized into a cluster of small asteroids and moonlets that now trail Toril's moon, Selûne (the celestial body shares her name). These are the Tears of Selûne, numbering in the hundreds, some large enough to harbor hidden temples, spelljammer ports, and Sharran ambushes. Selûne's pain literally reshaped the heavens. Created a permanent celestial feature used for navigation, spelljamming, and adventure sites. The Tears are considered holy to Selûnites and desecrated by Sharrans. Mechanically, they provide an asteroid field for Spelljammer campaigns. Theologically, they symbolize Selûne's suffering and endurance.

Accounts II
The Dark Moon Heresy and the War of Silver and ShadowFaiths & Avatars (1996), Powers & Pantheons (1997), Champions of Valor, The Grand History of the Realms

Historical, with peak heresy c. 1000-1300 DR; published 1996-1997

Shar's clergy promulgated the Dark Moon heresy: that Selûne and Shar are actually two faces of the same goddess, the Dark Moon, and that Selûne is merely Shar's light aspect that will eventually be reabsorbed into darkness. Selûnite clergy violently rejected this. The heresy led to centuries of shadow wars, assassinations of Selûnite priestesses, and the corruption of Selûne's temples from within by Sharran infiltrators posing as Selûnites. Selûne herself is said to have wept and sent visions to her faithful to expose the lie, culminating in open battles in Waterdeep and Silverymoon where Selûnite lycanthropes and Harpers fought Sharran shadow adepts. Solidified the theological distinction between Selûne and Shar for players, provided a major church-conflict adventure hook, and explained why Selûne's church is paranoid about Shar's infiltration. The heresy is now considered debunked in canon but persists as an in-universe conspiracy theory.

The Time of Troubles - Selûne Walks Among MortalsAvatar Trilogy (novels by Scott Ciencin, Troy Denning 1989), Faiths & Avatars, Forgotten Realms Campaign Setting 2E

1358 DR (in-universe); published 1989-1996

When Ao cast all gods down to Toril as mortals for stealing the Tablets of Fate, Selûne was incarnated as a mortal woman near Silverymoon. Unlike many gods who scrambled for power, she wandered, healing the sick, guiding lost travelers by moonlight, and protecting her faithful from Shar's mortal avatar, who hunted her. She allied with Mystra (as Midnight) and other good gods to survive. She regained divinity after the Tablets were restored, but her mortal experience deepened her compassion for mortals and lycanthropes. Her avatar's actions during this year are recorded as miracles: turning a battlefield's night into day to save Silverymoon's army. Demonstrated Selûne's willingness to suffer with mortals, increased her popularity post-Time of Troubles, and established her mortal avatar's appearance (silver-haired wanderer) that persists in later lore. Led to Ao's new stricture that gods must act through faithful rather than direct intervention.

The Silver Lady and the Lycanthropes - The Curse and the CureFaiths & Avatars p.112, Faiths and Pantheons p.25, Sword Coast Adventurer's Guide p.37, Monster Manual (5E) lycanthrope lore

Ongoing since c. -1000 DR; codified 1996-present

Selûne's most personal ongoing myth concerns lycanthropes. Originally, all lycanthropes were seen as cursed by Malar and Selûne was their enemy. Over centuries, Selûne's doctrine evolved: she offered those afflicted with lycanthropy who were not evil a chance at control and redemption. Legend tells of a werewolf chieftain who prayed to Selûne under a full moon and, instead of transforming into a ravening beast, retained his mind and protected his village from Sharrans. Selûne blessed him, creating the first good-aligned lycanthropes (Order of the Silver Fang). Since then, Selûnite rituals on full moons can suppress transformation or cure willing lycanthropes, while Sharran and Malarite rites seek to make the curse permanent and savage. Established Selûne as patron of good lycanthropes and those seeking cure, a unique niche among Faerûnian gods. Provides mechanical support for player characters with lycanthropy seeking redemption. Contrasts sharply with her earlier, more militant anti-lycanthrope stance in 1E, showing character development.

Selûne is the Greek goddess Selene imported into D&D. — Selûne is an original Forgotten Realms creation by Ed Greenwood (1987), etymologically inspired by Selene but with distinct lore, portfolio, and relationships (twin of Shar, mother of Mystryl). She has no direct mythological lineage beyond the name.

Selûne is an elven goddess or the same as Sehanine Moonbow. — Selûne is primarily a human (and universal) goddess; Sehanine Moonbow is the elven moon goddess of the Seldarine. They are allies who share a plane but are separate beings with different clergy and dogma. 4E's merged pantheon caused this misconception.

Selûne hates all werewolves and werebeasts and commands her followers to kill them. — Since 2E Faiths & Avatars (1996), Selûne is explicitly the patron of good-aligned lycanthropes and offers control and cure to willing afflicted. She opposes Malarite savage lycanthropes but protects those seeking redemption. Older 1E lore was more hostile, but has been retconned.

Selûne and Shar are two sides of the same goddess (the Dark Moon). — This is the in-universe Dark Moon heresy, explicitly labeled a false doctrine promulgated by Shar's clergy to subvert Selûnites. Canon (Faiths & Avatars, SCAG) is clear they are twin sisters and distinct greater goddesses, not aspects.
