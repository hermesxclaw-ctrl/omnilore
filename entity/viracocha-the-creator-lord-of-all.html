Who It Is

Viracocha (or Wiracocha, from Quechua wira = fat/abundance and qucha = lake/sea, perhaps 'Sea of Abundance') is the supreme creator-god of Inca theology — the primordial being who emerges from Lake Titicaca (the primordial chaos-sea) to fashion the cosmos. In Inca myth, Viracocha first created a race of giants from stone, but when they displeased him, he turned some to stone (the giant monoliths at Tiwanaku were said to be his 'first creation') and flooded the rest. He then made the second, true humanity — painting their clothes and dividing them into nations (marking each with its language, crops, and customs) — and breathed life into them. Finally, he set the sun (Inti), the moon (Mama Quilla), and the stars in the sky. According to some accounts, the creator then disguised himself as an old, ragged traveler (much like the wandering buddha cycle in Buddhism) and walked the length of the Andes, teaching the arts of civilization and performing miracles before walking across the Pacific Ocean westward — his cloak spreading like sails. The temple at Cacha (Raquchi) held a colossal stone statue of Viracocha, and the Christian chroniclers who heard Inca priests describe Viracocha were struck by the parallels to the biblical God.
