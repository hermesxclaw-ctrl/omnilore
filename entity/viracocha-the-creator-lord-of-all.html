Who It Is

Viracocha is a complex and multifaceted deity, with a rich history and mythology. He is associated with creation, wisdom, power, and healing, and is said to have created the world and all living things. Despite his importance in Inca and pre-Inca mythology, there is a lack of information about his early life and his relationships with other gods and goddesses.

Viracocha is often described as a wise and powerful deity, who created the world and all living things. He is said to be a just and fair ruler, who maintains balance and order in the universe.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Viracocha is often depicted as a bearded man, dressed in a long cloak, with a staff in one hand and a book in the other. He is sometimes shown with a crown or a hat, symbolizing his status as a supreme deity. In some representations, Viracocha is depicted as a fish or a serpent, reflecting his association with water and the sea.

Symbols — Viracocha is associated with various symbols, including the staff, the book, the crown, and the fish or serpent. These symbols represent his power, wisdom, and connection to the natural world.

Domains — Viracocha is associated with the Andes region of South America, where he is said to have created the world and all living things. He is also associated with the sea and the water, reflecting his connection to the natural world.
