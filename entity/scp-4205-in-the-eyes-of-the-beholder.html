Who It Is

A pair of antique binoculars. When looking through them at a person, the viewer sees that person as their 'true self' — if the target is kind, they appear more beautiful; if cruel, they appear monstrous. Foundation testing confirmed the binoculars reveal genuine moral character: D-Class personnel convicted of violent crimes appear as twisted, hideous creatures, while personnel with verified histories of altruism appear radiantly beautiful. The implication — that people's inner nature is physically visible — has caused severe psychological distress among staff.

Form

A pair of brass opera glasses, late 19th century, with lenses of an unknown crystalline material. Engraved on the side in small script: 'See them as they are.'
