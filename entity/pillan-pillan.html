Who It Is

Pillán are powerful spirits in Mapuche religion, the most important class of spirits after Ngenechen himself. They are the spirits of deceased Mapuche ancestors who have achieved a higher state of being, and they dwell in volcanoes — which are considered living beings. The greatest Pillán is Antu (the Sun), and the Pillán are responsible for natural phenomena including volcanic eruptions, earthquakes, thunder, and lightning. The name 'Pillán' is also used for the supreme deity in some Mapuche traditions.

Field Notes

۩ The Villarrica Volcano, one of the most active in Chile, is considered by the Mapuche to be one of the most powerful Pillán spirits — its eruptions are seen as direct communications from the ancestral world.

۩ The word 'Pillán' is also used in Chilean Spanish to describe a very strong person or a powerful natural force.

۩ Some Mapuche elders say that before a volcanic eruption, the Pillán appears to a shaman (machi) in a dream, warning of the coming event.

Cultural Significance

The Mapuche live in the shadow of the Andes, one of the most volcanically active mountain ranges in the world. The Pillán's association with volcanoes reflects a deep understanding of the geological forces that shape their world. The Mapuche have developed sophisticated systems of prediction and response to volcanic activity based on ancestor spirits. The word 'Pillán' entered Spanish (as 'pillán') to refer to volcanic spirits, and the Mapuche tradition of respecting volcanoes has contributed to modern environmental awareness in southern Chile.
