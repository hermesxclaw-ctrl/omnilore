Identity

Sanson

Pronunciation In French, approximately 'sahn-SOHN'; in English, often 'SAN-sən'.

Etymology

The surname Sanson is a variant of Samson, derived from the Hebrew name Shimshon, meaning 'of the sun' or 'sun child'. It is common in French-speaking regions.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily French, with notable bearers in French history, arts, and sports. Also present in other cultures via Spanish variant Sansón.

Disputed Identifications

The name is sometimes confused with the biblical Samson, particularly in Spanish contexts where 'Sansón' is the standard form. The fictional character Andre Sanson is a distinct creation.

Executioners in the Sanson family

Source Provided source material

Description

Charles-Henri Sanson and Henry-Clément Sanson were public executioners in France, a role that involved carrying out death sentences. This is a matter of historical record, not personal moral judgment.

Scholarly Context

The Sanson family held the office of executioner in Paris for several generations, a position that was legally defined and socially ostracized.
