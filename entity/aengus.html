Who They Are

Aengus (or Óengus) is the god of love, youth, poetic inspiration, and dreams — the 'Mac Óg' ('Young Son') of the Tuatha Dé Danann. He is the son of the Dagda and Boann (the goddess of the River Boyne). Aengus lived at Bru na Bóinne (Newgrange), a Neolithic passage tomb the Dagda granted him by a trick: the Dagda let Aengus stay for 'a day and a night,' but Aengus claimed the site forever because days and nights are infinite. Aengus's most famous myth is 'The Dream of Aengus,' in which he falls in love with a woman he saw in a dream. He searched all of Ireland for her, eventually finding her as Caer Ibormeith, who alternated between human and swan form on Samhain. Aengus transformed himself into a swan to be with her — they flew away together, making the trees of the world resonate with their song (a myth explaining why swans sing beautifully at Samhain). Aengus wields a magical sword and has four birds of inspiration who whisper poetry to lovers. He also has the power to heal with kisses — anyone kissed by him would be granted eternal youth. He is the patron of young lovers and poets, representing the beauty, intensity, and fleeting nature of youthful love.
