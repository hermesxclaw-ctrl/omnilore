Who It Is

The Badalisc (also Badalisk) is a mythical creature from the folklore of Val Camonica, a valley in the Lombardy region of northern Italy, specifically associated with the village of Andrista. The creature is described as a goat-like or serpentine being with a large head, glowing eyes, a long snout, and covered in shaggy fur or scales, often depicted with chains around its body. According to local tradition, the Badalisc emerges from the forests each year during the Epiphany season (specifically on January 5 and 6) to enter the village. Once captured by local youths, the creature is paraded through the streets before being brought to the town square to deliver the 'Badalisc's Speech' (Cant del Badalisc) — a satirical monologue recited by a disguised villager that reveals the town's gossip, secrets, and scandals from the past year. The speech is delivered in a mix of local dialect and mock-ancient language, exposing adulteries, feuds, crimes, and petty grudges in rhyming verse. The Badalisc is then symbolically killed or driven back into the forest, signaling the end of the festival. The tradition blends pagan forest-spirit motifs with medieval carnivalesque social inversion: the Badalisc serves as a conduit for unspoken truths, allowing the community to publicly air grievances through humor without direct confrontation. The festival has been celebrated continuously for centuries and remains one of Italy's most distinctive regional folk events.
