Who It Is

The Hinn (also Hin) are, according to some Arabic folk traditions and tafsir (Quranic exegesis), a race of pre-Adamic beings — jinn-like creatures who inhabited the earth before the creation of Adam and are distinct from the jinn created from smokeless fire. The Hinn are said to have been created from the 'samum' (poisonous wind) or, in some sources, from the 'marij min nar' (smokeless flame of fire, the same substance as the jinn, making them a subset or precursor). According to folk tradition recorded by Al-Qazwini and others, the Hinn ruled the earth for thousands of years before the jinn, and the jinn in turn ruled for thousands of years before the creation of Adam. The Hinn were eventually destroyed or driven to remote corners of the earth by the jinn, and their remnants — degenerate, almost bestial — still exist in the most desolate deserts and mountains. Some medieval Arabic sources identify the Hinn as the weakest and lowest class of jinn, below even the Jann. In other accounts, the Hinn are the aboriginal inhabitants conquered by the jinn, much as the jinn were later challenged and partially displaced by humanity. The Hinn are associated with pre-Islamic Arabian beliefs about 'the people of the past ages' (al-umam al-khaliya) who left ruins, inscriptions, and monuments — a folk explanation for the archaeological remains (Nabataean, Thamudic, etc.) that dotted the Arabian landscape. The Hinn concept reflects the medieval Islamic understanding of deep time and the earth's pre-human history, encoded in jinn genealogies and folk histories that filled gaps in scriptural accounts with creative speculation. They are the ghosts of the pre-human past, the trace of whatever was here before Adam.
