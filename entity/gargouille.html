Who It Is

La Gargouille was a fearsome dragon that emerged from the waters of the Seine to terrorize the region around Rouen. Described as a serpentine creature with a long neck, bat-like wings, and the ability to spew water and fire, it devoured livestock, capsized boats, and consumed anyone who dared approach the river. Saint Romanus, the bishop of Rouen, volunteered to subdue the beast on the condition that he be accompanied only by a condemned prisoner. Armed with a crucifix and holy relics, Romanus confronted the Gargouille, making the sign of the cross that paralyzed the dragon. He then led it into the town where the citizens burned it at the stake. Only the head and neck, tempered by the dragon's own fiery breath, refused to burn. These were mounted on the cathedral as a warning, establishing the tradition of gargoyle architectural ornaments — grotesque stone waterspouts designed to ward off evil spirits. The name 'gargoyle' itself derives from 'gargouille,' related to the French 'gargouiller' (to gurgle), reflecting the sound of water through the carved spouts. The Rouen festival celebrating this deliverance included the annual pardoning of one prisoner, a tradition that lasted until 1790.
