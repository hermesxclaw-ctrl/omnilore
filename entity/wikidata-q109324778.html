Who It Is

On a ruined Cronos where cities are crushed under the treads of the Gyandlar war-machine, a blonde princess in wolf-pelt armor lifts a sword taller than she is — and the battlefield goes quiet. That is Leina Ashta: not the gentle royalty of Saturday-morning toy commercials, but the re-imagined, muscle-bound wolf-warrior who stepped out of a children's mecha anime and into one of the 1980s' most notorious adult-oriented OVAs.

TV: Attested as compassionate, brave, and diplomatic — younger sister of protagonist Rom Stol, often mediating between humans and Machine Robo, willing to fight but not bloodthirsty; archetypal heroic princess. OVA: Reconstructed as stoic, taciturn, trauma-hardened survivor; less dialogue, more physical acting. Motivated by protection of the weak, revenge against Gyandlar mutants/warlords, and mastery of the Wolf Sword. Both versions value loyalty and refuse to abandon comrades. Personality differences are adaptation-specific, not evidence of character development within a single continuity

Powers
Wolf Sword Mastery (Kenrou Ken)

Wielding of an oversized two-handed Wolf Sword, capable of cleaving armored mutants, war machines, and fortified gates. In OVA, attested feats include single-stroke bisection of giant mutants and parrying energy weapons with the blade.

Limit: Requires two hands and immense strength; attested to exhaust Leina after prolonged use; cannot be wielded effectively by normal humans (in OVA lore, only Leina's modified/mutant physiology allows it). No magical ranged projection attested.

Superhuman Strength and Durability

Attested to lift and throw opponents several times her mass, shatter stone, survive falls and blunt trauma that would kill ordinary humans. OVA explicitly frames her as bio-engineered / mutant-adjacent warrior.

Limit: Not invulnerable: attested to be wounded by blades, energy weapons, and mutant claws; requires recovery time; vulnerable to exhaustion, poison, and overwhelming numbers.

Expert Hand-to-Hand and Survival Combat

Mastery of unarmed combat, grappling, and improvised weapons; tracking, hunting, and wasteland survival. Defeats multiple armed soldiers unarmed in OVA I market fight.

Limit: No formal military training attested; relies on instinct and experience; disadvantaged against heavy artillery or aerial bombardment without cover.

Leadership and Charisma (TV incarnation)

Ability to rally Machine Robo tribes and human resistance, negotiate alliances (notably with Blue Jet and the Jet Tribe), and inspire morale as princess of Cronos.

Limit: Political authority limited by age and Gyandlar occupation; often requires rescue by Rom and allies; not a tactical commander — defers to Rom and elders.

Enhanced Agility and Reflexes

Attested acrobatic dodging of gunfire and mutant attacks, high leaps, and sword-form katas at superhuman speed in OVA animation.

Limit: Animation-dependent; no canonical quantification; still bound by gravity and stamina. No flight or teleportation.

Accounts I
The Gyandlar Invasion of CronosMachine Robo: Cronos no Gyakushuu TV, episodes 1-5 (1986)

1986

The dimensional empire Gyandlar, led by Emperor Gadess (and later Garudi/Garudan), invades the peaceful planet Cronos to seize its secret of Hyribead energy. Prince Rom Stol escapes the fallen capital with his younger sister Leina Stol and the Machine Robo Blue Jet. Leina's early arc establishes her as a protected princess who chooses to join the resistance rather than remain in hiding, witnessing the destruction of her home and the enslavement of Cronos citizens. Establishes Leina's motivation (liberation of Cronos), her relationship as Rom's sister and moral anchor, and the franchise's central war. Sets up episodic liberation of Cronos regions.

Flight Across Cronos and the ResistanceMachine Robo: Cronos no Gyakushuu TV, episodes 6-30 (1986-1987)

1986-1987

Rom and Leina travel across Cronos gathering allies among the Machine Robo tribes (Jet, Battle, etc.). Leina repeatedly acts as envoy and healer, befriending tribes and human survivors, and is captured and rescued several times — a common 80s heroine plot device. She pilots no large mecha herself but coordinates support and provides intelligence. The arc shows her growing from passive royalty to active participant. Develops Leina's relational network and reputation; cements her non-pilot, human-heart role in a mecha show. No Wolf Sword appears in this continuity.

Leina: Wolf Sword Legend Act I - Rebirth in the WastelandLeina Kenrou Densetsu Act I OVA (1988-03-10)

1988

In an alternate post-apocalyptic Cronos, Leina Ashta is found as a lone survivor among ruins, raised or reawakened as a wolf-warrior. She claims the massive Wolf Sword from a ruined shrine/forge and defends a village from Gyandlar-descended mutants and warlord soldiers. The episode is largely dialogue-light, focusing on showcase fights, transformation from clothed refugee to armored warrior, and establishment of the Wolf Sword as her burden. Reintroduces Leina as solo action heroine for the OVA market; establishes iconic wolf-armor design and sword that defines all later reception. Explicitly separate continuity from TV per liner notes.

Accounts II
Wolf Sword Legend Act II - The Mutant FortressLeina Kenrou Densetsu Act II OVA (1988-12-10)

1988

Leina infiltrates a Gyandlar fortress-factory where humans are enslaved and mutated into soldiers. She battles the fortress commander — a heavily mutated Garudi-type warlord — and frees captives, suffering serious wounds that demonstrate her limits. The episode expands lore: Gyandlar bio-experiments explain mutants, and Leina's own superhuman physiology is hinted to be linked to the same experiments or to Cronos's Hyribead legacy. Deepens dark history (slavery, body-horror) and shows Leina as liberator rather than princess; cliffhanger leads into Act III.

Wolf Sword Legend Act III - The Final HowlLeina Kenrou Densetsu Act III OVA (1990-01-10)

1990

Wounded and hunted, Leina makes a final assault on the central Gyandlar citadel to destroy its power source and prevent the wasteland from being rendered uninhabitable. She duels the final warlord in extended sword combat, destroys the citadel's reactor, and walks away into the wasteland as a mythic lone wolf — ending on an open, wandering-hero note rather than restoration of a kingdom. No coronation or return to royalty; the OVA ends with survival, not triumph. Concludes OVA trilogy without sequel; cements Leina's image as post-apocalyptic wandering swordswoman, influencing later 'battle beauty' archetypes. No canonical continuation in TV continuity.

The Alternate Continuity Problem — TV Princess vs OVA Wolf WarriorFranchise meta-narrative: Bandai Visual liner notes and Ashi Productions interviews (1988-1990); corroborated by Anime News Network encyclopedia entries

1988-1990 (retrospective)

Official paratext explicitly frames the OVA as a 'parallel world' or 'if' story using the same character design base but not continuing TV plot. Leina Stol (TV, 1986) and Leina Ashta (OVA, 1988) share a name and visual lineage but have incompatible histories, ages, power levels, and costumes. This meta-story explains why Leina can be both a 14-year-old princess and a 20-something superhuman barbarian without contradiction — they are not the same timeline. Explains most fan confusion and justifies treating Q109324778 (Ashta) as distinct from the TV character entry. Essential for accurate citation and to avoid conflating children's and adult materials.

Leina Ashta is the original form and Leina Stol is a mistranslation. — Leina Stol (TV, 1986) is the original children's character; Leina Ashta/Wolf Warrior is a later (1988) adult OVA reimagining that reuses the design. The TV version predates the OVA by two years.

Leina wields the Wolf Sword in the Machine Robo TV series. — The Wolf Sword is OVA-only. In TV she carries no signature sword and is not a superhuman barbarian; her role is diplomatic/support.

Wolf Sword Legend is a sequel to Fist of the North Star and Leina is from that universe. — No narrative connection to Fist of the North Star; similarity is stylistic (post-apocalyptic muscular barbarians) and staff overlap in 80s Ashi Productions action anime. Leina is Machine Robo franchise only.

Leina Ashta is a fully original folklore/myth figure. — She is a 1980s commercial anime character created by PLEX/Bandai to sell toys and videos, not a figure from Japanese folklore or prior literature.
