Who It Is

In the shadowed corridors of Hellsing Manor, a butler moves with the grace of a dancer and the precision of a surgeon. Walter C. Dornez, the 'Angel of Death,' serves tea and dispenses death with equal aplomb, his monomolecular wires singing through the air. But beneath the impeccable service lies a man haunted by obsolescence, a servant who would rather burn the world than be forgotten.

Walter is the epitome of the perfect butler: polite, composed, and utterly loyal to the Hellsing family. He is meticulous in his duties, whether serving tea or eliminating enemies. However, beneath his calm exterior lies a deep-seated fear of becoming obsolete. As he ages, he becomes increasingly resentful of his declining abilities and the rise of younger, more powerful beings like Alucard. This jealousy and desire to be remembered drive him to betray Integra and join the Major. In his youth, he is more impulsive and eager to prove himself, but still shows the same loyalty and skill.

Powers
Monomolecular Wire Manipulation

Walter uses ultra-thin, high-velocity wires that can cut through almost any material. He can control these wires with incredible precision, allowing him to slice enemies from a distance, create traps, and even use them for mobility.

Limit: The wires are only effective at close to medium range. Walter must have physical control over the wires, and they can be dodged or blocked by faster or more durable opponents.

Enhanced Physical Abilities

Even as a human, Walter possesses peak human strength, speed, and agility, honed through years of training and service.

Limit: As a human, he is still vulnerable to injury and aging.

Vampiric Powers (Post-Transformation)

After being turned into a vampire by the Major, Walter gains superhuman strength, speed, regeneration, and the ability to use his wires more effectively. He also appears to have a healing factor and increased durability.

Limit: As a vampire, he is vulnerable to sunlight, stakes, and other traditional vampire weaknesses, though he retains his intellect and skills.

Form & Signs

He typically wears a black three-piece suit with a white shirt and a black tie, befitting his role as the Hellsing family butler. In his youth, as depicted in 'Hellsing: The Dawn,' he has dark hair and a more athletic build. Symbols — Walter is associated with his monomolecular wires, which he uses as his primary weapon. The wires are nearly invisible and can slice through flesh and bone with ease. He is also often depicted with a teacup or serving tray, symbolizing his dual role as butler and assassin.

Domains — Walter's primary domain is Hellsing Manor, where he serves as the head butler. He is also seen in various locations during missions, such as the streets of London and the Major's zeppelin. In 'Hellsing: The Dawn,' he operates in Warsaw, Poland, during World War II.

Accounts I
Hellsing: The Dawn (Prequel)Hellsing: The Dawn manga

World War II, 1944

Walter, as a young butler-in-training, accompanies Alucard on a mission to Warsaw, Poland, to stop the Millennium organization from creating an army of artificial vampires. During the mission, Walter demonstrates his prodigious skill with wires, earning the nickname 'Angel of Death.' He also witnesses the cruelty of war and the ruthlessness of Alucard, which shapes his worldview. The mission is successful, but Walter is deeply affected by the experience. He becomes aware of the Major's plans and the nature of Millennium, setting the stage for his future betrayal.

Accounts II
Betrayal and Final BattleHellsing manga and Hellsing Ultimate

Present day (1990s)

After decades of loyal service, Walter becomes disillusioned with his aging body and the prospect of being forgotten. He secretly allies with the Major, who promises him a chance to regain his youth and power. During the assault on London, Walter reveals his betrayal, attacking Integra and Alucard. He is transformed into a vampire and engages in a brutal battle with Alucard, ultimately being defeated. Before dying, he asks Integra to remember him, revealing his tragic motivation. Walter's betrayal leads to the destruction of Hellsing Manor and the deaths of many. His defeat allows Alucard to focus on the Major, leading to the final confrontation. Walter's death serves as a tragic end to a character who could not accept his own mortality.
