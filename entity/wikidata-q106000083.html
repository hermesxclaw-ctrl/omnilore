The Approach

In the early 1990s, a foreign-born filly with a name evoking the legendary women warriors of Greek myth tore through Japan's racing establishment, defying restrictions that barred her from the sport's most coveted Classics. Hishi Amazon, a bay daughter of Theatrical, not only conquered her filly peers but repeatedly beat colts and older males, earning three consecutive JRA Awards and a place in the hearts of racing fans as a symbol of tenacity and excellence against the odds.

Content advisory. No dark material. Contains references to horse racing and historical restrictions on foreign-born horses, which may be of interest but not distressing.

Stories & Myths

Racing Career Highlights

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1993-1996

Summary

Hishi Amazon began racing on dirt due to periostitis, then transitioned to turf. She won the Hanshin Sansai Himba Stakes as a two-year-old, earning her first JRA Award. In 1994, she won five graded races in a row, including the Queen Elizabeth II Commemorative Cup, and placed second in the Arima Kinen. In 1995, she won the Sankei Sho All Comers and Kyoto Daishoten, and placed second in the Japan Cup. She retired in 1996.
