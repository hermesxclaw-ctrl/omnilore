Eyeless Jack

The Organ Thief, The Cannibal Surgeon

TypeInternet-Born Entity (Creepypasta)

Who It Is
Form

Eyeless Jack's appearance is built around a single absence and a single substance. The character is described as humanoid in overall form and roughly adult male in build, wearing a plain dark hooded sweatshirt — the garment is essentially standardised across all depictions — with the hood characteristically raised. The face is covered by a smooth blue-grey or slate-coloured mask, featureless except for two eye holes and a mouth opening, and it is a point of persistent ambiguity in the tradition whether this is a mask worn over a face or the face itself, hardened and discoloured; the original story does not settle the question and fan depictions split roughly evenly. Behind the eye holes there are no eyes. The sockets are empty and filled instead with a viscous black substance that wells up and runs down the cheeks in slow streaks, an element the character's creator has identified as his own addition to the borrowed image. In the later background material this substance is specified as a mixture of hot tar and blood poured into the emptied sockets during a failed ritual, but in the original story it is simply a black goop with no explanation offered. The skin, where visible at the hands and wrists, is grey-blue and unhealthy. The character is conventionally shown with a scalpel or short surgical blade, the instrument of the organ removal that defines his behaviour, and this is the one iconographic element that is functional rather than decorative. What is most notable about the design, considered as a piece of horror construction, is its restraint: there is no distortion of scale, no impossible anatomy, no supernatural signalling. The figure would pass for a person in a hooded sweatshirt at any distance beyond a few metres, and the horror is concentrated entirely in the two things you would only see up close — that there is nothing behind the eye holes, and that something black is coming out of them.

Powers

Sight without eyes The character navigates unlit interiors, locates sleeping victims, and performs precise surgical extraction despite having no eyes. The original story explicitly leaves this unexplained — the ability to see and survive without eyes is among the things never accounted for — and that refusal is deliberate. Fan and later material variously attribute it to residual supernatural perception, to the black substance in the sockets functioning as a sensory medium, or to an eldritch nature that does not require organs at all.

Surgical organ extraction The character's defining capability: the removal of internal organs, most consistently kidneys, from a living and sleeping victim with sufficient precision that the victim is not woken and survives. This is presented as skill rather than magic, and its clinical quality is what places the character in the medical-horror tradition rather than the slasher one. The wound is typically discovered by the victim after the fact.

Silent intrusion Entry into occupied homes and approach to sleeping occupants without detection. As with Jeff the Killer, the narrative treats this as an unremarked competence rather than a stated power, but it functions as one, and it is the mechanism by which the character's threat is made domestic.

Consumption of human tissue The character is described as having at least partially eaten the organs he removes. This converts the extraction from mutilation into feeding and supplies the character's only implied motive — hunger — which the original text does not develop further. In the later background material the compulsion is a consequence of his transformation rather than a choice.

Eldritch transformation and amnesia (later-attributed) In the supplementary background material published by the author in 2014, the failed sacrificial ritual transformed Jack Nyras into an eldritch abomination and erased his memory of his human life. This grants the character a non-human ontology and explains both his survival without eyes and his lack of stated motive. It is absent from the 2012 story and is documented here as a later authorial addition rather than an original feature.

Incident Log

The original story is a first-person encounter narrative. Its opening is preserved verbatim in the wiki's surviving revision history: "Hello. My name is Mitch. I'm here to tell you guys about an experience I had. I don't know if it was paranormal or whatever stupid words people use to describe supernatural phenomena..." — a deliberately flat, sceptical, conversational register that establishes the narrator as an unwilling and unimpressed witness rather than a horror protagonist. The account describes an encounter with an entity that had stolen and at least partially eaten human organs. The story's structure is built on withholding: it supplies the encounter and the aftermath, and refuses to supply the entity's origin, purpose, nature, or the mechanism by which a creature with no eyes locates and operates on its victims. There is no confrontation in the conventional sense and no resolution. The horror is located in the discovery of what has been taken and in the narrator's inability to construct any account of what took it.

Two years after the original story, its author published background material supplying the origin the story had withheld. In it the character has a human past under the name Jack Nyras. He is used as the victim of a human sacrifice in which his eyes are torn out and the sockets filled with a mixture of hot tar and blood. The ritual fails. Rather than completing whatever it was intended to accomplish, the failure transforms Jack into an eldritch abomination, and in the process erases his memory of his human life entirely. The material thus explains the empty sockets, the black substance, the absence of stated motive, and the character's continued function without eyes, all in a single stroke — while preserving the character's ignorance of his own origin, so that the entity in the 2012 story cannot explain himself because he genuinely does not know.

On 21 November 2014 the Eyeless Jack story was removed from Creepypasta Wiki at the request of its original author. The context was the wiki's removal of the original Jeff the Killer story, part of a broader quality-driven purge of early, poorly written, high-traffic pages; Azelf5000 is reported to have preferred to withdraw his own work before administrators removed it. The wiki's handling of the deletion is documented and unusually careful: the page now contains only a redirect to an administrative archive that states the story "has been removed from the main site per the request of the original author", notes that the revision history has been left intact for historical purposes, links to a list of other deleted pages, and carries a __NOINDEX__ directive instructing search engines not to index the notice.

Alongside and largely displacing the authorial origin, a fan-written backstory credited to the writer La Mishi Mishi — better known as the creator of the creepypasta character Sally Williams — became the most widely circulated account of the character's origins. More broadly, the vacuum left by the original story's refusal to explain anything was filled by an enormous body of fan work that granted Eyeless Jack a personality he never had: melancholy, reluctant, capable of speech and restraint, driven by compulsion rather than malice, and frequently placed in domestic or romantic situations alongside other creepypasta figures. This fan Jack is now the dominant version by a wide margin and is the form in which most audiences encounter the character.

Scholarly Debates
Which origin story is authoritative
The author's 2014 background material is authoritative. Azelf5000 created the character, and his account — Jack Nyras, the failed sacrifice, the tar and blood poured into the emptied sockets, the eldritch transformation and amnesia — is the only origin with any claim to authorial sanction. The fan version by La Mishi Mishi is derivative regardless of how widely it circulates. ⇌ Neither is authoritative, and the fan version is functionally canonical. The 2012 story deliberately explained nothing, and any origin — including the author's own, published two years later and eight days before he withdrew the work — is a retrofit. In a medium where the transmitted version is the real one, the La Mishi Mishi origin wins on the only criterion that applies: it is the one people know.
Whether the deletion should be respected
It should. The author explicitly requested removal of his own work, the wiki honoured that request and even applied __NOINDEX__ to the notice, and continued hosting of the story on mirror sites and narration channels overrides a creator's clearly expressed wishes about his own writing. Attribution-free circulation is not a virtue simply because it is customary. ⇌ It cannot be, and the question is somewhat beside the point. The character had passed into the commons within months of publication, assembled from a name the author borrowed and an image he did not make. Withdrawing the text removed one document from one site; it could not and did not withdraw a figure that dozens of communities were already elaborating independently.
Whether the mask is worn or is the face
It is a mask. The blue-grey surface is smooth, uniform, and clearly delineated from the neck and hands in most depictions, and the eye holes read as apertures cut in a covering rather than as orbital sockets. ⇌ It is the face, transformed. The 2014 origin describes a body reconfigured by a failed ritual into something no longer human, which accounts for a hardened, discoloured facial surface without requiring an object. The black substance emerging from the sockets makes more sense flowing from a body than from behind a plastic shell.
Whether Eyeless Jack has any folkloric depth or is purely a composite
He is a genuine piece of contemporary folklore. The organ-theft motif at his centre is one of the most widely circulated contemporary legends of the last half-century, and the character functions as a personification of it — the kidney-heist story given a face and a name from the Jack tradition of Anglophone folk narrative. ⇌ He is a composite with no depth beneath the assembly. The name came from a DeviantArt comic, the image from an unrelated 4chan thread about video-game creatures, and the story from a third party who admits the organ-theft resonance was subconscious. Reading folkloric structure into an accidental collage mistakes coincidence for tradition.
What We Don't Know

The largest gap is the original text itself. Because the story was deleted from Creepypasta Wiki at the author's request in November 2014, the authoritative version could not be retrieved, and the only fragment verified directly in this research pass is the opening sentence preserved in the page's retained revision-history edit summary. All description of the story's content beyond that fragment rests on secondary documentation and on mirror versions of unverified fidelity. Second, Eyeless Jack has no Wikipedia article, so no encyclopedic treatment of the kind available for Jeff the Killer, Ben Drowned, or the Russian Sleep Experiment could be consulted, and the sourcing here is correspondingly weaker — several claims about the character's creation, including the 4chan image origin, the Pastamonsters name derivation, and the La Mishi Mishi fan origin, come from community-run horror-fiction sites rather than from independently verifiable records. Third, the identity of Azelf5000 beyond the reported alternative names Thunder and Joey is not established, and no direct statement by the author was retrieved. Fourth, the 2014 background material was not obtained directly; its content is reported from secondary summary. Fifth, whether the deletion request was made freely or under community pressure during the wiki's quality purge is characterised in sources as the author preferring to act before administrators did, but no primary statement confirming this was located. Sixth, no peer-reviewed scholarship on this character was found. Seventh, the fan corpus is characterised qualitatively rather than measured.

A way to hold it in your mind

You wake up in your own bed, and something is wrong. There's a dressing taped to your side that you did not put there, and underneath it a line of neat sutures — surgical work, careful, done by someone who knew exactly what they were doing. A kidney is gone. Nobody broke a window. Nobody woke you. And the thing that did it has no eyes at all: just two empty sockets behind a blue-grey mask, with something black and slow running out of them and down its cheeks. Here is the part that has kept people talking for over a decade — the man who wrote this story asked for it to be taken off the internet in 2014, and it was. The character never even slowed down.
