Who It Is

You wake up in your own bed, and something is wrong. There's a dressing taped to your side that you did not put there, and underneath it a line of neat sutures — surgical work, careful, done by someone who knew exactly what they were doing. A kidney is gone. Nobody broke a window. Nobody woke you. And the thing that did it has no eyes at all: just two empty sockets behind a blue-grey mask, with something black and slow running out of them and down its cheeks. Here is the part that has kept people talking for over a decade — the man who wrote this story asked for it to be taken off the internet in 2014, and it was. The character never even slowed down.

The original story establishes behaviour and withholds everything else, and this asymmetry is the character's defining feature. Behaviourally, Eyeless Jack enters homes at night, reaches sleeping occupants, and removes organs — most consistently the kidneys — with surgical precision, leaving the victim alive. He is described as at least partially consuming what he takes. The surgical competence is emphasised: incisions are clean, the work is done without waking the victim, and the victim survives to discover the loss, which is the horror the original narrative turns on. Powers
Sight without eyes

The character navigates unlit interiors, locates sleeping victims, and performs precise surgical extraction despite having no eyes. The original story explicitly leaves this unexplained — the ability to see and survive without eyes is among the things never accounted for — and that refusal is deliberate. Fan and later material variously attribute it to residual supernatural perception, to the black substance in the sockets functioning as a sensory medium, or to an eldritch nature that does not require organs at all.

Limit:

Surgical organ extraction

The character's defining capability: the removal of internal organs, most consistently kidneys, from a living and sleeping victim with sufficient precision that the victim is not woken and survives. This is presented as skill rather than magic, and its clinical quality is what places the character in the medical-horror tradition rather than the slasher one. The wound is typically discovered by the victim after the fact.

Limit:

Silent intrusion

Entry into occupied homes and approach to sleeping occupants without detection. As with Jeff the Killer, the narrative treats this as an unremarked competence rather than a stated power, but it functions as one, and it is the mechanism by which the character's threat is made domestic.

Limit:

Consumption of human tissue

The character is described as having at least partially eaten the organs he removes. This converts the extraction from mutilation into feeding and supplies the character's only implied motive — hunger — which the original text does not develop further. In the later background material the compulsion is a consequence of his transformation rather than a choice.

Limit:

Eldritch transformation and amnesia (later-attributed)

In the supplementary background material published by the author in 2014, the failed sacrificial ritual transformed Jack Nyras into an eldritch abomination and erased his memory of his human life. This grants the character a non-human ontology and explains both his survival without eyes and his lack of stated motive. It is absent from the 2012 story and is documented here as a later authorial addition rather than an original feature.

Accounts I
The Encounter (the 2012 original)Creepypasta Wiki page "Eyeless Jack", created by Azelf5000

published 2012-02-25; removed from the originating site 2014-11-21

The original story is a first-person encounter narrative. Its opening is preserved verbatim in the wiki's surviving revision history: "Hello. My name is Mitch. I'm here to tell you guys about an experience I had. I don't know if it was paranormal or whatever stupid words people use to describe supernatural phenomena..." — a deliberately flat, sceptical, conversational register that establishes the narrator as an unwilling and unimpressed witness rather than a horror protagonist. The account describes an encounter with an entity that had stolen and at least partially eaten human organs. The story's structure is built on withholding: it supplies the encounter and the aftermath, and refuses to supply the entity's origin, purpose, nature, or the mechanism by which a creature with no eyes locates and operates on its victims. There is no confrontation in the conventional sense and no resolution. Jack Nyras and the Failed Sacrifice (the authorial origin, 2014)Supplementary background material published by Azelf5000

first installment 2014-06-14; final installment 2014-11-13

Two years after the original story, its author published background material supplying the origin the story had withheld. In it the character has a human past under the name Jack Nyras. He is used as the victim of a human sacrifice in which his eyes are torn out and the sockets filled with a mixture of hot tar and blood. The ritual fails. Rather than completing whatever it was intended to accomplish, the failure transforms Jack into an eldritch abomination, and in the process erases his memory of his human life entirely. The material thus explains the empty sockets, the black substance, the absence of stated motive, and the character's continued function without eyes, all in a single stroke — while preserving the character's ignorance of his own origin, so that the entity in the 2012 story cannot explain himself because he genuinely does not know. The material was published in the months immediately preceding the story's removal, with the final installment appearing on 13 November 2014 and the deletion following eight days later on 21 November. The DeletionCreepypasta Wiki administrative record; page "Creepypasta Wiki:Deleted Page/Eyeless Jack"

2014-11-21

On 21 November 2014 the Eyeless Jack story was removed from Creepypasta Wiki at the request of its original author. The context was the wiki's removal of the original Jeff the Killer story, part of a broader quality-driven purge of early, poorly written, high-traffic pages; Azelf5000 is reported to have preferred to withdraw his own work before administrators removed it. The wiki's handling of the deletion is documented and unusually careful: the page now contains only a redirect to an administrative archive that states the story "has been removed from the main site per the request of the original author", notes that the revision history has been left intact for historical purposes, links to a list of other deleted pages, and carries a __NOINDEX__ directive instructing search engines not to index the notice. The deletion is the most scholarly interesting event in the character's history because it constitutes a controlled experiment in whether authorial withdrawal can stop folkloric circulation. The Fan Origin and the Character's Second LifeFan-made origin story credited to La Mishi Mishi; wider fan corpus

c. 2012–present

Alongside and largely displacing the authorial origin, a fan-written backstory credited to the writer La Mishi Mishi — better known as the creator of the creepypasta character Sally Williams — became the most widely circulated account of the character's origins. More broadly, the vacuum left by the original story's refusal to explain anything was filled by an enormous body of fan work that granted Eyeless Jack a personality he never had: melancholy, reluctant, capable of speech and restraint, driven by compulsion rather than malice, and frequently placed in domestic or romantic situations alongside other creepypasta figures. This fan Jack is now the dominant version by a wide margin and is the form in which most audiences encounter the character. The character became substantially more popular after his authoritative text was deleted than before, and the version that predominates is one the original author neither wrote nor endorsed. This is the clearest available demonstration that in this medium the transmitted version, not the authored one, is the canonical one.

Which origin story is authoritativeThe author's 2014 background material is authoritative. Azelf5000 created the character, and his account — Jack Nyras, the failed sacrifice, the tar and blood poured into the emptied sockets, the eldritch transformation and amnesia — is the only origin with any claim to authorial sanction. The fan version by La Mishi Mishi is derivative regardless of how widely it circulates.Neither is authoritative, and the fan version is functionally canonical. The 2012 story deliberately explained nothing, and any origin — including the author's own, published two years later and eight days before he withdrew the work — is a retrofit. In a medium where the transmitted version is the real one, the La Mishi Mishi origin wins on the only criterion that applies: it is the one people know.
Whether the deletion should be respectedIt should. The author explicitly requested removal of his own work, the wiki honoured that request and even applied __NOINDEX__ to the notice, and continued hosting of the story on mirror sites and narration channels overrides a creator's clearly expressed wishes about his own writing. Attribution-free circulation is not a virtue simply because it is customary.It cannot be, and the question is somewhat beside the point. The character had passed into the commons within months of publication, assembled from a name the author borrowed and an image he did not make. Withdrawing the text removed one document from one site; it could not and did not withdraw a figure that dozens of communities were already elaborating independently.
Whether the mask is worn or is the faceIt is a mask. The blue-grey surface is smooth, uniform, and clearly delineated from the neck and hands in most depictions, and the eye holes read as apertures cut in a covering rather than as orbital sockets.It is the face, transformed. The 2014 origin describes a body reconfigured by a failed ritual into something no longer human, which accounts for a hardened, discoloured facial surface without requiring an object. The black substance emerging from the sockets makes more sense flowing from a body than from behind a plastic shell.
Whether Eyeless Jack has any folkloric depth or is purely a compositeHe is a genuine piece of contemporary folklore. The organ-theft motif at his centre is one of the most widely circulated contemporary legends of the last half-century, and the character functions as a personification of it — the kidney-heist story given a face and a name from the Jack tradition of Anglophone folk narrative.He is a composite with no depth beneath the assembly. The name came from a DeviantArt comic, the image from an unrelated 4chan thread about video-game creatures, and the story from a third party who admits the organ-theft resonance was subconscious. Reading folkloric structure into an accidental collage mistakes coincidence for tradition.
