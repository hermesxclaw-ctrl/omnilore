Who It Is
Form

790 presents as a disembodied robotic head, roughly approximating the dimensions of a human head, constructed from a combination of plastic and metallic materials that reflect the Divine Order's aesthetic of utilitarian industrial design—slightly antiquarian, recalling mid-twentieth-century science fiction production design rather than sleek futurism. The most visually distinctive feature of 790's face is the tri-screen arrangement that replaces a conventional face: two rectangular screens occupying the position of eyes, each displaying animated digital representations of pupils and irises capable of conveying a range of emotional states through pupil dilation, movement, and squinting; and a third, wider screen at the lower portion of the face displaying animated lips that move in synchronization with voiced dialogue. Crucially, the lip footage visible on 790's mouth screen consists of the real lips of voice actor Jeffrey Hirschfield, recorded separately and composited onto the prop, lending an uncanny, slightly grotesque verisimilitude to 790's facial expressions. The dome of 790's head above the screens is smooth and hairless, with a blunt, almost featureless profile from above; the lower structure terminates approximately where a neck would attach, with no surviving neck, torso, or limbs. The overall construction style reflects the practical effects aesthetic of 1990s science fiction television production, which was noted by critic Keith Booker as appearing somewhat simplistic in comparison to the series' other special effects work, though this apparent crudeness may be a deliberate design choice to emphasize 790's indignity and comic vulnerability. 790 moves through the Lexx's organic corridors seated on a small motorized cart or platform, giving the severed head a peculiarly domestic mobility. Internally, the head houses a hybrid architecture: a small but functionally vital piece of living human brain tissue—the preserved remnant of Squeeze Pimmel's brain—integrated into an extensive cybernetic memory and processing matrix, the interface between biology and mechanism being the key to 790's emotional capacity and its claim to personhood.

Powers

Encyclopedic Computational Memory 790's cybernetic architecture encompasses an extraordinary store of knowledge and recall capacity. The Russian Wikipedia entry explicitly notes that '790's head is indispensable when solving any technical problems, as it knows everything and remembers everything' (translated). This positions 790 as the crew's primary reference intelligence: it can retrieve technical specifications, navigational data, historical records, and operational procedures on demand. The depth of this knowledge base reflects its origin as a production-line service drone on the Cluster, one of the Divine Order's most advanced installations, where comprehensive information access was a core operational requirement.

Remote Machine Interface and Cybernetic Hacking 790 can wirelessly access and control a wide range of machines and cybernetic systems. Demonstrated applications include opening and locking computerized security doors, controlling the motorized transport platform it uses for mobility, interfacing with the Lexx's navigation and control systems, and presumably accessing Cluster-standard computer networks. This ability makes 790 a significant tactical asset in infiltration or emergency scenarios. The range and precise mechanism of this remote access are not fully specified in the series, but it appears to operate at close-to-medium range and requires no physical interface beyond 790's own processing systems.

Extended Autonomous Battery Endurance 790 contains built-in accumulators (internal power cells) capable of sustaining continuous, uninterrupted operation for 309 years without any external power source. This figure is explicitly stated in the Russian Wikipedia account. This extraordinary endurance reflects the Divine Order's engineering requirements for long-duration service drones that might operate in isolated or resource-scarce environments. In practical narrative terms, it means that 790 is never at risk of simply running down, and that destroying it physically is the only reliable way to neutralize it—a fact 790 itself is aware of and occasionally invokes.

Compulsive Total-Devotion Conditioning (Love Slave Brainwashing) As a direct result of exposure to the Lusticon's mental conditioning sequence—the brainwashing component of the love slave transformation process—790's brain contains permanently installed compulsive devotion programming. This conditioning causes 790 to experience absolute, operationally total infatuation with the first person it perceives following each activation or mind-reset. This is not a voluntary power but a fundamental cognitive constraint; however, 790 deploys the resulting devotion with considerable tactical creativity, including hacking enemy systems, composing persuasive rhetoric, and forming strategic alliances, all in service of the beloved. The conditioning also appears to be reset-able: Kai's repair of 790 following Prince's damage effectively re-triggered the awakening, causing 790 to fall in love with Kai instead of Xev.

Real-Time Poetry Composition and Rhetorical Virtuosity 790 composes elaborate verse—frequently darkly romantic, melodramatic, or sarcastically inverted—in real-time, often in the midst of crisis situations. This capability reflects both the partial human-brain substrate (Squeeze Pimmel presumably had some capacity for linguistic and emotional expression) and the vast computational memory available to 790. The poetry serves multiple social functions: expressing devotion to the beloved, deploying wit as a weapon against antagonists (especially Stanley Tweedle), and establishing 790 as a character of genuine if bizarre interiority. Jeffrey Hirschfield and Lex Gigeroff shared the writing of 790's verse compositions roughly equally, per Hirschfield's own account.

Functional Fearlessness (Absence of Self-Preservation Drive) 790 explicitly lacks a functioning self-preservation instinct, as confirmed by direct dialogue: when threatened with destruction, 790 has responded that the effort is wasted because it can always be reassembled. This absence of fear of death—or rather, of any meaningful death-equivalent in 790's case—produces a distinctive behavioral profile: 790 will take positions and actions that a self-preserving agent would avoid, up to and including physically confronting larger and more powerful entities. This fearlessness is genuine rather than performative; it arises from 790's material understanding of its own nature as a repairable/reassemblable mechanism.

Organic-Cybernetic Hybrid Consciousness 790's cognitive architecture is not purely mechanical but hybrid: it contains a 'small section of living brain' (Russian Wikipedia: 'Голова дроида содержит маленький участок живого мозга, который органично связан с его обширной кибернетической памятью') seamlessly integrated with extensive cybernetic memory. This organic component is the seat of 790's emotional capacity—its ability to love, to express passion, and to compose genuine poetry—distinguishing it from pure calculation machines in the same universe. The organic component also raises the ethical stakes of 790's treatment: it is not merely equipment but a living entity with a subjective inner life, however constrained and redirected by the brainwashing it has undergone.

Accounts

On the planet B1B, a young woman named Squeeze Pimmel worked as a production-line hostess serving drinks to Divine Order clerics. Prohibited from social engagement with clerics, she misread a footsie gesture from an older cleric as coming from an attractive younger one, flashed the young cleric her breasts, and was immediately arrested. Transported to the Cluster for trial, she was convicted of making inappropriate advances to a cleric of His Divine Shadow and of public indecency. Her sentence was presented as a mercy: rather than execution, her vital organs—including parts of her brain—would be harvested and repurposed as components in a model 790 robotic drone. With no memory of her former life, the newly created 790 was assigned to operate the Lusticon, a machine that transformed convicted criminals into love slaves. During the transformation of Zev Bellringer (convicted of failing to perform her wifely duties), an escaped Cluster Lizard devoured 790's biological body. As the Lusticon's transformation sequence had already initiated, Zev—strengthened by inadvertent Cluster Lizard DNA—broke free and placed 790's severed head into the Lusticon's brainwashing apparatus. 790 awakened to see Zev and received the full love slave conditioning. Zev subsequently became 790's entire universe.

For the first two seasons, 790 is essentially a fixture of the Lexx crew defined by its adoration for Zev (and after episode 2.2 'Terminal,' her replacement Xev). It provides critical technical information, hacks systems when asked, and composes romantic verse. Its relationship with the rest of the crew is uniformly antagonistic: it despises Stanley Tweedle with inventive persistence, barely tolerates Kai (an undead assassin who is literally incapable of emotion and therefore impervious to 790's emotional manipulations), and treats the Lexx itself as a necessary conveyance rather than a crewmate. 790 will do virtually anything to protect Zev and serve her interests; the few instances of genuine crew cooperation typically involve 790 calculating that helping others will ultimately benefit Zev.

In the episode titled '791,' 790 is given the opportunity to inhabit a new physical body—a 791-model robot chassis, a more advanced production than its own. The prospect of embodiment appears to resolve 790's most fundamental frustration: the inability to physically act in the world or express love through physical presence. However, the 791 chassis is equipped with processing systems significantly more powerful than 790's own, and these systems are capable of subverting the personality of any head attached to them. The result is a temporarily transformed 790: homicidal, erratic, and stripped of the characteristic wit and devotion that define the character. The situation is eventually resolved, restoring 790 to its normal condition.

At the beginning of Season 3, the Lexx crew arrives at a system dominated by two twin planets: Fire and Water. Prince, the enigmatic and dangerous ruler of the planet Fire, interacts with the crew and at some point causes 790 to be dropped, physically damaging the robot head. Kai subsequently repairs 790, but the repair process inadvertently resets 790's mind to its initial awakening state. When 790 opens its eye-screens after the repair, the first person it sees is Kai—the undead last of the Brunnen-G. 790's entire devotion system immediately and irrevocably re-orients toward Kai. The experience is further complicated by 790 now declaring itself female, consistent with the love slave conditioning logic (the original Squeeze Pimmel was female; loving a male, Kai, 790 may have accessed a more authentically Pimmel-aligned gender identity).

In the final episode of the series, as the narrative reaches its conclusion on Earth, the young Lexx (a second living spacecraft of the same species as the original) consumes 790. The robot head is literally eaten by the creature, ending 790's existence in the series universe. The death is characteristic of the show's mixture of epic stakes and darkly absurd ends: a character defined by its longing for embodiment and its deathless perseverance is ultimately digested by a living spaceship.

Scholarly Debates
Identity continuity: Is 790 the same person as Squeeze Pimmel?
790 is a continuation of Squeeze Pimmel's personhood. When 790 briefly recovers memories of Pimmel's life, it speaks of them in the first person ('I'). The organic brain component that animates 790's emotional and creative capacities is directly derived from Pimmel's actual brain. From a philosophy-of-personal-identity standpoint, psychological continuity—shared memories, first-person reference—supports treating 790 as Pimmel persisting in a transformed substrate. ⇌ 790 is a functionally new entity that happens to be built from Pimmel's brain matter. The brainwashing conditioning has effectively overwritten Pimmel's personality, relationships, and values with an entirely new orientation. 790 has no sustained access to Pimmel's memories and lived its entire active existence under the compulsive love programming rather than under any motivations Pimmel would have recognized as her own. The reduction to a single brain component among extensive cybernetic systems also challenges the idea that sufficient substrate continuity remains to ground identity.
Gender identity: Is 790's gender transition from male to female authentic or mechanistically determined?
790's shift from male identification (Seasons 1–2, while loving Zev/Xev) to female identification (Season 3 onward, while loving Kai) represents an authentic gender identity that was suppressed or distorted by the initial programming context, and the reset allowed Squeeze Pimmel's actual gender to reassert itself. Under this reading, 790 is a transgender science fiction character whose true identity had been masked by externally imposed conditioning. ⇌ 790's gender identification at any given time is simply an artifact of the love slave brainwashing logic: the conditioning apparently orients the subject's gender identity in whatever configuration makes the love relationship heterosexually coherent (female loving male Kai; the ambiguity with Zev—a female love slave's conditioning—creates the male identification in Seasons 1–2). Under this reading, 790 has no authentic gender identity independent of the conditioning; its declarations of gender are symptoms of the brainwashing rather than expressions of genuine selfhood.
Tonal register: Is 790 primarily a comic character or a figure of genuine pathos?
790 is primarily a comic character—an absurd, deliberately ridiculous figure whose function in the ensemble is to provide sarcastic one-liners, antagonize Stanley Tweedle, and generate dark comedy through the collision of its impossible situation (a head in love) with the extreme circumstances of the Lexx universe. The cult-tv-lounge review explicitly notes that '790 is very much a comic character' and that it sits awkwardly in Season 3's more serious tone. ⇌ 790's comedy is inseparable from its genuine pathos. The character's situation—a person reduced to a head, permanently incapable of physical love, longing for embodiment, and trapped in an obsessive devotion they cannot choose or escape—is objectively tragic. The dark comedy works precisely because the underlying reality is painful. The best moments of the character (its poetry, its fearlessness, its first-person recollection of Pimmel) function as tragicomedy in which the humor and the pathos are simultaneously present. To read 790 as purely comic is to miss the depth the writers invested in the character's situation.
Production quality: Is 790's appearance a deliberate aesthetic choice or a budget limitation?
Academic commentator Keith Booker noted that 790's appearance looks 'silly' relative to the series' other special effects, suggesting a budget-constrained or technically limited prop. The prop's relatively simple construction—three screens on a plastic/metal head—does reflect the practical constraints of 1990s Canadian-German co-production budgets. ⇌ 790's deliberately low-fi appearance is consistent with the show's overall aesthetic strategy: Lexx is knowingly, ironically camp and uses cheap-looking design as a tonal choice rather than a limitation. 790's screen-face is visually distinctive and memorable in ways that a more polished CGI face would not be; the simplicity is part of the character's comedy and its uncanny quality. The decision to use Hirschfield's real lips for the mouth screen adds an additional layer of deliberate craft to what might superficially appear to be a simple prop.

A way to hold it in your mind

790 is perhaps the only major science fiction television character whose defining characteristic is that it has no body and therefore cannot act on its most powerful emotion — and who responds to this impossible situation with sarcasm, poetry, and increasingly unhinged devotion. It raises questions that still feel unresolved: if a human brain is installed in a machine, brainwashed into love, and lives for centuries as a severed head, is that a person? Is the love real? Is 790 Squeeze Pimmel or something else entirely? The show never fully answers these questions, and the character is more interesting for it.
