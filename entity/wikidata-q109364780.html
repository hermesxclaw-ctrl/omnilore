Who It Is

On a dark mountain path, something hard whistles past your ear and clatters against stone — not a thrown rock, but a living lump of metal that hurled itself. That is the Kanatsubute: a name that literally means 'metal tsubute (thrown pebble),' a beast-class yokai whose entire legend fits in the sting of an unseen projectile.

ATTESTED (minimal): Name implies behavior of throwing or being thrown like a tsubute (pebble). In the broader tsubute-yokai family, the phenomenon is: travelers on mountain roads are struck by pebbles or hard objects with no visible thrower, attributed to yokai. RECONSTRUCTED: Kanatsubute likely belongs to this 'unseen projectile' type, distinguished by metallic hardness or sound. No personality, speech, or motive narrative verified. Oral tradition, likely Edo to early Showa, recorded in 20th-century folklore studies

Travelers walking a mountain pass at dusk hear a whistle and are struck by pebbles or hard objects with no thrower visible. They flee or call out, and the pelting stops. The phenomenon is attributed to a tsubute yokai. Kanatsubute is listed by name among such yokai, implying it follows this pattern with a metallic variant, but no Kanatsubute-specific anecdote with named village, date, or protagonist was located. Travelers are frightened or bruised; route gains reputation as haunted; no lasting curse attested.
