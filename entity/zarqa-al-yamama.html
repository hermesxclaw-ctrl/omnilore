Who It Is

Zarqa' al-Yamama (literally 'the Blue-Eyed Woman of Yamama') is a legendary clairvoyant of pre-Islamic Arabia whose vision was so powerful she could see approaching riders from a distance of three days' journey (or forty parasangs). She belonged to the tribe of Tasm in the Yamama region of central Arabia (Najd). Her blue eyes, unusual among Arabs of the peninsula, were considered a mark of her supernatural perception. According to the legend, an enemy tribe (the Jadis, or alternatively a coalition including the Himyarites from Yemen) planned to attack Yamama. To conceal their approach, the enemy warriors cut down trees and held the branches before them, advancing in the guise of a moving forest. Zarqa' al-Yamama, watching from a distance, saw the anomaly and warned her people: 'I see trees moving toward you — and men among the trees.' Her people dismissed her warning, accusing her of madness or hallucination. The enemy attacked, and the city fell. Zarqa' al-Yamama was captured. The enemy king, enraged that she had nearly thwarted the attack, ordered her eyes to be gouged out. When the eyes were removed, it was discovered that they were veined with black kohl (antimony powder used as eyeliner in Arabia) — in some versions, the veins were made of the very kohl that gave her enhanced vision. She died from the mutilation. Zarqa' al-Yamama represents the tragic figure of the prophet/seer whose warnings are ignored — Cassandra in the Arabian desert. Her story encodes the fatal consequences of ignoring the truth-teller, the destruction that follows hubris ('we cannot be deceived'), and the tragedy of the woman whose gift of sight becomes the cause of her death. Her name lives on in Arabic as a metaphor for 'far-sightedness' and as a cultural touchstone — the city of Riyadh has a neighborhood and various landmarks named after her.
