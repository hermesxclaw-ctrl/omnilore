Who It Is

Imagine having eyes that could see an enemy army from forty miles away—but no one believed you. That was the tragic fate of Zarqa' al-Yamama, a legendary seer whose extraordinary gift became her downfall. Her story, passed down through centuries, raises haunting questions: What happens when wisdom is ignored? And what does it cost to be right too soon?

Zarqa' al-Yamama is portrayed as a wise and perceptive woman, often serving as a seer or advisor to her tribe. She is depicted as calm and composed, but also deeply concerned for the safety of her people, as she could foresee impending dangers. In the legend, she repeatedly warns her tribe of approaching enemies, but her warnings are dismissed due to her gender or the perceived impossibility of her claims. Powers
Clairvoyant Vision

Zarqa' al-Yamama possessed the ability to see objects and people from an extraordinary distance, often described as forty miles or three days' journey. This power allowed her to detect approaching enemies or caravans long before they arrived, giving her tribe a strategic advantage. However, the power was also a burden, as it made her warnings seem implausible and led to her being dismissed as a liar or madwoman. In some versions, her vision was so acute that she could see the color of a rider's clothing or the number of camels in a caravan, but this detail is not always consistent. The power is often interpreted as a metaphor for foresight or intuition, rather than purely physical sight, and it is central to the tragedy of her story.

Accounts I
The Warning of Zarqa' al-YamamaAl-Tabari, 'Tarikh al-Rusul wa al-Muluk'

Legendary, pre-6th century CE

Zarqa' al-Yamama lived among the tribe of Tasm in the region of al-Yamama. One day, while gazing from her vantage point, she saw a distant cloud of dust that she recognized as an approaching army. She warned her people that a large force was coming, but they laughed at her, saying that no one could see so far. She insisted, describing the enemy's weapons and banners, but the tribe dismissed her as delusional. The next day, the enemy—the tribe of Jadis—attacked and massacred the Tasm, fulfilling Zarqa's prophecy. Her warning had been accurate, but it was too late to save her people. The Tasm tribe was nearly annihilated, and Zarqa' al-Yamama was captured. The Jadis tribe, angered by her accurate sight, gouged out her eyes as punishment. She died from the wounds, becoming a martyr for her foresight. The event led to a cycle of revenge, as other tribes sought to avenge the Tasm, eventually leading to the extinction of the Jadis.

Accounts II
The Destruction of the JadisAl-Mas'udi, 'Muruj al-Dhahab'

Legendary, pre-6th century CE

After the massacre of the Tasm, the Jadis tribe celebrated their victory, but their triumph was short-lived. A survivor of the Tasm, named Riyad ibn al-Mukallal, sought revenge and enlisted the help of the Himyarite king, Tubba' al-Akbar. The king sent an army that destroyed the Jadis, fulfilling a prophecy that the Jadis would be wiped out. Zarqa' al-Yamama's death was avenged, but the region of al-Yamama was left desolate, and the story became a cautionary tale about the consequences of ignoring wisdom and the cycle of violence. The Jadis tribe was exterminated, and the region of al-Yamama was depopulated. The story reinforced the idea that the gods or fate would punish those who acted with arrogance and cruelty. It also highlighted the importance of heeding the advice of seers and wise individuals.
