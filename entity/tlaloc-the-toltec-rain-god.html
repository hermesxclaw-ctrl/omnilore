Who They Are

Tlaloc was the rain god of the Toltec civilization, inherited from earlier Teotihuacan and later passed to the Aztecs. The Toltec version of Tlaloc was the god of the Tula mountains — providing the rain that made the fertile valley productive. The famous 'Chac Mool' figures at Tula are associated with Tlaloc's cult, representing intermediaries who received offerings for the rain god.

Cultural Significance

The Toltec Tlaloc cult represented a continuation and expansion of the rain god tradition that began with the Olmec and flourished at Teotihuacan. The Chac Mool — first appearing at Tula — became one of the most widespread ritual objects in Mesoamerica, found from the Toltec heartland to Chichen Itza and the Aztec capital. The Toltec foundations of Tlaloc worship were directly inherited by the Aztecs, who regarded the Toltecs as the inventors of all proper religious practice. The temple of Tlaloc at Tula stood as a precedent for the Templo Mayor's Tlaloc shrine in Tenochtitlan.
