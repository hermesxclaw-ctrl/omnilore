Comparative context: Kuzunoha - The Fox Mother of Abe no Seimei (NOT a Nora Gitsune story)Kuzunoha legend - recorded in Otogi-zōshi, Abe no Seimei monogatari, and Nozaki 1961 pp. 45-58

Heian period origins, Muromachi-period textualization (14th-16th c.)

A white fox takes the form of a woman, Kuzunoha, marries Abe no Yasuna, bears the child who becomes the onmyōji Abe no Seimei, and is forced to reveal her true form when her child sees her tail. She leaves a farewell poem and returns to Shinoda Forest. Included strictly as archetypal heroic-legend fox mother narrative to contextualize what a 'heroic-legend' kitsune story looks like; no source links this to Nora Gitsune. Establishes template of fox as devoted parent and tragic exile; influences later Inari worship and kabuki (Ashiya Dōman Ōuchi Kagami).

Muromachi to Edo period textualization; Heian-period setting

A beautiful courtesan, Tamamo-no-Mae, serves Emperor Toba, is revealed by Abe no Seimei as a nine-tailed fox (kyūbi no kitsune), flees to Nasu Plain as the Sesshōseki (Killing Stone), and is eventually subdued. Anti-heroic but demonstrates nine-tail power scale and fox-as-political-threat motif. Context only; no link to Nora Gitsune. Explains Sesshōseki landmark, moral about deception and imperial corruption; later reinterpreted sympathetically in modern fiction.

Japanese folklore divides foxes into zenko/tenko (celestial, Inari's messengers, benevolent) and yako/nogitsune (field foxes, mischievous or malevolent). A heroic-legend fox would typically be framed as zenko or reformed nogitsune. This structural distinction is essential for understanding any future Nora Gitsune lore, but no classification for Nora Gitsune is attested.
