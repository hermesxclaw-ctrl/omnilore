Enlil, the great mountain, the king of the gods, whose wind brings life and whose wrath brings the flood.

Who They Are

Enlil is the god of wind, storms, and breath — the supreme king of the gods in the Sumerian pantheon (before Marduk's rise). He is the son of An (Heaven) and Ki (Earth), and he separates them, creating the space for human existence. Enlil dwells in the Ekur temple at Nippur, the religious center of Sumer. He holds the tablet of destinies and the royal scepter, granting kingship to earthly rulers. Enlil is a complex figure: he is the beneficent giver of agricultural prosperity (through the fertility brought by winds and rains), but also the destroyer who sends the Great Flood to wipe out humanity (in the Atrahasis Epic) because their noise disturbs his sleep. He is responsible for the confusion of languages (the myth of Enmerkar). His temper is legendary — he is quick to anger and slow to relent. Enlil's sacred number is 50, and his name means 'Lord Wind' or 'Lord Air.' He represents the raw, untamed power of nature that can nurture or annihilate. The Greeks identified him with Cronus.

Attested in Sumerian Temple Hymns / Atrahasis Epic / Gilgamesh EpicEra Sumerian to Neo-Babylonian (c. 3000-500 BCE)
