Who It Is

Imagine a mouse that lives inside a burning tree, its fur woven into a cloth that fire itself cleans. This is the huoshu, the fire rat of Chinese lore, a creature that blurs the line between animal, mineral, and legend, and whose pelt once challenged a suitor in one of Japan's oldest tales.

Dwells within fire, is unharmed by flames, but dies if water is poured on it. It does not exhibit other behaviors in the sources.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Chinese 火 (huǒ, 'fire') and 鼠 (shǔ, 'rat' or 'mouse'). The Japanese readings include hinezumi (from the Wamyō Ruijushō) and kaso (on'yomi).

Pronunciation — Hwo-shoo (Chinese); hee-neh-zoo-mee (Japanese)

Aliases — 火鼠, fire rat, fire mouse, hinezumi, kaso, hi no nezumi

Ownership — Chinese (originating in Chinese classical texts) and Japanese (adopted and featured in classical literature).

Fire immunity

The creature lives in fire and is unharmed by it.

Limit: It dies if water is poured on it.

Fire-resistant fur

Cloth woven from its hair is cleaned by fire, not burned.

Limit: The cloth must be woven from the hair; the fur itself is not said to be fireproof when on the animal.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Generally described as a rat or mouse living in fire. In the Shenyi Jing, it weighs 100 or 1000 jin and has white hair 2 chi long, fine as silk. The Shizhou Ji's huo guang shou is rat-sized with hair 3-4 cun. The Baopuzi's white rat weighs several jin with 3-cun hair.

Symbols — Often associated with fire, immortality, and purification. The cloth made from its hair symbolizes purity and resilience.

Domains — Fire Mountains in southern China (Shenyi Jing), the Mountain of Flame on Kunlun (Soushen Ji), Yan Zhou (Shizhou Ji), Xiao Hill in Nanhai (Baopuzi), Rinan commandery (Wu Li), and the Western Regions (Sui Shu).

Accounts I
The Fire Rat of the Fire MountainsShenyi Jing

Han dynasty (attributed)

In the southern Fire Mountains, there are incombustible trees that burn day and night. Within the fire dwells a mouse with white hair, which can be woven into cloth that is cleansed by fire. The cloth becomes a valuable item, equated with fire-laundered cloth (asbestos).

Accounts II
The White Rat of Xiao HillBaopuzi

4th century

On Xiao Hill in the South China Sea, a white rat with hair 3 cun long lives among plants that burn in spring and extinguish in autumn. Its hair and plant materials are used to make fireproof cloth. Provides three types of fireproof cloth.

The Fire Rat's Pelt RobeThe Tale of the Bamboo Cutter

10th century

Princess Kaguya demands a robe made from the fire rat's pelt. Abe no Miushi attempts to obtain it but is deceived by a merchant who gives him a fake robe that glows with golden light. Abe fails the task and is exposed.

The fire rat's fur is fireproof. — Cloth woven from its hair is said to be cleaned by fire, but the fur itself is not described as fireproof on the animal.
