Who It Is

In the rugged mountains of ancient Arcadia, a town bore the name of a hero so obscure that only a single thread of myth connects him to the land: Phigalus, son of the impious Lycaon, who may have given his name to the city of Phigalia—or perhaps it was a dryad who claimed that honor.

No specific personality traits are recorded. As an eponymous founder, he is likely viewed as a culture hero, but his deeds are not narrated.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Phigalus (Φίγαλος) is likely derived from the Arcadian town Phigalia, or vice versa. The ancient sources do not provide a clear etymology; it may be related to the Greek word for 'sparing' or 'frugal' (φειδωλός), but this is speculative.

Pronunciation — /ˈfɪɡələs/ (English approximation); in Ancient Greek: Φίγαλος (Phígalos), with stress on the first syllable.

Aliases — Phigalus

Ownership — Greek mythology, specifically Arcadian regional myth. The story is local to the town of Phigalia in Arcadia, a region in the central Peloponnese.

Accounts I
Founding of PhigaliaPausanias (indirectly, as per the source material; the exact passage is not quoted but the reference is given)

Mythical era (before the Trojan War, as Lycaon is a primordial king)

Phigalus, son of Lycaon, is said to have founded the town of Phigalia, which was named after him. Alternatively, the town was named after the dryad Phigalia. During the reign of Phialus, son of Bucolion, the town was renamed Phialia, but it later reverted to Phigalia. The town of Phigalia retained its name after the temporary change to Phialia, indicating that the tradition of Phigalus as founder was strong.
