The Approach

In the rugged mountains of ancient Arcadia, a town bore the name of a hero so obscure that only a single thread of myth connects him to the land: Phigalus, son of the impious Lycaon, who may have given his name to the city of Phigalia—or perhaps it was a dryad who claimed that honor.

Identity

Phigalus

Pronunciation /ˈfɪɡələs/ (English approximation); in Ancient Greek: Φίγαλος (Phígalos), with stress on the first syllable.

Etymology

The name Phigalus (Φίγαλος) is likely derived from the Arcadian town Phigalia, or vice versa. The ancient sources do not provide a clear etymology; it may be related to the Greek word for 'sparing' or 'frugal' (φειδωλός), but this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically Arcadian regional myth. The story is local to the town of Phigalia in Arcadia, a region in the central Peloponnese.

Disputed Identifications

Some accounts attribute the founding of Phigalia to the dryad Phigalia instead of the hero Phigalus. Also, the town was renamed Phialia during the reign of Phialus, son of Bucolion, but later reverted to Phigalia. There is no clear consensus in ancient sources on which figure was the true eponym.

Description

Appearance

No physical description of Phigalus survives. As a mythological hero and son of Lycaon, he would be imagined as a mortal man, but no sources describe his appearance.

Symbols & Iconography

No known symbols or iconography associated with Phigalus. He is not depicted in ancient art, and his only connection is to the town of Phigalia.

Behaviour & Personality

No specific personality traits are recorded. As an eponymous founder, he is likely viewed as a culture hero, but his deeds are not narrated.

Domains & Habitats

He is associated with the town of Phigalia in Arcadia, a mountainous region. No other domains are known.

Version Differences

The main difference is whether the town was named after Phigalus or the dryad Phigalia. Also, the town was temporarily renamed Phialia, which suggests a competing tradition involving Phialus, son of Bucolion.

Founding of Phigalia

Source Pausanias (indirectly, as per the source material; the exact passage is not quoted but the reference is given)Culture Greek (Arcadian)Period Mythical era (before the Trojan War, as Lycaon is a primordial king)

Summary

Phigalus, son of Lycaon, is said to have founded the town of Phigalia, which was named after him. Alternatively, the town was named after the dryad Phigalia. During the reign of Phialus, son of Bucolion, the town was renamed Phialia, but it later reverted to Phigalia.

Consequences

The town of Phigalia retained its name after the temporary change to Phialia, indicating that the tradition of Phigalus as founder was strong.

Variants

The alternative tradition with the dryad Phigalia suggests a nature-spirit connection. The renaming to Phialia introduces another hero, Phialus, but it was not permanent.
