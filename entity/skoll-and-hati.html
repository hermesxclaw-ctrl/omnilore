Who It Is

Imagine looking up at the sky and knowing that the sun and moon are not moving on their own—they are running for their lives. Every dawn is a narrow escape, every sunset a desperate flight. For the Norse, the sun and moon were not just celestial bodies; they were prey, chased by two monstrous wolves who will one day catch them and swallow them whole, plunging the world into darkness. That is the story of Sköll and Hati, the wolves who hunt the heavens.

They are relentless, eternal pursuers. Sköll chases the sun (Sol) during the day, and Hati chases the moon (Máni) during the night. They are not merely following—they are hunting, driven by an insatiable hunger. Their pursuit explains the apparent motion of the celestial bodies across the sky. At Ragnarök, they will finally catch their prey, causing the sun and moon to be swallowed, plunging the world into darkness.

Powers
Eternal Pursuit

They can chase the sun and moon across the sky without tiring, maintaining their speed for all of time until Ragnarök. This is not a magical ability but a fundamental aspect of their nature.

Limit: They cannot catch their prey until Ragnarök; their failure to do so is part of the cosmic order.

Devouring the Sun and Moon

At Ragnarök, Sköll will swallow the sun, and Hati will swallow the moon, causing total darkness. This is a one-time event, not a repeatable power.

Limit: Only occurs at the end of the world; they cannot do this beforehand.

Superhuman Strength and Size

As monstrous wolves, they are large enough to be cosmic entities, capable of catching celestial bodies. Their size is implied by their role, not explicitly described.

Limit: Not specified; they are not said to fight in Ragnarök, only to devour the sun and moon.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Sköll and Hati are described as monstrous wolves of enormous size, though no specific physical details (color, eye color, fur texture) are given in the sources. They are cosmic in scale, large enough to chase the sun and moon across the sky. In later artistic depictions, they are often shown as black or grey wolves with glowing eyes, their jaws open wide, ready to devour their prey.

Symbols — They symbolize the inevitability of fate, the destructive power of nature, the cycle of day and night, and the looming threat of Ragnarök. Sköll is often associated with the sun's death, Hati with the moon's. Their howling is sometimes linked to the end of the world.

Domains — The sky, specifically the tracks of the sun and moon. They are not bound to any earthly realm but roam the heavens, their chase visible to all. They are also associated with the forest Járnviðr (Ironwood), where their mother, an unnamed giantess, raises them and other monstrous wolves.

Accounts I
The Chase of the Sun and MoonPoetic Edda (Grímnismál), Prose Edda (Gylfaginning)

Viking Age (c. 800-1100 CE), recorded in 13th century

The gods placed the sun (Sol) and moon (Máni) in chariots to ride across the sky, marking the passage of time. But two wolves, Sköll and Hati, were set loose (or were born) to chase them. Sköll pursues the sun, Hati the moon. Their eternal chase explains why the sun and moon move across the sky—they are fleeing their predators. The wolves will never catch them until Ragnarök, when they will finally devour the celestial bodies, and the world will be plunged into darkness. The daily cycle of day and night, the passage of time, and the eventual doom of the world at Ragnarök.

Accounts II
The Witch of JárnviðrPoetic Edda (Völuspá)

Viking Age, recorded in 13th century

In Völuspá, a seeress describes a giantess (often interpreted as a witch) living in Járnviðr (Ironwood), who raises the kin of Fenrir. Among these are the wolves that will steal the sun and moon. The passage is brief but establishes the wolves' lineage and their role in the coming apocalypse. Establishes the wolves as part of the monstrous brood destined to destroy the world.

Sköll and Hati are the sons of Loki. — They are the sons of Fenrir, who is himself the son of Loki. Thus, they are Loki's grandsons, not his sons.

Sköll and Hati are the same wolf. — The sources clearly name two different wolves, one for the sun and one for the moon.

They chase the sun and moon because they want to eat them. — This is true, but it is not a matter of want—it is their fate, decreed by the gods. They are agents of cosmic destiny, not merely hungry animals.

They are evil creatures. — They are not 'evil' in a moral sense; they are forces of nature, fulfilling their role in the cosmic cycle. Their actions are destructive but not malicious.

The eclipse is caused by the wolves temporarily catching the sun or moon. — This is a modern folk explanation, not attested in Norse sources. The sources say the wolves will only catch the sun and moon at Ragnarök, not during eclipses.
