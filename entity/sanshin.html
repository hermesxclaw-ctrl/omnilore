Who It Is

Sanshin (San-sin), the Mountain Spirit, is the most pervasive and enduring deity in Korean religious culture, representing the animistic core of Korean spirituality that predates and survives alongside Buddhism, Confucianism, and Christianity. Nearly every major mountain in Korea is believed to be inhabited by its own mountain spirit, typically envisioned as an elderly male figure with a long white beard, accompanied by a tiger (the sacred animal of Korean mountains) and often seated beneath a gnarled pine tree. The mountain spirit is simultaneously a guardian of the mountain's wildlife and natural features, a bestower of fertility and prosperity, and, in Buddhism, a protector of the dharma. The ubiquity of Sanshin worship is evidenced by the sansingak (mountain spirit shrine), a small building found behind virtually every traditional Korean Buddhist temple, where the mountain spirit is honored with offerings of clear water, fruit, and rice cakes. This syncretic practice—where a pre-Buddhist animistic deity is given a place of honor within Buddhist temple grounds—is unique to Korean Buddhism and demonstrates the profound integration of indigenous Korean spirituality with imported traditions. Sanshin paintings (sansintaenghwa) are highly prized folk art, depicting the spirit as a dignified elder with symbolic attributes: a walking stick of knotted wood, a gourd bottle, a crane, and always the tiger. The mountain spirit is not a single entity but a category of deity; each mountain has its own Sanshin with distinct personality and local legends. The most famous include the Sanshin of Mount Paektu (sacred to Koreans as the mythological birthplace of Dangun) and Mount Jirisan. Sanshin worship remains active in contemporary Korea, with hikers routinely leaving offerings at mountain shrines.
