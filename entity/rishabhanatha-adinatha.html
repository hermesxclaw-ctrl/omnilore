Who It Is

Rishabhanatha (also Ādinātha, 'The First Lord') is the 1st Tīrthaṅkara of Jainism — the first being in the current descending half of the time cycle (avasarpinī) to rediscover and preach the eternal Jain path. He is not a historical figure but a cosmic one: according to Jain cosmology, Rishabhanatha lived billions of years ago at the dawn of the current era, when human bodies were enormous and lifespans were measured in thousands of years. He was born to King Nābhirāj and Queen Marudevā in Ayodhya. Before his renunciation, Rishabhanatha ruled as the first king of the current era, teaching humanity the arts of civilization: agriculture, cooking, writing, law, and governance. He introduced the institution of kingship and the social order. After ruling for millions of years, he saw a dancer's necklace fall and break, understood the impermanence of all things, and renounced the world. After a year of fasting, he attained kevala (omniscience) under a banyan tree. He preached the Jain teachings and established the fourfold order. The Ādi Purāṇa by Jinasena (9th century CE) is the most famous text describing his life. He attained nirvana on Mount Kailash (or Mount Ashtapada). His emblem is the bull (vrishabha), from which his name derives.

Origins

Rishabhanatha was born in the first of the six periods of avasarpinī, when humans were huge and lifespans were enormously long. He taught humanity the essential arts of civilization. After ruling as a king, he renounced, attained omniscience, and preached the Jain faith as the first Tīrthaṅkara.
