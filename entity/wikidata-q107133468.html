Who It Is

In the shadowed corners of modern folklore, few names evoke such a visceral blend of dread and fascination as Samara—a figure who, though born from the digital age's imagination, has come to embody the timeless terror of an unstoppable, vengeful spirit. Yet, behind the iconic image of a wet-haired girl crawling from a television screen lies a complex tapestry of myth, media, and misinterpretation that demands careful unravelling.

Samara/Sadako is characterized by a relentless, vengeful nature. She kills anyone who views the cursed videotape after seven days, unless they make a copy and show it to someone else. Her motives are rooted in a tragic backstory: she was murdered and thrown into a well, and her spirit seeks revenge. She is often portrayed as emotionless and unstoppable, but some interpretations hint at a desire for connection or release.

Powers
Cursed Videotape

Samara/Sadako imprints her curse onto a videotape. Anyone who watches it receives a phone call and dies seven days later unless they copy the tape and show it to another person.

Limit: The curse can be transferred by copying the tape; if the victim makes a copy and shows it to someone else, they are spared. The curse is tied to the physical tape in the original, but later works explore other media.

Psychic Abilities

Samara/Sadako possesses powerful psychic abilities, including telekinesis, pyrokinesis, and the ability to project her consciousness into images and video. She can also create illusions and manipulate technology.

Limit: Her powers are linked to her emotional state and her connection to the well. In some versions, she is weakened by being separated from her remains or by certain rituals.

Post-Mortem Manifestation

She can manifest her physical form in the real world, often emerging from reflective surfaces, televisions, or water. She can also appear as a ghostly apparition.

Limit: Her manifestation is often tied to the curse or to places with strong emotional resonance, such as the well. She may be bound to certain locations or objects.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Samara is typically depicted as a young girl with long, dark hair covering her face, wearing a white dress (or in the Japanese version, a white nightgown). Her skin is pale, sometimes with a bluish tint, and she is often shown wet, as if emerging from water. Symbols — The iconic image of a girl with long black hair emerging from a television screen or a well has become a symbol of J-horror. The well itself is a recurring symbol of confinement and death. The videotape with its distorted imagery and the telephone call ('seven days') are also iconic.

Domains — Samara/Sadako is associated with water (wells, rain), technology (televisions, videotapes), and liminal spaces (the boundary between life and death). She can manifest anywhere, but the well is her primary dwelling place.

Accounts I
The Original Novel 'Ring'Koji Suzuki's 1991 novel

1990s

Reporter Kazuyuki Asakawa investigates a cursed videotape that kills viewers seven days after watching. He discovers the tape was created by Sadako, a woman with psychic powers who was murdered and thrown into a well. Asakawa and his ex-wife race to find her remains and break the curse. Asakawa and Takayama find Sadako's remains, but the curse is not fully broken; it evolves into a viral pandemic.

Accounts II
Japanese Film 'Ring' (1998)Hideo Nakata's film

1990s

A reporter, Reiko Asakawa, investigates the cursed tape after her niece dies. She and her ex-husband Ryuji trace the tape to Sadako, who was killed by her father and thrown into a well. They retrieve her remains, but Ryuji is killed, and the curse continues. The film ends with the curse still active, as Reiko's son becomes a carrier.

American Film 'The Ring' (2002)Gore Verbinski's film

2000s

Journalist Rachel Keller investigates the same curse. She discovers Samara, an adopted girl with psychic powers who was killed by her mother and thrown into a well. Rachel finds Samara's remains, but the curse persists, and Rachel's daughter becomes a new host. The curse is not broken; Rachel's daughter copies the tape to save herself, perpetuating the cycle.
