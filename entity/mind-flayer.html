Who It Is

The Mind Flayer, or Illithid, is D&D's quintessential psionic horror — a humanoid creature with an octopus-like head, four facial tentacles, pale mauve skin, and blank, pupil-less white eyes. Standing roughly 6 feet tall with a gaunt, almost skeletal build, the Illithid's most terrifying feature is its feeding method: it locks its tentacles around a victim's skull and, through a combination of crushing bone and psionic manipulation, extracts and consumes the brain while the victim is still conscious, savoring the memories and emotions as a delicacy. This process, called ceremorphosis, can also transform a humanoid host into a new Mind Flayer by implanting an illithid tadpole into the victim's brain — the tadpole consumes and replaces the host's identity over a horrifying week-long transformation.

Mind Flayers are not solitary monsters — they are an ancient, fallen empire. Eons ago, the Illithid Empire ruled countless worlds from their organic nautiloid spelljamming ships, enslaving entire species and breeding them like cattle. Their empire collapsed in a rebellion led by the Gith race (who split into the Githyanki and Githzerai), and today the Illithids lurk in the Underdark, rebuilding their strength through subterfuge, psionic domination, and the cultivation of thrall populations. An elder brain — a massive, sentient pool of brine containing the merged consciousnesses of deceased Illithids — rules each colony, coordinating the hive through telepathic control.

Illithids possess formidable psionic powers: mind blast (a conical psychic assault), dominate monster, plane shift, levitate, and the ability to extract thoughts from a victim's mind. Their society is rigidly hierarchical, utterly alien in morality, and devoted to the goal of extinguishing all suns in the multiverse so they may once again rule in endless darkness. The Mind Flayer has been featured in countless D&D products including the classic adventure module 'Expedition to the Barrier Peaks', R.A. Salvatore's novels, the Stranger Things series (where the Mind Flayer serves as a recurring antagonist), and most prominently in Baldur's Gate 3 (2023), where the player character itself begins the game infected with an illithid tadpole.
