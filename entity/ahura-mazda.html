Who It Is

Ahura Mazda (meaning 'The Wise Lord' or 'Lord of Wisdom') is the supreme deity, the uncreated creator, and the source of all goodness in Zoroastrianism—one of the world's oldest continuously practiced religions. Revealed through the prophet Zoroaster (Zarathustra) in approximately the second millennium BCE on the Central Asian steppes, Ahura Mazda represents the most developed form of monotheism in the pre-Abrahamic world, with profound influence on Judaism, Christianity, and Islam during and after the Babylonian exile and through the Persian empires. Ahura Mazda is the personification of asha (truth, order, righteousness)—the cosmic principle of harmony and justice that opposes druj (falsehood, chaos, evil). He is not a nature deity but a transcendent, ethical, and spiritual being: the source of light, life, wisdom, and goodness. Zoroaster's revolutionary teaching was that Ahura Mazda is the sole uncreated being; all other divine entities, the Amesha Spentas (Holy Immortals), are his emanations or creations—aspects of his divine nature rather than independent gods. The Achaemenid kings, particularly Darius I and Xerxes, prominently invoked Ahura Mazda in their royal inscriptions at Persepolis and Behistun, depicting the deity as the source of their legitimacy and the guarantor of their rule. The famous Faravahar, the winged disc symbol often associated with Zoroastrianism, likely represents the khvarenah (divine glory) bestowed by Ahura Mazda or possibly Ahura Mazda himself in symbolic form. Ahura Mazda's cosmic struggle against Angra Mainyu (the Destructive Spirit) is not a dualism of equal powers—in orthodox Zoroastrian theology, Ahura Mazda is ultimately supreme, and evil will be definitively vanquished at the end of time in a great renovation (Frashokereti). This eschatological framework—a linear, progressive history ending in the triumph of good—was a radical departure from the cyclical cosmologies of other ancient religions and profoundly shaped Western religious thought.
