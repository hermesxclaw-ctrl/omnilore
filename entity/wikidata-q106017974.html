The Approach

In the hush of a Welsh night, a sound like a whispered command—'dewch, dewch'—might drift from the darkness. To those who know the old tales, it is not a summons to follow, but a herald of the end: the Aderyn y Corff, the corpse bird, perched invisibly at the threshold of the dying.

Content advisory. Discusses death omens and traditional beliefs about mortality; may be unsettling to some readers.
Identity

Aderyn y Corff

Pronunciation ah-DER-in uh KORFF (approximate; Welsh phonetics: /aˈdɛrɨn ə kɔrɸ/)

Known As

Aderyn corffDeryn corffAderyn y Corph (archaic)

Etymology

Welsh: aderyn = bird, corff = corpse/body; thus 'corpse bird' or 'bird of the corpse'.

Cultural Ownership

Welsh folklore; specifically associated with rural Wales and traditional death omens.

Disputed Identifications

Some modern commentators suggest the bird is a screech owl (Tyto alba or similar) misinterpreted in folklore, but this is not universally accepted and lacks direct historical evidence.

Description

Appearance

Described as a bird with no feathers and no wings, giving it an unnatural, perhaps skeletal appearance. It is not depicted in traditional iconography; visual representations are modern and speculative.

Symbols & Iconography

No historical symbols or iconography are associated with the Aderyn y Corff. It is primarily an auditory omen.

Behaviour & Personality

The bird is said to chirp outside the door of a person who is about to die. Its cry is interpreted as the Welsh word 'dewch' (come), which is perceived as a summons to death. It is not described as aggressive or malicious, but as a passive harbinger. It is said to reside on another plane of existence when not delivering its omen.

Domains & Habitats

It is said to live on another plane of existence, but it appears in the mortal world near the homes of the dying, typically at night.

Version Differences

Variations in spelling (corff vs corph) reflect orthographic changes. Some accounts may describe it as a screech owl, but this is a modern rationalization rather than a traditional variant.

Stories & Myths

The Corpse Bird's Call

Source Welsh folklore (oral tradition)Culture WelshPeriod Unknown; likely pre-modern, but recorded in 19th-20th century collections

Summary

A person, often a family member or neighbor, hears a bird chirping outside the door of a sick or elderly person. The cry sounds like 'dewch' (come). Within a short time, that person dies. The bird is never seen, only heard.

Participants

The dying person

The hearer (often a relative or caregiver)

The Aderyn y Corff

Consequences

The death of the person; the hearer is left with the chilling knowledge that the omen was fulfilled.

Variants

Some accounts may specify that the bird is heard at night, or that it appears only to certain individuals. No regional variants are documented in the available sources.

Contradictions & Disputes

Identification as screech owl

Position A

Position A — evidence

Position B

It is actually a screech owl, attracted to lights, and its call is misinterpreted.
