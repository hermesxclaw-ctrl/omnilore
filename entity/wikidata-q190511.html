Apophis is the lord of the Duat (the underworld), specifically the chaotic waters of Nun and the dark, dangerous regions of the afterlife. He is the master of darkness, storms, earthquakes, and all forms of destructive chaos. He is associated with the night, with the hours when the sun is hidden, and with the primordial abyss that existed before creation. He is not a god of a specific city or cult, but a universal enemy of the world order.

Form

Apophis is consistently depicted as a colossal serpent, often described as being 450 cubits long (approximately 230 meters or 750 feet). He is usually shown as a giant snake with a thick, coiled body, sometimes with multiple coils. In some depictions, he has a serpent's head, but in others, he may have a human head or the head of a different creature. He is often shown being pierced with spears, bound with ropes, or hacked with knives by the gods, particularly by Set, Horus, and the pharaoh. His color is not always specified, but he is sometimes depicted as a dark or reddish serpent, representing the darkness and chaos he embodies. He is often shown in the underworld, in the waters of Nun, or on a sandbank in the Duat.

Powers

Immense Size and Physical Strength Apophis is described as a colossal serpent, 450 cubits long. His physical strength is enough to threaten the solar barque and to cause earthquakes and storms. Attested in the Book of the Dead and Amduat. Despite his size, he is consistently defeated or repelled by the gods and the pharaoh, suggesting his power is not absolute. None.

Control over Darkness and Chaos Apophis is the embodiment of 'Isfet' (chaos) and the forces of darkness. He can create darkness, storms, and eclipses by attacking the sun. Attested in the Book of the Dead and various hymns. His power is limited to the night and the underworld; he cannot act in the light of day. None.

Hypnotic Gaze and Roar Apophis is described as having a 'roar' that can cause chaos and a 'gaze' that can petrify or overwhelm his enemies. Attested in the Book of Overthrowing Apep. These powers can be countered by the magic of the gods and by reciting specific spells. None.

Serpentine Venom As a serpent, Apophis is presumably venomous, though this is not explicitly described. His bite is likely fatal. Inferred from his serpentine nature. Not directly attested. None.

Ability to Swallow the Sun In some texts, Apophis is said to swallow the sun, causing a solar eclipse. This is a temporary state, as the sun is always eventually freed or passes through him. Attested in the Book of the Dead and later texts. The sun is always freed, either by the gods or by Ra's own power. Some texts describe the sun passing through the serpent's body, others describe it being repelled.

Resilience and Regeneration Apophis is extremely difficult to destroy. He is often shown being hacked into pieces, but he is able to regenerate or be resurrected, which is why the battle against him is a nightly occurrence. Attested in the Book of Overthrowing Apep and the Amduat. His regeneration is not absolute; he can be temporarily defeated and bound. None.

Behavior & Habitat

Apophis's behavior is singular: he seeks to disrupt the cosmic order (Ma'at) by attacking the solar barque of Ra during its nightly journey through the underworld. He is described as a roaring, hissing, chaotic force that creates storms, darkness, and earthquakes. He is not a cunning or intelligent villain in the modern sense, but a primordial force of destruction that acts on instinct. He is the embodiment of 'Isfet' (chaos, injustice, and disorder), the opposite of Ma'at. He is said to swallow the waters of the Nile (causing drought) and to swallow the sun (causing eclipses). He is persistently hostile to the gods and to the deceased who travel with Ra.

Accounts

Each night, the sun god Ra travels through the underworld (Duat) in his solar barque. As he enters the darkest hours, the great serpent Apophis, who lies in the waters of Nun, attacks the barque. The gods who accompany Ra, particularly Set, Horus, and the goddess Serqet, defend the barque with spears, nets, and magic. The pharaoh, as the earthly representative of Ra, also participates in the battle. The battle is a cosmic struggle between order (Ma'at) and chaos (Isfet). If Apophis were to win, the world would be plunged into darkness and chaos. The gods and the deceased who have been judged worthy (the 'justified dead') help to repel the serpent. The battle is depicted in detail in the Amduat, where Apophis is bound, hacked, and speared, but never permanently destroyed.

According to the Book of Overthrowing Apep, Apophis was born from the saliva of the goddess Neith, who spat into the primordial waters of Nun during the act of creation. This makes him a primordial being, created from the chaos that existed before the world. He is not a created god but a byproduct of the creative process, an inherent flaw or chaotic element in the universe. This origin story explains why he is so difficult to destroy: he is part of the very fabric of pre-creation.

To ensure the sun's victory over Apophis, the Egyptians performed a daily ritual. Priests would create a wax figure of a serpent, sometimes with a human head, and then destroy it. The figure was burned, mutilated, spat upon, and trampled. The ritual was accompanied by the recitation of spells that described the destruction of Apophis and the victory of Ra. The ritual was performed in the temple of Ra, and it was believed that by destroying the wax figure, the priests were helping to destroy the real Apophis in the underworld. The ritual was so important that it was performed multiple times a day, at dawn, noon, and dusk.

In some depictions, Apophis is defeated by the 'Great Cat' (Mau), an aspect of the sun god Ra. The cat is shown cutting off the head of the serpent with a knife beneath a sacred tree (often a persea or ished tree). This is a more direct and violent depiction of the defeat of Apophis, and it may have been a popular story among the common people. The cat is a symbol of the sun god, and its ability to kill snakes makes it a natural enemy of Apophis.

Scholarly Debates
Set's Role
Set is a heroic defender of Ra, using his spear to repel Apophis. ⇌ Set is a chaotic deity who is sometimes conflated with Apophis or seen as a disruptive force.
Evidence for A: New Kingdom texts (Amduat, Book of Gates) depict Set as a defender.
Evidence for B: Late Period texts (e.g., some Ptolemaic texts) demonize Set and associate him with Apophis.
Origin of Apophis
Apophis was born from the saliva of Neith. ⇌ Apophis existed from the beginning of time as a primordial force of chaos.
Evidence for A: Bremner-Rhind Papyrus (Late Period).
Evidence for B: Implied in earlier texts where Apophis is present in the Nun from the start.
Mode of Defeat
Apophis is bound and hacked into pieces by the gods. ⇌ Apophis is swallowed or passes through the sun god's body.
Evidence for A: Amduat, Book of Gates, temple reliefs.
Evidence for B: Some texts describe the sun passing through Apophis's body, or Apophis being swallowed by the sun.
What We Don't Know

1. The exact etymology of the name 'Apophis' is uncertain. 2. The origin of Apophis is not fully clear; the Neith story is late, and earlier origins are not specified. 3. The relationship between Apophis and the constellation Hydra is not fully understood. 4. The full extent of Apophis's role in the 'Book of the Faiyum' and other late texts requires further study. 5. The reception of Apophis in modern popular culture is a topic that could be explored in more depth, particularly in anime, manga, and video games.

A way to hold it in your mind

Apophis is the ancient Egyptian equivalent of a final boss in a video game, but one that respawns every night. He's not a cunning villain with a master plan; he's the embodiment of a game glitch—a bug in the cosmic code that threatens to crash the whole system. The gods are the players, and the sun is the save file. Every night, they have to defeat the boss again to keep the game running.
