The Approach

In the forgotten archives of the Forgotten Realms, where knowledge is both treasure and weapon, there exists a deity whose portfolio is the written word itself. Deneir, the Lord of All Glyphs and Images, is the patron of scribes, artists, and those who record the world's truths. But his path is not one of passive preservation—it is a divine mandate to ensure that what is written endures, and what is hidden is brought to light.

Appearance

Not described in the available source. In D&D canon, Deneir is often depicted as a scholarly human male, robed, carrying a scroll and a quill.

Symbols & Iconography

The symbol of Deneir is a lit candle above an open book, or a single star above a crescent moon, depending on the source.

Behaviour & Personality

Deneir is a neutral good deity who values knowledge, truth, and the preservation of written records. He is calm, methodical, and protective of scribes and artists.

Domains & Habitats

Deneir resides in the House of Knowledge on the plane of Mount Celestia, according to standard D&D cosmology.

No version differences noted in the source; however, across D&D editions, his portfolio and domains have been consistent, though his role in the pantheon has been more or less prominent.

Stories & Myths

The Creation of the Written Word

Source Forgotten Realms mythology (not in the provided source, but part of the setting's canon)Culture Forgotten RealmsPeriod Prehistoric (in-world)

Summary

Deneir is said to have taught mortals the art of writing, giving them the means to record history, laws, and stories. This act is considered a gift to civilization.

Participants

Deneir

Mortals

Consequences

The establishment of written language and the preservation of knowledge.

Variants

Some sources attribute this to Oghma, the god of knowledge, but Deneir is specifically the patron of the written form.
