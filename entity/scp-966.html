Who It Is

SCP-966 designates a species of nocturnal predatory creatures that are invisible to the naked eye. They can only be seen through cameras or other imaging devices, or by individuals suffering from extreme sleep deprivation. The entities are approximately 1.5 meters tall, vaguely humanoid but emaciated, with long, spindly limbs ending in sharp claws. Their skin is hairless and pale, and their faces are featureless except for a wide mouth filled with needle-like teeth. SCP-966 instances are completely silent and invisible to any human who has had adequate sleep. However, their primary hunting strategy involves depriving their prey of sleep. They emit an anomalous frequency that disrupts the brain's ability to enter REM sleep, causing victims to experience progressively worsening insomnia. As the victim's sleep deprivation increases — typically after 48-72 hours — they begin to see the creatures. First as shadows in peripheral vision, then as increasingly clear and present figures. By the time the victim can fully see an SCP-966 instance, they are too weakened from sleep deprivation to effectively fight or flee. The creatures then attack, consuming the victim. The Foundation has tracked SCP-966 activity globally, with a particular concentration in urban areas. They are believed to nest in abandoned buildings, sewers, and other dark, quiet spaces. The entities are vulnerable to physical damage when they can be seen, but targeting them requires the attacker to be severely sleep-deprived, which presents obvious tactical challenges. Foundation containment involves tracking reports of mass insomnia and deploying task forces equipped with infrared cameras. SCP-966 is a uniquely terrifying predator because it attacks not the body first but the mind — it slowly, methodically destroys the victim's ability to rest, turning the universal human need for sleep into a vulnerability that is exploited over days before the creature ever makes itself visible.
