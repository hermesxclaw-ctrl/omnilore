Who It Is

Among the fifty daughters of Thespius, Terpsicrate is a name that survives only in the margins of Heracles' myth—yet her story, like that of her sisters, is woven into one of the most controversial episodes of Greek heroic legend: the mass begetting of the Thespiades, the fifty sons who would carry the hero's blood across Boeotia. Her son, Euryopes, is one of the few offspring recorded, but her own fate, whether as a willing bride or an unwitting victim, remains a shadowy footnote in the ancient sources.

No specific personality traits are recorded. In the myth, she is one of the daughters who sleeps with Heracles, though the circumstances vary. In one version, she is a willing participant; in another, she is deceived.

Mythical (Heroic Age)

Heracles, while hunting the Cithaeronian lion, was hosted by King Thespius for fifty days. Each night, Thespius sent one of his daughters to Heracles, and in his drunken state, Heracles believed he was with the same woman each time. All fifty daughters bore sons, including Terpsicrate, who gave birth to Euryopes. In some versions, the encounters happened over seven nights, with seven daughters each night. In another account, all but one daughter slept with Heracles in a single night; the one who refused was punished with perpetual virginity and service as his priestess. The fifty sons, known as the Thespiades, became the founders of Thespiae and spread Heracles' lineage throughout Boeotia. Terpsicrate's son Euryopes is one of the few named offspring.

Terpsicrate is a muse or goddess. — She is a mortal princess, one of the fifty daughters of Thespius, and is not associated with the Muses.

Heracles raped the daughters. — The ancient sources describe the encounters as orchestrated by Thespius, with Heracles unaware he was with different women. They do not explicitly describe force or violence; the issue is deception, not force.

All fifty sons were named. — Only a few are named in the surviving sources, including Euryopes, the son of Terpsicrate.
