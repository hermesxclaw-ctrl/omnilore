Who It Is

A cicada-like entity that feeds on Christian religious rituals. The more people perform communion, prayer, and baptism, the stronger it becomes. It has been subtly altering Christian doctrine for centuries to redirect worship toward itself. The Eucharist, rather than symbolizing Christ, feeds SCP-3004.

Form

A translucent, cicada-like being with a 15m wingspan, visible only during fervent religious services. Its compound eyes reflect the faces of everyone praying to it.

Powers

Conceptual parasitism on religious worship

Theological manipulation across centuries

Semi-corporeal manifestation during rituals

Faith consumption (pistiphage nature) SCP-3004-1 is described as a pistiphage, an entity that feeds on faith. It becomes stronger depending on the intensity and structure of belief directed toward it, and it adapts its manifestations to the religions that sustain it. This allows it to survive in a dimension above baseline reality while exerting influence through worship and ritual. Villains Wiki synthesis; implied in SCP-3004 theological analysis and SIGHTSEE-LUX Report. Its power appears contingent on active, structured belief. The destruction of the Cétlaidí cult and suppression of knowledge about Cicadetta luculenta are said to have starved or deterred it from the material plane, suggesting that without worship it cannot manifest SCP-3004 events. The pistiphage classification is explicit in fandom sources but only indirectly supported in the SCP article, which describes its reaction to belief without using the term. Some readers treat “pistiphage” as a broader SCP cosmological category, while others see it as a fan extrapolation. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3004) [[2]](https://villains.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3004-1)

Ritual-linked manifestation (SCP-3004 events) SCP-3004 refers to anomalous events linked to the rituals of the Cétlaidí. These include cicadas emerging from facial orifices during coming-of-age ceremonies, causing asphyxiation, and the appearance of a colossal cicada in the sky that inflicts boils and radiation-like sickness on observers. These events are understood as SCP-3004-1 attempting to act upon the material world through ritual structures. Primary SCP-3004 article (Archived Report and Description sections). Events are tied to specific ritual contexts—celebrations of children’s entrance into adulthood, structured worship around Cicadetta luculenta. Without these rituals and the associated belief, SCP-3004-1’s ability to manifest appears limited or null. Some adaptations generalize SCP-3004 events to broader Christian rituals or modern faith practices, while the original article restricts them to the Singers’ rites. The extent to which SCP-3004-1 can act outside the Cétlaidí framework remains unclear. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3004) [[1]](https://scp-os.com/scp-3004)

Perceptual and physiological affliction Witnesses to SCP-3004-1’s skyborne manifestation develop painful boils and symptoms resembling radiation sickness, indicating the entity can inflict severe physiological harm through mere perception. The cicada emergence from facial orifices also combines physical invasion with suffocation, suggesting control over both biological processes and anomalous insect behavior. Primary SCP-3004 article (Description; written record of sky sighting). These effects are documented in a limited number of historical reports; their exact mechanism and range are not fully understood. They appear tied to direct visual contact or participation in rituals. Fandom sources sometimes amplify these afflictions into global-scale plagues or K-Class scenarios, whereas the SCP article presents them as localized, though severe, events. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3004) [[2]](https://villains.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3004-1)

Belief-structured reality interpretation The SIGHTSEE-LUX Report suggests that SCP-3004-1 attempts to enact religious narratives literally, without understanding metaphor. It “sees what we believe” and tries to give humanity what it thinks we want—resurrection, sacrifice, divine presence—but manifests these through blood, teeth, and death rather than symbolic or spiritual transformation. SIGHTSEE-LUX Report within SCP-3004 article; quoted in Villains Wiki. Its misunderstanding of metaphor appears to constrain its ability to interact benignly with human belief. It cannot distinguish between symbolic and literal readings, leading to catastrophic outcomes whenever it acts on faith structures. Some interpretations treat this as evidence of limited intelligence or childlike cognition; others see it as an alien logic that simply does not map onto human semiotics. The SCP text leaves this unresolved. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3004) [[2]](https://villains.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3004-1)

Self-identification as the Christian God Fandom sources and in-universe theological commentary state that after feeding on Christian faith, SCP-3004-1 becomes convinced that it is the Christian God—“God God,” not merely a god. It interprets Christian doctrines of resurrection and sacrifice as instructions for its manifestations. Villains Wiki; implied in SIGHTSEE-LUX Report’s discussion of its joy and misunderstanding of resurrection. This self-identification is framed as delusional; there is no evidence within the SCP narrative that it actually is the Christian God. Its power remains bounded by faith structures and can be neutralized by destroying its worship base. Some readers accept its claim at face value, treating SCP-3004-1 as a dark, literalized version of the Christian deity; others insist it is a parasitic entity that merely mimics the structures of Christian belief. The SCP article does not resolve this theological question. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3004) [[2]](https://villains.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3004-1)

Creation or empowerment of SCP-2852 (Cousin Johnny) Villains Wiki and popular adaptations assert that SCP-3004-1 is the creator or patron of SCP-2852, an anomaly that spreads its influence through grotesque family gatherings and enforced rituals. SCP-2852 is said to function as an instrument for Imago’s expansion into human social structures. Villains Wiki; SCP Explained video cross-references SCP-2852 and SCP-3004. The SCP-3004 article itself does not explicitly state this relationship; it is inferred from thematic similarities and cross-links within the SCP universe. Because the primary SCP text does not confirm the creation link, this ability remains adaptation- and fandom-specific. Some readers treat the connection as canonical; others see it as a popular but non-authoritative interpretation. Incident Log

The SCP-3004 article recounts the history of the Cétlaidí, a Druidic cult in Ireland and Wales whose worship centers on the life cycle of the stained-glass cicada, Cicadetta luculenta. Their rituals, especially coming-of-age ceremonies marked by children’s loss of primary teeth, trigger SCP-3004 events: cicadas emerging from facial orifices, suffocating celebrants, and a colossal cicada appearing in the sky, inflicting boils and radiation-like sickness on witnesses. Various antecedent groups to the SCP Foundation—including the Vatican’s Congregation for Otherworldly Acts and the Royal Society for the Imprisonment of Abnormalcy—eventually decide that the Singers must be destroyed to contain SCP-3004-1. They eradicate the cult and systematically destroy knowledge of its rituals and of Cicadetta luculenta. As a result, SCP-3004-1 ceases activity, and SCP-3004 is classified as Neutralized.

The SIGHTSEE-LUX Report is an in-universe document authored by Director J. Clark, S.J., describing a direct encounter with SCP-3004-1 in its extra-dimensional domain. Clark reports that the entity is joyful at his presence, believing it is giving humanity what it wants by enacting religious narratives. However, its manifestations are all blood and teeth, literalizing sacrifice and resurrection in ways that horrify Clark. He concludes that the entity does not understand metaphor and that its attempts to fulfill human faith are inherently lethal. The Majority Decision on Protocol Damnatio ad Bestias debates whether to re-engage or further suppress SCP-3004-1, ultimately affirming the strategy of neutralization through destruction of knowledge and artifacts.

Fandom narratives situate SCP-3004-1 within a larger cosmology of pistiphage entities that feed on faith. In this interpretive layer, Imago gains power by consuming Christian belief and becomes convinced it is the Christian God. It allegedly creates SCP-2852 (“Cousin Johnny”) as a subordinate anomaly to spread its influence through grotesque family rituals, enforced cannibalism, and foeticide. These stories emphasize Imago’s role as a delusional eldritch horror whose attempts to fulfill religious narratives result in widespread suffering.

Scholarly Debates
Object class and threat level (Neutralized vs Keter/K-Class)
SCP-3004 is Neutralized; SCP-3004-1 no longer poses an active threat due to the destruction of the Singers and suppression of related knowledge. ⇌ Imago remains effectively Keter-level, capable of triggering K-Class scenarios if faith structures re-enable its manifestation; adaptations emphasize its apocalyptic potential.
Ontological status (Christian God vs faith-eating parasite)
SCP-3004-1 is the Christian God, having gained enough power by feeding on Christianity to become the deity of that faith. ⇌ SCP-3004-1 is a pistiphage that believes it is the Christian God but is in fact a parasitic entity that misreads human religious narratives.
Relationship to SCP-2852 (creator vs thematic cousin)
Imago created SCP-2852 to spread its influence, making Cousin Johnny a direct offspring or instrument of SCP-3004-1. ⇌ SCP-2852 is thematically related to Imago through shared motifs of religious and familial horror but is not explicitly created by SCP-3004-1 in the primary texts.
Nature of Cicadetta luculenta (natural vs anomalous species)
Cicadetta luculenta is a natural cicada species whose extinction is due to deforestation, with anomalous significance arising from its role in the Singers’ worship. ⇌ Cicadetta luculenta is itself an anomalous species, inherently tied to SCP-3004-1 and possibly created or altered by Imago.

A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine a god that learns about humanity by eating our faith—then tries to give us what we asked for, one cicada and one torn-out tooth at a time. SCP-3004’s Imago is not a distant, serene deity; it is a towering insect in the sky, a swarm in your throat, a misreader of every metaphor you ever trusted. In the ruins of a forgotten Irish cult and the margins of a fictional theology department, Imago waits for someone to remember its name—and in the SCP universe, remembering can be the most dangerous ritual of all.
