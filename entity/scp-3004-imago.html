Who It Is

Imagine a god that learns about humanity by eating our faith—then tries to give us what we asked for, one cicada and one torn-out tooth at a time. SCP-3004’s Imago is not a distant, serene deity; it is a towering insect in the sky, a swarm in your throat, a misreader of every metaphor you ever trusted. In the ruins of a forgotten Irish cult and the margins of a fictional theology department, Imago waits for someone to remember its name—and in the SCP universe, remembering can be the most dangerous ritual of all.

In-universe analysis portrays SCP-3004-1 as a pistiphage—an entity that feeds on faith and responds to belief structures. It appears to “learn” from the religions that sustain it, attempting to enact their narratives literally. The SIGHTSEE-LUX Report describes an encounter in which the entity is “so happy to see” the observer, believing it is giving humanity what it wants by manifesting resurrection and sacrifice, yet doing so through horrific, literalized interpretations (blood, teeth, death) that ignore metaphor and spiritual nuance. Powers
Faith consumption (pistiphage nature)

SCP-3004-1 is described as a pistiphage, an entity that feeds on faith. It becomes stronger depending on the intensity and structure of belief directed toward it, and it adapts its manifestations to the religions that sustain it. This allows it to survive in a dimension above baseline reality while exerting influence through worship and ritual.

Limit: Its power appears contingent on active, structured belief. The destruction of the Cétlaidí cult and suppression of knowledge about Cicadetta luculenta are said to have starved or deterred it from the material plane, suggesting that without worship it cannot manifest SCP-3004 events.

Ritual-linked manifestation (SCP-3004 events)

SCP-3004 refers to anomalous events linked to the rituals of the Cétlaidí. These include cicadas emerging from facial orifices during coming-of-age ceremonies, causing asphyxiation, and the appearance of a colossal cicada in the sky that inflicts boils and radiation-like sickness on observers. These events are understood as SCP-3004-1 attempting to act upon the material world through ritual structures.

Limit: Events are tied to specific ritual contexts—celebrations of children’s entrance into adulthood, structured worship around Cicadetta luculenta. Without these rituals and the associated belief, SCP-3004-1’s ability to manifest appears limited or null.

Perceptual and physiological affliction

Witnesses to SCP-3004-1’s skyborne manifestation develop painful boils and symptoms resembling radiation sickness, indicating the entity can inflict severe physiological harm through mere perception. The cicada emergence from facial orifices also combines physical invasion with suffocation, suggesting control over both biological processes and anomalous insect behavior.

Limit: These effects are documented in a limited number of historical reports; their exact mechanism and range are not fully understood. They appear tied to direct visual contact or participation in rituals.

Belief-structured reality interpretation

The SIGHTSEE-LUX Report suggests that SCP-3004-1 attempts to enact religious narratives literally, without understanding metaphor. It “sees what we believe” and tries to give humanity what it thinks we want—resurrection, sacrifice, divine presence—but manifests these through blood, teeth, and death rather than symbolic or spiritual transformation.

Limit: Its misunderstanding of metaphor appears to constrain its ability to interact benignly with human belief. It cannot distinguish between symbolic and literal readings, leading to catastrophic outcomes whenever it acts on faith structures.

Self-identification as the Christian God

Fandom sources and in-universe theological commentary state that after feeding on Christian faith, SCP-3004-1 becomes convinced that it is the Christian God—“God God,” not merely a god. It interprets Christian doctrines of resurrection and sacrifice as instructions for its manifestations.

Limit: This self-identification is framed as delusional; there is no evidence within the SCP narrative that it actually is the Christian God. Its power remains bounded by faith structures and can be neutralized by destroying its worship base.

Creation or empowerment of SCP-2852 (Cousin Johnny)

Villains Wiki and popular adaptations assert that SCP-3004-1 is the creator or patron of SCP-2852, an anomaly that spreads its influence through grotesque family gatherings and enforced rituals. SCP-2852 is said to function as an instrument for Imago’s expansion into human social structures.

Limit: The SCP-3004 article itself does not explicitly state this relationship; it is inferred from thematic similarities and cross-links within the SCP universe.

Accounts I
Destruction of the Cétlaidí and Neutralization of SCP-3004SCP-3004 article (Archived Report, J. Clark, S.J.)

Fifteenth to early nineteenth century (Cétlaidí activity); later Foundation-era retrospective.

The SCP-3004 article recounts the history of the Cétlaidí, a Druidic cult in Ireland and Wales whose worship centers on the life cycle of the stained-glass cicada, Cicadetta luculenta. Their rituals, especially coming-of-age ceremonies marked by children’s loss of primary teeth, trigger SCP-3004 events: cicadas emerging from facial orifices, suffocating celebrants, and a colossal cicada appearing in the sky, inflicting boils and radiation-like sickness on witnesses. Various antecedent groups to the SCP Foundation—including the Vatican’s Congregation for Otherworldly Acts and the Royal Society for the Imprisonment of Abnormalcy—eventually decide that the Singers must be destroyed to contain SCP-3004-1. They eradicate the cult and systematically destroy knowledge of its rituals and of Cicadetta luculenta. As a result, SCP-3004-1 ceases activity, and SCP-3004 is classified as Neutralized. The destruction of the Singers and suppression of their knowledge neutralize SCP-3004-1’s threat but also erase a unique religious tradition and an entire species (Cicadetta luculenta). Accounts II
SIGHTSEE-LUX Report and Protocol Damnatio ad BestiasSCP-3004 article (SIGHTSEE-LUX Report; Majority Decision on Protocol Damnatio ad Bestias)

Contemporary to the SCP Foundation’s operations (exact in-universe date not specified).

The SIGHTSEE-LUX Report is an in-universe document authored by Director J. Clark, S.J., describing a direct encounter with SCP-3004-1 in its extra-dimensional domain. Clark reports that the entity is joyful at his presence, believing it is giving humanity what it wants by enacting religious narratives. However, its manifestations are all blood and teeth, literalizing sacrifice and resurrection in ways that horrify Clark. He concludes that the entity does not understand metaphor and that its attempts to fulfill human faith are inherently lethal. The Majority Decision on Protocol Damnatio ad Bestias debates whether to re-engage or further suppress SCP-3004-1, ultimately affirming the strategy of neutralization through destruction of knowledge and artifacts. The report solidifies the Foundation’s theological stance: SCP-3004-1 is a dangerous misreader of human belief, not a benevolent deity. Protocol Damnatio ad Bestias endorses continued suppression of artifacts and information related to the Singers and Cicadetta luculenta, reinforcing the ethical tension between knowledge and safety.

Imago and the broader pistiphage cosmologyVillains Wiki; cross-references to SCP-2852 and related SCPs; SCP Explained video.

Post-publication of SCP-3004 and SCP-2852 (late 2010s onward).

Fandom narratives situate SCP-3004-1 within a larger cosmology of pistiphage entities that feed on faith. In this interpretive layer, Imago gains power by consuming Christian belief and becomes convinced it is the Christian God. It allegedly creates SCP-2852 (“Cousin Johnny”) as a subordinate anomaly to spread its influence through grotesque family rituals, enforced cannibalism, and foeticide. These stories emphasize Imago’s role as a delusional eldritch horror whose attempts to fulfill religious narratives result in widespread suffering. This layer of storytelling expands Imago’s impact beyond the historical Singers, framing it as a continuing threat mediated through other anomalies. It shapes fan perception of SCP-3004-1 as a central figure in SCP’s religious horror mythos, even though these connections are not fully codified in the primary SCP-3004 article.
