The Approach

In the blood-soaked forests of feudal Japan, few demons command as much fear and respect as Sesshomaru, the silver-haired lord of the western lands. Born of a great dog demon and raised to inherit his father's legacy, he begins as a cold-hearted killer who despises humans and half-demons alike. Yet his journey is one of profound transformation—from a ruthless antagonist to a reluctant protector, and finally to a father who defies his own nature to save his daughters. His story is not just a tale of power, but of the slow thawing of a frozen heart.

Content advisory. Contains themes of violence, death, and demonic conflict. Some scenes depict emotional abuse and neglect, particularly in Sesshomaru's early interactions with his brother and his later treatment of his daughters. Parental abandonment is a recurring theme in the sequel series.
Identity

Sesshomaru

Etymology

The name Sesshomaru (殺生丸) combines the kanji for 'kill' (殺), 'life' (生), and '丸' (maru), a common suffix in male names, often meaning 'circle' or 'perfection'. The name can be interpreted as 'the one who takes life' or 'killer of the living', reflecting his fearsome nature.

Cultural Ownership

Sesshomaru is a fictional character created by Rumiko Takahashi, appearing in the manga and anime series Inuyasha. He is part of Japanese popular culture and has gained international recognition. He is not a figure from traditional folklore, but draws on elements of Japanese demon lore (yōkai).

Disputed Identifications

Some fans have mistakenly linked Sesshomaru to the mythological creature 'Inugami' or to the historical figure 'Sesshō' (a title for regents), but these are not canonical. There is no official identification with any specific yōkai from Japanese mythology.

Description

Appearance

Sesshomaru has a tall, slender build with long silver hair, golden eyes with slit pupils, and red markings on his cheeks. He wears a white kimono with red and blue accents, and a fluffy white fur boa around his neck. He often carries a sword at his hip. In his true form, he is a giant dog demon with enormous size and power.

Symbols & Iconography

His iconic symbols include his crescent moon mark on his forehead (which he loses later), his two swords: Tenseiga and Bakusaiga, and his fur boa. His color scheme of white and red is associated with his noble and demonic nature.

Behaviour & Personality

Initially, Sesshomaru is cold, arrogant, and ruthless, showing disdain for humans and half-demons. He is obsessed with power and seeks to obtain his father's sword, Tessaiga. Over time, he becomes more complex, showing a stoic but protective side, especially towards Rin and his daughters. He is not overtly emotional, but his actions reveal a deep sense of honor and care for those he considers his own.

Domains & Habitats

Sesshomaru's domain is the western lands of feudal Japan, though he travels extensively. He is often seen in forests, mountains, and other wilderness areas. He has a castle that appears later in the series, but he rarely stays there.

Version Differences

In the manga, Sesshomaru's development is more gradual and nuanced. The anime adds some filler episodes that expand on his character. In Yashahime, he is portrayed as a more distant and enigmatic figure, with his actions often misinterpreted by others. The English dub may have slight differences in dialogue and tone.

Stories & Myths

The Battle for Tessaiga

Source Inuyasha manga, chapters 5-7, and anime episodes 5-7Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod Feudal era (Sengoku period) and modern era (flashbacks)

Summary

Sesshomaru attacks Inuyasha to take Tessaiga, the sword their father left to Inuyasha. He believes he deserves it, but Inuyasha refuses. They battle, and Sesshomaru is initially overpowering, but Inuyasha's transformation into a full demon and the intervention of others force Sesshomaru to retreat.

The Resurrection of Rin

Source Inuyasha manga, chapters 88-90, and anime episodes 44-46Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod Feudal era

Summary

Rin, a human girl, is killed by wolves. Sesshomaru, who had previously shown no concern for humans, uses Tenseiga to resurrect her. This is a turning point for his character, as he begins to care for Rin and allows her to travel with him.

The Birth of Towa and Setsuna

Source Yashahime: Princess Half-Demon, episode 1 and later episodesCulture Japanese animePeriod Feudal era (main timeline) and modern era (flashbacks)

Summary

Sesshomaru and Rin have twin daughters, Towa and Setsuna. Sesshomaru, due to a prophecy, must separate the twins and place them in different worlds. He leaves Towa in the modern era and Setsuna in the feudal era, entrusting them to others. His actions are misinterpreted as abandonment by many, but they are part of a larger plan to protect them from a powerful threat.

Type companion, later romantic partner and spouseSource Inuyasha manga/anime, Yashahime

Rin is a human girl whom Sesshomaru resurrects. She becomes his loyal companion, and he grows to care for her deeply. In Yashahime, it is revealed that they have twin daughters, implying a romantic relationship.

Dark History
Content advisory. Depictions of violence, death, and emotional neglect. Sesshomaru kills without remorse in his early appearances. He also shows callous disregard for human life, and his treatment of Rin in Yashahime (keeping her in a state of suspended animation) has been criticized as problematic.

Violence and murder

Source Inuyasha manga/anime, particularly early episodes

Description

Sesshomaru kills many demons and humans without hesitation in his early appearances. He is shown to be ruthless and merciless, often killing for pleasure or to prove his superiority.

Scholarly Context

This is typical of villainous characters in shonen manga, but Sesshomaru's violence is portrayed as cold and calculated.

Emotional neglect and abandonment

Source Yashahime anime

Description

In Yashahime, Sesshomaru separates his infant daughters and leaves them with others, seemingly abandoning them. He also keeps Rin unconscious for years to protect her, which is seen as a form of emotional neglect.

Scholarly Context

This has sparked debate among fans about the ethics of his actions, even if they are intended for protection.

Misogyny and dehumanization

Source Inuyasha manga/anime

Description

Sesshomaru initially views humans and half-demons as inferior, and he refers to them with disdain. His attitude reflects a common trope in supernatural fiction where demons look down on humans.

Scholarly Context

This is a character flaw that he overcomes through his interactions with Rin and others.

Contradictions & Disputes

Sesshomaru's age

Position A

Sesshomaru is over 200 years old.

Position A — evidence

Official guidebook states he is over 200 years old.

Position B

Some fans estimate he is much older, possibly 500+ years.

Position B — evidence

His father died 500 years before the main events, and Sesshomaru was likely born before that.

Scholarly Consensus

The official guidebook gives a minimum age, but the actual age is ambiguous.

Sesshomaru's romantic feelings for Rin

Position A

Sesshomaru loves Rin romantically.

Position A — evidence

In Yashahime, it is revealed they have children together, implying a romantic relationship.

Position B

His feelings are paternal or protective, not romantic.

Position B — evidence

The original series never explicitly shows romantic feelings; his interactions with Rin are more like a master-servant or father-daughter dynamic.

Scholarly Consensus

The sequel confirms a romantic relationship, but the transition is not depicted, leaving some ambiguity.

Sesshomaru's power level compared to Inuyasha

Position A

Sesshomaru is stronger than Inuyasha.

Position A — evidence

Sesshomaru is a full-blooded demon and has more experience.

Position B

Inuyasha eventually surpasses him.

Position B — evidence

Inuyasha gains new powers and defeats Naraku, while Sesshomaru also gains Bakusaiga but does not directly defeat Naraku.

Scholarly Consensus

Both are extremely powerful, but the series portrays them as equals in the final battles.
