Who It Is

Sif is the golden-haired goddess associated with the harvest and grain, wife of Thor, and mother of Ullr (by a previous marriage) and of Thrud (with Thor). Her most famous myth involves Loki sneaking into her bedroom and shaving off all her glorious golden hair while she slept. Thor, enraged, caught Loki and would have broken every bone in his body, but Loki swore to make amends. He went to the dwarves, who fashioned new hair from gold that would grow like real hair, along with the spear Gungnir for Odin and the foldable ship Skíðblaðnir for Freyr. In a creative contest between dwarves, Brokkr and Sindri also created Thor's hammer Mjölnir. Sif's golden hair thus indirectly led to the creation of many of the gods' greatest treasures. Sif represents the golden fields of grain at harvest time — her hair is the crop, golden and abundant. After a long winter, the return of golden Sif to Thor (who represents the summer sun and thunderstorms) symbolizes the return of fertility to the land.

Origins

Sif is a goddess of the Aesir, daughter of unspecified parentage. The name 'Sif' means 'in-law' or 'relation by marriage,' making her the bridge between different families of gods. She had a son, Ullr, before marrying Thor, by whom she had Thrud.

Attested in Poetic Edda (Lokasenna, Hymiskviða) / Prose Edda (Skáldskaparmál)Era Viking Age (c. 800-1100 CE), recorded 13th century

Form

Sif is described in the Prose Edda as 'the fairest of women' (Old Norse: 'hón er allra kvenna vænst'). Her defining feature is her hair, which is famously golden. In the myth, her original hair is cut off by Loki, and she is given a replacement made of gold by the dwarves, which 'grew' naturally. This has led to interpretations of her as a grain or harvest goddess, with her golden hair representing golden fields.

Powers

Golden Hair Sif's golden hair is a divine artifact in itself. After Loki cuts off her original hair, the dwarves forge a new head of hair from gold that is described as growing naturally. This is a unique object in Norse mythology, being a gift of the dwarves and a symbol of her husband's honor and her own beauty. Prose Edda, Skáldskaparmál 35 The hair has no described powers beyond its natural growth and beauty. It is a symbol of status and a testament to the skill of its makers. None.

Prophecy In Lokasenna, Loki accuses Sif of having invited him to her bed, but a prose note in the same text states that 'Sif is a prophetess, and she is called by the name Sif'. This is the only hint of any supernatural ability beyond her physical beauty. Poetic Edda, Lokasenna (prose introduction) The nature and extent of this prophecy are entirely unexplored. It is a single, unelaborated mention. This is a single, unelaborated mention. It is not referenced anywhere else.

Behavior & Habitat

Sif has very little agency in the surviving myths. She is primarily defined by her relationships: she is Thor's wife, the mother of Ullr, and the target of Loki's crude insult in Lokasenna. The Lokasenna passage suggests she was a peacemaker, as she is shown offering a drink to Loki and trying to calm the brewing conflict. She is not described as a warrior, nor does she have any known magical powers of her own.

Accounts

For reasons not given, Loki maliciously cuts off all of Sif's hair. Thor is furious and captures Loki, threatening to break every bone in his body. Loki pleads for his life and promises to get the dark elves to make a new head of hair for Sif that will grow like natural hair. He goes to the dwarves, the Sons of Ivaldi, who craft the golden hair, along with Odin's spear Gungnir and Freyr's ship Skíðblaðnir. Loki then wagers his own head with the dwarf Brokkr that Brokkr's brother Sindri cannot make three treasures as good as these. The wager results in the creation of Thor's hammer Mjölnir, which is judged the best of all.

At a feast in the hall of the sea god Ægir, Loki begins insulting every god and goddess present. When he gets to Sif, she offers him a drink of mead and calls him 'a great talker' (possibly a peacemaking gesture or a subtle insult). Loki responds by accusing her of having invited him to her bed. Before she can respond, Thor arrives and threatens Loki with his hammer, silencing him. Loki calls Thor a coward for hiding behind his threats, but Thor's presence ends the flyting.

What We Don't Know

The primary sources are sparse. Sif has no surviving myths of her own; she exists entirely in relation to Thor, Loki, and her son. Her potential prophetic abilities are mentioned once and never elaborated. The identity of Ullr's father is unknown. The exact nature of Loki's accusation in Lokasenna is left ambiguous. All modern interpretations of her as a fertility or earth goddess are speculative.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Sif as the 'First Lady of Asgard'. She has no official job description, but her presence legitimizes the throne. Her hair is like the crown jewels: a symbol of the realm's wealth and the king's honor. When Loki cuts it off, he's not just playing a prank; he's attacking the very symbol of Thor's household. And when the dwarves forge a new one, they are restoring order and creating the ultimate status symbol. She is the quiet center of the storm, the reason Thor fights.
