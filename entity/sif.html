Who It Is

She is the wife of the thunder god, a woman whose hair was so famous it became a divine artifact, and yet she has no story of her own. In the surviving myths, Sif is a golden-haired figure standing in the shadow of Thor's hammer, defined by what others do to her. But her silence has made her a canvas: for Marvel, she is a fierce warrior; for modern pagans, a harvest queen; for the gods themselves, she is the 'kin-woman', the tie that binds the family of Asgard together. Her story is one of beauty, violation, and the quiet power of being the center of a family that holds the cosmos together.

Sif has very little agency in the surviving myths. She is primarily defined by her relationships: she is Thor's wife, the mother of Ullr, and the target of Loki's crude insult in Lokasenna. The Lokasenna passage suggests she was a peacemaker, as she is shown offering a drink to Loki and trying to calm the brewing conflict. She is not described as a warrior, nor does she have any known magical powers of her own.

Powers
Golden Hair

Sif's golden hair is a divine artifact in itself. After Loki cuts off her original hair, the dwarves forge a new head of hair from gold that is described as growing naturally. This is a unique object in Norse mythology, being a gift of the dwarves and a symbol of her husband's honor and her own beauty.

Limit: The hair has no described powers beyond its natural growth and beauty. It is a symbol of status and a testament to the skill of its makers.

Prophecy

In Lokasenna, Loki accuses Sif of having invited him to her bed, but a prose note in the same text states that 'Sif is a prophetess, and she is called by the name Sif'. This is the only hint of any supernatural ability beyond her physical beauty.

Limit: The nature and extent of this prophecy are entirely unexplored. It is a single, unelaborated mention.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Sif is described in the Prose Edda as 'the fairest of women' (Old Norse: 'hón er allra kvenna vænst'). Her defining feature is her hair, which is famously golden. In the myth, her original hair is cut off by Loki, and she is given a replacement made of gold by the dwarves, which 'grew' naturally. This has led to interpretations of her as a grain or harvest goddess, with her golden hair representing golden fields.

Symbols — Her primary symbol is her golden hair. This is not just an aesthetic feature but a physical object of great value and craftsmanship, created by dwarves. It is one of the three great treasures Loki secures for the gods in the same story (along with Odin's spear Gungnir and Freyr's ship Skíðblaðnir), and is used as a bargaining chip in a wager with the dwarf Brokkr.

Domains — Her domains are not clearly defined in the sources. Her name ('kinship') suggests a role as a domestic and familial figure. Her golden hair has inspired modern theories of a connection to grain/harvest, but this is a reconstruction, not an attested fact. She resides in Þrúðvangr (Thor's realm) with her husband.

Accounts I
Loki Cuts Sif's HairProse Edda, Skáldskaparmál 35

Attested 13th century, earlier oral tradition

For reasons not given, Loki maliciously cuts off all of Sif's hair. Thor is furious and captures Loki, threatening to break every bone in his body. Loki pleads for his life and promises to get the dark elves to make a new head of hair for Sif that will grow like natural hair. He goes to the dwarves, the Sons of Ivaldi, who craft the golden hair, along with Odin's spear Gungnir and Freyr's ship Skíðblaðnir. Loki then wagers his own head with the dwarf Brokkr that Brokkr's brother Sindri cannot make three treasures as good as these. The wager results in the creation of Thor's hammer Mjölnir, which is judged the best of all. Sif is given the golden hair. Thor obtains his hammer Mjölnir. Loki is ultimately forced to have his lips sewn shut to avoid losing his head, proving that words and actions have consequences even for the gods.

Accounts II
The Flyting of Loki (Lokasenna)Poetic Edda, Lokasenna

Attested 13th century, earlier oral tradition

At a feast in the hall of the sea god Ægir, Loki begins insulting every god and goddess present. When he gets to Sif, she offers him a drink of mead and calls him 'a great talker' (possibly a peacemaking gesture or a subtle insult). Loki responds by accusing her of having invited him to her bed. Before she can respond, Thor arrives and threatens Loki with his hammer, silencing him. Loki calls Thor a coward for hiding behind his threats, but Thor's presence ends the flyting. Sif's reputation is left ambiguous. The accusation is never proven or disproven. The episode highlights the tension between Loki and Thor, and Sif is caught in the middle.

Sif is a goddess of the harvest. — This is a modern reconstruction based on her golden hair. The primary sources never connect her to agriculture or fertility.

Sif is a warrior goddess. — In the original myths, Sif is a passive, domestic figure. Her only actions are offering a drink and being victimized by Loki. The warrior persona is an invention of Marvel Comics.

Sif's golden hair is a metaphor for the sun. — The hair is a physical object, forged from gold by dwarves. It is a symbol of beauty and craftsmanship, not a celestial body.
