The Approach

In the dust of the Trojan plain, a name barely survives: Persinous, an Achaean warrior whose entire legend is a single, fatal encounter with the Amazon queen Penthesilea. His story is a footnote in the epic cycle, yet it illuminates the brutal cost of the war and the fleeting nature of mortal fame.

Content advisory. Depictions of violence and death in battle.
Identity

Persinous

Pronunciation Per-SIN-oh-us (English approximation); in Greek: Περσίνοος (Persinoos).

Etymology

The name Persinous (Περσίνοος) may derive from Greek roots: 'pers-' (related to 'pertho', meaning to destroy or sack) and '-noos' (mind). Thus, it could be interpreted as 'destroyer of the mind' or 'mind-destroying', though this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Greek mythology, specifically the Epic Cycle tradition.

Disputed Identifications

No other figure named Persinous is known in Greek mythology. The name is unique to this brief mention.

Stories & Myths

Death at the hands of Penthesilea

Source Quintus Smyrnaeus, The Fall of Troy, Book 1Culture Ancient GreekPeriod Composed in the 4th century AD, but set during the Trojan War (traditionally dated c. 1194-1184 BC).

Summary

During the Trojan War, the Amazon queen Penthesilea, daughter of Ares, arrives to aid the Trojans. In her first battle, she performs great deeds, killing several Greek warriors, including Persinous. He is mentioned only in a list of her victims, with no further details.
