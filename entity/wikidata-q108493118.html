Who It Is

The green hair bobs, the iron mask grins, and reality itself stutters — when Tricky the Clown drops onto the screen, physics files a resignation letter. Born as a disposable assassin-clown in a black-and-white wasteland of endless gunfights, Tricky became Madness Combat's most unstoppable anomaly: a cackling, teleporting corpse-puppet juiced by the Improbability Drive until death was just a loading screen.

Attested: Non-verbal; communicates through cackling, screeching, and exaggerated body language. Exhibits manic, sadistic playfulness, erratic teleportation, and obsessive fixation on killing Hank J. Wimbleton and later Jesus (Jebus). Shows no self-preservation instinct after Drive integration - repeatedly charges into gunfire, laughing. Reconstructed from creator comments: Krinkels describes Tricky as 'a psychotic clown who just wants to kill everyone, but is also kind of a puppet' - implying limited agency when possessed by the Improbability Drive/Auditor. Powers
Improbability Drive Integration / Reality Warping

After being implanted/resurrected with an Improbability Drive (MC5 onward), Tricky can violate physics: teleport instantly, levitate, summon structures, distort backgrounds, and resurrect after fatal wounds. The Drive is described in Project Nexus as a device that manipulates probability fields to make the impossible probable.

Limit: Drive requires energy; can be overloaded or temporarily disabled (Hank destroys Drive in MC7, but Auditor restores it). Tricky appears to lose control when Drive is damaged - becomes more feral. Not omnipotent - can be stalled by Hank and Sanford/Deimos with heavy firepower.

Superhuman Durability and Regeneration

Survives gunshots, impalement, decapitation, being crushed by buildings, and being ripped apart. Regenerates or reassembles within seconds when Drive is active. In MC7, his head is shot off and he continues fighting headless.

Limit: Without Drive (MC3), he dies to conventional gunfire. Even with Drive, sustained damage from Hank's dragon sword (MC11) can temporarily incapacitate him.

Teleportation and Rapid Movement

Blinks across the screen, appears behind opponents, dodges bullets by phasing. Often accompanied by green static/distortion effect.

Limit: Appears to have short cooldown; cannot teleport while being actively restrained in some game sequences.

Possession / Auditor Puppetry

In later lore, the Auditor (the series' ultimate antagonist) can directly possess and enhance Tricky, turning him into a hell-demon. Tricky's will is subsumed - he becomes an extension of the Auditor's power.

Limit: Possession requires Tricky's body as vessel; Auditor can withdraw, leaving Tricky inert. Krinkels noted Tricky 'doesn't really have agency anymore' by MC11.

Weapon Proficiency and Stop Sign Melee

Proficient with firearms (MP5, M16, shotguns) and signature melee weapon: a large red stop sign used as axe/club. In demon form, uses giant skeletal hands and fire projectiles.

Limit: Prefers melee when Drive-empowered; firearms used mainly in early appearances.

Hellclown Transformation

Final form: massive, flaming skeleton with exposed ribcage, horns, and molten flesh. Can grow to building size, summon hellfire, and warp Nevada into a hellscape. Attained after full Auditor amplification.

Limit: Transformation appears irreversible and consumes Tricky's original personality; vulnerable to Hank's halo-imbued sword in MC11.

Accounts I
Madness Combat 3: Avenger - First DeathMadness Combat 3: Avenger (Newgrounds, 2003)

2003

Hank J. Wimbleton is hired to kill the Sheriff. Tricky, a clown assassin hired as backup, ambushes Hank in a club. After a prolonged gunfight, Hank kills Tricky by shooting him in the head and leaving his body on stage. This is Tricky's canonical first appearance and first death. Establishes Tricky as Hank's recurring nemesis; introduces clown faction; sets precedent for resurrection trope.

Madness Combat 5: Depredation - Resurrection with the Improbability DriveMadness Combat 5: Depredation (Newgrounds, 2005)

2005

After being dead for two episodes, Tricky is revived when the Auditor's agents attach an Improbability Drive to his corpse. He immediately massacres agents and hunts Hank and Jesus through a canyon, demonstrating teleportation and reality warping for the first time. Hank destroys the Drive, temporarily killing Tricky again. Reveals Improbability Drive as source of Tricky's immortality; shifts Tricky from mortal clown to anomalous entity; begins Auditor-Tricky link.

Madness Combat 7: Consternation - Demon AscensionMadness Combat 7: Consternation (Newgrounds, 2007)

2007

Tricky, now fully integrated with a larger Improbability Drive, battles Hank and Jesus in a church and then in a hell-dimension. Mid-battle, the Auditor supercharges him, transforming him into a giant flaming demon skeleton (Hellclown). He kills Jesus and nearly kills Hank before Hank uses Jesus's halo to escape. The episode ends with Nevada itself warping. Definitive demonization of Tricky; death of Jesus (later revived); revelation that Tricky is Auditor's enforcer; most iconic Tricky design originates here.

Accounts II
Madness Combat 9-11: Aggregation to Expurgation - The Auditor's PuppetMadness Combat 9: Aggregation (2009), 10: Abrogation (2010), 11: Expurgation (2011)

2009-2011

Tricky returns as the Auditor's fully possessed vessel. In MC9 he is absent (Auditor builds army); in MC10 he is summoned as a massive skeletal head that devours agents; in MC11 Expurgation, Hank, Sanford and Deimos confront Auditor-possessed Tricky in Hell. Hank, now empowered by the Auditor's halo, finally decapitates and dismembers Tricky, and the Auditor abandons the body. Tricky's corpse is left burning as Nevada collapses. Apparent final death of Tricky; Auditor revealed as true mastermind; Hank's apotheosis; cliffhanger for Madness Combat's ongoing story (still incomplete as of 2026).

MADNESS: Project Nexus - Clown Prototype LoreMADNESS: Project Nexus (Steam, 2021) Story Mode

2021

The game expands lore: Clowns are failed supersoldiers from Project Nexus, created by the Auditor to be disposable shock troops. Tricky is encountered as a boss in the Nexus facility - an unstable prototype whose Improbability Drive malfunctions, causing arena-wide reality glitches. Player characters (Hank, Sanford, Deimos, etc.) must destroy his Drive to progress. Codex entries confirm Tricky's brain was heavily damaged, explaining his mania. Provides in-universe scientific explanation for Tricky's powers; canonizes clown origins; makes Tricky fightable outside animation continuity.

Vs. Tricky - The FNF Hellbeats SagaVs. Tricky Friday Night Funkin' Mod (GameBanana, 2021) - Songs: Improbable Outset, Madness, Hellclown, Expurgation

2021

Non-canonical crossover: Boyfriend (FNF protagonist) rap-battles Tricky in Nevada. Tricky starts in classic form (Improbable Outset), becomes Drive-empowered (Madness), then Hellclown (Hellclown), and finally full demon (Expurgation) where he towers over the stage and the background burns. The mod became one of FNF's most downloaded (over 5 million downloads) and introduced Tricky to a generation unfamiliar with Madness Combat. Massive resurgence in Madness Combat popularity; Tricky becomes FNF icon; Krinkels officially endorsed the mod; many fans mistakenly believe Tricky originated in FNF.

Tricky originated in Friday Night Funkin'. — Tricky debuted in Madness Combat 3 (2003), 18 years before the FNF mod (2021). FNF introduced him to a new audience but did not create him.

Tricky is the main villain of Madness Combat. — Tricky is a major recurring antagonist, but the overarching villain is the Auditor, who employs and possesses Tricky. The true protagonist/antagonist axis is Hank vs Auditor.

Tricky's mask is his real face. — The iron mask is affixed after his first death; early Tricky has a cloth/clown mask with visible skin. The iron mask is welded on and later fused by the Drive. His true face is never clearly shown.

Tricky can be killed permanently by normal weapons. — Post-MC5, conventional weapons only temporarily incapacitate him while the Improbability Drive is active. Permanent death requires destroying or removing the Drive/Auditor's influence, as Hank does in MC7 and MC11.

All clowns in Madness Combat are Tricky clones. — Clowns are a mass-produced caste from Project Nexus; Tricky is one exceptional individual, not the template for all. Other clowns have different builds and no Drive.
