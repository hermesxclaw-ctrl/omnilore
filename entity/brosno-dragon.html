Who It Is

The Brosno Dragon (Brosnya) is Russia's most famous lake monster, reported from Lake Brosno in the Tver region northwest of Moscow. According to a 13th-century chronicle, a giant reptile emerged from the lake and devoured a Tatar-Mongol army that had camped on its shores -- the warriors and their horses were supposedly dragged into the water. Modern sightings began in 1854, when multiple villagers reported seeing a large, dragon-like creature with a reptilian head on a long neck surface in the lake. In the 20th century, several more reports emerged, and in 2001-2002, the Kosmopoisk research organization conducted sonar surveys of the lake, reporting the detection of a large, moving object at a depth of about 60 feet, approximately 16 feet long. The creature is described as having a long, snake-like neck, a reptilian head, a bulky body covered in scales, and a length of 15 to 20 feet. It is said to sometimes emerge onto the shore and attack livestock. Lake Brosno is relatively small (about 2.5 miles long and 1 mile wide) but deep (up to 140 feet), with underwater caves and thermal vents that some researchers propose could create gas bubbles that appear like a surfacing creature. Skeptics explain sightings as large pike, beavers swimming in a line (creating the illusion of humps), or gas bubble eruptions from the lake's bottom sediments. The Brosno Dragon has become a minor tourist attraction in the Tver region, featured in Russian cryptozoology documentaries and local folklore festivals.
