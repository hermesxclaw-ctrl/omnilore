The Approach

The name Fels carries the weight of two continents and a century of social reform, from a soap manufacturer who funded land-value taxation to a cave that yielded the oldest known figurative art. But is it a family, a place, or a geological layer? The answer is all three, and more.

Identity

Fels

Pronunciation In English, typically pronounced /fɛlz/ (rhymes with 'bells'), but in German it is /fɛls/.

Etymology

German for 'rock' or 'cliff', from Middle High German 'vels', from Old High German 'felis'.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily German and Jewish (Ashkenazi) origin, but also found in other cultures as a surname.

Disputed Identifications

None noted. The surname is distinct from similar names like 'Feld' (field) or 'Fels' as a place name.

The Fels family and social reform

Source Biographical records of Joseph and Mary FelsCulture American, JewishPeriod Late 19th to early 20th century

Summary

Joseph Fels, a soap manufacturer, and his wife Mary, were prominent philanthropists and Georgists, advocating for land-value taxation. They funded social reform efforts in the US and UK.

Fels-Naptha soap

Source Brand historyCulture AmericanPeriod Late 19th century to present

Summary

Fels-Naptha is a brand of bar laundry soap originally produced by Joseph Fels. It became a household name known for its effectiveness.
