Who It Is

Storsjoodjuret ('the Great Lake Animal') is Sweden's most famous lake monster, reported from Lake Storsjon in the province of Jamtland in central Sweden. Uniquely among lake monsters, its legend has a tangible ancient artifact: the Froso Runestone, dating from approximately 1050 CE, is believed by some scholars to depict a great serpent in the lake. Written records of the creature date to 1635, when clergyman Mogens Pedersen recorded a local legend about a water serpent created by trolls that grew so large it threatened boats. Since the 19th century, over 200 sightings have been documented. The creature is described as 20 to 40 feet long, with a serpentine body, humps, a dog-like or horse-like head with large eyes, and grayish-brown or dark green skin. It has been observed swimming, floating on the surface, and occasionally chasing fish close to shore. In 1894, a local sea captain named Jonas Hansson claimed to have captured a young Storsjoodjuret and kept it in a tank, but the creature escaped. In 1986, the Jamtland county administrative board placed the creature under legal protection -- an official acknowledgment of its cultural significance, though the protection was later revoked controversially. Scientific investigations have included hydrophone recordings (which detected unexplained sounds) and sonar sweeps of the lake's depths (up to 250 feet). Skeptics suggest large pike, eels, or floating debris. The lake's depth, cold temperatures, and abundant fish population could theoretically support a population of large aquatic animals.
