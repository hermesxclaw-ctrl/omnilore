The Approach

A humiliated student who stole a relic to prove his worth, Waver Velvet stumbled into the Holy Grail War only to find himself riding alongside a king—and in the process, discovered the true measure of greatness. His journey from insecure youth to the revered Lord El-Melloi II is a tale of growth, loss, and the quiet pursuit of wisdom.

Content advisory. Depictions of violence, death, and psychological trauma in the context of the Holy Grail War. Some scenes may be intense for younger audiences.
Identity

Waver Velvet

Pronunciation WAY-ver BEL-vet (English); Weibā Berubetto (Japanese)

Known As

Lord El-Melloi IIZhuge Liang (Pseudo-Servant)Zhuge Kongming

Etymology

The name 'Waver' may suggest a wavering or uncertain nature, reflecting his initial insecurity. 'Velvet' could imply softness or hidden strength. 'El-Melloi II' is a title adopted after the previous Lord El-Melloi, signifying his role as successor.

Cultural Ownership

Owned by Type-Moon, created by Gen Urobuchi and Takashi Takeuchi for the Fate franchise.

Disputed Identifications

As a Pseudo-Servant, Waver's identity is merged with Zhuge Liang, leading to debates about which personality is dominant. Some fans question whether the adult Waver in Case Files is the same as the youth in Fate/Zero, but official materials confirm continuity.

Description

Appearance

As a teenager in Fate/Zero, Waver is a slender young man with messy black hair and sharp, often nervous eyes. He wears a casual outfit of a dark jacket and jeans. As an adult in Case Files, he has longer hair tied back, wears a suit or formal attire, and carries a cane, projecting a more authoritative and weary demeanor.

Symbols & Iconography

The El-Melloi crest, Zhuge Liang's feather fan and bagua mirrors, and the Holy Grail War command seals.

Behaviour & Personality

Waver starts as insecure, defensive, and desperate for recognition, often acting arrogant to mask his self-doubt. Through his experiences, he matures into a pragmatic, thoughtful, and compassionate mentor. As Lord El-Melloi II, he is known for his sharp analytical mind, dry humor, and dedication to his students, though he still carries guilt over his past.

Domains & Habitats

The Clock Tower in London, the El-Melloi classroom, Fuyuki City during the Holy Grail War, and various locations in Case Files.

Version Differences

The youthful Waver in Fate/Zero is more impulsive and emotional, while the adult Waver in Case Files is more composed and experienced. In Fate/Grand Order, his Pseudo-Servant form combines his personality with Zhuge Liang's strategic brilliance, resulting in a more calculating demeanor.

Powers & Abilities

Magecraft

Source Version All versions

Description

As a mage, Waver has basic magical abilities, including reinforcement and elemental spells, but he is not particularly powerful. His strength lies in his tactical thinking and knowledge of magecraft theory.

Limitations

Lacks the raw magical energy of other mages; relies on intellect.

Contradictions

None noted.

Zhuge Liang's Abilities (Pseudo-Servant)

Source Version Fate/Grand Order

Description

In Fate/Grand Order, Waver gains the powers of the legendary strategist Zhuge Liang, including the use of bagua mirrors for energy beams and elemental spells, as well as the Noble Phantasm 'Unreturning Formation' which traps enemies, and 'Chu Shi Biao' which buffs allies.

Limitations

As a Pseudo-Servant, his powers are limited by the vessel; he cannot access Zhuge Liang's full divine abilities.

Contradictions

Some sources suggest Waver's personality dominates, while others imply a fusion.

Strategic Genius

Source Version All versions

Description

Waver is a brilliant tactician and teacher, able to analyze complex situations and devise effective plans. This is highlighted in Case Files where he solves magical mysteries.

Limitations

His strategies can be undermined by unexpected factors or superior power.

Contradictions

None noted.

Stories & Myths

The Fourth Holy Grail War

Source Fate/ZeroCulture Japanese light novel/animePeriod Early 2000s (in-universe)

Summary

Waver steals his teacher's artifact to summon Rider (Iskandar) and enters the Holy Grail War. Despite initial friction, he forms a strong bond with Rider, who teaches him about kingship and courage. In the final battle, Rider sacrifices himself to allow Waver to escape, and Archer (Gilgamesh) spares Waver in tribute to Rider's spirit.

Waver survives and gains a new purpose, eventually returning to the Clock Tower and becoming a professor.

Source The Case Files of Lord El-Melloi IICulture Japanese light novel/animePeriod 10 years after Fate/Zero

Summary

Waver, now Lord El-Melloi II, teaches at the Clock Tower and investigates magical incidents with his assistant Gray. He confronts his past and helps students, while unraveling mysteries tied to the El-Melloi family and his own guilt.

Contradictions & Disputes

Waver's personality in Pseudo-Servant form

Position A

Waver's personality dominates, with Zhuge Liang's powers being a tool.

Position A — evidence

In Fate/Grand Order, Waver often speaks in a modern, informal manner, and his actions reflect his own desires.

Position B

Zhuge Liang's personality is more dominant, and Waver is merely a vessel.

Position B — evidence

Some dialogues and ascension lines suggest Zhuge Liang's influence, especially in battle strategies.

Scholarly Consensus

Official materials state it is a fusion, but the exact balance is ambiguous and left to interpretation.

Responsibility for Kayneth's death

Position A

Waver is directly responsible because he stole the relic.

Position A — evidence

Waver himself feels guilty and takes on the El-Melloi name to atone.

Position B

Kayneth's death was a consequence of the War and not solely Waver's fault.

Position B — evidence

Kayneth's own actions and the War's nature contributed to his demise.

Scholarly Consensus

The narrative supports both, but emphasizes Waver's guilt as a key theme.
