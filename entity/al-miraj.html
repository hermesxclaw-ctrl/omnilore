Who It Is

On a remote island in the Indian Ocean, a creature no larger than a hare sends entire herds of wild beasts fleeing in terror. With a single black horn and a coat of yellow or gold, the al-mi'raj is a paradox of the medieval bestiary: a tiny herbivore that commands the respect of predators, a gift from grateful islanders to a world-conquering king, and a beast whose very name shifts from manuscript to manuscript, as elusive as the island it calls home.

It is said to cause all wild animals to flee at the sight of it, indicating a supernatural aura of fear. It is not described as aggressive; rather, it is a passive but terrifying presence.

Causes all wild animals to flee upon seeing it. This power is shared with the karkadann.

Limit: Only affects wild animals; no effect on humans or domestic animals is mentioned.

Horn

A single black horn, but no specific magical powers are attributed to it in the sources. It may be symbolic of its unicorn-like nature.

Limit: Not described as having any special properties.

Form & Signs

Appearance — A small hare or rabbit with a single horn, typically black. In al-Qazwini, it is described as yellow in color. In al-Idrisi's account, it has a coat of shiny gold. The horn is always black. In some illustrations, it appears as a normal hare; in others, it is depicted as a hybrid creature resembling a fierce hound.

Symbols — The one horn is the defining feature, symbolizing power and uniqueness. The creature is often depicted in manuscripts alongside the dragon it is associated with, emphasizing the contrast between small size and great power.

Domains — It lives on Jazīrat al-Tinnīn ('Sea-Serpent Island' or 'Dragon Island') in the Indian Ocean, according to al-Qazwini. In al-Idrisi's version, it is found on the island of Mustashiayn, off the coast of Western Africa. The island is also inhabited by a dragon that preys on livestock.

Accounts I
The Gift to Iskandar after the Dragon-Slayingal-Qazwini, 'Marvels of Things Created'

13th century

Iskandar (Alexander the Great) arrives at Jazīrat al-Tinnīn, where a dragon has been devouring livestock. He kills the dragon by feeding it oxen stuffed with reactive substances (e.g., sulfur, quicklime, arsenic, iron hooks). The grateful islanders present him with the al-mi'raj, a yellow hare with a single black horn, as a gift. Iskandar obtains the al-mi'raj; the island is freed from the dragon.

Accounts II
The Trade on Lāqāal-Idrisi, 'Nuzhat al-Mushtaq'

12th century

Iskandar harvests aloeswood on the island of Lāqā. The wood initially has no fragrance, but after departing, it turns into fine-scented dense black wood. He trades the best specimen along with other goods, including the baqrāj, a hare-like creature with a shiny gold coat and a single black horn. The baqrāj is acquired through trade; the aloeswood becomes valuable.

The al-mi'raj is a type of jackalope. — The al-mi'raj is a distinct creature from medieval Arabic literature, not a modern North American folkloric creature. The jackalope is a 20th-century hoax.

The al-mi'raj's name means 'ascent' or 'ladder'. — The etymology is uncertain; the connection to mi'raj (ladder) is speculative.

The al-mi'raj is a peaceful creature. — It causes wild animals to flee, which is a form of supernatural intimidation, but it is not aggressive.
