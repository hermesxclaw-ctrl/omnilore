Who It Is

In the luminous haze of the Maya cosmos, a figure of immense age and power sat upon a celestial throne, his gaze encompassing the cycles of time, the sprouting of maize, and the destinies of kings. This was Itzamná, the supreme creator deity, a being whose very name whispers of sorcery and the sacred fluids that nourish the world. From the silent stone of Classic monuments to the painted pages of the Dresden Codex, he appears as the aged god of wisdom, the divine asperser, and the primordial caiman whose body forms the earth itself. Yet despite his prominence, he remains an enigma, a deity of paradoxes, whose true nature scholars still debate.

As an upper god, Itzamná is a creator and ruler, governing agriculture, hunting, and the affairs of the sky. He is a source of wisdom and priestly knowledge, having invented writing. He can be subject to narrative actions, such as being hunted or sacrificed, suggesting a complex mythological role.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Itzamná is composed of itzam and na. Itzam may derive from the root itz, meaning 'secretions' (dew, sap, semen) and 'sorcery', with an agentive suffix -am, possibly meaning 'asperser' or 'sorcerer'. Na means 'house', but Thompson's 'lizard house' interpretation is now largely abandoned. The

Pronunciation — [it͡samˈna] (Yucatec Maya)

Aliases — God D, Itzamnaaj, Zamna, Hun Itzamná, Hunab Ku (possibly), Kinich Ahau Itzamná

Ownership — Maya civilization, particularly Yucatec Maya of the Classic and Postclassic periods, with earlier roots in Pre-Classic iconography (e.g., San Bartolo).

Powers
Creation

Itzamná is a creator deity, responsible for creating humankind and, in some traditions, the Earth itself. He is also said to have divided the land of Yucatán and named its features.

Limit: Not explicitly stated, but his creative acts may be delegated or shared with other deities.

Priestly Knowledge and Writing

Itzamná invented the priestly art of writing and is invoked as 'the first priest' in book rituals.

Limit: None stated.

Rulership and Governance

He is the god of rulership, bestowing royal authority on kings, as seen in Palenque Temple XIX and his association with royal headbands.

Limit: None.

Transformation

Itzamná can transform into a caiman (the Earth) and into the Principal Bird Deity.

Limit: Not explicitly stated.

Patronage of Medicine

He is invoked as a god of medicine in the month of Zip.

Limit: None.

Governance of Agriculture and Hunt

Seated on his celestial throne, he governs agricultural and hunting affairs.

Control over Time

Form & Signs

Appearance — Itzamná is typically depicted as an aged man with a sunken mouth, toothless, and sometimes with a large, prominent nose. He often wears a headdress, sometimes resembling a bird, and may be shown with a shell or other ornaments. In his caiman aspect, he appears as a two-headed caiman representing the earth. In his bird aspect, he is the Principal Bird Deity, often with a bicephalous snake in its beak, and wings inscribed with signs for 'daylight'

Symbols — Associated symbols include the caiman, the bird (Principal Bird Deity), the snake (often bicephalous), the headband of rulership, and signs for day/night. He is also associated with the tonsured maize god in his aged form.

Domains — Itzamná resides in the sky on a celestial throne. As the Earth Caiman, he is the crust of the earth. His bird avatar perches on world trees. He is associated with the four directions and the cycle of time.

Accounts I
The Book Ritual of UoLanda, Relación de las cosas de Yucatán

16th century

In the month of Uo, a ritual aspersion of the books took place under invocation of Kinich Ahau Itzamná, 'the first priest', to ensure the success of the coming year's agricultural and priestly activities. Reinforced the sacred nature of books and the priestly class.

The Creation of the World and HumankindHernández, Relación de algunas cosas de Yucatán; Las Casas, Apologética Historia Sumaria

16th century

According to Hernández, Itzamná created humankind and was the father of the Bacab, a fourfold deity of the interior of the Earth. In another tradition recorded by Las Casas, Itzamná and Ixchel had thirteen sons, two of whom created the Earth and humankind. Established the Maya world order and the role of the Bacab in supporting the sky.

Accounts II
Itzamná as Earth CaimanDresden Codex; Books of Chilam Balam

13th-16th centuries

In the Dresden Codex, Itzamná's head appears within the serpent maw of a two-headed caiman representing the Earth. This caiman may correspond to the Itzam Cab Ain (Itzam Earth Caiman) of a creation myth in the Chilam Balam books, where the earth is formed from a cosmic caiman. Establishes Itzamná's role as a cosmic earth deity.

The Shooting of the Principal Bird DeityClassic Maya iconography; Popol Vuh (for comparison)

Classic Period (c. 250-900 AD); Popol Vuh from Colonial era

In Classic Maya art, the Hero Twins are depicted shooting the Principal Bird Deity, which is often identified as an avatar of Itzamná. This episode is a major mythological narrative, but scholars argue against equating the Principal Bird Deity with Vucub Caquix from the Popol Vuh. Reinforces the Hero Twins' heroic status and possibly the establishment of cosmic order.

Itzamná Bestows RulershipPalenque Temple XIX platform

c. 700 AD

On the platform, a dignitary wearing the Principal Bird Deity headdress, referred to as Itzamnaaj, presents the king with his royal headband, symbolizing the divine bestowal of rulership. Legitimizes royal authority through divine sanction.
