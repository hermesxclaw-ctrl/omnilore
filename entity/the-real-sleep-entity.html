Who It Is

In the Local 58 episode 'Real Sleep,' a VHS tape is presented as a medical treatment for dream suppression — supposedly developed by a 'Thought Research Initiative.' The tape uses subliminal imagery and audio to prevent the viewer from dreaming, because dreaming is dangerous. The entity behind this danger is never named or shown, but the implications are clear: something reaches humans through their dreams. The Moon is connected. When people dream, they become vulnerable to this entity, which enters their minds, alters their perception, and ultimately leads them to self-destruction. The 'treatment' uses rapid image flashing, backward audio messages, and distorted visual patterns to suppress the brain's ability to form dreams. But even this treatment is flawed — some of the subliminal messages embedded in the tape are not from the doctors but from the entity itself, co-opting the very tool meant to fight it. 'Real Sleep' is a masterful piece of metafictional horror: the viewer is watching a tape that is trying to alter their brain, and in doing so, the viewer becomes complicit in the fiction. The concept of a dream-based entity that uses sleep as its vector for influence taps into universal human vulnerabilities — everyone must sleep, everyone dreams, and no one is fully in control of their subconscious. The entity, like the Moon itself, is never directly described, which makes it more terrifying: our imagination fills in the gaps with whatever we personally find most frightening. 'Real Sleep' is widely considered one of the most disturbing episodes in analog horror for its use of subliminal techniques that make the viewer feel genuinely manipulated by the video they're watching.
