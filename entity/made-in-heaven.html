Who It Is

On a dusk-lit shore in Montreux, a bronze Freddie Mercury gazes across Lake Geneva as three bandmates face the Alps—a cover that captures an album built from the final breaths of a dying star. Made in Heaven is not just Queen's last record; it is a séance in sound, where the living completed the songs of the dead, and the result became a monument to love, loss, and the refusal to say goodbye.

The album is a posthumous collaboration, blending Mercury's original vocal and piano recordings with new instrumentation by the surviving members. The music ranges from rock anthems to ballads, reflecting the diverse styles of Queen. The emotional weight of the project is evident in the careful production and the poignant final track 'Mother Love'.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The title is derived from the song of the same name, originally written and recorded by Freddie Mercury for his solo album Mr. Bad Guy (1985). The phrase 'Made in Heaven' suggests something divinely ordained or perfect, which contrasts with the earthly struggles of the album's creation.

Pronunciation — /meɪd ɪn ˈhɛvən/

Aliases — Made in Heaven

Powers
Posthumous collaboration

The ability to create new musical works using recordings of a deceased artist, integrating them with new instrumentation and production.

Limit: The surviving members had to work with existing material, which limited the scope of new compositions. They could not create new vocal performances.

Emotional resonance

The album's ability to evoke strong emotional responses due to its connection to Mercury's death and the circumstances of its creation.

Limit: The emotional weight may be overwhelming for some listeners.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The album cover features a photograph of Irena Sedlecká's bronze statue of Freddie Mercury, located on the shore of Lake Geneva in Montreux, Switzerland. The CD cover shows the statue at dusk, with the band members Brian May, Roger Taylor, and John Deacon depicted on the rear cover gazing at the Alps. Symbols — The statue itself is a powerful symbol of Mercury's legacy, with his right arm raised in a characteristic pose. The album title 'Made in Heaven' suggests a celestial origin, and the natural settings of dawn/dusk evoke themes of endings and beginnings, life and death.

Domains — The album was recorded at Mountain Studios in Montreux, Switzerland, where Queen had a long association. The songs are set in various emotional landscapes, from the introspection of 'Mother Love' to the celebratory 'Heaven for Everyone'.

Accounts I
The Final SessionsVarious interviews and the Champions of the World documentary

1991

After completing Innuendo, Freddie Mercury, knowing he was dying, recorded as many vocal parts as possible with the instruction that the band complete them later. The sessions were sporadic, whenever Mercury felt well enough, and were focused on maximizing his contributions. These sessions produced the raw material that would become the core of Made in Heaven.

Accounts II
Completing the AlbumInterviews with Brian May

1992-1995

After Mercury's death, the band initially worked separately, but May felt the direction was wrong. They regrouped in 1993 and reworked existing Mercury solo tracks and demos into Queen songs, adding new instrumentation and arrangements. The album was completed and released in November 1995, becoming a commercial success.

The Last VocalBrian May's website

1991

The song 'Mother Love' was the last song Mercury and May co-wrote. Mercury recorded his vocals in May 1991 but was unable to finish the final verse due to his declining health. May sang the final verse himself. This song stands as Mercury's final recorded vocal performance.
