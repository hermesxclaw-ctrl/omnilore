Who It Is

In the shadowed halls of the Fenian Cycle, where heroes carve their names in blood and fate, there exists a woman whose story is as fragmented as the myths themselves—Uirne, sister of Muirne, mother of hounds, and a figure whose very identity is contested by scholars.

Uirne is portrayed as a woman who endures multiple marriages and a magical transformation. She appears as a mother and a victim of a queen's jealousy, but her own personality is not detailed in the sources.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Uirne is interpreted as 'sharp-mouth', possibly derived from Old Irish roots suggesting a sharp or pointed mouth, perhaps indicating a sharp tongue or biting speech.

Pronunciation — Unknown; likely pronounced 'OOR-nuh' or 'WIR-nuh' in reconstructed Old Irish, but no definitive source found.

Aliases — Tuirn, Tuirne

Ownership — Irish mythology, specifically the Fenian Cycle (an Fhiann).

Accounts I
Uirne's Marriages and the Birth of Bran and SceólangFenian Cycle texts, particularly the tales surrounding Fionn's hounds.

Medieval (but based on earlier oral tradition)

Uirne, daughter of the druid Tadg, marries Conall and bears a son, Dáire. She then marries Imcad, son of the king of Dal n-Araide. The queen, jealous of Uirne, transforms her into a dog while she is pregnant. Uirne gives birth to two hounds, Bran and Sceólang, who become the famed hunting dogs of Fionn. Later, she marries Lugaid Lága and bears three human children: Eogan Ruad, Sciath Brecc, and Cael Croda. Bran and Sceólang become loyal companions to Fionn, aiding him in many adventures. The transformation and its aftermath highlight themes of enchantment and the blurred boundary between human and animal.
