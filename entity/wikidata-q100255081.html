Who It Is

In the shadow of the New Testament's towering figures, Silas stands as a quiet pillar of the early Church—a prophet, a prisoner, and a companion of Paul whose broken chains became a symbol of divine deliverance. Yet behind the biblical narrative lies a tangle of names, a possible secret disciple, and a legacy that spans denominations and centuries.

Described as a leader among the brothers, a prophet, and an encouraging speaker (Acts 15:32).

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Silas is the Greek rendition of the Aramaic Seila (שְׁאִילָא), a version of the Hebrew Saul (שָׁאוּל). The Roman name Silvanus means 'of the forest'.

Pronunciation — SY-ləs (English), SEE-las (Greek)

Aliases — Silvanus, Seila, Saul, Sela (in Emmerich's visions)

Ownership — Early Christian community, 1st century AD

Accounts I
Selection at the Jerusalem CouncilActs 15:22

c. 49 AD

Silas and Judas Barsabbas were chosen by the church elders to return with Paul and Barnabas to Antioch with a letter. Silas later became Paul's companion on his second missionary journey.

Imprisonment in PhilippiActs 16:16-40

c. 50 AD

Paul and Silas were arrested, beaten, and imprisoned. At midnight they prayed and sang hymns; an earthquake freed them. The jailer and his household were converted. The jailer converted; Paul and Silas were released with apology.

Accounts II
Mission to Thessalonica and BereaActs 17:1-15

c. 50 AD

Paul, Silas, and Timothy preached in Thessalonica, faced hostility, then moved to Berea where they had success until agitators came. Paul left for Athens; Silas and Timothy stayed in Berea, then joined Paul in Corinth.

Journey to CorinthActs 18:1-5

c. 50-51 AD

Silas and Timothy joined Paul in Corinth, where Paul preached and faced opposition. Paul wrote 1 Thessalonians from Corinth, with Silas as co-author.

Silas is just a minor character with little importance. — He was a leading member of the early Christian community, a prophet, and a co-author of two New Testament letters.

Silas and Silvanus are different people. — They are traditionally considered the same person, though the name relationship is debated.

Silas was a Roman citizen. — Acts does not explicitly say Silas was a Roman citizen; only Paul claims citizenship.
