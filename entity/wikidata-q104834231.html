The Approach

In the shadowed corners of Afro-Brazilian spiritual practice, where the drumbeat of the terreiro meets the silence of the forest, there exists a figure known as Caboclo Lua Cheia—the Caboclo of the Full Moon. He is a spirit of the night, a guardian of the wild, and a bridge between the ancient indigenous peoples of Brazil and the enslaved Africans who forged new faiths in the crucible of the Americas. His name evokes the silver light that illuminates the rituals of Umbanda, where he is invoked for healing, wisdom, and the unyielding strength of the moon itself.

Identity

Caboclo Lua Cheia

Pronunciation kah-BOH-kloo LOO-ah SHAY-ah

Etymology

The name is Portuguese: 'Caboclo' refers to a spirit of indigenous Brazilian origin in Afro-Brazilian religions, and 'Lua Cheia' means 'Full Moon'. Thus, the name translates to 'Caboclo of the Full Moon'.

Cultural Ownership

Afro-Brazilian religious traditions, particularly Umbanda, and to a lesser extent Candomblé (though Caboclos are more prominent in Umbanda). The figure represents a syncretic fusion of indigenous Brazilian spiritual beliefs and African religious practices.

Disputed Identifications

There is no known dispute about Caboclo Lua Cheia specifically, but the broader category of Caboclos is sometimes debated among scholars regarding their exact origins and the degree of indigenous vs. African influence.

Appearance

No specific description is available. In general, Caboclos are often depicted as indigenous Brazilian warriors, wearing feathered headdresses, carrying bows and arrows, and adorned with animal skins. They may also appear as wise elders or shamans. For Caboclo Lua Cheia, it is likely that lunar imagery (such as a crescent moon or silver accents) would be associated with his appearance, but this is speculative.

Symbols & Iconography

The full moon is the primary symbol associated with this entity, though no specific iconography is documented. In Umbanda, Caboclos are often associated with natural elements, and the moon could represent intuition, night, and the feminine principle.

Behaviour & Personality

Caboclos are typically characterized as wise, noble, and protective, with a deep connection to nature. They are often called upon for healing, guidance, and strength. Caboclo Lua Cheia, given his lunar association, might be seen as particularly attuned to the night, dreams, and the emotional realm, but this is inferred.

Domains & Habitats

Caboclos are associated with forests, rivers, and natural landscapes. The full moon suggests a connection to the night sky and possibly nocturnal activities. In ritual, Caboclos are invoked in terreiros (temples) during ceremonies that include drumming, dancing, and singing.
