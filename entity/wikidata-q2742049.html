Form

Alignak is typically described as a powerful young man, often a hunter, dressed in furs. In the sky, he appears as the moon itself. The dark spots on the moon are his house, or the remnants of his camp, or his dog-sled and team. In some accounts, he carries a torch (the moon's light) that he holds to light his way; in others, he is the torch. His face is sometimes said to be visible in the moon's craters, looking down on Earth.

Powers

Lunar Control Alignak governs the phases of the moon. His actions in the sky (hunting, resting) directly cause the waxing and waning. A full moon means he is successful and powerful; a new moon means he is weak and hungry. Attested across multiple oral traditions (Boas, Rasmussen, Rink). He is bound to his cycle; he cannot break free of the chase after his sister. None known.

Tide Mastery He controls the tides, pulling the ocean with his lunar power. This gives him influence over the sea, which is crucial for hunting and survival. Commonly attested in coastal Inuit communities. His control is tied to his lunar cycle. None known.

Weather Manipulation He can bring storms, especially winter blizzards, and is believed to control the cold. He is a bringer of both life-sustaining snow (for igloos) and deadly storms. Attested in shamanic invocations and oral traditions. Unknown; he is a sky god, so his power over weather is significant. In some traditions, this power is more strongly attributed to Sila.

Psychopomp / Guide of the Dead He guides the souls of those who died by violence or drowning to the afterlife. He is a protector of the dead who did not receive a proper burial. Attested in some Central and Eastern Arctic traditions. He only guides a specific subset of the dead; others go to Sedna's realm or other afterlives. Some traditions assign this role to Sedna or to a separate spirit.

Shamanic Patron He is a source of power for shamans. They can travel to him in their spirit journeys to gain knowledge, healing power, and protection. Attested in shamanic traditions across the Arctic. Only trained shamans can access this power. None known.

Divine Justice He punishes those who break taboos, particularly those related to incest, murder, and mistreatment of animals. He is a stern enforcer of cosmic law. Attested in oral traditions where he is seen as a moral arbiter. His justice is often seen as harsh and unforgiving. In some versions, his own actions are the result of a taboo violation, making him a tragic figure rather than a purely just one.

Hunter's Patron As a celestial hunter, he blesses hunters on Earth with success. His dog-sled is a model for the ideal hunting team. Attested in oral traditions and the iconography of the moon's craters. His blessing is conditional on the hunter respecting the animals and the taboos. None known.

Accounts

In a communal snow house, a young woman (Malina) is secretly visited by a man at night. She suspects it is her brother, Alignak. To identify him, she smears soot on her hands and wipes it on his face. The next night, she sees the soot on her brother's face and realizes the truth. Horrified and ashamed, she flees the house. Alignak chases her. She grabs a torch (or a piece of moss) and runs into the sky, becoming the sun. Alignak grabs another torch, but he trips and spills the embers, which become the stars. He becomes the moon. He chases her across the sky, but she is always ahead of him. He grows hungry and weak as he chases, which is why the moon wanes. When he rests and eats, he grows full again, which is why the moon waxes.

A powerful shaman is taken to the moon by a spirit. He meets Alignak, who is a great hunter. Alignak shows the shaman his house, which is made of the bones of a giant whale. He teaches the shaman songs and rituals that will help his people. The shaman returns to Earth with this knowledge, becoming an even more powerful angakkuq. This story establishes Alignak as a source of shamanic power and a teacher of sacred knowledge.

During an eclipse, the people believe that Alignak has descended to Earth to visit his sister, or that he is being attacked by a monstrous being. To protect him, the people make noise, beating drums and pots, and shout to scare away the evil spirit. They also believe that during the eclipse, the moon's shadow is a sign that Alignak is angry or that a taboo has been broken.

Scholarly Debates
Identity: Aningaat vs. Alignak
Aningaat (Greenland) and Alignak (Canada) are the same deity, with regional name variations. ⇌ They are distinct figures with different origins and myths, later conflated by anthropologists.
Nature of the Chase
Alignak is a tragic figure, driven by longing and guilt, not malice. ⇌ Alignak is an aggressive, violent figure who is punishing his sister and the community.
Role in the Afterlife
Alignak is a psychopomp, guiding the dead to the afterlife. ⇌ Sedna is the sole ruler of the dead, and Alignak has no role in the afterlife.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Alignak as the tragic hero of an Arctic epic. He's not a distant, all-powerful god like Zeus or Odin. He's more like a powerful, flawed human — a hunter who made a terrible mistake and is now cursed to an eternity of longing and pursuit. He's the embodiment of the harsh, beautiful, and unforgiving Arctic environment: a source of light in the darkness, but also a force of cold and storm. He's the patron of those who struggle, the judge of those who break the rules, and the guide for those who have lost their way. If you've ever felt the ache of a mistake you can't undo, or the loneliness of a path you can't leave, you can understand Alignak.
