Who It Is

A term for the 'Great Archon' in Gnostic Christianity, particularly associated with the Basilidean sect. The name appears in Gnostic texts such as the Holy Book of the Great Invisible Spirit and the Apocalypse of Adam, as well as in the Greek Magical Papyri. Abraxas stones — antique engraved gems bearing the image of a being with a rooster's head, human torso, and serpent legs, holding a whip and shield — were used as amulets in the Greco-Roman world. The seven letters of the Greek spelling Ἀβρασάξ (A-B-R-A-S-A-X) numerologically sum to 365 — the number of days in a solar year and the number of heavens in Basilidean cosmology — marking him as the ruler of the 365 celestial spheres. In Carl Jung's 'Seven Sermons to the Dead,' Abraxas appears as a supreme deity beyond the opposition of good and evil, encompassing both.
