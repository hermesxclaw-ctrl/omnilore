Who They Are

Cikap-kamuy is the Ainu god of birds, master of all winged creatures. He governs the migrations of birds — essential natural signals for hunting and fishing seasons — and appears most often in the form of the owl, the cuckoo, or the eagle. He is a messenger god, carrying prayers between earth and the sky kamuy.

Cultural Significance

Birds played a crucial practical and spiritual role in Ainu life. The Blakiston's fish owl was considered so sacred that its very presence was a blessing — villages where it nested were thought to be especially protected. The Ainu hunted certain seabirds for food and feathers but never the owl, which was strictly taboo to harm. The cuckoo's first call of spring was celebrated with a small festival. Cikap-kamuy's feathers were used to create arrows and the fletching that carried prayers on the wind. Today, the Blakiston's fish owl is endangered, and Ainu groups in Hokkaido work with conservationists to protect both the bird and the cultural traditions surrounding it.
