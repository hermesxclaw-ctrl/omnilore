Atl also governs the cardinal direction of the east, associated with fertility and rebirth. In the Aztec calendar, it is one of the 20 day signs, each with its own patron deity and symbolic meaning.

Form

As a day sign, Atl is depicted in Aztec codices as a blue or turquoise stream of water, often with a conch shell, a fish, or a water lily. In some representations, it appears as a water vessel or a pool, emphasizing its role as a source of life and fertility. The color blue dominates, symbolizing the sky and the sea, while the shell motif recalls the ocean and the origins of life.

Powers

Divinatory Influence Atl, as a day sign, was believed to influence the fate of those born on this day, granting them a connection to water and its life-giving properties. It was also consulted for auspicious timing of water-related activities. Aztec calendar Limited to the tonalpohualli (260-day calendar) and divinatory practices. Some sources suggest Atl was also associated with the west, while most place it in the east.

Elemental Association Atl embodies the element of water, serving as a bridge between the earthly realm and the underworld (Mictlan). It is linked to the rain god Tlaloc and the earth goddess Tlaltecuhtli, representing the primordial waters that existed before creation. Aztec cosmology Not an active power but a symbolic and cosmological principle. The exact relationship between Atl and Chalchiuhtlicue (goddess of lakes) is unclear; some sources treat them as synonymous, others as distinct.

Behavior & Habitat

As a day sign, Atl is not a personified deity with behavior but a temporal marker with divinatory significance. It was considered a favorable day for activities related to water, such as fishing, bathing, and rituals to rain gods. Those born on this day were believed to be destined for prosperity, emotional depth, and a connection to the aquatic world.

Accounts

In the Aztec creation myth, the gods Tezcatlipoca and Quetzalcoatl tore apart the earth goddess Tlaltecuhtli. From her body, the world was formed, and her blood became the waters. Atl, as the primordial water, filled the oceans, rivers, and lakes, becoming the lifeblood of the earth.

The day sign Atl was one of the 20 day signs in the Aztec tonalpohualli. Those born on this day were believed to be fortunate, with a destiny linked to water. It was a day for honoring the rain gods and performing water-related rituals.

Scholarly Debates
Directional association
Atl is associated with the east, as in the Codex Borbonicus and Florentine Codex. ⇌ Some sources associate Atl with the west, possibly due to the setting sun over the ocean.
Evidence for A: Codex Borbonicus, Florentine Codex Book 4
Evidence for B: Codex Magliabechiano (variant readings)
Atl vs. Chalchiuhtlicue
Atl is a distinct day sign and water principle, not a personified deity. ⇌ Atl is simply another name for Chalchiuhtlicue, the goddess of lakes and streams.
Evidence for A: Primary codices treat Atl as a day sign with no cult or mythology of its own.
Evidence for B: Some colonial-era sources use Atl and Chalchiuhtlicue interchangeably.
What We Don't Know

Further research is needed on regional variations in the interpretation of the day sign Atl across different Nahua communities. The relationship between Atl and Chalchiuhtlicue in pre-conquest sources requires deeper analysis. The role of Atl in Aztec ritual practices beyond divination is not well documented.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Atl as the Aztec equivalent of 'Water' in a zodiac sign — not a god to pray to, but a cosmic principle that shapes personality and timing. If you were born under Atl, you were believed to be as deep and fluid as the sea itself.
