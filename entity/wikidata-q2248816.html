Who It Is

In the vast blue of the Aztec cosmos, before the gods tore the earth apart, there was water — and its name was Atl. Not a god with a trident, not a goddess with a flowing gown, but something more elemental: the very idea of water, carved into a day sign that still whispers to those who read the old calendars. In a world where every day was a god, Atl was the day the waters spoke.

As a day sign, Atl is not a personified deity with behavior but a temporal marker with divinatory significance. It was considered a favorable day for activities related to water, such as fishing, bathing, and rituals to rain gods. Those born on this day were believed to be destined for prosperity, emotional depth, and a connection to the aquatic world.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Atl' is derived from the Nahuatl word 'ātl', meaning 'water'. In Aztec mythology, this term forms the root of many water-related names and concepts, including 'Apan' (place of water), 'Atlan' (beside the water), and the deity 'Atlatonan' (our mother of the water). Divinatory Influence

Atl, as a day sign, was believed to influence the fate of those born on this day, granting them a connection to water and its life-giving properties. It was also consulted for auspicious timing of water-related activities.

Limit: Limited to the tonalpohualli (260-day calendar) and divinatory practices.

Elemental Association

Atl embodies the element of water, serving as a bridge between the earthly realm and the underworld (Mictlan). It is linked to the rain god Tlaloc and the earth goddess Tlaltecuhtli, representing the primordial waters that existed before creation.

Limit: Not an active power but a symbolic and cosmological principle.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a day sign, Atl is depicted in Aztec codices as a blue or turquoise stream of water, often with a conch shell, a fish, or a water lily. In some representations, it appears as a water vessel or a pool, emphasizing its role as a source of life and fertility. The color blue dominates, symbolizing the sky and the sea, while the shell motif recalls the ocean and the origins of life.

Symbols — Conch shell, fish, water lily, blue stream, water vessel. The shell symbolizes the ocean and the cycle of life, while the fish represents abundance and the sustenance provided by water. The color blue is paramount, evoking both the sky and the sea.

Domains — Water in all its forms: rain, rivers, lakes, oceans, and the underworld waters. Atl also governs the cardinal direction of the east, associated with fertility and rebirth. In the Aztec calendar, it is one of the 20 day signs, each with its own patron deity and symbolic meaning.

Accounts I
Creation of the WorldHistoria de los Mexicanos por sus Pinturas

Post-classic (c. 1535)

In the Aztec creation myth, the gods Tezcatlipoca and Quetzalcoatl tore apart the earth goddess Tlaltecuhtli. From her body, the world was formed, and her blood became the waters. Atl, as the primordial water, filled the oceans, rivers, and lakes, becoming the lifeblood of the earth. The waters became essential for life, agriculture, and ritual, establishing the Aztec connection between water and fertility.

The Day Sign AtlFlorentine Codex, Book 4

Post-classic (c. 1577)

The day sign Atl was one of the 20 day signs in the Aztec tonalpohualli. Those born on this day were believed to be fortunate, with a destiny linked to water. It was a day for honoring the rain gods and performing water-related rituals. Individuals born on this day were often dedicated to water deities and held roles as fishermen or ritual specialists.

The name 'Atl' is the source of the legend of Atlantis. — The name 'Atlantis' comes from Plato's Greek myth, not from Nahuatl. The similarity is coincidental.
