Who It Is

Nyarlathotep is the most terrifying entity in Lovecraft's mythos precisely because he is the most human. Unlike the vast, mindless Outer Gods (Azathoth) or the indifferent, dreaming Great Old Ones (Cthulhu), Nyarlathotep possesses intelligence, will, and a deliberate, sadistic interest in humanity. He is the 'soul and messenger' of Azathoth, the one Outer God who walks freely among mortals in a thousand different forms, spreading madness, manipulating civilizations, and delighting in human suffering. Lovecraft introduced him in a prose poem (Nyarlathotep, 1920) where he appears as a modern-day traveling showman — a tall, dark man of vaguely Egyptian appearance who emerges from the desert to demonstrate impossible scientific marvels that drive audiences slowly insane. This form is only one of his many 'masks' or avatars. In The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath, he appears in the Dreamlands as a majestic 'Black Pharaoh' of ancient Egypt, served by night-gaunts and shantak-birds. In The Haunter of the Dark (1936), he manifests as a winged, three-lobed burning eye that haunts an abandoned church in Providence, Rhode Island. Other avatars include: The Black Man (a cloven-hoofed Satanic figure worshipped by witches), The Faceless God (a faceless sphinx worshipped in forgotten African temples), The Bloated Woman (a monstrous feminine form that lures victims), The Black Wind (an invisible destroyer), and The Dweller in Darkness (a formless, hungry entity that manifests through possessed bodies). What unites all these forms is Nyarlathotep's conscious, malevolent intelligence — he is the only Lovecraftian entity who seems to genuinely enjoy cruelty. He treats humanity as a game, appearing throughout history in different guises to guide civilizations toward self-destruction. He was the dark god of the witch-cults of medieval Europe, the inspiration of mad prophets, and the secret patron of science gone wrong. His ultimate loyalty is to Azathoth and the Outer Gods, whom he serves as executor of their mindless, destructive will — but within that role, he exercises creative cruelty that the mindless Outer Gods could never conceive. Later Mythos writers expanded Nyarlathotep's pantheon enormously: his thousand forms include everything from the Bloody Tongue (a monstrous, clawed giant) to various human avatars who manipulate world events. Nyarlathotep has become the face of Lovecraftian horror in popular culture — a villain who can appear anywhere, in any form, with any motive, and whose only constant is a terrifying, cosmic contempt for human existence paired with a perverse fascination with human suffering.
