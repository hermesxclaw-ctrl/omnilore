Who It Is

He is a tiny green dragon who decided, alone and apparently unprompted, to invade Hell. In 1984, before Mario had saved a princess or Link had lifted a sword, Shigeru Miyamoto sent Tamagon — round as an egg, armed only with borrowed holiness — into the Devil's shifting labyrinth, where the walls themselves try to crush him and every dot can only be eaten after stealing a Cross from the Devil's own floor.

Tamagon has no dialogue or explicit personality text in-game; characterization is inferred from manual premise and gameplay role. The manual premise states Tamagon 'decides to attack the Devil's World' — an active, courageous, almost reckless heroic motive rather than being summoned or defending. In gameplay he is agile but vulnerable: without a Cross or Bible he is completely helpless, unable to eat dots (Boa Boa Dots) or defeat enemies and must evade. Powers
Cross-Empowered Fire Breath

After touching a Cross placed in the maze, Tamagon gains the ability to breathe a short-range stream of fire and to eat Boa Boa Dots and enemy monsters. The fire projectile extends approximately one maze cell ahead and can defeat patrolling monsters on contact.

Limit: Power is temporary and expires after a set timer (approx. 7-10 seconds depending on version/difficulty). Without a Cross, Tamagon cannot breathe fire, cannot eat dots, and cannot defeat enemies — contact with any monster is fatal. Fire range is very short; requires close proximity. Crosses respawn only after maze completion or specific conditions.

Bible-Empowered Fire Breath and Seal Activation

In second-stage maze type, collecting one of four Bibles grants the same fire-breathing and dot-eating ability as a Cross, and allows Tamagon to carry the Bible to the central Seal. Placing all four Bibles into the Seal completes the maze and forces the Devil to flee to the next maze.

Limit: Only one Bible can be carried at a time; must be ferried individually to the Seal while avoiding enemies and moving walls. Bible power also temporary, same timer as Cross. If power expires while carrying, Tamagon becomes vulnerable. Dropping or losing Bible not attested — Bible remains with Tamagon until sealed.

Maze Navigation and Dot Consumption

Tamagon can move in four directions through maze corridors, consume small dots (Boa Boa Dots) only when empowered, and push/collect Bonus Boxes in bonus mazes. Movement speed is constant, slightly faster than most enemies when not empowered.

Limit: Cannot pass through walls; cannot eat dots without power-up, making maze completion impossible without Cross/Bible. Movement can be blocked or Tamagon can be crushed if caught between a maze wall and the scrolling outer boundary moved by the Devil's minions.

Vulnerability: Wall-Crush Death

Unique hazard: when the Devil points in a direction, his four minions pull the maze via pulleys, scrolling the entire labyrinth within the outer frame. If Tamagon is positioned between a wall and the outer border during this scroll, he is squished and loses a life.

Limit: Not an ability but a defining weakness; requires player to anticipate Devil's pointing direction (indicated by Devil's arm) and stay in open corridors. No defense against crush except positioning.

Bonus Maze Collection

In interlude bonus mazes (no enemies, no Devil), Tamagon can collect six Bonus Boxes under a time limit for extra points. No power-up required in this mode.

Limit: Time-limited; no combat ability needed or granted. Boxes must be touched in maze without enemies; maze still scrolls? Bonus maze is static in most versions.

Accounts I
The Attack on Devil World - PremiseDevil World instruction manuals (Famicom 1984, NES PAL 1987) and in-game premise

1984 (Showa 59)

Tamagon, a small green dragon, decides of his own volition to attack the Devil's World — a demon-controlled realm of shifting mazes. He enters the Devil's domain to confront the winged Devil who rules it from above, navigating labyrinths patrolled by the Devil's monster minions. The premise frames Tamagon as the aggressor/hero invading enemy territory, not a defender. Establishes the cyclical structure of the entire game: Tamagon must repeatedly clear mazes to push the Devil onward. No narrative ending beyond loop; premise justifies endless maze combat.

The Maze of the CrossDevil World gameplay - Stage Type A

1984

In the first maze type, Tamagon enters a dot-filled labyrinth patrolled by monsters. He is powerless until he locates and touches a Cross. Upon touching it, he ignites with fire-breathing power, allowing him to eat dots and devour monsters for a limited time. He must clear all dots while managing the Devil's wall-scrolling interference. When all dots are eaten, the maze is cleared. Clearing this maze advances Tamagon to the Bible/Seal maze. Demonstrates core Pac-Man-like power-pellet loop but with religious icon as power source.

The Four Bibles and the SealDevil World gameplay - Stage Type B

1984

After the Cross maze, Tamagon enters a second maze type where four Bibles are scattered. He must collect each Bible — each grants fire-breathing power — and carry it to a central Seal (a square enclosure in the middle of the maze). Only one Bible can be carried at a time. After all four are placed in the Seal, the Seal activates, the Devil is repelled and flies to the next maze, and Tamagon advances. This is the game's second objective loop and its most distinctive religious imagery. Completion triggers the Devil's flight animation and progression to next level pair. Failure (death) resets Bible positions.

Accounts II
The Bonus MazeDevil World gameplay - Bonus Stage

1984

Between the main maze pairs, Tamagon enters a bonus maze free of enemies and the Devil. Six Bonus Boxes are placed in the maze. Tamagon has a limited time to collect all boxes for bonus points. There is no combat and no power-up needed; pure collection and navigation under time pressure. Provides scoring opportunity and pacing break. Does not advance plot but contributes to high score and extra lives. Failure to collect all boxes simply ends bonus stage with partial points.

The Endless Loop - The Devil's FlightDevil World gameplay - Looping structure

1984-present (via re-releases)

After completing both Cross and Bible/Seal mazes plus bonus stage, the Devil flies away to a new, harder maze and the cycle repeats indefinitely with increasing difficulty. There is no final boss defeat or narrative conclusion; Tamagon's attack is endless. The game has no true ending — play continues until lives are exhausted, with difficulty scaling via faster enemies and more aggressive wall scrolling. Establishes Devil World as an arcade-style endless score attack rather than story-driven adventure. This looping design was typical of 1984 Famicom arcade ports but notable as Miyamoto's first console-exclusive endless maze game.

Tamagon's Forgotten Stardom - Captain RainbowCaptain Rainbow (Wii, 2008, Skip Ltd./Nintendo)

2008

In this Wii adventure game set on Mimin Island where forgotten Nintendo characters live, Tamagon appears as a supporting character. Having been largely forgotten since Devil World (which never released in North America), Tamagon has lost his confidence and heroic identity. The player, as Captain Rainbow (Nick), must help Tamagon recover his ability to breathe fire and remember his purpose by finding a special item and encouraging him. Tamagon's quest is a meta-narrative about obscurity and nostalgia. This is Tamagon's only significant narrative appearance outside Devil World, recontextualizing him from maze hero to washed-up celebrity. It cemented his status as a cult 'forgotten' Nintendo character and provided his only voiced/personality depiction beyond the silent 1984 sprite.

Whether Tamagon is Hero or VillainTamagon is heroic attacker of Devil's WorldTamagon is intruder/villain invading Devil's domain

Tamagon is the Devil or the villain of Devil World — Tamagon is the green dragon hero who attacks the Devil's World. The Devil is a separate large winged demon at the top of the screen who controls the maze. Confusion arises from the title Devil World.

Devil World and Tamagon were never released outside Japan — Devil World was released for NES in Europe/PAL regions in 1987 and on Wii Virtual Console in PAL in 2008. It was only North America that had no release until the Nintendo Switch Online version in 2023.

Tamagon can always breathe fire and eat dots — Tamagon is completely helpless without first touching a Cross or Bible. Without empowerment he cannot eat dots or defeat monsters and must evade. Fire-breathing is temporary and timed.

Tamagon is a well-known Nintendo mascot like Mario or Yoshi — Tamagon is an extremely obscure, single-game protagonist (plus one cameo) who was unknown to most Western players for decades due to lack of NA release. Nintendo itself treats him as a 'forgotten' character (as depicted in Captain Rainbow).

Devil World is just a Pac-Man clone with no original mechanics — While Pac-Man-inspired, Devil World adds unique mechanics: the Devil-directed scrolling maze via pulleys that can crush Tamagon, the two-stage Cross then Bible/Seal objective, and bonus mazes. Tezuka's rejected hole mechanic and retained crush system show deliberate differentiation.
