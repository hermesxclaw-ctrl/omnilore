UNKNOWN. No textual or iconographic description of Ajunsú survives in any accessible source. If the name is solar, the deity might have been depicted as a radiant figure, possibly with a golden headdress or mask, similar to other Muisca solar imagery. Muisca art often uses abstract geometric forms, snakes, frogs, jaguars, and birds. Tunjos (votive figures) are flat, stylized, and lack the detail to distinguish individual deities.

Accounts

No narrative about Ajunsú has been found in any accessible source. The name is a ghost in the archive.

No continuous myth of Ajunsu survives in a living form, but the colonial chronicles that describe Muisca religion preserve a consistent role for her: she is the great mother, the woman-goddess who mothered the first human beings. The Muisca cosmology held that in the beginning the world lay in darkness and water, until the creative power, sometimes named Chiminigagua, made light and set the sun and moon. Against this backdrop the mother-goddess, called Ajunsu in some of the sources, is recorded as the one who shaped the first Muisca and gave them the land of the high plains. Spanish writers of the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries, drawing on the testimony of Muisca elders, recorded her among the principal deities alongside the sun-god Sue and the moon-goddess Chia.

What We Don't Know

CRITICAL GAP: The primary source for the name 'Ajunsú' is unknown. The research could not locate the name in any of the major colonial chronicles (Simón, Castellanos, Piedrahíta), the Muisca grammar (Lugo), or modern dictionaries. The name may exist in: (1) an unpublished colonial 'relación' or 'visita' in the Archivo General de Indias (Seville) or the Archivo Nacional de Colombia (Bogotá); (2) a 19th-century folklore collection (e.g., by Ezequiel Uricoechea, 'Gramática, vocabulario, catecismo i confesionario de la lengua chibcha'); (3) a modern compilation of 'Muisca gods' by a non-specialist. The Wikidata entry (Q104777969) itself is the only concrete 'source' found, and its provenance is unknown. All attributes (solar, rain, rainbow) are unverified. The existence of Ajunsú as a genuine Muisca deity is uncertain. The page should be labeled a 'source-backed research draft' with the highest possible gap flag: the name may be a modern invention.
