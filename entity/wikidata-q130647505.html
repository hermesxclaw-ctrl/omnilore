Who It Is

Before it was a UNESCO-listed city of Roman ruins and Van Gogh's sun-drenched streets, Arles was Arelate — a city so important that it had its own goddess. She wasn't a mythic heroine with epic adventures, but a face on a coin, a guardian in a crown of stone walls, and a symbol of Roman might at the crossroads of the Rhône. Her story is not one of gods and monsters, but of power, prosperity, and the rise of an empire in a single city.

As a personification, Arelate does not have a narrative personality in myths. Her 'personality' is that of the city itself: a proud, wealthy, and strategically important Roman colony. She embodies the civic virtues of Romanitas, prosperity, and loyalty to the Empire. Her cult would have been celebrated through public festivals, sacrifices, and games, reinforcing the city's identity and its connection to the Emperor.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Arelate' is of Gaulish origin, likely derived from a Celtic root meaning 'near the marsh' or 'by the fen', reflecting the city's original topographical setting. The later Latin name 'Arelatum' and the modern French 'Arles' descend from this Gaulish toponym.

Pronunciation — /əˈrɛləteɪ/ (uh-REL-uh-tay) in Latin; /aʁl/ (arl) in modern French.

Aliases — Arelas, Arelatum, Colonia Iulia Paterna Arelatensium, Arles

Powers
Civic Prosperity

As the divine embodiment of the city, Arelate was believed to ensure the city's wealth, trade, and agricultural abundance. The city's strategic position on the Rhône made it a vital trading hub, and the deity's favor was sought for continued prosperity.

Limit: Her power was tied to the city's fortune; if the city suffered, the deity's power was seen as diminished.

Imperial Favor

Arelate was closely associated with the Roman Emperor, particularly from the reign of Constantine, who resided in the city. The deity's cult reinforced the city's loyalty and its special relationship with the imperial house.

Limit: This power was political and symbolic, not mythic.

Guardianship

Like all city genii, Arelate's primary function was to protect the city, its citizens, and its institutions. This was a protective, tutelary role, ensuring the city's survival against enemies and natural disasters.

Limit: This guardianship was invoked through ritual and sacrifice; it was not a proactive power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a city personification on Roman coins, Arelate is depicted as a female bust. She typically wears a mural crown (a crown shaped like city walls with towers), which is the standard iconographic attribute for city deities and personifications (like Tyche of Antioch or Dea Roma). Symbols — The primary symbol of Arelate is the mural or turreted crown, signifying her status as a city. Other symbols associated with her, as with other city personifications, include the cornucopia (prosperity), a rudder (fortune/guidance), or a globe (imperial power). She may also be associated with the Rhône River, which was crucial to the city's trade and identity.

Domains — Arelate's domain is the city of Arelate (modern Arles) and its territory. The city was located on the Rhône River, near its delta, in the Roman province of Gallia Narbonensis. It was a major commercial hub and, later, a key imperial residence and administrative center. Her habitat is the urban landscape of the Roman city, including its forum, temples, and public spaces.

Accounts I
Foundation by CaesarRoman historical tradition, literary sources

1st century BCE

Arelate was founded as a Roman colony for veterans of the Legio VI Ferrata and possibly other legions, following Julius Caesar's campaigns in Gaul. The city was established on the site of an earlier Greek or Gaulish settlement. It was a deliberate act of Romanization, creating a bastion of Roman culture and loyalty in southern Gaul. Arelate grew into one of the most important cities in Roman Gaul, becoming a major port and administrative center. Its foundation set the stage for its later prominence.

Accounts II
The Rivalry with MassaliaRoman historical tradition

1st century BCE

Arelate's rise was partly at the expense of Massalia (Marseille), a Greek city that had sided with Pompey against Caesar. After Caesar's victory, Massalia was punished, and Arelate, which had supported Caesar, was favored. This led to a transfer of commercial and political influence to Arelate. Arelate became the premier port of southern Gaul, cementing its economic and strategic importance.

Imperial Residence of ConstantineRoman history, literary sources

4th century CE

Emperor Constantine I spent considerable time in Arelate, making it a de facto imperial residence. He hosted a major church council (the Council of Arles in 314 CE) and used the city as a base for campaigns. This imperial presence elevated the city's status and prestige. Arelate became a major center of early Christianity and a key administrative hub for the Western Empire. The city's divine personification would have been associated with this imperial glory.
