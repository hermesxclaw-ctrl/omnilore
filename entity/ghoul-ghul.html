Who It Is

A demonic, shape-shifting creature of the desert from pre-Islamic Arabian folklore. The ghūl (Arabic: غول) inhabits graveyards and desolate places, luring travelers astray to consume their flesh and drink their blood. It is also a grave-robber, digging up corpses to eat. The ghūl can take the form of a beautiful woman to seduce unwary men, then transform into a monstrous hyena-like creature to attack. The term entered English via Antoine Galland's translation of One Thousand and One Nights and evolved in modern usage to mean a grave-robbing corpse-eater. Unlike the zombie, the ghoul is not a reanimated human but a separate demonic species — an eater of the dead rather than the dead themselves.
