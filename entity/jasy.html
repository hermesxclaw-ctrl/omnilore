Who It Is

Jasy (also Jasy Jatere in some contexts) is the Guarani moon goddess, the consort of Tupã, and the mother of the stars. Her name simply means 'moon' in the Guarani language, and she is the divine feminine principle that balances Tupã's masculine creative power. In Guarani cosmology, Jasy worked alongside Tupã during creation, and her realm encompasses the night, women's fertility, the tides of the great rivers (the Paraná, Paraguay, and Uruguay), and the cyclical time measured by the lunar phases. Guarani myth tells that Jasy was once a beautiful mortal woman who was loved by Tupã. He lifted her into the sky to be his eternal companion, and her radiance lights the darkness so that humans need not fear the night. Her phases — waxing, full, waning, new — are her monthly life cycle, dying and being reborn. The Guarani, like many indigenous peoples, plant and harvest by the moon, and Jasy's rhythm governs agricultural life. She is also intimately associated with the region's great rivers, and fishermen invoke her for safe catches. In some regions, Jasy is conflated with the Virgin Mary (as syncretized through the Jesuit missions), but in more traditional communities, the distinction persists — Jasy belongs to the indigenous night, to the forest and the river, not to the church. Her etiological myths include the origin of certain plants, animals, and the sacred yerba mate (Ilex paraguariensis), which is said to have been a gift from Jasy and Tupã to humanity.
