Who It Is

The Queensland Tiger, also called the Yarri by Aboriginal Australians, is a cryptid marsupial carnivore reported from the dense rainforests and scrublands of northeastern Queensland. Described as a cat-like animal about the size of a large dog (4 to 5 feet long including tail), with a striped coat (black stripes on a tawny or reddish background), a long tail, a feline face with prominent canine teeth, and semi-arboreal habits (it climbs trees), the creature reportedly preys on wallabies, possums, and occasionally livestock. Aboriginal traditions describe the Yarri as a real, dangerous predator. European accounts began in 1871, when naturalist George Sharp described a 'tiger-cat' the size of a dingo but with stripes like a tiger. Several specimens were reportedly shot in the late 19th century, but none survive in museum collections. The most famous encounter occurred in the 1940s, when a supposed Queensland Tiger was shot by a farmer and photographed, but the body was lost. Cryptozoologists propose the creature is a surviving population of Thylacoleo carnifex -- the marsupial lion, which was Australia's apex mammalian predator during the Pleistocene (extinct ~46,000 years ago). Thylacoleo was a leopard-sized marsupial with powerful jaws, retractable claws, and a striped coat pattern inferred from rock art. Alternatively, it could be a surviving population of the thylacine (Tasmanian tiger, Thylacinus cynocephalus) on mainland Australia -- though thylacines had stripes on their hindquarters, not a tiger-like full-body pattern. Skeptics propose feral cats of unusual size, swamp wallabies seen in poor light, or the cultural memory of extinct predators preserved through Aboriginal storytelling and rock art.
