The Approach

In the shifting sands of legend, the name Ramses echoes across millennia—a title of kings, a name of power, and a figure whose story is as much about the weight of history as it is about the man who bore it. But which Ramses? The historical pharaohs of Egypt's Nineteenth and Twentieth Dynasties, or the fictional hero of a modern tale? The answer, as with all great legends, is layered and contested.

Identity

Ramses

Pronunciation English pronunciation varies: 'RAM-seez' or 'ra-MEE-seez'. Ancient Egyptian pronunciation is reconstructed as 'Riʕmosisu' or similar.

Etymology

The name 'Ramses' (also spelled Ramesses, Rameses, Ramses) is derived from the Egyptian 'Ra-meses' meaning 'Born of Ra' or 'Ra is the one who bore him'. It was a theophoric name honoring the sun god Ra.

Cultural Ownership

The name is of ancient Egyptian origin, used by pharaohs of the Nineteenth and Twentieth Dynasties. Modern usage spans multiple cultures, especially in Western media and literature.

Disputed Identifications

The primary dispute is whether the entity refers to a specific historical pharaoh (e.g., Ramesses II) or a fictional character. No specific fictional character named 'Ramses' has been verified in this research; the name is often used in fiction as an allusion to Egypt or to a specific historical figure.

Description

Appearance

For historical Ramesses II: known from statues and mummy, he was tall, with a prominent nose and red hair (from mummy analysis). In popular depictions, he is often shown with a beard, crown, and regalia. For fictional versions, appearance varies; no specific description exists for the unidentified entity.

Symbols & Iconography

Historical Ramesses II used the cartouche, the uraeus, the double crown, and the scepter. He is depicted in numerous statues and reliefs, often smiting enemies or offering to gods. Fictional versions may use these symbols to evoke ancient Egypt.

Behaviour & Personality

Historical Ramesses II was known for his military ambition, building projects, and self-promotion. He is often portrayed as a strong, authoritarian ruler. Fictional portrayals vary, from heroic to tyrannical, depending on the narrative.

Domains & Habitats

Ancient Egypt, particularly the Nile Delta and the city of Pi-Ramesses (modern Qantir). The entity's realm is not specified beyond 'fictional character'.

Version Differences

Major differences exist between historical accounts, biblical interpretations, and modern fictions. The historical Ramesses II is documented, while the biblical 'Pharaoh of the Exodus' is debated. Fictional versions often exaggerate or invent events.
