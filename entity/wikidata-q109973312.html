Who It Is

For 3,700 years, one teenager stayed awake by counting. While every other human on Earth stood frozen as stone, Senku Ishigami counted the seconds — one by one, without pause — until the acid rain that freed him finally came. He woke up naked in a world where Tokyo had become jungle, where lions prowled Shibuya, and where his only tools were his memory and a stubborn, exhilarating faith that science could rebuild everything from scratch.

Senku is curt, hyper-rational, sardonic, and relentlessly optimistic under the catchphrase 'This is exhilarating!' (Japanese: 'Sossori shiyou ze' / 'Juu oku percent' / 'Sore wa kyoumi bukai' variants; localized as 'This is exhilarating!' and 'Ten billion percent'). Inagaki explicitly modeled his brusque, no-nonsense logic on Agon Kongo from Eyeshield 21, but inverted from antagonist to heroic mentor. Powers
Encyclopedic Scientific Knowledge (Chemistry, Physics, Engineering, Biology, Astronomy)

Senku possesses genius-level recall and understanding of modern science, from nitric acid revival fluid (nital) to sulfa drugs, glass, gunpowder, electricity, and spacecraft. He can recreate technologies from Stone Age to space age using only Stone World materials. This is presented as learned knowledge, not supernatural.

Limit: Knowledge is limited to what a prodigy could plausibly know and what Stone World resources allow. He cannot create materials without precursors (e.g., needs tungsten for filaments, needs iron sand for steel). He frequently requires collaborators (Chrome, Kaseki, Gen, Ukyo) for labor, craftsmanship, and specialized skills. He is not omniscient — he fails to immediately identify the petrification device's mechanism and must experiment.

Rapid Experimentation and Applied Invention

Ability to design and execute multi-step chemical and engineering processes under primitive conditions: revival fluid (nitric acid + alcohol), iron smelting, glassblowing, antibiotics, vacuum tubes, telephone, tank, drone, and ultimately rocketry. He uses trial-and-error and iterative prototyping.

Limit: Requires time, manpower, and precise conditions; many inventions fail first (e.g., early lightbulb filaments). He cannot mass-produce without infrastructure. Physical weakness means he cannot perform heavy forging himself.

Analytical Deduction and Logical Reasoning

Senku instantly deconstructs phenomena, identifies lies (notably Gen's deceptions), and formulates strategies in Stone Wars. He uses 10-billion-percent confidence as rhetorical framing for probabilistic reasoning.

Limit: Emotionally detached logic can alienate allies; he underestimates emotional factors (e.g., Tsukasa's love for Mirai). He is outmaneuvered socially by Gen and Ibara at times.

Superhuman Mental Endurance (3700-Year Count)

Senku remained conscious while petrified for 3,719 years, 5 months, etc., by counting seconds to stay sane and track time, allowing him to awaken and know the date (April 5738). This feat is presented as extreme willpower, not a power, and is the foundation of his legend.

Limit: Caused severe psychological strain; only Senku and later a few others (Taiju, etc.) are shown able to do it; most humans lost consciousness. He still suffered disorientation upon awakening.

Charismatic Leadership and Pedagogy

Senku builds and leads the Kingdom of Science (Five Wise Generals) by inspiring and teaching Stone World natives. He turns Chrome from self-taught sorcerer into apprentice scientist, and mobilizes entire villages through clear division of labor and showmanship.

Limit: Not a natural orator like Gen; relies on demonstration over persuasion. His bluntness can offend (e.g., early interactions with Kohaku).

Physical Weakness and Low Combat Ability (Inherent Limitation)

Senku is explicitly weak, poor at fighting, hunting, and heavy labor. Comic Book Resources and canon repeatedly emphasize he cannot carry heavy logs, loses arm-wrestling to Kohaku, and relies on allies for protection. This is a deliberate contrast to Tsukasa's superhuman strength.

Limit: Cannot defend himself alone; nearly killed by Tsukasa (Chapter 7), Hyoga, and Ibara. Must avoid direct combat.

Accounts I
The Petrification and the 3,700-Year CountDr. Stone Chapter 1: 'Stone World' (Weekly Shonen Jump 2017), Episode 1

2017 (publication); in-universe June 2019 → April 5738 AD

High school prodigy Senku and his friend Taiju are in high school when a green light petrifies all humans (and swallows). Taiju is mid-confession to Yuzuriha. Senku, turned to stone, refuses to lose consciousness and counts every second for 3,700 years to maintain his mind and track time. Acidic nitric acid from a cave (nital) cracks his stone, reviving him in a jungle-covered Tokyo. He discovers the world has reverted to wilderness, establishes a base camp at his old school, and begins experimenting to free others. Establishes core premise, Senku's mental endurance feat, and his mission to rebuild civilization and cure petrification. Reveals revival requires nitric acid + alcohol. Sets ideological foundation: science vs. stone.

Revival Fluid and the Schism with Tsukasa ShishioDr. Stone Chapters 2-12, Episodes 2-6

2017-2018 (publication); in-universe 5738 AD

Senku revives Taiju using nital (nitric acid + ethanol from grapes). Together they prepare enough fluid to revive Yuzuriha, but Taiju insists. Instead they revive Tsukasa Shishio, the 'Strongest Primate High Schooler,' to survive lion attack. Tsukasa, who believes petrified adults should stay dead to create a pure world without corrupt adults, smashes Senku's petrified Yuzuriha statue (actually a decoy) and offers Senku a choice: abandon science or die. Senku recalls his childhood love of science and friendship with Taiju, refuses, and is seemingly killed by Tsukasa's blow to the neck. Taiju and Yuzuriha are forced to join Tsukasa's Empire of Might. Creates central ideological conflict: Senku's universal revival (science for all) vs. Tsukasa's selective revival / primitivist eugenics. Senku's 'death' forces him to survive alone and seek allies elsewhere; leads directly to Ishigami Village arc.

Ishigami Village and Becoming ChiefDr. Stone Chapters 13-45, Episodes 7-18

2018 (publication); in-universe 5738 AD

Wounded Senku stumbles upon Ishigami Village, a settlement of ~40 descendants of the six ISS astronauts who survived petrification (including his adoptive father Byakuya). Mistaken for a sorcerer, he befriends Chrome — a self-taught 'sorcerer' who collects minerals — and makes him his apprentice, plus Kohaku, Kinro, Ginro, and craftsman Kaseki. He cures village priestess Ruri (Kohaku's sister) with sulfa drugs, wins the Grand Bout, and becomes the 43rd Chief. He learns he is already a legend: Byakuya founded the village and preserved Senku's memory through the '100 Tales' (Hyakumonogatari). He unveils tungsten for vacuum tubes and begins building electricity, glass, and communications. Establishes Kingdom of Science, Senku's found family, and technological base (iron, glass, electricity, lightbulb). Reveals origin of village and Senku's inherited legacy. Chrome's apprenticeship embodies Inagaki's theme of science as transmissible.

Accounts II
Stone Wars — Kingdom of Science vs. Empire of MightDr. Stone Chapters 46-83, Season 2: Stone Wars (2021)

2018-2020 (publication); in-universe 5738-5739 AD

Senku prepares for war against Tsukasa's empire, which has revived strong warriors (Hyoga, Homura) and controls the cave of miracles. With Chrome and Kaseki, he creates high-temperature heating for tungsten filaments, builds a hydroelectric plant, vacuum tubes, telephones, and ultimately a tank and dynamite. He uses psychological warfare: Gen Asagiri (mentalist) defects after Senku makes cola and ramen, and Senku broadcasts Lillian Weinberg's voice to trick Tsukasa's army. Final battle at Tsukasa's camp: Senku demands truce to revive Tsukasa's comatose sister Mirai with revival fluid, proving petrification can heal. Hyoga is defeated, Tsukasa is mortally wounded then placed in cryosleep (cold sleep) to preserve him for future revival, and the two kingdoms merge under Senku's Five Wise Generals. Ends first major war, unites humanity, establishes Senku's leadership of combined ~150 people, and pivots story from survival to global exploration to solve petrification mystery. Tsukasa's cryosleep becomes long-term plot thread.

Age of Exploration — Treasure Island and the Petrification DeviceDr. Stone Chapters 84-138, Season 3 (2023)

2020-2021 (publication); in-universe 5739 AD

Senku announces plan to build the ship Perseus to find the source of petrification. After building the ship with Ryusui Nanami (revived navigator), they sail to Treasure Island (Takara Jima), where Byakuya's crew descendants live and where the petrification device (Medusa) is held. The island is ruled by Ibara, who petrifies inhabitants at will. Senku's team is largely petrified; Senku himself is petrified and revived via science (rebuilding Medusa's activation conditions). He outwits Ibara by using the Medusa's voice-activation and range limits, recovers the device, and learns it was created by an advanced intelligence. He revives the islanders and gains new allies (Amaryllis, Matsukaze). First acquisition of a functional Medusa device, proof that petrification is technological, not natural. Expands worldbuilding to global scale and introduces Why-man as antagonist. Sets up global voyage.

New America, South America and the Why-Man RevelationDr. Stone Chapters 139-212, Anime Season 3 Part 2 / Season 4

2021-2022 (publication); in-universe 5740 AD

Perseus sails to North America, encountering Dr. Xeno Houston Wingfield — a NASA scientist and Senku's former mentor figure — and his militarized fortress with Stanley Snyder. Xeno, who also seeks to rebuild science but via dictatorship, becomes Senku's scientific rival. After a gunpowder and science battle (including corn city, motorcycles, and a sniper duel), Senku wins Xeno to his side. The combined team travels to South America, the epicenter of the original petrification (revealed to be near the Amazon). There they discover the Medusa swarm and that Why-man is not a person but a parasitic mechanical lifeform that petrifies humans to survive, communicating via radio waves. Senku communicates with Why-man, who has been mimicking Senku's voice. Reveals true cause of petrification: parasitic Medusas seeking to preserve humans as hosts. Establishes final science vs. parasitic intelligence conflict and need for space travel to confront Why-man on the moon.

The Moon Mission — Building a Rocket to Confront Why-ManDr. Stone Chapters 213-232 (Final Arc), Anime Season 4: Science Future (2024-2025)

2022 (manga finale); in-universe 5741-5742 AD

Senku declares humanity will go to the moon to confront Why-man at its source. With global resources and revived scientists worldwide (including Xeno's team), the Kingdom of Science builds a rocket city, develops computer, GPS, and ultimately a three-stage rocket. Senku, Chrome, Ryusui, and others train as astronauts. Byakuya's legacy culminates as Senku fulfills his childhood dream of space exploration. The final chapters show the launch, Senku's negotiation with Why-man (offering to build Medusas a new habitat if they stop petrifying), and an epilogue where Tsukasa is revived from cryosleep and humanity enters a new scientific age. Senku's final line reaffirms 'ten billion percent' faith in science. Concludes Senku's arc from Stone World survivor to astronaut who saves humanity through diplomacy and engineering, not violence. Fulfills Inagaki's promise that Senku would never gain fantasy powers — victory is purely scientific. Epilogue confirms revival of all petrified humans.

Does Senku Become Physically Strong Later?Senku remains physically weak throughout; his strength is intellectual and never becomes combat-capable.Some new viewers expect shonen power creep where Senku trains to fight like Tsukasa.

Senku has superpowers or eventually gains magic/science-magic abilities. — Inagaki explicitly stated Senku will never develop fantasy powers; his abilities are strictly learned science. The only 'impossible' element is the petrification device itself, not Senku. He wins through chemistry, engineering, and teamwork.

Senku is a cold, arrogant mad scientist who doesn't care about others. — Despite curt speech and smug grin, Senku is deeply altruistic — his core principle is reviving everyone and using science to elevate all people. He risks his life for friends, weeps for Byakuya, and mentors Chrome selflessly. Critics (Manga.Tokyo, CBR) emphasize he subverts mad scientist archetype with caring nature.

Dr. Stone and Senku's science is 100% accurate and replicable at home. — While Inagaki and Boichi research real science and include footnotes, many processes are simplified, timelines compressed, and some steps omitted for safety (e.g., handling sulfuric acid, making sulfa drugs, gunpowder). The revival fluid (nitric acid + alcohol) is fictionalized. Do not attempt without professional lab.

Senku is the biological son of Byakuya Ishigami. — Senku is Byakuya's adoptive son. Byakuya (an astronaut) took in Senku as a child; their different surnames are shared via adoption, not blood. Byakuya's biological lineage is not Senku's.

Senku's catchphrase is 'Ten billion percent' in Japanese. — Senku uses multiple catchphrases: 'Juu oku percent' (ten billion percent) for confidence, 'Sossori shiyou ze' and 'Sore wa kyoumi bukai na' (This is exhilarating) for excitement. English localization consolidated to 'This is exhilarating!' and 'Ten billion percent.' Gadget Tsūshin listed the exhilarating line, not just the percent line.
