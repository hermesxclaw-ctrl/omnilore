Who It Is

Walpurgisnacht (the night of April 30 to May 1) is the legendary gathering of witches on the Brocken — the highest peak of the Harz Mountains in northern Germany. According to folk tradition, witches from across Europe ride broomsticks, goats, or pitchforks to the summit for a great sabbat presided over by the Devil himself. They dance wildly around bonfires, fornicate with demons, plot blights against crops and livestock, and initiate new witches into their coven. The mountain earned its folkloric status through a combination of factors: its eerie mist-shrouded peak, optical illusions (the 'Brocken spectre' — a magnified shadow cast on clouds), and its association with the pre-Christian spring festival later christianized as Saint Walpurga's Eve. Goethe immortalized the Walpurgis Night in 'Faust' Part I, where Mephistopheles brings Faust to the Brocken revels — a phantasmagoric scene of witches, demons, and grotesque spirits celebrating in a parody of divine creation. The witch trials of early modern Germany drew heavily on the Walpurgisnacht mythology — accused witches were routinely questioned about their attendance at the Brocken sabbats. Today, Walpurgisnacht is celebrated across Germany and Scandinavia with bonfires, costumes, and folk festivals that blend pagan echoes, Christian demonology, and Romantic nostalgia.
