Who It Is

Imagine the sky above a battlefield not as empty space, but as a crowded highway of divine arbiters. They are not angels of mercy, but auditors of death, riding down from the realm of the gods to decide, in an instant, who lives to see another sunrise and who is taken to an eternal hall of feasting and war. Their name, Valkyrie, is a job description: Chooser of the Slain. They are the original, terrifying 'HR department' of Valhalla, and their selection process is a single, brutal battle.

Their primary function is to select which warriors die in battle and which are taken to Valhalla. They are decisive and martial, embodying the chaos and selection of the battlefield. In Valhalla, they serve as cup-bearers to the Einherjar (the chosen slain), providing mead from the goat Heiðrún. They are also connected to fate, as they 'determine who shall be slain' (Snorri). They are not depicted as gentle or nurturing in the primary texts; they are forces of destiny and war.

Powers
Chooser of the Slain

The Valkyries' core power is to determine the outcome of battles and decide which warriors die and which are taken to Valhalla. They are agents of fate, and their choice is absolute.

Limit: They act under Odin's command; they are not independent arbiters of all death, only of those in battle.

Ride the Sky

They can ride horses through the air and over the sea, traveling between Midgard and Asgard. Their passage is often marked by light or supernatural phenomena.

Limit: None stated.

Shape-shifting (Swan-maiden aspect)

Some Valkyries, particularly in the Helgi poems, are described as having the ability to change into swans. This is a common motif in Germanic folklore.

Limit: In the stories, if a man steals their swan-skin, they are trapped in human form.

Supernatural Combat Prowess

They are formidable warriors, bearing spears and shields. They are not just passive choosers; they participate in the battle's chaos.

Limit: They are not invincible; e.g., Brynhildr is punished and put to sleep by Odin for disobedience.

Serve the Einherjar

In Valhalla, they serve mead and ale to the fallen warriors, ensuring the eternal feast continues.

Limit: This is a service role, not a power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The sources describe Valkyries as female figures, often riding horses and bearing spears or shields. They are typically imagined as beautiful and radiant, sometimes described as 'shining' or 'glowing.' They wear armor, though the specifics (helmets, byrnies, etc.) are mentioned in passing. They are often associated with light and the sky. In the 'Helgakviða Hundingsbana I', they are described as 'warrior maidens' riding through the sky, their arm

Symbols — Key symbols include: the spear (a weapon of Odin, often associated with his power), the shield, the horse, the raven, the wolf, and the mead-horn (in Valhalla). Their name itself, 'Choosers of the Slain', is their primary symbol. They are often depicted in art as winged or with swan-like attributes (some sources suggest they can shape-shift into swans), though this is more prominent in later romanticized depictions.

Domains — The battlefield is their primary domain, where they descend to choose the slain. They also reside in Valhöll (Valhalla), Odin's great hall in Asgard, where they serve the warriors. They traverse the sky, often riding over the battlefield. The concept of 'Valföll' (the field of the slain) is their hunting ground.

Accounts I
The Awakening of BrynhildrPoetic Edda (Sigrdrífumál), Völsunga saga

13th century CE (compilation), older oral tradition

The Valkyrie Brynhildr (Sigrdrífa) disobeys Odin by granting victory to a warrior he had doomed. As punishment, Odin pricks her with a sleep-thorn, casts her into a deep sleep, and surrounds her with a wall of fire. The hero Sigurd (Siegfried) rides through the flames, awakens her, and they fall in love. She teaches him runes and wisdom before their tragic fate unfolds. This story forms the core of the Völsunga saga and the Nibelungenlied, leading to Sigurd's death and Brynhildr's suicide. It establishes the Valkyrie as a tragic, semi-mortal figure with agency and a doomed love.

Accounts II
The Battle of the Hjaðningar (Hjaðningavíg)Skáldskaparmál (Prose Edda), various skaldic verses

13th century CE (compilation), earlier tradition

Hildr, a Valkyrie, is kidnapped by the warrior Hedin. Her father Högni pursues them. Hildr, rather than stopping the conflict, appears to resurrect the slain warriors each night so the battle can continue forever. This eternal battle is known as the 'Hjaðningavíg'. This story illustrates the Valkyrie's connection to the endless cycle of battle and death, and their role as instigators of conflict, not just its adjudicators.

The Valkyries and Helgi HundingsbanePoetic Edda (Helgakviða Hundingsbana I and II)

13th century CE (compilation), older tradition

The Valkyrie Sigrún (in one version) protects and aids the hero Helgi. She rides through the sky with her sisters, and her love for Helgi is central to the story. She defies her family to be with him, leading to tragedy. This story shows the Valkyrie as a protector and lover of a mortal hero, a theme common in the Helgi poems.

Valkyries are winged women who fly over the battlefield. — The primary sources do not describe them as having wings. They ride horses through the sky. The winged depiction is a later artistic invention, popularized by 19th-century paintings and Wagner's operas.

Valkyries are a band of identical warrior women. — The sources name individual Valkyries with distinct personalities and stories. Brynhildr is a tragic heroine, Hildr is a instigator of eternal warfare, and others are more generic. They are not a monolithic group.

Valkyries only take fallen warriors to Valhalla. — While their primary function is to select for Valhalla, some sources suggest they can also choose who dies, and their role is not limited to a 'reward' system. They are arbiters of death, not just transporters of the brave.

Valkyries are the same as 'shield-maidens'. — Shield-maidens (skjaldmær) are human female warriors in the sagas (e.g., Lagertha, Hervör). Valkyries are supernatural or semi-divine beings. The terms are often conflated in modern media, but they are distinct concepts.
