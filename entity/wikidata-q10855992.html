Who It Is

Perched on a street corner with a golden koban gleaming on its forehead, Meowth is the only Pokémon that taught itself to talk like a human — not for battle, not for glory, but to impress a girl who didn't love him back. Part lucky cat, part broke hustler, part Saturday-morning villain, this bipedal stray turned Team Rocket's motor-mouthed schemer into one of the most recognizable cats in global pop culture.

Game Pokédex: Nocturnal, loves round/shiny objects, especially coins; mischievous, collects loose change, scratches, territorial, sleeps during day. Intelligence high for a Pokémon, but feral wild Meowth do not speak.

Anime Team Rocket Meowth (individual): Unique personality - street-smart, wisecracking, Brooklyn-esque gangster affectation (English dub) vs. using first-person pronoun 'nyā' and ending sentences with 'nya' in Japanese. Name & Tongue

Etymology — English 'Meowth' is a portmanteau of 'meow' (onomatopoeia for a cat's cry) + 'mouth' (referencing its talkative anime incarnation) and/or 'myth' per early localization notes, adapted from Japanese 'Nyarth' (ニャース). 'Nyarth' combines 'nya' (にゃあ, Japanese cat meow) + 'arth' possibly from English 'mouth

Pronunciation — English: /miˈaʊθ/ mee-OWTH (rhymes with 'mouth'). Japanese: Nyāsu [ɲaːsɯ], often rendered NYARTH in early romanization. Anime English Meowth pronounces with a slightly nasal, New York-inflected accent in performance.

Aliases — Nyarth, Nyasu, ニャース, Alolan Meowth, Galarian Meowth, Gigantamax Meowth

Ownership — Created by Game Freak, owned by Nintendo / Creatures Inc. / Game Freak via The Pokémon Company. Japanese origin (1996). Design by Ken Sugimori and Atsuko Nishida team under Satoshi

Powers
Pay Day

Signature move. Meowth throws coins (from forehead koban) at opponent; after battle, player receives money equal to twice Meowth's level times number of times move was used. Normal-type, 40 power, 100% accuracy.

Limit: Low power, only useful for farming money. Does not work if Meowth faints before battle ends in some gens. In anime, not depicted as combat-effective.

Pickup / Technician / Unnerve (Abilities)

Pickup: picks up used/consumed items after battle or finds items in wild. Technician: boosts power of moves with base power ≤60 (e.g., Fury Swipes, Aerial Ace). Unnerve (Hidden): prevents foes from eating Berries. Alolan Rattled: raises Speed when hit by Bug/Ghost/Dark. Galarian Tough Claws: boosts contact moves.

Limit: Pickup only works if not holding item; Technician only affects low-power moves; Unnerve situational.

Fury Swipes / Scratch / Bite / Fake Out

Standard physical movepool: Scratch, Fury Swipes (2-5 hits), Bite (Dark, may flinch), Fake Out (priority flinch, only first turn). Also learns Taunt, Nasty Plot (Alolan), Metal Claw (Galarian), Screech, Feint.

Limit: Low base stats (total 290; 90 Speed is highest). Not competitively viable without evolution. Anime Meowth's Fury Swipes is its main attack, often ineffective vs. Pikachu.

Human Speech and Bipedalism (Anime Individual)

Team Rocket's Meowth taught itself to speak human language (Japanese and English) fluently, walk upright like a human, and read/write, to impress a female Meowth (Meowzie). Acts as translator for other Pokémon. Also builds and operates Team Rocket mechas.

Limit: Speech is learned, not innate; Meowth sacrificed learning Pay Day to learn speech (in-universe explanation). Still cannot use many Pokémon moves effectively. Literacy limited. Other Pokémon and humans are shocked by it, confirming rarity.

Gigantamax Power - G-Max Gold Rush

Gigantamax Meowth's exclusive G-Max move. Giant coin throws produce massive gold coins that scatter on battlefield, damaging opponents and scattering money that gives extra in-game currency after battle and confuses opponents (in-game effect: scatters coins, opponent confused).

Limit: Only usable in Galar Max Raid/Dynamax battles, three turns, one use per battle. Gigantamax form is temporary battle transformation, not permanent evolution.

Evolutions - Persian and Perrserker

Kantonian Meowth evolves into Persian (Normal) at Level 28: larger, sleeker, jewel on forehead. Alolan Meowth evolves into Alolan Persian (Dark) via high friendship. Galarian Meowth evolves into Perrserker (Steel) at Level 28: Viking cat with iron headbutt. Perrserker has Steely Spirit ability boosting Steel moves.

Limit: Anime Team Rocket Meowth refuses to evolve; evolution is species-level, not mandatory for individual. Persian/Perrserker not interchangeable across forms.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kantonian (Original) Meowth: Small bipedal feline, 0.4 m (1'04") tall, 4.2 kg. Cream / sand-colored fur with brown hind paws and tail tip, whiskers, oval eyes, and a prominent oval gold koban coin embedded in forehead. Ears black-tipped, long tail, hind legs more humanoid for bipedal stance though often depicted on all fours in early sprites. Expressive face with Cheshire-like grin. Shiny variant is more golden-yellow. Alolan Meowth: Sleek, blue

Symbols — Core symbol is the koban (oval gold coin from Edo period) on forehead - direct reference to maneki-neko (beckoning cat) which traditionally holds/stands on a koban inscribed with 'one million ryo' for good fortune. Meowth embodies neko ni koban proverb: cat with money it doesn't understand. Pay Day move scatters coins that player collects as money after battle, literalizing fortune-bringing. The Stray Who Learned to Talk - Go West Young MeowthPokémon anime Episode 70 'Go West Young Meowth' (Japanese: ニャースのあいうえお) + flashbacks in later episodes

1998 (in-universe: Meowth's kittenhood in Hollywood analogue)

As a starving alley kitten in Hollywood (based on Hollywood), Meowth watches a movie and sees a female Meowth (Meowzie/Madonna) owned by a wealthy woman. To win her love, he teaches himself to walk on two legs like a human by watching humans, then learns human speech by sounding out words from a picture book and practicing pronunciation for months, enduring isolation. He returns to impress Meowzie, declaring his love in human words, but she calls him a 'freak' and rejects him for being neither human nor proper Pokémon, preferring the Persian who owns her. Heartbroken, Meowth joins Team Rocket, finding belonging with Jessie and James. The story explains why this Meowth can talk and why he hates Persians. Establishes Meowth as tragic, self-made outsider; explains his speech, bipedalism, and inferiority complex. Becomes foundational character lore referenced in dozens of later episodes (e.g., EP086 'Go West Young Meowth' sequel, Chronicles). Humanizes Team Rocket trio.

Pay Day Alley Cat - The Original Kantonian MeowthPokémon Red, Green, Blue, Yellow (1996-1998) and Pokédex entries Gen I-VIII

1996 - present (game timeline: contemporary Kanto)

In Kanto's cities, Meowth is a nocturnal urban scavenger that loves anything round and shiny. It wanders streets at night collecting coins and trinkets, sleeping in daylight. Its forehead koban is said to bring fortune but Meowth itself cannot use money. Trainers who catch it can use its Pay Day move to farm money. At Level 28 it evolves into Persian, a larger, elegant predator. The Pokédex repeatedly notes its love of coins and its habit of fighting with other Meowth over shiny objects. Defines base species biology and gameplay role as money-farming Normal-type. Inspires real-world maneki-neko merchandise tie-ins. Establishes evolution line used for 20+ years.

The Fallen Royals - Alolan Meowth's BetrayalPokémon Sun and Moon (2016), Ultra Sun/Ultra Moon, Pokémon.com lore and NPC dialogue in Alola (Route 1, Royal Avenue)

2016 (in-game: historical Alola monarchy era to present)

When Alola was a kingdom, the royal family imported Meowth from Kanto and bred them as pampered pets for their rarity and elegance. Only royalty could own them, and Meowth became arrogant and prideful, learning cunning and vanity. When the monarchy fell (after Alola's shift to island kahuna system), the Meowth were released and turned feral, becoming sly, selfish Dark-types that will do anything for profit and betray even their Trainer for money. Their eyes became half-closed with disdain, fur turned blue-grey. They evolve into Alolan Persian via friendship, but with a rounder, more smug face. Explains type change Normal->Dark as cultural adaptation, not just palette swap. Provides in-universe colonial/post-monarchy narrative unique among regional forms. Alolan Meowth becomes symbol of corrupted luxury.

Iron Beard of the North - Galarian Meowth and the Perrserker LinePokémon Sword and Shield (2019), Pokédex entries, in-game NPC on Route 4 and Galar Mine

2019 (in-game: Galar's seafaring Viking-inspired history)

Meowth that migrated to Galar with seafaring Vikings lived harsh lives on cold, iron-rich seas. To survive, their bodies hardened, fur became steel-like and grew into a bushy beard, claws and teeth sharpened, coin turned to iron. This Galarian Meowth is Steel-type, bold and savage, and no longer evolves into Persian; instead at Level 28 it evolves into Perrserker, a bipedal Viking cat with a helmet-like iron head and axe-like claws that lives for battle. Perrserker's Steely Spirit boosts allies' Steel moves, reflecting clan warfare. Creates first branching evolution for Meowth line, separating Kanto/Alola vs Galar. Explains Steel typing via environmental adaptation. Perrserker becomes competitive doubles support Pokémon.

The Long Cat - Gigantamax MeowthPokémon Sword and Shield - Gigantamax Meowth (Mystery Gift 2019-2020), official trailer and Pokédex

2019-2020 (Dynamax era)

When exposed to Dynamax energy in Galar's Power Spots, a Meowth with Gigantamax Factor transforms into Gigantamax Meowth: its body stretches to over 33 meters long, its coin swells to enormous size and glows, and its body becomes highly elastic. Its exclusive move G-Max Gold Rush showers the battlefield with giant gold coins that damage foes and confuse them while generating money. The form is temporary, lasting three turns, and is said to be inspired by the 'long cat' internet meme and the exaggerated maneki-neko statues. Meowth's personality remains playful but its size makes it a kaiju-like spectacle. Provides comedic Gigantamax form distinct from other Pokémon's bulk-ups; becomes fan-favorite meme due to absurd length. Only Meowth and Pikachu/Eevee got special long Gigantamax forms.

The Perpetual Heist - Team Rocket's Meowth and the Pikachu ChasePokémon anime (1997-2023), 1000+ episodes, starting Episode 2 'Pokémon Emergency!'

1997-2023 (Ash Ketchum era, concluded in 2023)

After joining Team Rocket under Giovanni, Meowth becomes the third member of the Jessie-James-Meowth trio, serving as strategist, translator, and mecha pilot. Their standing orders are to steal rare Pokémon, but they obsessively pursue Ash's Pikachu, believing delivering it will earn Giovanni's favor. Each episode they devise elaborate traps, disguises, and giant Meowth-shaped robots, recite the Team Rocket motto, and are inevitably blasted off again ('Team Rocket's blasting off again!'). Meowth often breaks the fourth wall, translates for other Pokémon, and provides comic relief, but also shows loyalty, sacrificing for Jessie and James. Despite being villains, they are beloved anti-heroes. Makes Meowth one of the most enduring characters in anime history (26 years, >1100 episodes, movies). Establishes Team Rocket motto and 'blasting off' as cultural touchstone. Meowth's speech becomes defining trait that elevates species popularity in games.

Every Meowth can talk like Team Rocket's Meowth. — Only the single Team Rocket Meowth learned human speech after months of study to impress Meowzie; wild and game Meowth only cry 'meow' or 'nya'. The Pokédex and games never attribute speech to the species.

Meowth's coin is just decoration or can be removed. — The koban is part of Meowth's biology, embedded in forehead, linked to Pay Day and fortune lore. It is not a held item; removing it would be like removing a body part. In some anime gags it falls off when hit, but it regrows.

Alolan and Galarian Meowth are just recolors or costumes. — They are distinct regional forms with different types (Dark, Steel), abilities, lore, and evolutions (Alolan -> Alolan Persian, Galarian -> Perrserker). They represent in-universe adaptation to Alola's royalty and Galar's harsh seas, not cosmetic skins.

Meowth is weak and useless, only good for comic relief. — In competitive play, Technician Meowth with Fake Out and high Speed has niche use, and Perrserker (its evolution) is viable in doubles with Steely Spirit. In-universe, Pay Day provides economic utility. Anime Meowth is weak in battle by choice (focused on speech), not species limit.

Meowth was intended as a playable fighter in Super Smash Bros. — Rumors circulated that Meowth was considered for Smash 64, but director Masahiro Sakurai has never confirmed this; no design documents or interviews verify it. Meowth remains a Poké Ball summon and trophy only.
