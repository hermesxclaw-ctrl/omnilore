Etymology

The name 'Omega' is the 24th and final letter of the Greek alphabet, derived from the Phoenician letter 'ayin' and meaning 'great O'. In Greek numerals, it has a value of 800. The word is often used to denote the end or the last of something, especially in the phrase 'Alpha and Omega' from the Christian Book of Revelation.

Powers & Abilities

Omega Effect

Source Version DC Comics: Darkseid (Jack Kirby's Fourth World, 1971)

Description

In DC Comics, Darkseid's Omega Effect is a powerful energy beam that can disintegrate, teleport, or erase targets from existence. It is often depicted as a pair of red eye-beams.

Limitations

The Omega Effect can be resisted by powerful beings or with the use of certain artifacts (e.g., the Anti-Life Equation).

Contradictions

The exact capabilities of the Omega Effect have varied over time and by writer.

Omega-level mutant classification

Source Version Marvel Comics: X-Men (1986, first use in Uncanny X-Men #208)

Description

In Marvel Comics, an Omega-level mutant is one whose power is deemed to have no known upper limit and could potentially affect the entire world or reality. Examples include Jean Grey (Phoenix), Iceman, and Magneto.

Limitations

The classification is not a power itself but a designation; it does not grant additional abilities.

Contradictions

The criteria for Omega-level status have been inconsistently defined and retconned.

Stories & Myths

The Alpha and Omega in Revelation

Source Book of RevelationCulture Early ChristianPeriod 1st century CE

Summary

In the Book of Revelation, God declares 'I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end' (Rev 1:8, 21:6, 22:13). This phrase emphasizes God's eternal existence and his role as the creator and consummator of all things.

Participants

God

John of Patmos (author)

Consequences

The phrase became a central confession of Christian faith, used in liturgy and art.

Variants

Some manuscripts omit the phrase in certain verses, but it is widely attested.

Omega the Unknown (Marvel Comics)

Source Marvel ComicsCulture American pop culturePeriod 1976-1977

Summary

Omega the Unknown is a silent, alien humanoid who arrives on Earth with a young boy named James-Michael Starling. Omega protects the boy from mysterious forces while struggling to understand human society. The series was created by Steve Gerber and Mary Skrenes, and was known for its unconventional narrative.

Participants

Omega

James-Michael Starling

The Foolkiller

Consequences

The series was cancelled after 10 issues, but the character gained a cult following and was later revived in a 2007 mini-series.

Variants

The 2007 revival by Jonathan Lethem and Karl Rusnak reimagined the character and setting.

Darkseid's Omega Effect (DC Comics)

Source DC ComicsCulture American pop culturePeriod 1971-present

Summary

Darkseid, a cosmic tyrant, uses his Omega Effect to eliminate enemies. The effect is often depicted as a pair of red beams from his eyes that can disintegrate or teleport victims. The Omega Effect is one of Darkseid's signature powers.

Participants
