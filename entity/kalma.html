Who It Is

Kalma (from Finnish 'kalma,' meaning 'stench of a corpse' or 'grave') is the Finnish personification of the grave and the resting place of the dead. She is the daughter of Tuoni (god of death) and Tuonetar (queen of the underworld), presiding specifically over the physical grave rather than the journey of the soul. Kalma embodies the cold, silent, decayed realm of the cemetery. She is described as a pale or decaying figure who sits atop burial mounds and guards the remains of the dead against disturbance. In Finnish folk tradition, graves were called 'Kalman kammio' (Kalma's chambers), and disturbing a burial was considered an offense against Kalma herself. Offerings were left at graves for Kalma to ensure the dead rested peacefully. Unlike Surma (violent death), Kalma is neutral — she is not a punisher but simply the natural state of decomposition and return to earth. The name appears in Finnish place names — Kalmari, Kalmisto, Kalmasuo — indicating sites of ancient burials. The power of Kalma could be invoked in curses: 'May Kalma take you' was a potent malediction meaning 'May you die and rot forgotten.'
