Who It Is

Mama Cocha ('Mother Sea') is the Inca goddess of the ocean — the immense, life-giving salt water that borders the western edge of Tawantinsuyu. While the Inca heartland was highland (Cusco sits at 3,400 m), the empire extended along the entire Peruvian coast, and Mama Cocha was the feminine personification of the Pacific. She was the source of the marine life that fed coastal populations, and fishermen made offerings of coca, shells (spondylus shells from Ecuador were especially precious), and miniature balsa-wood boats to ensure her favor. The Incas conceptualized the ocean as a living entity that 'breathed' with the tides and could be angered by disrespect. Mama Cocha was also the mother of the coastal peoples, distinct from Pachamama (who governed the earth) but complementary: earth and sea, the highlands and the coast, were the twin domains of these two mothers.
