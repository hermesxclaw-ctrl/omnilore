Who It Is

Quetzalcoatl, in his Toltec form as Ce Acatl Topiltzin, is the legendary priest-king of Tollan (Tula), the capital of the Toltec Empire. He is both a historical human ruler and a divine figure — a culture hero who brought the arts of civilization, forbade human sacrifice, and ruled justly until he was corrupted by his enemies and exiled. His departure and promised return became one of the foundational stories of Mesoamerican civilization.

Cultural Significance

The story of Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl is the most important historical-legendary narrative in Mesoamerican culture. The Toltecs were regarded by the Aztecs as the pinnacle of civilization — artists, craftsmen, and wise rulers — and Quetzalcoatl was the archetypal Toltec. The Aztec emperors claimed descent from the Toltec rulers of Tollan. The site of Tula (Hidalgo, Mexico) with its famous Atlantean warrior columns is believed by many to be the Tollan of legend. The prophecy of Quetzalcoatl's return in a Ce Acatl year (which recurred every 52 years) directly intersected with the arrival of Cortés in 1519 (which was a Ce Acatl year), shaping the course of the Spanish conquest.
