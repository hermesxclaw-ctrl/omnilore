Who It Is

Qareen (Arabic: 'constant companion') is the personal spirit attached to every human being in Islamic theology — a jinn or spirit that accompanies a person from birth to death. In the Quran, the qareen is described as the recording angel's counterpart: the angel records good deeds while the qareen is associated with the human's lower tendencies and temptations. At the Day of Judgment, each person's qareen will testify against them, as in Surah 50: 'And his companion (qareen) will say: This is that which I have ready (to testify).' In popular Islamic belief (influenced by earlier Arabian concepts), the qareen is every person's double — a jinn born at the same moment as the human, sharing their life and fortune, and influencing their character. If the person is righteous, their qareen is neutralized or even converted to Islam; if they are sinful, the qareen leads them further astray. The qareen explains the feeling of being watched when alone, the nagging inner voice of doubt or temptation, and the experience of déjà vu. A person's qareen is also vulnerable: in some traditions, sneezing is an expulsion of the qareen's influence, and saying 'Alhamdulillah' (Praise be to God) after a sneeze wards off the qareen's interference. The qareen concept shares features with the Roman genius, the Greek daimon, and the Egyptian ka — the idea that each human has a spiritual double that shadows their life from first breath to final judgment.
