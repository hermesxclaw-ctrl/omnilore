Who It Is

In the unseen architecture of the Islamic cosmos, every soul is shadowed by a constant companion—a qareen—a spirit double that whispers, tempts, and testifies. This silent partner, born of jinn or angel, walks with you from the womb to the grave, and in some traditions, even beyond. Is it your other self, a demonic tempter, or a guardian? The answer shifts across scripture, theology, and folk belief, revealing a profound and often unsettling relationship between the human and the spiritual.

The qareen is a constant companion, whispering suggestions to the human. It can be a devil (shaytan) that tempts towards evil, or an angel that counsels good. In folklore, it may be jealous and cause harm, especially to pregnant women and brides. The qareen of holy men may persist after death.

Powers
Whispering (waswās)

The qareen influences the human by whispering suggestions, either evil (from the shaytan) or good (from the angel).

Limit: Cannot compel; the human has free will. The qareen of Muhammad submitted to Islam and only whispered good.

Temptation

The qareen (when a devil) leads humans astray, encouraging sin and denial of truth.

Limit: Only with God's permission, as a test of faith.

Shape-shifting (folk belief)

In Egyptian folk belief, the qareen may transform into a cat or dog at night.

Limit: Not mentioned in classical sources.

Testimony on Judgment Day

The qareen will testify against the human on the Day of Judgment (Surah Qaf 50:23).

Limit: Exact nature of testimony debated.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The qareen is generally invisible, being a spirit. In folk belief, it may manifest as a cat or dog at night, especially in Egypt. There is no canonical description of its form.

Symbols — No standard iconography. In Islamic art, spirits are rarely depicted. Amulets and rituals involving the number seven are associated with protection against the qareen.

Domains — The qareen accompanies a specific human from birth (or conception) throughout life. It is not tied to a location but to a person.

Accounts I
Muhammad's Qareen Submits to IslamSahih Muslim

7th century CE

The Prophet Muhammad stated that every person has a qareen from the jinn. When asked if this included him, he said yes, but that God helped him subdue his qareen, who then converted to Islam. Thereafter, the qareen only whispered good to him. Establishes the qareen as a spiritual companion that can be reformed. Muhammad is seen as achieving insan-kamil (the perfect human), to whom the shaytan bows.

Accounts II
The Qareen in the Mu'allaqatMu'allaqat of Amr bin Kulthum

6th century CE

In his famous poem, Amr bin Kulthum uses 'qareen' metaphorically: 'every tribe has taken fear of us as a qareen,' meaning that their fear is a constant companion. Shows the pre-Islamic usage of the word as 'companion' without spiritual connotation.
