Archive Dossier — Identity

Qareen

Pronunciation ka-REEN (Arabic: qarīn)

Etymology

From the Arabic root q-r-n (ق ر ن), meaning 'to be joined' or 'to accompany'. The word qareen literally means 'constant companion' or 'one who is joined'.

Cultural Ownership

Islamic tradition, with roots in pre-Islamic Arabian and broader Near Eastern beliefs. Also present in Muslim cultures across the Middle East, North Africa, Turkey, Russia, and South Asia.

Disputed Identifications

The qareen is sometimes confused with the qarinah, a female childbed demon. Some scholars identify the qareen as a separate jinn, while others see it as an aspect of the self. The Quranic term is not always spiritual, and its demonic association is a later development.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the unseen architecture of the Islamic cosmos, every soul is shadowed by a constant companion—a qareen—a spirit double that whispers, tempts, and testifies. This silent partner, born of jinn or angel, walks with you from the womb to the grave, and in some traditions, even beyond. Is it your other self, a demonic tempter, or a guardian? The answer shifts across scripture, theology, and folk belief, revealing a profound and often unsettling relationship between the human and the spiritual.

Your qareen was born the same moment you were born, and on the Day of Judgment, every secret you kept, your shadow knows.

Who It Is

Qareen (Arabic: 'constant companion') is the personal spirit attached to every human being in Islamic theology — a jinn or spirit that accompanies a person from birth to death. In the Quran, the qareen is described as the recording angel's counterpart: the angel records good deeds while the qareen is associated with the human's lower tendencies and temptations. At the Day of Judgment, each person's qareen will testify against them, as in Surah 50: 'And his companion (qareen) will say: This is that which I have ready (to testify).' In popular Islamic belief (influenced by earlier Arabian concepts), the qareen is every person's double — a jinn born at the same moment as the human, sharing their life and fortune, and influencing their character. If the person is righteous, their qareen is neutralized or even converted to Islam; if they are sinful, the qareen leads them further astray. The qareen explains the feeling of being watched when alone, the nagging inner voice of doubt or temptation, and the experience of déjà vu. A person's qareen is also vulnerable: in some traditions, sneezing is an expulsion of the qareen's influence, and saying 'Alhamdulillah' (Praise be to God) after a sneeze wards off the qareen's interference. The qareen concept shares features with the Roman genius, the Greek daimon, and the Egyptian ka — the idea that each human has a spiritual double that shadows their life from first breath to final judgment.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

The qareen is generally invisible, being a spirit. In folk belief, it may manifest as a cat or dog at night, especially in Egypt. There is no canonical description of its form.

Symbols & Iconography

No standard iconography. In Islamic art, spirits are rarely depicted. Amulets and rituals involving the number seven are associated with protection against the qareen.

Behaviour & Personality

The qareen is a constant companion, whispering suggestions to the human. It can be a devil (shaytan) that tempts towards evil, or an angel that counsels good. In folklore, it may be jealous and cause harm, especially to pregnant women and brides. The qareen of holy men may persist after death.

Domains & Habitats

The qareen accompanies a specific human from birth (or conception) throughout life. It is not tied to a location but to a person.

Version Differences

In the Quran, the term qareen often refers to an earthly companion or a human tempter, not a spirit. In hadith, it is clearly a spiritual companion from the jinn. In later folklore, it becomes a personal doppelgänger. Some scholars (e.g., Ibn Hanbal) hold that there are two qareens: one from devils and one from angels. Others, like Al-Uthaymin, see it as an evil jinn. The concept of the qareen as an inner self or 'other self' is a later interpretation.

Attested in Quran (Surah 50:23–27, 'his qareen'); hadith (Sahih Muslim); Islamic eschatologyEra Pre-Islamic (concept of the double) through Islamic theology

Archive Dossier — Powers

Whispering (waswās)

Source Version Hadith (Tirmidhi, Muslim)

Description

The qareen influences the human by whispering suggestions, either evil (from the shaytan) or good (from the angel).

Limitations

Cannot compel; the human has free will. The qareen of Muhammad submitted to Islam and only whispered good.

Contradictions

Some scholars see the qareen as a separate being, others as an aspect of the self.

Temptation

Source Version Quran (43:36, 4:38), Al-Uthaymin's interpretation

Description

The qareen (when a devil) leads humans astray, encouraging sin and denial of truth.

Limitations

Only with God's permission, as a test of faith.

Contradictions

The Quranic verses may refer to human tempters, not spirits.

Shape-shifting (folk belief)

Source Version Folk tradition, Egypt

Description

In Egyptian folk belief, the qareen may transform into a cat or dog at night.

Limitations

Not mentioned in classical sources.

Contradictions

None.

Testimony on Judgment Day

Source Version Quran 50:23

Description

The qareen will testify against the human on the Day of Judgment (Surah Qaf 50:23).

Limitations

Exact nature of testimony debated.

Contradictions

Suyuti is unsure whether the qareen in 50:23 is a devil or an angel.

Some traditions say it is present in the womb; others say it appears at birth.

Shaytan (Satan/Devil)

Description

The qareen is often identified with a devil (shaytan) that tempts towards evil.
