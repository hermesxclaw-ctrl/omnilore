Who It Is

The cherubim are a fascinating and complex entity, with a rich history and significance in various cultures and traditions. Explore the stories and symbolism surrounding the cherubim to gain a deeper understanding of their importance.

Cherubim are often depicted as guardians of the throne of God, protecting the entrance to the Garden of Eden and serving as messengers between God and humanity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Cherubim are often depicted as hybrid creatures with the body of a lion, the wings of an eagle, the face of a man, and the tail of a serpent. They are sometimes shown with multiple faces, each representing a different aspect of their nature.

Symbols — Cherubim are often associated with the symbols of the lion, eagle, and serpent, which represent strength, courage, and wisdom.

Domains — Cherubim are often associated with the heavenly realm, but may also be found in the Garden of Eden and other sacred spaces.

Accounts I
The Fall of ManGenesis 3

Ancient Near East

The story of the Fall of Man describes how Adam and Eve were expelled from the Garden of Eden by the cherubim. The expulsion from the Garden of Eden led to the loss of innocence and the introduction of sin into the world.

Accounts II
The Cherubim and the Ark of the CovenantExodus 25:18-22

Ancient Near East

The story of the cherubim and the Ark of the Covenant describes how the cherubim were placed on the Ark to serve as guardians of the covenant between God and the Israelites. The placement of the cherubim on the Ark served as a symbol of the covenant between God and the Israelites.
