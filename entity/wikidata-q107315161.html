Etymology

The term 'sage' originates from the Latin 'salvia' (from 'salvare', to save or heal), referring to the herb's medicinal properties. As a philosophical term, it derives from the Latin 'sapiens' (wise).

Cultural Ownership

The term is not owned by a single culture; it is used globally. In philosophy, it refers to a wise person in classical traditions. In Hinduism, 'rishi' is translated as 'sage'.

Disputed Identifications

The term 'sage' is ambiguous, referring to multiple unrelated concepts. There is no dispute about its meaning in each context, but its broad usage can cause confusion.

Description

Appearance

As a plant, common sage (Salvia officinalis) is a small evergreen subshrub with gray-green leaves and purple flowers. As a color, sage is a grey-green hue resembling dried sage leaves. As a person, the term refers to a wise old man or philosopher.

Symbols & Iconography

In art and culture, a sage is often depicted as an elderly bearded man in robes, holding a staff or book, symbolizing wisdom. The herb sage is associated with healing and culinary use.

Behaviour & Personality

A sage in philosophy is someone who has attained wisdom, known for sound judgment and insight. In fiction, sage characters often serve as mentors or advisors.

Domains & Habitats

Common sage is native to the Mediterranean region and grows in dry, sunny habitats. As a concept, sages are found in philosophical traditions worldwide, including Greek, Indian, and Chinese thought.

Version Differences

The term has multiple distinct meanings: a herb, a color, a philosophical archetype, a fictional character, a software, an organization, etc. Each version is unrelated to the others.
