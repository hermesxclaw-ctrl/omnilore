Who It Is

The Balor is D&D's most powerful non-unique demon — a towering, winged horror that stands 12 feet tall and is wreathed in flames that burn with the hatred of the Abyss itself. In its earliest incarnation, D&D called this creature a 'Balrog' — a direct borrowing from Tolkien's Lord of the Rings (the fire-demon that Gandalf fights in Moria). When the Tolkien Estate threatened legal action over this and other borrowed names (including hobbits, ents, and Nazgûl), TSR renamed the creature 'Balor,' after the one-eyed giant-king of Irish mythology — and the Balor became a distinctly D&D entity in its own right.

Physically, a Balor is a demon of classical proportions: a muscular humanoid body with deep red-black skin, great leathery bat-wings spanning 20 feet, curling ram-like horns, and a face contorted into a permanent snarl of rage. It wields two weapons simultaneously: a many-thonged whip of living flame and a massive sword that crackles with lightning — both capable of cutting through magical defenses and dealing damage that resists healing. Its entire body radiates an aura of heat so intense that merely standing near a Balor can set flammable objects alight, and when a Balor is finally slain, it does not simply die — it explodes in a death throes of apocalyptic fire damage to everything within 100 feet, its hatred so absolute that it seeks to destroy its killer even in death.

Balors serve as generals in the demonic legions of the Abyss, commanding armies of lesser demons in the eternal Blood War against the devils of the Nine Hells and in the chaotic power struggles among demon lords. Each Balor is a master strategist of chaos — though they answer to demon lords like Demogorgon or Orcus, every Balor simultaneously schemes toward its own ascension. A successful Balor who amasses enough souls, territory, and power can transform into a unique demon lord, earning its own layer of the Abyss.

The Balor's design has been enormously influential. It established the 'fire demon' archetype in fantasy gaming, and its appearance — horned, winged, flame-wreathed humanoid — has become one of the most recognizable demonic images in popular culture. It appears across D&D video games (Baldur's Gate 2 famously features a Balor summoned as a boss enemy), and its transformation from 'legally-actionable Tolkien derivative' to 'original D&D icon' is a landmark case in gaming's evolution from derivative to original fantasy. Despite the name change, veteran players still sometimes refer to Balors as 'Balrogs' — a testament to their shared origin.
