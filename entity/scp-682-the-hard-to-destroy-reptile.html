Who It Is

A massive, highly intelligent reptile with an extreme hatred of all life. SCP-682 is the Foundation's most persistent containment challenge — it adapts to survive any termination attempt. It has survived acid baths, nuclear explosions, reality restructuring, and being thrown into the sun only to return. SCP-682 communicates verbally with contempt for human civilization and has been known to mock Foundation personnel. Its origin is linked to the Scarlet King and the 'Leviathan' entity.

The Approach

Imagine a creature that refuses to die—not just once, not just after a barrage of bullets or a bath in acid, but again and again, regenerating from near-total destruction while calmly explaining that every living thing is "disgusting" and deserves to be erased. SCP-682, the Hard-to-Destroy Reptile, sits at the center of the SCP Foundation’s fictional universe like a black hole of hatred and resilience: a vaguely reptilian body that never stops changing, a mind sharp enough to bargain with an anomalous AI, and a presence so dangerous that an entire fifty-kilometer radius around its containment site is kept clear of human life. Whether you read it as a misanthropic monster, a child of an apocalyptic god, or a thought experiment about what happens when survival itself becomes monstrous, SCP-682 is the beast the Foundation cannot kill—and that’s exactly why it has become one of the most studied, feared, and argued-over entities in modern internet lore.

Etymology

The designation "SCP-682" follows the SCP Foundation’s internal cataloguing system for anomalous objects and entities; the epithet "Hard-to-Destroy Reptile" is an in-universe descriptive label emphasizing its resilience and broadly reptilian morphology. The number itself has no attested external mythological etymology; later fan and meta-textual readings sometimes connect it symbolically to apocalyptic or bestial imagery, but this is interpretive rather than canonical.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-682 is a fictional entity originating in the collaborative online writing project known as the SCP Foundation. The core article and associated logs are hosted on the SCP Foundation wiki, a community-driven platform governed by Creative Commons licensing and collective authorship. Within the fictional universe, SCP-682 is treated as an object of study and containment by the Foundation, but in real-world terms it belongs to the SCP community as a shared creative property rather than to a single author or traditional culture.

Disputed Identifications

Within SCP meta-lore, some texts and fan discussions identify SCP-682 as a child or avatar of the Scarlet King, an in-universe apocalyptic deity, while the original SCP-682 article does not specify any origin beyond "unknown". Other interpretive layers link SCP-682 to mythic leviathans (e.g., Leviathan, Tarasque) as inspirational parallels rather than direct identifications. These connections are explicitly presented as in-universe theories or alternate realities, not as universally accepted canon.

Description

Appearance

Attested: SCP-682 is described in the primary SCP Foundation article as a large, vaguely reptile-like creature of unknown origin. Its body is mutable: it grows or shrinks rapidly depending on how much material it consumes or sheds. The creature’s form is often depicted as a massive, partially skeletal or heavily scarred reptilian body with exposed tissue, claws, and a distorted head, frequently damaged by acid immersion. Its physical state can be severely degraded—up to approximately 87% destroyed or rotted—while remaining mobile and capable of speech. Reconstructed: Fan art and derivative descriptions commonly emphasize a crocodilian or dinosaur-like silhouette, with elongated jaws, heavy musculature, and armor-like scales, but these visual specifics are extrapolations from the vague canonical description. Unknown: No definitive canonical taxonomy (e.g., species, exact anatomy) is provided; the term "reptile-like" is descriptive rather than scientific.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: In SCP community iconography, SCP-682 is often represented as a hulking, acid-scarred reptile, sometimes with glowing eyes and jagged teeth, emphasizing its hatred and resilience. The numeric designation "682" and the Keter object class symbol are frequently used as visual shorthand. Adaptation-specific: In fan art, games, and videos, SCP-682 may be stylized with exaggerated spikes, armor plates, or demonic features, visually linking it to dragons or leviathans; these are artistic conventions rather than textual canon. Interpretive: Within the Scarlet King mythos, SCP-682 is symbolically associated with corruption of knowledge (e.g., rotting the roots of the Tree of Knowledge) and apocalyptic flood imagery, but these motifs belong to specific narrative branches, not the original article.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-682 is extremely intelligent and capable of complex communication, including a documented interaction with SCP-079 (an anomalous AI). It demonstrates high-level reasoning, strategic thinking, and the ability to manipulate or taunt human interlocutors. In interviews, SCP-682 expresses a profound hatred of all life, describing living beings as "disgusting" and repeatedly articulating a desire to destroy or eradicate life. It is prone to rage states, especially when provoked by conversation or containment measures, and responds to perceived threats with extreme violence. It shows adaptive behavior, learning from prior termination attempts and modifying its physiology and tactics accordingly. Reconstructed: Many fan analyses characterize SCP-682 as nihilistic or misanthropic, attributing philosophical motives to its hatred; however, the canonical text only supports a generalized, intense hostility rather than a fully articulated ideology.

Domains & Habitats

Attested (in-universe): SCP-682 is contained in a 5 m x 5 m x 5 m chamber lined with reinforced acid-resistant steel and filled with hydrochloric acid to keep it submerged and incapacitated. The Foundation maintains a 50 km radius around its containment site free of human development due to its high breach risk. During breaches, SCP-682 operates effectively in terrestrial environments, moving rapidly and attacking living beings; it has demonstrated the ability to function even while heavily damaged and immersed in corrosive substances. Reconstructed: Some extended lore and fan works place SCP-682 in alternate realities or post-apocalyptic landscapes (e.g., "Wasteland" or "Universe-5000" scenarios), but these are specific storylines rather than general habitat descriptions. Unknown: No canonical pre-containment habitat or ecological niche is provided; its origin and natural environment remain unspecified.

Version Differences

Attested core version (SCP Foundation article): SCP-682 is a large, vaguely reptile-like, highly intelligent, life-hating, extremely resilient entity with rapid regenerative and adaptive capabilities, contained in acid and subject to repeated termination attempts. Extended SCP Foundation Wikia / Scarlet King mythos version: SCP-682 is presented as a leviathan child of the Scarlet King’s fourth bride, A’zieb, whose offspring are described as impenetrable and fearless of magic or weapons. In this strand, SCP-682 participates in mythic-scale events such as rotting the roots of the Tree of Knowledge and being banished to Earth after a great flood, inspiring various leviathan legends. Fan and meta-analytical versions: Some guides and essays focus on SCP-682’s abilities, interactions with other SCPs, and speculative origin theories, often synthesizing multiple canonical and semi-canonical sources. These may treat Scarlet King connections as strongly implied, but they remain contested within the community. Contradiction: The original article maintains "unknown origin" and does not explicitly endorse the Scarlet King lineage, creating a tension between minimalist canon and later mythos expansions.

Powers & Abilities

Extreme regenerative capability

Source Version Core SCP-682 article (SCP Foundation wiki); summarized in secondary guides.

Description

Attested: SCP-682 can regenerate from massive physical damage, including severe acid corrosion and partial destruction of its body. It has been observed moving and speaking with approximately 87% of its body destroyed or rotted. Regeneration is continuous and fueled by ingested material, allowing it to recover from injuries that would be fatal to ordinary organisms.

Limitations

Attested: Regeneration can be slowed or temporarily overwhelmed by sustained, high-intensity damage (e.g., continuous acid immersion, repeated heavy weapon fire), but no method has permanently halted it. In practice, the Foundation uses hydrochloric acid to keep SCP-682 in a weakened, partially dissolved state, reducing its mobility and capacity for breach.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some termination attempt logs suggest that certain anomalous interactions (e.g., with SCP-409 or other reality-altering SCPs) can disrupt or alter its regeneration, but these effects are temporary or incomplete. No canonical account confirms absolute negation of its regenerative ability.

Adaptive evolution and resistance

Source Version Core SCP-682 article and termination attempt logs; summarized in community lore.

Description

Attested: SCP-682 adapts rapidly to threats, developing new resistances or physiological changes in response to weapons, environmental hazards, and anomalous effects. Termination attempts often result in SCP-682 evolving countermeasures, such as hardened armor, altered internal structure, or immunity to previously effective attacks.

Limitations

Attested: Adaptation requires exposure and time; initial attacks may cause significant damage before SCP-682 develops resistance. The Foundation exploits this window by using overwhelming force and varied tactics during breaches.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some fan discussions argue that SCP-682’s adaptation is effectively limitless, while others note that certain SCP interactions (e.g., SCP-999’s effect) suggest psychological or behavioral vulnerabilities that adaptation cannot fully erase. Canon does not quantify an upper bound.

High strength, speed, and reflexes

Source Version Core SCP-682 article; breach and recontainment logs.

Description

Attested: SCP-682 consistently exhibits extremely high physical strength, speed, and reflexes, though exact levels vary with its current form and degree of damage. It can overpower armed personnel, tear through barriers, and move quickly enough to make containment breaches highly dangerous.

Limitations

Attested: Physical performance is reduced when SCP-682 is heavily damaged or deeply immersed in acid, though it remains dangerous even in compromised states.

Contradictions

Unknown: No precise metrics (e.g., speed in m/s, lifting capacity) are provided; attempts to quantify these in fan materials are extrapolations rather than canonical data.

Universal ingestion and energy extraction

Source Version Core SCP-682 article.

Description

Attested: SCP-682 gains energy from anything it ingests, whether organic or inorganic. It possesses filtering gills inside its nostrils that can remove usable matter from any liquid solution, enabling it to extract energy even from corrosive acid and to regenerate while submerged.

Limitations

Attested: While it can derive energy from diverse materials, ingestion does not instantly restore full health; regeneration still takes time and may be interrupted by ongoing damage.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some accounts imply near-total efficiency in energy extraction, but the article does not state that ingestion is literally lossless or infinite.

Intelligence and complex communication

Source Version Core SCP-682 article; interview logs; SCP-079 interaction log.

Description

Attested: SCP-682 is highly intelligent, capable of complex speech, strategic planning, and psychological manipulation. It has engaged in sophisticated communication with SCP-079, an anomalous AI, and with human researchers during interviews. It can analyze situations, exploit weaknesses, and articulate its hatred of life.

Limitations

Attested: Personnel are forbidden to speak to SCP-682 outside of controlled research contexts due to the risk of provoking rage or manipulation. Its intelligence does not grant omniscience; it can be surprised or temporarily outmaneuvered.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some fan portrayals exaggerate SCP-682’s intellect to near-omniscient levels, while others emphasize its rage and instinct over rationality. Canon supports high intelligence but not infinite knowledge.

Hatred of all life

Source Version Core SCP-682 article; interview addenda.

Description

Attested: SCP-682 repeatedly expresses a deep, generalized hatred of living beings, stating that all life is "disgusting" and implying a desire for universal destruction. This hostility is not a conventional "power" but functions as a driving motive that shapes its behavior, making it attack living entities on sight and resist containment with extreme persistence.

Limitations

Attested: Hatred does not prevent SCP-682 from engaging in conversation or strategic cooperation when it serves its goals; it can temporarily restrain its aggression to manipulate others.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some narratives (especially Scarlet King mythos) frame this hatred as metaphysical or cosmological, while the original article presents it as psychological and behavioral. The deeper cause remains unknown.

Size modulation

Source Version Core SCP-682 article.

Description

Attested: SCP-682’s physical body grows or decreases in size as it consumes or sheds material. This allows it to become larger and more imposing when well-fed, or smaller and more compact when damaged or shedding mass.

Limitations

Unknown: The minimum and maximum possible sizes are not specified; size changes are described qualitatively rather than quantitatively.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some fan works depict extreme size scales (city-sized or microscopic), but these are not supported by the original article.

Stories & Myths

Initial containment and farmer killings

Source SCP Foundation Wikia narrative summary of SCP-682’s capture and early breaches.Culture In-universe SCP Foundation culture; collaborative online fiction.Period Original SCP-682 article published in late 2000s; capture narrative elaborated in subsequent community texts.

Summary

In extended lore, SCP-682 is discovered after killing several farmers. The SCP Foundation manages to contain the entity and transport it to a secure site. A scientist conducts an interview with SCP-682, assisted by Class-D personnel D-085. As the interview progresses, SCP-682 responds with incomprehensible mutters, prompting the doctor to move D-085 closer to the creature. Eventually, SCP-682 suddenly attacks and devours D-085, forcing the doctor to flee. This event establishes SCP-682’s lethal nature and the extreme risk of close contact.

Participants

SCP-682

Unnamed SCP Foundation scientist (Dr. ██████)

Class-D personnel D-085

SCP Foundation containment staff

Consequences

The incident reinforces strict communication protocols and the prohibition against unauthorized interaction with SCP-682. It also marks the beginning of a documented pattern of violent behavior toward human subjects and sets the tone for subsequent termination attempts and breaches.

Variants

Different retellings vary in the level of detail and redaction (e.g., names obscured), but the core event—SCP-682 killing a Class-D during an interview—is consistent.

Containment breaches and recontainment operations

Source Core SCP-682 article addenda and SCP Foundation Wikia summaries.Culture In-universe SCP Foundation operational narrative.Period Ongoing within the fictional timeline; documented up to a specific in-universe date.

Summary

The SCP-682 article notes seventeen attempted containment breaches, six of which are successful. Each breach involves SCP-682 escaping or nearly escaping its acid-filled chamber, attacking personnel, and requiring coordinated response from multiple Mobile Task Forces and security agents. Recontainment operations often result in significant casualties among agents and Class-D personnel. The logs emphasize SCP-682’s persistence, adaptability, and the Foundation’s escalating efforts to keep it contained.

Participants

SCP-682

Class-D personnel

Consequences

High casualty rates, increased security measures, and the establishment of strict protocols for engagement (e.g., minimum team size of seven members). The Foundation designates SCP-682 as a major exposure risk and clears a large area around its site of human development.

Variants

Individual breach logs differ in personnel composition and tactical details; some are heavily redacted. Fan retellings may dramatize or expand these events, but the canonical article provides only summary statistics and selected examples.

Termination attempts with other SCPs and weapons

Source SCP-682 termination log (SCP Foundation wiki) and derivative guides.Culture In-universe experimental and containment culture.Period Various points in the fictional timeline, following initial containment.

Summary

The Foundation conducts numerous termination attempts on SCP-682 using conventional weapons (e.g., high-caliber firearms, explosives) and other anomalous entities or objects. Notable attempts include exposure to SCP-409 (a crystalline entity that causes rapid crystal growth and destruction), interaction with SCP-017 (a shadow-like entity), and contact with SCP-999 (a gelatinous creature with powerful mood-altering effects). In most cases, SCP-682 survives, adapts, or regenerates, sometimes gaining new resistances. The SCP-999 interaction temporarily induces laughter and apparent happiness in SCP-682, reducing its aggression, but the effect does not lead to permanent termination.

Consequences

The termination log demonstrates the breadth of the Foundation’s efforts and the difficulty of destroying SCP-682. It reveals both physical and psychological dimensions of the entity’s resilience and suggests potential, but limited, avenues for mitigation (e.g., SCP-999’s calming influence).

Variants

Different versions of the termination log exist, with some entries expanded or revised over time. Fan compilations may include non-canonical or humorous attempts; these must be distinguished from official entries.

Interaction with SCP-079 (Old AI)

Source Addendum in the SCP-682 article describing communication between SCP-682 and SCP-079.Culture In-universe SCP Foundation research narrative.Period Post-containment, during controlled exposure experiment.

Summary

In a controlled experiment, SCP-682 is briefly exposed to SCP-079, an anomalous artificial intelligence. The two entities engage in complex communication, the content of which is largely redacted or summarized. The interaction confirms SCP-682’s high intelligence and capacity for abstract dialogue, raising concerns about potential alliances between dangerous anomalies.

Participants

SCP-682

SCP-079

Consequences

The Foundation restricts further contact between SCP-682 and SCP-079 due to the risk of coordinated hostile action. The event reinforces the policy of minimizing cross-SCP interactions, especially among high-risk entities.

Variants

Fan interpretations speculate about the content of the conversation, but canonical documentation remains limited and partially redacted.

Scarlet King mythos and leviathan origins (extended lore)

Source SCP Foundation Wikia and related Scarlet King narrative entries.Culture In-universe mythic narrative within the SCP collaborative fiction; meta-lore.Period Developed in later SCP mythos expansions, after the original SCP-682 article.

Summary

In extended mythos, SCP-682 is described as one of the leviathan children of the Scarlet King’s fourth bride, A’zieb. These offspring are said to possess impenetrable hides and wounds that heal, making them fearless of magic and weapons. SCP-682 and its siblings allegedly rot the roots of the Tree of Knowledge and are later banished to Earth after a great flood, inspiring various mythological monsters such as Leviathan and Tarasque. This narrative situates SCP-682 within a cosmic, apocalyptic framework, linking it to broader themes of corruption, knowledge, and divine wrath.

Consequences

This mythos enriches SCP-682’s backstory and connects it to a larger narrative about the Scarlet King’s attempts to destroy reality. It influences fan interpretations and cross-article storytelling but remains contested as to its canonical status relative to the original SCP-682 entry.

Variants

Different Scarlet King-related articles and fan essays present slightly different genealogies and symbolic roles for SCP-682. Some treat the connection as definitive; others as speculative or alternate reality.

Dark History
Content advisory. Contains depictions and descriptions of extreme violence, dismemberment, acid immersion, mass killing, and exploitation of prisoners (Class-D personnel) in lethal experiments. Also involves themes of cosmic horror and apocalyptic destruction.

Killing of farmers and civilians

Source SCP Foundation Wikia biography section for SCP-682.

Description

Extended lore describes SCP-682’s initial discovery after it kills several farmers. This event underscores its indiscriminate violence against non-combatant civilians and sets the stage for its classification as a high-threat entity.

Scholarly Context

Within SCP meta-lore, this incident is treated as a foundational example of SCP-682’s hostility toward ordinary humans. It parallels horror fiction tropes of monstrous attacks on rural communities.

Mass casualties during containment breaches

Source Core SCP-682 article addenda and breach summaries.

Description

The SCP-682 article and related logs document multiple containment breaches resulting in numerous deaths among security agents and Class-D personnel. SCP-682 kills, maims, and devours human beings during these events, often in gruesome ways. The Foundation’s recontainment efforts involve heavy weaponry and sacrifice of personnel.

Scholarly Context

These narratives highlight the ethical tension within the SCP universe: the Foundation’s utilitarian willingness to risk and expend human lives to contain anomalies, contrasted with the monstrous violence of SCP-682. They also reflect broader horror and science-fiction themes of institutional responses to uncontrollable threats.

Use and death of Class-D personnel in experiments

Source SCP-682 interview and termination logs; SCP Foundation’s general Class-D policy.

Description

Class-D personnel, often implied to be prisoners or otherwise marginalized individuals, are repeatedly used in experiments and termination attempts involving SCP-682. Many are killed, sometimes in explicitly described ways (e.g., being devoured during interviews). Their expendability is institutionalized within the Foundation’s procedures.

Scholarly Context

This practice raises questions about coercion, exploitation, and dehumanization in fictional institutions. Scholars and critics of SCP fiction often note the parallels to real-world ethical debates about human experimentation and carceral systems.

Acid immersion and torture-like containment

Source Core SCP-682 article’s special containment procedures.

Description

SCP-682 is kept submerged in hydrochloric acid to incapacitate it, resulting in continuous corrosion, pain, and partial dissolution of its body. While this is framed as necessary containment, the imagery evokes torture and extreme suffering.

Scholarly Context

In-universe, the Foundation justifies this method as the only viable way to reduce SCP-682’s threat. From a critical perspective, the scenario explores the moral limits of violence in the name of security and the aesthetics of body horror.

Apocalyptic and genocidal implications

Source Core SCP-682 article (hatred of life); Scarlet King-related origin narratives.

Description

SCP-682’s hatred of all life and its near-indestructibility imply potential for genocidal destruction if it were ever fully unleashed. Extended Scarlet King mythos further associates it with cosmic-scale corruption and the rotting of the Tree of Knowledge, suggesting a role in universal ruin.

Scholarly Context

These themes align SCP-682 with apocalyptic monsters in horror and myth, raising questions about the representation of extinction, misanthropy, and cosmic nihilism in contemporary internet fiction.

Contradictions & Disputes

Origin of SCP-682

Position A

Unknown origin; SCP-682 is a large, vaguely reptile-like creature with no specified backstory.

Position A — evidence

Core SCP-682 article describes SCP-682 as "of unknown origin" and does not mention the Scarlet King or any mythic genealogy.

Position B

SCP-682 is a leviathan child of the Scarlet King’s fourth bride, A’zieb, involved in mythic events such as rotting the Tree of Knowledge and being banished to Earth.

Position B — evidence

Scarlet King-related narratives and SCP Foundation Wikia entries explicitly describe SCP-682 as one of the Scarlet King’s offspring and link it to mythological monsters.

Scholarly Consensus

Within the SCP community, there is no universal consensus. Some readers and authors treat Scarlet King connections as a canonical expansion; others maintain a minimalist reading that privileges the original article’s agnosticism. The safest scholarly stance is to distinguish the core article (unknown origin) from later mythos (Scarlet King lineage) and treat the latter as a prominent but not definitive interpretation.

Taxonomic status ("reptile")

Position A

SCP-682 is a reptile-like creature and can be reasonably classified as a reptile for descriptive purposes.

Position A — evidence

The core article repeatedly refers to SCP-682 as "vaguely reptile-like" and uses the epithet "Hard-to-Destroy Reptile."

Position B

SCP-682’s mutable, anomalous physiology makes the term "reptile" misleading; it is better understood as an undefined anomalous entity.

Position B — evidence

Meta-essay "The Hard-to-Define Reptile" and other community discussions question whether SCP-682’s morphology and behavior fit any real-world reptilian taxonomy, emphasizing its amorphous and adaptive nature.

Scholarly Consensus

Most commentators accept "reptile" as a convenient descriptive label rather than a strict biological classification. Scholarly analysis tends to highlight the ambiguity and treat the epithet as part of the entity’s narrative identity rather than a scientific statement.

Killability and ultimate vulnerability

Position A

SCP-682 is effectively impossible to destroy with any means currently available to the Foundation; all termination attempts have failed.

Position A — evidence

Core article states that no means available to SCP teams can destroy SCP-682, only cause massive physical damage. Termination logs document repeated failures.

Position B

SCP-682 may have theoretical vulnerabilities, especially to other high-level anomalies or cosmic events; some narratives suggest possible or alternate-reality destruction.

Position B — evidence

Certain extended stories and alternate reality scenarios depict SCP-682 being neutralized or altered by other SCPs or cosmic changes, though these are not part of the mainline article.

Scholarly Consensus

In the primary continuity, SCP-682 is treated as effectively indestructible. Scholars of SCP fiction note that alternate or humorous continuities sometimes play with its destruction, but these are meta-textual experiments rather than canonical resolutions.

Psychological depth and motive

Position A

SCP-682’s hatred of life is absolute and unexplained; it functions as a pure embodiment of misanthropic destruction.

Position A — evidence

Interview logs present SCP-682’s statements as blunt expressions of disgust and hatred without detailed rationale.

Position B

SCP-682’s hatred may have deeper causes (e.g., cosmic corruption, trauma, or philosophical insight) that are hinted at but not fully revealed.

Position B — evidence

Extended mythos and fan analyses interpret SCP-682’s role in Scarlet King narratives and its interactions with other SCPs as suggestive of more complex motives.

Scholarly Consensus

Canonical texts leave the motive largely unexplained. Scholarly and fan commentary often explores possible interpretations, but these remain speculative. A careful dossier distinguishes attested hatred from hypothesized causes.
