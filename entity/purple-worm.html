Purple Worm

The Deep Gnawer, The World-Tunneler, The Maw That Moves Mountains

TypeMonstrosity
CultureDungeons & Dragons (Wizards of the Coast / Hasbro)
RealmThe Underdark / Deep Subterranean Tunnels
ScaleIndividual (colossal predator)
AlignmentUnaligned (bestial)

You hear it before you see it — a rumble like thunder beneath your feet. Then the floor opens up, and there's nothing below you but teeth.

Who It Is

The Purple Worm is D&D's ultimate subterranean apex predator — a gargantuan annelid that can reach 80 feet in length and 5 feet in diameter, its segmented body a deep, glistening purple. It 'swims' through solid rock by excreting a powerful solvent from its skin, dissolving stone and soil ahead of it and leaving behind perfectly cylindrical tunnels that can stretch for miles. Its primary sensory organ is vibration — a Purple Worm can detect footsteps from a thousand feet away through solid stone — and its maw is a circular, lamprey-like orifice lined with row upon row of dagger-sized teeth that can chew through plate armor as easily as flesh.

A Purple Worm's hunting strategy is devastatingly simple: it senses vibrations on or near its tunnel, bursts through the floor/wall/ceiling (creating an instant sinkhole), swallows the largest target whole, and retreats back into the earth. Any creature swallowed by a Purple Worm takes crushing damage from muscular contractions and acid damage from digestive enzymes — and unlike many 'swallow whole' monsters, the Purple Worm's gullet is so massive that even a horse can fit inside it. Its tail is tipped with a venomous stinger large enough to impale an ogre, delivering a paralytic poison that immobilizes prey for easier swallowing.

The Purple Worm is not intelligent — it is pure biological impetus — but its size and power make it a threat that high-level parties must still respect. A Purple Worm that decides a settlement is in its feeding path can collapse buildings from below, swallow citizens by the dozen, and retreat before any organized defense can form. Experienced Underdark travelers learn to recognize the signs: perfectly circular tunnels with unnaturally smooth walls, a faint acidic smell, and an ominous, ground-shaking rumble that announces the worm's approach.

The creature was directly inspired by the sandworms of Frank Herbert's Dune (though D&D's version has adapted to underground rather than desert environments) and by the legendary Mongolian Death Worm. It has appeared across D&D video games, including Baldur's Gate 3 (where a memorable encounter in the Underdark features a Purple Worm), and has become the standard for 'giant burrowing worm' in fantasy gaming. In some D&D settings, particularly the Forgotten Realms, Purple Worm tunnels form a secondary transportation network in the Underdark — drow, duergar, and deep gnomes all make use of old worm tracks as roads, at the constant risk of encountering the track's maker.
