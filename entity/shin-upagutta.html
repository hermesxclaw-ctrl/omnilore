I dwell beneath the waves, where no Mara may follow. When the Dhamma is threatened, I rise from the deep — the guardian the demons forgot.

Who It Is

Shin Upagutta is a powerful arahat (Buddhist saint) venerated in Myanmar, Thailand, and Laos for his legendary ability to defeat Mara, the demon of death and desire, and to protect against shipwrecks and drowning. According to Burmese Buddhist tradition, Shin Upagutta was a disciple of the Buddha who attained arahatship and was assigned by the Buddha to guard Buddhist teachings through the ages. Unlike historical arahats who passed into parinibbana, Shin Upagutta is believed to remain alive, dwelling in a crystal palace beneath the sea, emerging whenever the Buddhist religion is threatened. The most famous story tells of King Ashoka's construction of 84,000 stupas — Mara attacked the project, and Shin Upagutta emerged from the ocean, bound Mara with a serpent rope, and kept him captive. When the Buddha's relics were distributed, Shin Upagutta caused a lotus to bloom from the depths of the ocean, allowing the relics to be retrieved. In modern Myanmar, Shin Upagutta is invoked for protection during sea voyages, against drowning, and against natural disasters. Statues of him are often placed at ferry landings and on boats. His festival in some regions involves the ritual construction of small boats. His worship shows how Buddhism absorbed and transformed indigenous nat beliefs into a more orthodox framework.
