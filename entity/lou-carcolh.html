Who It Is

Lou Carcolh is a monstrous creature from Gascon folklore in southwestern France, said to inhabit the underground caves and tunnels beneath the town of Lavardens in the Gers department. Described as a hybrid of a giant serpent and a colossal slug, the Carcolh was said to have a slimy, elongated body covered in viscous mucus, with a massive, snail-like shell on its back. Its most distinctive feature was its enormous mouth, which could expand to an alarming size, surrounded by long, hairy tentacles or feelers that it used to drag prey — primarily livestock, but also unwary humans — into its underground lair. The creature would then consume its victims slowly within its hidden subterranean tunnels. According to local accounts, the Carcolh never emerged fully into the light; it attacked from cave mouths, crevices, and sinkholes. The tunnels of Lavardens, which run beneath the medieval town, were long believed to still house the creature or its descendants. The legend may have been inspired by sightings of the Limax cinereus maximus (an unusually large gray slug native to the region) combined with folk memories of ancient boas or pythons. The Carcolh remains a unique figure in French bestiary lore, representing a rare mollusk-based mythical creature in European folklore.
