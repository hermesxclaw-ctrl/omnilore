Who It Is

In the rolling hills of Gascony, where the Adour River winds toward the Atlantic, there is a town perched on a rounded hill like a snail's shell. The locals call it Lou Carcolh—the beast that is also a place, a warning, and a legend. Beneath the streets of Hastingues, the story goes, a monstrous snail-serpent with hairy tentacles and a slime that glistens in the dark waits to drag the unwary into its cavern. But is the Carcolh a real creature, a metaphor for the town's shape, or a playful threat to pretty women? The answer is as tangled as the beast's own tentacles.

The Carcolh is a predator that lies in wait inside its cavern. It uses its long, hairy tentacles to reach out and grab prey, dragging it into the cave without leaving its lair. Its slime serves as a warning of its approach, but also perhaps as a lure or a means to track prey. The creature is not described as intelligent or malevolent beyond its predatory nature.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Carcolh' is likely derived from the Gascon/Occitan word for snail or shell, related to the Latin 'cochlea' (snail). It may also be connected to the French 'escargot' or the regional word for a rounded hill, reflecting the town's shape.

Pronunciation — Approximately 'loo kar-KOL' (Gascon), with the 'lh' resembling a 'y' sound, as in 'carpet' but with a 'y' glide.

Aliases — Carcolh, Lou Carcolh (Gascon), The Carcolh of Hastingues

Ownership — French (Gascon/Occitan) folklore, specifically the town of Hastingues in the Landes department of southwestern France.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Lou Carcolh is described as a large, slimy, snail-like serpent. It has a large shell, similar to a snail's, but with a serpentine body. Its most distinctive feature is its hairy tentacles, which are long and can reach great distances. The creature is covered in a slime that can be seen glistening before it appears, suggesting a luminous or visible trail.

Symbols — No specific iconography is documented. The creature may be symbolically associated with the rounded hill of Hastingues, representing the town's shape, and perhaps with the dangers of the unknown underground.

Domains — It inhabits a cavern beneath the town of Hastingues, in the Landes region of southwestern France. The cavern is likely a natural cave system under the hill on which the town is built.

Unknown, likely medieval or early modern

Lou Carcolh is a monstrous creature that lives in a cavern beneath the town of Hastingues. It is said to have a large shell, a slimy body, and hairy tentacles that it uses to grab and drag prey into its cave. The slime it leaves can be seen from a distance, warning of its approach. The creature is used as a warning to young women: 'the carcolh will catch you!' The legend serves as a cautionary tale, warning people, particularly women, to be wary of the dangers that may lurk in the area. It also gives the town a distinctive identity.
