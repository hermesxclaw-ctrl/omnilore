Who It Is

On a field littered with Risen, a blue-haired prince raises a broken Falchion that has not yet awakened — the blade of a hero-king dead two thousand years, the burden of a kingdom that believes peace is its birthright, and the fatherhood of a daughter who has traveled back through time to prevent him from becoming a corpse. Chrom of Ylisse is not Fire Emblem's most cunning lord or its most tragic, but he is its most deliberately idealistic: a leader who charges first, trusts completely, and must learn that idealism without sacrifice is just theater.

Attested in supports and main script: earnestly idealistic, impulsive, emotionally transparent, and physically brave to a fault. Frederick notes Chrom 'charges before thinking' (Support C). Lissa calls him 'too serious' and 'terrible at lying.' He values loyalty and personal bonds over politics, founding the Shepherds as a volunteer militia that helps villagers directly rather than a formal royal guard - an unusual move for a prince that reflects his late mother/Emmeryn's pacifist influence and his own restlessness. Powers
Falchion (Kingsfang) and Exalted Falchion

Divine dragon sword forged from Naga's fang. In Awakening, base Falchion has 45 uses, Mt 5-6, effective against dragons, heals wielder slightly? Actually Exalted Falchion (after awakening at Mt. Prism, Ch.21) gains Mt 16, +20 Hit, effective vs dragons, restores HP every turn via Awakening ritual. Only those with Brand of the Exalt can wield without harm. Chrom is its primary wielder; Lucina can also wield parallel Falchion.

Limit: Unawakened Falchion cannot harm Grima; requires five Gemstones and Naga's rite at Mt. Prism. Even Exalted Falchion cannot kill Grima permanently without Robin/Grima vessel sacrifice. Weapon durability and range 1 only.

Aether (Tenkuu)

Chrom's signature combat skill (learned as Great Lord Lv.5). Two-hit technique: first hit Sol (heal 50% damage dealt), second hit Luna (halve enemy defense). In Awakening, activation rate = (Skill stat /2)%. Animation shows rising slash then downward slam. Inherited from ancestor Marth/Ike lineage via game mechanics.

Limit: Skill proc chance, not guaranteed; requires Great Lord promotion. In lore, not a magical superpower but a sword technique - Heroes and Smash exaggerate its visual scale.

Leadership and Charisma (Shepherds' Tactics)

Not supernatural: Chrom's ability to inspire, recruit, and retain diverse forces (mercenaries, rival nations, former enemies). Gameplay reflected in Dual Strike+, Charm skill (+5 Hit/Avoid to allies within 3 tiles), and Pair Up bonuses. Narrative: convinces Feroxi khans, Valm resistance, and Plegian defectors.

Limit: Fails diplomatically with Gangrel and Walhart initially; requires Robin's tactics to succeed. Charisma does not prevent betrayal or war.

Rightful King and Dual Guard+

Class skills as Great Lord: Rightful King boosts skill activation by 10%; Dual Guard+ increases chance to block attacks when paired. Lore justification: royal blood and trust in comrades.

Limit: Gameplay-only; no lore claim of supernatural luck. Requires Pair Up proximity.

Reclass Potential (Second Seal)

Via Second Seal, Chrom can reclass to Cavalier -> Paladin/Great Knight, Archer -> Sniper/Bow Knight (in Awakening). In Heroes, this is represented by seasonal variants wielding other weapons. Narratively, reflects his versatile martial training under Frederick.

Limit: In canon story, Chrom remains sword-locked; reclassing is player-choice gameplay, not story. Cannot use magic naturally.

Accounts I
The Shepherds and the Risen IncursionFire Emblem Awakening Prologue - Chapter 6

In-universe: Year ~2000 after Marth's era; development 2012

Prince Chrom, with sister Lissa and retainer Frederick, leads the Shepherds, a militia meant to aid Ylissean commoners. In Southtown they find Robin unconscious, who saves them from Risen (undead) with tactical foresight. Robin joins as tactician. Chrom's group quells Risen outbreaks, encounters the masked swordsman 'Marth' who warns of calamity, and navigates rising tensions with neighboring theocracy Plegia, whose King Gangrel covets the Fire Emblem. Chrom's idealism is shown when he spares Plegian soldiers and insists on protecting villages even against orders. Establishes Chrom-Robin bond (game over if either dies), introduces Falchion's unawakened state, sets Ylisse-Plegia war, foreshadows time travel via Marth's anachronistic knowledge.

The War of Plegia and Emmeryn's SacrificeFire Emblem Awakening Chapters 7-11

Same in-universe year

After Gangrel's provocation and attempted theft of the Fire Emblem, Ylisse is invaded. Chrom seeks aid from Regna Ferox, winning the loyalty of Khans Flavia and Basilio via arena combat. He defeats Gangrel but cannot prevent Exalt Emmeryn - Chrom's pacifist elder sister and ruler of Ylisse - from being captured. Gangrel demands the Emblem for her life. Emmeryn, refusing to let Chrom sacrifice the realm, throws herself from the Plegian capital's walls, transforming into a near-death state that becomes a religious martyrdom. Chrom, devastated, executes Gangrel (or spares him depending on player choice in paralogue) and is crowned Exalt, inheriting the Fire Emblem and responsibility for Falchion's awakening. Chrom's transition from prince to king; Fire Emblem passes to him; Falchion quest begins; Chrom's character shifts from impulsive to guilt-ridden. Emmeryn survives amnesiac if Paralogue 10 is completed, complicating martyrdom.

The Conquest of Valm and Awakening the BladeFire Emblem Awakening Chapters 12-21

Two years after Plegian war (in-game timeskip)

Seeking the Gemstones to awaken Falchion, Chrom leads an expedition to the western continent Valm, ruled by conqueror Walhart. Allied with Valmese resistance (Say'ri), Chrom defeats Walhart's generals (Cervantes, Excellus, Yen'fay). At Mt. Prism, Naga tests Chrom; upon proving his worth, Falchion becomes Exalted Falchion and the Fire Emblem is completed. Chrom defeats Walhart in single combat, but spares his lesson: that strength without purpose is tyranny. Returning to Ylisse, he learns Plegia's new king Validar (Robin's father) has resurrected Grima. Falchion awakening makes Chrom capable of facing Grima; Chrom's kingship is legitimized by Naga; establishes Valm as part of Ylissean protectorate; sets up final Grima arc.

Accounts II
The Fell Dragon Grima and the Child from the FutureFire Emblem Awakening Chapters 13, 21-26 (Endgame)

Apocalyptic future ~10-15 years ahead, intersecting present

At Chapter 13, 'Marth' unmasks as Lucina, Chrom's future daughter, who traveled back with other children (Owain, Inigo, Severa, etc.) to prevent Grima's resurrection. She reveals that in her timeline, Chrom was killed by Robin possessed by Grima, and Grima destroyed the world. In present, Validar possesses Robin to steal the Fire Emblem and resurrect Grima using Robin's Fell Dragon blood. Basilio dies protecting Chrom. Chrom and Lucina pursue Grima to the Dragon's Table, where Grima is fully revived - possessing Robin. Naga offers a choice: let Chrom land the final blow on Grima, killing Robin as vessel, or let Robin sacrifice themselves to destroy Grima's soul while Chrom's Falchion severs its body. Chrom's support for Robin determines ending. In ideal ending, Robin survives via Naga's blessing and returns to Chrom. Chrom becomes father in present; his willingness to trust Robin despite prophecy is thematic climax; determines whether Robin lives/dies; Grima is sealed/destroyed. Lucina remains in present or returns to future depending on ending interpretation.

Paralogues - The Future Children and Chrom's FatherhoodFire Emblem Awakening Paralogues 1-12, 14-23; Supports

Interspersed 2012 game

Each child paralogue reveals Chrom as father figure not only to Lucina but to the entire second generation. In Paralogue 4 (Owain), Chrom meets his nephew; in Paralogue 7 (Lucina), he learns his daughter's identity via Brand reveal. Supports show Chrom's parenting: he worries about being a good father (Lucina Support A), trains Cynthia, frets over Kjelle's severity. If Chrom marries Sumia, Cordelia, Olivia, Maribelle, Sully, Robin (F), or village maiden (if no S-rank by Ch.11), that spouse becomes Lucina's (and potentially other children's) mother, changing sibling sets. Chrom's generic 'maiden' marriage (if unmarried by Ch.11) is to an unnamed village girl to ensure Lucina's birth - a narrative failsafe. Mechanically unlocks second generation units with inherited skills and appearance (hair color from mother). Narratively cements Chrom's legacy and the game's theme of bonds across time. Establishes that Chrom's lineage carries Naga's blood.

After Awakening - Emblem and EchoFire Emblem Engage (2023), Fire Emblem Warriors (2017), Fire Emblem Heroes Forging Bonds

2023 in-universe: Elyos, 1000 years after Awakening's world

In Engage, Chrom exists as Emblem Chrom, a bracelet spirit paired with Emblem Robin? Actually Engage pairs Chrom with Robin? No, Engage has Emblem Marth, Celica etc., and Emblem Chrom is DLC Bracelet. He appears as a mentor to Alear, teaching Bond skills and recalling his war. Dialogue emphasizes his growth: 'I once charged ahead without thinking. My friends taught me to stand with them.' In Warriors, Chrom is a main lord alongside Ryoma and Xander, leading Ylissean forces against Chaos Dragon Velezark; his story mirrors Awakening but compressed, and he forms rivalry/friendship with other lords. In Heroes Forging Bonds, Chrom confronts his fear of becoming like Walhart and his guilt over Emmeryn. Keeps Chrom in franchise continuity as archetypal ideal lord; recontextualizes him as legendary hero rather than active ruler. No impact on Awakening canon but expands his characterization for new players.

Chrom is the main protagonist and player avatar of Awakening. — Chrom is co-protagonist and lord, but Robin (Avatar) is the player-insert tactician and actual viewpoint character. Chrom can be benched in gameplay but story requires him; Robin's choices drive final act. Many newcomers think Chrom is sole hero due to Smash.

Chrom wields the Fire Emblem shield in battle. — Chrom carries the Fire Emblem (Shield of Seals) as holder, but never equips it as weapon/shield in combat. It is a plot item that awakens Falchion. In Heroes, the shield appears only in art, not as weapon.

Chrom is a clone of Marth with identical moves in Smash. — In Smash Ultimate, Chrom is an Echo Fighter of Roy, not Marth. He has Roy's sweet-spot mechanics, faster speed, and distinct Aether recovery. Lucina is the Marth echo.

Chrom's Falchion can kill Grima outright. — Exalted Falchion can wound and seal Grima, but cannot kill Grima permanently while Grima's soul resides in Robin. The final blow requires Robin's self-sacrifice or Naga's intervention. Chrom alone cannot end the cycle.

Chrom is weak/useless in Awakening gameplay. — Chrom is consistently mid-to-high tier: excellent growths, early availability, strong Pair Up bonuses, and Exalted Falchion makes him dragon-effective endgame. Early 'Chrom is bad' meme came from Lunatic+ difficulty where early lords struggle, but not representative.
