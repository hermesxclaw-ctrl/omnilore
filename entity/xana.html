Who It Is

In the green hills of Asturias, where streams run cold and clear, there are whispers of a woman of unearthly beauty who combs her golden hair by the water's edge. She is the xana, a fairy of the springs, who can reward the pure-hearted with love and gold—or drive the wicked to madness with her song. But beware: she may steal your child and leave a changeling in its place, a creature that ages in months and speaks of centuries. To unmask it, you must place eggshells by the fire and listen for the telltale cry: 'I was born one hundred years ago...'

Dual nature: can be beneficial or malicious. Offers 'love water' and rewards to the worthy; hypnotic voices heard on spring and summer nights. Pure souls feel peace and love; impure souls feel suffocation and may go insane. Some attack people and steal food.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Always female, of extraordinary beauty. Often depicted with long hair, sometimes combing it. Associated with pure water sources.

Symbols — Fountains, rivers, waterfalls, caves, forested regions with pure water. Toponyms referencing xanas are common in Asturias.

Domains — Fountains, rivers, waterfalls, caves, forests with pure water.

Accounts I
The ChangelingAsturian folktale tradition

Traditional (pre-modern)

A xana replaces a human baby with her own xanín. The human mother notices the baby grows unusually fast. To unmask the changeling, she places eggshells near the fire; the xanín exclaims, 'I was born one hundred years ago, and since then I have not seen so many egg shells near the fire!' The changeling is revealed; the human baby is presumably returned.

The Enchanted XanaAsturian folktale tradition

Traditional

A xana is under a spell that can be broken by a secret ritual. If a human performs the ritual correctly, the spell is broken. The xana is freed from the spell.

Accounts II
The Treasure GuardianAsturian folktale tradition

Traditional

A xana possesses treasures, either accidentally, through donation, or theft. A human may try to obtain the treasure, but usually fails. Often the human does not get the treasure.

The Malicious XanaAsturian folktale tradition

Traditional

A xana enters a home through a keyhole, enchants someone, transforms into an animal, or provides a magic belt. These tales highlight her dangerous side. The victim may be enchanted or harmed.
