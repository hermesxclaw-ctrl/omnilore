Who It Is

A three-faced giant embedded waist-deep in the frozen lake of Cocytus at the absolute center of Hell. Each mouth eternally chews a notorious traitor: Judas Iscariot (center), Brutus, and Cassius (the betrayers of Christ and Caesar). His six bat-wings generate the icy wind that freezes the lake.

Attested in Inferno (Canto XXXIV), Divine Comedy (1320)

Cultural Significance

The passage where Dante and Virgil climb down Satan's fur and emerge on the other side of the world ('And we came forth to see again the stars') is one of the most famous endings in literature. The frozen-Satan image inverts the fire-and-brimstone expectation.
