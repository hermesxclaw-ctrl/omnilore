Ted the Caver

We heard it. Clear as anything. Something breathing on the other side of that passage. Something big. We are not alone down here.

Who It Is

Ted the Caver is one of the earliest examples of internet horror — a story told through an online cave exploration diary that predates the term 'creepypasta.' Posted to a personal Angelfire website in 2001, the story is presented as the real journal of a man named Ted who, along with his friend B, discovers a narrow passage in a previously explored cave. The passage leads to a series of strange discoveries: a pictograph of a humanoid figure carved into the rock, inexplicable sounds from deeper in the cave, and eventually, a smooth, perfectly round 'portal stone' that seems to be some kind of doorway. The horror unfolds with agonizing slowness over multiple journal entries, each describing increasingly frightening events. Tools go missing. Sounds are heard — scraping, moving, something breathing — from just beyond the next passage. On the final night, Ted and his friends camp in the cave and experience what they can only describe as something large moving past them in the darkness, blocking the airflow, smelling of decay. The story ends with Ted writing that he is going back into the cave one last time, and the website was never updated again — a brilliantly effective use of the medium to imply the narrator's fate. For years, readers debated whether Ted the Caver was fact or fiction. Its power came from its format — a personal, mundane website, with no narrative framing or author credit, discovered by readers who weren't sure if they'd stumbled onto a real person's tragedy or a masterful work of fiction. Ted the Caver established the 'found journal' format that would later be used by countless creepypasta stories, and it remains one of the most effective slow-burn horror narratives on the internet.
