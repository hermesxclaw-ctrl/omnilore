Who It Is
Form

??? presents itself exclusively as a disembodied left hand that emerges from the interior of the toilet bowl located in the Stock Pot Inn's ground-floor restroom in East Clock Town, Termina. In the original Nintendo 64 version of Majora's Mask (2000), the hand has a pale, slightly greenish-gray complexion — a color palette evocative of death, decomposition, or otherworldly pallor entirely distinct from any living human skin tone rendered elsewhere in the game. The fingers are long and slender, with a skeletal, almost emaciated quality that suggests extreme thinness — either the thinness of starvation, dehydration, or the papery quality associated with long-deceased corporeal forms. The fingernails are rendered in a dark color — black or very dark gray — adding a morbid, grotesque quality to the hand's appearance and lending it a distinctly supernatural rather than merely human character. The hand extends upward from the toilet bowl in an outstretched, imploring gesture, as though making a desperate plea or reaching for aid. The remainder of ???'s body is at no point visible to the player — no arm is fully shown, no face, no body — suggesting that either the body does not extend beyond the hand, is submerged entirely within the toilet's plumbing infrastructure, or exists in some other dimensional plane accessible only through the toilet's drain. In the Nintendo 3DS remake, Majora's Mask 3D (2015), the hand received a graphical upgrade consistent with the remake's higher-resolution assets; the fingers appear more anatomically detailed and the skin texture is rendered with greater fidelity, enhancing the uncanny quality of the encounter and making it appear simultaneously more realistic and more disturbing than the N64 original. The hand occupies approximately the same spatial scale as a human adult hand — neither visually oversized like a monster limb nor tiny like a child's hand — suggesting the invisible body to which it belongs is roughly human-proportioned. The total absence of any identifying visual context — no face, no garment, no voice, only a written request for 'paper' — leaves the player's imagination entirely responsible for supplying the horrific implications that the developers deliberately withheld, making ??? one of the most psychologically effective uses of negative space in the franchise.

Powers

Liminal Manifestation ??? possesses the ability to manifest exclusively between midnight (12:00 AM) and dawn (6:00 AM) on all three days of Termina's final three-day cycle, emerging from within a toilet bowl — a classically liminal space in Japanese folk cosmology — in the Stock Pot Inn restroom. This temporal constraint aligns precisely with the ox hour (ushi no koku, roughly 2:00–3:00 AM) traditionally identified in Japanese folk belief as the most spiritually active period of the night, when the boundary between the living world and the spirit world is thinnest. The mechanism of manifestation is instantaneous: between valid hours, ??? is present; outside those hours, ??? is entirely absent with no transition animation or residual trace.

Heart Fragment Bestowal Upon receiving any valid paper item from Link, ??? causes a Piece of Heart to materialize inside the toilet bowl — the same receptacle from which it emerged. This is a remarkable ability in the context of Majora's Mask's gameplay: Pieces of Heart are among the most valuable collectibles in the game, permanently expanding Link's maximum health when four are combined. The ability to produce a Piece of Heart ex nihilo — not from a treasure chest, not from a battlefield, but from a toilet bowl in response to a paper offering — reads simultaneously as a magical transaction, a folkloric exchange gift (in the manner of the fox spirit or kitsune rewarding generous mortals), and a mordant comedy of manners. No other character in Majora's Mask produces a Piece of Heart in this manner.

Total Invulnerability ??? cannot be damaged, stunned, killed, or affected by any of Link's weapons, items, or abilities. Attacking ??? with the Kokiri Sword, Gilded Sword, Great Fairy Sword, Bow, Bombs, Bombchu, Hookshot, Longshot, Deku Nuts, Fire Arrows, Ice Arrows, Light Arrows, or any other offensive item produces no reaction. The hand simply continues to extend and wait. This invulnerability is not explicitly coded as an immunity in developer statements but is a functional property of the entity in all versions of the game. Whether this represents supernatural durability, phased existence in a dimension adjacent to the physical, or simply the game engine declining to register attacks on a non-hostile NPC, is not specified.

Bombers' Notebook Registration ??? is one of only twenty characters in all of Termina's three-day cycle whose needs are formally tracked in the Bombers' Notebook — the in-game organizational system that logs every sidequest protagonist. This registration grants ??? a kind of bureaucratic reality: whatever it is metaphysically, it is acknowledged by the in-game record-keeping apparatus as a being with needs that can be met and satisfied. This registration is itself remarkable: the Bombers' Notebook lists characters like Anju, Kafei, and the soldier who is dying of a curse — figures with full narrative arcs and emotional depth — alongside a nameless hand in a toilet. The co-listing implies a formal narrative equivalence, a parity of need and recognition that is itself an implicit statement about the value of every being's unspoken suffering.

Spatial Anchoring ??? is bound to a single, specific location in all of Termina: the ground-floor restroom of the Stock Pot Inn in East Clock Town. Unlike the vast majority of NPCs in Majora's Mask, who follow detailed schedules and move through multiple locations across the three-day cycle, ??? has no schedule-based movement, no secondary location, and no transitions between days beyond its midnight arrival and 6:00 AM departure. This absolute spatial anchoring reinforces the folkloric figure of the haunting entity tied to a specific location (the zashiki-warashi, or room child, is a classic example from Japanese folklore), as distinct from the wandering ghost.

Paper-Based Communication The only form of communication ??? engages in is a single text prompt requesting 'paper,' triggering when Link enters the restroom during the midnight-to-6-AM window while holding nothing in particular, or when interacting with the hand directly. This paper request is not a verbal statement (??? produces no voice or sound beyond ambient environmental audio) but a textual prompt that appears in the game's dialogue box interface. The paper request is satisfied by any of six title deeds (Town Title Deed, Swamp Title Deed, Mountain Title Deed, Ocean Title Deed, Ranch Deed, Lakeside Deed) or any in-game letter. The transaction is silent, instantaneous, and final: paper is accepted, Piece of Heart appears, ??? vanishes.

Accounts

Between midnight and 6:00 AM on any of the three days of the Final Three Days countdown in Majora's Mask, if Link enters the first-floor restroom of the Stock Pot Inn in East Clock Town, he finds a disembodied hand extending upward from the toilet bowl. A text prompt appears: the hand, identified only as '???', requests paper. If Link provides any paper item — including a land title deed or an in-game letter — the hand accepts it without comment, vanishes, and a Piece of Heart materializes in the toilet bowl. If Link has nothing to offer, the hand remains extended until dawn, then retreats without consequence.

Japanese folk tradition contains a persistent subgenre of bathroom horror narratives in which supernatural entities — typically hands, faces, or whole beings — emerge from or haunt traditional squatting-style toilets (washiki toire). The Kappa, documented by Toriyama Sekien and described extensively in regional folklore of the Edo period, is associated with reaching up through latrines to steal the shirikodama from living victims, causing death. The Toire no Hanako-san (Hanako of the Toilet), a post-World War II urban legend first widely documented in the 1980s–1990s, describes the ghost of a young girl haunting school bathrooms, occasionally dragging victims into the toilet. Aka Manto (Red Cloak), a modern urban legend of similar vintage, describes a masked figure offering red or blue paper in female toilet stalls, with any choice of paper resulting in violent death.

In The Legend of Zelda: Skyward Sword, Link encounters Phoeni — explicitly identified by Fi as 'the spirit of a young girl' — haunting the bathroom of the Knight Academy dormitory on Skyloft. Phoeni asks Link for the love letter that Cawlin, a student, has written for Karane. If Link gives Phoeni the letter rather than delivering it to Karane, Phoeni falls in love with Cawlin and begins haunting his bedside at night, stroking his cheek while he sleeps. Cawlin is shown having nightmares. This constitutes a full narrative arc: the toilet ghost character achieves a named identity (Phoeni), a motivated backstory (young girl's ghost), and a consequential emotional arc (obsession, indirect psychological harm).

In Oracle of Ages, Link encounters a disembodied hand extending from an outhouse in Lynna Village at a specific point in the trading quest. The hand, labeled simply 'Hand' rather than '???', requests stationary — the item Link has received earlier in the trading sequence. If Link provides the stationary, the Hand gives him the Stink Bag, the next item in the chain. Unlike ???, the Oracle of Ages Hand is part of a strict linear trading quest and is available throughout the day rather than locked to a midnight window.

In Link's Awakening, a character named '???' — sharing only the typographic designation with the Majora's Mask character — is a friendly ghost who emerges from the second house in Mabe Village and asks Link to take it for a walk. If Link agrees, ??? accompanies him, visible as a transparent white spirit hovering behind Link, and reacts with expressed happiness at visiting various locations. Upon visiting Richard's Villa, ??? reveals it wants to be taken to the cemetery, where it is reunited with its grave and departs peacefully. This ??? has a visible ghostly body (not merely a hand), communicates in full sentences, is friendly and conversational, and has a clear narrative arc of resolution.

Scholarly Debates
Primary folkloric inspiration: Kappa versus Aka Manto
The Kappa is the primary folkloric inspiration for ???. Kellen Russoniello (Zelda Universe, 2019) argues that the Kappa is the strongest candidate because Miyamoto's 2012 description — 'a hand starting to grab you from beneath in the traditional style of the Japanese toilet' — most precisely describes the Kappa's documented behavior (reaching up through riverside latrines to steal the shirikodama). Additionally, the architectural context is specific: traditional Japanese squatting toilets (washiki toire) were historically located near rivers, which is precisely the Kappa's natural domain. The Kappa is also the oldest and most thoroughly documented of the proposed precursors, with Toriyama Sekien's 1776 illustrated depiction and extensive Edo-period regional variant documentation. The Kappa's association with water and drains further supports the identification. ⇌ Aka Manto (Red Cloak) is a more structurally relevant inspiration due to its paper-offering mechanic. Game Rant and Screen Rant analyses emphasize that Aka Manto is the only folk horror figure that specifically involves an entity offering paper in a toilet context. The thematic inversion is striking: Aka Manto kills anyone who accepts its paper offering, while ??? rewards anyone who provides paper. This inverted mirror relationship suggests a conscious engagement with the Aka Manto narrative rather than mere parallel development. The Kappa, by contrast, does not involve paper at all — its defining mechanic is physical violation (shirikodama theft), not paper exchange. Proponents of this view argue that Miyamoto's vague description was deliberately evasive and that the paper mechanic points specifically to Aka Manto as the primary template.
Gender of ???
??? is most naturally interpreted as gender-indeterminate or genderless. The character has no canonical gender assignment in any Nintendo publication, dialogue, or supplementary material. The hand is not explicitly gendered by any in-game text, the Bombers' Notebook, the Zelda Encyclopedia, or developer statements. Assigning a gender to ??? projects contemporary or Western assumptions onto a deliberately ambiguous entity. The deliberate refusal to name the character extends to refusing to gender it — both the name and the gender are withheld as part of the character's fundamental inscrutability. ⇌ The physical characteristics of ???'s hand — its slender fingers, delicate proportions, and pallid complexion — are read by a significant portion of the fanbase and by some analysts as conventionally feminine. This reading aligns with the Hanako-san folklore candidate (Hanako-san is explicitly female) and with Skyward Sword's Phoeni, the 'successor' toilet ghost character who is explicitly identified by Fi as 'the spirit of a young girl.' If ??? is a deliberate folkloric invocation of the female bathroom ghost tradition, female gender would be the appropriate interpretation. Some fan art depicts ??? with a visible young woman's face, extending this reading.
Narrative relationship between Majora's Mask ??? and Link's Awakening ???
The two ??? characters are entirely distinct characters who happen to share a typographic naming convention. The Zelda Wiki explicitly states they are different characters. The Link's Awakening ??? is a visible, full-bodied, conversational, friendly ghost with a complete narrative arc that predates the Majora's Mask ??? by seven years. The Majora's Mask ??? is an anonymous, silent disembodied hand in a toilet with no visible body and no dialogue beyond a paper request. The shared name reflects a recurring design choice — using '???' for entities that resist conventional naming — not a shared identity. The Zelda Encyclopedia lists them separately. ⇌ A minority fan position holds that the two ??? figures may share a narrative or ontological connection within the broader Zelda multiverse. Proponents argue that the recurring use of the same typographic designation is too specific to be coincidental — developers could have assigned any name or designation and chose '???' twice. The argument extends to the possibility that both ??? figures represent a class of unnamed beings in the Zelda cosmology: entities whose fundamental nature is being unknown and unclassifiable. This view has no canonical support but persists in fan theory communities as an interesting interpretive lens.
Whether ??? constitutes effective horror design or merely absurdist comedy
??? is a masterwork of minimalist horror design. By presenting only a hand, withholding all context, restricting the encounter to the witching hours, and embedding it in a bathroom — the most private and vulnerable space in the game's architecture — the developers achieve maximum psychological impact with minimum explicit content. The horror is entirely supplied by the player's imagination, making it infinitely scalable to individual fear responses. The folk horror substrate (Kappa, Aka Manto) adds a cultural depth that increases the dread for players aware of these traditions. This is the consensus view among game studies analysts and horror game designers who cite Majora's Mask. ⇌ ??? is at least as funny as it is frightening, and treating it primarily as a horror achievement misreads the tonal register. The premise — a ghost hand in a toilet desperately needs toilet paper — is intrinsically comedic. Multiple YouTube commentators and Reddit threads have noted that the encounter produces laughter as often as dread, particularly on second playthroughs when the player knows exactly what it is and is simply farming the Piece of Heart. Nintendo's E rating for the game implicitly acknowledges that ??? is not seriously threatening content. The character's memetic life on social media leans heavily comedic rather than horrific, suggesting that cultural reception has settled on ??? as charming-creepy rather than genuinely frightening.
