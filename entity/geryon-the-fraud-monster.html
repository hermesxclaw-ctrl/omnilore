Who It Is

Dante's most original monster: a winged creature with the face of an honest man, the body of a serpent, hairy paws, and a scorpion's sting. It flies Dante and Virgil from the Seventh to the Eighth Circle. Geryon's honest face concealing a monstrous body makes it the perfect emblem of fraud.

Attested in Inferno (Canto XVII), Divine Comedy (1320)

Cultural Significance

The honest face / serpent body dichotomy is the controlling metaphor for the Malebolge — every sin punished there involves some form of deception. Geryon also represents the uneasy fascination of the beautiful-monstrous.
