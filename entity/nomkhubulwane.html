Who It Is

Nomkhubulwane (also Inkosazana) is the Zulu goddess of rain, agriculture, fertility, and the harvest. Her name means 'She Who Chooses the State of the Land' or 'The Princess of the Heavens.' She is believed to manifest as a beautiful woman who appears before young girls in the fields, instructing them in the proper rituals to ensure abundant crops and rain. She is sometimes identified as the daughter of Unkulunkulu. The most important ritual to honor her, the Nomkhubulwane festival, involved planting a sacred garden, abstaining from work, and girls dressed in special attire singing songs petitioning her for rain. During periods of drought, the Zulu people would send maidens to fields carrying clay pots and offerings to beseech her intercession. Unlike warrior deities, Nomkhubulwane embodies gentle, life-giving power — the feminine principle of natural abundance and seasonal renewal. Anthropologist Axel-Ivar Berglund documented that Zulu communities traditionally believed she had the power to shape-shift, sometimes appearing as an animal. Her worship has experienced a revival in contemporary South Africa as part of broader indigenous spiritual reclamation movements.
