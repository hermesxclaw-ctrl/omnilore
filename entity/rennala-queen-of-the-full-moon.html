Who It Is

Rennala, Queen of the Full Moon, is one of Elden Ring's most tragic and visually stunning demigods — not a warrior-tyrant like the others but a brilliant sorceress and loving mother whose life was destroyed when her husband Radagon abandoned her to become the consort of Queen Marika. Rennala was originally the queen of the Carian royal family, a human sorceress of unparalleled power who challenged the Golden Order's military might and fought Radagon to a standstill — twice. Radagon, impressed by her strength and beauty, married her, and they had three children: Radahn, Rykard, and Ranni. This union brought peace between the Carian royal family and the Golden Order. But when Godfrey was stripped of grace and banished, Radagon left Rennala to marry Queen Marika (becoming the second Elden Lord), leaving Rennala a broken shell of the queen she had been.

When the player encounters Rennala in the Grand Library of Raya Lucaria Academy, she is not the warrior-scholar of legend but a shattered woman clutching an amber egg containing a Great Rune — the 'Rune of the Unborn.' She obsessively 'rebirths' her juvenile scholars (the sweetings crawling on the library floor), recreating them again and again in an attempt to perfect them, to create something that won't leave her. She barely acknowledges the player's presence, lost in her grief and her amber egg. The boss fight's first phase is mechanically unique — the player must kill her rebirthing scholars to break her protective golden shield, while she floats above the fray, singing a melancholic Latin lullaby.

The second phase is not truly Rennala — it is an illusion created by Ranni, her daughter, to protect her mother. The illusion depicts Rennala in her prime: a majestic sorceress standing on a lake of moonlight, summoning spirit wolves, a dragon, and a bloodhound knight, and hurling comet-azures and full-moon sorceries. Her design in this phase is one of Elden Ring's most beautiful — a tall, regal figure in flowing blue robes, a crescent-moon crown, and the serene, distant expression of a woman who has seen beyond the stars. The arena itself is a lake of shallow water reflecting a massive full moon — the moon that Rennala 'encountered' in her youth, which taught her lunar sorceries and made her the Queen of the Full Moon.

After her defeat, Rennala is not killed. She remains in the library, still clutching her egg, now offering the player the service of 'Rebirth' (respecing their character stats) in exchange for Larval Tears. She is one of the few demigods the player does not have to murder — a kindness in a game defined by tragedy. Her story — a brilliant woman abandoned by a man who used her, left alone in an empty academy with nothing but an egg and memories — is one of Elden Ring's most poignant. She is a victim, not a villain, and the player's 'victory' over her feels less like triumph and more like intrusion.
