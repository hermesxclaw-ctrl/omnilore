Who It Is

In the misty moors of Scotland, a single glimpse of a spectral double could foretell your doom—such is the wraith, a ghostly omen that has haunted folklore for centuries and evolved into a myriad of forms in modern fantasy.

Wraiths are generally malevolent or ominous, appearing to warn of death or to haunt the living. They may be silent or emit eerie sounds. In some tales, they are vengeful spirits of the dead, in others, they are merely apparitions of the dying.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The word 'wraith' is of uncertain origin, first attested in Scottish English in the early 16th century. It may derive from Old Norse 'vǫrðr' (guardian) or Gaelic 'bhreath' (apparition), but no definitive etymology has been established.

Pronunciation — /reɪθ/

Aliases — Wraith

Ownership — Primarily Scottish and English folklore, later adopted into global popular culture as a generic term for a ghost or spirit.

Powers
Incorporeality

Traditional wraiths are often intangible, able to pass through solid objects and immune to physical harm.

Limit: May be affected by iron, holy symbols, or certain rituals.

Omen of Death

A wraith may appear to a living person as a portent of their imminent death, or as a double of a person who is about to die.

Limit: Not a controllable power; it is a supernatural manifestation.

Life Drain

In Dungeons & Dragons, a wraith's touch drains life energy, potentially reducing a victim's vitality or turning them into another wraith.

Limit: Requires a successful melee attack; may be resisted by saving throws.

Feeding on Life Force

In Stargate Atlantis, Wraith feed on the life force of humans to sustain themselves, causing their victims to age rapidly and die.

Limit: Need to physically attach their feeding hand to a human; can be interrupted.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In traditional folklore, a wraith is typically described as a pale, translucent, or shadowy figure, often resembling a deceased person or an omen of death. It may appear as a double of a living person (a 'fetch') or as a harbinger of doom. Descriptions vary widely by region and story.

Symbols — Wraiths are often associated with darkness, mist, and chill. In art, they are depicted as cloaked, ghostly figures with glowing eyes or indistinct forms. No standardized iconography exists across cultures.

Domains — Wraiths are said to haunt places associated with death, tragedy, or the dying person. They may appear at funerals, battlefields, or the homes of the soon-to-deceased. They are often tied to specific locations or families.

Accounts I
The Wraith of the DyingScottish folklore

16th-19th century

A common motif: a person sees a spectral double of themselves or a loved one, which is an omen of death. The wraith may appear at the moment of death or shortly before, often at a distance. The death of the person is foreseen; the witness may be haunted by the memory.

Wraiths in Gothic LiteratureVarious novels and poems (e.g., works by Walter Scott, Emily Brontë)

18th-19th century

Wraiths appear as ghostly figures in Gothic fiction, often symbolizing guilt, unresolved grief, or supernatural vengeance. The wraith often drives the plot toward a moral or emotional resolution.

Accounts II
The Wraith in Dungeons & DragonsD&D rulebooks (e.g., Monster Manual)

1970s-present

Wraiths are undead creatures that drain life energy and are often found in dungeons or haunted places. They are feared for their ability to create more wraiths from their victims. Players may lose levels or become wraiths themselves if not careful.

The Wraith in Stargate AtlantisStargate Atlantis (TV series)

2004-2009

The Wraith are a powerful alien race that feeds on human life force. They are the primary antagonists of the series, threatening the human population of the Pegasus galaxy. The Wraith are a constant threat, leading to many conflicts and ethical dilemmas.
