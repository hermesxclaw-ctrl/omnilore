The Approach

In the misty moors of Scotland, a single glimpse of a spectral double could foretell your doom—such is the wraith, a ghostly omen that has haunted folklore for centuries and evolved into a myriad of forms in modern fantasy.

Content advisory. Themes of death and the supernatural.
Identity

Wraith

Pronunciation /reɪθ/

Etymology

The word 'wraith' is of uncertain origin, first attested in Scottish English in the early 16th century. It may derive from Old Norse 'vǫrðr' (guardian) or Gaelic 'bhreath' (apparition), but no definitive etymology has been established.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily Scottish and English folklore, later adopted into global popular culture as a generic term for a ghost or spirit.

Disputed Identifications

The term is often used interchangeably with 'ghost', 'spectre', or 'apparition', but some traditions distinguish a wraith as a specific type of spectral double or omen of death. In modern fiction, 'wraith' has been redefined in various franchises (e.g., Stargate, D&D) with unique characteristics.

Description

Appearance

In traditional folklore, a wraith is typically described as a pale, translucent, or shadowy figure, often resembling a deceased person or an omen of death. It may appear as a double of a living person (a 'fetch') or as a harbinger of doom. Descriptions vary widely by region and story.

Symbols & Iconography

Wraiths are often associated with darkness, mist, and chill. In art, they are depicted as cloaked, ghostly figures with glowing eyes or indistinct forms. No standardized iconography exists across cultures.

Behaviour & Personality

Wraiths are generally malevolent or ominous, appearing to warn of death or to haunt the living. They may be silent or emit eerie sounds. In some tales, they are vengeful spirits of the dead, in others, they are merely apparitions of the dying.

Domains & Habitats

Wraiths are said to haunt places associated with death, tragedy, or the dying person. They may appear at funerals, battlefields, or the homes of the soon-to-deceased. They are often tied to specific locations or families.

Version Differences

In modern fiction, wraiths have been reimagined as: undead creatures in Dungeons & Dragons (drained life force, incorporeal), alien vampires in Stargate Atlantis (feeding on life energy), and a class of ghost in Phasmophobia (with specific behaviors). These differ significantly from traditional folklore.

Stories & Myths

The Wraith of the Dying

Source Scottish folkloreCulture ScottishPeriod 16th-19th century

Summary

A common motif: a person sees a spectral double of themselves or a loved one, which is an omen of death. The wraith may appear at the moment of death or shortly before, often at a distance.

Participants

The dying person

The witness

Consequences

The death of the person is foreseen; the witness may be haunted by the memory.

Variants

In some tales, the wraith is the spirit of the deceased appearing to a living relative. In others, it is a guardian spirit.
