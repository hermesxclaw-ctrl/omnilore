Who It Is

In the cold, silent realm of Saturn, a small moon bears a scar so vast it nearly tore the world apart. Mimas, a ball of ice and rock just 400 kilometers wide, is home to Herschel Crater—a 139-kilometer-wide wound that makes it look uncannily like the Death Star. But this is no fictional superweapon; it's a real world with a hidden ocean, a violent past, and a future that could rewrite our understanding of habitable environments.

Mimas is a geologically dead world with no detected atmosphere or magnetic field. Its rotation is tidally locked with Saturn, showing one face always toward the planet. It experiences tidal heating, which may maintain a subsurface ocean.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Named after Mimas, one of the Giants in Greek mythology, son of Uranus and Gaia. The name was proposed by John Herschel in 1847.

Pronunciation — MY-məs (common), or MY-mas (less common). Adjective: Mimantean or Mimantian.

Aliases — Saturn I, Saturn I Mimas

Ownership — Discovered by William Herschel in 1789; named by his son John. Part of the Saturnian system, named after mythological figures associated with Cronus (Saturn).

Powers
Gravitational influence on Saturn's rings

Mimas's gravity creates and maintains the Cassini Division and other ring gaps through orbital resonances.

Limit: Limited to its mass and orbit; only affects particles in specific resonances.

Tidal heating

Tidal interactions with Saturn generate internal heat, potentially maintaining a subsurface ocean.

Limit: Heat flux is low; no surface tectonic activity observed.

Subsurface ocean

A liquid water ocean likely exists 20-30 km below the icy crust, estimated to be less than 25 million years old.

Limit: Not directly observed; inferred from orbital dynamics.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Mimas is a heavily cratered, oblate spheroid with a mean diameter of 396.4 km. Its surface is dominated by water ice, giving it a bright, almost white appearance. The most prominent feature is the 139-km-wide Herschel Crater, with walls 5 km high, a floor 10 km deep, and a central peak 6 km tall. The surface is saturated with smaller craters, but the south polar region has fewer large craters, suggesting resurfacing.

Symbols — No official symbol, but Denis Moskowitz proposed a combined Greek mu and Saturn symbol. In popular culture, it is associated with the Death Star and Pac-Man due to its appearance.

Domains — Mimas orbits Saturn at a distance of about 185,539 km, within the outer part of the main rings. It is responsible for clearing the Cassini Division. Its surface temperature averages around -200°C.

Accounts I
Discovery by William HerschelHerschel's 1789 observation log

18th century

Herschel used his 40-foot telescope to spot Mimas on September 17, 1789, the same year he discovered Enceladus. He noted it as the 'seventh satellite' of Saturn. Expanded knowledge of Saturn's moon system; later named by his son John.

Accounts II
The Herschel ImpactGeological analysis of Cassini imagery

Ancient (estimated 4.1 billion years ago)

A massive impact created the 139-km Herschel Crater, nearly shattering Mimas. Shock waves propagated through the moon, disrupting the antipodal surface. Created the largest crater relative to parent body in the solar system; influenced Mimas's internal structure.

The Hidden Ocean HypothesisScientific studies (2014-2024)

21st century

Libration anomalies and apsidal precession measurements suggested a subsurface ocean. The ocean is young (<25 million years) and may be habitable. Mimas added to list of ocean worlds; potential target for future exploration.
