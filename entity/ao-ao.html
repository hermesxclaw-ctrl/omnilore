Who It Is

Ao Ao is a monstrous cannibalistic spirit in Guarani mythology — described as an enormous, woolly sheep-like creature with razor-sharp teeth, massive claws, and an insatiable appetite for human flesh. Its name echoes its cry: 'Ao Ao Ao!' — a terrifying call that echoes through the hills and is said to paralyze its victims with fear. Ao Ao is associated with the wild hills and mountain slopes, particularly the cerros of Paraguay and the surrounding region. It is said to hunt in packs, and once it selects a human target, it pursues them relentlessly. The only escape from Ao Ao, according to Guarani folklore, is to climb a palm tree — the creature, being both massive and hoofed, cannot climb, though it will circle the tree for days, howling for its prey. This detail may encode an ancient survival instruction: the pindo palm (Syagrus romanzoffiana) is a common feature of Paraguay's landscape and provides edible fruit as well as refuge. The Ao Ao myth likely also contains an ecological warning about the dangers of the wild hills, where jaguars and other predators once roamed. In some versions, Ao Ao is not merely a monster but the guardian spirit of certain hills — a being that protects a sacred place from human intrusion by consuming those who venture there without permission. Like many Guarani forest spirits, Ao Ao has survived into modern Paraguayan folklore and is still invoked to warn people — especially children — against wandering into dangerous, isolated areas.
