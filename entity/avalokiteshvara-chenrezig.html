Who It Is

Avalokiteshvara (Tibetan: Chenrezig, 'The One with the Compassionate Eye') is the bodhisattva of infinite compassion and the most important bodhisattva in Tibetan Buddhism. He is the embodiment of the compassion of all Buddhas. According to the Karandavyuha Sutra, when Avalokiteshvara saw the suffering of beings in samsara, his head shattered into eleven pieces from grief. The Buddha Amitabha reassembled the pieces into eleven heads (giving him the eleven-headed form). Then, when he reached out to help beings, his arms shattered; Amitabha gave him a thousand arms, each with an eye in the palm, so he could see and reach all suffering. The six-syllable mantra of Chenrezig — Om Mani Padme Hum — is the most famous and most recited mantra in Tibetan Buddhism, carved into rocks and prayer wheels across the Himalayas, Tibet, Mongolia, and beyond. The current Dalai Lamas are believed to be emanations of Chenrezig, making the deity both spiritually and politically central to Tibetan identity. The Potala Palace in Lhasa is named after Mount Potalaka, the celestial abode of Avalokiteshvara. Chenrezig is depicted in many forms: the four-armed form is most common, but the eleven-headed thousand-armed form (Chuchik Zhel Chuchak) is the most dramatic and recognizable. His worship embodies the core Mahayana ideal: the vow to postpone one's own liberation until every last being is freed from suffering.
