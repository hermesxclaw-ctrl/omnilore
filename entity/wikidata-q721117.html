Who It Is

Before the gods of Babylon were sorted into a cosmic bureaucracy, there was a being of pure, searing light who carried a torch into the darkness of the human soul. His name was Asaruludu, and his job was to burn away the curses that clung to the guilty and the unlucky alike. He was the divine janitor of the Mesopotamian cosmos, the one who swept away the spiritual filth that accumulated from broken oaths and accidental taboos. But woe to the oath-breaker who crossed his path: his cleansing fire could just as easily manifest as leprosy on the skin as a light in the darkness. He was a god of terrifying mercy, a healer who could just as easily wound, a being of light who was not always gentle.

Asaruludu is consistently portrayed as a purifier and protector. He acts against demons, witches, and malevolent forces, cleansing both individuals and sacred spaces. In the Surpu series, he is invoked to break curses and undo ritual impurity. His behavior is that of a divine healer-priest, wielding light as a weapon against darkness.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is Sumerian in origin, though its exact meaning is debated. A common scholarly interpretation is 'the shining one' or 'the brilliant god,' derived from components suggesting radiance and purity. The name appears in early Sumerian god lists and incantations.

Pronunciation — ah-sah-roo-LOO-doo

Aliases — Asarualudu, Asaruludug, Asaralu-du, Marduk's ancestor

Powers
Purification

Asaruludu's primary power is the ability to cleanse spiritual and physical impurity. He removes the effects of broken oaths, curses, and taboo violations. In the Surpu series, his invocation is the central ritual act for absolving the afflicted.

Limit: Requires proper ritual procedure; the afflicted must also confess and make restitution.

Light Manipulation

Asaruludu wields light as a weapon against demons and evil spirits. His 'shining face' and 'burning torch' drive away malevolent forces.

Limit: The light is specifically anti-demonic; it does not appear to have offensive applications against mortals.

Oath Enforcement

Asaruludu punishes oath-breakers with skin diseases (leprosy, scabies) and other afflictions. This makes him a terrifying witness to treaties and legal agreements.

Limit: Punishments are inflicted on the oath-breaker and their descendants; no evidence of mercy or revocation.

Exorcism

Asaruludu is invoked in exorcism rituals to expel demons from possessed individuals and haunted spaces.

Limit: Requires a trained exorcist (āšipu) to perform the ritual correctly.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No direct visual depictions of Asaruludu have been identified with certainty. In textual descriptions, he is associated with radiance and light, often described as carrying a torch or lamp. In the Surpu incantations, his 'shining face' is said to repel evil spirits. Given his association with purification, he may have been depicted with water vessels or purification implements, though this remains speculative.

Symbols — The torch or lamp (his primary symbol), water for purification, possibly the 'shining garment' mentioned in some texts. His association with light made him a natural symbol of divine truth and justice, particularly in oath-taking contexts.

Domains — Purification, ritual cleansing, exorcism, protection against evil, oath enforcement. He is a god of the liminal space between the pure and the impure, the clean and the defiled. His domain extends to both the physical realm (disease, skin conditions) and the spiritual (demonic possession, curses).

Accounts I
The Fifty Names of Marduk (Enuma Elish)Enuma Elish, Tablet VI

c. 12th-7th centuries BCE

After Marduk's victory over Tiamat and his creation of the cosmos, the great gods bestow fifty names upon him, each representing an aspect of his power. Asaruludu is the forty-eighth name, given to honor Marduk's role as the purifier who 'carries the shining torch' and 'restores the gods to their shrines.' This text effectively absorbs the older deity Asaruludu into Marduk's identity, making him a manifestation of Marduk's purifying power. The independent cult of Asaruludu is effectively ended; he survives only as an epithet of Marduk in official theology, though incantation texts continue to invoke him by name.

Accounts II
The Purification of the Afflicted (Surpu)Surpu Incantation Series

c. 7th century BCE (extant copies)

The Surpu series addresses the problem of 'mamitu' - a curse or oath that has been inadvertently broken, causing suffering and misfortune. Asaruludu is invoked as the divine purifier who can break these curses. The ritual involves the burning of symbolic materials (garlic, dates, wool) while the exorcist recites incantations calling on Asaruludu to 'shine forth' and 'drive away the evil.' The afflicted person is then cleansed with water and declared pure. The ritual restores the person to ritual purity, allowing them to re-enter society and the presence of the gods.

The Witness of the Boundary StoneKudurru inscriptions

c. 12th-7th centuries BCE

Kudurrus (boundary stones) were carved with the images and names of gods who were invoked to protect land grants and legal agreements. Asaruludu is frequently listed among these divine witnesses. The inscriptions warn that any who violates the boundary or breaks the agreement will be struck with 'leprosy, scabies, and incurable skin diseases' by Asaruludu. This made him a terrifying guarantor of property rights. The threat of divine punishment served as a powerful deterrent against land disputes and legal violations.

Asaruludu is a 'demon' or 'evil god.' — Asaruludu is a beneficial deity of purification and protection against evil. His association with disease is as a punisher of oath-breakers, not as a source of random affliction.

Asaruludu is the same as Asalluhi (the god of incantations). — While their names and functions are similar, they may be distinct deities. The matter is debated by scholars.

Asaruludu is a 'minor' or 'obscure' god of little importance. — While not a major deity like Marduk or Ishtar, Asaruludu played a significant role in purification rituals and oath enforcement, as evidenced by his frequent invocation in incantation series and kudurru inscriptions.
