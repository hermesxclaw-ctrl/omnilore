The Approach

In the winter of her fourteenth year, Maria Campbell's world collapsed into a silence that would take decades to break. Born into a Métis community in Saskatchewan where fiddle music and trapping were as natural as breath, she was thrust into a survival that forced her into the streets of Vancouver, into addiction, and finally into a voice that would change Canadian literature forever. Her memoir, Halfbreed, was not just a story—it was a reckoning, a reclaimed history that continues to resonate with Indigenous women and men across generations.

Content advisory. This dossier contains frank discussions of racism, colonialism, sexual violence, domestic abuse, addiction, sex work, and suicide. Reader discretion is advised.
Identity

Maria Campbell

Pronunciation mə-RYE-ə KAM-bəl

Etymology

The name 'Maria' is a common given name of Latin origin, derived from the Hebrew 'Miriam.' 'Campbell' is a Scottish surname, reflecting her mixed Scottish and Métis heritage.

Cultural Ownership

Métis (Canadian Indigenous), with mixed Scottish, French, Cree, English, and Irish ancestry.

Disputed Identifications

Campbell identifies as Métis, but she famously used the term 'halfbreed' in her memoir's title, a term that is now considered derogatory. She has noted the ongoing debates about the precise definition of 'Métis,' which influenced her choice.

Description

Appearance

Campbell is of Métis descent, with mixed Scottish, French, Cree, English, and Irish ancestry. She is described as a woman who carries the weight of her experiences, with a presence that commands respect. She is often photographed in traditional or dignified attire, reflecting her role as an Elder.

Symbols & Iconography

Her work is associated with symbols of Métis identity: the infinity flag, fiddle music, and the land. The title 'Halfbreed' itself is a symbol of reclaimed identity.

Behaviour & Personality

Campbell is known for her resilience, humor, and deep commitment to community. Despite the darkness of her experiences, her writing and public speaking are marked by a wry humor and a fierce determination to tell the truth. She is an activist and educator who emphasizes the importance of storytelling.

Domains & Habitats

Born in rural Saskatchewan, she later lived in Vancouver and Calgary. Her life spans the prairie Métis community and urban Indigenous life. She is a public figure and Elder in the Indigenous community.

Version Differences

The 1973 edition of Halfbreed omitted two pages that detailed her rape by RCMP officers at age 14. The 2019 restored edition includes these pages, altering the narrative's impact. Other works, such as her children's books, present a more spiritual and heritage-focused version of Métis life.
