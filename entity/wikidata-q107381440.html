Who It Is

In the winter of her fourteenth year, Maria Campbell's world collapsed into a silence that would take decades to break. Born into a Métis community in Saskatchewan where fiddle music and trapping were as natural as breath, she was thrust into a survival that forced her into the streets of Vancouver, into addiction, and finally into a voice that would change Canadian literature forever. Her memoir, Halfbreed, was not just a story—it was a reckoning, a reclaimed history that continues to resonate with Indigenous women and men across generations.

Campbell is known for her resilience, humor, and deep commitment to community. Despite the darkness of her experiences, her writing and public speaking are marked by a wry humor and a fierce determination to tell the truth. She is an activist and educator who emphasizes the importance of storytelling.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Campbell is of Métis descent, with mixed Scottish, French, Cree, English, and Irish ancestry. She is described as a woman who carries the weight of her experiences, with a presence that commands respect. She is often photographed in traditional or dignified attire, reflecting her role as an Elder.

Symbols — Her work is associated with symbols of Métis identity: the infinity flag, fiddle music, and the land. The title 'Halfbreed' itself is a symbol of reclaimed identity.

Domains — Born in rural Saskatchewan, she later lived in Vancouver and Calgary. Her life spans the prairie Métis community and urban Indigenous life. She is a public figure and Elder in the Indigenous community.

Accounts I
Halfbreed: The MemoirHalfbreed (1973, 2019)

1939-1970s

Campbell recounts her childhood in a Métis community, the racism she faced, her mother's death, her abusive marriage, her descent into sex work and addiction in Vancouver, and her eventual recovery and activism. The memoir became a landmark of Indigenous literature, sparking debates about Métis identity and influencing generations.

These books teach Métis spirituality and heritage to children, using traditional stories and figures like the buffalo and the fire spirit. They contribute to the preservation and transmission of Métis culture.
