The Approach

In the sweltering Kingston summer of 1966, a teenage drummer named Joe Isaacs couldn't keep up with the frantic pace of ska. So the band slowed down—and in that moment, a genre was born that would forever change the sound of Jamaica and the world.

Content advisory. No explicit dark content. Contains references to rude boy culture and social unrest in 1960s Jamaica, including delinquency and violence, but treated historically.
Identity

Rocksteady

Pronunciation /ˈrɒkˌstɛdi/

Known As

Rock steadyRock SteadyRocksteady music

Etymology

The term 'rocksteady' comes from a popular slower dance style, as referenced in Alton Ellis's song 'Rocksteady'. The name suggests a steady, rocking rhythm.

Cultural Ownership

Jamaican popular music, originating in Kingston, Jamaica.

Disputed Identifications

There is debate over which single is the first rocksteady record, with candidates including Alton Ellis's 'Rocksteady', Hopeton Lewis's 'Take It Easy', Derrick Morgan's 'Tougher Than Tough', and Roy Shirley's 'Hold Them'.

Description

Appearance

Not applicable (music genre).

Symbols & Iconography

Not applicable, but visual iconography includes rude boy fashion: pork pie hats, slim suits, and two-tone shoes.

Behaviour & Personality

Rocksteady is characterized by a slower tempo than ska, with a greater focus on bass lines, soulful vocals, and romantic or socially conscious lyrics. The music often features offbeat guitar rhythms and prominent bass melodies.

Domains & Habitats

Originated in Kingston, Jamaica, particularly in recording studios like Studio One and Treasure Isle. It was popular in dance halls and sound system culture.

Version Differences

Rocksteady is distinct from ska (faster tempo, more brass) and reggae (slower, more complex bass, more political/religious lyrics). It is a transitional genre.

Stories & Myths

The Birth of Rocksteady

Source Various accounts, including Studio One Drummie documentary and interviewsCulture JamaicanPeriod 1966

Summary

At Studio One, young drummer Joe Isaacs replaced Lloyd Knibbs of the Skatalites. Unable to keep up with ska's fast tempo, the music was slowed down, creating a new sound. Another account credits Lynn Taitt for suggesting a slower tempo during the recording of Hopeton Lewis's 'Take It Easy'.

Participants

Joe Isaacs

Jackie Mittoo

Lynn Taitt

Gladstone Anderson

Consequences

The new sound became rocksteady, dominating Jamaican music for two years.

Variants

Multiple origin stories exist, with different first records claimed.

The Rude Boy Era

Source Various rocksteady songs and historical accountsCulture JamaicanPeriod 1966-1968

Summary

Rocksteady coincided with the rise of rude boys, young delinquents in Kingston. Songs often depicted their lifestyle, sometimes critically, such as 'Rude Boy Gone A Jail' and 'Judge Dread'.

Participants

Rude boys

Artists like Alton Ellis, Prince Buster

Consequences

Rocksteady music reflected social tensions in post-independence Jamaica.

Variants

Some artists were sympathetic, others critical.

Transformation into Reggae

Source Historical analysesCulture JamaicanPeriod 1968-1970

Summary

Key arrangers emigrated to Canada, technology improved, and bass lines became more complex. The Rastafari movement grew, leading to more political lyrics. Dub versions of rocksteady tracks became popular, with U-Roy deejaying over Treasure Isle rhythms.

Participants

Jackie Mittoo

Lynn Taitt

U-Roy

King Tubby

Consequences

Rocksteady evolved into reggae, which gained international fame.
