Who It Is

In the sweltering Kingston summer of 1966, a teenage drummer named Joe Isaacs couldn't keep up with the frantic pace of ska. So the band slowed down—and in that moment, a genre was born that would forever change the sound of Jamaica and the world.

Rocksteady is characterized by a slower tempo than ska, with a greater focus on bass lines, soulful vocals, and romantic or socially conscious lyrics. The music often features offbeat guitar rhythms and prominent bass melodies.

Accounts I
The Birth of RocksteadyVarious accounts, including Studio One Drummie documentary and interviews

1966

At Studio One, young drummer Joe Isaacs replaced Lloyd Knibbs of the Skatalites. Unable to keep up with ska's fast tempo, the music was slowed down, creating a new sound. Another account credits Lynn Taitt for suggesting a slower tempo during the recording of Hopeton Lewis's 'Take It Easy'. The new sound became rocksteady, dominating Jamaican music for two years.

Accounts II
The Rude Boy EraVarious rocksteady songs and historical accounts

1966-1968

Rocksteady coincided with the rise of rude boys, young delinquents in Kingston. Songs often depicted their lifestyle, sometimes critically, such as 'Rude Boy Gone A Jail' and 'Judge Dread'. Rocksteady music reflected social tensions in post-independence Jamaica.

Transformation into ReggaeHistorical analyses

1968-1970

Key arrangers emigrated to Canada, technology improved, and bass lines became more complex. The Rastafari movement grew, leading to more political lyrics. Dub versions of rocksteady tracks became popular, with U-Roy deejaying over Treasure Isle rhythms. Rocksteady evolved into reggae, which gained international fame.
