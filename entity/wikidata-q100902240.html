Who It Is

In the brutal arena of the Future Diary game, where survival hinges on predicting death, Tsubaki Kasugano appears as a fragile shrine maiden—yet her devotion to a cult masks a heart consumed by vengeance and a diary that commands the loyalty of thousands.

Initially, Tsubaki presents herself as a kind and devout shrine maiden, beloved by her followers. However, this is a facade. In truth, she is deeply traumatized by her past, harboring intense hatred and a desire for revenge. She is manipulative, using her followers' devotion to control them and further her own goals. Her personality shifts between a fragile victim and a cold, calculating leader, reflecting her fractured psyche.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The surname 'Kasugano' (春日野) evokes 'spring field', while 'Tsubaki' (椿) means 'camellia', a flower symbolizing love and devotion in Japanese culture.

Pronunciation — Tsu-ba-ki Ka-su-ga-no

Aliases — Sixth, Shrine Maiden of the Omekata Cult

Ownership — Japanese pop culture, originating from the manga and anime series 'Future Diary'.

Powers
Cult Diary (盲従日記, Mōjū Nikki)

Tsubaki's Future Diary allows her to issue commands that her followers will obey without question, as they believe her words are divine revelations. She can use this to coordinate large groups, gather information, and even order her followers to sacrifice themselves.

Limit: The diary's power is limited to her followers; it does not affect non-believers. Additionally, her commands must be phrased as predictions or divine guidance, and she cannot directly control the actions of other Diary Holders.

Manipulation and Leadership

Even without her diary, Tsubaki is highly skilled at manipulating people's emotions and loyalty. She has built a large cult through charisma and playing the role of a victim, which allows her to exploit others' compassion.

Limit: Her manipulation requires time and face-to-face interaction; it is less effective on people who are skeptical or aware of her true nature.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Tsubaki is a teenage girl with long, dark hair and distinctive red eyes. She wears a traditional shrine maiden (miko) outfit, complete with a white blouse and red hakama, reflecting her role as a priestess of the Omekata cult. In her initial appearance, she appears gentle and innocent, but her expressions can shift to reveal a darker, more sinister side.

Symbols — Her diary, the 'Cult Diary' (盲従日記, Mōjū Nikki), is depicted as a small, ornate book with a red cover. The cult's symbol, a stylized eye, represents their belief in seeing the future through their leader. Tsubaki's miko attire and the cult's iconography connect her to themes of faith, manipulation, and hidden darkness.

Domains — Her primary domain is the Omekata cult's temple, a compound that serves as both a sanctuary and a prison for her followers. She is rarely seen outside this location, as she is confined there by her role and her own psychological chains.

Accounts I
The Sixth Diary Holder's ArcFuture Diary (manga/anime) - Chapters 15-20 / Episodes 10-11

Contemporary (set in modern Japan)

Tsubaki is introduced as the leader of the Omekata cult, which worships the future. She contacts Yuki and Yuno, claiming she is being held against her will by the cult and needs their help to escape. Yuki, sympathetic, agrees to help. However, it is revealed that Tsubaki is actually the mastermind behind the cult's activities, using her diary to control her followers. She had orchestrated her own 'imprisonment' to lure other Diary Holders to her temple, where she could eliminate them. In a confrontation, Tsubaki's true nature is exposed, and she is killed by Yuno, who uses her own diary to counter Tsubaki's commands. Tsubaki's death eliminates the Sixth Diary Holder from the game, and Yuki and Yuno escape the temple. The arc reveals the darker side of the Diary Game and sets the stage for further conflicts.
