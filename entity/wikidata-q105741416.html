The Approach

In the shadowed halls of the Greek underworld, a name echoes with the weight of death itself—Hades, the unseen god who rules the realm of the departed. But across the world, in the land of the rising sun, another queen of the dead holds court: Izanami, the mother who became the mistress of Yomi. Their stories, born from different cultures, yet bound by the universal human struggle with mortality, offer a fascinating glimpse into how civilizations personify the inevitable. This dossier explores the depths of both figures, their origins, their powers, and their enduring legacy in myth and modern imagination.

Content advisory. Contains themes of death, the afterlife, divine conflict, and mythological violence. Some versions of the Izanami myth include themes of decay and pollution. Reader discretion is advised for younger audiences.
Identity

Hades (Ἁΐδης)

Pronunciation Hades: /ˈheɪdiːz/ (HAY-deez). Izanami: /iːzɑːˈnɑːmi/ (ee-zah-NAH-mee).

Known As

PlutoPloutonAidoneusThe Unseen OneZeus KatachthoniosIzanami (イザナミ)Izanami-no-MikotoIzanami-no-Kami

Etymology

Hades: from Greek ἀϊδής (aïdēs), 'unseen', possibly from *ἀ-ϝιδ- 'not seen'. Izanami: from Old Japanese, likely 'she who invites' (from いざなう, izanau, 'to invite').

Cultural Ownership

Hades is a figure from ancient Greek religion and mythology, belonging to the Hellenic cultural sphere. Izanami is a figure from Japanese mythology, belonging to the Shinto and Japanese cultural sphere.

Disputed Identifications

Hades is sometimes conflated with the underworld itself, and with Pluto (the Roman equivalent). Izanami is sometimes identified with the earth goddess or with a death goddess, but she is specifically the goddess of creation and death in Shinto. There is no direct identification between Hades and Izanami in any known tradition; the pairing here is a research request.

Description

Appearance

Hades: Typically depicted as a bearded, mature man, often with a dark beard, holding a bident or a staff, sometimes with Cerberus at his side. He is often shown with a helmet (the Helm of Darkness) that renders him invisible. In art, he is sometimes shown on a throne, or with Persephone. Izanami: In Japanese art, she is often depicted as a beautiful woman in Heian-era court dress, but after her death and descent to Yomi, she is described as decaying and maggot-ridden. In some depictions, she is shown as a demonic figure.

Symbols & Iconography

Hades: The bident, the Helm of Darkness, the key to the underworld, Cerberus, the cypress, the narcissus flower, and the scepter. Izanami: The spear (Amenonuhoko) used in creation, and the symbols of creation and death, such as the underworld and the land of the dead.

Behaviour & Personality

Hades: In Greek myth, Hades is often portrayed as stern, unyielding, but not evil. He is just and impartial, though his abduction of Persephone is a central myth. He is rarely seen in myths, and his name was often avoided to prevent attracting his attention. Izanami: In Japanese myth, she is initially a creator goddess, but after her death, she becomes a goddess of the underworld, and she is vengeful and possessive, threatening to kill a thousand people a day when Izanagi sees her decayed form.

Domains & Habitats

Hades: The underworld, also called Hades, a realm beneath the earth, divided into regions like Elysium, Asphodel Meadows, and Tartarus. He also has a palace in the underworld. Izanami: Yomi, the land of the dead, which is a dark and polluted underworld, from which she cannot return.

Version Differences

Hades: In earlier Greek sources, he is a shadowy figure; later, he becomes more prominent, especially in mystery cults. Roman Pluto is more benign and associated with wealth. Izanami: The Kojiki and Nihon Shoki have slightly different accounts of her death and her relationship with Izanagi, with the Nihon Shoki offering alternative versions.

Powers & Abilities

Ruler of the Underworld

Source Version Hades: Homeric Hymn to Demeter, Iliad; Izanami: Kojiki

Description

Hades has absolute authority over the dead and the underworld. He decides the fate of souls and ensures that no one leaves his realm without permission. Izanami, as ruler of Yomi, holds dominion over the dead and can command the spirits that reside there.

Limitations

Hades is bound by the Fates and the laws of the cosmos; he cannot bring the dead back to life permanently. Izanami is confined to Yomi and cannot leave, as she has eaten the food of the underworld.

Contradictions

In some myths, Hades is invincible, but he is also subject to Zeus's authority. Izanami's power is limited to Yomi; she cannot affect the living world directly except through curses.

Helm of Darkness

Source Version Hesiod's Theogony, later mythographers

Description

Hades possesses a helmet that grants invisibility, given to him by the Cyclopes. He lends it to heroes like Perseus and Athena.

Limitations

Only makes the wearer invisible, not intangible.

Contradictions

None known.

Creation and Death

Source Version Kojiki, Nihon Shoki

Description

Izanami, along with Izanagi, created the Japanese islands and many deities. After her death, she became a death goddess, capable of causing death to the living.

Limitations

Her creative power was diminished after death; she is unable to return to the living world.

Contradictions

Some versions suggest she still has creative power in Yomi, but it is twisted.

Stories & Myths

The Abduction of Persephone

Source Homeric Hymn to DemeterCulture Ancient GreekPeriod Archaic period (c. 7th century BCE)

Summary

Hades, with Zeus's permission, abducts Persephone while she is picking flowers in a meadow. Demeter, her mother, searches for her and causes a famine. Eventually, a deal is struck: Persephone spends part of the year with Hades and part with her mother, explaining the seasons.

Participants

Consequences

The establishment of the seasons; the Eleusinian Mysteries; Persephone becomes queen of the underworld.

Variants

Some versions have Persephone eating a pomegranate seed, binding her to the underworld; others have her willingly staying with Hades.

The Creation of the World and the Death of Izanami

Source KojikiCulture JapanesePeriod Mythological age (recorded in 712 CE)

Summary

Izanami and Izanagi, the divine pair, are tasked with creating the land. They stir the ocean with a jeweled spear, and the first island forms. They marry and give birth to many islands and deities. When Izanami gives birth to the fire god, Kagutsuchi, she is severely burned and dies. She descends to Yomi, the land of the dead. Izanagi follows her, but she has already eaten the food of the underworld and cannot return. He sees her decayed body, and she becomes enraged, pursuing him. He escapes and blocks the entrance with a boulder, and they divorce.

Participants

Izanami

Izanagi

Kagutsuchi

Consequences

Izanami becomes the ruler of Yomi; Izanagi performs purification rituals, giving birth to other deities, including Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo. The separation of life and death is established.

Variants

The Nihon Shoki has several variants, including one where Izanami is killed by a different deity, and another where she is transformed into a demon.

Abduction of Persephone

Source Homeric Hymn to Demeter

Description

Hades abducts Persephone without her consent, causing her mother's grief and a worldwide famine. This is a non-consensual act, though in later versions it is sometimes romanticized.

Scholarly Context

Scholars debate the original meaning, some seeing it as a metaphor for the cycle of death and rebirth, others as a reflection of marriage customs in ancient Greece.

Izanami's Death and Decay

Source Kojiki

Description

Izanami dies giving birth to the fire god, and when Izanagi visits her in Yomi, he finds her body rotting and covered in maggots. She is ashamed and enraged, and she pursues him to kill him.

Scholarly Context

This myth reflects Japanese concepts of death pollution (kegare) and the separation of life and death.

Contradictions & Disputes

Hades' character

Position A

Hades is evil and malevolent.

Position A — evidence

Some modern adaptations portray him as a villain.

Position B

Hades is just and impartial, not evil.

Position B — evidence

Ancient sources rarely depict him as malicious; he is simply the ruler of the dead.

Scholarly Consensus

Scholars generally agree that ancient Hades was not evil, but a stern and fair god.

Izanami's creation role

Position A

Izanami is a death goddess only.

Position A — evidence

Her role in Yomi is prominent.

Position B

Izanami is a creator goddess who becomes a death goddess.

Position B — evidence

She participates in the creation of the islands and deities.

Scholarly Consensus

She is both a creator and death goddess; her death is a pivotal event.

The nature of the underworld

Position A

The Greek underworld is a place of punishment.

Position A — evidence

Tartarus is for the wicked.

Position B

The Greek underworld is a neutral place for all dead.

Position B — evidence

Most souls go to the Asphodel Meadows, a neutral realm.

Scholarly Consensus

The underworld has multiple regions with different purposes.
