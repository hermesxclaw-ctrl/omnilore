Who It Is

In the shadowed halls of the Greek underworld, a name echoes with the weight of death itself—Hades, the unseen god who rules the realm of the departed. But across the world, in the land of the rising sun, another queen of the dead holds court: Izanami, the mother who became the mistress of Yomi. Their stories, born from different cultures, yet bound by the universal human struggle with mortality, offer a fascinating glimpse into how civilizations personify the inevitable. This dossier explores the depths of both figures, their origins, their powers, and their enduring legacy in myth and modern imagination.

Hades: In Greek myth, Hades is often portrayed as stern, unyielding, but not evil. He is just and impartial, though his abduction of Persephone is a central myth. He is rarely seen in myths, and his name was often avoided to prevent attracting his attention. Izanami: In Japanese myth, she is initially a creator goddess, but after her death, she becomes a goddess of the underworld, and she is vengeful and possessive, threatening to kill a thousand people a day when Izanagi sees her decayed form.

Powers
Ruler of the Underworld

Hades has absolute authority over the dead and the underworld. He decides the fate of souls and ensures that no one leaves his realm without permission. Izanami, as ruler of Yomi, holds dominion over the dead and can command the spirits that reside there.

Limit: Hades is bound by the Fates and the laws of the cosmos; he cannot bring the dead back to life permanently. Izanami is confined to Yomi and cannot leave, as she has eaten the food of the underworld.

Helm of Darkness

Hades possesses a helmet that grants invisibility, given to him by the Cyclopes. He lends it to heroes like Perseus and Athena.

Limit: Only makes the wearer invisible, not intangible.

Creation and Death

Izanami, along with Izanagi, created the Japanese islands and many deities. After her death, she became a death goddess, capable of causing death to the living.

Limit: Her creative power was diminished after death; she is unable to return to the living world.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Hades: Typically depicted as a bearded, mature man, often with a dark beard, holding a bident or a staff, sometimes with Cerberus at his side. He is often shown with a helmet (the Helm of Darkness) that renders him invisible. In art, he is sometimes shown on a throne, or with Persephone. Izanami: In Japanese art, she is often depicted as a beautiful woman in Heian-era court dress, but after her death and descent to Yomi, she is described as decay

Symbols — Hades: The bident, the Helm of Darkness, the key to the underworld, Cerberus, the cypress, the narcissus flower, and the scepter. Izanami: The spear (Amenonuhoko) used in creation, and the symbols of creation and death, such as the underworld and the land of the dead.

Domains — Hades: The underworld, also called Hades, a realm beneath the earth, divided into regions like Elysium, Asphodel Meadows, and Tartarus. He also has a palace in the underworld. Izanami: Yomi, the land of the dead, which is a dark and polluted underworld, from which she cannot return.

Accounts I
The Abduction of PersephoneHomeric Hymn to Demeter

Archaic period (c. 7th century BCE)

Hades, with Zeus's permission, abducts Persephone while she is picking flowers in a meadow. Demeter, her mother, searches for her and causes a famine. Eventually, a deal is struck: Persephone spends part of the year with Hades and part with her mother, explaining the seasons. The establishment of the seasons; the Eleusinian Mysteries; Persephone becomes queen of the underworld.

Accounts II
The Creation of the World and the Death of IzanamiKojiki

Mythological age (recorded in 712 CE)

Izanami and Izanagi, the divine pair, are tasked with creating the land. They stir the ocean with a jeweled spear, and the first island forms. They marry and give birth to many islands and deities. When Izanami gives birth to the fire god, Kagutsuchi, she is severely burned and dies. She descends to Yomi, the land of the dead. Izanagi follows her, but she has already eaten the food of the underworld and cannot return. He sees her decayed body, and she becomes enraged, pursuing him. He escapes and blocks the entrance with a boulder, and they divorce. Izanami becomes the ruler of Yomi; Izanagi performs purification rituals, giving birth to other deities, including Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo. The separation of life and death is established.
