Who It Is

At the gates of Enies Lobby, two giants stood taller than the courthouse itself — axes in hand, loyalty bought with a lie that had held for fifty years. One of them was Kashii, a warrior of Elbaph whose strength could hurl a warship but whose trust could be stolen with a single false story.

Attested personality: loyal, good-natured, simple and trusting, with strong warrior pride and reverence for Dorry and Brogy as legendary bosses. Easily deceived due to faith in Marine lie that their captains were imprisoned and would be freed after 100 years of guard duty (actually 50 years served at time of Enies Lobby). Shows remorse and gratitude when Usopp reveals truth (Ch.392-393), immediately defects from World Government and aids Straw Hats and Galley-La. Name & Tongue

Etymology — In-universe, name given by Eiichiro Oda; out-of-universe shares orthography with Japanese toponym Kashii (香椎) in Higashi-ku, Fukuoka. Toponym 香椎 combines 香 ka 'fragrance, incense' + 椎 shii 'chinquapin / Castanopsis tree' (Japanese evergreen oak). The One Piece character name is written in katakana カ

Pronunciation — Japanese: /ka.ɕi.i/ (ka-shi-i), three morae, pitch accent flat; English dub: /kɑːˈʃiː/ kah-SHEE. Fan and official romanization consistently 'Kashii' with long final vowel.

Powers
Giant Physiology — Superhuman Strength and Durability

As a giant, Kashii possesses strength orders of magnitude above humans: can lift and swing a building-sized axe, demolish stone gates, knock out multiple Galley-La shipwrights and Marine battleship structures, and endure cannon fire and Franky/Galley-La counterattacks. Durability allows survival of falls and blunt trauma that would kill humans. Lifespan ~3x human (Elbaph giants live ~300 years).

Limit: Not invulnerable; defeated by combined Galley-La Company, Franky Family, and later by Straw Hat alliance tactics (being tripped and tied). Vulnerable to deception and lacks Haki attestation. Strength is raw physical, not Devil Fruit-based. Size makes him an easy target for siege weapons.

Axe Mastery — Giant Battle Axe

Wields a massive double-headed axe with haft taller than a Marine warship mast. Can cleave stone, create shockwaves, and perform sweeping attacks covering wide area. Technique is brute-force chopping, not refined swordsmanship.

Limit: Weapon is unwieldy against small, agile opponents (e.g., Luffy, Zoro). Requires both hands; disarmed when tripped. No named technique (unlike Dorry/Brogy's 'Hakoku').

Warrior Experience — 50 Years Guard Duty and Piracy

Former member of Giant Warrior Pirates before capture; 50 years combat experience as Enies Lobby gate guard. Familiar with Marine/Government protocols and Elbaph warrior code.

Limit: Experience is limited to guard duty and prior piracy as junior member; not a captain-level fighter. Easily outwitted by World Government lie for decades, indicating low cunning/political awareness.

Allied Combat — Paired Fighting with Oimo

Fights in tandem with fellow giant Oimo, coordinating gate defense and combined strikes. Duo can hold a narrow strait against hundreds of attackers.

Limit: Dependent on partnership; when separated or when truth revealed, duo abandons post. No combination attack name attested.

Accounts I
The Giant Guards of Enies LobbyOne Piece Manga Chapters 377-384; Anime Episodes 266-272

2005 (publication), in-universe 1522 (Enies Lobby incident)

Kashii and Oimo are introduced as the two giant guards protecting the gates of Enies Lobby, the judicial island where CP9 holds Nico Robin and Franky. The Straw Hat Pirates, Galley-La Company and Franky Family storm the island. Kashii and Oimo initially repel the combined force, crushing ships and blocking the drawbridge with their bodies and axes. Usopp (as Sogeking) confronts them, and after initial defeat, the alliance uses strategy — Franky and Sodom/Gomorrah trip the giants with ropes — to bring them down and pass through. This is Kashii's primary combat showcase. Gates breached, allowing invasion of Enies Lobby proper; establishes giants as sympathetic antagonists rather than villains; sets up reveal of their deception.

The Fifty-Year Lie — Dorry, Brogy and the World Government DeceptionOne Piece Manga Chapters 392-393; Anime Episode 271

2005 (publication), in-universe flashback c. 1472-1522 (50 years prior)

Flashback reveals Kashii and Oimo were once junior members of the Giant Warrior Pirates led by Dorry and Brogy. After their captains were left dueling on Little Garden, the two were captured by Marines. A Marine (later revealed to be part of World Government manipulation) told them Dorry and Brogy were imprisoned and would be executed unless Kashii and Oimo served as guards at Enies Lobby for 100 years. Believing the lie out of loyalty, they served faithfully for 50 years. Usopp reveals the truth: Dorry and Brogy were never prisoners and had been dueling voluntarily on Little Garden the entire time, as witnessed by the Straw Hats. Realizing they were tricked, Kashii and Oimo defect. Kashii and Oimo switch sides, attack Marine battleships during the Buster Call, help the Straw Hats escape, and return to Elbaph. Establishes theme of Elbaph honor vs World Government deceit that recurs throughout series.

Escape from the Buster Call and Return to ElbaphOne Piece Manga Chapters 398-430; Anime Episodes 277-312

2005-2006 (publication), in-universe 1522

After defecting, Kashii and Oimo fight alongside the Straw Hats against the Marine Buster Call fleet (10 battleships, 5 Vice Admirals). They smash Marine ships, rescue the Galley-La and Franky Family, and help secure the escape route via the Bridge of Hesitation. After Enies Lobby's destruction, they are seen sailing away with the Straw Hats' allies, intending to return to Elbaph and reunite with Dorry and Brogy. Oda's cover story later confirms their safe return. Completes redemption arc; giants regain freedom; foreshadows Elbaph as future destination for Straw Hats.

Accounts II
Reformation as New Giant Warrior Pirates under HajrudinOne Piece Manga Chapters 713, 716-718, 869 (Dressrosa and Whole Cake Island cover story)

2013-2017 (publication), in-universe 1522 (post-timeskip, 2 years after Enies Lobby)

Two years later, Kashii and Oimo reappear as members of Hajrudin's New Giant Warrior Pirates, a crew of six Elbaph giants seeking to revive the Giant Warrior Pirates' glory. They work as mercenaries in Buggy's Delivery (a pirate mercenary dispatch). In Dressrosa, Hajrudin competes in the Corrida Colosseum for the Mera Mera no Mi; Kashii and comrades (Gerd, Goldberg, Stansen, Road) are seen supporting him. Later, in the 'From the Decks of the World' cover story (Ch.866), Kashii and Oimo are shown working with Hajrudin, having left Buggy's service after Hajrudin pledges loyalty to Straw Hat Luffy as part of the Straw Hat Grand Fleet (Chapter 800). Re-establishes Kashii in current continuity; ties Enies Lobby giants to Elbaph's next generation; sets up their role in final saga.

The Elbaph Arc — Return Home (Ongoing)One Piece Manga Chapters 1130-1136+; Anime not yet adapted as of 2025

2024-2025 (publication), in-universe 1522 (Egghead aftermath)

With the Straw Hats finally arriving at Elbaph (Ch.1130 onward), Kashii appears among the Elbaph villagers welcoming the crew and reuniting with Dorry and Brogy, who have returned as kings. Kashii is shown in village scenes, celebrating, providing exposition on Elbaph history, the Adam Tree, and Prince Loki's imprisonment. While not yet given a solo fight, he participates in the defense of Elbaph against the God's Knights intrusion (Ch.1134-1135) alongside other giants, wielding his axe against Holy Knight forces. Arc is ongoing; full role not yet concluded. Pays off 20-year foreshadowing of Elbaph; Kashii transitions from comic gate guard to member of Elbaph defense force; Usopp's promised reunion with giants fulfilled.

Disambiguation Story — Kashii-gū and the Kashii District (Non-Fictional Counterpart)Kashii-gū Engi, Nihon Shoki references, Fukuoka City municipal history; Shinto shrine records

Legendary 3rd century (Empress Jingū) to present; shrine founded traditionally 200 CE, current structures 1801

Separate from the One Piece character, the toponym Kashii refers to a district in Higashi-ku, Fukuoka, centered on Kashii-gū shrine. Legend holds Empress Jingū built the shrine after returning from Korean expedition, enshrining Emperor Chūai. The name appears in Heian records and gave its name to the Battle of Kashii (1615) during the Osaka Summer Campaign, the Katori-class cruiser Kashii (launched 1940), and the JR Kashii Line (1904). This story is included to prevent conflation: the manga character shares only phonetics with this millennium-old place name. Explains why search results mix shrine, stations, warship and manga character; clarifies that Oda's naming is likely coincidental or toponymic homage, not mythological borrowing.

Kashii is a Shinto deity or shrine spirit from Kashii-gū — Kashii-gū enshrines Empress Jingū and Emperor Chūai, not a giant named Kashii. The One Piece character is a fictional giant with no connection to Shinto theology. The shared name is homophony between toponym 香椎 and katakana カシイ.

Kashii and Oimo are the same character or interchangeable — They are distinct giants: Kashii wields an axe and has a different helmet/beard design; Oimo wields a club. They have separate Vivre Card entries and are always listed as a pair but are two individuals.

Kashii is a captain-level giant like Dorry and Brogy — Kashii was a junior member of the original Giant Warrior Pirates and later a subordinate in Hajrudin's New Giant Warrior Pirates. He has never held captain rank; Hajrudin is captain.

Kashii appears as a major playable character in most One Piece games — Kashii is a minor NPC/boss in Pirate Warriors and Treasure Cruise; he is not playable in most titles. Playable giants are typically Dorry, Brogy, Hajrudin.

The cruiser Kashii and the One Piece character are related (ship named after character) — Chronologically impossible: cruiser Kashii launched 1940, One Piece debuted 1997. Ship was named after Kashii-gū shrine per IJN naming convention for training cruisers (shrines).
