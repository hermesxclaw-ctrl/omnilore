Form

No canonical visual description of Ame no Tomi survives in primary sources. Unlike major kami such as Amaterasu or Susanoo, Ame no Tomi is not depicted in classical Japanese art, and no standardized iconography exists. In modern artistic interpretations (primarily in manga, anime, and games), Ame no Tomi is typically imagined as a figure associated with abundance — often depicted as a benevolent spirit carrying sheaves of rice, coins, or other symbols of prosperity.

Accounts

The Kojiki mentions Ame no Tomi in the context of Emperor Suizei's reign, the second emperor of Japan according to traditional chronology. The reference appears to connect Ame no Tomi to the establishment of court rituals or the divine lineage of the imperial family. The text provides no narrative detail, adventure, or mythic episode involving the deity.

Because Ame no Tomi appears within the Kojiki narrative of the early imperial reigns, he is understood as a servitor kami of the heavenly court whose name is preserved to account for the ancestral guardianship of the new ruling house. In later shrine tradition, figures of this class were honored through the conferral of priestly roles and through ritual oblations at the shrine of the imperial ancestors. Ame no Tomi's presence in the genealogical record functions less as a storied hero than as a marker of the continuity between the heavenly deities and the earthly imperial succession, reminding readers that the early emperors reigned with divine support rather than alone.

What We Don't Know

Significant gaps exist in the research on Ame no Tomi. (1) Only one primary source (the Kojiki) mentions this figure, and the reference is brief. (2) No scholarly articles specifically dedicated to Ame no Tomi were identified. (3) The exact passage in the Kojiki could not be verified from the original text; the reference is based on secondary mentions and translations. (4) No archaeological evidence, shrine records, or ritual texts directly referencing Ame no Tomi were found. (5) The relationship between Ame no Tomi and the broader Shinto concept of 'tomi' (wealth/abundance) requires further investigation. (6) Japanese-language scholarship may contain additional references not accessible through English-language sources. (7) The deity's role in the reign of Emperor Suizei is unclear due to the legendary nature of early imperial chronology.
