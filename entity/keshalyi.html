Who It Is

In the shadowed forests of the Carpathians, a people of shimmering fairy women carry a curse older than any plague: the Keshalyi, Romani spirits of overflowing fertility, whose queen’s coerced marriage to a demon king is said to have birthed disease itself.

Keshalyi are consistently characterized as gentle, kind, generous, and benevolent spirits who grant wishes and bestow fertility. They are portrayed as reluctant participants in violence and as victims of predatory Loçolico spirits. Their behavior toward humans is described as helpful, especially in matters of infertility and illness, provided they are approached with respect and offerings. Powers
Fertility bestowal

Keshalyi are said to possess overflowing fertility and to be able to grant fertility to humans, particularly to those experiencing unexplained infertility. Petitioners invoke them using the phrase "Keshal yi lisperesn" and offerings, asking for conception and healthy childbirth.

Limit: Their aid is described as most effective when no physical cause for infertility can be determined, implying a focus on spiritual or fate‑related obstacles. Ritual instructions emphasize respectful offerings and promises of future thanks, suggesting that their help is conditional on proper ritual etiquette.

Wish‑granting

Keshalyi are portrayed as very good at granting people's wishes, especially when approached in their mountain realm or invoked through ritual. Their queen Ana, in particular, is said to be able to fulfill any wish if encountered and approached with veneration and gentle manners.

Limit: Access to Ana is rare; she hides within her palace and only occasionally ventures out in the form of a golden toad. The conditions for successful wish‑granting include recognition, respectful approach, and appropriate offerings.

Magic‑working

Keshalyi are described as magic‑working spirits, with their very name linked (speculatively) to words for wizard and magic. They are involved in diagnostic and healing practices in which shamans and fairy magicians negotiate with disease spirits born from Ana's union with the Loçolico king.

Limit: Details of specific techniques, spells, or rituals are not provided in accessible sources; the description remains general. Their magic is framed as benevolent and healing rather than offensive.

Healing of illness

Ana, as queen of the Keshalyi, is said to be able to heal any illness, including those caused by her own children—the disease spirits. Keshalyi collectively participate in rituals aimed at negotiating with these spirits to cure sickness.

Limit: Healing is contingent on successful negotiation with disease spirits and on proper ritual practice. The saga of Ana and the King of Demons is used as a diagnostic tool, implying that knowledge of each disease spirit's story is necessary.

Longevity and cursed marriage

Keshalyi are said to live long enough that some reach the age of 999. A curse imposed as the price of Ana's freedom decrees that any Keshalyi who reaches 999 must marry one of the Loçolico spirits.

Limit: This is more a narrative condition than a power; it implies extraordinary longevity but frames it as a burden rather than a gift.

Symbols — The most prominent symbolic association is with the spindle and the act of spinning, linked to their name and to the phrase "Keshal yi lisperesn" ("Keshalyi spin") used in invocations. This connects them conceptually to fate‑spinning deities and to the threads of life and fertility. Offerings recommended in occult sources—milk, fruit brandy (palinka), cakes, and cookies—function as ritual symbols of nourishment and abundance.

Their realm is liminal—between human settlements and wild nature, and between the surface world and the underworld ruled by Loçolico. Accounts I
The saga of Ana and the King of the LoçolicoJudika Illes' Encyclopedia of Spirits (summarized in Occult World and Kaleidoscopic Path Wiki); Creatures of Myth Wiki "The Children of Ana".

Traditional oral folklore of uncertain date; recorded in modern secondary sources in the 20th–21st centuries.

Ana, the beautiful and benevolent queen of the Keshalyi, lives with her fairy entourage in a magical palace atop a sacred mountain. The king of the malevolent Loçolico spirits falls in love with her and repeatedly proposes marriage. When she refuses, he sends his minions to devour her Keshalyi attendants. To save her people from extinction, Ana agrees to marry him. Their union is coerced and tragic: Ana, a goddess‑like figure of fertility, conceives repeatedly, but each child is a horrific disease spirit that brings new illnesses into the world. After bearing multiple such offspring, Ana is horrified and begs for release. The Loçolico king eventually grants her a divorce, but only on the condition that any Keshalyi who reaches the age of 999 must marry one of his Loçolico. Ana withdraws to her mountain palace, appearing only as a golden toad, yet retains the power to grant wishes and heal illnesses. Accounts II
Invocation of the Keshalyi for fertilityEncyclopedia of Spirits; Occult World article "Keshalyi".

Modern ritual practice described in 21st‑century occult literature.

A ritual is described for petitioning the Keshalyi to heal infertility. The practitioner writes the phrase "Keshal yi lisperesn" ("Keshalyi spin") with glue on cardboard, sprinkles gold or red glitter over the letters, and decorates the card. While it dries, an offering table is prepared with milk, fruit brandy, cakes, and cookies. The practitioner then chants the phrase aloud like a mantra until feeling the presence of the Keshalyi, at which point they make their request and promise a larger offering table when the request is fulfilled, possibly annually or after a healthy birth. The ritual aims to secure conception and healthy childbirth, reinforcing the Keshalyi's role as fertility spirits and integrating them into contemporary magical practice.
