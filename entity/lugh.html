Who They Are

Lugh (Lugus in Gaulish) is the god of all skills, crafts, war, and the harvest — known as 'Samildánach' ('Many-Skilled'). He is the grandson of the Fomorian tyrant Balor, whose death Lugh engineers in the Second Battle of Mag Tuired. Lugh gained entry to the court of King Nuada by declaring himself a master of every craft: smith, carpenter, poet, harper, warrior, sorcerer, physician, and more. When challenged, he demonstrated each skill in turn. Lugh leads the Tuatha Dé Danann against the Fomorians (including his grandfather Balor) and slays Balor with a sling shot (or spear) through his evil eye. Lugh is the father of the hero Cú Chulainn (with Deichtine, Conchobar's sister). He is the patron of festivals, especially Lughnasadh (August 1), the harvest festival that marks the beginning of autumn. Lugh is associated with the sun, but not as a 'sun god' in the strict sense — rather, he embodies the bright, radiant aspect of skill and intelligence. His spear (one of the four treasures of the Tuatha Dé Danann) was invincible in battle. He is depicted as a handsome young warrior carrying a spear and a sling. The city of Lugdunum (modern Lyon, France) was named after him.

Attested in Cath Maige Tuired / Lebor Gabála ÉrennEra Medieval Irish (recorded 11th-12th c.), reflecting earlier oral tradition
