Who It Is

From Earth, Jupiter’s Great Red Spot looks like a timeless storm—a swirling red eye that has watched our planet for centuries. In the SCP Foundation’s universe, that eye is not weather at all, but the scar of a broken weapon: SCP-2399, a vast alien machine lodged deep in Jupiter’s atmosphere, quietly repairing itself under a constant stream of commands from another galaxy. It is not angry, not vengeful, not even aware of us in any human sense. It is simply doing what it was built to do—finish its repairs, leave Jupiter, and turn Earth into a barren world.

Attested: SCP-2399 exhibits no personality in the human sense; it behaves as an automated weapon platform executing programmed directives. Its behavior includes continuous self-repair via deployment of octopoid drones, reception and decoding of electromagnetic signals from the Triangulum Galaxy, and occasional activation of antimatter-based weaponry to maintain or test its systems. It demonstrates precise targeting and tracking capabilities, suggesting sophisticated sensor arrays and computational control. Powers
Antimatter-based planetary weaponry

Attested: SCP-2399 possesses highly advanced antimatter-based weapons capable of creating spatial disruptions and devastating atmospheric phenomena. In the SCP article, these weapons are responsible for the large red vortex in Jupiter’s atmosphere, identified with the Great Red Spot. The implication is that, at full operational capacity, these weapons could strip a planet’s atmosphere, destabilize its crust, and render it barren—an SK-Class "Barren Earth" end-of-the-world scenario.

Limit: Attested: Due to extensive damage, SCP-2399’s weapon systems are not fully functional. Its current output is sufficient to maintain the Great Red Spot but not to leave Jupiter and attack Earth. The Foundation’s BARRIER Array attempts to interfere with its command signals, potentially limiting coordinated weapon activation.

Self-repair via octopoid drones

Attested: SCP-2399 continuously deploys numerous small, octopoid mechanical drones that search Jupiter’s atmosphere and nearby moons for components. These drones retrieve materials and perform repairs on the main structure, gradually increasing its completion percentage. Computer models in the article estimate its repair progress and annual rate.

Limit: Attested: The repair process is slow and constrained by available materials and interference from Foundation satellites. The article notes a completion rate that has increased over decades but remains below full functionality.

Limitless internal power supply

Attested: SCP-2399 appears to possess a virtually limitless power source, enabling continuous operation of repair drones, weapon systems, and communication arrays despite severe damage and centuries of activity.

Limit: Unknown: The nature of this power source (fusion, zero-point energy, antimatter reactors, or other exotic mechanisms) is not described. No explicit failure modes are given.

Advanced electromagnetic shielding

Attested: SCP-2399 is protected by sophisticated electromagnetic shielding, making direct physical or energy-based attacks by human technology ineffective. This shielding also complicates observation and interference.

Limit: Attested: The Foundation’s BARRIER Array can jam incoming command signals, indicating that while SCP-2399’s shielding is formidable, it is not absolute against carefully engineered long-range interference.

Precise tracking and targeting system

Attested: SCP-2399 possesses a highly accurate targeting system capable of tracking planetary bodies and, by implication, specific locations on those bodies. This system underlies its capacity to aim antimatter weaponry and coordinate drone deployment.

Limit: Attested: Damage to the structure may impair some targeting functions, contributing to its current focus on repair rather than offensive action.

Intergalactic communication reception

Attested: SCP-2399 receives a continuous stream of complex, encoded electromagnetic communications from the Triangulum Galaxy, interpreted as command signals instructing it to complete repairs and execute its mission. The Foundation’s BARRIER Array is designed to intercept and jam these transmissions.

Limit: Attested: The BARRIER Array can disrupt or block these signals within a defined perimeter, though the long-term reliability of this jamming is uncertain.

Planetary destruction capability (SK-Class "Barren Earth" scenario)

Attested: The SCP file explicitly classifies SCP-2399 as capable of causing an SK-Class "Barren Earth" end-of-the-world event if it reaches full operational status and targets Earth. This implies the ability to render the planet uninhabitable, likely through atmospheric stripping, crustal destabilization, and large-scale energy release.

Limit: Attested: As of the article’s timeframe, SCP-2399 is below 75% completion, and the Foundation has contingency plans (Protocol LEGIONNAIRE-5) to respond if it surpasses that threshold.

Accounts I
SCP-2399 - A Malfunctioning Destroyer (core containment file)SCP Wiki main article (scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-2399)

Mid-2010s onward

The core SCP file presents SCP-2399 as a massive, damaged alien weapon embedded in Jupiter’s lower atmosphere. Since its visual discovery in 1963, Foundation satellites have observed it using antimatter weaponry to maintain the Great Red Spot and deploying octopoid drones to repair itself. Computer models estimate its completion at 59%, with an annual repair rate that has increased over decades. Physical containment is impossible; instead, the Foundation suppresses public knowledge, monitors reconstruction, and operates the BARRIER Array to jam command signals from the Triangulum Galaxy. If SCP-2399 surpasses 75% completion or breaches the jamming perimeter, Protocol LEGIONNAIRE-5 will be enacted as a last-resort response to prevent an SK-Class "Barren Earth" scenario. The file establishes SCP-2399 as one of the most severe existential threats in the SCP universe, reshaping Foundation priorities around long-term surveillance, misinformation campaigns, and interplanetary infrastructure. It also reframes the Great Red Spot as a visible sign of ongoing alien weapon activity.

Arrival and the Great Red Spot (historical backstory)SCP Foundation Wikia (Fandom) summary and cross-references to SCP Wiki tales

Narrative set in 1665; written in the 2010s

According to extended lore summarized on the Fandom page, SCP-2399 entered the Solar System in 1665 and collided with Jupiter’s moon Io before coming to rest in Jupiter’s atmosphere. Italian astronomer Giovanni Cassini allegedly witnessed its arrival, and the activation of its weaponry caused the formation of the Great Red Spot. This retroactive mythologization ties a real historical figure and astronomical phenomenon into the SCP narrative, suggesting that humanity has unknowingly observed the effects of SCP-2399 for centuries. The story deepens the sense of cosmic dread by implying that SCP-2399 has been present and active long before modern occult organizations existed, and that a major planetary feature is the scar of its weaponry.

The Broken God and Baja California (occult war event)SCP Foundation Wikia (Fandom) meta page; cross-links to SCP-001 TwistedGears-Kaktus proposal and related documents

Narrative set in 1942; written in the 2010s

Extended lore describes a major event during World War II in which Robert Bumaro of the Church of the Broken God created a false Mekhane (the Broken God) in Baja California. The Allied Occult Initiative and the SCP Foundation collaborated to teleport SCP-2399 from Jupiter to Baja California, using it as a weapon to destroy the false deity. SCP-2399 succeeded but sustained catastrophic damage, necessitating its return to Jupiter. From 1971 to 2015, its drones worked to repair this damage, explaining its current incomplete state and increased repair rate. This story situates SCP-2399 within a broader occult war, showing the Foundation’s willingness to weaponize an existential threat against another. It also provides an in‑universe explanation for its damaged condition and long repair timeline.

Accounts II
State of Repair and Protocol LEGIONNAIRE-5Core SCP-2399 article (addenda) and Fandom summaries

Mid-2010s onward

Addenda to the SCP-2399 file track its repair progress, noting an increase from approximately 12% completion in 1970 to nearly 60% by the article’s timeframe, with an annual rate of 0.78%. The Foundation’s BARRIER Array intercepts and logs command signals, while internal memos discuss Protocol LEGIONNAIRE-5, a classified contingency plan to be enacted if SCP-2399 surpasses 75% completion or if the jamming perimeter fails. The specifics of the protocol are redacted, heightening tension and uncertainty. The narrative emphasizes the slow, inexorable approach of a potential apocalypse and the Foundation’s desperate, secretive preparations. It frames SCP-2399 as a long-term ticking clock rather than an immediate crisis.

The Last ManSCP-2847 "The Last Man" and associated tales (linked via Fandom meta)

Written in the 2010s; set in a future timeline

In the alternate reality explored in SCP-2847 and related tales, SCP-2399’s repair efforts eventually succeed or Foundation countermeasures fail, leading to catastrophic planetary destruction or near-extinction. The story follows a lone survivor (the "Last Man") navigating a devastated world shaped by SCP-2399’s actions and the fallout of occult warfare. SCP-2399 functions as the unseen architect of the apocalypse, its presence felt through ruined landscapes and fragmented records. This narrative extrapolates the worst-case scenario of SCP-2399’s activation, exploring psychological and societal consequences rather than technical details.

Project Isorropía and VanguardSCP Wiki tales and projects (e.g., "Project Isorropía", "Vanguard") referenced in Fandom meta

2010s–2020s

Project Isorropía and Vanguard are extended narrative arcs in which the SCP Foundation and allied groups attempt to rebalance or reconfigure the cosmic threat landscape, including SCP-2399. These stories explore advanced occult technology, interstellar strategies, and the possibility of confronting or redirecting SCP-2399 rather than merely jamming its signals. SCP-2399 appears as a looming factor in strategic calculations, sometimes as a potential tool, sometimes as an unavoidable doom. These arcs broaden SCP-2399’s role from a single anomaly to a node in a larger cosmic conflict, emphasizing themes of hubris, desperation, and the limits of human agency.
