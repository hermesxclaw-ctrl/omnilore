Who It Is

Before there were kings, before there were cities, before humans knew how to cook food or sew skins, there was a single shining figure on a mountain — Keyumars, the Living Mortal, who held the light of kingship itself and watched over a world that had not yet learned to die.

Attested behavior differs sharply by layer. In Pahlavi cosmogony he is passive and contemplative: for 3000 years he lives without eating, speaking or praying, inwardly contemplating (menōg thought). He is Uhrmazd's helper, innocent and vulnerable, guarded by Astovidat. He does not fight until his time comes. In the Shahnameh he is active, wise, just and beloved: the first to establish royal custom (āyin-e shāhi), to prepare food and clothing properly, to practice law and justice (pēšdād), and to receive homage from all humans and animals. Powers
Xvarenah / Farr (Divine Glory)

Supernatural radiance granted by Ahura Mazda/Ohrmazd, reserved for legitimate kings. In Shahnameh, God grants Keyumars the farr, making all humans and animals willingly pay homage and legitimizing his kingship. It is visible as light and is withdrawn upon moral failure (not in his case).

Limit: Not a personal magic but a divine endowment; can be lost by sin. Keyumars retains it until death and passes kingship to Hushang. No attested active use as weapon.

Primordial Vitality and Contemplative Endurance

Created perfect, he lives 3000 years without need for food, speech or prayer, sustained inwardly, and after Ahriman's assault survives 30 years under demonic guard without being killed before his fated time.

Limit: Vulnerability is temporal (zamān): Astovidat cannot kill him before his appointed time, but he is not immortal. He dies after 30 years and his death is necessary for future humanity.

Generative Power - Semen as Seed of Humanity

Upon death he falls on his left side and his semen (tukhm) spills to earth, is purified and fertilized by the sun (Khorshed), and after 40 years grows as a single rhubarb plant from which Mashya and Mashyana emerge as intertwined stalks, later separated into man and woman.

Limit: Requires solar purification and 40-year gestation; not voluntary reproduction. In variant tradition he is not the progenitor but the brother of Mashya/Mashyana via Spenta Armaiti.

Pēšdād - Institution of Law, Kingship and Culture

As pēšdād he is the first to practice justice, establish royal practices (crown, throne, court), teach preparation of food, clothing from skins, and shelter. In Shahnameh he introduces civilization to cave-dwelling humans.

Limit: Cultural hero role, not supernatural; his people still live primitively in mountains. No attested law code survives, only the title.

Command over Humans and Animals

So powerful and just that all humans, tame (ram) and wild (dad) animals pay homage (namāz bordand) voluntarily. Represents the ideal of universal kingship where nature acknowledges legitimacy.

Limit: Moral authority, not animal control magic; animals do not fight for him. His son Siāmak must still raise an army against demons.

Accounts I
Creation from Mud in Erān-vēj on the Veh-DāitBundahishn (Greater Bundahishn) Ch.1-3; Zabihollah Safa summary of Middle Persian texts

Sasanian compilation (6th-9th c CE) of older Avestan lore

In the sixth Gāh (period) of creation, after Ohrmazd creates the primordial ox Gavevagdat from mud on the right bank of the Veh-Dāit in Erān-vēj (center of earth), he creates Gayōmart from mud on the left bank to be his helper. Gayōmart is formed as a 15-year-old youth, luminous, neither eating nor speaking, inwardly contemplating. He and Gavevagdat live 3000 years in peace while Ahriman lies stunned by the Ahunawar prayer. Establishes the dyad of first human and first animal as Ohrmazd's material creation; sets the 3000-year delay before evil's assault; explains the pairing of humanity and cattle in Zoroastrian cosmology.

Ahriman's Assault at Nowruz and the Death of the Primordial OxBundahishn Ch.4; Denkard 8 (Chihrdad Nask summary); Safa Hamaseh-sarayi dar Iran

Sasanian Pahlavi

At the end of the 3000-year stillness, the demoness Jēh (Jahi) cries out and awakens Ahriman from his stupor. Ahriman and his Dīvs attack the light. On the first day of Farvardin (spring equinox, Nowruz) Ahriman leaps onto earth as a dragon, creating death, illness, lust, thirst, hunger, and releasing Xrafstra (evil creeping creatures - reptiles, insects, rodents). In the catastrophe Gavevagdat dies - symbolically the old year giving way to the new, as depicted in Persepolis reliefs - and Ahriman assigns the demon Astovidat to guard Gayōmart, but cannot kill him before his fated time. Introduces death and evil into creation; explains origin of noxious creatures; establishes Nowruz as anniversary of both assault and renewal; Gayōmart's delayed death becomes necessary for future humanity.

The Thirty-Year Dying and the Rhubarb Birth of Mashya and MashyanaBundahishn Ch.14-15; Shaki (2000) Encyclopaedia Iranica 'Gayomard'; Cereti (2015)

Sasanian Pahlavi

Guarded by Astovidat, Gayōmart lives 30 years after the assault, then dies, falling on his left side. His semen spills to earth, is purified by the sun, and after 40 years grows as a rhubarb plant (rīvās) with a single stalk and 15 leaves. From this stalk emerge Mashya and Mashyana, intertwined, later separated by Ohrmazd into man and woman, the parents of all humanity. Their names are forms of marətan 'mortal'. Explains origin of humanity from Gayōmart's seed; links human origin to plant life; establishes 40-year vegetative gestation; Mashya/Mashyana become first couple who then populate the world and first commit sin (eating, lying).

Accounts II
Variant Parentage - Son of Ohrmazd and Spenta ArmaitiPahlavi traditions cited in Shaki and Cereti; Bundahishn variant manuscripts

Late Sasanian / early Islamic Pahlavi

In this tradition, Ahura Mazda (Ohrmazd) marries his daughter Spenta Armaiti (the Earth, Amesha Spenta) and she gives birth to Keyumars. Later she gives birth to Mashya and Mashyana. Thus Keyumars, Mashya and Mashyana share the same mother and are siblings, not father-children. This is noted as a considered tradition but not the mainstream Bundahishn account. Creates theological problem of divine incest, discussed by scholars as cosmogonic incest motif common in Indo-European myth (earth as daughter-wife). Highlights fluidity of Pahlavi cosmogony and need to separate attested vs. disputed layers.

Keyumars as First Shah - The Pēšdād and the Leopard-Skin PeopleFerdowsi, Shahnameh, Pādshāhi-ye Kayūmars (Khaleghi-Motlagh vol.1); Davis translation

c. 1010 CE (Ghaznavid period)

Ferdowsi opens with a bardic question: who first wore the crown? Keyumars arose first among humans who lived in mountain caves and wore leopard skins. He was the first to establish kingship, royal custom, food preparation, and justice (pēšdād). God granted him farr. All humans and animals paid homage. He ruled from the mountains in glory. His beloved son Siāmak was hated by Ahriman, who raised a demonic army under his own demonic son (the Black Div). The angel Sorush warned Keyumars. Siāmak led an army, accepted single combat, and was killed by the demon. Keyumars mourned a year, then on Sorush's counsel sent Siāmak's son Hushang, now grown, who defeated, bound and beheaded Ahriman's son. Keyumars died after a 30-year reign, leaving throne to Hushang, founder of the Pishdadian dynasty. Establishes the Pishdadian (Pishdad) dynasty and the model of just kingship; introduces the farr concept into epic history; sets up the eternal Iran vs. demonic chaos conflict; Hushang's succession continues civilization (discovery of fire, etc.).

The Election of Keyumars - Rationalized Constitutional OriginAl-Masudi, Muruj al-Dhahab vol.1; also cited in Safa

c. 947 CE (Abbasid period)

Al-Masudi reports that between Adam and Noah people spoke Syriac and obeyed no king. Keyumars was simply the most powerful and noble among his contemporaries. The people recognized that revolt, envy, tyranny and hatred are innate in man and only fear of authority can restrain them. After deliberation they came to Keyumars and said: 'You are the greatest and noblest among us, the last descendant of our common father, with no equal in this age. Take charge of our affairs and become our leader; we will offer respect, obedience and absolute submission.' Keyumars accepted after making them swear to obey and renounce revolt. Thus kingship is presented as social contract, not divine creation. Demystifies mythic first king into political theory; reflects Mu'tazilite and Iranian-Islamic ideas of kingship by consent; contrasts with Shahnameh's divine farr model.

Tabari's Historicized Keyumars at Dunbawand with Thirty WivesAl-Tabari, Tarikh al-Rusul wa al-Muluk vol.1; Rosenthal translation

c. 915 CE

Tabari, after noting disputed identifications (Adam, son of Adam, Gomer) and refusing to adjudicate genealogy, describes Kayumarth as a long-lived lord (mu'ammar) who settled in the mountains of Dunbawand (Damavand) in Tabaristan. He had 30 wives, and his two favorite children were Mari and Mariyanah, whom he promoted; their descendants became the later kings of Persia. This euhemerizes the rhubarb twins Mashya/Mashyana into human children Mari/Mariyanah and multiplies wives to explain tribal origins. Attempts to fit Keyumars into biblical-Islamic chronology and geography; localizes mythic Erān-vēj to real Damavand; transforms cosmogonic plant birth into polygamous human genealogy; Tabari's agnosticism about pedigree models scholarly caution.

Keyumars is the same as Adam, the first man in Islam and the Bible. — Gayōmart is the Zoroastrian first mortal created from mud in Erān-vēj, with a distinct cosmogony (3000-year stillness, rhubarb birth of Mashya/Mashyana). Islamic historians like Tabari reported attempts to equate him with Adam or Gomer but explicitly refused to endorse them. Ferdowsi and Pahlavi sources treat him as Iranian first king, not Semitic prophet.

Keyumars and Mashya/Mashyana are the same story - Keyumars is the father of all humans via normal reproduction. — In mainstream Pahlavi, Mashya/Mashyana grow from Gayōmart's semen as a rhubarb plant after 40 years, not from a wife. In a variant they are his siblings, not children. Shahnameh omits the rhubarb entirely and makes Keyumars father of Siāmak, not of Mashya/Mashyana. Tabari historicizes them as Mari/Mariyanah, sons of a polygamous lord. No single 'normal' parentage is attested.

The Shahnameh's Keyumars lived in a palace and ruled a civilized kingdom. — Ferdowsi explicitly says humans then lived in mountain caves and wore leopard skins; Keyumars was Gar-shāh (King of the Mountains) whose court was on the mountain, and he was first to introduce royal practices, food preparation and justice. Civilization begins with him, not before him.

Keyumars had supernatural powers like superhuman strength or magic. — His attested 'powers' are the farr (divine glory) granted by God, primordial vitality (3000 years without food), and generative power via post-mortem semen. He does not fight with magic; his son and grandson do the fighting, and he requires angelic counsel (Sorush). No source attributes combat magic to him.

The rhubarb birth is a later folk addition or Islamic invention. — The rhubarb (rīvās) birth of Mashya/Mashyana from Gayōmart's seed is already in the Bundahishn and is central to Pahlavi cosmogony, with Avestan roots in Mashya/Mashyana as marətan. It is pre-Islamic Zoroastrian doctrine, not folk or Islamic.
