Who They Are

Centeotl is the Toltec maize god, the divine personification of corn — the crop that was the foundation of Toltec civilization. He is depicted as a young man carrying a maize ear, representing the annual cycle of planting, growth, harvest, and rebirth. His worship was intimately connected to the agricultural calendar and the seasonal rituals that ensured bountiful harvests.

Cultural Significance

Centeotl was the patron of the most essential economic activity in Toltec society — maize agriculture. The Toltecs were renowned throughout Mesoamerica as the greatest agricultural innovators, credited with developing the advanced irrigation systems that made the valley of Tula productive. The maize god was therefore central to Toltec identity as a people of agricultural sophistication. The name 'Toltec' itself became synonymous with 'master craftsman' and 'artist,' and the cultivation of maize was considered the highest form of art in the agricultural realm.
