Who They Are

Centeotl is the Toltec maize god, the divine personification of corn — the crop that was the foundation of Toltec civilization. He is depicted as a young man carrying a maize ear, representing the annual cycle of planting, growth, harvest, and rebirth. His worship was intimately connected to the agricultural calendar and the seasonal rituals that ensured bountiful harvests.

The Approach

In the heart of the Aztec world, where maize was life itself, there stood a young god whose body was painted yellow like ripened corn. Centeōtl, the divine embodiment of the sacred crop, was born from the earth and the sun, and his story is woven into the very fabric of Mesoamerican time and ritual. To understand Centeōtl is to understand the soul of an empire that fed the sun with its most precious harvest.

Content advisory. None. The subject is a deity associated with agriculture and fertility. Descriptions of ritual practices are historical and non-explicit.
Identity

Centeōtl

Pronunciation senˈteoːt͡ɬ

Known As

CenteōcihuātlCinteōtlCinteotl

Etymology

From Nahuatl cintli (dried maize still on the cob) and teōtl (deity), meaning 'Maize Deity'.

Cultural Ownership

Aztec (Mexica) civilization, central Mexico, post-classic period (c. 1300-1521 CE).

Disputed Identifications

Some specialists identify Centeōtl with the maize goddess Chicomecōātl, but this is debated. Also connected to the Maya maize god.

Description

Appearance

Usually portrayed as a young man with yellow body colouration, indicative of maize. Often wears a headdress with maize elements. A distinctive black line runs down the eyebrow, through the cheek, to the jawline.

Symbols & Iconography

Maize cobs and plants, yellow color, facial markings, headdress with maize.

Behaviour & Personality

As a fertility and agriculture deity, he is associated with growth, sustenance, and the agricultural cycle. He is seen as benevolent and life-giving.

Domains & Habitats

Earth, maize fields, agricultural lands. Associated with the Pleiades star cluster.

Version Differences

Some sources present Centeōtl as female or identical to Chicomecōātl. The gender and identity vary across traditions.

Cultural Significance

Centeotl was the patron of the most essential economic activity in Toltec society — maize agriculture. The Toltecs were renowned throughout Mesoamerica as the greatest agricultural innovators, credited with developing the advanced irrigation systems that made the valley of Tula productive. The maize god was therefore central to Toltec identity as a people of agricultural sophistication. The name 'Toltec' itself became synonymous with 'master craftsman' and 'artist,' and the cultivation of maize was considered the highest form of art in the agricultural realm.
