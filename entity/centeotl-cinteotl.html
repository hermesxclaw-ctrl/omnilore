Who It Is

In the heart of the Aztec world, where maize was life itself, there stood a young god whose body was painted yellow like ripened corn. Centeōtl, the divine embodiment of the sacred crop, was born from the earth and the sun, and his story is woven into the very fabric of Mesoamerican time and ritual. To understand Centeōtl is to understand the soul of an empire that fed the sun with its most precious harvest.

As a fertility and agriculture deity, he is associated with growth, sustenance, and the agricultural cycle. He is seen as benevolent and life-giving.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Usually portrayed as a young man with yellow body colouration, indicative of maize. Often wears a headdress with maize elements. A distinctive black line runs down the eyebrow, through the cheek, to the jawline.

Symbols — Maize cobs and plants, yellow color, facial markings, headdress with maize.

Domains — Earth, maize fields, agricultural lands. Associated with the Pleiades star cluster.

Accounts I
Birth of CenteōtlFlorentine Codex

Post-classic

Centeōtl is born to Tlazōlteōtl (earth goddess) and Piltzintecuhtli (solar deity, associated with Mercury) on the day 1 Xōchitl. Establishes Centeōtl as a major deity of sustenance.

Accounts II
Bringing of MaizeAztec mythology

Post-classic

Maize was brought to the world by Quetzalcōātl, and Centeōtl is associated with this gift. Maize becomes the staple crop and central to Aztec life.

Agricultural RitualsHistorical accounts

Post-classic

Ritual dances and festivals honor Centeōtl during planting and harvest, including women dancing bare-breasted and picking five ears of corn. Ensures fertility and abundance.
