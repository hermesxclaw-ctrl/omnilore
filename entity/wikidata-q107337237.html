Who It Is

In the granaries of Manipur, a gleaming black stone rests upon a bed of rice, and as long as it shines, prosperity is believed to abide. This stone is Phouoibi, the Lady of the Paddy, a goddess of harvest and wealth whose love is as fleeting as the monsoon rains. Her story is one of passion, betrayal, and transformation, echoing the rhythms of a land where rice is life itself.

Fickle in love, inconstant in favor to mankind, reflecting the unpredictability of wealth and harvest; anger provoked by neglect of paddy or rice.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Phouoibi (or Phouleima) derives from Meitei 'phou' (unhusked rice, paddy) and 'oibi', from the verb 'oiba' (to become) with feminine suffix 'i', thus 'Lady of the Paddy'.

Pronunciation — Pho-oy-bee (approximate)

Aliases — Phouleima, Fouoibi

Ownership — Meitei people of Manipur, India; associated with Ancient Kangleipak.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Often depicted as a beautiful maiden in a glowing dress; also represented as a gleaming black stone kept in a pot inside the granary.

Symbols — Round black stone in a pot; associated with paddy, grains, and harvest; appears in ancient potteries.

Domains — Agriculture, crops, fertility, grains, harvest, paddy, rice, wealth; associated with granaries and fields.

Accounts I
Phouoibi and AkongjambaMoirang Saiyon legends; Phouoibi Waron

Ancient

Phouoibi, journeying with her sisters/friends Ngaleima and Thumleima, meets Akongjamba at a river in Moirang; they fall in love. Later, she visits his home disguised as a tribal woman, but his mother treats her poorly. She flees to a henhouse and transforms into a chicken. The next day, she reveals herself and showers golden grain before leaving. Akongjamba follows, but she refuses to stay, saying they are not destined to be together. She drowns crossing a river, but is saved by a fish. She curses the deer that lied about the river's depth. Phouoibi and Akongjamba are fated to be apart; the deer species loses teeth if they eat paddy; the ngamhai fish gains silvery beauty; the place Phayeng is named.
