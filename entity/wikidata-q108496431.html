Who It Is

Imagine a 40-foot purple gorilla perched delicately on the roof of a tiny yellow van, agreeing with everything you say by repeating his own name twice — "Grape Ape, Grape Ape" — in a deep, childlike rumble that sends everyone screaming "YEOW! A gorill-ill-ill-ill-la!" as they flee. That is Grape Ape, Hanna-Barbera's gentle giant of 1975, a walking contradiction: terrifying at first sight, tender to tears that can flood a town.

Childlike mind and affect despite immense size. Agreeable, eager to please, repeats his own name as affirmation after others speak. Literal-minded, easily led by Beegle Beagle. Emotionally sensitive: cries when homesick for family (tears cause localized flooding), sneezes with hurricane force, apologizes profusely with drawn-out 'I'm soooorrrry!' which Beegle anticipates and sometimes joins playfully. Powers
Colossal Size and Superhuman Strength

Stated 40 ft tall; can lift, carry, or move objects far beyond human scale (vehicles, buildings, large props). Presence alone causes panic. Strength is cartoon-proportional, not quantified with feats like planet-level.

Limit: Size is inconsistent across appearances; cannot fit indoors; weight paradoxically supported by van roof via cartoon physics. Strength not used aggressively; often accidental damage.

Hurricane-Force Sneeze

A single sneeze generates wind equivalent to a hurricane, blowing away people, objects, and scenery.

Limit: Involuntary, triggered by dust/pepper/allergy gags; not controllable as weapon. Effects are temporary slapstick displacement.

Flood-Causing Tears

When crying (often from homesickness for family), produces voluminous tears capable of flooding areas, requiring rescue or drainage gags.

Limit: Emotional trigger only; not weaponized. Crying is soothed by Beegle's reassurance.

Seismic Steps / Bounce Effect

Footsteps create tremors that bounce Beegle Beagle into the air; Beegle's legs continue walking mid-air without breaking stride.

Limit: Effect is comedic and localized; does not cause structural collapse beyond gag. Only affects Beegle consistently.

Van Rev and Cartoon Physics Endurance

Can 'rev up' the small yellow van like a friction toy and ride on its roof without crushing it; roof supports 40-ft weight via toon logic. High durability to slapstick impacts.

Limit: Dependent on cartoon logic; no canonical invulnerability statement. Still expresses pain/sadness and says 'I'm sorry' when he does wrong.

Catchphrase Mimicry / Affirmation

Repeats 'Grape Ape, Grape Ape' after others' statements as agreement/acknowledgement; not a superpower but defining behavioral trait often mistaken for limited vocabulary.

Limit: Can speak fuller sentences (e.g., 'I'm sorry!') so not limited to name only; catchphrase is voluntary social habit.

Form & Signs

Appearance — A 40 ft (12 m) tall anthropomorphic gorilla, covered in shaggy purple fur with a lighter muzzle and chest. Large expressive eyes, broad nose, and heavy limbs emphasizing gentle-giant scale. Often depicted sitting cross-legged or perched improbably on the roof of Beegle Beagle's small yellow van. No clothing except occasional costume gags (e.g., All-American Ape sports outfit, idol costume). Scale is intentionally inconsistent in later cameos (6-1

Symbols — Color purple as primary identifier (merchandising and title pun). Yellow van as secondary icon (Beegle's vehicle that Grape Ape inexplicably rides atop). Catchphrase text 'Grape Ape, Grape Ape' often rendered in captions. Fright-reaction text 'YEOW! A gorill-ill-ill-ill-la!' as visual gag. No religious or mythic symbols; iconography is purely Saturday-morning cartoon slapstick.

Domains — No fixed habitat; itinerant road-travel format across rural and small-town America (countryside, highways, fairs, deserts, cities). Van is mobile home. In Laff-A-Lympics, athletic arenas and cartoon sports venues. In Jellystone! (2021-), lives near the town of Jellystone as 'Director of Moving Large Objects.' No cosmological realm; grounded in contemporary (1970s) United States with cartoon physics.

Accounts I
That Was No Idol, That Was My ApeThe Great Grape Ape Show, Season 1, Segment (paired with The All-American Ape)

1975-09-06 (premiere block)

Grape Ape is mistaken for a giant idol or statue by locals/explorers (island/jungle idol gag common to Hanna-Barbera). Beegle Beagle tries to exploit or explain the confusion while Grape Ape's innocent movements cause panic and slapstick destruction. Resolved when Grape Ape apologizes profusely and the misunderstanding is cleared, with the pair driving off in the van. Establishes core formula: mistaken identity due to size + Beegle as mediator + Grape Ape's apology. Released on DVD Saturday Morning Cartoons 1970's Vol. 2 as preservation.

The All-American ApeThe Great Grape Ape Show, Season 1, Segment (paired with That Was No Idol...)

1975

Grape Ape attempts to participate in all-American activities/sports (e.g., baseball/football or patriotic contest). His size makes him both star player and accidental hazard. Beegle coaches or manages him. Gags include hurricane sneeze disrupting game and flood tears after a setback, ending with Grape Ape's earnest 'I'm sorry' and crowd acceptance. Reinforces gentle-giant sports parody; also preserved on Saturday Morning Cartoons 1970's Vol. 2 DVD.

The Indian Grape CallThe Great Grape Ape Show, Episode 11

1975

The duo encounters Rosie O'Lady, the only person who does not scream 'YEOW! A gorill-ill-ill-ill-la!' upon seeing Grape Ape. When Beegle asks why, she deadpans, 'You've seen one 40-foot purple ape, you've seen them all.' The episode plays the 'Indian call' as a plot device to summon or communicate with Grape Ape, with Beegle misadventures around it. Notable as the sole subversion of the series' signature fright gag; frequently cited in summaries as evidence of self-aware humor.

Accounts II
Ali Beagle and the Forty GrapesThe Great Grape Ape Show, Segment

1975

Parody of Ali Baba and the Forty Thieves. Beegle Beagle takes Ali Baba role ('Ali Beagle'), Grape Ape as giant companion among 'Forty Grapes' (pun on thieves/grapes). Includes the sentient wooden city-limits sign gag: after overhearing no one else reacted, the sign itself displays 'YEOW!' and unfolds extra segments for each 'ill-a' syllable before folding up and hopping away in fear, with Beegle reading aloud. Showcases series' pun-heavy parody structure and meta-gag where even inanimate objects perform the fright reaction.

Scooby's All-Star Laff-A-Lympics - The Yogi Yahooeys EraScooby's All-Star Laff-A-Lympics / Scooby's All-Stars (ABC)

1977-1979

Grape Ape is drafted as member of The Yogi Yahooeys team (led by Yogi Bear) competing against The Scooby Doobies and The Really Rottens in Olympic-style cartoon events. Often paired with Yakky Doodle for relay/comedy beats. Size is flexibly scaled to allow interaction with human-sized teammates. No van; focus is on team slapstick and Hanna-Barbera universe crossover. Extended Grape Ape's visibility beyond solo series by two seasons; cemented his place in Hanna-Barbera shared universe. Later referenced in comics Laff-A-Lympics #1-11, #13.

Harvey Birdman, Attorney at Law - 'Grape Juiced'Harvey Birdman, Attorney at Law (Adult Swim / Cartoon Network), Episode 'Grape Juiced'

2000s (series 2000-2007)

Adult parody reimagining: Grape Ape (voiced by John Michael Higgins) is on trial, with Beegle Beagle (Doug Preis) as witness/client. The episode satirizes celebrity, substance humor (title pun on 'grape juice'), and legal tropes, inverting the innocent childlike giant into a defendant needing Harvey Birdman's defense. Cameo-driven humor relies on audience nostalgia. Introduced Grape Ape to adult audience as nostalgia parody; demonstrated character's afterlife in ironic revival era.

Grape Ape is a villain or monster who intentionally terrifies people. — He is a gentle, childlike protagonist whose size accidentally frightens people; he is apologetic and non-aggressive. The fright gag ('YEOW!') is about others' prejudice, not his intent.

Grape Ape can only say 'Grape Ape.' — Catchphrase is habit after others speak, but he also says 'I'm sorry!' (frequently), 'Beegly Beagly,' and other simple sentences. Original voice actor Bob Holt performed full lines.

Grape Ape is 40 feet tall in every appearance. — 40 ft is solo-series canon, but Laff-A-Lympics, comics, Jellystone! and films scale him down to interact with other characters. Height is production-dependent.

The Great Grape Ape Show was a long-running hit with many seasons. — Only 16 half-hour episodes (32 ten-minute segments) produced in 1975; remainder was reruns repackaged in three different ABC blocks through 1978. Short original production, long rerun tail.

Grape Ape is in the public domain or a folklore figure. — Corporate-owned fictional character created 1975 by Hanna-Barbera, now Warner Bros. Discovery. Not folklore, not public domain; licensed appearances require permission.
