Who It Is

Olodumare (also called Olorun, 'Owner of Heaven') is the supreme deity of the Yoruba people — the creator of the universe, the source of all life, and the ultimate authority over all orishas, spirits, and humans. Unlike the orishas who are actively involved in daily human affairs, Olodumare is a transcendent, somewhat remote figure who delegated the work of creation and governance to the orishas, particularly Obatala. He is the divine monarch who rules from the highest heaven, rarely intervening directly in earthly matters but accessible through the intercession of the orishas — especially Eshu, the divine messenger.

In Yoruba theology, Olodumare is not typically depicted in art or sculpture — he is considered too vast and abstract for human representation. His presence is felt in the sky (hence the name Olorun, 'Owner of the Sky'), in the breath of life (emi) that animates every living being, and in the destiny (ori) that each soul receives before birth. His colors are the white of pure light and the gold of the sun, and his symbols include the sun, the sky, and the breath of wind. He is sometimes described as having no shrine and no direct worship because he is beyond all physical forms — though in practice, many Yoruba people do pray directly to Olodumare, particularly in moments of ultimate crisis.

The central myth of Olodumare describes the creation of the world. In the beginning, there was only water and the sky — a vast, formless expanse. Olodumare summoned Obatala (or, in some versions, Oduduwa) and gave him the task of creating dry land. He provided a golden chain, a snail shell full of earth, and a five-toed hen. Obatala descended the chain, poured the earth onto the waters, and released the hen, which scratched and spread the soil to form the first land at Ile-Ife. Olodumare then sent the other orishas to populate and organize the world. When creation was complete, Olodumare withdrew to the highest heaven — but not before bestowing the breath of life into the first humans molded by Obatala.

Olodumare's relationship with humanity centers on destiny and accountability. Before birth, each soul (ori) appears before Olodumare and chooses its destiny for the coming life — its lifespan, its major challenges, its potential achievements. After death, the soul returns to Olodumare for judgment. Those who have lived good lives are permitted to join the ancestors (egungun); those who have lived evil lives may be sent back to earth for reincarnation as a form of correction. This theology establishes a moral universe governed by ultimate justice, even when earthly circumstances seem unjust.

The name Olodumare itself is revelatory: it combines 'Olo' (owner), 'Odu' (fullness/container of destiny), and 'Mare' (that does not move) — suggesting a being who contains all destinies and is eternally constant. In the African diaspora, particularly in Brazil and Cuba, the supreme deity is often called Olorun or Olodumare, with the orishas serving as intermediaries — a structure that facilitated syncretism with Catholic saints. Olodumare's transcendence has led some scholars to describe Yoruba religion as a form of 'diffused monotheism,' where a single supreme being is acknowledged alongside many lesser divine agents.
