Who It Is

Bo Bo Gyi ('Great Grandfather') is a beloved and ubiquitous nat (spirit) in Burmese folk religion, serving as the guardian of Buddhist pagodas, monasteries, and crossroads. Unlike the more formalized Thirty-Seven Nats, Bo Bo Gyi is a folk spirit — earthy, approachable, and informal. He is depicted as a jovial, white-bearded old man in traditional Burmese attire, often shown with a cheroot (cigar) in his mouth and a bottle of palm wine nearby, reflecting his enjoyment of worldly pleasures. Statues of Bo Bo Gyi are found at the entrances of almost every pagoda and monastery in Myanmar — before entering the main shrine, worshippers often stop to pay respects to Bo Bo Gyi, offering him a lit cheroot, a splash of liquor, or a handful of flowers. He is not a celestial being like Thagyamin but a localized guardian spirit, somewhat mischievous but protective of his domain. Those who disrespect the pagoda — thieves, vandals, those who enter without proper reverence — are said to be punished by Bo Bo Gyi, who may cause them to become lost, fall ill, or suffer accidents. He is also a psychopomp of sorts, guiding the souls of the deceased to their next existence. Bo Bo Gyi represents the persistent pre-Buddhist animist beliefs that continue to coexist alongside orthodox Theravada Buddhism in Myanmar.

The Approach

In the golden twilight of a Burmese pagoda, an elderly figure stands with a curved cap and a pointing finger—not a statue of a saint, but a guardian spirit whose touch is said to grant wishes instantly. Bo Bo Gyi, the 'hallowed grandfather,' bridges the celestial and the everyday, from blessing new cars to guiding the construction of the most sacred stupa in Myanmar.

Identity

Bo Bo Gyi

Pronunciation [bó bó dʑí]

Known As

Thep Than Chai (Thai)U Bo Bo Gyi

Etymology

From Burmese 'ဘိုးဘိုးကြီး' (bo: bo: kri:), literally 'hallowed grandfather' or 'great grandfather'. 'Bo' means grandfather or elder, 'gyi' means great or big.

Cultural Ownership

Burmese (Myanmar) Buddhist tradition; also venerated in Thailand as Thep Than Chai.

Disputed Identifications

Some sources identify Bo Bo Gyi as an avatar of Indra (Thagyamin in Burmese), but this is not universally accepted; other interpretations see him as a local guardian spirit (nat) distinct from Indra.

Description

Appearance

Bo Bo Gyi is typically depicted as a nearly life-sized elderly man, often wearing a curved cap and sometimes carrying a cane to signify old age. He may be shown pointing his finger, referencing the legend of guiding the construction of Shwedagon Pagoda.

Symbols & Iconography

Curved cap, cane, pointing finger, often depicted in a shrine with offerings of scarves and paso (a traditional Burmese garment).

Behaviour & Personality

As a guardian deity, Bo Bo Gyi is benevolent and protective. He is believed to bless worshippers, grant wishes, and bring luck, especially when one touches his pointing finger.

Domains & Habitats

Guardian of Buddhist temples and pagodas in Myanmar; shrines are found throughout the country. Notable shrine: Shwenyaungbin (Golden Banyan Tree) between Yangon and Bago, popular for car blessings. Also venerated at Botataung Pagoda in Yangon.

Version Differences

In Thai tradition, he is known as Thep Than Chai, emphasizing his wish-granting aspect. In Burmese tradition, he is more broadly a guardian spirit. Some versions emphasize his role as an avatar of Indra, while others treat him as a distinct nat.

Stories & Myths

The Founding of Shwedagon Pagoda

Source Legend of Shwedagon PagodaCulture Burmese BuddhistPeriod Traditional legend (exact period unknown)

Summary

The Gautama Buddha's hair relics were given to two Mon merchants, who later gave them to the King of Yangon. The king wanted to enshrine the relics in a pagoda. Bo Bo Gyi, as an avatar of Indra, descended to earth and pointed to the proper location, where the Shwedagon Pagoda was subsequently built.

Participants

Bo Bo Gyi

Two Mon merchants

King of Yangon

Gautama Buddha (via relics)

Consequences

Establishment of the Shwedagon Pagoda, one of the most sacred Buddhist sites in Myanmar.

Variants

Some versions may not mention Bo Bo Gyi's role as Indra, or may depict him as a local spirit instead.

Contradictions & Disputes

Identity as Indra

Position A

Bo Bo Gyi is an avatar of Indra (Thagyamin).

Position A — evidence

The Shwedagon legend explicitly states Bo Bo Gyi came as an avatar of Indra.

Position B

Bo Bo Gyi is a distinct guardian spirit (nat) not to be conflated with Indra.

Position B — evidence

In general Burmese nat worship, Bo Bo Gyi is treated as a local guardian spirit with his own cult, not as Indra.

Scholarly Consensus

No clear consensus; the identification may be a later syncretic development.
