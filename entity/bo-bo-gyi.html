Who It Is

In the golden twilight of a Burmese pagoda, an elderly figure stands with a curved cap and a pointing finger—not a statue of a saint, but a guardian spirit whose touch is said to grant wishes instantly. Bo Bo Gyi, the 'hallowed grandfather,' bridges the celestial and the everyday, from blessing new cars to guiding the construction of the most sacred stupa in Myanmar.

As a guardian deity, Bo Bo Gyi is benevolent and protective. He is believed to bless worshippers, grant wishes, and bring luck, especially when one touches his pointing finger.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Burmese 'ဘိုးဘိုးကြီး' (bo: bo: kri:), literally 'hallowed grandfather' or 'great grandfather'. 'Bo' means grandfather or elder, 'gyi' means great or big.

Pronunciation — [bó bó dʑí]

Aliases — Thep Than Chai (Thai), U Bo Bo Gyi

Ownership — Burmese (Myanmar) Buddhist tradition; also venerated in Thailand as Thep Than Chai.

Powers
Wish Granting

Bo Bo Gyi is believed to grant wishes immediately, especially in Thai tradition as Thep Than Chai. Worshippers pray, wish, and touch the deity's pointing finger for luck.

Limit: Not specified; likely requires sincere devotion.

Guardianship

As a guardian deity, Bo Bo Gyi protects temples and pagodas, and blesses devotees and their possessions, such as cars.

Limit: Guardianship is typically tied to specific shrines or pagodas.

Divine Guidance

According to legend, Bo Bo Gyi, as an avatar of Indra, pointed to the location for building Shwedagon Pagoda, demonstrating his role in guiding sacred construction.

Limit: This ability is specific to the legend and not a general power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Bo Bo Gyi is typically depicted as a nearly life-sized elderly man, often wearing a curved cap and sometimes carrying a cane to signify old age. He may be shown pointing his finger, referencing the legend of guiding the construction of Shwedagon Pagoda.

Symbols — Curved cap, cane, pointing finger, often depicted in a shrine with offerings of scarves and paso (a traditional Burmese garment).

Domains — Guardian of Buddhist temples and pagodas in Myanmar; shrines are found throughout the country. Notable shrine: Shwenyaungbin (Golden Banyan Tree) between Yangon and Bago, popular for car blessings. Also venerated at Botataung Pagoda in Yangon.

Accounts I
The Founding of Shwedagon PagodaLegend of Shwedagon Pagoda

Traditional legend (exact period unknown)

The Gautama Buddha's hair relics were given to two Mon merchants, who later gave them to the King of Yangon. The king wanted to enshrine the relics in a pagoda. Bo Bo Gyi, as an avatar of Indra, descended to earth and pointed to the proper location, where the Shwedagon Pagoda was subsequently built. Establishment of the Shwedagon Pagoda, one of the most sacred Buddhist sites in Myanmar.
