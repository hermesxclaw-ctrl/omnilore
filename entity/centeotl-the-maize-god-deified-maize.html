Who It Is

Centeotl (from centli = dried maize ear and teotl = god) is the male personification of the maize plant, the counterpart to the female maize-goddesses Chicomecoatl and Xilonen. He is the young, vigorous god of the growing corn, whose body is literally the maize plant: his forehead is decorated with a painting of a maize ear, and his headdress forms the tassel (the male flower of the maize at the top of the stalk). In the Aztec calendar, Centeotl was associated with the feast of Uey Miccailhuitl ('Great Feast of the Dead'), reflecting the cycle of planting-and-harvest as a death-and-resurrection metaphor. Centeotl's color is yellow (maize kernels) and his cult was particularly strong among the agricultural commoners of the Valley of Mexico.
