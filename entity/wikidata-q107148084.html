The Approach

In the autumn of 2002, a bay filly with a name that promised elegance swept through Japan's premier filly races without a single defeat, leaving a trail of records and a question that would never be answered: how far could she have gone? Fine Motion, the Irish-bred daughter of Danehill, was a meteor that blazed across the turf and then vanished into the quiet fields of Hokkaido, her story a blend of brilliance, fate, and a genetic twist that silenced her legacy.

Appearance

Bay mare, typical of Danehill offspring, with a strong, athletic build suitable for turf racing.

Behaviour & Personality

Known for her front-running style, she showed early speed and determination, often leading from the start and winning by wide margins. She was consistent and reliable in her early career.

Domains & Habitats

Trained in Japan, raced on turf courses, primarily at Hanshin, Kyoto, Sapporo, and Tokyo.

Version Differences

In Umamusume: Pretty Derby, she is anthropomorphized as a horse girl, a fictional representation that deviates from her real-life counterpart.

Stories & Myths

Undefeated Triple Crown final leg

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2002

Summary

Fine Motion won the Rose Stakes, Shūka Shō, and Queen Elizabeth II Cup in 2002, becoming the first undefeated winner of the final leg of the Fillies' Triple Crown and the first undefeated winner of an open-age Grade 1 race in Japan.
