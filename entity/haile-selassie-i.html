Who It Is

Haile Selassie I (born Tafari Makonnen, 1892–1975) occupies a unique position in global mythology as a living monarch who was declared God incarnate during his lifetime. As Emperor of Ethiopia (1930–1974), he was already a significant historical figure: the 225th descendant of King Solomon and the Queen of Sheba (according to Ethiopian tradition), the leader who resisted Fascist Italian invasion, and a founding figure of the Organization of African Unity. However, his mythological dimension emerged from the Rastafari movement in Jamaica, which took Marcus Garvey's 1920s prophecy — 'Look to Africa, when a Black king shall be crowned, for the day of deliverance is near' — as referring to Ras Tafari's 1930 coronation. Rastafari theology identifies Haile Selassie as Jah (God) incarnate, the returned messiah, the Lion of the Tribe of Judah foretold in Revelation. He is simultaneously a historical person and a transcendent spiritual reality — 'Selassie I liveth' is a declaration of his eternal nature. Complexly, Haile Selassie himself was a devout Ethiopian Orthodox Christian and reportedly never claimed divinity (when asked directly during his 1966 visit to Jamaica, he replied with characteristic reserve: 'Who do you think I am?'). His 1975 death (or 'disappearance' in Rasta theology) did not end belief in his divinity. For millions of Rastafari worldwide, he represents Black liberation, African unity, and the divine presence in history. His image — the stern bearded face beneath the crown — is one of the most recognizable icons of 20th-century spirituality.
