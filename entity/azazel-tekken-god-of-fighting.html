Who It Is

A colossal, golden entity resembling a winged jackal, Azazel is the ancient god of destruction sealed within the earth of the Tekken world. It was awakened by the Mishima Zaibatsu's conflicts and the imbalance of the Devil Gene. Azazel seeks to judge humanity and destroy those found wanting, purging the world of corruption.

The Approach

In the heart of the Tekken universe, where martial arts transcend human limits, there slumbers a colossal beast of golden bone and molten fury—Azazel, the self-proclaimed God of Fighting. Born from humanity's darkest instincts, this entity exists to judge and destroy a world that has lost its way, yet its own existence is a paradox of creation and annihilation.

Content advisory. Contains themes of apocalyptic destruction, cosmic judgment, and body horror.
Identity

Azazel

Pronunciation AH-zah-zel

Known As

Tekken God of FightingThe God of Fighting

Etymology

The name 'Azazel' is borrowed from Jewish and Islamic angelology, where it refers to a fallen angel or scapegoat demon. In Tekken, the name is used to evoke a sense of ancient, otherworldly power, though no direct mythological narrative is followed.

Cultural Ownership

Owned by Bandai Namco Entertainment, created for the Tekken series. The character first appeared in Tekken 6 (2007 arcade, 2009 console).

Disputed Identifications

Some fans theorize that Azazel is connected to the Devil Gene or to the ancient civilization that created the Devil Gene, but this is unconfirmed in official sources. Others conflate him with the biblical Azazel, but the game makes no explicit reference to that mythology.

Description

Appearance

Azazel is a massive, quadrupedal beast with a skeletal, golden exterior. His body is composed of metallic plates and bone-like structures, with glowing orange cracks that emit fire. He has a long, serpentine neck, a horned head with a gaping jaw, and a tail that ends in a blade-like appendage. His overall design evokes ancient, demonic power, with a mix of organic and mechanical elements.

Symbols & Iconography

Fire and gold are dominant symbols. The fire represents destruction and divine wrath, while the gold suggests immortality and untouchable power. His skeletal form symbolizes death and the end of eras.

Behaviour & Personality

Azazel is depicted as a malevolent, arrogant deity who believes humanity is beyond redemption. He is patient and calculating, waiting for the right moment to awaken and judge the world. He speaks in a deep, resonant voice, often mocking the player's efforts. Despite his power, he is ultimately a force of annihilation, not creation.

Domains & Habitats

Azazel resides in a hidden temple deep beneath the desert, accessible only through a portal that opens when the world is on the brink of chaos. The temple is filled with traps and ancient mechanisms, reflecting his role as a guardian of final judgment.

Version Differences

In the arcade version of Tekken 6, Azazel's backstory is minimal, and he appears only as a final boss. In the console version's Scenario Campaign, he is given a more defined role as the antagonist behind the war, and his temple becomes a playable stage. In Bloodline Rebellion, his moveset and AI are slightly adjusted for balance, but his story remains the same.

Stories & Myths

The Awakening of Azazel

Source Tekken 6 Scenario CampaignCulture Japanese video game culturePeriod Modern (fictional 21st century)

Summary

After the King of Iron Fist Tournament 5, the world is plunged into a global war. The Mishima Zaibatsu, led by Jin Kazama, uses its military might to conquer nations. Amidst this chaos, it is revealed that the Zaibatsu's actions are part of a plan to awaken Azazel, a malevolent deity who will judge humanity. The protagonist, Lars Alexandersson, and his allies uncover the truth and fight through Azazel's temple to confront him. They ultimately weaken him, but Azazel is not fully destroyed; he is sealed away, leaving the possibility of his return.

Consequences

The war ends, but the world is left in ruins. Jin Kazama disappears, and the Mishima Zaibatsu is dismantled. Azazel is presumed dormant, but his influence lingers.

Variants

In some character endings (e.g., Jin's), Azazel is defeated more directly. In others, he is mentioned as a looming threat. The exact details of his defeat vary by character.

The Devil Gene Connection

Source In-game dialogue and character endings, fan analysisCulture Japanese video game culturePeriod Modern (fictional 21st century)

Summary

Azazel is deeply connected to the Devil Gene, a cursed power passed down in the Kazama bloodline. Jin Kazama, a Devil Gene user, is the only one who can challenge Azazel. It is implied that Azazel is the source of the Devil Gene or is drawn to it, but this is never explicitly confirmed. Jin's final battle against Azazel in his ending shows him using his Devil powers to strike the killing blow, suggesting that the Devil Gene is the key to defeating Azazel.

Participants

Azazel

Jin Kazama

Consequences

Jin's actions lead to the death or sealing of Azazel, but at the cost of his own humanity and the destruction of the Zaibatsu.

Variants

Some endings show other Devil Gene users (like Kazuya) also capable of harming Azazel, but Jin is the central figure.

Contradictions & Disputes

Azazel's origin and nature

Position A

Azazel is an ancient deity who has existed for millennia, waiting to judge humanity.

Position A — evidence

In-game dialogue from Azazel himself, who claims to be eternal and speaks of past judgments.

Position B

Azazel is a recent creation, possibly born from the Devil Gene or from the Mishima Zaibatsu's experiments.

Position B — evidence

No official lore explains his origin, and some characters express doubt about his divine nature.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus; official sources are vague. Fans are divided.

Azazel's connection to the Devil Gene

Position A

Azazel is the source of the Devil Gene.

Position A — evidence

The Devil Gene is the only thing that can harm him, and he is drawn to Devil Gene users.

Position B

Azazel is merely vulnerable to it and has no direct connection.

Position B — evidence

No official statement confirms a link; the Devil Gene is often depicted as a separate curse.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus; the ambiguity is intentional to leave room for future stories.

Azazel's defeat and status

Position A

Azazel is destroyed at the end of Tekken 6.

Position A — evidence

In Jin's ending, Azazel is shown being destroyed in a massive explosion.

Position B

Azazel is only sealed or weakened, and could return.

Position B — evidence

In other endings, Azazel's fate is left ambiguous, and in Tekken Tag Tournament 2, he appears in some form, suggesting he still exists.

Scholarly Consensus

Most fans believe Azazel is not permanently dead, as the series often resurrects characters.
