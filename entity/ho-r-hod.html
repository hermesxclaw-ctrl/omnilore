Who It Is

Höðr (Hod, Hodr) is the blind Norse god, son of Odin and Frigg, who — deceived and guided by Loki — threw the mistletoe dart that killed his brother Baldr. His story is one of tragedy and innocence: because Baldr was invulnerable to all things except mistletoe, and Höðr was blind, Loki placed the mistletoe dart in his hand and guided his aim. Höðr thus became the unwitting instrument of the most pivotal death in Norse mythology — the event that sets Ragnarök in motion. After Baldr's death, Höðr was killed by Vali, another son of Odin (born specifically to avenge Baldr). In the Gesta Danorum (Saxo Grammaticus's version), Höðr is a heroic mortal king who defeats Baldr in battle for the love of Nanna. In the Eddas, however, Höðr is a tragic figure — a god who killed his brother without malice, through deception. Höðr is destined to return after Ragnarök, reconciled with Baldr, to rebuild the world.

Origins

Höðr is the son of Odin and Frigg. When Frigg extracted oaths from all things not to harm Baldr, she overlooked the mistletoe because it seemed too young and harmless. Loki learned this, took a mistletoe shoot, made a dart from it, and tricked the blind Höðr into throwing it at Baldr during a game.
