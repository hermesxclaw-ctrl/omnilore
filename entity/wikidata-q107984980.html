Who It Is

Two skateboards clatter down a Hong Kong alley, two caps flash yellow and red, and a pair of identical kung-fu prodigies launch into the air before you can blink — Yun, the cloud-kicking twin who turned Street Fighter's stoic martial arts lineage into youthful, airborne swagger, is the living bridge between old Chinatown kung fu cinema and the neon rush of 1990s arcade culture.

Attested in official profiles and win quotes: Brash, cocky, energetic, and impulsive — the more hot-headed twin compared to calm, analytical Yang. Speaks in youthful, informal Japanese (ore, ze) and Cantonese-inflected English localization. Motivated by proving his kung fu is the strongest, protecting his neighborhood, and showing off. Shows respect for elders (Chun-Li, his grandfather figure) but impatient with formality. Powers
Chinese Kung Fu (Bajiquan / Kung Fu variant)

Attested base style: rapid palm strikes, shoulder rams, and kung fu chain punches. In lore described as 'Kung Fu' or 'Chinese Kenpo'; supplementary guides suggest Bajiquan influence via shoulder attack Tetsuzanko.

Limit: Requires close range; no projectile; reliant on footspeed and execution. In-story, still a student, not yet master level like Gen or Chun-Li.

Zesshou Hohou (Absolute Razor) and Kobokushi (Palm Thrust)

Multi-hit palm thrusts and lunge palms; core pressure tools. Zesshou Hohou is rapid palm combo; Kobokushi is advancing palm that can be feinted.

Limit: Unsafe on block if mistimed; requires precise spacing. No super armor.

Tetsuzanko (Iron Mountain Lean) / Shoulder Tackle

Shoulder ram charge, often used as combo ender and anti-fireball approach. Derived from Bajiquan's tie shan kao.

Limit: Charge motion in some versions; punishable if whiffed; loses to throws.

Nishikyaku (West Kick) / Somersault Kick

Dragon-kick style rising somersault, functions as anti-air. Light version short, heavy version travels.

Limit: No invincibility on light version in some games; trading hits common.

Genei Jin (Phantom Shadow Formation) — Super Art

Signature Super Art: creates translucent afterimages that mimic Yun's movements, enabling extended juggles and custom combos. Lore-wise described as speed-induced illusion, not magic. Defines Yun's top-tier status in 3rd Strike.

Limit: Timed (approx. 8 seconds in 3rd Strike); drains Super meter; requires high execution and meter management; vulnerable before activation. In SFV, duration and juggle properties nerfed for balance.

You Hou (Cobra Quarrel) and Sourai Rengeki (Total Thunder Combination)

You Hou: multi-hit palm super ending with shoulder ram. Sourai Rengeki: rapid flurry of punches and kicks culminating in palm. Appear as alternative Super Arts / Ultra Combos.

Limit: You Hou requires close activation; Sourai Rengeki has stricter hit-confirm. Both less versatile than Genei Jin in competitive play.

Skateboard Mobility (non-combat)

In animations Yun rides a skateboard for movement and taunts, indicating high urban mobility and balance. Not a combat weapon.

Limit: Board disappears during actual fight; purely aesthetic. Cannot be used as weapon per canon.

Accounts I
Street Fighter III: New Generation — The Twins Enter the World StageCapcom. Street Fighter III: New Generation (arcade, 1997) — in-game prologue and ending

1997 (in-universe contemporary)

After hearing that a new fighting tournament organized by the mysterious Gill has drawn world warriors, 19-year-old Yun and his twin Yang leave the streets of Hong Kong to test their kung fu against the world. Yun fights to prove that their style — inherited from their family — can stand alongside karate, wrestling and military arts. In his ending, Yun returns to Hong Kong, celebrated by local kids, and resumes skateboarding with Yang, vowing to keep training. Establishes Yun/Yang as successors to classic kung fu archetype; introduces Hong Kong stage and youth street culture to Street Fighter; sets up rivalry with Gill's Illuminati.

Street Fighter III: 2nd Impact — Giant AttackCapcom. Street Fighter III: 2nd Impact (arcade, 1997) — arcade story and official guide

1997

Yun again enters Gill's tournament, now with Hugo and Urien added. His pre-fight dialogue emphasizes impatience and confidence. Defeating Gill, Yun rejects Gill's offer of utopia, preferring the freedom of the streets. The story reinforces Yun's anti-authoritarian streak — he is unimpressed by Gill's messianic rhetoric. Deepens Yun's characterization as street-level hero vs. cult-like Illuminati; gameplay-wise introduces Genei Jin refinements.

Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike — Genei Jin LegendCapcom. Street Fighter III: 3rd Strike (arcade, 1999; Dreamcast/PS2 ports) — story and Shadaloo C.R.I. profile

1999 (in-universe shortly after 2nd Impact)

In the definitive version, Yun's journey is framed as a coming-of-age test. He seeks worthy opponents to hone his Genei Jin technique. His ending shows him and Yang returning to Hong Kong, where Hoimei and Shaomei scold them for disappearing, grounding the epic tournament in everyday neighborhood life. No world-saving stakes — Yun fights for personal growth and local pride. Cements Yun as fan-favorite and competitive top-tier; Genei Jin becomes iconic and later defines 3rd Strike meta for two decades.

Accounts II
Street Fighter Alpha 3 — Hidden Youth (Non-canonical placement)Capcom. Street Fighter Alpha 3 (arcade 1998; console ports 1998-1999) — hidden character data

1998 (out-of-universe; in-universe Alpha timeline is 1980s, before SFIII)

Yun appears as a hidden playable character in console versions of Alpha 3 (PS1, Dreamcast, PSP). No dedicated story route; he is a gameplay bonus, using his SFIII sprites scaled down. His presence is an anachronism, as Alpha is set years before SFIII, implying a younger Yun fighting Shadaloo-era opponents. Officially considered non-canonical fan service. Demonstrates Capcom's reuse of popular sprites; creates timeline confusion addressed later by Capcom stating Alpha 3 console extras are 'dream matches'.

Super Street Fighter IV: Arcade Edition — The SIN Tournament DetourCapcom. Super Street Fighter IV: Arcade Edition (2010) — rival scenes and ending movie

2010 (in-universe post-SFII, pre-SFIII)

Hearing rumors of strong fighters gathering for S.I.N.'s tournament, Yun drags Yang along to test their skills before the events of SFIII. His rival is Chun-Li, who lectures him about recklessness; his other rival is Yang, settling who is stronger. After losing/winning, Yun realizes there are still many stronger adults, and returns to Hong Kong to train harder, eating pork buns with Yang. Bridges Yun's timeline between SFII and SFIII; re-establishes him for new generation of players; gameplay-wise reintroduces Genei Jin as Super and Sourai Rengeki/You Hou as Ultras.

Capcom vs. SNK 2: Mark of the Millennium 2001 — Dream MatchCapcom / SNK. Capcom vs. SNK 2 (arcade/GameCube/PS2, 2001)

2001 (non-canonical crossover)

In this explicitly non-canonical festival tournament sponsored by the Garcia Financial Clique, Yun is a selectable fighter representing Capcom's youth. He has special intro dialogue with SNK characters like Terry Bogard and Kyo Kusanagi, where his skateboarding and kung fu are contrasted with SNK's Kyokugen karate. No story consequences carry to main Street Fighter canon. Expands Yun's exposure to SNK audience; shows Capcom's willingness to position Yun as generational representative.

Udon's Street Fighter Unlimited / Street Fighter III comics — Streets of Hong KongUdon Entertainment. Street Fighter comics (2003-present), Street Fighter Unlimited #4-6, Street Fighter III backup stories

2016-2017 (publication)

Udon expands Yun's off-screen life: he and Yang run errands for their elderly guardian, protect Hoimei and Shaomei from local thugs, and spar with Chun-Li who visits Hong Kong on Interpol duty. Yun is portrayed as well-meaning but show-offy, learning humility after being humbled by older masters. These stories are licensed but secondary canon. Provides the most detailed slice-of-life lore for Yun, filling gaps left by games' minimal story; influences fanon but not game canon.

Yun and Yang are the same character with palette swap — They are distinct characters with different movesets, Super Arts (Genei Jin vs. Seiei Enbu), and personalities since SFIII: New Generation. They share some normals but diverge significantly, especially in 3rd Strike where Yun is rush-down and Yang is more poke-oriented.

Yun is Chinese for 'cloud' so his powers are wind/cloud magic — No official source gives Yun cloud powers. The name's etymology (云 = cloud) is out-of-universe wordplay with Yang (sun); in-universe his abilities are purely physical kung fu and speed illusion, not elemental magic.

Yun is from mainland China — Official nationality is Hong Kong (listed as Hong Kong in all Capcom profiles). This reflects 1997 handover-era Hong Kong identity and street culture, not generic mainland China.

Genei Jin is unbeatable / makes Yun always win — Genei Jin is extremely powerful in 3rd Strike but requires meter, execution, and can be countered by parries, throws, and specific matchups (e.g., Chun-Li, Kuroda's Q). In SFIV and SFV, Genei Jin was deliberately nerfed and Yun is not top-tier.

Yun (Street Fighter) is the same as Yun (the Chinese surname or Brother Yun) — Completely unrelated. Yun the fighter is a 1997 Capcom creation; Yun as surname (云/雲) is millennia-old Chinese family name; Brother Yun is contemporary Christian figure Liu Zhenying. Shared romanization causes search-engine conflation.
