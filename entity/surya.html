Who It Is

Surya (Sūrya) is the Hindu god of the sun, the visible aspect of the divine whose daily journey across the sky sustains all life on earth. He is one of the most ancient deities in the Vedic pantheon, celebrated in numerous Rigvedic hymns, and his worship formed the basis of the Saura sect, one of the five major branches of classical Hinduism alongside Vaishnavism, Shaivism, Shaktism, and Ganapatya. Surya is typically depicted riding a chariot drawn by seven horses (representing the seven colors of light, the seven days of the week, or the seven Vedic meters), driven by his charioteer Aruna (the personification of dawn's reddish glow). He is golden-armed, radiant, and wears a crown of light. In iconography, he often holds lotus flowers in both hands. Surya's mythological narratives span the Vedas, Puranas, and epics. In the Ramayana, the sage Agastya taught Rama the Aditya Hridayam (Heart of the Sun) hymn to invoke Surya's blessing before the final battle with Ravana. In the Mahabharata, Karna is revealed to be the son of Surya, born with divine armor (kavacha) and earrings (kundala) that made him nearly invincible. The Sun Temple at Konark in Odisha, built in the 13th century as a colossal stone chariot with twelve elaborately carved wheels, is the supreme architectural monument of Surya worship. Surya is also central to the practice of Surya Namaskar (Sun Salutation), the yogic sequence of postures performed at dawn. As the source of light, heat, energy, and the sustainer of all living things, Surya is simultaneously a personal deity and a cosmic principle. He is identified with the eye of Purusha (the cosmic being), and the Gayatri Mantra, the most sacred of all Vedic chants, is addressed to Savitr, a form of Surya, as the divine light that illuminates all worlds and all minds.
