Who It Is

In a world where heroes are ranked by official associations and monsters spawn from human obsession, one man declared war on the entire system. Garou, the 'Hero Hunter,' emerged from the shadows of the Hero Association's ranks to challenge every hero he could find, driven by a twisted ideal that monsters, not heroes, deserve to win. His journey is a brutal, philosophical descent that blurs the line between villain and tragic figure, and it all begins with a childhood bullied by those who called themselves heroes.

Garou is arrogant, sadistic, and driven by a twisted sense of justice. He believes that heroes are hypocrites and that true justice belongs to the underdog, which he equates with monsters. He is highly intelligent and strategic, often taunting his opponents and exploiting their weaknesses. Despite his ruthless exterior, he shows moments of compassion, particularly towards children and those he deems innocent. He has a deep-seated desire to become the 'absolute evil' to unite humanity against a common foe, but his methods are brutal and self-defeating.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Garou' is likely derived from the French 'loup-garou' (werewolf), fitting his monstrous appearance and predatory behavior. In Japanese, it is written as ガロウ, which can also be interpreted as 'gallery' or 'howl,' but the werewolf connection is the most thematically resonant.

Pronunciation — GAH-roo (English approximation); in Japanese, 'Gah-rou' with a rolled 'r'.

Aliases — Hero Hunter, Human Monster, The Absolute Evil, The One Who Hunted Heroes, Saitama's Rival (unofficial)

Powers
Martial Arts Mastery

Garou is a martial arts prodigy, having learned the Water Stream Rock Smashing Fist from his master Bang, and later developing his own style. He is able to analyze and counter his opponents' techniques after seeing them once.

Limit: His skills are initially limited by his human body, but he pushes beyond his limits through sheer willpower.

Superhuman Physical Conditioning

Garou possesses incredible strength, speed, and durability, far beyond that of a normal human. He can punch through concrete, dodge bullets, and withstand powerful blows.

Limit: These abilities are not as high as Saitama's, and he can be overwhelmed by multiple powerful opponents.

Monsterization

When pushed to the brink, Garou's body undergoes a transformation that grants him monstrous powers, increasing his physical abilities and granting him new abilities such as regeneration and energy projection.

Limit: The transformation is painful and mentally destabilizing, and it ultimately fails to grant him the power to defeat Saitama.

Water Stream Rock Smashing Fist

A martial art taught by Bang that focuses on redirecting an opponent's force. Garou uses it to deflect attacks and counter with devastating blows.

Limit: Requires precise timing and control; if his mind is clouded, he cannot use it effectively.

Monster Calamity God Slayer Fist

A technique Garou develops himself, combining his knowledge of various martial arts with his monstrous power. It is a powerful, destructive move that can level buildings.

Limit: It is a last-resort technique that puts immense strain on his body.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Garou is a tall, muscular young man with a distinctive wild hairstyle and sharp, intense eyes. He typically wears a black martial arts gi with a dark blue or black undershirt, and often has a menacing scowl. As his 'monsterization' progresses, his appearance becomes more inhuman: his hair turns white, his skin becomes pale, and he grows horn-like protrusions. Symbols — Garou's iconic symbol is his 'Hero Hunter' moniker, often associated with images of him defeating heroes. His martial arts style, the 'Water Stream Rock Smashing Fist' (borrowed from Bang) and his own 'Monster Calamity God Slayer Fist,' are symbolic of his rejection of conventional heroism. His transformation into a 'monster' is visually represented by his growing fangs, claws, and horn.

Domains — Garou is a wandering fighter, often found in cities where he can find heroes to challenge. He frequents the Hero Association headquarters, martial arts tournaments, and the ruins of battles. He has no fixed home, living on the fringes of society.

The Hero Hunter's OriginOne Punch Man (webcomic/manga) - flashback in the Hero Hunter arc

Modern (fictional)

Garou was a child who idolized heroes, but after being bullied by a group of kids who claimed to be heroes, he became disillusioned. He saw that 'heroes' could be cruel and that 'monsters' were often the underdogs. This led him to declare that he would become the ultimate monster to teach the world a lesson. Garou's hatred for heroes grows, and he begins his quest to hunt down all heroes, starting with low-rank ones and working his way up.

Accounts II
The Hero Hunter ArcOne Punch Man (webcomic/manga/anime)

Modern (fictional)

Garou systematically attacks heroes, including members of the Hero Association, leaving them severely injured but alive. He gains notoriety as the 'Hero Hunter.' He encounters Saitama early on, but Saitama defeats him without effort, leaving Garou confused and obsessed with understanding Saitama's power. Garou continues his rampage, eventually facing off against the martial arts master Bang and his brother Bomb, but escapes. He later infiltrates the Monster Association, intending to use them to further his goals. Garou becomes one of the most wanted criminals, and the Hero Association launches a massive manhunt. His actions inadvertently lead to the Monster Association's rise and the eventual conflict with the heroes.

The Monster Association ArcOne Punch Man (manga/webcomic)

Modern (fictional)

Garou, after being captured by the Monster Association, is offered a chance to join them. He refuses and instead fights his way through the association, defeating many monsters and executives. He undergoes a partial monsterization, gaining immense power. He confronts the heroes who have invaded the association, defeating many of them, until he faces Saitama. In an epic battle, Saitama easily defeats Garou, breaking his spirit and forcing him to confront the futility of his path. Garou is ultimately spared and left broken, with Saitama telling him to go home. Garou's defeat at Saitama's hands leads to his mental breakdown and eventual redemption. He is taken into custody by the Hero Association but later escapes, and his whereabouts become unknown.
