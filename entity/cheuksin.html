Who It Is

Cheuksin (Cheuk-sin), also known as the outhouse goddess or the guardian of the household periphery, is the Korean deity who protects the outer boundaries of the home, particularly associated with the outdoor privy and the areas beyond the main living quarters. In the traditional Korean cosmic geography of the household, each space had its tutelary deity: Jowangshin in the kitchen, Samshin in the inner room, Seongjushin (the house-beam god) at the ridgepole, and Cheuksin at the outer edge, particularly the toilet area. Cheuksin is typically conceived as a fearsome female spirit with wild, disheveled hair and a terrifying countenance. Korean folk belief held that the outhouse was a liminal space where the domestic and the wild, the clean and the polluted, the human and the spirit world intersected. As its guardian, Cheuksin was both a protector and a potentially dangerous entity who required propitiation. Rituals dedicated to Cheuksin were performed during regular household ceremonies and particularly during the tenth lunar month. Her ambivalent nature—simultaneously protective and frightening—reflects the Korean folk understanding of the spirit world, where deities could be both benefactors and punishers depending on human conduct. With the modernization of Korean sanitation, the traditional role of Cheuksin has naturally declined, but she persists in Korean folklore and shamanic tradition as a reminder of the complex spiritual geography that once governed every Korean household.
