Evening Star

The Beautiful Woman; Mother of Humanity; Guardian of the West

TypeFertility Deity / Celestial Deity
CulturePawnee
Who It Is
Form

The Evening Star is described in Pawnee mythology as a beautiful woman of radiant appearance, often depicted with a glowing, celestial aura that mirrors the brightness of Venus in the evening sky. She is said to have long, flowing hair that resembles the trailing light of the planet, and her garments are woven with the colors of twilight—purples, oranges, and deep blues. In some depictions, she carries a bundle of sacred objects, including corn and other fertility symbols, and she is often accompanied by the Morning Star, her husband, who appears as a warrior. Her face is said to be so luminous that it can be seen from the earth, guiding travelers and hunters as they return home at dusk. In Greek art, Hesperus is typically depicted as a young man holding a torch or a branch, but the Pawnee conception is distinctly feminine and maternal.

Powers

Fertility and Agricultural Blessing The Evening Star has the power to bestow fertility on the earth, ensuring bountiful crops, particularly corn. She is believed to control the growth of plants and the reproduction of animals, and she is invoked in planting and harvesting ceremonies. Her blessing is essential for the survival of the Pawnee people, as she provides the staple food of corn.

Celestial Guidance As the Evening Star, she guides travelers and hunters during the twilight hours, providing light and direction. She is also a navigational aid for the Pawnee, who used the stars for seasonal and ceremonial calendars. Her position in the sky marks the time for planting and other important activities.

Creation of Humanity In Pawnee mythology, the Evening Star, along with the Morning Star, created the first humans. She shaped the first woman from the earth and breathed life into her, making her the mother of all humanity. This power establishes her as a creator deity and a central figure in Pawnee origin stories.

Protection and Intercession She acts as a protector of her people, warding off evil spirits and malevolent forces. She intercedes with the higher gods on behalf of humans, offering prayers and sacrifices to ensure their well-being. Her protective power is invoked in times of danger or illness.

Accounts

In the beginning, the Evening Star and the Morning Star were the first beings in the sky. They decided to create humans to populate the earth. The Morning Star, a powerful warrior, and the Evening Star, a beautiful woman, came together and created the first man and woman. The Evening Star shaped the woman from the earth and gave her life, while the Morning Star gave the man strength. They placed the humans on the earth and taught them how to live, including the cultivation of corn, which the Evening Star provided. This act established the Pawnee as the children of the stars, and the Evening Star became their mother.

The Pawnee performed an annual sacrifice to the Morning Star, which involved a captive, usually a young woman, who was offered to the Morning Star. The Evening Star was believed to be the wife of the Morning Star, and she was said to be jealous of the sacrifice, as it was a gift to her husband. In the ritual, the captive was tied to a scaffold and shot with arrows, symbolizing the Morning Star's power. The Evening Star was invoked to ensure the fertility of the earth and the success of the crops. The sacrifice was a complex ceremony that involved the entire community, and it was believed to renew the cosmic order.

A young Pawnee man, lost in the forest, encounters a beautiful woman who is the Evening Star in disguise. She gives him a gift of corn and teaches him how to plant and harvest it. She tells him that she is the mother of all, and that he must share this knowledge with his people. The man returns to his village and teaches the people, and they become prosperous. The Evening Star then disappears, but her spirit remains in the cornfields, watching over the people.

Scholarly Debates
Gender of the Evening Star
In Pawnee mythology, the Evening Star is a female deity, known as 'The Beautiful Woman.' ⇌ In Greek mythology, Hesperus is a male deity, personifying the planet Venus as a young man.
Relationship to the Morning Star
In Pawnee tradition, the Evening Star is the wife of the Morning Star, and they are equal partners in creation. ⇌ In some interpretations, the Morning Star is considered superior, and the Evening Star is subordinate, as seen in the sacrifice ritual where the Morning Star is the primary recipient.
Identification with Venus
The Evening Star is universally identified with the planet Venus in its evening appearance. ⇌ Some scholars argue that the Pawnee Evening Star may also be associated with other celestial objects, such as the star Spica or the constellation Virgo, due to its agricultural connections.
What We Don't Know

There is limited documentation on the Evening Star's role in pre-contact Pawnee society, as much of the oral tradition was lost due to colonization and forced assimilation. The specific rituals and ceremonies dedicated to her are not fully recorded, and the details of her iconography in material culture (e.g., pottery, textiles) are sparse. Additionally, the relationship between the Evening Star and other Pawnee deities, such as the creator god Tirawa, is not well understood. Further research is needed to explore the variations in her myth across different Pawnee bands and to compare her with similar deities in other Native American cultures.

A way to hold it in your mind

Did you know that the Evening Star, the brilliant planet Venus, was not just a celestial body for the Pawnee people, but a living goddess who created humanity and gave them the gift of corn? Her story is a testament to the deep connection between the sky and the earth, and it challenges our modern understanding of astronomy and mythology.
