Who It Is

Ganon — also known as Ganondorf in his human form — is the principal antagonist of Nintendo's The Legend of Zelda series, a Demon King locked in an eternal cycle of death and rebirth with the goddess Hylia's chosen hero, Link, and her mortal incarnation, Princess Zelda. Originally a Gerudo king born once every hundred years, Ganondorf coveted the Triforce — the divine relic of the Golden Goddesses — and seized the Triforce of Power, transforming into the demonic beast Ganon. His curse, born of hatred and the demon Demise's dying breath, ensures that his malice will reincarnate across the ages, forever plaguing Hyrule.

Origins & Fall

Ganondorf was born as the sole male of the Gerudo tribe — a desert people where a male is born only once every hundred years, destined by tradition to be their king. His surrogate mothers, the twin witches Kotake and Koume, raised him in the ways of dark magic. In Ocarina of Time (1998), Ganondorf swore fealty to the King of Hyrule while secretly seeking the Triforce in the Sacred Realm. When Link opened the Door of Time and drew the Master Sword, Ganondorf followed him into the Sacred Realm. Touching the Triforce, his heart — already consumed by greed — caused the relic to split. He retained only the Triforce of Power, while Wisdom went to Zelda and Courage to Link. This imbalance transformed him into the Demon King Ganon and began the seven-year reign of darkness. The deeper origin, revealed in Skyward Sword (2011), traces Ganon's evil to Demise, a primordial demon who, upon defeat by the first Link, cursed the bloodline of the goddess and the spirit of the hero to be forever pursued by an incarnation of his hatred — which manifests as Ganon.

Form

Ganon appears in multiple forms across the series. As Ganondorf the Gerudo, he is a towering, powerfully built man with olive-brown skin, sharp amber eyes, a prominent aquiline nose, and fiery red hair typically worn long. He wears ornate armor — often black and gold — with a prominent red cape, a crown or diadem, and heavy jewelry marking his royal status. His forehead bears the symbol of the Gerudo. In his demonic boar form (Ganon), he becomes a colossal, bipedal or quadrupedal monster resembling a massive boar with glowing yellow or red eyes, massive tusks, and a mane of reddish fur. Breath of the Wild's Calamity Ganon is a swirling mass of Malice — a purple-black miasma infused with the energy of ancient Sheikah technology — manifesting as a grotesque spider-scorpion hybrid before transforming into Dark Beast Ganon, a mountain-sized boar made of pure hatred. In Tears of the Kingdom, the Demon King form is Ganondorf transformed by the Secret Stone, his skin darkening, eyes turning red, and horns emerging — a demonic warlord clad in ancient Zonai-inspired armor.

Powers

Ganon's powers encompass dark magic, immense physical strength, and the supernatural authority granted by the Triforce of Power. He wields powerful sorcery — levitation, energy projection in the form of dark spheres and lightning, teleportation, summoning of monsters, and necromancy (raising the dead as ReDeads and Stalfos). The Triforce of Power grants him near-immortality and remarkable regeneration; he can survive impalement by the Master Sword, being crushed under a collapsing castle (Ocarina of Time), being sealed in the Sacred Realm for centuries, and even the destruction of his physical body. In Twilight Princess, he survives a sword through the chest from the Sages and returns. His skill with weapons — particularly the massive blade wielded in Twilight Princess and Tears of the Kingdom — is formidable. As Calamity Ganon, he can corrupt technology, possess ancient machines (the Divine Beasts, Guardians), and spread the Malice that poisons the land. His signature attack in several games is the Dead Man's Volley — a tennis-like energy blast that must be reflected back. He can also transform into a massive shadowy form, create phantom doubles, and in Tears of the Kingdom, he can unleash Gloom — a life-draining substance that permanently reduces the health of those it touches.

Behavior & Habitat

Ganondorf in his human form is calculating, proud, and ruthless — a brilliant strategist who manipulates political systems from within before revealing his true nature. He feigns loyalty, exploits trust (as with the King of Hyrule in Ocarina of Time), and uses people as pawns (Zant in Twilight Princess, the Twili, the Gerudo). He respects strength and despises weakness. A cruel streak runs through all his forms: he enjoys gloating over fallen enemies, curses those who cross him (as with the Deku Tree, Jabu-Jabu, and the Gorons in Ocarina of Time), and rules through fear and destruction. Yet there is tragedy beneath the malice — in The Wind Waker, his final monologue reveals a man who coveted Hyrule's green fields because the desert winds brought only death. In Calamity Ganon form, higher intelligence is lost to pure destructive instinct — it exists only to consume, destroy, and reform. The TOTK Ganondorf is a narcissist who believes himself chosen by destiny to rule, utterly convinced of his own supremacy.

Weaknesses & Wards

Ganon's primary vulnerability is the Master Sword — the Blade of Evil's Bane, forged by the goddess Hylia and tempered in sacred flames. It is the only weapon that can deliver a truly decisive blow. Holy Light Arrows, particularly those wielded by Zelda, can also pierce his defenses, stunning him long enough for Link's blade to strike. In his pig-demon form, he is often vulnerable after his energy attacks are reflected back at him. Silver Arrows are required to defeat him in the earliest games. The Triforce of Power, paradoxically, can be turned against him — when the three pieces are united, a wish on the Triforce can undo his plans entirely. His arrogance is a persistent psychological weakness; he routinely underestimates Link and Zelda, allowing them crucial openings. Calamity Ganon is weakened by the absence of a physical form and by the divine laser of the Divine Beasts, and Dark Beast Ganon is vulnerable at the glowing points on its body — the concentrated sources of Malice. Gloom-based attacks in TOTK can be countered by Sundelion-infused food and by the Light Dragon's power.

Accounts

Ganondorf, the Gerudo king, pledges fealty to the King of Hyrule while secretly hunting the Triforce. When the young Link draws the Master Sword and is sealed in the Sacred Temple for seven years, Ganondorf enters the Sacred Realm, touches the Triforce, and claims only its Power — having an unbalanced heart. He conquers Hyrule, transforms Castle Town into a wasteland of undead, and curses the Deku Tree, the Gorons, and the Zora. Link returns as an adult, awakens the Sages, and confronts Ganondorf in his floating castle. After a fierce battle, Ganondorf uses the Triforce of Power to transform into the monstrous Ganon, but is ultimately sealed in the Evil Realm by the combined might of Link, Zelda, and the Seven Sages.

After breaking free from the seal, Ganondorf returns to find Hyrule drowned beneath the Great Sea — the goddesses having flooded the land to stop him. He resurfaces, still seeking the Triforce and the restoration of Hyrule. At the story's climax, atop a submerged Hyrule Castle, Ganondorf delivers an unexpectedly poignant monologue: 'My country lay within a vast desert. When the sun rose into the sky, a burning wind punished my lands, searing the world. And when the moon climbed into the dark of night, a frigid gale pierced our homes. No matter when it came, the wind carried the same thing... Death. But the winds that blew across the green fields of Hyrule brought something other than suffering and ruin. I coveted that wind, I suppose.' Before he can claim the full Triforce, the King of Hyrule touches it and wishes for Hyrule to be washed away forever. Link plunges the Master Sword into Ganondorf's forehead, and he turns to stone beneath the waves.

Ten thousand years before the events of Breath of the Wild, Hyrule defeated Calamity Ganon using an army of Guardians and four Divine Beasts piloted by Champions. Anticipating his return, the kingdom of Hyrule prepared — but Ganon corrupted the Guardians and Divine Beasts, murdered the four Champions, and gravely wounded Link. Zelda sealed herself with Ganon in Hyrule Castle for a century. When Link awakens in the Shrine of Resurrection, he must reclaim the Divine Beasts, recover his memories of the fallen Champions, and confront Calamity Ganon in the ruined sanctum of Hyrule Castle. The final battle occurs in two phases: the spider-scorpion Calamity Ganon within the castle, and Dark Beast Ganon — a mountain-sized boar of pure Malice — on the fields of Hyrule, where Zelda bestows the sealing power and the Bow of Light for the killing blow.

Field Notes

⛧ In early concept art for the original The Legend of Zelda (1986), Ganon was designed as a pig-humanoid because the developers associated pigs with greed — the same reason the Dragon Ball series has Oolong the shape-shifting pig.

⛧ Ganondorf's laugh in Ocarina of Time — a deep, echoing 'Gwah hah hah!' — was performed by voice actor Takashi Nagasako and remains one of the most recognizable villain laughs in gaming.

⛧ In the CD-i game Zelda: The Wand of Gamelon, Ganon's dialogue is notoriously poor due to limited cartridge space for voice samples, resulting in stilted, meme-worthy delivery.

⛧ Matthew Mercer voiced Ganondorf in Tears of the Kingdom (2023), bringing the character's first fully-voiced portrayal in a mainline game.

⛧ Ganon's Horse — the massive black stallion he rides in Ocarina of Time and Twilight Princess — can be tamed by Link in Breath of the Wild as the Giant Horse, found in the Taobab Grassland.

⛧ In Breath of the Wild, if you go directly to Hyrule Castle without completing any Divine Beasts, you face Calamity Ganon at full power — plus the four Blight Ganons in sequence — making it one of the toughest possible boss gauntlets.

Cultural Significance

Ganon is one of the most enduring and frequently appearing antagonists in video game history, appearing in nearly every mainline Zelda title across five decades. He established the model of the video game 'final boss' — a multi-phase, escalating confrontation that tests every skill the player has acquired. His design — the Gerudo king and his demonic pig-beast transformation — draws from multiple mythological sources: the desert sorcerer-king archetype, the demonic boar (present in Celtic, Japanese, and Hindu mythologies), and the Faustian bargain for forbidden power. In Tears of the Kingdom (2023), Ganondorf's character gained new depth — the first voiced, fully fleshed-out origin for the character, performed by Matthew Mercer, revealing a narcissistic warlord whose ambition predates Hyrule itself. Ganon has also become a staple in Nintendo crossover properties, most notably as a playable fighter and boss in the Super Smash Bros. series. His name is known even to those who have never played a Zelda game.

Modern Interpretations

Ganon has been a playable fighter in Super Smash Bros. Melee, Brawl, 4, and Ultimate — where his moveset is partially based on Captain Falcon's, a source of endless fan humor. He appears in Hyrule Warriors and Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity as both a playable character and antagonist, exploring his timeline-altering schemes in a Dynasty Warriors format. The CD-i Zelda games (Link: The Faces of Evil, Zelda: The Wand of Gamelon) famously produced the meme-immortal line 'I can't wait to bomb some Dodongos!' — though technically spoken by Link, it's indelibly associated with that era of Zelda. Ganon has been the subject of countless fan works, including the popular webcomic 'A Tale of Two Rulers' exploring a Zelda/Ganon political marriage AU. The Nintendo Switch era (Breath of the Wild, Tears of the Kingdom) reinvigorated Ganon's mythos, expanding his origin and exploring the cyclical nature of his reincarnation with a depth never before seen. A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine you are a king born in a barren desert, watching your people suffer under the burning sun while a verdant, prosperous kingdom sprawls just over the mountains. You are the chosen one of your people — their first male born in a century — and you possess sorcerous power that could reshape the world. But the gods who blessed those green fields have never shown you any favor. You decide that if the gods won't give you what you deserve, you'll take it. And you do — you seize divine power and crown yourself the Demon King. But a boy in a green tunic and a princess with golden hair keep standing in your way, life after life, age after age. Every time you rise, they rise to meet you. You've been stabbed, sealed, drowned, incinerated, and imprisoned — and you always come back, because your hatred is literally a demon's dying curse made manifest. You are not just a villain; you are a fundamental force of nature, as eternal as the cycle of death and rebirth itself. That's Ganon: a demon king, a man who wanted more, and a curse that will never let go.
