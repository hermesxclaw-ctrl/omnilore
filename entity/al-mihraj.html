Who It Is

Al-Mihraj (also Balhut, or simply 'the Great Fish') is a cosmic sea creature of medieval Arabic and Islamic cosmology — a fish so immense that it serves as the foundation upon which rests the Bull (Kujata/Thawr) that carries the earth on its back (or, in other versions, the fish carries the earth directly). This cosmological model derives from early Islamic interpretations of Quranic verses (e.g., Q 68:1 'Nun. By the pen and what they inscribe') where 'Nun' is interpreted as the name of the great fish/whale. According to Al-Qazwini's 'Aja'ib al-Makhluqat,' the earth is carried on the horn of a great bull (Thawr), which stands on the back of the great fish Al-Mihraj, which swims in the Encircling Ocean that surrounds the universe. When the fish moves, earthquakes result. Below the fish is water, below the water is darkness, and below the darkness is that which only God knows. A related version (associated with the Kitab al-Bulhan and other cosmographic texts) describes the fish Bahamut (from the Hebrew Behemoth, transformed in Arabic transmission) as the foundation of the cosmic hierarchy. In the Arabian Nights, the 'island that is a fish' trope — sailors land on what they think is an island, light a fire, and the fish sinks beneath them — appears as a repeated motif, deriving from this cosmic fish mythology. Al-Mihraj/Bahamut represents the medieval Islamic synthesis of biblical (Leviathan/Behemoth), pre-Islamic Arabian, and Indian cosmological ideas into a coherent universe-foundation myth. The great fish is the ultimate unknowable layer of creation — the creature below which even angels cannot see, representing the mystery at the foundation of existence.

Attested in Medieval Arabic cosmographies (Al-Qazwini, Ibn al-Wardi) / Islamic traditionEra Medieval Islamic period (9th-14th century)
