Etymology

The name 'Al-Mihraj' is likely derived from Arabic, possibly related to 'mihraj' meaning 'a great fish' or 'whale', though the exact root is uncertain. Some sources connect it to the Persian 'mihrag' or 'mahr', but this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Arabian / Islamic, though it shares traits with other cosmic sea monsters in Near Eastern and world mythology.

Disputed Identifications

Al-Mihraj is often conflated with Bahamut, Leviathan, and Jormungandr, but it is a distinct entity in its own right. Some scholars argue it is a localized variant of Bahamut, while others see it as a separate myth. The lack of a dedicated article makes this identification uncertain.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the vast, star-flecked oceans of Arabian imagination, there swims a creature so immense that sailors mistake its back for an island, and its breath for the wind that fills their sails. This is Al-Mihraj, the Great Fish, the Whale-Island, a cosmic leviathan whose story is woven into the fabric of creation itself.

Who It Is
Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Al-Mihraj is described as a colossal fish or whale of such immense size that its back appears as a sandy island, often covered with vegetation and mistaken for dry land by sailors. Its exact form is not described in detail, but it is often depicted as a whale-like creature with a flat back.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols are associated with Al-Mihraj, but it is often depicted in medieval Islamic manuscripts as a giant fish with a tree or mountain on its back, symbolizing the cosmic scale of creation.

Behaviour & Personality

Al-Mihraj is generally passive and unknowing of the life it supports on its back. It is not malevolent but is a force of nature, sometimes causing destruction when it moves or dives, leading to the drowning of those who mistook it for land.

Domains & Habitats

Al-Mihraj dwells in the primordial ocean or the cosmic sea, often considered one of the layers of the earth or the foundation of the world. It is part of the chain of creation that supports the earth.

Version Differences

In some versions, Al-Mihraj is identified with Bahamut, the giant fish that holds the earth on its back, supported by an angel and a bull. In others, it is a separate entity that roams the ocean. The whale-island motif appears in various cultures, including the story of Sinbad the Sailor, where a whale is mistaken for an island.

Form

Al-Mihraj is often depicted as a massive fish or whale with a back that resembles land, sometimes with a single horn or a crown-like structure on its head. Its body is said to be as long as a mountain range, and its fins are as wide as a desert oasis.

Powers

Control over the oceans and the weather Al-Mihraj is said to have the power to control the oceans and the weather, and is often associated with the mythological storms and tidal waves of the Arabian Peninsula.
