Who It Is

The D&D Troll is a towering, rubbery-limbed humanoid giant — typically 9 feet tall and weighing 500 pounds — whose single most terrifying ability is regeneration. Unlike the stone trolls of Norse myth or the bridge-trolls of fairy tales, the D&D Troll can heal from almost any wound, regrowing severed limbs in minutes and closing fatal gashes in seconds. Only fire and acid can halt this regeneration; any other damage, no matter how catastrophic, is temporary. A Troll with its head cut off will grow a new one. A Troll reduced to scattered chunks will pull itself back together. This makes the Troll one of the most memorably frightening enemies for new players, who learn the hard way that 'it's still moving' means 'it's not dead.'

Physically, D&D Trolls are hideous. Their skin is a mottled green-gray, warty and perpetually slick with slime. Their bodies are gangly and disproportionate, with arms that drag to the ground and hands ending in black claws like iron nails. Their faces are elongated, with a snout-like nose, small eyes that glow green with malice, and a mouth full of jagged, constantly regrowing teeth. They are perpetually hungry — a Troll's metabolism, driven by its regeneration, requires constant fuel, and a Troll will eat literally anything organic, from deer to goblins to adventurers to rotten wood.

Trolls are not intelligent, but they possess a low cunning that makes them dangerous predators. They understand the value of ambush, of separating prey from a group, of dragging victims into water (Trolls are excellent swimmers with webbed hands and feet). Their lairs, typically in swamps or near underground rivers, are garbage heaps of bones, rusted armor, and the half-eaten remains of their victims — the regeneration means a Troll doesn't need to be careful about injury while hunting, and they fight with a reckless savagery that belies any instinct for self-preservation.

D&D has expanded Trolls into numerous variants: the two-headed Ettin-troll crossbreed, the massive and intelligent Dire Troll, the acid-resistant Venom Troll, the four-armed Rot Troll, and the aquatic Scrag, among others. The D&D Troll's regeneration mechanic has become so iconic that 'troll regeneration' is now standard in almost every fantasy game — from World of Warcraft to The Elder Scrolls to Dark Souls — and the specific weakness to fire and acid has become tabletop gaming's universal 'you need to read the Monster Manual' lesson.
