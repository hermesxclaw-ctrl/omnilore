Who It Is

Auðr djúpúðga (Aud the Deep-Minded, also called Unnr in some sources) is one of the most remarkable figures in the settlement of Iceland — a female chieftain who led her own expedition, claimed vast tracts of land, and established a dynasty that features in multiple sagas. She was the daughter of Ketill flatnefr (Flat-Nose), a Norwegian chieftain, and the wife of Óláfr hvíti (Olaf the White), King of Dublin. After the deaths of her husband and son (Þorsteinn rauðr, Thorstein the Red, who was killed in Caithness, Scotland), Auðr commissioned a knörr (ocean-going ship) built secretly in a forest — an act of quiet audacity that marks her character. She captained the ship to Orkney (where she married off a granddaughter), then to the Faroe Islands, and finally to Iceland, arriving with twenty free men in her crew. In Iceland, she claimed an enormous territory — all the land of the Dalir (the Dales) in western Iceland — and distributed it to her followers, family members, and freed slaves (whom she manumitted and gave land). She established her seat at Hvammr, where she raised crosses (she was a Christian in a still-pagan Iceland) and presided over a community that became one of Iceland's most important political centers. Her descendants include the protagonists of Laxdæla saga, Eyrbyggja saga, and many other sagas — she is the genealogical keystone of Icelandic literature. Auðr represents a model of female authority in the Viking Age: she acts as a chieftain (goði-like authority), distributes land, commands a ship's crew, makes political marriages for her grandchildren, and exercises 'djúpúðga' (deep-mindedness) — wisdom, foresight, and strategic patience — rather than the masculine 'injury-price' (drengskapr, heroic courage). She is not a warrior but a builder, and her legacy outlasts any berserker's fame.
