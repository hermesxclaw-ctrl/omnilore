Form

No visual depiction of the Alaisiagae survives. The altars themselves are plain, without figural reliefs, which is unusual for Romano-British dedications to goddesses. Their appearance is entirely unknown and must be considered unattested.

Powers

Protection of Military Units The Alaisiagae are invoked by two distinct Germanic military units stationed at Housesteads. Accounts

A unit of Frisians (cunei Frisiorum) stationed at Housesteads on Hadrian's Wall dedicated an altar to the Alaisiagae, Beda and Fimmilena. The dedication was made for the welfare of the Emperor Severus Alexander and his household, and for the unit itself. This act represents the integration of a local or Germanic cult into the Roman military religious framework.

A second altar dedicated to the Alaisiagae was erected at Housesteads by the 'numerus Hnaudifridi'—a warband named after its leader, Hnaudifrid. This unit, likely of Germanic origin like their Frisian counterparts, invoked the goddesses for protection and favor. The dedication uses slightly different grammatical formulations than RIB 1594, addressing the Alaisiagae in the singular ('Deae Alaisiagae') while still naming both Beda and Fimmilena. This grammatical ambiguity has fueled scholarly debate about whether the Alaisiagae are best understood as a single goddess with two aspects or as a distinct pair. The existence of two separate dedications by two different Germanic military units within the same short time period suggests the cult was well-established among the fort's garrison and perhaps served as a marker of Germanic ethnic identity within the cosmopolitan mix of Hadrian's Wall. The warband leader Hnaudifrid is otherwise unknown to history—his name survives only on this single altar stone—making the dedication as much a monument to his command as to the goddesses themselves.

What We Don't Know

The primary gap is the complete absence of any narrative mythology or iconography for the Alaisiagae. We have their names and the fact of their worship, but nothing else. The etymology of all three names (Alaisiagae, Beda, Fimmilena) is uncertain. The exact nature of the cult (pair vs. triad) is unresolved. The relationship between the two dedicating units and the goddesses is unknown. The geographic extent of the cult (if any) is unknown. The lack of a comprehensive, up-to-date, book-length study of the religious life of Housesteads is a secondary gap.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of the Alaisiagae as the patron saints of a single, specific army unit. They weren't famous or widely worshipped. They were the local guardians, the spiritual backup for a group of foreign soldiers stuck on the edge of the known world. In a modern sense, they're like the mascot or the 'good luck charm' of a regiment, but with the full weight of a religious dedication behind them. They are a testament to the personal, localized nature of ancient religion.
