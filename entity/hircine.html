Who It Is

The Daedric Prince of the Hunt, Sport, and the Chase. Known as the Huntsman and the Father of Manbeasts. He created all lycanthropic diseases, making him the father of werewolves and all were-creatures. His realm is the Hunting Grounds, an endless wilderness where the ultimate hunt is eternal. His artifacts include the Spear of Bitter Mercy and the Ring of Hircine.

The Approach

In the pantheon of Tamriel's Daedric Princes, Hircine stands apart as the Hunt Father, a being of primal instinct and relentless pursuit. His realm, the Hunting Grounds, is a boundless wilderness where the thrill of the chase is eternal, and his followers, the werewolves, carry his blessing and curse across Nirn.

Content advisory. Depictions of hunting, violence, and transformation; themes of savagery and the predator-prey dynamic.

Etymology

The name 'Hircine' likely derives from the Latin 'hircus', meaning 'he-goat', referencing his bestial nature and hunting prowess.

Cultural Ownership

Hircine is a Daedric Prince in the fictional universe of The Elder Scrolls, created by Bethesda Game Studios. He is not part of any real-world culture or religion.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans have speculated connections to real-world deities like Cernunnos or Herne the Hunter, but these are not supported by official lore.

Description

Appearance

Hircine is depicted as a massive, imposing figure with the head of a stag or wolf, often with antlers. He is typically shown wearing animal hides and wielding a spear. In Skyrim, he appears as a large, dark-furred werewolf-like being with glowing eyes.

Symbols & Iconography

Common symbols include the stag, the wolf, and the spear. His Daedric artifact, the Savior's Hide, is a cuirass made from the hide of a werewolf. The Ring of Hircine grants its wearer control over lycanthropy.

Behaviour & Personality

Hircine embodies the hunt in all its forms: the thrill of the chase, the skill of the hunter, and the ferocity of the predator. He is not inherently malevolent but is utterly indifferent to the suffering of prey. He respects those who hunt fairly and despises those who hunt for sport or cruelty.

Domains & Habitats

Hircine's realm is the Hunting Grounds, a vast, untamed wilderness filled with endless forests, plains, and mountains. It is a place of eternal hunting, where prey is always abundant and the hunt never ends.

Version Differences

In Morrowind, Hircine is more obscure and his quest is optional. In Oblivion, he is a central figure in the Shivering Isles expansion, though he appears in the base game. In Skyrim, his quest is one of the Daedric quests, and he has a more prominent role. In ESO, he is featured in multiple zones and storylines, with expanded lore.

Powers & Abilities

Lycanthropy

Source Version All games

Description

The ability to transform into a werewolf or grant this ability to mortals. Hircine is the source of lycanthropy in Tamriel.

Limitations

Those afflicted must manage their transformations and may lose control under the full moon. The Ring of Hircine can grant voluntary control.

Contradictions

Some sources suggest lycanthropy is a curse, while others view it as a blessing. The exact mechanics vary between games.

The Hunter's Path

Source The Elder Scrolls IV: OblivionCulture DaedricPeriod Third Era

Summary

The Champion of Cyrodiil is tasked with hunting a unicorn in the Shivering Isles. Hircine appears and challenges the Champion to a hunt, offering a powerful artifact as a reward.

Participants

Hircine

Champion of Cyrodiil

Consequences

The Champion gains the Savior's Hide or the Spear of Bitter Mercy, depending on choices.

Variants

The quest can be completed by either killing the unicorn or sparing it, leading to different rewards.

Ill Met By Moonlight

Source The Elder Scrolls V: SkyrimCulture DaedricPeriod Fourth Era

Summary

The Dragonborn encounters a werewolf named Sinding who has been cursed by Hircine. The Dragonborn must help Sinding either kill the hunters sent by Hircine or kill Sinding himself.

Sinding

Dragonborn

Consequences

The Dragonborn receives the Ring of Hircine and can choose to let Sinding live or die.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nature of lycanthropy

Position A

Lycanthropy is a blessing from Hircine, granting power and freedom.

Position A — evidence

Some werewolves in Skyrim and ESO embrace their condition.

Position B

Lycanthropy is a curse that brings suffering and loss of control.

Position B — evidence

Sinding in Skyrim laments his curse, and many NPCs view werewolves as monsters.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus; it is a matter of perspective within the lore.

Number of aspects

Position A

Hircine has three aspects: Hunter, Prey, and Beast.

Position A — evidence

In ESO, there are shrines to three aspects.

Position B

Hircine has only two aspects: Hunter and Prey.

Position B — evidence

In Skyrim, only two aspects are mentioned in dialogue.

Scholarly Consensus

The lore is inconsistent; the three-aspect model is more detailed in ESO.
