The Approach

From the shadow of a legendary film score to the concert hall, Icelandic composer Veigar Margeirsson carved a niche in cinematic music with a single, powerful adaptation that has since become a staple of movie trailers worldwide.

Identity

Veigar Margeirsson

Pronunciation VAY-gar MAR-gairs-son (approximate Icelandic pronunciation: [ˈveiːɣar ˈmarkeiːr̥sˌsɔːn])

Etymology

Veigar is an Icelandic male name, possibly derived from Old Norse 'veig' meaning 'strength' or 'power'. Margeirsson is a patronymic surname, meaning 'son of Margeir'.

Stories & Myths

Composing 'Mind the Gap'

Source IMDb and film reviewsCulture American independent cinemaPeriod 2004

Summary

Veigar composed the original score for Eric Schaeffer's film 'Mind the Gap', a drama about five strangers whose lives intersect. The score contributed to the film's emotional tone.

Participants

Veigar Margeirsson

Eric Schaeffer

Consequences

Established Veigar as a film composer.

Variants

None.

Creating 'Requiem for a Tower'

Source Various online articles and interviewsCulture Film trailer music industryPeriod 2002

Summary

Veigar and collaborators arranged Clint Mansell's 'Lux Aeterna' into a more epic, orchestral-choral version specifically for the 'The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers' trailer. The piece became iconic and was later used in numerous other trailers, though it was not part of the film's score.

Participants

Veigar Margeirsson

Clint Mansell

The Lord of the Rings production team

Consequences

The piece gained widespread popularity and became a staple in trailer music, but also led to confusion about its origin.

Variants

The piece is sometimes misattributed to Howard Shore or to the film's official soundtrack.

Premiering 'Rætur'

Source Iceland Symphony Orchestra recordsCulture Icelandic classical musicPeriod 2007

Summary

Veigar composed a saxophone concerto based on Icelandic folk songs for saxophonist Sigurður Flosason. It was premiered by Flosason and the Iceland Symphony Orchestra.
