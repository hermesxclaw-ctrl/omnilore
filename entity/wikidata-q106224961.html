Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Barton' derives from Old English 'bere' (barley) and 'tūn' (enclosure, settlement), meaning 'barley farm' or 'barley enclosure'. In the English West Country, it historically referred to a feudal demesne, later a large farmhouse or manor house.

Accounts I
The Feudal DemesneHistorical records

Medieval

In the English West Country, a 'barton' was a feudal demesne, the land retained by the lord for his own use, as opposed to land rented to tenants. Over time, the term came to mean a large farmhouse or the manor house itself. The term 'barton' became a place-name, leading to many settlements named Barton across England.

Accounts II
Migration and ExpansionHistorical records

Colonial era to present

People with the surname Barton migrated to various parts of the world, including the Americas, Australia, and Canada, taking their name with them. This led to the naming of many places after individuals or as a transfer of the original place-name. The name Barton is now found in many countries, with numerous towns, counties, and other geographical features bearing the name.
