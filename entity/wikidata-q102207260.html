Who It Is

Four is the only number that is both the sum and product of two equal numbers—2+2 and 2×2—and its shape has evolved from a simple cross scratched in ancient India to the open-topped glyph on your calculator. Yet in East Asia, it is a whisper of death, so feared that buildings skip the fourth floor. This is the story of a digit that is at once a mathematical cornerstone, a cultural taboo, and a typographic chameleon.

As a number, 4 is stable, square, and foundational. It is the smallest composite number and the first square number after 1. In numerology, 4 is often associated with practicality, order, and hard work.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The English word 'four' descends from Proto-Indo-European *kʷetwóres, which also gave rise to Latin 'quattuor', Greek 'téssares', and Sanskrit 'catvāraḥ'. The digit's shape evolved from Brahmi numerals, where four was represented by a cross-like symbol.

Pronunciation — /fɔːr/ (British), /fɔːr/ (American)

Aliases — IV (Roman numeral), 四 (Chinese/Japanese/Korean), 4 (Arabic numeral), tetra- (prefix), quadri- (prefix)

Ownership — The concept of the number 4 is universal, but the specific glyph has a history rooted in Indian Brahmi numerals, later transmitted through Arabic and European adaptations. Cultural

Powers
Square number

4 is the square of 2 (2×2). It is the only square number N such that N−1 is prime (4−1=3).

Limit: None

Smallest semiprime and composite number

4 is the smallest composite number and the smallest semiprime (product of two primes, specifically 2×2).

Limit: None

Four-color theorem

The four-color theorem states that any planar map can be colored with at most four colors such that no adjacent regions share the same color. This was the first major theorem proven using a computer.

Limit: Three colors are not sufficient in general.

Lagrange's four-square theorem

Every positive integer can be expressed as the sum of at most four squares.

Limit: None

Hopf fibrations

There are four Hopf fibrations of hyperspheres, which are locally trivial fibrations mapping S^{2n-1} → S^n for n = 1, 2, 4, 8 (with n=1 being trivial).

Limit: Only for specific dimensions (1,2,4,8).

Klein four-group

The smallest non-cyclic group has four elements and is called the Klein four-group.

Limit: None

Knuth's up-arrow property

2+2 = 2×2 = 2^2 = 2↑↑2 = 2↑↑↑2 = ... = 4 for any number of up-arrows.

Limit: Only for the number 2.

Tetraphobia

In East Asian cultures, the number 4 is considered unlucky because it sounds like the word for 'death' in Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. This leads to the avoidance of the number in buildings, phone numbers, and other contexts.

Limit: Not universal; varies by region and individual belief.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The digit 4 in most modern typefaces has an ascender, with a closed top and a diagonal stroke. In text figures (old-style numerals), the glyph has a descender and may appear as an open-top variant. On seven-segment displays, the 4 is typically shown with an open top, resembling a 'h' without the left vertical. Some television channel logos use an 'open 4' with the opening on the side, resembling the Canadian Aboriginal syllabics letter ᔦ.

Symbols — The number 4 is often associated with the square, the cross, and the tetrahedron. In alchemy, it represents the four elements (earth, air, fire, water). In Chinese culture, it is a homophone for death and is often avoided. In the Zia tribe, four is sacred, representing the four cardinal directions, the four seasons, and the four stages of life.

Domains — The number 4 appears throughout mathematics, science, culture, and technology. It is the number of dimensions in spacetime (Minkowski space), the number of fundamental forces, and the number of seasons in temperate climates. It is ubiquitous in everyday life, from the four cardinal directions to the four suits in a deck of cards.

Accounts I
Evolution of the Hindu-Arabic digitWikipedia (supplied source material)

Ancient to medieval

The Brahmi numeral for 4 was a cross, which later gained a horizontal line in Shunga, and evolved through Kshatrapa and Pallava scripts. Arabic numerals simplified the cross into a single stroke, and Europeans eventually closed the top to form the modern digit. The modern digit 4 is used worldwide, but regional variations persist (e.g., open-top 4 on seven-segment displays).

Accounts II
Tetraphobia in East AsiaWikipedia (supplied source material)

Ongoing

In many East Asian languages, the word for 'four' is a homophone for 'death'. This has led to the number being avoided in daily life: buildings often skip the fourth floor, phone numbers may avoid 4, and gifts are not given in sets of four. The number 4 carries a powerful negative connotation, influencing architecture, business, and social customs.

The sacred four of the ZiaWikipedia (supplied source material)

Traditional, ongoing

The Zia people consider four a sacred number, representing the four cardinal directions, the four seasons, the four stages of life, and the four sacred obligations. The Zia symbol, a four-pointed sun, is featured on the New Mexico state flag. The number 4 is deeply embedded in Zia cosmology and iconography.
