Who It Is

Imagine you are walking home through a dark forest in the Balkans. The silence is broken not by a twig snapping, but by a sound that freezes your blood: the desperate, piercing cry of a baby. You know there is no village nearby. You know there are no other travelers. You know, with a cold certainty, that what you are hearing is not a child. It is the Drekavac — the Yeller — the soul of a child who died without a name, condemned to scream its torment into the night forever. It is not a vampire that thirsts for blood, but something far more tragic: a voice that cannot be silenced, a soul that cannot find rest.

Its primary behavior is screaming. The cry is its defining feature. It is a piercing, high-pitched shriek that can sound like a baby's cry, a woman's wail, or a horrifying, inhuman screech. The cry is used to terrify, to lure, and to express its own torment. It is nocturnal, active from dusk to dawn. It haunts the outskirts of villages, forests, fields, rivers, and graveyards. It is a malevolent trickster in some tales, leading travelers astray or mimicking the cries of a lost child to lure them into danger. Powers
Screaming/Crying

The Drekavac's signature ability. It can project its voice over long distances, mimicking the cries of a baby or a woman. This is not just a sound; it is a weapon that induces fear, panic, and despair. It can be used to lure victims into dangerous places.

Limit: It cannot speak words or communicate rationally. It is a purely sonic and emotional attack. It cannot physically harm with the scream, only terrify.

Shape-shifting

It can alter its form, appearing as a shadow, a blur, a small animal (like a dog or cat), or a malformed humanoid. This is used for stealth and to create fear.

Limit: It cannot hold a perfect human form. It is always somewhat distorted or unnatural. It cannot turn into a beautiful person to seduce.

Invisibility/Intangibility

It can become completely invisible, making it impossible to track. It can pass through solid objects, as it is a spirit.

Limit: It is still vulnerable to apotropaic objects (holy symbols, certain herbs).

Cursing

In some tales, merely hearing its scream brings misfortune or illness to the listener. It is a carrier of bad luck.

Limit: This is a passive, incidental curse, not a deliberate spell.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Drekavac is amorphous and shape-shifting. It is rarely seen; it is primarily heard. When seen, it is described in two main forms: 1) A small, emaciated, hairless creature with a disproportionately large head and long, thin limbs, sometimes with sharp claws. Symbols — The scream itself is the symbol. It represents the voice of the voiceless, the anger of the innocent wronged, and the horror of a soul trapped in limbo. The unbaptized child is the key symbol, representing the tragedy of a life that never began and a soul that cannot be saved. The Drekavac is a symbol of parental sin (especially the mother's) and the social shame of illegitimate birth.

Domains — The liminal zones between the human world and the wilderness: forests, rivers, marshes, graveyards, abandoned houses, crossroads, and the edges of villages. It is a creature of the 'unclean dead' who cannot enter the afterlife, so it lingers in the spaces between.

Accounts I
The Crying Child at the CrossroadsOral tradition, recorded in 19th-century Serbian ethnographies.

19th century (belief is older)

A traveler is walking home at night and hears a baby crying near a crossroads. He searches for the child, fearing it has been abandoned. He finds a small, pale creature with a huge head and glowing eyes, which lets out a deafening shriek and vanishes. The traveler is found the next day, raving mad with fear. The traveler loses his sanity. The story serves as a warning to not trust the cries of a child in the wilderness, and to not travel alone at night.

Accounts II
The Unbaptized Child's RevengeOral tradition, collected in Bosnia.

19th-20th century

A woman gives birth to an illegitimate child and, in shame, kills it and buries it secretly in the forest. Soon after, the village is plagued by terrible screams at night. The crops fail, and the livestock become sick. An old wise woman (a 'vračara') tells the mother that the child's soul has become a Drekavac and must be placated. The mother must go to the grave, confess her sin, and perform a ritual to give the child a name (a form of 'baptism by intent'). Only then does the screaming stop. The ritual restores peace, but the mother is forever haunted by her guilt. The story reinforces the social stigma of illegitimacy and the importance of Christian burial rites.

The Scream at the Water's EdgeOral tradition, Croatia.

20th century

A fisherman on a river hears a woman's desperate screams for help from the reeds. He rows towards the sound, but the screaming seems to move further away, always just out of sight. He realizes it is not a woman, but a Drekavac, trying to lure him into deep water. He crosses himself and rows back to shore, the screaming turning into a furious, inhuman shriek behind him. The fisherman escapes, but is shaken. The story highlights the Drekavac's connection to water and its deceptive nature.

The Drekavac is a vampire. — It is a non-corporeal spirit, not a reanimated corpse. It does not drink blood, and it cannot be killed by a stake. It is a screaming soul, not a feeding monster.

The Drekavac is a type of water spirit like the Rusalka. — It can haunt water, but its primary domain is the wilderness and the edge of civilization. It is not exclusively a water spirit, and its origin is tied to baptism, not drowning.

The Drekavac is a specific, named monster with a fixed appearance. — It is a category of restless dead, much like a ghost. Its appearance is variable and often unseen. It is more of a phenomenon (a scream) than a creature with a stable form.

It is a 'Balkan Banshee' that foretells death. — The Banshee is a harbinger of death for a specific family. The Drekavac is the dead itself, crying out for its own fate. It does not predict death; it is the result of a death.
