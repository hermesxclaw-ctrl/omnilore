Who It Is

The Drekavac (literally 'the screamer,' from 'dreka' — to scream) is a terrifying undead creature of South Slavic folklore, born from the soul of a child who died unbaptized. He appears as a small, emaciated, humanoid figure — sometimes bird-like, sometimes dog-like — with an oversized head, huge luminous eyes, and long claws. His most distinctive feature is his paralyzing scream: a high-pitched, multi-toned wail that pierces the forest at night, capable of freezing travellers in place and causing permanent madness or death. The Drekavac haunts forests, cemeteries, and abandoned houses, emerging after dark to terrify but rarely to physically attack — the scream is his weapon. In some traditions, the Drekavac is a restless spirit who seeks baptism from any passerby; if you call out 'Come, I baptize you,' the creature departs in peace (at least for a time). But those who mock the Drekavac or try to hunt it find their homes besieged by its screaming, night after night, until someone goes mad or dies. The Drekavac is closely related to the 'nav' or 'navje' — the unbaptized dead in Slavic belief — and represents one of the most primal folk anxieties: the soul trapped between worlds, denied both heaven and a proper place in the ancestral community, screaming its grief into the night. Modern Balkan urban legends have updated the Drekavac for contemporary settings as a cryptid-like creature or a warning against neglecting children.

Form

The Drekavac is amorphous and shape-shifting. It is rarely seen; it is primarily heard. When seen, it is described in two main forms: 1) A small, emaciated, hairless creature with a disproportionately large head and long, thin limbs, sometimes with sharp claws. It is not a full corpse but a ghastly, semi-material spirit. 2) A shadowy, indistinct figure that moves quickly and erratically. Some accounts describe it as a blur or a dark mist. It has no consistent color, but is often described as a sickly pale or grey. Its eyes are often said to glow or be unnaturally bright. It is NOT a fully decayed zombie; it's a malformed, wailing soul.

Powers

Screaming/Crying The Drekavac's signature ability. It can project its voice over long distances, mimicking the cries of a baby or a woman. This is not just a sound; it is a weapon that induces fear, panic, and despair. It can be used to lure victims into dangerous places. Universal across all accounts. It cannot speak words or communicate rationally. It is a purely sonic and emotional attack. It cannot physically harm with the scream, only terrify. Some accounts say the scream is a warning of death (like a banshee), while others say it is a lure.

Shape-shifting It can alter its form, appearing as a shadow, a blur, a small animal (like a dog or cat), or a malformed humanoid. This is used for stealth and to create fear. Common, but not universal. Details vary. It cannot hold a perfect human form. It is always somewhat distorted or unnatural. It cannot turn into a beautiful person to seduce. None significant.

Invisibility/Intangibility It can become completely invisible, making it impossible to track. It can pass through solid objects, as it is a spirit. Implied in most accounts, explicitly stated in some. It is still vulnerable to apotropaic objects (holy symbols, certain herbs). Some accounts say it can be struck or physically interacted with, while others say it is purely spectral.

Cursing In some tales, merely hearing its scream brings misfortune or illness to the listener. It is a carrier of bad luck. Less common, but present. This is a passive, incidental curse, not a deliberate spell. None.

Behavior & Habitat

Its primary behavior is screaming. The cry is its defining feature. It is a piercing, high-pitched shriek that can sound like a baby's cry, a woman's wail, or a horrifying, inhuman screech. The cry is used to terrify, to lure, and to express its own torment. It is nocturnal, active from dusk to dawn. It haunts the outskirts of villages, forests, fields, rivers, and graveyards. It is a malevolent trickster in some tales, leading travelers astray or mimicking the cries of a lost child to lure them into danger. In other tales, it is a pitiful, tragic figure, its screams being an expression of its own grief and anger at its unjust fate. It cannot speak in human language, only scream. It is not a physical fighter; its power is in fear and psychological torment. It is repelled by consecrated ground, holy water, and the sign of the cross, though it is not a vampire and does not fear garlic.

Accounts

A traveler is walking home at night and hears a baby crying near a crossroads. He searches for the child, fearing it has been abandoned. He finds a small, pale creature with a huge head and glowing eyes, which lets out a deafening shriek and vanishes. The traveler is found the next day, raving mad with fear.

A woman gives birth to an illegitimate child and, in shame, kills it and buries it secretly in the forest. Soon after, the village is plagued by terrible screams at night. The crops fail, and the livestock become sick. An old wise woman (a 'vračara') tells the mother that the child's soul has become a Drekavac and must be placated. The mother must go to the grave, confess her sin, and perform a ritual to give the child a name (a form of 'baptism by intent'). Only then does the screaming stop.

A fisherman on a river hears a woman's desperate screams for help from the reeds. He rows towards the sound, but the screaming seems to move further away, always just out of sight. He realizes it is not a woman, but a Drekavac, trying to lure him into deep water. He crosses himself and rows back to shore, the screaming turning into a furious, inhuman shriek behind him.

Field Notes

⛧ The Drekavac is one of the few monsters whose name is literally a description of its behavior: 'the one who screams'.

⛧ In some regions, it is said that the Drekavac can be silenced by shouting its name back at it, a form of 'baptism by word'.

⛧ The belief was so strong that some families would hold a symbolic baptism for a stillborn or unbaptized child to prevent it from becoming a Drekavac.

⛧ The Drekavac is a prime example of a 'social monster' — a creature born from a specific social taboo (illegitimacy) and religious rule (baptism).

Scholarly Debates
Origin of the Drekavac
It is exclusively the soul of an unbaptized child. ⇌ It can also be the soul of an adult who died a violent or unjust death, or was cursed.
Evidence for A: The vast majority of collected folk tales and ethnographic records.
Evidence for B: A minority of accounts, particularly from regions with less strict baptism practices, describe adult origins.
The Nature of its Cry
The cry is a lure to lead people into danger. ⇌ The cry is an expression of its own torment and grief, not a deliberate trap.
Evidence for A: Tales of travelers being led astray or into rivers.
Evidence for B: Tales where the Drekavac is placated by a ritual, suggesting it is seeking resolution, not victims.
Physical Form
It has a semi-material, malformed body. ⇌ It is a purely invisible, spectral presence.
Evidence for A: Descriptions of a small, hairless creature with a big head.
Evidence for B: Accounts of only hearing the scream and seeing a shadow or blur.
What We Don't Know

Most primary sources are in Serbian/Croatian and have not been translated into English. A comprehensive, English-language academic monograph on the Drekavac is lacking. The exact geographic distribution of the belief is not fully mapped. The relationship between the Drekavac and pre-Christian Slavic 'nav' beliefs is under-researched. Modern oral accounts from the 21st century are scarce and not well documented.

A way to hold it in your mind

If a ghost is a soul trapped in a house, the Drekavac is a soul trapped in a scream. It is not bound to a place, but to a sound. It is the auditory manifestation of grief, guilt, and a life that was never allowed to begin. It is the sound of a mother's shame and a child's anger, echoing through the forests of the Balkans.
