Who It Is

Supay is the Inca god of the underworld (Ukhu Pacha), death, and the mineral wealth of the mountains. In the tripartite Inca cosmos (Hanan Pacha = upper world of the gods; Kay Pacha = this world of living humans; Ukhu Pacha = the inner/subterranean world of the dead, ancestors, and minerals), Supay was the ruler of Ukhu Pacha. He was not initially an 'evil' deity — death in Inca thought was a natural transition, and the dead (as mummified ancestors) continued to play an active role in community life through their huacas (sacred relics). However, the Spanish conquistadors, encountering a figure who ruled the underworld and received the dead, identified Supay with Satan and transformed him into the chief devil of colonial Andean Christianity. In the mines of Potosí, Supay survived as 'Tío Supay' (Uncle Supay) — a feared and propitiated underground lord whose statues, with cigarette and coca in mouth, still receive offerings from Bolivian miners. This syncretism represents one of the most dramatic examples of indigenous gods surviving within colonial Catholicism.
