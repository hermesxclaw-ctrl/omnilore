The persistent belief that Tupac Shakur faked his own death and is living in hiding — sometimes in Cuba (where his aunt Assata Shakur, a Black Panther exile, resides), sometimes on a secret island, sometimes spotted at malls and concerts. The myth is fueled by the music video for 'I Ain't Mad at Cha' (released days after his death, in which Tupac is shot and goes to heaven), his album 'The Don Killuminati: The 7 Day Theory' (recorded under the alias 'Makaveli' — evoking the philosopher Machiavelli, who faked his own death), and the sheer number of posthumous albums released.

Cultural Significance

The Tupac myth reflects the profound political and spiritual significance invested in him as an artist — a figure whose death could not be accepted because his work felt unfinished. The hologram performance at Coachella 2012 literally resurrected him on stage, blurring the line between myth and technology.
