Who It Is

In the vast, fire-lit cosmos of ancient Iran, where the forces of good and evil waged an eternal war, there existed a divine being whose very name was a promise of comfort: Airyaman, the 'Friend'. While the thunderous gods of war and creation dominated the pantheon, it was this gentle healer, invoked in whispered prayers against plague and despair, who held the key to the world's ultimate salvation.

Airyaman is primarily a healing deity and a 'friend' to humanity. He is invoked to counteract disease, pestilence, and the forces of evil. He is also a helper in the final restoration of the world. The Airyaman Ishya prayer (Yasna 54.1), one of the four great prayers of Zoroastrianism, occupies a unique position in the liturgical tradition. Unlike the Ahuna Vairya or the Ashem Vohu—prayers of cosmic declaration and personal righteousness, respectively—the Airyaman Ishya is specifically a prayer of healing, community, and eschatological restoration. Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Airyaman' is derived from the Avestan root 'airyaman-', which is related to the concept of the Aryan or Iranic peoples, and is often interpreted as 'friend' or 'one who brings healing'.

Pronunciation — Eye-ree-ah-mahn

Aliases — Airyaman, Airyaman Ishiia, Airyaman Yazata

Powers
Healing

Airyaman is invoked to cure diseases and ward off illness. He is the yazata (divine being) of healing.

Limit: He works in conjunction with the recitation of specific prayers (the Airyaman Ishya) and the purity laws of Zoroastrianism.

Friendship and Community

His name itself means 'friend', and he is seen as the protector of social bonds and the well-being of the Aryan community.

Limit: This is more a conceptual domain than a specific 'power'.

Eschatological Helper

At the end of time, Airyaman will assist the saoshyant (savior) in the final renovation of the world, helping to make it perfect and immortal.

Limit: This is a future role, not an immediate power.

Form & Signs

Accounts I
The Healing of the WorldYasht 3, Vendidad 22

Ancient (c. 1500-1000 BCE)

Airyaman is invoked in a ritual context to heal the world from the nine thousand diseases created by the evil spirit Angra Mainyu. He is the divine response to the plague of sickness that afflicts creation. The recitation of the Airyaman Ishya prayer is established as a powerful ritual for healing and protection.

Accounts II
The Final Renovation (Frashokereti)Bundahishn, later Pahlavi texts

Late Antiquity (c. 3rd-7th century CE)

In the end times, Airyaman will assist the savior (Saoshyant) in the great act of making the world fresh and immortal, resurrecting the dead and purging all evil, including death itself. This event brings about the final state of perfection, where evil is defeated and time ends.

The Coming of the Airyaman Ishya PrayerVendidad (Vīdēvdād), Fargard 22; later Zoroastrian exegetical tradition

Ancient (oral tradition c. 1500-1000 BCE, written c. 3rd-7th century CE)

According to the Vendidad, after Ahura Mazda created the material world, the evil spirit Angra Mainyu (Ahriman) counter-created 99,999 diseases to afflict humanity and all living things. In response, Ahura Mazda summoned Airyaman and instructed the prophet Zarathustra to invoke him through a specific prayer—the Airyaman Ishya. When Zarathustra recited this prayer, Airyaman descended with his healing power and drove away the diseases one by one, neutralizing Angra Mainyu's assault on creation. This narrative establishes the Airyaman Ishya as a divinely ordained weapon against illness and frames Airyaman himself as the cosmic physician, a specialized healer created specifically to address the problem of disease within the broader cosmic conflict between good and evil. The Airyaman Ishya prayer was permanently enshrined in the Zoroastrian liturgy as the primary ritual for healing and exorcism. Its recitation is understood to activate Airyaman's protective and curative presence in the material world.
