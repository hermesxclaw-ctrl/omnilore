Who It Is

In the heart of the Aztec Empire, a ruler whose name means 'He who makes sacrifices' took the throne of Tenochtitlan—yet his reign remains one of the most enigmatic and debated in Mesoamerican history. Tizoc's story is a tapestry of ambition, stone, and shadow, where the monuments he raised outlast the truth of his death.

Described as possibly less warlike than his predecessors; his reign is noted for few conquests and a focus on religious construction.

Political authority

As tlatoani, held supreme political and military command over Tenochtitlan and its tributaries.

Limit: Limited by the council of nobles and the need to maintain tribute flows.

Military leadership

Led military campaigns, though with limited success compared to his predecessors.

Limit: Fewer conquests recorded; possibly due to illness or internal opposition.

Religious authority

Oversaw major religious construction, including the rebuilding of the Great Pyramid.

Limit: None specified.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description survives. Typically depicted in codices with royal regalia, including the turquoise diadem (xiuhuitzolli) and nose pendant.

Symbols — Associated with the Stone of Tizoc, a large cylindrical monument depicting his military conquests.

Domains — Ruled from Tenochtitlan, the capital of the Aztec Empire.

Accounts I
Rebuilding of the Great PyramidTownsend (2000)

1481-1486

Tizoc initiated the reconstruction of the Templo Mayor, a project completed by his successor Ahuitzotl in 1487. Strengthened religious legitimacy and provided employment.

Accounts II
Suppression of Matlatzincan RebellionHassig (1988)

c. 1480s

Tizoc put down a rebellion among the Matlatzinca peoples of the Toluca Valley. Reasserted control over the region.

Conquests recorded in Codex MendozaCodex Mendoza

1481-1486

The codex lists several altepetl conquered during Tizoc's reign, including Tonalimoquetzayan, Toxico, Ecatepec, Cillán, Tecaxic, Tolocan, Yancuitlan, Tlappan, Atezcahuacan, Mazatlán, Xochiyetla, Tamapachco, Ecatliquapechco, and Miquetlan. Expanded tributary network.
