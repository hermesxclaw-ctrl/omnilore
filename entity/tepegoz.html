Who It Is

In the shadowed valleys of the Oghuz Turks, where the Book of Dede Korkut echoes with the deeds of heroes, there lurks a being that defies nature itself: Tepegöz, the one-eyed giant born of a shepherd and a peri, whose insatiable hunger and cunning made him the scourge of a people, until a hero's wit and a single burning brand brought him low.

Tepegöz is portrayed as a cruel, gluttonous, and cunning monster. He devours humans and animals indiscriminately, and he demands tribute from the Oghuz people, requiring them to send him young men and women to eat. Despite his brute strength, he is not entirely stupid; he uses his size and cunning to terrorize the people, and he is capable of speech and reasoning.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Turkish 'tepe' (hill, top) and 'göz' (eye), literally 'hill-eye' or 'eye on the top of the head'. The name refers to his single eye located on his forehead.

Pronunciation — teh-PEH-göz

Aliases — Tepegoz, Tepgöz, Tepagöz, Tepe Göz, Depe Göz

Ownership — Turkic, specifically Oghuz Turkish; part of the oral epic tradition recorded in the Book of Dede Korkut (Kitab-ı Dede Korkut), a foundational work of Turkish literature.

Powers
Superhuman strength and size

Tepegöz is a giant with immense physical power, capable of crushing rocks, uprooting trees, and easily killing warriors.

Limit: None explicitly stated, but he is mortal and can be killed by a weapon to his eye.

Invulnerability (partial)

His body is largely invulnerable to weapons, except for his eye. This is not explicitly stated as magical, but it is implied that his monstrous nature makes him resistant to harm.

Limit: His eye is his only weak point.

Devouring

He can swallow a human being whole, and he regularly eats people, especially young men and women.

Limit: None; he is a cannibal.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Tepegöz is depicted as a monstrous giant, with a single eye on his forehead, hence his name. He is described as having a huge body, a face like a lion's, and a voice like thunder. His fingers are like the branches of a tree, and his body is covered with coarse hair. He is often described as having a mouth that can swallow a man whole.

Symbols — The single eye on the forehead is the primary symbol. In art, he is often shown as a giant with a cyclopean eye, sometimes with a turban or headgear, reflecting his semi-human origin. The eye is also a symbol of his vulnerability.

Domains — He dwells in a cave in the mountains, near a spring and a meadow, where he grazes his flocks. This location is often identified as being in the vicinity of the Oghuz homeland in Central Asia or Anatolia, but the story does not specify a precise geography.

Accounts I
The Story of Basat and TepegözBook of Dede Korkut

Medieval (story likely older, oral tradition)

Tepegöz is born from a peri who was captured by a shepherd. The shepherd had been seduced by the peri, who then gave birth to a monstrous child. The child is abandoned, but is found and raised by a lioness, and later captured by the Oghuz. He is given to Aruz, a noble, to raise, but he becomes violent and is banished. He then terrorizes the Oghuz, demanding tribute of young men and women. Basat, the son of Aruz, confronts him. After a long struggle, Basat discovers Tepegöz's weakness: his eye. Basat blinds him with a burning brand and then kills him. The Oghuz are freed from Tepegöz's tyranny. Basat becomes a hero.
