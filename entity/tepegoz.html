Who It Is

Tepegöz ('Single Eye' in Turkish) is a monstrous one-eyed giant from the Book of Dede Korkut, the foundational epic of the Oghuz Turks. He is the son of a human woman and the Perizat (a fairy or nature spirit), marked at birth by his single bulging eye and a voracious appetite that drives him to devour humans by the dozen. When the Oghuz tribe tries to flee from him, Tepegöz imprisons them in a cave, eating ten men and several hundred sheep each day. He is invulnerable to swords and arrows because only his eye (the only soft part of his body) can be wounded. The hero Basat, who was raised in the wilderness by a lioness after being lost as a child, finally confronts Tepegöz. Using wit as much as strength, Basat forces Tepegöz to fall asleep, then drives a hot iron spit through his eye, blinding him. A desperate struggle follows — Tepegöz, though blind, nearly kills Basat by choking him — but Basat finally cuts off Tepegöz's head, ending the terror. The Tepegöz story shares a clear Indo-European typology with the Cyclops Polyphemus from Homer's Odyssey (the blinding of a one-eyed giant who eats humans in a cave), suggesting either an ancient common source (Proto-Indo-European) or later cultural transmission. Tepegöz represents the blind, consuming force of brute power — the tyrant who eats the people and must be outwitted by a hero who knows that strength alone cannot defeat him.
