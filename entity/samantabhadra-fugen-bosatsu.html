Who It Is

Samantabhadra is the bodhisattva of universal virtue, right practice, and the protector of the Buddha's teaching — the embodiment of the perfection of practice and the active commitment to benefiting all beings. He is one of the most prominent bodhisattvas in Mahayana Buddhism, often depicted alongside the Buddha Śākyamuni and Mañjuśrī: Mañjuśrī on the left (wisdom), and Samantabhadra on the right (practice). He is usually shown riding a six-tusked white elephant, symbolizing the victory of wisdom over defilements (the six tusks representing the six perfections). Samantabhadra is the author of the 'Vow of Beneficial Action' in the Avatamsaka Sutra — ten great vows that define the bodhisattva path, including honoring all Buddhas, praising all Tathāgatas, practicing the Dharma, and dedicating merit to all beings. He is the patron of Mount Emei, one of China's four sacred Buddhist mountains. In Vajrayana, Samantabhadra appears as the primordial Buddha (Samantabhadra/Adibuddha) in the Nyingma tradition's Dzogchen teachings — the naked, blue primordial Buddha who represents the pristine, uncontrived nature of mind itself. In East Asian Buddhism, he is the protector of the Lotus Sutra.

Origins

Samantabhadra appears in the Lotus Sutra and the Avatamsaka Sutra (c. 1st-3rd centuries CE). He is the patron of Mount Emei, and his image is often paired with Manjushri in temples. In Tibetan Buddhism, he also exists as the primordial Buddha (Kuntuzangpo), representing the dharmakaya.
