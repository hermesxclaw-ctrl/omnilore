Who It Is

Hell is not only fire. At its very bottom, Islamic tradition imagines a counter-hell of shattering cold — Zamhareer — where blizzards flay, flesh freezes and shatters, and even the demons made of fire finally feel pain.

Not a sentient being; as a place/punishment, its 'behavior' is to inflict cold-torture. Traditions personify Jahannam as complaining to God and breathing (hadith of two breaths), with Zamhareer as its cold breath in winter. Guardians (zabaniyah) are said to drive or allow transfer of sinners between fire and cold. The locale is passive but inescapable; supplicants beg to leave it.

Powers
Extreme Cold Torture (Adhab al-Bard)

Inflicts punishment through unbearable, penetrating cold that freezes, cracks, and tears flesh and bone. Described as blizzards, ice, and snow that dismember the damned. Functions as counterpart to burning.

Limit: Only operates by divine permission within Jahannam; not an independent power. Sinners cannot acclimate; cold is described as inescapable and worse than fire for those transferred there. No attested resistance except divine mercy.

Hell's Respiration - Winter Emanation

Jahannam is granted two breaths per year; its winter breath releases zamharir upon the earth, causing earthly winter cold. Cosmological ability linking eschatological realm to earthly climate.

Limit: Occurs only twice yearly by divine allowance; not continuous. Earthly cold is only a portion/part (nafas) of Hell's cold, not its full intensity.

Punishment of Fire-Created Beings

Because jinn and shayatin were created from fire (Q. 15:27, 55:15), fire would not harm them; Zamhareer provides an effective punishment via its opposite element. Iblis/Satan is described as frozen, immobilized, and impotent at the bottom.

Limit: Theological reasoning (qiyas), not explicit scriptural statement. Not all scholars accept that jinn are immune to fire; Quran states jinn will be punished in Fire generally.

Bone-Scattering Pit

As a pit (jub), Zamhareer scatters and shatters the bones of the damned, tearing them apart with frost. Implies disintegration and reconstitution cycle of punishment.

Limit: Isnad considered weak by some hadith critics; not in canonical sahih collections. Described as athar, not prophetic hadith.

Forced Oscillation Between Heat and Cold

Sinners beg guardians to escape fire, are moved to Zamhareer, then beg to return to fire due to cold's severity, creating an endless oscillation between extremes with no relief.

Limit: Narrative motif to illustrate hopelessness; not described as voluntary power of Zamhareer itself but as divine punishment regime administered by zabaniyah.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Zamhareer has no fixed iconography or archaeological depiction, being an immaterial eschatological locale. Texts describe it as a bottom or pit (qa'r, jub, asfal) of Jahannam characterized by unbearable cold: blizzards, ice, snow, frost, and darkness. The cold is described as biting, bone-scattering, and flesh-shattering — so intense that skin and bones crack and are torn apart. No light or sun is present, contrasting with paradise's temperate sh

Symbols — No pre-modern visual tradition. Symbols associated in texts: ice, snow, blizzard wind (rih sarsar), scattered bones, frostbite, darkness, breath/vapor. In popular contemporary da'wah graphics, depicted as blue-black icy cavern or frozen desert at the bottom of a fiery abyss, but these are modern illustrative inventions, not canonical iconography. No talismans or ritual objects attested.

Domains — Domain: the lowest or most interior part of Jahannam (Jahannam al-zamharir). Cosmology: Islamic hell is often described as having seven gates/levels (abwab/darakat); Zamhareer is not consistently listed among the seven named gates but is placed at the bottom (asfal al-nar) in zuhd literature. Its earthly reflex is winter's bitter cold — theologically, winter cold on earth is an emanation of Hell's cold breath. Contrasted domain is Janna

Accounts I
The Paradise Without Sun or ZamharirQur'an 76:13 (Surat al-Insan)

c. 610-632 CE, early 7th century

In a Meccan/Medinan surah describing the reward of the righteous (al-abrar), God describes the inhabitants of Paradise reclining on couches, shaded, experiencing neither the scorching sun (shams) nor bitter cold (zamharir). The verse establishes a temperate,痛less ideal, free from climatic extremes that afflict earthly and hellish existence. Exegetes later inverted this: if paradise lacks zamharir, hell must contain it. Establishes zamharir as the antithesis of paradisiacal comfort; becomes proof-text for hell's cold. Basis for all later Zamhareer theology.

Hell Complains and Is Allowed Two BreathsSahih al-Bukhari 3260; Sahih Muslim 617a-c; also Sunan al-Tirmidhi, Musnad Ahmad

9th century compilation; attributed to 7th century prophetic statement

Hell (Jahannam) complains to its Lord that its parts are consuming one another from intensity. God permits it to take two breaths: one in winter and one in summer. The heat experienced in summer is from Hell's hot breath, and the bitter cold (zamharir) in winter is from its cold breath. This hadith theologically legitimates Hell as possessing both thermal extremes and explains seasonal temperature as eschatological exhalation. Canonical basis for doctrine that Hell is not only fire; provides sahih authority for Zamhareer's existence as Hell's cold aspect. Frequently cited in tafsir of Q. 76:13 and in sermons during cold weather.

The Flight from Fire to Zamhareer and BackIbn Abi al-Dunya, Sifat al-Nar; al-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Tha'labi citing Mujahid ibn Jabr and Ka'b al-Ahbar

8th-9th century athar (reports of successors)

The damned, unable to bear the Fire, plead with the guardians (khazana) of Hell to relieve them. The guardians move them to Zamhareer, a region of intense cold. There, frost and blizzards tear their flesh and bones. The suffering is so unbearable that they beg to be returned to the Fire, preferring burning to freezing. They are thus oscillated between extremes, finding no rest. One version specifies Zamhareer as a pit where bones are scattered. Creates vivid oscillation motif illustrating hopelessness of Hell; used in sermons to warn that Hell's punishments are multifaceted. Reinforces Zamhareer as active torture locale.

Accounts II
The Pit Where Bones Are ScatteredAthar cited in al-Qurtubi, al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an 19:129 commentary on Q. 76:13; Ibn Abi al-Dunya

10th-13th century tafsir compilations

Zamhareer is described not as an open desert but as a deep pit (jub) or well in the lowest depth of Hell. In it, the bones (izam) of the damned are strewn and ripped apart by cold. The image emphasizes dismemberment and dehumanization, with cold acting like a blade. Some reports add that the damned's skin cracks and falls off from frostbite. Provides topographical specificity to Zamhareer; influences later popular descriptions of Hell's vertical structure with frozen bottom.

The Punishment of the Jinn and Iblis Frozen at the BottomLater theological elaboration; discussed in Qisas al-Anbiya, al-Ghazali, and modern analysis by Christian Lange; Quranic basis Q. 15:27, 55:15 (jinn created from fire)

11th century onward; systematized in medieval theology

Since jinn and shayatin (devils) were created from smokeless fire (nar al-samum), the Fire of Hell would not harm them. Therefore God created Zamhareer — cold as the opposite element — to punish them appropriately. Iblis (Satan), their leader, is depicted as frozen, chained, and impotent at the very bottom of Hell, his power neutralized by immobilizing cold. His frozen state symbolizes ultimate abasement and restriction after his rebellion. Explains theological problem of how fire-beings are punished; elevates Zamhareer to necessary cosmological counterpart to Fire; reinforces Iblis's impotence.

The Supplication in Extreme Cold (Du'a al-Zamharir)Hadith-adjacent du'a literature; popular preaching based on hadith of Hell's breath; external article 'Zamharir: The Cold Section of Hell & the Du'a During Cold Weather'

Modern (20th-21st century popularization, based on classical material)

Building on the hadith that winter cold is from Hell's breath, preachers teach a supplication when experiencing severe cold: to seek refuge from Zamhareer and ask God for warmth and protection, as one seeks refuge from Hell's heat in summer. Some narrations (weak) quote the Prophet saying 'When it is extremely cold, Hell breathes...' and encourage saying 'La ilaha illa Allah, ma ashadda barda hadha al-yawm, Allahumma ajirni min zamharir Jahannam.' Though the specific wording's authenticity is debated, the practice links daily weather experience to eschatological mindfulness. Brings Zamhareer into lived piety and seasonal ritual; widely shared on Islamic websites and social media during cold snaps.

Hell in Islam is only fire and heat. — Canonical hadith (Bukhari/Muslim) and tafsir explicitly state Hell possesses both extreme heat and extreme cold (zamharir); cold is a distinct, sahih-attested punishment.

It is not a deity or pre-Islamic mythological figure.
