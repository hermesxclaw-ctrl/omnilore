Who It Is

La Pantanera (also Pantànera or Pantanèra in local dialects) is a terrifying swamp spirit from the folklore of Lombardy and Piedmont, particularly the marshy regions of the Po Valley. She takes the form of a tall, gaunt woman dressed in tattered, mud-soaked black or gray clothing, with long, tangled black hair and glowing red or yellow eyes. She stalks the edges of marshes, rice paddies (risaie), and stagnant ponds, preying upon lone travelers — especially children and young women. The Pantanera is said to mimic human voices, calling out the names of passersby in the voice of a loved one to lure them into the swamp, where they become mired in the mud and drown. She is frequently associated with the will-o'-the-wisp (fuoco fatuo), leading travelers into treacherous bogs by swinging a spectral lantern. In some versions, the Pantanera is the ghost of a woman who was betrayed by her lover and drowned herself in the marshes, now cursed to lure others to the same watery fate. Rice paddy workers of the Po Valley traditionally told Pantanera stories to explain the disappearance of workers during the malaria-infested rice season. She is also known as the 'Signora della Palude' (Lady of the Swamp) and is considered the Italian equivalent of the will-o'-the-wisp and the marsh-hag figures found in other European traditions. Amulets of garlic and iron are said to repel her, as is the recitation of specific prayers.
