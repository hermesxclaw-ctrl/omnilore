Who It Is

For more than three decades, the crunching, galloping riff behind some of Germany's most enduring heavy metal anthems has often come from the same hands: Uwe Lulis, the quietly pivotal guitarist who helped shape Grave Digger's 1990s renaissance, built his own Viking saga with Rebellion, and then spent eleven years anchoring Accept's modern reunion era.

Described in band statements and interviews as a riff-oriented songwriter and studio-focused producer. Long tenures in collaborative bands (13 years in Grave Digger, 10 years in Rebellion, 11 years in Accept) suggest stable collaborative role rather than frontman persona. Known as producer and studio owner (Black Solaris Studios), indicating technical/engineering orientation. No reliable psychological biography available; personality assessments beyond professional role would be speculative. Accounts I
Joining Grave Digger and the Digger Transition (1987-1993)Band history and discography: Stronger Than Ever (1987) credited to Digger, then Grave Digger discography

1987-1993

Lulis joined Grave Digger (briefly operating as Digger) in 1987 at age 21. After the commercial detour of Stronger Than Ever (1987), the band reverted to Grave Digger and re-established its heavy metal identity. Lulis's first widely distributed Grave Digger album was The Reaper (1993), followed quickly by Symphony of Death (1993). His 13-year tenure began here, during which he became a core co-writer. Established Lulis as long-term guitarist and co-writer; anchored the band's 1990s creative resurgence after late-1980s instability.

The 1990s Concept Album Era with Grave Digger (1995-1999)Album liner notes and band history: Heart of Darkness (1995), Tunes of War (1996), Knights of the Cross (1998), Excalibur (1999)

1995-1999

As co-writer and guitarist, Lulis contributed to Grave Digger's most acclaimed 1990s run: the dark, Edgar Allan Poe-influenced Heart of Darkness (1995), the Scottish history concept Tunes of War (1996) and its EP The Dark of the Sun (1997), the Crusades-themed Knights of the Cross (1998), and the Arthurian Excalibur (1999). These albums defined the band's signature blend of historical storytelling and power metal. Cemented Grave Digger's international reputation in power metal; created the songwriting partnership between Lulis and Göttlich that would later form Rebellion.

Founding Rebellion with Tomi Göttlich (2000-2002)Band history: Rebellion formation 2000; debut album Shakespeare's Macbeth – A Tragedy in Steel (2002)

2000-2002

After leaving Grave Digger in 2000, Lulis and former Grave Digger bassist Tomi Göttlich formed Rebellion in 2000. The band's debut was the literary concept album Shakespeare's Macbeth – A Tragedy in Steel (2002), adapting Macbeth into heavy metal, followed by Born a Rebel (2003). Lulis served as guitarist, songwriter, and producer, operating from his Black Solaris Studios. Created an independent vehicle for Lulis's songwriting and production; established Black Solaris Studios as creative base; allowed exploration of literary and historical concepts outside Grave Digger.

Accounts II
The Viking History Trilogy (2005-2009)Rebellion discography: Sagas of Iceland – The History of the Vikings Volume 1 (2005), Miklagard (2006/2007), Arise: From Ginnungagap to Ragnarök – The History of the Vikings Volume 3 (2009)

2005-2009

Rebellion's central artistic project under Lulis was a three-volume musical history of the Vikings: Sagas of Iceland (2005), Miklagard – The History of the Vikings Volume 2 (2007) preceded by the Miklagard single (2006), and Arise: From Ginnungagap to Ragnarök (2009). The trilogy traced Norse exploration from Iceland to Byzantium (Miklagard) to cosmological myth, blending historical narrative with power metal. Became Rebellion's defining work; demonstrated Lulis's capacity for large-scale concept writing; remains the band's most cited output in metal encyclopedias.

Motorcycle Crash and Single-Guitar Interlude (October 2008)Band news post October 2008 (Rebellion/Massacre Records)

2008

In October 2008, Lulis broke his right leg in a motorcycle crash. Because of the injury, Rebellion performed its planned gigs with only one guitarist, rather than canceling shows. The incident was reported as a temporary lineup adjustment, not a departure. Temporary reduction to single-guitar live sound; no long-term effect on recording career; illustrates touring contingency in small-ensemble metal bands.

Eleven Years in Accept and Wacken Reunion with Grave Digger (2014-2025)Accept official announcements 2014 and 2025; Wacken Open Air 2025 report 31 July 2025

2014-2025

On 4 December 2014, Lulis joined Accept as replacement for guitarist Herman Frank, joining Wolf Hoffmann's reunited lineup. During his 11-year tenure he recorded four studio albums (The Rise of Chaos 2017, Too Mean to Die 2021, Humanoid 2024, plus contributions to live releases Symphonic Terror – Live at Wacken 2017 and Restless and Live 2017) and toured globally. The tenure ended by mutual parting on 25 September 2025. Shortly before, on 31 July 2025, Lulis rejoined Grave Digger on stage at Wacken Open Air for their 45th anniversary, his first performance with the band in 25 years. Extended Lulis's legacy from 1990s Grave Digger to modern Accept; provided continuity between two pillars of German heavy metal; Wacken appearance symbolically closed a 25-year gap with his original band.
