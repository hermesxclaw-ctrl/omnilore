The Approach

On the coral atolls of the Green Islands, a language thrives that most of the world has never heard of—a language where stress can silence a vowel, where labialized consonants color every word, and where three dialects weave a community together. Nehan, spoken by thousands in Papua New Guinea, is not just a means of communication; it is a living archive of Austronesian heritage, adapting to modernity while preserving its unique phonological character.

Etymology

The name 'Nehan' is likely derived from the local name for Nissan Island or the language itself. The alternative names 'Nissan' and 'Nihan' may be variants used by outsiders or in different contexts. The exact etymology is not documented in the source.

Cultural Ownership

The Nehan language is spoken by the Nehan people, indigenous to the Green Islands (Nissan and Pinipel) in the Autonomous Region of Bougainville, Papua New Guinea. It is an Austronesian language, part of the Oceanic subgroup.

Disputed Identifications

No disputed identifications are mentioned in the source. The language is clearly distinguished from other languages in the region, though it shares similarities with other Austronesian languages.

Behaviour & Personality

Nehan is the dominant language in its communities, used for everyday communication. It coexists with Tok Pisin, which is used in specific contexts like village meetings, courts, and markets. Older speakers prefer Nehan exclusively, while younger speakers may mix or use Tok Pisin more.

Domains & Habitats

Spoken on Nissan Island (dialects Uanuleik and Sirouatan) and Pinipel Island (dialect Pinipel) in the Green Islands, north of Bougainville, Papua New Guinea.

Version Differences

Three dialects: Uanuleik (about 5000 speakers), Sirouatan (about 1000 speakers), and Pinipel (about 1000 speakers). Lexical similarity between Uanuleik and Pinipel is about 83%, indicating moderate divergence.
