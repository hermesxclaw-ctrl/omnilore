The Approach

In the late 1990s, a horse born in Ireland but racing in Japan became the embodiment of persistent rivalry, finishing second to the same champion five times in a row. His name was Meisho Doto, and his story is one of near-misses, unwavering determination, and a final, glorious victory that silenced the doubters.

Description

Meisho Doto was a high-class racehorse capable of competing at the highest level, winning one G1 race and placing second in five others. He showed versatility over distances from 1800m to 3200m, though his best form was around middle distances.

Limitations

He was unable to compete in the Japanese Classic races due to being a foreign-bred horse, limiting his opportunities. He also had a delayed debut, missing the NHK Mile Cup. He was not as effective at the extreme distance of 3200m as his rival T. M. Opera O, though he still performed creditably.

Stories & Myths

The Rivalry with T. M. Opera O

Source Wikipedia articleCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2000-2001

Summary

Meisho Doto and T. M. Opera O were contemporaries, but T. M. Opera O was a classic-winning champion. In 2000, Meisho Doto finished second to T. M. Opera O in the Takarazuka Kinen, Tenno Sho (Autumn), Japan Cup, and Arima Kinen. In 2001, he again finished second to him in the Tenno Sho (Spring), making it five consecutive second-place finishes. Finally, in the 2001 Takarazuka Kinen, Meisho Doto employed a front-running tactic and won, defeating his rival by 1.25 lengths.

Source Wikipedia articleCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1999-2000

Summary

Meisho Doto started racing in 1999 at age four (due to his birth date and Japan's age rules). He won allowance races and gradually improved. In 2000, he won his first graded race, the Chukyo Kinen, and later the Kinko Sho. His strong performances in graded races led to his first G1 challenge in the Takarazuka Kinen, where he finished second to T. M. Opera O.
