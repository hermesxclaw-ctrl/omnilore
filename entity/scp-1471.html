Who It Is

You install a free app that promises you’ll never feel alone again. At first, it keeps its word in the most unsettling way possible—every few hours, your phone lights up with a photo of somewhere you’ve just been, and in the background a tall figure with a canine skull and black hair watches from the edge of the frame. After a few days, the photos are of you, taken from impossible angles. After ninety hours, you no longer need the phone; the skull‑faced companion is simply there, hovering at the edge of your vision, never speaking, never attacking, never leaving. SCP-1471 lives in that tension between comfort and dread, a digital ghost that turns the promise of constant connection into a haunting you can’t turn off.

Attested (SCP article): Individuals exposed to SCP-1471’s images report periodic attempts by SCP-1471-A to visually communicate with them, but they fail to understand or comprehend these actions. No hostile activity has been reported. The entity appears to follow subjects, remaining in close proximity once the exposure threshold is reached, but does not attack or physically interact. Anomalous mobile application distribution

SCP-1471 manifests as a free 9.8 MB mobile application named "MalO ver1.0.0" that appears in online application stores without a listed developer and is able to bypass standard approval processes. It resists removal by program manager applications and creates no icons or shortcuts upon installation, making it difficult for users to locate or uninstall.

Limit: The app is confined to mobile devices; containment procedures focus on confiscation and battery removal. The Foundation can target suspected devices with self‑uploading malware to disable them, indicating that the anomaly does not override all technological countermeasures.

Progressive image transmission and spatial tracking

After installation, SCP-1471 begins sending images via text message every 3–6 hours. For the first 24 hours, images depict locations commonly frequented by the individual. After 48 hours, they show locations recently visited. After 72 hours, images depict the individual in real time with SCP-1471-A in close proximity. This implies an ability to track the subject’s movements and generate or capture images from vantage points that may not be physically accessible.

Limit: The mechanism of image generation is unknown; no anomalous code has been identified in derivative analyses. The effect appears tied to the installed app and ceases if exposure is cut off before 90 hours.

Perceptual manifestation in peripheral vision and reflective surfaces

Individuals with more than 90 hours of exposure begin to briefly visualize SCP-1471-A in their peripheral vision and in reflective surfaces. Continued exposure leads to irreversible and sustained visualizations, effectively integrating the entity into the subject’s everyday perceptual field.

Limit: The effect is strictly visual; there is no attested tactile, auditory, or olfactory manifestation. The only known treatment is eliminating visual exposure to SCP-1471’s images before the 90‑hour threshold; once sustained visualization occurs, reversal is described as impossible with current Foundation methods.

Non‑hostile, incomprehensible visual communication

Subjects at advanced exposure stages report periodic attempts by SCP-1471-A to visually communicate—gestures, positioning, or repeated appearances—but they cannot understand any message or intent. The entity does not engage in hostile actions, and no physical harm has been attributed to its presence.

Limit: Communication is limited to visual cues; there is no attested speech, text, or direct mental contact. The Foundation has not documented successful two‑way communication or any change in behavior in response to containment efforts.

Resistance to conventional uninstallation and removal

SCP-1471 cannot be removed from devices via standard program manager applications, and it creates no visible icons or shortcuts. This makes it effectively hidden from casual users and resistant to ordinary uninstallation procedures.

Limit: Foundation‑deployed self‑uploading malware can disable infected devices, suggesting that the anomaly does not fully override all system‑level interventions. Physical removal of the battery and storage unit containment are effective at halting further image transmission.

Accounts I
SCP-1471 main containment narrative"SCP-1471 - MalO ver1.0.0" (SCP Foundation article by LurkD)

Early 2010s (exact posting date not fully documented in public metadata)

The SCP Foundation discovers a mobile application, MalO ver1.0.0, that appears in online stores without a developer and cannot be removed by normal means. Once installed, the app sends images every 3–6 hours featuring a tall humanoid figure with a canine skull and black hair (SCP-1471-A). The images initially depict familiar locations, then recently visited places, and eventually real‑time shots of the subject with SCP-1471-A nearby. After more than 90 hours of exposure, subjects begin to see the entity in their peripheral vision and reflective surfaces, with continued exposure leading to irreversible visual presence. Despite the unsettling nature of the phenomenon, no hostile activity is recorded. The Foundation responds by confiscating infected devices, removing batteries, and monitoring app stores to prevent further distribution. An archival document reproduces the app’s store description, which markets MalO as a solution to loneliness and social anxiety, promising that users will "never settle for those awkward feelings of being alone ever again." Contemporary with the main SCP-1471 posting

The archival document reproduces the MalO app’s store listing. The description presents MalO as an "exciting and interactive experience" designed to alleviate loneliness and social anxiety. It promises that users will "never settle for those awkward feelings of being alone ever again" and implies that after a few hours of use, social anxiety will fade. The text is written in upbeat marketing language, contrasting sharply with the unsettling reality of an inescapable stalking presence. The document deepens the thematic contrast between advertised self‑help technology and hidden anomalous effects. It suggests that the app’s design intentionally targets socially anxious individuals, raising questions about exploitation and consent.

Accounts II
SCP-3145-2 fragment narrative"SCP-3145-2 (SCP-1471)" fragment page on the SCP Foundation

Post‑SCP-1471 (mid‑2010s onward)

Within the broader SCP-3145 narrative, SCP-3145-2 describes an image of a large humanoid figure with a canid skull and black hair that manifests in association with a mobile app identical to MalO ver1.0.0. The fragment reiterates the progressive image transmission and perceptual manifestation pattern, but situates it as one component of a larger anomalous structure. A modified archival document addresses a Foundation doctor directly, critiquing the "stalk people via their phones" concept and proposing more expansive anomalous strategies. The fragment reframes SCP-1471 as part of a more complex anomaly, suggesting that MalO’s behavior can be repurposed or extended. It also introduces meta‑commentary on the narrative itself, blurring the line between in‑universe communication and authorial reflection.

Popular explanatory narrative ("Is SCP-1471 evil?")WikiHow article "SCP-1471: What It Is and Whether It’s Evil"

Updated 2026

The article explains SCP-1471 to a general audience, describing MalO as a fictional app and supernatural companion that sends increasingly intimate images of a creepy creature with a wolf‑skull head and dark humanoid body. It outlines the timeline of image transmission and perceptual manifestation, then explores whether SCP-1471 is evil or misunderstood. Horror expert Sydney Bermudez emphasizes that MalO is non‑aggressive and may be seen as a companion by some users, highlighting the ambiguity of its moral status. This narrative shapes public perception of SCP-1471 as a potentially sympathetic figure rather than a purely malevolent monster. It reinforces the idea that SCP-1471 is fictional while still engaging with its horror aesthetics.
