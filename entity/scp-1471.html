[No speech. Only images — you, sleeping. You, walking. You, reading this. And behind you, in every frame, something getting closer.]

Who It Is

SCP-1471 is a free mobile application called 'MalO ver1.0.0' that appears on app stores for Android and iOS at irregular intervals. The app has no developer information and no description beyond its name. Once downloaded and installed, the app cannot be deleted through normal means. After 24 hours, the user begins receiving images via the app — photographs of locations the user has visited, taken from angles that suggest the photographer was following them. After 48 hours, images begin appearing that show the user themselves in their own home, sleeping, or going about daily activities. After 72 hours, the images begin to include SCP-1471-A, a humanoid entity with a canine-like skull, long dark hair, and elongated limbs. The entity appears increasingly closer to the user in each successive image. Once SCP-1471-A has appeared in the images, users begin seeing it in their peripheral vision — a tall, dark figure standing in corners, doorways, or at the edge of their visual field. When they turn to look directly, it vanishes. The entity never directly harms the user, but the psychological toll of constant, inescapable surveillance is severe. Victims report inability to sleep, severe paranoia, and in some cases, self-harm as an attempt to end the stalking. The Foundation discovered that SCP-1471-A is physically real and exists in a space adjacent to normal perception; it can interact with the physical world in limited ways (moving objects, leaving fingerprints) but cannot be fully perceived without the app. The origin of MalO is unknown. Some researchers speculate it is an art project gone wrong; others believe it is a deliberate manifestation from an unknown entity seeking to anchor itself in baseline reality. SCP-1471 taps into the modern fear of being watched through our own devices — the phone that should connect us instead becomes a window through which something watches.

Form

Attested (canonical SCP article): SCP-1471-A appears as a large humanoid figure with a canid-like skull and black hair. The figure is consistently depicted as tall (roughly 2 meters), bipedal, with elongated limbs and a head resembling a wolf or large dog skull. The skull lacks expressive facial musculature; eye sockets are typically shown as dark or empty. The body is covered in dark, shaggy hair or fur, often matted, and the hands end in claw-like digits. In the original SCP-1471 image, the entity is represented by a person wearing a zombie werewolf costume, which has become iconic in fandom but is a photographic stand‑in rather than an in‑universe anatomical description. Fan reconstructions (non‑canonical) frequently add glowing eyes, more pronounced claws, or a leaner, more stylized silhouette to emphasize its uncanny presence.

Powers

Anomalous mobile application distribution SCP-1471 manifests as a free 9.8 MB mobile application named "MalO ver1.0.0" that appears in online application stores without a listed developer and is able to bypass standard approval processes. It resists removal by program manager applications and creates no icons or shortcuts upon installation, making it difficult for users to locate or uninstall. Canonical SCP-1471 article; mirrored in SCP-3145-2 fragment. The app is confined to mobile devices; containment procedures focus on confiscation and battery removal. The Foundation can target suspected devices with self‑uploading malware to disable them, indicating that the anomaly does not override all technological countermeasures. No major contradictions in canonical sources; derivative fan materials sometimes claim that containment procedures are "not required" or that the app is intentionally left available as a benign companion, which conflicts with the SCP article’s confiscation protocol.

Progressive image transmission and spatial tracking After installation, SCP-1471 begins sending images via text message every 3–6 hours. For the first 24 hours, images depict locations commonly frequented by the individual. After 48 hours, they show locations recently visited. After 72 hours, images depict the individual in real time with SCP-1471-A in close proximity. This implies an ability to track the subject’s movements and generate or capture images from vantage points that may not be physically accessible. Canonical SCP-1471 article; summarized in WikiHow and derivative explanatory sources. The mechanism of image generation is unknown; no anomalous code has been identified in derivative analyses. The effect appears tied to the installed app and ceases if exposure is cut off before 90 hours. Some fan interpretations treat the images as purely hallucinated or memetic constructs, while the SCP article presents them as actual text messages received by the device. The distinction between external data transmission and internal perception remains debated.

Perceptual manifestation in peripheral vision and reflective surfaces Individuals with more than 90 hours of exposure begin to briefly visualize SCP-1471-A in their peripheral vision and in reflective surfaces. Continued exposure leads to irreversible and sustained visualizations, effectively integrating the entity into the subject’s everyday perceptual field. Canonical SCP-1471 article; SCP-3145-2 fragment. The effect is strictly visual; there is no attested tactile, auditory, or olfactory manifestation. The only known treatment is eliminating visual exposure to SCP-1471’s images before the 90‑hour threshold; once sustained visualization occurs, reversal is described as impossible with current Foundation methods. Some derivative sources extend the timeline to weeks or months and suggest that uninstalling the app has no effect even before 90 hours, whereas the canonical article focuses on exposure duration rather than uninstall attempts. The exact reversibility window is therefore interpreted differently in fan materials.

Non‑hostile, incomprehensible visual communication Subjects at advanced exposure stages report periodic attempts by SCP-1471-A to visually communicate—gestures, positioning, or repeated appearances—but they cannot understand any message or intent. The entity does not engage in hostile actions, and no physical harm has been attributed to its presence. Canonical SCP-1471 article; echoed in explanatory secondary sources. Communication is limited to visual cues; there is no attested speech, text, or direct mental contact. The Foundation has not documented successful two‑way communication or any change in behavior in response to containment efforts. Fan portrayals often ascribe clear emotional motives (comforting the lonely, protecting the subject) or conversational abilities via the app interface, which are not supported by the canonical text.

Resistance to conventional uninstallation and removal SCP-1471 cannot be removed from devices via standard program manager applications, and it creates no visible icons or shortcuts. This makes it effectively hidden from casual users and resistant to ordinary uninstallation procedures. Canonical SCP-1471 article; SCP-3145-2 fragment. Foundation‑deployed self‑uploading malware can disable infected devices, suggesting that the anomaly does not fully override all system‑level interventions. Physical removal of the battery and storage unit containment are effective at halting further image transmission. Some derivative narratives claim that attempts to uninstall the app have no effect on the perceptual manifestations even at early stages, whereas the canonical article ties the progression explicitly to continued exposure to images, implying that early removal or isolation could prevent full infection.

Incident Log

The SCP Foundation discovers a mobile application, MalO ver1.0.0, that appears in online stores without a developer and cannot be removed by normal means. Once installed, the app sends images every 3–6 hours featuring a tall humanoid figure with a canine skull and black hair (SCP-1471-A). The images initially depict familiar locations, then recently visited places, and eventually real‑time shots of the subject with SCP-1471-A nearby. After more than 90 hours of exposure, subjects begin to see the entity in their peripheral vision and reflective surfaces, with continued exposure leading to irreversible visual presence. Despite the unsettling nature of the phenomenon, no hostile activity is recorded. The Foundation responds by confiscating infected devices, removing batteries, and monitoring app stores to prevent further distribution. An archival document reproduces the app’s store description, which markets MalO as a solution to loneliness and social anxiety, promising that users will "never settle for those awkward feelings of being alone ever again."

The archival document reproduces the MalO app’s store listing. The description presents MalO as an "exciting and interactive experience" designed to alleviate loneliness and social anxiety. It promises that users will "never settle for those awkward feelings of being alone ever again" and implies that after a few hours of use, social anxiety will fade. The text is written in upbeat marketing language, contrasting sharply with the unsettling reality of an inescapable stalking presence.

Within the broader SCP-3145 narrative, SCP-3145-2 describes an image of a large humanoid figure with a canid skull and black hair that manifests in association with a mobile app identical to MalO ver1.0.0. The fragment reiterates the progressive image transmission and perceptual manifestation pattern, but situates it as one component of a larger anomalous structure. A modified archival document addresses a Foundation doctor directly, critiquing the "stalk people via their phones" concept and proposing more expansive anomalous strategies.

The article explains SCP-1471 to a general audience, describing MalO as a fictional app and supernatural companion that sends increasingly intimate images of a creepy creature with a wolf‑skull head and dark humanoid body. It outlines the timeline of image transmission and perceptual manifestation, then explores whether SCP-1471 is evil or misunderstood. Horror expert Sydney Bermudez emphasizes that MalO is non‑aggressive and may be seen as a companion by some users, highlighting the ambiguity of its moral status.

Content Notes

SCP-1471’s core behavior—sending images that progressively bring SCP-1471-A closer to the subject, then manifesting in peripheral vision—constitutes a form of relentless stalking. The subject is never physically attacked, but is continuously watched and followed, with no clear way to escape once the exposure threshold is reached. The app’s marketing language explicitly targets individuals who feel alone or socially anxious, potentially exploiting their vulnerability.

Within horror studies, SCP-1471 is often cited as an example of digital or techno‑horror that focuses on surveillance and the erosion of privacy rather than physical violence. The exploitation of loneliness echoes broader concerns about apps and platforms that monetize or manipulate users’ emotional states.

SCP-1471 article (Description and Archival Document 1471-01); WikiHow explanatory article.

The MalO app description promises relief from "awkward feelings of being alone" and social anxiety, framing the anomaly as a self‑help tool. In reality, users become subject to an inescapable presence that may exacerbate anxiety. The tension between advertised benefit and hidden cost reflects a dark commentary on predatory technologies that target vulnerable populations.

Media and technology critics have drawn parallels between MalO and real‑world apps that claim to improve mental health or social connectivity while collecting data or fostering dependency. SCP-1471 thus participates in a broader discourse on digital exploitation.

Archival Document 1471-01; SCP-1471 article.

The SCP Foundation’s decision to confiscate devices and aggressively contain SCP-1471, despite the lack of hostile activity, raises ethical questions. Subjects lose access to their devices and any perceived companionship from MalO, and the entity itself is treated as a threat purely because it is anomalous.

In meta‑analyses of SCP narratives, SCP-1471 is used to discuss the ethics of containment and the potential for institutional overreach. The case illustrates how fear of the anomalous can lead to restrictive measures even when harm is unproven.

A way to hold it in your mind

You install a free app that promises you’ll never feel alone again. At first, it keeps its word in the most unsettling way possible—every few hours, your phone lights up with a photo of somewhere you’ve just been, and in the background a tall figure with a canine skull and black hair watches from the edge of the frame. After a few days, the photos are of you, taken from impossible angles. After ninety hours, you no longer need the phone; the skull‑faced companion is simply there, hovering at the edge of your vision, never speaking, never attacking, never leaving. SCP-1471 lives in that tension between comfort and dread, a digital ghost that turns the promise of constant connection into a haunting you can’t turn off.
