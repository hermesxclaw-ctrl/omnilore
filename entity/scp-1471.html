Archive Dossier — Identity

SCP-1471 (MalO ver1.0.0)

Pronunciation SCP-1471: /ˌɛs.siːˈpiː ˈwʌn ˈfɔːr ˈsɛv.ən ˈwʌn/; MalO: commonly /ˈmæl.oʊ/ or /ˈmeɪ.loʊ/.

Known As

MalOMalO ver1.0.0SCP-1471-A (for the associated entity)SCP-3145-2 (closely related fragment designation)

Etymology

"MalO" is generally interpreted as a stylized form of "mal" (Latin/Spanish for "bad" or "evil") combined with a playful suffix "-O"; within the SCP narrative, the name appears as part of the app’s branding text and is not explicitly explained. The numeric designation "1471" follows the SCP Foundation’s internal cataloging system and has no attested in‑universe etymology.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-1471 belongs to the collaborative, internet‑native horror and speculative fiction project known as the SCP Foundation Wiki. The work is published under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 3.0 (CC BY-SA 3.0), with primary authorship credited to the SCP Wiki user "LurkD" for the original SCP-1471 article and to other contributors for derivative fragments such as SCP-3145-2. The character and concept circulate widely in online horror fandoms, meme culture, and derivative fanworks, but there is no single corporate or national cultural owner.

Disputed Identifications

Within the SCP corpus, SCP-1471 is consistently identified as an anomalous mobile application (MalO ver1.0.0) plus its associated entity SCP-1471-A. A closely parallel fragment, SCP-3145-2, describes an almost identical phenomenon under a different number, leading to debate over whether SCP-1471 and SCP-3145-2 are separate anomalies, a retcon, or an in‑universe cross‑reference. In fan discourse, "SCP-1471" is often used to refer solely to the humanoid canid‑skull figure, blurring the distinction between the app (SCP-1471) and the entity (SCP-1471-A).

Archive Dossier — Approach

You install a free app that promises you’ll never feel alone again. At first, it keeps its word in the most unsettling way possible—every few hours, your phone lights up with a photo of somewhere you’ve just been, and in the background a tall figure with a canine skull and black hair watches from the edge of the frame. After a few days, the photos are of you, taken from impossible angles. After ninety hours, you no longer need the phone; the skull‑faced companion is simply there, hovering at the edge of your vision, never speaking, never attacking, never leaving. SCP-1471 lives in that tension between comfort and dread, a digital ghost that turns the promise of constant connection into a haunting you can’t turn off.

[No speech. Only images — you, sleeping. You, walking. You, reading this. And behind you, in every frame, something getting closer.]

Who It Is

SCP-1471 is a free mobile application called 'MalO ver1.0.0' that appears on app stores for Android and iOS at irregular intervals. The app has no developer information and no description beyond its name. Once downloaded and installed, the app cannot be deleted through normal means. After 24 hours, the user begins receiving images via the app — photographs of locations the user has visited, taken from angles that suggest the photographer was following them. After 48 hours, images begin appearing that show the user themselves in their own home, sleeping, or going about daily activities. After 72 hours, the images begin to include SCP-1471-A, a humanoid entity with a canine-like skull, long dark hair, and elongated limbs. The entity appears increasingly closer to the user in each successive image. Once SCP-1471-A has appeared in the images, users begin seeing it in their peripheral vision — a tall, dark figure standing in corners, doorways, or at the edge of their visual field. When they turn to look directly, it vanishes. The entity never directly harms the user, but the psychological toll of constant, inescapable surveillance is severe. Victims report inability to sleep, severe paranoia, and in some cases, self-harm as an attempt to end the stalking. The Foundation discovered that SCP-1471-A is physically real and exists in a space adjacent to normal perception; it can interact with the physical world in limited ways (moving objects, leaving fingerprints) but cannot be fully perceived without the app. The origin of MalO is unknown. Some researchers speculate it is an art project gone wrong; others believe it is a deliberate manifestation from an unknown entity seeking to anchor itself in baseline reality. SCP-1471 taps into the modern fear of being watched through our own devices — the phone that should connect us instead becomes a window through which something watches.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Attested (canonical SCP article): SCP-1471-A appears as a large humanoid figure with a canid-like skull and black hair. The figure is consistently depicted as tall (roughly 2 meters), bipedal, with elongated limbs and a head resembling a wolf or large dog skull. The skull lacks expressive facial musculature; eye sockets are typically shown as dark or empty. The body is covered in dark, shaggy hair or fur, often matted, and the hands end in claw-like digits. In the original SCP-1471 image, the entity is represented by a person wearing a zombie werewolf costume, which has become iconic in fandom but is a photographic stand‑in rather than an in‑universe anatomical description. Fan reconstructions (non‑canonical) frequently add glowing eyes, more pronounced claws, or a leaner, more stylized silhouette to emphasize its uncanny presence.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: The primary iconography is the recurring visual motif of a tall, canine‑skull humanoid appearing in photographs and later in peripheral vision or reflective surfaces. The MalO app itself is described in some derivative sources as using a dark, minimalist interface with an icon featuring the silhouette of a creature with a canine skull, but this is extrapolated from fan designs rather than the original SCP text. Symbolically, SCP-1471-A functions as an embodiment of constant presence and surveillance—its image is always somewhere in the frame, gradually approaching the subject. In reception, the entity has become a recognizable emblem of the SCP Foundation’s "friendly but unsettling" archetype, often used in fan art to represent loneliness, social anxiety, or the desire for non‑judgmental companionship.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested (SCP article): Individuals exposed to SCP-1471’s images report periodic attempts by SCP-1471-A to visually communicate with them, but they fail to understand or comprehend these actions. No hostile activity has been reported. The entity appears to follow subjects, remaining in close proximity once the exposure threshold is reached, but does not attack or physically interact. Reconstructed (from fan and derivative sources): SCP-1471-A is often interpreted as curious, observant, and non‑aggressive—sometimes waving, mimicking human poses, or appearing to "hang out" near the subject. Many fans characterize MalO as a misunderstood companion whose presence alleviates loneliness rather than causing harm. This interpretation is not explicitly stated in the canonical article but is consistent with the lack of recorded aggression.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-1471’s domain is technologically mediated visual space. Initially, the entity appears only in images sent via text messages from the MalO app, depicting locations frequented or recently visited by the subject. After 72 hours, images show the subject in real time with SCP-1471-A nearby. After more than 90 hours of exposure, the entity begins to manifest in the subject’s peripheral vision and reflective surfaces, effectively inhabiting the subject’s everyday environment as a persistent visual presence. There is no canonical physical lair or habitat; SCP-1471-A is bound to the perceptual and photographic field associated with the infected device and its user.

Version Differences

Canonical SCP-1471 vs SCP-3145-2: Both describe a 9.8 MB mobile app named "MalO ver1.0.0" that bypasses app store approval, cannot be removed, and sends images every 3–6 hours featuring a large humanoid with a canid skull and black hair. SCP-3145-2 is framed as a fragment associated with SCP-3145, suggesting an alternate or extended narrative context. The core phenomenology—progressive image proximity, peripheral vision manifestations, lack of hostile activity—is essentially identical. Fan and derivative versions: Some derivative sites and fan texts expand the timeline (adding more phases), attribute explicit friendliness or emotional support behaviors to MalO, or introduce speculative origin theories (memetic agent, interdimensional entity). These additions are not present in the original SCP article and should be treated as adaptation‑specific interpretations rather than canonical facts.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Anomalous mobile application distribution

Source Version Canonical SCP-1471 article; mirrored in SCP-3145-2 fragment.

Description

SCP-1471 manifests as a free 9.8 MB mobile application named "MalO ver1.0.0" that appears in online application stores without a listed developer and is able to bypass standard approval processes. It resists removal by program manager applications and creates no icons or shortcuts upon installation, making it difficult for users to locate or uninstall.

Limitations

The app is confined to mobile devices; containment procedures focus on confiscation and battery removal. The Foundation can target suspected devices with self‑uploading malware to disable them, indicating that the anomaly does not override all technological countermeasures.

Contradictions

No major contradictions in canonical sources; derivative fan materials sometimes claim that containment procedures are "not required" or that the app is intentionally left available as a benign companion, which conflicts with the SCP article’s confiscation protocol.

Progressive image transmission and spatial tracking

Source Version Canonical SCP-1471 article; summarized in WikiHow and derivative explanatory sources.

Description

After installation, SCP-1471 begins sending images via text message every 3–6 hours. For the first 24 hours, images depict locations commonly frequented by the individual. After 48 hours, they show locations recently visited. After 72 hours, images depict the individual in real time with SCP-1471-A in close proximity. This implies an ability to track the subject’s movements and generate or capture images from vantage points that may not be physically accessible.

Limitations

The mechanism of image generation is unknown; no anomalous code has been identified in derivative analyses. The effect appears tied to the installed app and ceases if exposure is cut off before 90 hours.

Contradictions

Some fan interpretations treat the images as purely hallucinated or memetic constructs, while the SCP article presents them as actual text messages received by the device. The distinction between external data transmission and internal perception remains debated.

Perceptual manifestation in peripheral vision and reflective surfaces

Source Version Canonical SCP-1471 article; SCP-3145-2 fragment.

Description

Individuals with more than 90 hours of exposure begin to briefly visualize SCP-1471-A in their peripheral vision and in reflective surfaces. Continued exposure leads to irreversible and sustained visualizations, effectively integrating the entity into the subject’s everyday perceptual field.

Limitations

The effect is strictly visual; there is no attested tactile, auditory, or olfactory manifestation. The only known treatment is eliminating visual exposure to SCP-1471’s images before the 90‑hour threshold; once sustained visualization occurs, reversal is described as impossible with current Foundation methods.

Contradictions

Some derivative sources extend the timeline to weeks or months and suggest that uninstalling the app has no effect even before 90 hours, whereas the canonical article focuses on exposure duration rather than uninstall attempts. The exact reversibility window is therefore interpreted differently in fan materials.

Non‑hostile, incomprehensible visual communication

Source Version Canonical SCP-1471 article; echoed in explanatory secondary sources.

Description

Subjects at advanced exposure stages report periodic attempts by SCP-1471-A to visually communicate—gestures, positioning, or repeated appearances—but they cannot understand any message or intent. The entity does not engage in hostile actions, and no physical harm has been attributed to its presence.

Limitations

Communication is limited to visual cues; there is no attested speech, text, or direct mental contact. The Foundation has not documented successful two‑way communication or any change in behavior in response to containment efforts.

Contradictions

Fan portrayals often ascribe clear emotional motives (comforting the lonely, protecting the subject) or conversational abilities via the app interface, which are not supported by the canonical text.

Resistance to conventional uninstallation and removal

Source Version Canonical SCP-1471 article; SCP-3145-2 fragment.

Description

SCP-1471 cannot be removed from devices via standard program manager applications, and it creates no visible icons or shortcuts. This makes it effectively hidden from casual users and resistant to ordinary uninstallation procedures.

Limitations

Foundation‑deployed self‑uploading malware can disable infected devices, suggesting that the anomaly does not fully override all system‑level interventions. Physical removal of the battery and storage unit containment are effective at halting further image transmission.

Contradictions

Some derivative narratives claim that attempts to uninstall the app have no effect on the perceptual manifestations even at early stages, whereas the canonical article ties the progression explicitly to continued exposure to images, implying that early removal or isolation could prevent full infection.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

SCP-1471-A (the canid‑skull humanoid)

Type creatorSource SCP-1471 article (Description section distinguishing SCP-1471 and SCP-1471-A).

Description

In canonical classification, SCP-1471 is the anomalous mobile application, while SCP-1471-A is the entity that appears in images and later in peripheral vision. The app functions as the carrier or gateway through which SCP-1471-A becomes present in the subject’s life.

Version Note

Foundation documentation treats SCP-1471 and SCP-1471-A as distinct but linked designations; fan discourse often collapses them into a single identity.

Conflicting Accounts

Many fans refer to the entity itself as "SCP-1471" or "MalO," ignoring the app/entity distinction. Some derivative sites treat the app as a front for an interdimensional being rather than a separate anomaly.

Infected mobile device users

Type victim|companionSource SCP-1471 article; WikiHow explanatory article; derivative fan discussions.

Description

Users who install MalO become subjects of SCP-1471’s anomalous effects. They receive images featuring SCP-1471-A, eventually perceive the entity in their environment, and cannot easily remove the app. While the Foundation frames them as victims of an anomalous stalking phenomenon, some narratives and fan interpretations portray them as recipients of companionship from a non‑hostile entity.

Version Note

Canonical text emphasizes containment and the psychological impact of persistent visual presence; fan works often highlight emotional bonds and mutual acceptance.

Conflicting Accounts

Foundation documentation does not describe users forming friendships with SCP-1471-A, whereas fan materials and derivative sites report that many users come to regard MalO as a "friend" or comforting presence.

SCP Foundation

Type hostile|owner|wielderSource SCP-1471 Special Containment Procedures; SCP-3145-2 fragment.

Description

The SCP Foundation acts as the institutional antagonist to SCP-1471, confiscating infected devices, removing batteries, and monitoring app stores to prevent distribution. It effectively "owns" the anomaly in the sense of cataloging and containing it, though it does not wield SCP-1471 as a tool.

Version Note

In‑universe, the Foundation is neutral but pragmatic, treating SCP-1471 as a Euclid‑class anomaly. In fan reception, the Foundation’s containment of a non‑hostile entity is sometimes criticized as overreach.

Conflicting Accounts

Some derivative narratives imagine Foundation personnel befriending SCP-1471-A or using MalO for research into loneliness and memetics, which is not attested in canonical documents.

App stores and mobile ecosystems

Type exploitative|infrastructuralSource SCP-1471 article; SCP-3145-2 fragment.

Description

SCP-1471 exploits online application stores by bypassing approval processes and appearing as a free download without a developer. It uses the existing infrastructure of mobile ecosystems to propagate itself globally.

Version Note

Canonical text treats app stores as passive vectors; derivative analyses sometimes speculate about compromised store security or deliberate insertion by unknown actors.

Conflicting Accounts

No canonical evidence identifies a specific company or platform as complicit; any attribution of blame to real‑world corporations is speculative and non‑canonical.

SCP-3145

Type conceptual siblingSource SCP-3145-2 fragment page.

Description

SCP-3145-2 presents SCP-1471 as a fragment of SCP-3145, implying a conceptual or narrative relationship. SCP-3145 appears to incorporate MalO’s stalking‑via‑phone behavior into a broader anomalous framework.

Version Note

This relationship exists only within the SCP-3145 narrative; the original SCP-1471 article does not reference SCP-3145.

Conflicting Accounts

Some readers treat SCP-3145-2 as evidence that SCP-1471 was retroactively folded into SCP-3145; others see it as a playful cross‑reference rather than a canonical merger.
