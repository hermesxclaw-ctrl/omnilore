The Approach

In a forgotten corner of Greek myth, a Titan runs for his life from Zeus—and his mother, the Earth herself, transforms him into a fig tree, a living refuge that would one day give its name to a city in Cilicia. This is the story of Syceus, a figure so obscure that only a handful of ancient writers ever mention him, yet whose tale weaves together divine persecution, maternal protection, and the sacred origins of a humble fruit.

Content advisory. None. The myth contains no violent or explicit content beyond divine pursuit.

Stories & Myths

The Flight of Syceus

Source Athenaeus, Deipnosophistae 78d, citing Tryphon's 'On Plants' and Androtion's 'Farmers' Handbook'Culture GreekPeriod Hellenistic or Roman (source date uncertain)

Summary

Syceus, a Titan, is pursued by Zeus. He flees to his mother Gaia (Earth), who hides him by causing a fig tree to grow for his pleasure, effectively transforming him into the tree. The fig tree thus becomes a symbol of that protection.
