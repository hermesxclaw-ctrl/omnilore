Who It Is

Imagine you're caught in a mountain storm, the rain so thick it feels like a curtain between you and the world. You see a woman standing by the path, perfectly still, her face hidden under a deep straw hat. She doesn't move. She doesn't speak. But you know, with a certainty that chills you more than the rain, that she is the reason the sky will not clear. This is Ameonna — not a demon, not a goddess, but the very spirit of a rain that has forgotten how to stop.

Ameonna is a passive, melancholic figure rather than an actively malicious one. She is said to appear only during rainstorms, wandering along roads, mountain paths, or the edges of rice paddies. She does not typically chase or attack humans. Instead, her presence is seen as an omen — her appearance portends that the rain will continue for a long time, potentially causing floods, crop damage, or preventing travelers from reaching their destinations. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Ameonna (雨女) literally translates to 'Rain Woman' — 'ame' (雨) meaning 'rain' and 'onna' (女) meaning 'woman.' The name directly describes her function and identity as a being intrinsically tied to precipitation.

Pronunciation — AH-meh-OHN-nah

Aliases — 雨女, Ame-onna, Rain Woman, Ame Onna

Powers
Rain Attraction/Intensification

Ameonna's presence is intrinsically linked to rain. She does not 'control' the weather in a dramatic sense, but her appearance is both a sign of and a catalyst for prolonged, heavy rainfall. She embodies the storm rather than commanding it.

Limit: She cannot create rain from clear skies; she is a creature of the storm, not its master. Her power is passive and tied to her own melancholic existence.

Omen of Prolonged Weather

Sighting Ameonna is considered a bad omen, specifically portending that the current rainy weather will not break. For farmers, this could mean ruined harvests; for travelers, it meant being stranded or facing dangerous conditions.

Limit: This is a passive, prophetic quality rather than an active curse. She does not 'cause' the bad luck; she merely signals it.

Psychic Melancholy Aura

Those who encounter Ameonna often report feeling an overwhelming sense of sadness, loneliness, and despair. This is not an active attack but an emanation of her own tragic state.

Limit: The effect fades once she is out of sight. It is not known to cause lasting harm.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ameonna is typically described as a woman appearing during heavy rain. She is often depicted wearing a traditional straw rain cloak (mino) and a conical sedge hat (kasa), which partially obscures her face. Her kimono is usually depicted as wet and disheveled, and her long black hair is plastered to her face and body by the rain. Her expression is typically melancholic, sorrowful, or eerily neutral. Symbols — Rain, the straw rain cloak (mino), the sedge hat (kasa), wet disheveled hair, the color of a grey storm sky, and the sound of heavy rain. Her iconography is heavily tied to the visual of a solitary, obscured woman in a downpour.

Domains — Ameonna's domain is the rainstorm itself. She is associated with heavy, prolonged rainfall, mountain passes, rural roads, and the boundary between the cultivated village and the wild, untamed mountains. She embodies the melancholic, isolating, and potentially destructive aspects of rain — as opposed to its life-giving qualities. She is a creature of the liminal space created by a storm, where the familiar world is obscured by water and mist.

Accounts I
The Woman on the Mountain PathRegional oral tradition (unspecified prefecture)

Undocumented (pre-modern)

A traveler caught in a sudden mountain storm sees a woman standing motionless by the side of the path, her face hidden by a deep sedge hat. She does not move or speak as he passes. He feels a chill that has nothing to do with the cold rain. When he looks back, she is gone, but the rain continues to fall for three more days, delaying his journey and flooding the lowlands. The traveler's journey is delayed, and the village he eventually reaches is suffering from flooding. The encounter is recounted as a warning about the dangers of the mountains during the rainy season.

Accounts II
The Abandoned WifeModern yōkai compilations (e.g., Mizuki Shigeru's works)

20th century CE

A folkloric explanation for Ameonna's existence: she is the spirit of a woman who was abandoned by her husband during a terrible storm, or who died of grief or exposure after being cast out. Her sorrow is so great that it manifests as an eternal rainstorm around her. She wanders, forever searching for the home she lost. This origin story frames Ameonna as a tragic figure rather than a monster, eliciting pity from those who hear it.

The Rain LickerRegional folklore (specific to certain mountainous areas)

Undocumented

In some specific, less common traditions, Ameonna is said to have an unnaturally long tongue which she uses to lick raindrops from the air, from leaves, or from the eaves of houses. This behavior is seen as deeply unsettling and is one of the few actively 'monstrous' traits attributed to her. This variant emphasizes her otherness and makes her more overtly frightening, distinguishing her from the more passive, sorrowful depictions.

Ameonna is a malevolent demon that attacks humans. — She is almost universally depicted as passive and sorrowful. She does not attack, chase, or curse humans. Her threat is the omen of bad weather, not direct violence.
