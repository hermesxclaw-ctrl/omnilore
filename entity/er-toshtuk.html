Who It Is

Er Töshtük is the legendary hero of the Uyghur and Kyrgyz epic cycle, one of the most important figures in Turkic epic literature. The epic of Er Töshtük tells the story of a mighty warrior who undertakes a perilous journey to the underworld to rescue his six brothers, who were kidnapped by demons. Er Töshtük is born supernaturally — his mother conceives after eating the heart of a slain deer — and he emerges from the womb fully armed. He possesses superhuman strength, an iron body, and a magical horse named Chalkuyruk (Grey Tail) who has wings and can fly. The epic takes him through fantastic landscapes: a forest with leaves of silk, a sea of blood, a mountain of bones, and the underworld kingdom of Erlik (the Lord of the Dead). In the underworld, Er Töshtük defeats the seven-headed demon and rescues his brothers, but he must also fight his way past various guardians and temptations. The epic incorporates elements of shamanic cosmology — the hero's descent to the underworld mirrors the shaman's journey to retrieve lost souls. Er Töshtük (also known as 'Er Sogotok' or 'Töshtük') is recited by bards (akhun or manaschi) in a highly stylized chant, often in sessions that last for hours or even days. The epic is part of the same tradition as the Kyrgyz Manas epic, the Kazakh Alpamysh, and the Altai epic cycles. It represents the pre-Islamic Turkic worldview with its shamanic elements, heroic ideals, and mythological geography.

Attested in Uyghur and Kyrgyz oral epic poetry (the Manas and Er Töshtük cycles)Era Pre-Islamic oral tradition; recorded in the 19th-20th centuries by ethnographers
