Who It Is

Gawain is a complex and fascinating figure, and his stories continue to captivate audiences to this day. His bravery, loyalty, and chivalry make him a compelling character, and his adventures are a testament to the power of the human spirit.

Gawain is often portrayed as a loyal and honorable knight, who is willing to risk his life to defend his lord and his people. However, he is also shown to be impulsive and prone to making rash decisions.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Gawain is derived from the Old Welsh word 'gwalch', meaning 'hawk' or 'eagle', and the Breton word 'mei', meaning 'lord' or 'noble'.

Pronunciation — /ɡɑːˈweɪn/

Aliases — Gwalchmei, Gawain the Courteous, Green Knight's Challenger

Ownership — Gawain is a figure from the Arthurian legendary cycle, which is a collection of stories and legends from medieval Wales, England, and France.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Gawain is typically depicted as a handsome and chivalrous knight, often wearing a green coat or tunic. He is described as being tall and strong, with a noble bearing. In some versions of the story, he is also depicted as being somewhat arrogant or prideful.

Symbols — The green coat or tunic is a symbol of Gawain's connection to the natural world and his role as a champion of chivalry. The hawk or eagle is also a symbol of his name and his association with the nobility.

Domains — Gawain's stories often take place in the courts and castles of medieval Europe, with a focus on the nobility and the chivalric code.
