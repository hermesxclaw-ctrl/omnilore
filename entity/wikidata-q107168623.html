The Approach

Ines Fujin was a dark-brown blur with a single white star, a colt who won Japan's most coveted juvenile prize, then took the Derby in front of nearly 200,000 spectators—only to vanish from the track months later, leaving behind a legacy that outlived him by decades.

Identity

Ines Fujin

Pronunciation ee-NESS foo-JIN (Japanese: ah-ee-ne-su fuu-jin)

Known As

アイネスフウジンAinesu FuujinInes Fūjin

Etymology

The name 'Ines' is likely a feminine given name (possibly Spanish/Portuguese), while 'Fujin' is Japanese for 'wind god' (風神). The combination may reflect a hope for swiftness or divine wind.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese Thoroughbred racing; bred and raced in Japan. The horse is owned by the Japanese racing community and later the Umamusume franchise.

Disputed Identifications

No disputed identifications found. The horse is distinct from other horses with similar names.

Description

Appearance

Dark brown coat with a small irregular white star on the forehead. Often raced wearing a light-colored blinker hood.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography associated with the real horse. In Umamusume, the character is depicted with a star motif and wind-themed imagery.

Behaviour & Personality

As a racehorse, known for consistent performances and a strong finish. In Umamusume, depicted as hard-working, responsible, and devoted to family.

Domains & Habitats

Raced on turf courses in Japan, primarily at Tokyo Racecourse, Hanshin, and Nakayama. Trained at Ritto Training Center.

Version Differences

The real horse is a male Thoroughbred; the Umamusume character is an anthropomorphized female with a distinct personality and storyline.

Powers & Abilities

Racing ability

Source Version Real-world racing record

Description

Ines Fujin was a top-tier racehorse, winning 4 of 8 races, including two Grade 1 races: the Asahi Hai Futurity Stakes (1989) and the Tokyo Yushun (Japan Derby, 1990). Also placed second in the Satsuki Sho (1990).

Limitations

Career cut short by tendonitis after the Derby; did not race beyond three-year-old season.

Stories & Myths

1989 Two-Year-Old Season

Source Racing recordsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1989

Summary

Ines Fujin began his career with two second-place finishes in newcomer races over 1600 meters, then won a maiden race. He capped the season with a victory in the Grade 1 Asahi Hai Futurity Stakes, earning the title of Best Two-Year-Old Colt.

1990 Three-Year-Old Season and Japan Derby

Source Racing recordsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1990

Summary

Ines Fujin won the Kyodo Tsushin Hai (GIII) over 1800 meters, then finished fourth in the Yayoi Sho (GII). In the Satsuki Sho (G1) he came second, and in the Japan Derby (G1) he triumphed over 22 runners in front of 196,517 spectators—a world record attendance for a horse race. He retired shortly after due to tendonitis.

Participants

Consequences

Won JRA Award for Best Three-Year-Old Colt; set world record attendance; retired due to injury.

Variants

Attendance record sometimes cited as 196,517; some sources note it has not been officially updated.
