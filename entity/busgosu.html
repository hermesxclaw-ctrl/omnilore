Who It Is

Deep in the mountain forests of Asturias, where mist clings to the beech trees and the wind carries the sound of unseen hooves, there walks a being who is both protector and trickster. The Busgosu, a shaggy, horned creature of the wild, is said to guard the shepherds and their flocks, yet he can lead the unwary astray with his mischievous ways. To the villagers who tell his tales, he is as real as the rocks and rivers, a living embodiment of the untamed forest that surrounds them.

The Busgosu is a protector of shepherds and their flocks, but he is also mischievous and can lead travelers astray. He is known to help those who are lost if they treat the forest with respect, but he punishes those who harm the natural world. He is solitary, rarely seen by humans, and prefers the company of animals.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Busgosu' is believed to derive from the Asturian or Cantabrian language, possibly related to 'bosque' (forest) or 'busgo' (a term for a wild or rustic person). The exact etymology is uncertain and debated among folklorists.

Pronunciation — boos-GOH-soo

Aliases — Busgosu, Busgoso, El Busgosu

Ownership — Asturias and Cantabria, northern Spain

Powers
Guardianship over shepherds and flocks

The Busgosu protects shepherds and their sheep from wolves and other dangers, ensuring their safety in the mountains.

Limit: His protection is conditional on the shepherds' respect for nature and their adherence to traditional practices.

Misleading travelers

The Busgosu can confuse travelers, making them lose their way in the forest, especially if they are arrogant or disrespectful.

Limit: He may guide them back if they apologize or show humility.

Control over forest animals

He can command the animals of the forest, using them as messengers or allies.

Limit: No specific limitations recorded.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Busgosu is typically described as a creature with a humanoid form but covered in thick fur or moss, with horns on his head and goat-like legs, similar to a satyr or faun. He is often depicted as tall and imposing, with piercing eyes that glow in the dark. Some accounts say he carries a staff or a shepherd's crook.

Symbols — The Busgosu is associated with the forest, particularly beech and oak trees. He is sometimes depicted with a wreath of leaves or flowers, and his horns symbolize his connection to the wild. In art, he is often shown in a pastoral setting with sheep or other forest animals.

Domains — The Busgosu dwells in the deep forests of the Cantabrian Mountains, particularly in Asturias and Cantabria. He is said to inhabit caves and grottoes, and to roam the high pastures during the summer months.

The Lost ShepherdOral tradition

Unknown

A shepherd gets lost in a storm and encounters the Busgosu, who guides him to shelter and protects his flock from wolves. The shepherd thanks him by leaving an offering of bread and cheese. The shepherd learns to respect the forest, and the Busgosu continues to protect his flock.

The Arrogant HunterOral tradition

Unknown

A hunter boasts that he does not fear the Busgosu and enters the forest with his dogs. The Busgosu leads him in circles until nightfall, and the hunter emerges exhausted and humbled. The hunter never boasts again and warns others to respect the forest.
