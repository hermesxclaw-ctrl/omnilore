Who It Is

You're driving on Route 62 outside Point Pleasant at midnight. Your headlights catch something standing in the road — a figure seven feet tall with wings folded behind it like a cape. It turns. Its eyes are two red circles, burning straight through the windshield into your brain. It doesn't attack. It just stares. And somehow that's so much worse. Two weeks later, the Silver Bridge collapses and 46 people die. You know, with a certainty that curdles your blood, that the thing was a warning. Not a threat. A warning.

Mothman is a cryptid first sighted in Point Pleasant, West Virginia, between November 1966 and December 1967. Described as a winged humanoid roughly seven feet tall with a wingspan of ten feet, no visible head, and two enormous glowing red eyes set directly into its chest, Mothman became the center of a year-long wave of sightings that culminated in the collapse of the Silver Bridge on December 15, 1967, which killed 46 people. After the disaster, the sightings stopped. The connection between the two events has made Mothman one of the most enduring and unsettling cryptid legends in American folklore.

Mothman is unique among cryptids in that it doesn't fit the 'undiscovered animal' paradigm. Witnesses consistently describe something that seems more supernatural than biological — a being that can fly at impossible speeds (over 100 mph without flapping its wings), that causes electronics to malfunction, and that appears to herald disaster. John Keel's 1975 book 'The Mothman Prophecies' (later a film starring Richard Gere) tied the sightings to UFOs, Men in Black, and a broader pattern of high strangeness. Whether Mothman is an alien, an interdimensional being, a supernatural omen, or simply a misidentified sandhill crane, it has become a permanent fixture in the American cryptid canon.

First Reports

The first reported sighting occurred on November 12, 1966, when five men digging a grave in Clendenin, West Virginia, saw a 'brown human being' with wings fly over their heads. On November 15, two young couples — Roger and Linda Scarberry and Steve and Mary Mallette — were driving near an abandoned TNT factory when a large winged figure with glowing red eyes chased their car at speeds exceeding 100 mph. Their terrified report to police made national news. Over the next 13 months, dozens of sightings were reported by credible witnesses — police officers, firefighters, military personnel — all describing the same entity. The sightings abruptly stopped after the Silver Bridge collapse.

Form

A tall (6-7 feet) humanoid or man-shaped figure covered in grey or dark brown skin, with enormous bat-like or moth-like wings spanning approximately 10 feet. It has no discernible head or neck — its massive, glowing red eyes are set directly into its upper chest or shoulders. These eyes are hypnotic and terrifying, described as 'bicycle reflectors' or 'burning coals.' It can fold its wings behind it and stand upright, or run on powerful legs before launching into flight. When it flies, it ascends straight up 'like a helicopter' and can keep pace with speeding cars without flapping.

Powers

Flight at speeds exceeding 100 mph with no visible wing movement. The ability to cause electronic interference — radios, headlights, and engines malfunction in its presence. It appears to sense or predict disasters, showing up before tragic events. Some witnesses report telepathic communication or a sense of being 'warned' rather than threatened. Its glowing red eyes seem to have a paralytic, hypnotic effect. It is apparently bulletproof or non-corporeal; shots fired at it have no effect. It can vanish, jump impossible distances, and move between locations with implausible speed.

Behavior & Habitat

Mothman does not attack. Across hundreds of reported encounters, there is not a single verified case of Mothman physically harming anyone. It chases cars, peers through windows at terrified families, and stands in roads blocking traffic — but it never strikes. This passivity is what makes it so unnerving. It is an observer, a harbinger, a messenger that does not speak. Its appearances precede disaster, but it does not cause them. It is the owl in the daylight, the animal behaving strangely before the earthquake — except it looks back at you with intelligent, burning red eyes that seem to know exactly what is coming.

Weaknesses & Wards

Bright lights may startle or repel it — many sightings end when witnesses shine headlights or flashlights directly at it. It appears to be non-aggressive by nature; it can be escaped by fleeing at high speed. It may be bound to a specific window of time and place, since the mass sightings stopped entirely after the Silver Bridge collapse. No known conventional weapon has any proven effect. Some theories suggest it is vulnerable to disbelief — a creature that exists only in the liminal space between reality and myth.

Field Notes

◉ The name 'Mothman' was coined by a newspaper copy editor in 1966 — it was never named by witnesses. The press just needed a catchy headline.

◉ Mothman was reportedly sighted in Chernobyl before the 1986 nuclear disaster and in New York before 9/11, according to some paranormal investigators.

◉ The original witnesses — the Scarberrys and Mallettes — were so terrified they convinced a police officer to drive them back to the TNT Area that same night. The officer saw nothing.

◉ A sandhill crane was proposed as the mundane explanation — their red facial markings could appear as glowing eyes when reflecting headlights.

◉ Mothman's local nickname in Point Pleasant is 'Indrid Cold,' after a mysterious grinning man who allegedly appeared during the same wave of events.

Cultural Significance

Mothman represents a distinctively American kind of folklore — born from mass media (newspapers and radio spread the sightings nationally), rooted in a specific time and place, and tied to a real, traumatic historical event. The Silver Bridge collapse gave the legend weight and permanence, transforming a regional oddity into a national myth. Mothman is the patron cryptid of West Virginia and a symbol of the state's identity. The annual Mothman Festival brings tourism and cultural pride to Point Pleasant, turning a tragedy into a celebration of the weird.

Modern Interpretations

The 2002 film 'The Mothman Prophecies' starring Richard Gere dramatized John Keel's book and cemented Mothman in mainstream pop culture. Mothman has appeared in video games ('Fallout 76,' which is set in West Virginia), animated series ('Gravity Falls'), and countless podcasts and YouTube documentaries. The 'indrid cold' theory — that Mothman is an alien emissary — persists in UFO circles. The Mothman statue and festival have become iconic, and Mothman's red eyes are instantly recognizable in cryptozoology merchandise. Some recent interpretations frame Mothman as a 'comfort cryptid' — scary but ultimately benign, a protector warning of danger.
