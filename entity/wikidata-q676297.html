Form

In Neo-Hittite reliefs (e.g., İvriz), Arma is depicted as a large, bearded male figure wearing a horned crown (the standard divine headdress in Anatolian art), a tasseled kilt, and sometimes a crescent-moon emblem on his crown or held in his hand. He often holds a curved staff or a lituus (augural staff). In earlier Hittite art, he is rarely depicted anthropomorphically; the crescent moon itself served as his symbol.

Powers

Lunar illumination and night protection As a moon-god, Arma provides light in darkness, guiding travelers and protecting those who move at night. In prayers, he is invoked to 'shine upon' the supplicant. Hittite prayers and rituals Naturally, his power wanes during the new moon; no text explicitly discusses this, but the lunar cycle was certainly observed in ritual contexts. None documented.

Oath enforcement Arma is called upon in treaties and oaths to witness agreements and to punish those who break them. This is a common role for sun and moon gods in ancient Near Eastern cultures (cf. Šamaš, Sin). Hittite treaties (e.g., Muršili II–Targašnalli) His punishment is typically implied (sickness, misfortune) rather than described in detail. None.

Healing In Luwian and Hittite ritual texts, Arma is invoked to cure illnesses, particularly those thought to be caused by divine anger or sorcery. He is associated with 'purification' rituals. Luwian rituals (e.g., the 'Arma' rituals of the Puriyanni tradition) His healing is often mediated by ritual practitioners (the 'old women' or 'physicians' of the texts). None.

Agricultural fertility In Neo-Hittite iconography (İvriz), Arma holds grapes and wheat, symbolizing his role in ensuring bountiful harvests. This is a later, more localized development of his cult. Neo-Hittite reliefs and inscriptions This role is not explicitly attested in the earlier Hittite texts. Some scholars argue the İvriz figure is the Storm-god Tarhunza, not Arma, based on the attributes of lightning and grain. The inscription, however, names Arma.

Accounts

Arma is invoked as one of a thousand gods who witness the treaty, ensuring that both parties keep their word. The treaty lists him among the 'lords of the oath' — a standard formula that places the moon-god among the highest divine authorities.

King Warpalawas of Tuwana stands in prayer before a colossal figure of Arma (if the identification is correct). The god, towering over the king, holds grapes and wheat, blessing the land with fertility. The accompanying inscription records Warpalawas's piety and his dedication of the monument.

In a famous Hittite prayer, the speaker (possibly king Kantuzzili) appeals to the Sun-god, but the prayer's structure is paralleled in hymns to other deities, including Arma. The supplicant pleads for justice, healing, and deliverance from suffering, framing the god as a compassionate judge.

Scholarly Debates
Identity of the İvriz figure
The colossal figure at İvriz is Arma, the moon-god, as stated in the accompanying Luwian inscription. ⇌ The figure is Tarhunza, the Storm-god, based on the attributes of grapes and wheat (fertility symbols) and the absence of a crescent moon.
Evidence for A: The inscription explicitly names Arma.
Evidence for B: The iconography (grapes, wheat, beard, horned crown) is typical of Storm-gods; the crescent moon is absent.
Arma as a Hittite vs. Luwian deity
Arma was originally a Hittite god, adopted by the Luwians. ⇌ Arma was originally a Luwian god, adopted by the Hittites.

Evidence for B: The name 'Arma' is clearly Luwian in etymology, and his later cult is predominantly Luwian.
Arma's consort
Arma's consort is the Sun-goddess of Arinna. ⇌ Arma's consort is Ḫepat (Hurrian influence).
Evidence for A: Hittite state cult lists pair Arma with the Sun-goddess in some texts.
Evidence for B: Hurrian-influenced texts pair Arma with Ḫepat.
What We Don't Know

No surviving mythology directly narrates Arma's deeds; his character is reconstructed from prayers, cult inventories, and iconography. No dedicated Hittite hymn to Arma has yet been identified (though fragments may exist). The İvriz relief's identification remains debated. The Luwian 'Arma' rituals (healing magic) are not yet fully published/translated. The full extent of his cult in the Hittite provinces is unknown. Modern reception is thin; no major film, game, or novel features Arma directly.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Arma as the Anatolian equivalent of the Greek Selene or the Mesopotamian Sin — a lunar deity who was both a cosmic power and a personal protector. If the Hittites had made a Marvel movie, Arma would be the wise, benevolent mentor figure who shows up when the hero needs guidance and a reminder of their oaths. He's the 'dad friend' of the Hittite pantheon: reliable, steady, and always watching over you — especially at night.
