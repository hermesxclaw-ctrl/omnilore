Who It Is

Yasy Yateré (or Jasy Jatere — 'Little Piece of the Moon' in Guarani) is a mysterious childlike forest spirit, said to be the brother or son of the moon goddess Jasy. He is described as a beautiful, small, blond-haired, blue-eyed boy — an unusual appearance in indigenous Guarani mythology that may reflect the traumatic colonial encounter. He carries a magical golden wand or staff and wanders the forest during the siesta hours (midday, the hottest part of the day when most people rest). His behavior is deeply ambiguous: he enchants children with his beauty and his magical staff, luring them deep into the forest where they become lost and disoriented. Some versions say he feeds them honey and wild fruits before releasing them; others say they are never seen again. He is associated with the siesta, the heat, and the disorientation that comes from wandering in the midday sun. Yasy Yateré also has a connection to birds: he can perfectly mimic the call of any bird and is sometimes called the 'Lord of the Birds' (a title he shares with the Pombero). His blond, blue-eyed appearance has been interpreted by scholars as a post-contact addition, possibly reflecting the shock of encountering European children or the syncretic incorporation of European fairy traditions (like the German Lorelei or the English Will-o'-the-Wisp) into the Guarani mythological framework. In Paraguay today, his name is used to warn children against wandering off at midday, and he remains a vivid figure in rural storytelling traditions.
