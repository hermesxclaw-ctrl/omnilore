Form

No ancient source describes Anchiale's physical appearance. As a nymph, she would conventionally be imagined as a beautiful young woman, but this is a generic trope, not an attested detail. Any specific description in modern media is an invention.

Powers

Nymph Status As a nymph, Anchiale is a semi-divine being, likely possessing immortality and the ability to inhabit or embody a natural place. However, no specific powers are ever attributed to her in the extant sources. All She has no independent myths or feats. Her power is entirely passive and genealogical. None, as she has no powers to contradict.

Accounts

The scholiast records that Anchiale, a nymph of Crete, lay with Apollo and gave birth to the Korybantes and the Daktyloi. These are groups of mythical, daemonic beings associated with the earth, metallurgy, and the ecstatic worship of the Great Mother (Rhea/Cybele). The story is a simple genealogical statement with no narrative development.

In the Bibliotheca, a comprehensive mythological handbook, Anchiale appears as the mother of Iasios, whom she bore to Apollo. This Iasios is a figure of near-total obscurity, mentioned only here. He is distinct from the more famous Iasion, the lover of Demeter, who was also a son of Zeus and Elektra.

Identity with the Cilician Nymph
The Cretan nymph Anchiale is the same figure as the eponymous nymph of the Cilician city of Anchiale. ⇌ They are two distinct figures who happen to share a name.
Evidence for A: The shared name and the general tendency of Greek myth to associate nymphs with specific places.

What We Don't Know

The primary gap is the lack of any primary source that describes Anchiale in any detail. We have only her name and her genealogical connections. There is no narrative, no description, and no cult. The exact nature of her connection to Cilicia, if any, is unknown. The possibility that she was a more significant figure in local Cretan cult is speculative. The research is limited by the paucity of ancient evidence.

A way to hold it in your mind

Anchiale is a mythological placeholder. She is a name that fills a slot in a genealogy, a necessary mother for a set of daemonic beings. She is not a character in a story, but a function in a system. Think of her as a 'node' in a network, a connection point that links Apollo to the Daktyloi and Korybantes, but which has no inherent content of its own. She is the ultimate example of a minor figure in a major mythology.
