SIREN HEAD

"It's not hunting you. It's broadcasting you."

TypeMonster / Cryptid
CreatorTrevor Henderson, 2018
Height~40 ft (12 m)
HabitatRural woodland

You're driving through deep country when the radio dissolves into static. Then a voice — your mother's voice — crackles through the speakers. "Pull over, sweetheart. It's not safe." You know your mother has been dead for six years. You look out the window and see it: a forty-foot tower of dried, mummified flesh striding through the treeline, twin sirens where a head should be, screaming her last words on a loop. It isn't hunting you. It's broadcasting you. You are the message now.

Case Summary

Siren Head is a fictional creature created by Canadian illustrator Trevor Henderson, first posted to social media in August 2018. It stands roughly 40 feet tall — a humanoid figure of desiccated flesh with two industrial emergency sirens fused where a head should be. It belongs to a new category of monster: the internet-native cryptid, credited to one artist rather than an anonymous forum thread like Slender Man.

A humanoid figure roughly 40 feet tall, built from dried, sinewy flesh stretched over an impossibly thin frame — rust-and-bone in color, ribcage visible beneath the skin. Limbs are elongated and spindly, ending in long claw-like fingers.
Where a head should be, a long flexible stalk of flesh supports two large fused emergency sirens — the kind mounted on police cars or air-raid towers — connected to its body by organic tubes and wires that look grown rather than attached. It has no eyes, no mouth, no face at all.
Perfect sound mimicry of any voice or sound it has ever heard, including full human speech. Volume range from a whisper to a shriek loud enough to cause physical pain and hearing damage. Infrasound emission — frequencies below hearing that cause nausea, anxiety, and unexplained dread.
Stillness camouflage — it can stand motionless and pass for a derelict utility pole or old siren tower. Surprising stealth for its size; it does not need to be fast when its voice does the hunting.
Witnesses (per Henderson's lore) describe its posture as hunched, heron-like, disturbingly patient. It does not need speed — only proximity, and the right voice.
Known Weaknesses & Wards

Siren Head is physical and can theoretically be harmed by heavy weaponry, though its durability is unknown. Its size makes it difficult to hide in open terrain. It can be tracked by its sound — it broadcasts, and that broadcast reveals its position. It may be vulnerable to its own weapon: loud, disorienting noise directed back at it. Destroying its sirens would presumably neutralize its primary threat. It appears to be solitary and does not call for reinforcements. The fact that it hunts through isolation means that staying in populated, well-lit areas may offer protection.

Field Incident Log

Siren Head is an ambush predator that wins psychologically before it ever moves. It listens. It learns a target's voice, or the voice of someone they've lost, and plays it back to lure them closer — a crying child, a friend calling for help, the radio station that was playing when someone died.
Prefers isolated terrain — deep woods, abandoned rural roads, dead towns where a scream won't reach anyone who could help. Henderson's lore suggests patience bordering on the ritualistic — it can wait, motionless, for days.
Nothing in canon confirms it can be killed, but its sheer size makes concealment difficult in open terrain, and its own broadcasts reveal its position the instant it uses them. Henderson has implied that loud, disorienting sound turned back on it might disrupt its hunting pattern, and destroying the sirens themselves would likely neutralize its main weapon.
Henderson has hinted there may be more than one Siren Head, and that they can communicate across long distances — raising the unconfirmed possibility of a distributed population rather than a single specimen.

TAPE_015 · CULTURAL ANALYSIS
Siren Head represents a new generation of internet horror — creator-owned (unlike the anonymous Slender Man), platform-native (designed for Twitter and Instagram's visual format), and participatory (Henderson encourages fan art, games, and interpretations). It demonstrates how individual artists can now launch global horror icons without institutional backing. Siren Head's emphasis on sound horror taps into primal fears about communication, the unreliability of familiar voices, and the violation of the soundscape — in an era of deepfakes and AI voice cloning, a monster that steals voices feels prophetically relevant.

Origin & Case History

Henderson posted the first image of Siren Head to his Twitter account — a blurry, "found-photograph" style composite of a tall, spindly figure with sirens for a head, captioned with a short piece of unsettling lore. This "unnerving found image" technique — presenting his creatures as though they were genuine trail-cam or archive photos rather than illustrations — is Henderson's signature style. Henderson has said the concept was partly rooted in the emergency sirens he grew up hearing in rural Canada, reworked into something that hunts using the very sound meant to warn and protect.
Siren Head represents a solo, credited artist launching a globally recognized horror icon with zero studio backing, built entirely on a platform-native visual format. It also normalized "analog horror" — creatures presented as documentary evidence rather than fiction — tapping into the same unease as deepfakes and voice-cloning: a monster that steals the sound of people you trust.
Siren Head has spread through dozens of indie horror games (notably itch.io and Roblox), fan animations, and YouTube "analog horror" series alongside entities like the Backrooms. It shares a loose universe with Henderson's other creatures — Cartoon Cat, Long Horse, and the Man with the Upside-Down Face — and remains his most culturally significant creation.

The Henderson Unnerving-Images Universe

Siren Head does not exist alone. Trevor Henderson has spent years quietly building a shared roster of creatures, all rendered in the same "found photograph" style, that occasionally cross paths in fan animations and games even though Henderson himself rarely confirms a single unified canon.

A grinning, black-and-white, rubber-hose-animation-style cat that stalks victims through suburban streets at night. Widely considered Henderson's second-most-famous creation after Siren Head, and the one most frequently paired with it in fan crossovers.
An impossibly long, horse-headed creature that stretches across roads and fields. Less predatory than Siren Head in most fan interpretations — often depicted as unsettling but passive, a fixture of the same rural nightmare landscape.
A humanoid figure whose facial features are inverted — among the earlier and stranger entries in Henderson's catalog, illustrating his consistent technique of taking one ordinary human feature and inverting it into wrongness.
Unlike the SCP Foundation (an open collaborative-fiction wiki with thousands of contributing authors) or Slender Man (which mutated anonymously across a 2009 forum thread before anyone could claim ownership), every creature in Henderson's universe traces back to one credited artist. That makes attribution simple — everything here is credited to Trevor Henderson — but it also means "canon" is looser and more author-driven than crowd-sourced horror franchises, and Henderson has been comfortable leaving connections between his creatures ambiguous rather than codified.

Fan Culture & Spread

Siren Head's jump from a single 2018 tweet to global recognition happened almost entirely through fan labor rather than any official studio push. Indie developers built dozens of unlicensed horror games around it on itch.io and Roblox before any official game existed. YouTube analog-horror creators folded it into anthology series alongside completely unrelated internet-horror properties like the Backrooms, cementing it as a recognizable face of the entire genre rather than a single creator's isolated work. Henderson has generally tolerated this spread rather than issuing takedowns — a permissiveness that likely accelerated the character's reach considerably, though it also means most "Siren Head content" a newcomer encounters today is fan-made, not canon.

Modern Media & Reception

Verified appearances across media. All entries confirmed via direct research.

Siren Head became a massive viral phenomenon, spawning countless fan arts, animations, and discussions. It is a staple of modern internet horror.
Numerous fan-made games have been created, often as free-to-play horror experiences on platforms like Roblox, Itch.io, and Steam. These games typically feature stealth and sound-based gameplay.
The creature is a popular subject for horror story narrations, found-footage style videos, and reaction content. Many videos feature 'sightings' or 'encounters' that are clearly fictional but presented for entertainment.
The creature's striking design has made it a popular subject for fan art and, more recently, cosplay, despite its impractical size.
The siren sound itself has become iconic, and many fan projects use distorted sirens and static to create an unsettling atmosphere.
