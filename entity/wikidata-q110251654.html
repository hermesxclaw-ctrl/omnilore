Who It Is

She was born on land but belonged to the sea - a baby with a fish's tail where legs should have been, wailing on a bamboo floor while the village outside whispered 'halimaw.' Dyesebel is not the ancient sirena who drags fishermen to their deaths; she is the mermaid who begged to be human, who traded her voice, her tail, and her home for a chance to walk on knives and be loved on shore. Invented in 1952 by Mars Ravelo on cheap komiks newsprint, she has since been played by six film queens and two primetime goddesses, each tail more glittering than the last - yet her story has always been about the same ache: what would you cut away of yourself to belong?

Attested as compassionate, gentle, self-sacrificing, and yearning for belonging. In the original serial and all screen versions, Dyesebel experiences rejection as a child due to her form - called 'halimaw' (monster) by humans - which instills shyness and a deep desire to be 'normal.' She is not a predatory sirena who lures sailors to death; she is explicitly non-violent toward humans. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Invented proper name created by Filipino writer Mars Ravelo (Marcelo Ravelo, 1916-1988). No attested pre-colonial etymology; the name is not derived from Tagalog, Spanish, or any documented folklore term. Ravelo never published an official etymology. Pronunciation — Tagalog: [ˌdʒɛː.sɛˈbɛl] (dyeh-seh-BEL), with stress on final syllable; English-language media often renders as /ˈdʒaɪsəbɛl/ or /djeɪˈsɛbɛl/, which is non-canonical.

Aliases — Dyesebel the Mermaid, Dyese, Anak ng Sirena

Ownership — Modern Filipino popular culture. Created as a commercial komiks character for Ace Publications (Pilipino Komiks) in the early 1950s. Copyright and trademark held by the Mars Ravelo

Powers
Mermaid Physiology - Aquatic Adaptation

As a mermaid, Dyesebel breathes underwater, swims at superhuman speed, withstands deep-water pressure and cold, and communicates with sea creatures. She can remain submerged indefinitely and leap high above the water.

Limit: Cannot survive indefinitely on land without transformation; prolonged exposure to air causes dehydration, weakness, and eventual death in mermaid form. Cannot walk or breathe air efficiently with tail.

Human Transformation via Sea-Witch Bargain

Through a pact with a sea witch (variously named Berta, Banak, or simply Bruha ng Dagat), Dyesebel exchanges something precious for fully human legs and the ability to live on land. The ritual involves a potion, incantation, or enchanted shell.

Limit: Transformation is painful (described as 'like walking on knives' in 2008 dialogue, echoing Andersen), often conditional or reversible if she returns to the sea or betrays the pact. In some versions, if her human love is unfaithful, she will turn to sea foam or revert. Requires the witch's consent; cannot self-transform.

Enchanting Voice and Song

Possesses a hauntingly beautiful singing voice that can calm storms, soothe sea animals, and captivate humans. Not used as a weapon to drown sailors like folk sirena, but as an expression of longing.

Limit: Voice is lost or muted after the bargain in versions where it is the price; in other versions she retains it. No attested power of mind control or mass hypnosis beyond emotional influence.

Enhanced Beauty and Allure

Described in-universe as surpassingly beautiful in both mermaid and human forms, causing immediate infatuation in Fredo and jealousy in rivals. Functions as narrative power rather than supernatural compulsion.

Limit: Beauty makes her a target for exploitation, mockery, and sexualization; does not grant protection or authority. In dark-history readings, this beauty is used to justify voyeuristic camera work in 1970s films.

Empathy with Marine Life

Can understand and command fish, dolphins, turtles, and other sea creatures, who aid her or warn her of danger.

Limit: Communication is empathic, not absolute control; larger predators like sharks are not consistently controllable.

Accounts I
The Birth of the Mermaid Child - Pilipino Komiks Original SerialMars Ravelo & Elpidio Torres, Pilipino Komiks #1 onward, Ace Publications

1952-1953

Lucia, a human woman, gives birth to a child with a fish tail instead of legs - Dyesebel. Her husband Tino is horrified and the village brands the infant a monster and bad omen. Lucia flees with the child to the sea to save her from being killed, where Dyesebel is raised among mermaids but never fully accepted because of her half-human blood. As a young adult, Dyesebel rescues a drowning human fisherman, Fredo, and falls in love with him from afar, watching him from the rocks. Desperate to join his world, she seeks the sea witch to become human. Establishes the core tragedy of otherness and the central bargain that drives all later versions. Ends with Dyesebel gaining human legs, marrying Fredo, and being accepted by the village - a melodramatic resolution typical of Ravelo's romance formula.

The Bargain and TransformationPilipino Komiks serial climax; reproduced in Dyesebel 1953 film (LVN Pictures)

1953

Dyesebel, now grown, confronts the sea witch in her underwater cave. The witch offers a potion that will split her tail into legs, warning that every step will bring pain and that if Fredo does not truly love her, she will die or turn to sea foam. Dyesebel drinks it, endures agonizing transformation on the shore, and is found unconscious by Fredo, who takes her in. She must learn to walk, speak, and live as a human while hiding her origin. Provides the template for all subsequent transformation scenes, directly echoing Hans Christian Andersen's The Little Mermaid (1837) but with a Filipino Catholic melodrama framing where love and sacrifice are rewarded rather than punished.

Anak ni Dyesebel (Child of Dyesebel)Tagalog Ilang-Ilang Productions film, directed by Gerardo de Leon, story by Mars Ravelo

1964

A direct sequel/spin-off. Dyesebel and Fredo, now married and fully human, have a daughter, Sabel (also called Alona in some synopses), who is born with a mermaid tail, repeating her mother's curse. The child is hidden and must navigate the same prejudice Dyesebel once faced, while Dyesebel must decide whether to let her daughter make the same bargain she did. The film explores generational trauma and whether acceptance has truly been achieved. First expansion of the Dyesebel universe beyond a single protagonist; establishes that the mermaid condition is hereditary. Commercially successful, it cemented the franchise potential and justified later remakes.

Accounts II
Dyesebel (1973) - Vilma Santos ReimaginingDyesebel (1973 film), Virgo Film Productions, directed by Emmanuel H. Borlaza

1973

A color remake that leans into adult romance and spectacle. Dyesebel is discovered as an infant by a fisherman couple, raised in isolation in a seaside hut, and kept hidden from the village. Her beauty as a young mermaid attracts both Fredo, a wealthy resort heir, and the jealous Betty, Fredo's human fiancée. The sea witch sequence is more elaborate, with underwater photography, and the human-world climax involves Betty's attempts to expose Dyesebel as a freak at a village fiesta. Shifted Dyesebel from innocent komiks heroine to 1970s sex symbol, reflecting changing censorship and audience tastes. Vilma Santos's performance became iconic and is still referenced as a definitive portrayal.

Mars Ravelo's Dyesebel (2008) - The 125-Episode EpicGMA Network television series, 125 episodes

2008

The most expansive retelling. Lucia (Jean Garcia) is a human who is raped or seduced by Tino, a merman prince, resulting in Dyesebel's birth. Rejected by both worlds, Dyesebel (Marian Rivera) grows up under the protection of Queen Dyangga of the mer-kingdom and her adoptive mother Banak. She falls in love with Fredo (Dingdong Dantes), now reimagined as Prinsipe Fredo, a human prince whose kingdom borders the sea. The series adds court intrigue among mermaids, a war between humans and merfolk, Betty (Katrina Halili) as a half-sister who becomes a mermaid through dark magic out of envy, and a prolonged transformation arc where Dyesebel must choose between her mother's people and Fredo's world. The sea witch is named Doña Berta and later Banak herself is revealed as a former witch. Became the highest-rated fantaserye of 2008, revived national interest in komiks adaptations, and generated extensive merchandising. Its world-building is now the de facto canon for younger audiences, though it diverges significantly from the 1952 original.

Mars Ravelo's Dyesebel (2014) - Anne Curtis RebootABS-CBN television series, 88 episodes

2014

A darker, more ecological reboot. Dyesebel (Anne Curtis) is again the child of Lucia (Andi Eigenmann) and Tino (Sam Milby), a merman. Raised in the ocean by the mermaid Liro, she is forbidden contact with humans. She saves Fredo (Gerald Anderson), a marine biologist/conservationist, from drowning, and their love becomes a bridge between species. Betty (Erich Gonzales) is reimagined as Fredo's childhood friend who becomes obsessed with becoming a mermaid herself, making a separate bargain with the sea witch Banak (Dawn Zulueta). The series emphasizes environmental themes - coral reef destruction, dynamite fishing - and Dyesebel's transformation is framed as both personal choice and ecological allegory. The finale allows Dyesebel to become human permanently but retain the ability to call the sea. Second major TV canon, creating a split fandom between 2008 and 2014 versions. Noted for higher CGI budget and for casting Anne Curtis, already a major star, which brought renewed international press. Confirmed Dyesebel's status as a perennial franchise rather than a one-off story.

Dyesebel is an ancient Philippine folklore mermaid, part of pre-colonial mythology like the sirena. — Dyesebel is a modern commercial creation from 1952, invented by Mars Ravelo for Pilipino Komiks. While she draws on the existing folk sirena/siyokoy tradition, she is not herself a folk figure and has no attested oral history before 1952. The folk sirena is typically a dangerous, alluring creature who drowns fishermen; Dyesebel is explicitly a sympathetic, non-predatory heroine.

Dyesebel is the same character as Darna or that Dyesebel can transform like Darna by shouting a magic word. — Dyesebel and Darna are separate Ravelo creations with unrelated powers and universes. Darna transforms via swallowing a magical stone and shouting 'Darna!'; Dyesebel transforms via a painful, conditional bargain with a sea witch. The 2005 Darna cameo (Ara Mina as Dyesebel) was a cross-promotional Easter egg, not evidence of shared powers.

Dyesebel's story is just a Filipino copy of Hans Christian Andersen's The Little Mermaid and therefore has no original content. — Ravelo openly borrowed the core bargain motif from Andersen (acknowledged by scholars), but Dyesebel's story is distinct in its Filipino Catholic melodrama framing, its focus on parental rejection and village prejudice rather than royal court, its happy ending (marriage and acceptance) versus Andersen's tragic death, and its later expansion into a multi-generational franchise with uniquely Filipino elements like the fishing village, Reyna Dyangga, and Betty.

There has only been one Dyesebel TV series (usually cited as either 2008 or 2014). — There have been two major, separate TV series - GMA's 2008 version (125 episodes, Marian Rivera) and ABS-CBN's 2014 version (88 episodes, Anne Curtis) - produced by rival networks with different canons, plus an announced but unproduced 2022 ABS-CBN project with Andrea Brillantes. Additionally, there were five theatrical films (1953, 1973, 1978, 1990, 1996) and a spin-off (1964).
