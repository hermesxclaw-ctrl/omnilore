Obatala

King of the White Cloth, Creator of Humanity, Lord of Purity and Wisdom

also known asOrisa NlaObatalaOshalaOxala
TypeDeity (Orisha)
CultureYoruba
Who It Is
Form

Obatala is often depicted as an elderly man dressed in white, symbolizing purity and wisdom. He carries a staff (opagbe) and sometimes a white dove. In Candomble he is syncretized with Our Lord of Bonfim, often shown wearing a long white robe with a blue cape.

Powers

Creation Known for creating the physical bodies of humanity from clay. Yoruba creation myths Dependent on Olodumare for the life force.

Purity and Truth Guardian of purity and truth, often involved in moral and ethical guidance. He punishes those who violate taboos around purity. Yoruba oral traditions None specified.

Healing Obatala is also invoked for healing, particularly for birth defects, paralysis, and other conditions associated with his own imperfection after drinking palm wine. Santeria and Candomble ritual practice None specified.

Accounts

Obatala is tasked by Olodumare to shape humans from clay. During his task, he consumes palm wine and creates some imperfect beings, leading to a vow of sobriety. This story explains physical imperfections and Obatala's association with people with disabilities.

While shaping humans from clay, Obatala was thirsty and drank palm wine. Intoxicated, he created deformed figures. Upon sobering and seeing what he had done, he vowed never to drink palm wine again and promised to protect those with physical differences.

Obatala descended from the heavens using a chain to reach the primordial earth. He brought with him the tools of creation and established the first settlements. This descent explains why mountains are considered sacred to Obatala -- they are the places where his chain touched the earth.
