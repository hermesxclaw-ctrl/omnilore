Who It Is

The Peluda (also known as La Peluda, meaning 'the hairy one') is a dragon-like monster from the folklore of La Ferté-Bernard in the Sarthe region of northwestern France. According to legend, the Peluda was one of a kind — a monstrous creature resembling a giant serpent covered not in scales but in thick, shaggy hair, with a head like a dragon and a long, sinuous body that could crush trees and houses. It possessed a deadly venomous stinger on the tip of its tail like a scorpion, and its breath could wither crops and poison the air for miles. The beast emerged from the Huisne River, where it lived, to devastate the countryside, devouring livestock and destroying villages. Like the Hydra of Greek myth, any of the Peluda's body parts that were cut off would regrow, making it seemingly invulnerable to conventional weapons. The hero to defeat it varied by telling: some versions credit a courageous knight who discovered its one weak spot — its soft underbelly — while others attribute its death to a local peasant who had been blessed by a saint. The story is sometimes linked to the legend of Saint Margaret (or Saint Martha in certain tellings). The Peluda belongs to the family of French guivre and is closely related in type to the Tarasque and the Graoully, all representing the serpent-dragon archetype in Gallic folklore.
