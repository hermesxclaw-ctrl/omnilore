Who It Is

In the green valleys of Sarthe, France, there once lurked a beast so terrible that even the floodwaters of Noah's Ark could not wash it away. The Peluda, or 'La Velue'—the Hairy One—was a serpent-tailed monster covered in long green fur and deadly poisoned spines, a terror that stalked the banks of the River Huisne and the streets of La Ferté-Bernard. It devoured livestock, snatched children and maidens, and withered crops with its fiery breath, until a young woman's fiancé struck its one vulnerable spot: the tail. This is the story of a creature that survived the Deluge and met its end at a bridge, but whose legend still lingers in fountains, games, and the annals of imaginary beasts.

The Peluda is a malevolent beast that terrorized the countryside and town. It devoured livestock, especially sheep, and snatched human victims, particularly children and young maidens. It used its tail to strike dead humans and animals, and when chased, it retreated to the River Huisne and caused floods that ruined crops, leading to famine. Some accounts say it shot fire from its mouth to wither crops. It was relentless and seemingly invulnerable except for one weak spot on its tail.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The French name 'La Velue' derives from the Old French 'velu' meaning 'hairy' or 'shaggy', referring to its fur-covered body. The Spanish name 'Peluda' has the same meaning, introduced by Jorge Luis Borges. The English translations 'Shaggy Beast' or 'Hairy Beast' follow this etymology.

Pronunciation — Peluda: peh-LOO-dah (Spanish); La Velue: lah vuh-LOO (French)

Aliases — La Velue, The Shaggy Beast of La Ferté-Bernard, The Hairy Beast of La Ferté-Bernard, The Hairy One

The beast can strike with its serpent-like tail to kill humans and animals instantly.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Peluda is described as serpent-headed and serpent-tailed, with an egg-shaped body the size of an ox. Its body is covered in long green fur, from which emerge sharp spikes tipped with deadly poison. It has broad, tortoise-like feet. The beast's fur and spikes are its most distinctive features, giving it a shaggy, fearsome appearance.

Symbols — In iconography, the Peluda is depicted as a dragon-like creature with fur and spikes. A drawing appears on the cover of the 1889 pamphlet. Another illustration is in Jean Paul Ronecker's book on dragons (2004). The terra cotta dragon at Tuffé is claimed to represent a velue, though this is uncertain. The 2007 fountain in Tuffé likely depicts the creature in a stylized form.

Domains — The Peluda lived on the banks of the River Huisne in the Sarthe region of France. It ranged from the river to the city of La Ferté-Bernard and the surrounding countryside, including areas near Connerré and Yvré-l'Évêque. It was active during the High Middle Ages, perhaps more specifically the 15th century.

High Middle Ages, possibly 15th century

The Peluda, a monstrous serpent-like beast with green fur and poisonous spikes, emerged from the River Huisne to terrorize the countryside and town of La Ferté-Bernard. It devoured livestock, killed humans with its tail, and caused floods that ruined crops. It was said to have survived the Deluge by being excluded from Noah's Ark. The beast was finally defeated when it captured the virtuous maiden l'Agnelle ('Little Lamb'), and her fiancé struck its tail with a sword, the only vulnerable spot, killing it instantly. The beast died at the bridge of Yvré-l'Évêque, and its body was stuffed or embalmed. The beast's death brought peace to the region. The victory was celebrated at La Ferté-Bernard and Connerré. The body was preserved as a trophy.

The Peluda is a Spanish mythical beast. — It is originally French (La Velue) from the Sarthe region. The name 'Peluda' was introduced by Borges in Spanish.

The Peluda is a typical dragon. — It is a unique beast with fur and spines, more like a chimera. It is often classified as a dragon, but its description is distinct.

The Peluda only breathed fire. — Some sources emphasize flood-causing rather than fire-breathing. Both are mentioned in different accounts.
