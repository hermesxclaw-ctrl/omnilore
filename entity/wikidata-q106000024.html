Who It Is

In the pantheon of Japanese horse racing, T. M. Opera O stands as a colossus—a chestnut titan whose undefeated 2000 season rewrote the record books and whose heart, literally, was larger than life. His story is one of late registration, relentless dominance, and a legacy that transcends the turf, from the pages of equine science to the vibrant world of anime.

Mentally tough as described by jockey Ryuji Wada: 'mentally he's so tough I wish he’d share some of it with me.'

Accounts I
1999 Classic VictoryWikipedia

1999

After winning three consecutive races, T. M. Opera O was dismissed at 11-1 in the Satsuki Sho but came from behind to win by a neck. Won the Satsuki Sho, becoming champion three-year-old colt.

Undefeated 2000 SeasonWikipedia

2000

Won all eight starts, including five domestic Grade I wins and one international Grade I win (Japan Cup). Became world's all-time leading money earner. Unanimous Japanese Horse of the Year; set a world record for earnings.

Accounts II
2001 Rivalry and RetirementWikipedia

2001

Won the Tenno Sho (spring) again but was defeated by Meisho Doto in the Takarazuka Kinen. Finished second in the Japan Cup and retired as world's all-time leading money earner. Retired with record earnings of ¥1,835,189,000.

Death and LegacyWikipedia

2018

Died of a heart attack at Hakuba Bokujo on May 17, 2018. His heart was studied and found to be unusually large. Funeral held on June 15, 2018; legacy continues in Umamusume.
