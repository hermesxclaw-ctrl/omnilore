Who They Are

Ek Chuaj is the Maya god of merchants, travelers, trade, and cacao (chocolate). He is a unique deity in the Maya pantheon as the patron of the merchant class, reflecting the importance of long-distance trade in Maya civilization. He is depicted as a black-painted figure carrying a backpack of goods and carrying a spear or walking stick, sometimes shown with the distinctive markings of a cacao pod.

Cultural Significance

Ek Chuaj reflects the sophistication of Maya trade networks, which stretched across thousands of miles and linked diverse cultures. The merchant class in Maya society (the ppolom) held significant social status, and Ek Chuaj's existence as a major deity underscores their importance. Cacao (chocolate) was so valuable that it was used as currency in the Maya world, and Ek Chuaj's role as the cacao god gave him an economic dimension unique among Mesoamerican deities. Offerings of cacao beans were made to Ek Chuaj before planting new cacao trees and at the beginning of harvest.
