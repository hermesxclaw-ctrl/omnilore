Who It Is

In the bustling markets of the Maya lowlands, merchants whispered prayers to a black-faced god who watched over trade, cacao, and the long roads that connected distant cities. He was Ek Chuaj, the 'Black Scorpion' or the 'Black War Chief,' a deity of commerce and travel whose image adorned the pages of Maya codices and whose favor could mean the difference between prosperity and ruin.

As a patron of merchants, Ek Chuaj is associated with wealth, trade, and travel. He may also have a warlike aspect, given the epithet 'Black War Chief', suggesting protection of caravans and military escort.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Ek Chuaj' likely derives from Yucatec Maya: 'ek' meaning 'black' or 'star', and 'chuaj' possibly related to 'scorpion' or 'war chief'. The exact etymology is debated.

Pronunciation — ek CHWAH

Aliases — Ek Chuah, God M, Black Scorpion, Black War Chief, Merchant Deity

Ownership — Maya civilization, particularly Yucatec Maya and lowland Maya regions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ek Chuaj is typically depicted with a black-painted face or body, sometimes with a scorpion tail. He may wear a merchant's pack and carry a staff or shield. In codices, he appears with distinctive black markings around his eyes.

Symbols — Scorpion, cacao pods, merchant's walking stick, backpack, and black body paint. He is often shown with a prominent nose and possibly a turban-like headdress.

Domains — Ek Chuaj presides over markets, trade routes, and travelers. He is also linked to cacao cultivation and possibly to the underworld due to his black color and scorpion associations.
