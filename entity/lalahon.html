Etymology

The name 'Laon' means 'the ancient one' in Visayan languages. The suffix '-sina' in 'Laonsina' means 'foreigner', possibly indicating an introduced deity.

Cultural Ownership

Visayan peoples of the Philippines, including Aklanon, Capiznon, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Karay-a, Suludnon, and Waray groups.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars conflate Laon with the Tagalog Bathala, but they are distinct deities. Also, the Bicolano Kalaon is a separate evil god, not the same as Laon.

Archive Dossier — Approach

On the slopes of Mount Kanlaon, where the volcano's breath still smolders, the ancient Visayans once climbed to honor a goddess who could bless the harvest or send locusts to strip the fields bare. She was Laon, the Ancient One, a creator whose dual nature held both justice and destruction, and whose name echoes across the islands in many forms.

I give you the rice that fills your bowl. I take it back with fire and ash. Respect the mountain, and the mountain will respect you.

Who It Is

Lalahon is the Visayan goddess of harvest and volcanoes, a dual-natured deity who brings both abundance and destruction. She dwells within Mount Kanlaon on Negros Island, one of the most active volcanoes in the Philippines. In times of favor, Lalahon blesses the fields with bountiful harvests of rice, corn, and root crops. When angered — by disrespect, insufficient offerings, or broken taboos — she sends plagues of locusts to devour crops or causes Mount Kanlaon to erupt, sending ash and lava over the land. She is depicted as a fierce but beautiful woman dressed in red and gold, carrying a basket of grain in one hand and a volcanic stone in the other. The pre-colonial Visayans offered Lalahon the first fruits of the harvest, burying them at the base of Mount Kanlaon. The dual nature of Lalahon — both nurturing mother and destructive force — reflects the precarious relationship of agricultural societies with volcanoes, whose ash fertilizes soil but whose eruptions destroy everything. She is also known simply as 'The One from Kanlaon' and was one of the most feared and respected deities in the Visayan pantheon.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

No specific physical description is recorded. She is usually described as female but has male aspects.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with the Kanlaon volcano, agriculture, and the sky. No known iconography from pre-colonial times.

Behaviour & Personality

In her female form, she is a milder, sympathetic deity of justice and equality. In her male aspect (Makapatag), she is destructive, punishing, and vengeful.

Domains & Habitats

Resides in the Kanlaon volcano on Negros Island. Also associated with the sky and the uppermost layer of the universe, where she is identified with Makaako.

Version Differences

Different Visayan groups have varying names and aspects: Laon (general), Lalahon (agricultural, female), Malaon (Waray, female aspect), Laonsina/Alunsina (Suludnon, sky goddess).
