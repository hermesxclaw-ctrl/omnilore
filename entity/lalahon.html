Who It Is

On the slopes of Mount Kanlaon, where the volcano's breath still smolders, the ancient Visayans once climbed to honor a goddess who could bless the harvest or send locusts to strip the fields bare. She was Laon, the Ancient One, a creator whose dual nature held both justice and destruction, and whose name echoes across the islands in many forms.

In her female form, she is a milder, sympathetic deity of justice and equality. In her male aspect (Makapatag), she is destructive, punishing, and vengeful.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Laon' means 'the ancient one' in Visayan languages. The suffix '-sina' in 'Laonsina' means 'foreigner', possibly indicating an introduced deity.

Pronunciation — lah-ON

Aliases — Lahon, Lalaon, Lalahon, Lauon, Malaon, Raom

Ownership — Visayan peoples of the Philippines, including Aklanon, Capiznon, Cebuano, Hiligaynon, Karay-a, Suludnon, and Waray groups.

Powers
Creation

As a supreme creator deity, she created the first creature (a bird) and indirectly caused the emergence of the first humans from a bamboo or rattan stem.

Limit: None recorded.

Agricultural Control

Controls harvests; can send locusts to spoil crops when displeased.

Limit: Only mentioned in agricultural context.

Divine Justice

As Makapatag, she enforces punishment and vengeance.

Limit: Male aspect is destructive.

Sky and Weather

As Laonsina, she is a sky goddess, possibly controlling celestial phenomena.

Limit: Not clearly defined.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description is recorded. She is usually described as female but has male aspects.

Symbols — Associated with the Kanlaon volcano, agriculture, and the sky. No known iconography from pre-colonial times.

Domains — Resides in the Kanlaon volcano on Negros Island. Also associated with the sky and the uppermost layer of the universe, where she is identified with Makaako.

Creation of the First CreaturesVisayan creation myths

Pre-colonial

Laon creates a bird (manaul) that finds the first islands and causes the emergence of other creatures, including the first man and woman found inside a bamboo or rattan stem. Establishment of the world and humanity.

Accounts II
Rituals on KanlaonDe Loarca (1582)

Pre-colonial

Babaylan (shamans) climb the volcano to perform rituals and offer gifts during harvest seasons or special ceremonies to honor Laon. Ensures good harvests and divine favor.

Laon is a fire goddess. — This stems from a mistranslation of De Loarca's account. The original Spanish says the volcano hurls fire, not the goddess.

Laon is the same as Bathala. — They are distinct deities from different cultural groups, though both are supreme creators.

Laon is purely female. — Laon has both female and male aspects, with the male aspect known as Makapatag.
