Who It Is

Yemoja (Yemọja) is the Yoruba orisha of the ocean, motherhood, and the universal womb from which all life emerges. Her name means 'Mother Whose Children Are the Fish,' reflecting her role as the primordial mother of all orishas and, by extension, all living beings. Originally a river deity associated with the Ogun River in Nigeria, she was transformed into an ocean goddess in the Americas, where the enslaved Yoruba people encountered the vast Atlantic — the same ocean that had carried them to the New World. She is depicted as a majestic woman in flowing blue and white garments, often holding a fan or mirror, emerging from the waves. Her nurturing aspect is paramount: she is the protector of children, pregnant women, and families, and her embrace is said to be infinitely comforting. However, she can also be fiercely protective, washing away those who threaten her children. In Santería, she is syncretized with Our Lady of Regla, and her feast day is celebrated on September 7. Her worship extends through Cuba, Brazil (as Iemanjá, especially celebrated on New Year's Eve), Trinidad, and Haiti.
