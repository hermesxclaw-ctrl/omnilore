Who It Is

In the blood-slick courtyard of Arlong Park, a mountain of muscle in a tattered karate gi bows with perfect form before trying to kill you with a palm thrust that fires through water itself — that's Kuroobi, the black belt whose name is a rank, a joke, and a warning. Eiichiro Oda's manta-ray fish-man turns the real-world prestige of the kuro-obi into a living martial-arts monster, and his single arc distills everything One Piece wants to say about racism, power, and what happens when mastery curdles into cruelty.

Attested: Stoic, disciplined, and arrogant; speaks in formal martial-arts register, insists on 'proper' karate and honor while serving a cruel pirate crew. Loyal to Arlong's fish-man supremacist doctrine, openly contemptuous of humans ('lowly humans'). Sadistic in battle — toys with Sanji before attempting to kill him, mocks Sanji's chivalry and injured state. Unlike the hot-headed Chew or comic Hatchan, Kuroobi is calm and methodical until provoked, then enraged when his technique is insulted. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese kuro (黒, black) + obi (帯, belt/sash). Kuro-obi literally means 'black belt,' the rank indicating expertise/dan grade in Japanese budo (judo, karate) systematized by Jigoro Kano in the 1880s. Oda uses the term as a speaking name: the character wears a black belt and claims mastery of Fish-Ma

Pronunciation — Japanese: [kɯɾoːbi] (ku-ro-o-bi, four morae, pitch accent kuROObi). English dub: /kəˈroʊbi/ kuh-ROH-bee. Hepburn romanization: Kuroobi (long o).

Aliases — Kuroobi of the Arlong Pirates, クロオビ (Kuroobi), Black Belt (literal translation)

Ownership — Fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda, owned and published by Shueisha (Weekly Shonen Jump), licensed internationally by VIZ Media (manga) and Toei Animation/Funimation/Crunc

Powers
Fish-Man Karate (Gyojin Karate)

Mastery of Fish-Man Karate, a martial art that manipulates water in air and in the body. Kuroobi claims 40th dan (yonju-dan), highest claimed rank among Arlong officers. Techniques include water bullets and shockwaves from palm thrusts (e.g., Senmaigawara Seiken - Thousand Tile True Punch, Hyakumaigawara Seiken). Can strike through water itself, sending vibrations to hit distant targets.

Limit: Requires water or moisture in air to transmit force effectively; less effective in completely dry environments (per later Jinbe lore, not explicitly shown for Kuroobi). Reliant on physical strength and stance; can be countered by superior speed/technique (Sanji defeats him). 40-dan claim is hyperbolic and not recognized outside Arlong crew — real dan system caps at 10.

Fish-Man Physiology - Superhuman Strength and Aquatic Adaptation

As a manta ray fish-man, baseline strength ten times that of average human (Oda's stated fish-man multiplier), enhanced further by training. Can breathe underwater, swim at high speed, survive deep pressure. Durable: withstands Sanji's kicks longer than normal human. Underwater combat dominance: can grapple and drown opponents.

Limit: Still vulnerable to blunt force, cutting, and superior martial skill; Sanji's kicks eventually break his guard. Overconfidence leads to tactical errors (takes fight underwater where Sanji, despite being human, adapts). Not shown to use Fish-Man Jujutsu (water manipulation at higher level like Jinbe).

Master Grappler / Submission and Throwing Technique

Uses karate grappling, holds, and throws to restrain Sanji underwater and attempt drowning. Demonstrates formal kata and defensive blocks before shifting to brutal brawling.

Limit: Sanji's refusal to use hands (chivalric code) initially limits Sanji, but once Sanji commits to full Black Leg style, Kuroobi's grappling is overcome by speed and aerial kicks. No weapon use.

Accounts I
Origins in Fish-Man District and the Sun PiratesOne Piece manga Ch. 620-624 (Fisher Tiger flashback), Vivre Card

Fictional timeline: ~16-12 years before main story (publication 1998-2011)

Kuroobi grows up in the segregated Fish-Man District of Fish-Man Island, a ghetto of orphans and outcasts. He joins Fisher Tiger's Sun Pirates after Tiger's raid on Mary Geoise liberates fish-man slaves. Serves alongside Arlong, Hatchan, Chew, and Jinbe under the sun-mark that hides slave brands. This period establishes his loyalty to Arlong's radical separatist faction. After Tiger's death and Jinbe's succession, ideological split leads Arlong's extremists to leave the Sun Pirates. Kuroobi follows Arlong to East Blue, setting up future occupation.

The Arlong Pirates' Conquest of Conomi IslandsOne Piece manga Ch. 69-73, 77 (Nami's backstory)

Fictober: 8 years before main story (publication 1998)

After entering East Blue, Arlong's crew violently seizes Conomi Islands, establishing Arlong Park as fortress. Kuroobi serves as officer enforcing Arlong's monthly tribute (100,000 Beli per adult, 50,000 per child), destroying villages that resist. Participates in murder of Bellemere and subjugation of Cocoyasi Village, enabling Nami's forced cartography servitude. Eight-year tyranny; Nami's trauma and 100-million Beli deal to buy back village. Establishes Kuroobi as complicit in systemic extortion and racial terror, not just a fighter.

Duel at Arlong Park: Kuroobi vs. SanjiOne Piece manga Ch. 85-86 'Three Sword Style vs. Fish-Man Karate!' / Anime Ep. 42-43

Fictober: Main story, Arlong Park arc (publication 1998)

When Luffy's crew assaults Arlong Park to free Nami, Kuroobi faces Sanji (Black Leg). Kuroobi demonstrates Fish-Man Karate water projectiles and mocks Sanji's refusal to fight with hands and his chivalry. He drags Sanji underwater to drown him, exploiting fish-man aquatic advantage. Sanji, unable to breathe, uses underwater kicks and finally defeats Kuroobi with a barrage of kicks on land after escaping, proving human technique can overcome fish-man strength. Kuroobi is knocked unconscious, first officer to fall; demoralizes Arlong forces. Thematically resolves human vs. fish-man martial arts debate and Sanji's character beat (protecting hands for cooking). Arlong Park's fall follows shortly.

Accounts II
Fall of Arlong Park and Marine ArrestOne Piece manga Ch. 94-95 'The Sea of Hope'

Fictober: Immediately after duel (publication 1999)

After Luffy defeats Arlong and destroys Arlong Park, Marines under Captain Nezumi (corrupt, bribed by Arlong) arrive to claim credit, then are driven off by Luffy. Kuroobi, Hatchan, and Chew are shown defeated and bound; later confirmed captured by Marines. Cocoyasi Village is liberated. End of Arlong Pirates' East Blue reign; Kuroobi disappears from main narrative for hundreds of chapters. Establishes Marine custody as canonical fate, contradicted only by non-canon filler escapes.

Hatchan's Sea-Floor Stroll Cover Story - AftermathOne Piece manga cover story Ch. 183-195 (Hatchan's Sea-Floor Stroll)

Publication 2001-2002; fictober: months after Arlong Park

In the serialized cover mini-arc following Hatchan's post-defeat wanderings, Kuroobi appears in a flashback panel among the captured Arlong Pirates, and later Hatchan reflects on his former crewmates. The story confirms Kuroobi remains in custody / separated from Hatchan, who seeks redemption. Kuroobi does not receive individual redemption arc. Provides canonical closure that Kuroobi is not active; Hatchan's redemption contrasts with Kuroobi's unrepentant status. No further appearances in main story as of 2026.

Legacy in Fish-Man Island Arc - Ideological EchoOne Piece manga Ch. 620-648 (Fish-Man Island arc)

Publication 2011-2012; fictober: 2 years after Arlong Park (in-world)

Kuroobi does not appear physically, but Arlong's ideology — which Kuroobi enforced — is revisited through Hody Jones and the New Fish-Man Pirates, who idolize Arlong. Jinbe's exposition on Fish-Man Karate and the history of discrimination recontextualizes Kuroobi's 40-dan boast as part of a generational cycle of hatred stemming from human oppression and fish-man supremacist reaction. Retroactively deepens Kuroobi's narrative function: not just a martial-arts obstacle but a product of systemic racism. Scholarly and fan reception re-evaluates Arlong Park officers as victims/perpetrators of inherited hate.

Kuroobi's name means 'black wolf' or is unrelated to martial arts — Kuroobi is directly kuro (black) + obi (belt), meaning 'black belt.' Oda confirms via kanji pun and his black belt costume. The wolf confusion comes from kuro-okami or misreading obi as bi.

Kuroobi is the strongest fish-man or stronger than Arlong/Jinbe — Within Arlong Pirates, Arlong is strongest; Kuroobi is officer-level but defeated by East Blue Sanji, while Jinbe (later) is vastly stronger (Warlord level). Kuroobi's 40-dan does not equal combat superiority — Jinbe's mastery without numbered rank far exceeds him.

Fish-Man Karate water bullets are magic or Devil Fruit power — Fish-Man Karate is a martial art manipulating water via physical technique and fish-man physiology, not a Devil Fruit. Kuroobi has no Devil Fruit; he can swim (Devil Fruit users cannot). Later lore (Jinbe) explains it as controlling water in air and bodies.

Kuroobi appears in Netflix live-action One Piece — He does not appear in Season 1 (2023). The live-action condenses Arlong's crew to Arlong, Hatchan, and Chew, omitting Kuroobi entirely. His fight is cut.

Black belt means master, so Kuroobi must be a master in real-world terms — As the source material notes, black belt (shodan) means 'first step' — competence in basics, not mastery. Kuroobi's 40-dan parodies the Western 'black belt as master' stereotype and McDojo belt factories that guarantee rank for money/time. Real dan grades above shodan denote seniority, with 6th dan+ wearing red-white belts in judo.
