Who It Is

Deep in the Amazon, where the river meets the forest, there are whispers of a being that can slip between two worlds—a creature of the water that wears a human face, charming and dangerous in equal measure. This is the encantado, the enchanted river dolphin of Brazilian folklore, a shapeshifter whose story is as fluid as the currents it calls home.

The encantado is known for its charm and seductive nature. It is said to be an excellent dancer and musician, and it often appears at village parties to court young women. It is also known for its intelligence and cunning. However, it can be vengeful if rejected or crossed, and it may abduct people or cause illness. In some stories, it is a benevolent being that helps humans, but it is generally seen as a trickster.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'encantado' comes from Portuguese, meaning 'charmed' or 'enchanted'. In folklore context, it refers to a being that is under a spell or possesses magical qualities. The word 'boto' refers to the Amazon river dolphin (Inia geoffrensis), also known as the pink river dolphin.

Powers
Shapeshifting

The encantado can transform from a dolphin into a human form, typically a handsome man. It can also shift back at will. In human form, it often retains a distinguishing feature, such as a blowhole or a scar, which reveals its true nature.

Limit: The transformation is not perfect; the encantado must wear a hat to hide its blowhole. It also cannot speak in human form without a hoarse voice, and it may be forced to reveal its true form if its hat is removed or if it is exposed to certain conditions.

Seduction and Charm

The encantado is irresistibly charming, able to attract and seduce humans, particularly young women. It uses this charm to lure them into relationships or to take them to its underwater kingdom.

Limit: Its charm may not work on those who are aware of its true nature or who are protected by religious symbols or charms.

Control Over Water

Some stories depict the encantado as having control over rivers, including the ability to cause floods, calm waters, or create whirlpools.

Limit: This power is not consistently attributed; it may be limited to specific stories or regions.

Invisibility

In some tales, the encantado can become invisible, allowing it to move unseen among humans.

Limit: Not widely attested.

Curses and Blessings

The encantado can bestow curses or blessings on humans, depending on its mood. It may cause illness, madness, or misfortune, or grant wealth, health, and good luck.

Limit: The effects are often unpredictable.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The encantado is typically described as a handsome, well-dressed man who appears at dances and festivals in riverine communities. He is often dressed in white or elegant clothing, and wears a hat to hide the blowhole on his head. In its true form, it is a large pink river dolphin, with a long snout and a flexible neck. Some accounts describe it as having a human body but with dolphin-like features, such as a dorsal fin or a tail.

Symbols — Common symbols associated with the encantado include the pink river dolphin itself, the river (especially the Amazon and its tributaries), and the hat used to conceal its true nature. In art, it is often depicted as a man in a white suit with a hat, or as a dolphin leaping out of the water.

Domains — The encantado is primarily associated with the Amazon River and its tributaries, but it can also be found in other rivers in Brazil. It is believed to live in an underwater kingdom called the 'Encante', which is a paradise-like realm. The encantado can also appear on land, particularly at night, when it attends dances and social gatherings.

Accounts I
The Seduction at the DanceOral tradition, recorded by Candace Slater in 'The Encantado: A Study of a Brazilian Folk Character'

20th century

A young woman attends a village dance and meets a handsome, well-dressed stranger who dances with her all night. She falls in love with him and he promises to marry her. He takes her to his home, which turns out to be an underwater palace. She lives there happily for a while, but eventually she becomes homesick and returns to her village. She discovers she is pregnant, and when the child is born, it is a dolphin. The encantado returns to claim the child, but the woman's family protects it with charms. The woman is left with a child that is half-dolphin, and the encantado continues to visit, sometimes taking the child away for periods. The story warns against trusting strangers at dances.

Accounts II
The Encantado and the FishermanOral tradition, recorded by Luís da Câmara Cascudo

20th century

A fisherman rescues a dolphin that is caught in a net. The dolphin turns out to be an encantado, and in gratitude, it grants the fisherman three wishes. The fisherman wishes for a large catch, a strong boat, and a beautiful wife. The encantado grants the wishes, but the fisherman becomes greedy and asks for more. The encantado becomes angry and curses him, causing him to lose everything. The fisherman learns a lesson about greed. The story emphasizes the encantado's power to reward and punish.

The Encantado as a LoverOral tradition, collected by Nigel J. H. Smith

20th century

An encantado falls in love with a married woman and visits her every night, taking the form of her husband. The husband becomes suspicious and follows his wife one night, discovering the encantado. He attacks the encantado, who flees, but the woman is left with a child that is not her husband's. The child is born with a deformity, and the family is shunned by the community. The woman's reputation is ruined, and the child is seen as a curse. The story serves as a cautionary tale about infidelity and the dangers of the encantado.
