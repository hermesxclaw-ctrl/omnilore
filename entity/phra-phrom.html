Who It Is

Phra Phrom (พระพรหม) is the Thai Buddhist incarnation of the Hindu creator god Brahma, worshipped primarily at the famous Erawan Shrine in central Bangkok, which has become one of the most important and internationally recognized religious sites in Thailand. Unlike Brahma in India, whose worship is relatively rare compared to Vishnu and Shiva, Phra Phrom enjoys immense popular devotion in Thailand, where he is seen as a benevolent, wish-granting deity that transcends the usual categories of Buddhist and Hindu practice. His most distinctive iconographic feature is his four faces, each facing one of the four cardinal directions, symbolizing his omniscience and his capacity to hear the prayers of all beings from all directions simultaneously. He is typically depicted with four (or sometimes eight) arms, holding a scripture, a rosary, a scepter, and a water vessel. The Erawan Shrine was built in 1956 to ward off misfortunes during the construction of the Erawan Hotel (now the Grand Hyatt Erawan). Since then, it has become a major site of worship for Thais seeking blessings for business, health, and personal matters, as well as for international tourists, particularly from East Asia, who credit Phra Phrom with granting wishes. Devotees make offerings of incense, candles, jasmine garlands, and small wooden elephants, and when a wish is granted, they return to commission traditional Thai dance performances as a form of thanksgiving. Phra Phrom's worship represents the complex syncretism of Thai religious culture, where Theravada Buddhist orthodoxy coexists with Brahmanical rituals (performed at royal ceremonies), animistic spirit houses, and Chinese folk deity worship. He is invoked as a protector, a giver of prosperity, and a compassionate listener to the prayers of ordinary people—a role closer to that of a bodhisattva than the distant creator Brahma of Indian tradition.
