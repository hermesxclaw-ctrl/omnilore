Who It Is

The Kongamato ('overwhelmer of boats' or 'breaker of canoes') is a terrifying flying cryptid reported from the swamps and rivers of Central Africa, particularly Zambia, Zimbabwe, and Angola. Described as a large, reddish or black creature with leathery, bat-like wings, a beak full of sharp teeth, and a wingspan of 4 to 7 feet, the Kongamato's description strikingly resembles that of a pterosaur -- specifically a rhamphorhynchoid pterosaur with its long tail and toothed beak. Indigenous peoples regard it with genuine terror, claiming it attacks and overturns canoes to drown and devour the occupants. It is also said to dig up and consume human corpses. In 1923, British colonial administrator Frank Melland published 'In Witchbound Africa,' in which he reported that local Kaonde people immediately identified pictures of pterodactyls as the Kongamato when shown illustrations from a book. In 1956, engineer J.P.F. Brown reported seeing two creatures near Lake Bangweulu that matched the pterosaur description. A 1957 account from a hospital patient at Fort Rosebery described being attacked by a large flying creature with a long beak and wings. Skeptics propose the giant saddle-billed stork or shoebill stork as possible misidentifications, though these birds lack the leathery wings and toothed beak. The consistency of indigenous accounts across widely separated regions and their immediate recognition of pterosaur illustrations remains compelling to cryptozoologists.
