Who It Is

The Iris is the central cosmic horror entity of Gemini Home Entertainment, an analog horror series created by Remy Abode and presented as a collection of VHS tapes from a fictional educational video distributor. The Iris is introduced gradually: at first, it is simply Neptune — but something is wrong with Neptune. Educational tapes show the planet's Great Dark Spot, but it's not a storm. It's an eye. A vast, staring eye. The Iris is a sentient, predatory planet — a living world that travels through space, consuming and assimilating other celestial bodies. It entered our solar system and has been slowly, methodically corrupting everything it touches. Its influence manifests on Earth through several vectors: the Woodcrawlers, the Deep Root Disease, and Nature's Mockery are all extensions of its will. The Iris communicates through its 'Mockery' creations — humanoid entities that deliver distorted messages about the Iris's arrival. The ultimate horror of the Iris is its scale. This is not a monster you can fight or hide from; it is a planet. It is bigger than Earth. Its arrival is an extinction event occurring in slow motion, and the educational VHS tapes — meant to inform and protect — instead document humanity's helplessness in the face of an adversary so vast that comprehension itself is a form of contamination. The Iris draws on Lovecraftian cosmic horror but filters it through the medium of 1980s-90s educational television: calm narrators describing the end of the world in the same tone they'd use for a documentary about whales. This contrast between medium and message — a friendly science video about something that wants to eat the Earth — is Gemini Home Entertainment's signature contribution to the analog horror genre.
