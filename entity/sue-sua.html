Who They Are

Sué is the Muisca sun god, the primary male deity of the Muisca pantheon and the husband of Chía (the moon goddess). He governs the day, the warmth that makes crops grow, and the solar year that regulates Muisca agriculture. His temple at Suamox (modern Sogamoso) was the most sacred site in the Muisca Confederation — the 'Rome of the Muisca' — and was guarded by the most powerful priests in the realm.

Cultural Significance

The Temple of the Sun at Sogamoso was the most important religious structure in the Muisca world — a national sanctuary that united the various Muisca chiefdoms under a single cult. When the Spanish conquistadors under Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada arrived in 1537, they burned the temple to the ground, destroying the golden statue of Sué and scattering the sacred relics. The site remains one of the most important Muisca archaeological zones in Colombia. The Muisca solar calendar, organized around Sué's annual cycle, divided the year into 20 months of roughly 18 days each — an intricate system that governed planting, harvesting, and festival dates.
