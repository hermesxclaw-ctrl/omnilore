Who It Is

Hwanin is the supreme deity of the Korean founding myth, the Lord of Heaven who presides over the celestial realm and grants authority to those who descend to govern the earthly domain. In the Dangun myth as recorded in the Samguk Yusa, Hwanin is the father of Hwanung. When Hwanung repeatedly expressed his desire to descend to the human world to bring order and civilization, Hwanin ultimately granted permission. He surveyed the earth from heaven, selected Mount Taebaek as the optimal location for his son's earthly mission, and provided Hwanung with the three Cheonbuin (heavenly seals or talismans), which symbolized divine mandate and the authority to rule. These three seals—represented as a bronze sword, a bronze mirror, and a set of bronze bells in some interpretations—conferred legitimate sovereignty and the ability to communicate between heaven and earth. Hwanin's role in Korean mythology is primarily cosmological: he represents the ultimate source of divine authority, the celestial father whose sanction transforms mortal rule into sacred kingship. While he rarely appears directly in narrative beyond the Dangun myth, his position at the apex of the divine hierarchy establishes the theological framework of early Korean state religion. Some scholars identify Hwanin with the Indo-European sky-father deity archetype (cognate with Dyaus Pita, Zeus, and Jupiter), suggesting ancient influences on Korean mythology through Inner Asian cultural exchange. In contemporary Korean new religious movements, particularly Daejonggyo, Hwanin has been elevated to the status of a supreme trinitarian godhead alongside Hwanung and Dangun.
