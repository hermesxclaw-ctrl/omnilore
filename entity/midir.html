Who It Is

Midir is a fascinating figure in Irish mythology, with a complex and intriguing story. This dossier provides an in-depth look at Midir's life and relationships, and explores the themes of love, power, and the complexities of the sidhe.

Midir is described as a cunning and magical figure, able to use his powers to win the love of a beautiful princess. He is also associated with the theme of love and relationships, as seen in the story of Midir and Etain.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Midir is described as a son of the Dagda, a powerful figure in Irish mythology. He is associated with the sidhe of Brí Léith and is said to have lived there after the Tuatha Dé Déanann were defeated by the Milesians. As a member of the Tuatha Dé Déanann, Midir would have been a supernatural being with magical powers.

Symbols — The symbol most associated with Midir is the sidhe of Brí Léith, a mystical and otherworldly realm. The sidhe is often depicted as a beautiful and enchanting place, but also as a place of great power and danger.

Domains — Midir is associated with the sidhe of Brí Léith, a mystical and otherworldly realm. He is also said to have lived in the mortal world, but his primary domain is the sidhe.

Mythological Cycle

Midir and Etain fall in love, but their relationship is complicated by the fact that Etain is already married to Midir's brother. The story explores the themes of love, relationships, and the complexities of the sidhe.
