Who It Is

The character of Father Dagon / Mother Hydra is a fascinating and complex figure, with a rich history and a deep connection to the Cthulhu Mythos. This dossier provides a comprehensive overview of the character's origins, powers, and relationships, as well as his role in the Cthulhu Mythos.

Father Dagon is typically portrayed as a malevolent deity who demands worship and sacrifice from his followers, while Mother Hydra is seen as a mysterious and enigmatic figure, often associated with the sea and its secrets.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Dagon' is derived from the Akkadian deity Dagon, a fish-god worshipped in ancient Mesopotamia. The association with Mother Hydra is a later development, drawing from Greek mythology.

Pronunciation — DAH-gon / HY-druh

Aliases — Dagon, Mot, Hydra, The Deep Ones' Patron

Ownership — The concept of Father Dagon / Mother Hydra is a product of Western horror fiction, specifically the Cthulhu Mythos created by H.P. Lovecraft and his contemporaries.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Father Dagon is often depicted as a massive, tentacled creature with a humanoid torso, while Mother Hydra is shown as a serpentine being with multiple heads. In some accounts, they are described as having a humanoid form with fish-like features.

Symbols — The symbol associated with Father Dagon is the trident, while Mother Hydra is often linked to the image of a serpent or a coiled dragon.

Domains — The domains of Father Dagon / Mother Hydra are the oceans and the underwater city of Y'ha-nthlei, where the Deep Ones reside.

1928

The story follows a group of investigators as they uncover the existence of Father Dagon and the Deep Ones, leading to a confrontation with the ancient deity. The story concludes with the investigators realizing that Father Dagon is a powerful and malevolent force that cannot be defeated.
