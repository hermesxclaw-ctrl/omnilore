Who It Is

Colt Vahn wakes up drunk on a black-sand beach with a gun in his hand, a headache that feels like yesterday, and a woman on the radio who has already killed him today. He has been murdered by the same assassin for what might be decades — and he cannot remember why he volunteered for any of it.

Attested personality via voiced dialogue (Jason E. Kelley): wry, profane, weary, and self-aware, frequently breaking fourth wall to comment on video-game loop tropes. Unlike silent protagonists in Arkane's Dishonored, Colt is talkative, jokes under stress, and expresses doubt, fear and moral fatigue. He is determined to break the loop but also nostalgic and guilt-ridden about his past role. Powers
Reprise (Slab)

Temporal rewind that allows Colt to die and resurrect twice per loop (three lives total). On death, time rewinds seconds, restoring Colt nearby. Narratively explained as Residuum-infused slab harvested from the anomaly; mechanically core to roguelite loop.

Limit: Only two rewinds per loop; third death sends Colt back to morning beach and resets day. Reprise does not prevent loop reset; does not restore Residuum spent. Lore: Reprise was originally Julianna's slab, gifted/traded? Dialogue suggests Colt acquired it early and it stabilizes his memory.

Residuum Infusion and Recall

Ability to infuse weapons, slabs and trinkets with Residuum to retain them across loops. Also underpins Colt's gradually stabilizing memory — Residuum exposure allows retention of information (codes, Visionary locations).

Limit: Requires harvesting Residuum from objects and Visionaries; limited Residuum per loop; infused items lost if not re-infused after sacrifice.

Shift (Slab, optional)

Short-range teleport/dash, similar to Dishonored's Blink. Acquired from Charlie Montague or Egor Serling.

Limit: Range-limited, requires line of sight for extended upgrade, cooldown, Residuum cost to infuse.

Aether (Slab, optional)

Near-invisibility, dampens sound and visibility. Acquired from Egor Serling.

Limit: Drains power, movement breaks stealth at higher speed, does not hide Residuum glow.

Karnesis (Slab, optional)

Telekinesis — lift and throw enemies/objects. Acquired from Aleksis Dorsey.

Limit: Requires targeting, limited lift mass without upgrades, loud.

Havoc (Slab, optional)

Damage amplification and explosive recoil, with damage resistance. Acquired from Fia Zborowska.

Limit: Short duration, cooldown, broadcasts position.

Nexus (Slab, optional)

Links enemies so damage/effects propagate between them. Acquired from Harriet Morse.

Limit: Requires line of sight to link, breaks on distance, limited number of links without upgrade.

Peak Human Combatant and AEON Security Training

Non-supernatural abilities: expert marksman, hand-to-hand combat, infiltration, knowledge of Blackreef's security systems and AEON protocols from his tenure as Head of Security. Attested via dialogue and dossiers.

Limit: Ages, tires, vulnerable to gunfire and slabs without Havoc/Aether; suffers hangovers and psychological fatigue.

Appearance — Colt Vahn is depicted as a Black man in his late 30s to early 40s, athletic build, shaved head with short beard/stubble, brown eyes. His signature outfit is a weathered brown leather bomber jacket with shearling collar over a dark shirt, fitted trousers, boots, and fingerless gloves. He wears a distinctive prosthetic or augmented left arm brace (interpreted as Residuum-infused gear) and carries AEON-issued weapons. Early concept art explored more

Accounts I
The AEON Program and the AnomalyDeathloop — In-game documents: Egor Serling's anomaly research, Wenjie Evans lab notes, AEON recruitment posters; Bakaba interviews 2021

Fictional 1960s-?; game released 2021

Scientist Egor Serling discovers a temporal anomaly around Blackreef that causes a single day to repeat. With Wenjie Evans and financier Aleksis Dorsey, he founds the AEON Program, promising eternal life through an endless party within the loop. Colt Vahn, a former military pilot and later Head of Security for AEON, initially believes in the project and helps establish security. He becomes disillusioned after witnessing the moral decay and violence of the loop and after his own memory fragments, leading him to seek to break it. Creation of the time loop that traps all inhabitants; Colt's initial complicity and later rebellion drive the entire plot.

Waking on the Beach — The First LoopDeathloop — Opening cinematic and tutorial (Morning, Updaam Beach)

Loop Day, indefinite repetition; game 2021

Every loop begins with Colt waking hungover on the black-sand beach below Updaam, a pistol nearby, with no memory of how he arrived. A radio message from Julianna Blake taunts him and warns she will hunt him. Colt discovers instructions he wrote to himself and learns the loop's rules: kill eight Visionaries in one day before midnight or the loop resets. He learns he retains knowledge via Residuum and his Reprise slab. Establishes core gameplay loop and Colt's amnesiac-but-learning arc; introduces player to Residuum infusion and Visionary assassination puzzle.

Hunting the Eight VisionariesDeathloop — Core campaign missions across four districts and four time periods

Loop Day 2021 (fictional)

To break the loop, Colt must assassinate all eight Visionaries — the AEON leaders who sustain the anomaly — in a single loop by manipulating their schedules: Aleksis Dorsey (party at Updaam night), Wenjie Evans (lab in The Complex), Egor Serling (invisible at The Complex), Charlie Montague (conditioned game in Updaam), Fia Zborowska and Charlie together (Fristad Rock), Frank Spicer (radio station in Karl's Bay), and Harriet Morse (hangar in Karl's Bay). Colt solves temporal puzzles to align their locations, using slabs and Residuum. Each kill yields Residuum and slabs. Each Visionary's death destabilizes the loop; collecting all slabs makes final confrontation possible. Reveals each Visionary's decadence and complicity.

Accounts II
Julianna Blake — Daughter and RivalDeathloop — Late-game revelations: Colt's apartment, Julianna's diary, final confrontation at Updaam; Bakaba interview IGN 2021-09-09

Loop Day, revealed gradually

Through notes and dialogue, Colt learns Julianna is his daughter with Lila Blake, conceived before the loop. Julianna remembers every loop (like Colt after Reprise stabilizes) and has chosen to protect the loop, believing eternity is freedom; Colt wants to end it to give her a real future. Their relationship oscillates between lethal antagonism and reluctant familial love. Julianna invades Colt's loop to kill him, embodying both antagonist and protector. Reframes the central conflict from hunter vs. assassin to father vs. daughter with opposing philosophies about time, memory and freedom. Determines endings.

The Three Endings — Break, Remain, or Walk AwayDeathloop — Final mission 'The Longest Day' (Updaam evening), three player choices

Loop Day midnight

After killing seven Visionaries, Colt confronts Julianna at the rocket pad. He can: (1) Break the Loop — spare Julianna, both sit and watch the loop break as time moves forward (canon-leaning ending, Colt wakes on beach with Julianna, uncertain future); (2) Remain in the Loop — kill Julianna and take her place, sitting alone as loop resets, preserving eternity; (3) Walk Away — kill Julianna but refuse to break loop, resetting. Goldenloop update adds extended dialogue where Colt and Julianna discuss Lila and the outside world if spared. Determines fate of Blackreef and Colt's character arc — self-sacrifice and paternal reconciliation vs. nihilistic preservation of the party.

Colt as Former Head of Security — The Betrayal MemoryDeathloop — Colt's memories, Wenjie's logs, Egor's logs, Karl's Bay bunker

Pre-loop and early loops (flashbacks)

Recovered memories reveal Colt was not always prisoner: he was AEON's Head of Security, complicit in luring people to Blackreef and enforcing the loop. He attempted to break the loop early, killing Wenjie and others, but was killed by Julianna and lost memory due to loop trauma. His present quest is thus both redemption and repetition — he has tried to break the loop many times before, writing notes to himself that he keeps forgetting and rediscovering. Establishes Colt as unreliable narrator and morally gray protagonist; explains why Eternalists recognize him and why Julianna is weary of his attempts.
