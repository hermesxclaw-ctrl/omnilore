Who It Is

Iblis is the chief devil in Islamic theology, the leader of the rebellious jinn who refused to prostrate before Adam when commanded by Allah. According to the Quran, Iblis was created from smokeless fire ('samum') and was among the jinn (not a fallen angel, as in Christian tradition). When Allah created Adam from clay and commanded all beings to bow, Iblis refused, arguing that fire is superior to clay. For his pride and disobedience, he was cursed and cast out, but he begged for respite until the Day of Judgment, swearing to lead humanity astray from the Straight Path. Allah granted this respite, making Iblis the ultimate tempter. Unlike the Christian Satan, Iblis is not a cosmic adversary of God but an instrument of divine purpose — he is allowed to tempt, but he cannot compel; his power is limited to whispers (waswasa). The name 'Iblis' derives from the Arabic root b-l-s (to despair, to be cut off from mercy), meaning 'the Despairing One.' He is also called 'Shaitan' (Satan, from the root sh-t-n, meaning to be distant or to burn). In Islamic folk tradition, Iblis appears in many forms — an old man, a serpent, a stranger — and commands legions of shayatin (devils) and ifrit. Despite his rebellion, Iblis is a tragic figure in some Sufi interpretations: he loved God so absolutely that he would not bow to anyone other than God, even on God's own command — making him either the worst sinner or the most perfect monotheist, depending on the perspective.
