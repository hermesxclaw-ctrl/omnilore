Who It Is

In the mists of Ainu tradition, a being of smoke and flame descends from the heavens to teach humanity the arts of civilization—weaving, carving, and the sacred songs of the oyna. This is Ae-oyna-kamuy, the 'god we pass on in our lore,' a culture hero whose deeds echo through the forests of Hokkaido and beyond, yet whose very identity blurs with other divine figures in a tapestry of myth and time.

A culture hero and benefactor of humanity, teaching skills and fighting for humans. In the provided myth, he is brave, strong, and protective, but also grows old and departs. He is respectful of divine beasts (venerates the giant deer).

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Ae-oyna-kamuy: 'god whom we pass on (in our lore)' or 'god concerning whom we sing the oyna' (Kindaichi, Philippi). The prefix 'ae-' means 'we' (inclusive). 'Oyna' refers to the sacred tradition or a genre of epic songs. Alternative interpretation by Chiri: 'god who engages in shamanism'. Ainurakkur

Pronunciation — Ae-oyna-kamuy: /a.e oi.na ka.muj/ (approximate). Oyna-kamuy: /oi.na ka.muj/. Ainurakkur: /ai.nu.rak.kur/. Okikurmi: /o.ki.kur.mi/.

Aliases — Oyna-kamuy, Ayoyna-kamuy, Ainurakkur, Okikurmi, Okikirmiy

Ownership — Ainu people of Hokkaido (Japan), and also Sakhalin and the Kuril Islands. The figure is a pan-Ainu culture hero, with variations across different Ainu regions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Described as a large man wreathed in smoke. When the smoke parts, he is seen to be surrounded by flames from his waist to his feet, and wearing a coat of elm bark and a sword. He also wields a magical spear of mugwort. The flames indicate his virtuous character.

Symbols — Elm bark coat, sword, spear of mugwort (yomogi), flames, snowshoes (from which he creates deer and fish). Associated with lightning (as a manifestation).

Domains — Heaven (where he descends from), Earth (where he teaches and fights), the Underworld (where he rescues the Swan Princess). He is associated with Mount Oputateshike (in one origin myth).

Origin and Teaching of HumanityGeneral Ainu mythology; specific versions from Shiunkot village and others

Unknown (mythological time)

Ae-oyna-kamuy is born of a divine parent and an elm tree (or is the son of the sun or thunder). He is taught by the hearth deity Kamuy-huci and descends from the heavens to teach humans weaving, carving, fishing, hunting, gathering, architecture, medicine, religious ritual, law, and singing. Humans gain civilization and skills.

Destruction of Famine and Creation of GameGeneral mythology

Unknown

Ae-oyna-kamuy fights a personification of famine and destroys it with his spear of mugwort. Then he creates deer and fish from the snow on his snowshoes. Humanity is saved from famine and provided with game.

Accounts II
Departure and Return AttemptGeneral mythology

Unknown

Disappointed at the decline of the Ainu, Ae-oyna-kamuy departs for another country or returns to the heavens. In one myth, when he returns to the heavens, the gods send him back because he reeks of humans. He then leaves his clothes on earth to return. His old sandals turn into the first squirrels. He leaves the human world, but squirrels are created.

The Detailed Myth: Birth, Battles, and DepartureThe provided source material (likely from a modern retelling or a specific regional tradition)

Unknown (mythological time)

Aynurakkur is born from the union of the thunder deity Kannakamuy and Princess Chikisani, the spirit of the Japanese elm. His mother ascends to heaven via a thunderbolt, falls, and burns for six days, becoming charcoal but providing eternal warmth. Tokapchkamuy (solar deity) becomes his guardian. As he grows, he plays with human children and creates tools. At 16, he is tasked to lead humanity and is destined to marry Swan Princess Retacchir. He defeats a giant deer (a divine test), but the sorceress Uesoyoma kidnaps the princess and blinds him. After recovering, he descends to the Underworld, massacres its inhabitants, and rescues the princess. He marries her, grows old, and departs to a far land, but promises to return as lightning. Humanity is protected, the Underworld is destroyed, and Aynurakkur departs, leaving lightning as a sign of his presence.
