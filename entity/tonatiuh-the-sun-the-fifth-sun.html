Tonatiuh (the Sun / the Fifth Sun)

also known asthe Sun / the Fifth Sun
Typegod (the sun, the current cosmic age)
Who It Is
Form

Tonatiuh is typically depicted as a fierce, red-painted figure wearing an eagle headdress. He is often shown with a solar disk on his chest, and his face is usually contorted in a fierce expression. In some representations, Tonatiuh is depicted with a bow and arrow, symbolizing his role as a hunter and warrior. The Aztecs believed that Tonatiuh's appearance was a manifestation of his power and authority, and that his red paint symbolized the life-giving energy of the sun. Tonatiuh's eagle headdress, meanwhile, represented his connection to the sky and the heavens.

Powers

Solar Energy Tonatiuh has the power to control the sun's energy, using it to nourish and sustain life on earth. He is said to be able to summon the sun's power to aid him in battle, and to use its energy to heal and protect his followers.

Accounts

According to Aztec mythology, Tonatiuh was the fifth sun in a series of creations, each of which had ended in catastrophe. The story tells of how the gods gathered to create a new sun, and how Tonatiuh was chosen to be the new solar deity. The mythological context of this story is essential to understanding the significance of Tonatiuh in Aztec mythology, and highlights the complexities and nuances of the Aztec creation myth.

According to Aztec mythology, Tonatiuh is responsible for the daily journey of the sun across the sky. The story tells of how Tonatiuh rises each morning in the east, and travels across the sky to set in the west. The mythological context of this story is essential to understanding the significance of Tonatiuh in Aztec mythology, and highlights the complexities and nuances of the Aztec solar myth.
