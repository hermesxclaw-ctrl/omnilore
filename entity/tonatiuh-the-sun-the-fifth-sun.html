Who It Is

Tonatiuh ('He Who Goes Forth Shining') is the sun-god of the fifth and final cosmic age — the current era (Nahui-Ollin, 4-Movement) that will eventually be destroyed by earthquakes. According to the Teotihuacan creation myth, after four previous suns had ended in disaster, the gods gathered at the ruined city to sacrifice themselves to create a new sun. The humble, pustule-covered god Nanahuatzin ('the Blemished One') bravely threw himself into the fire and emerged as Tonatiuh — the magnificent but demanding sun of the present age. The wealthy god Tecuciztecatl ('Lord of the Snails') hesitated but followed, emerging as a diminished second sun (the moon). Tonatiuh's face — the calendar date 4-Movement with a protruding tongue (the obsidian knife of sacrifice) and eagle talons — appears in the center of the Aztec Calendar Stone, the most iconic artifact of Aztec cosmology. Tonatiuh's daily journey from east to zenith required the 'precious water' (chalchihuatl) of human hearts; without it, the sun would falter and the fifth age would end in darkness. The 'flowery death' (xochimiquiztli) of warriors on the battlefield or on the sacrifice-stone was therefore a cosmic necessity — the fuel of the sun.
