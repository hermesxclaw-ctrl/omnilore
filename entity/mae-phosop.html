The rice in your bowl is a gift from Mae Phosop. Treat it with respect — never waste a single grain, for it is her body.

Who It Is

Mae Phosop is the Thai rice goddess, the maternal spirit who dwells in the rice fields and protects the grain from field to table. She is central to the agricultural cycle of Thailand — farmers perform rituals at every stage of rice cultivation to honor and appease her. At planting time, offerings of flowers, incense, and boiled eggs are made to call Mae Phosop's spirit to the field. Before harvest, a ceremony called 'Awakening Mae Phosop' invites her spirit to allow the rice to be cut. After threshing, the first grains are offered to her in gratitude. The belief is that if Mae Phosop is displeased, the harvest will be poor, pests will infest the fields, or the rice will spoil in storage. She is depicted as a beautiful woman with golden skin, dressed in a traditional sarong, holding a sheaf of rice. The tradition has Buddhist syncretic elements — monks may lead the ceremonies. Although modernization has reduced the number of farmers, Mae Phosop rituals persist in rural Thailand and have become part of national cultural heritage. The similar Lao rice goddess is called Mae Khaukhao or Nang Khosop, and in Cambodia, Neang Kakey serves a parallel role. Mae Phosop represents the deep spiritual relationship between the Thai people and rice, the cornerstone of their civilization.
