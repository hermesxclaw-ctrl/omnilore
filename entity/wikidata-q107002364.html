Identity

Inori

Etymology

Inori (祈り) is a Japanese word meaning 'prayer' or 'supplication'. It is derived from the verb 'inoru' (祈る), meaning 'to pray'. The kanji 祈 combines the radical 示 (shimesu, meaning 'to show' or 'altar') with 斤 (kin, meaning 'axe'), possibly symbolizing a ritual act. As a given name, it is written in hiragana as いのり or in kanji as 祈り, though other kanji combinations are possible.

Description

Appearance

As a name and word, Inori has no inherent physical appearance. The kanji 祈り is visually composed of the radical 示 (altar) and 斤 (axe), which together evoke ritual and offering. In hiragana, it is written as いのり, a flowing script. The characters associated with the name vary widely in appearance, from human to anthropomorphic.

Symbols & Iconography

The kanji 祈 is a symbol of prayer and spiritual devotion in Japanese culture. It appears in religious contexts, such as ema (votive tablets) and shrine rituals. As a given name, it conveys a sense of hope, faith, or a wish for the child's future.

Behaviour & Personality

As a name, Inori does not have a personality. However, the word's meaning of 'prayer' suggests qualities such as devotion, hope, and sincerity. Individuals and characters named Inori often exhibit these traits; for example, Inori Yuzuriha is calm and introspective, while Inori Yamabuki is energetic and caring.

Domains & Habitats

The name Inori is used in Japan and among Japanese-speaking communities. It appears in real life, anime, manga, video games, and music. The word 'inori' is used in religious and spiritual contexts, such as Shinto and Buddhist practices.

Version Differences

There is no single 'original' version of Inori. The name has been used for various unrelated individuals and characters, each with their own distinct traits and stories. The word itself has a consistent meaning, but its usage as a name is flexible and can be written with different kanji or in hiragana.
