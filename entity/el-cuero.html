Who It Is

El Cuero ('the leather' or 'the hide') is one of South America's most bizarre aquatic cryptids. Unlike the classic plesiosaur-shaped lake monster, El Cuero is described as a flat, leathery, cowhide-like creature that floats on the surface of remote Patagonian lakes, resembling a discarded animal skin. When unsuspecting prey -- animals or humans wading, swimming, or boating -- approach, the creature wraps its membranous body around the victim like a living blanket, drags it underwater, and consumes it by enveloping the prey and digesting it through its skin. The creature is said to have eyes on the upper surface of its 'blanket' and a sucker-like mouth or multiple mouths along its underside. Its edges are described as sharp or clawed, capable of gripping. The Mapuche people consider El Cuero an extremely dangerous water spirit -- a predator that can be repelled by loud noises or by throwing branches of the calafate bush onto the water (the plant supposedly burns it). Some cryptozoologists have noted its resemblance to a giant freshwater stingray or a previously unknown freshwater cephalopod. Others suggest it is a folkloric explanation for whirlpools, underwater currents, or large lake trout dragging fishing lines. The creature's unique morphology -- a living, predatory hide -- sets it apart from stereotypical lake monsters and connects it to a broader South American folklore tradition of blanket-like water monsters (similar to the Bolivian 'Bufeo Colorado' and the Brazilian 'Hueque').
