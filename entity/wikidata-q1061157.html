Who It Is

From the banks of the Euphrates to the candlelit altars of Renaissance Europe, love magic has been a persistent and potent force in human culture—a way to bend desire, secure affection, or break a heart, using spells, amulets, and rituals that blur the line between sacred and profane.

Love magic is performed with intent to start, preserve, or break up a relationship. Practitioners may be men or women, clergy or laity. The practice is often secretive and may involve rituals, incantations, and the use of physical objects.

Powers
Erotic attraction (eros)

Spells to induce lust or sexual desire in a target, often used by men or prostitutes.

Limit: Often considered aggressive and may cause harm to the caster.

Affection and fidelity (philia)

Spells to create or maintain affection, love, or friendship, often used by women to keep husbands faithful.

Limit: Considered non-aggressive, but may still involve compulsion.

Relationship preservation or dissolution

Magic to start, preserve, or break up a relationship, as defined by scholars.

Limit: Believed to cause severe damage to the caster; often expensive.

Use of material components

Dolls, talismans, amulets, potions, written spells, and rituals are used.

Limit: Requires access to materials and knowledge of rituals.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Love magic itself has no appearance; its material components include dolls, talismans, amulets, scrolls, potions, and other objects, which vary by culture and period.

Symbols — Common symbols include hearts, arrows, cords, knots, and images of the desired person. In Renaissance Europe, Christian elements like holy candles and the Host were incorporated.

Domains — Love magic is practiced in domestic settings, temples, churches, and other sacred spaces. It is found in both urban and rural contexts across cultures.

Accounts I
Heracles' death by DeianeiraGreek mythology, as referenced in the source material

Mythic

Deianeira, Heracles' wife, uses a love charm (the blood of the centaur Nessus) to try to regain Heracles' love, but it is actually a poison that kills him. Heracles dies; Deianeira commits suicide in remorse.

Love spells in the Greek Magical PapyriGreek Magical Papyri

2nd century BCE - 3rd century CE

Numerous spells for erotic attraction and compulsion, often invoking deities and using ritual objects. Believed to compel love or lust.

Renaissance love magic practicesHistorical records and trial documents

Renaissance

Love magic was used to overcome social and economic barriers to marriage, often incorporating Christian elements like holy candles and the Host. Could lead to social advancement or severe penalties if discovered.
