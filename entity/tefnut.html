Who It Is

To the Heliopolitan priests who first recited the Pyramid Texts, the entire cosmos began the moment their self-created sun-god sneezed — and out of his nose and mouth emerged the first pair, brother and sister, dry breeze and damp life: Shu and Tefnut. But the goddess who carries half the world's weather in her leonine form is no passive first-born. In her wrath she can burn Egypt dry from the sky and stalk Nubia as a cat that kills any being who dares approach, until only the scribe-god Thoth can sweet-talk her home. Moisture and scorch, first daughter and queen-mother of her own son — Tefnut is the cosmic hinge of Egyptian creation who breaks the world when she is angry and restores it when persuaded back.

Tefnut displays a double temperament typical of the Eye-of-Ra goddesses: a cooling, moist, life-giving, purifying face (dew, rain, the 'pure waters' she produces, the wabet purification she grants the Heliopolitan priest) set against a wrathful, scorching, destructive face. As a lioness she could 'display a wrathful aspect' and in her flagship story she storms off to Nubia in a rage — jealous of her grandchildren's higher worship — becoming a cat that 'destroyed any man or god that approached'. Powers
Moisture / dew / rain generation

Tefnut is the feminine counterpart of the air god Shu and is the archetypal moisture goddess: Egyptian sources link her to dew, rain, water, and fertility. A Pyramid Texts passage describes her producing pure waters from her vagina, and Egyptologists read her role by parallelism with Shu's dry breeze.

Limit: Her moisture function is inferred partly from parallelism and dedicatory epithets rather than a single controlling myth; the article notes her 'mythological function is less clear than that of Shu.'

The fiery Eye of Ra

Tefnut is one of the leonine goddesses who can be the Eye of Ra — the feminine, protective-and-vengeful projection of the sun god that scorches his enemies. As eye-goddess she is 'mistress of the flame,' adorned with a flame as protection against enemies, and her wrathful lioness aspect is the dangerous face of solar heat.

Limit: She does not monopolize the Eye — the goddess behind the mask changes by text and temple; her eye-role is one register among several.

Purification of the Heliopolitan priest (wabet)

At Heliopolis Tefnut participates in the temple rite that purifies the wabet (priest): 'I have passed by Tefnut, even while Tefnut was purifying me.' She grants ritual purity and legitimacy to the priesthood, framing her benevolence as cultic and institutional.

Limit: Ritual-specific — belongs to the Heliopolis sanctuary called the Lower Menset.

Rage, drought and the destructive cat

In her flagship withdrawal myth Tefnut's rage has ecological force: when she storms off to Nubia and becomes a cat that destroys 'any man or god that approached,' her absence from Egypt withholds moisture — drought, sterility, and ruin — until her negotiated return. She thus wields cosmic leverage over weather and fertility through her own departure.

Limit: The destructive power is primarily a withdrawal/absence effect rather than a weapon aimed deliberately.

Shape-shifting / leonine transformation

Tefnut manifests in multiple animal and hybrid forms: full lioness, lion-headed woman, lion-headed serpent, human woman, and (in paired iconography) a double-headed form shared with Shu on collar counterpoises. In the withdrawal myth she changes into a cat.

Limit: Her forms are iconic rather than a hard 'power set'; the cat form belongs to the Shu/Geb-cycle retelling.

Geb-consort queenship (cosmological succession)

In the Book-of-the-Heavenly-Cow / Geb register, Tefnut becomes chief queen-consort of her son Geb after Geb challenges Shu's leadership — an element of the divine-succession cosmology where Tefnut grounds Geb's claim to kingship.

Limit: Does not confer sovereignty itself; Tefnut is the legitimizing consort in a patrilineal succession frame.

Accounts I
Sneeze and Spit: The Birth of Shu and TefnutPyramid Texts UT 527; Coffin Texts; Bremner-Rind Papyrus; Memphite Theology

Old Kingdom through Ptolemaic copies

The self-created primordial god Atum-Ra gives rise to the first divine couple. In one account he sneezes in Heliopolis and 'brother and sister were born—that is Shu and Tefnut.' In another he spits saliva — a pun on the tef sound ('to spit') embedded in Tefnut's name. The Coffin Texts refine the imagery: Shu is sneezed from the nose, Tefnut spat from the mouth. Tefnut is in every version the product of parthenogenesis (no female partner for Atum) generated through a bodily fluid. Establishes Shu and Tefnut as the first pair of cosmic elements, ancestors of the whole Ennead — parents of Geb and Nut, grandparents of Osiris, Isis, Set, Nephthys, and (in some versions) Horus the Elder, and great-grandparents of Horus the Younger.

The Nubian Flight and the Return of the Distant GoddessShu article (Old-Kingdom weather-disaster register) + Tefnut article's anger motif

Late Old Kingdom onward (an interpreted climate-disaster memory)

Tefnut quarrels with Shu (or, in the Tefnut article's framing, grows wrathful jealous of her grandchildren's higher worship) and leaves Egypt for temperate Nubia. As she withdraws, moisture recedes and the land suffers drought. Shu quickly realizes he misses her, but she has changed into a cat that destroys any man or god who approaches. Only the god Thoth — often disguised — succeeds in persuading her to return, granting her the title 'honorable' and drawing the lifegiving moisture (and the king's cult) back to Egypt. Restoration of order, moisture, and fertility; the resolution anchors the eye-goddess cycle in which a far-off, angry female deity must be recalled and appeased by a negotiator.

Geb's Reprisal: The Son Who Takes His Mother as QueenGeb article; Book of the Heavenly Cow register

New Kingdom cosmological succession texts

After Shu forcibly separates his children Geb (earth) and Nut (sky), Geb rises against his father. In a mirror of what Shu did to him — separating Geb from his sister-wife Nut — Geb takes Shu's own wife Tefnut as his chief queen-consort. This act of incestuous reprisal unseats Shu from his primacy in the divine family and installs Geb as the heir of the departing sun god, a step in the generational succession toward Osiris. Geb replaces Shu at the head of the cosmological lineage; the 'Book of the Heavenly Cow' implies Geb's inheritance, and Geb later passes the throne to Osiris before becoming a judge in the Divine Tribunal.
