Who It Is

Macha is a fascinating and complex goddess who plays a significant role in Irish mythology.

Macha is often depicted as a strong-willed and determined goddess, who is not afraid to assert her power and authority. She is said to be fiercely protective of her people and her land, and will stop at nothing to defend them.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Macha is often depicted as a powerful and fierce goddess, associated with the land, fertility, kingship, war, and horses. She is said to be one of three sisters known as 'the three Morrígna'. Her appearance is often described as majestic and awe-inspiring.

Symbols — Macha is associated with the symbols of the land, fertility, and war, including the horse, the sword, and the crown. Her iconography often features her riding a horse, wielding a sword, or wearing a crown.

Domains — Macha is associated with the province of Ulster, particularly the sites of Navan Fort and Armagh, which are named after her. She is said to be the goddess of sovereignty, and is often invoked in rituals and ceremonies related to kingship and fertility.

The Táin Bó Cuailnge is an ancient Irish epic that tells the story of Macha and her role in the battle between the armies of Ulster and the armies of Queen Medb of Connacht. The battle between the armies of Ulster and the armies of Queen Medb of Connacht has significant consequences for the people of Ulster and the kingdom of Connacht.
