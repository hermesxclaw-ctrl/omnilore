The Half-Being, Shaqq, The Split One

I leap on one leg across the sand, calling your name in your mother's voice. Half a body, half a face, half a soul — and all hunger. The desert made me, and the desert keeps me.

Who It Is

The Nasnas (also Shaqq) is one of the strangest creatures in Arabian folklore — a being that is literally half a human. According to medieval Arabic sources such as Al-Qazwini's 'Aja'ib al-Makhluqat' (Wonders of Creation), the Nasnas is a creature with one arm, one leg, half a head, half a body, and one eye — moving by leaping great distances on its single leg. It is said to be the offspring of a Shaqq (a human-like jinn) and a human, or a degenerate descendant of the pre-Adamic races that inhabited the earth before humanity. The Nasnas inhabits the remote deserts of Yemen and Hadhramaut, where it leaps upon lone travellers and kills them, often by crushing them with its single powerful limb. It can speak Arabic and sometimes calls out to travellers by name, luring them by mimicking a familiar voice. Al-Jahiz (9th century) mentions the Nasnas in his 'Kitab al-Hayawan' (Book of Animals) as a creature that the Arabs of the desert claim to have encountered. The Nasnas is related to the broader Islamic concept of jinn and pre-Adamic beings, but is distinguished by its bizarre, incomplete morphology — it is a creature of division and half-ness, embodying the horror of the incomplete. The Nasnas also connects to the concept of the Shiqq, a jinn type that manifests as half a human and is considered one of the more dangerous jinn forms. The Nasnas represents the terror of the empty desert — the place where the normal laws of God's creation break down, where half-things hop through the sand, and where the lone traveller is most vulnerable.
