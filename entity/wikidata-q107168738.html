The Approach

In the spring of 2001, a chestnut colt with a physicist's name and a sprinter's heart tore through Japan's racing elite, winning all four of his starts with a different tactic each time—then vanished from the track as suddenly as he had arrived, leaving behind a legacy that would reshape the breed.

Etymology

Named after tachyons, hypothetical particles that travel faster than light.

Japanese Thoroughbred racing

Disputed Identifications

Description

Appearance

Chestnut colt, typical Thoroughbred conformation. No distinctive markings are recorded in available sources.

Symbols & Iconography

In the Umamusume franchise, the character is depicted with a lab coat and scientific motifs, reflecting her mad-scientist personality.

Behaviour & Personality

On the track, Agnes Tachyon showed versatility and a strong will, capable of leading or closing from behind. Off the track, he was known for his fragile legs, which ultimately ended his career. In Umamusume, the character is portrayed as an eccentric and obsessive scientist.

Domains & Habitats

Trained in Japan, raced on turf tracks. Stud at Shadai Stallion Station in Hokkaido.

Version Differences

The real horse is distinct from the fictional Umamusume character. The character is an anthropomorphized version with exaggerated traits, not a direct historical representation.
