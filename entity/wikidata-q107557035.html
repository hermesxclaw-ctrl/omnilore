The Approach

Tohru is a name that echoes across very different worlds: a shapeshifting dragon from Japanese mythology, a beloved anime heroine, and a figure in modern fiction. But which Tohru does the archive hold? This dossier untangles the threads, documenting what is known and what remains uncertain.

Content advisory. None. The subject is a fictional character; no dark material is documented in available sources.
Identity

Tohru

Pronunciation TOH-roo (Japanese: [toːɾɯ]; English approximations vary)

Etymology

The name Tohru (トール, Tōru) is a common Japanese given name. It can be written with various kanji, such as 徹 (to penetrate, clear), 透 (transparent, to pass through), or 亨 (to receive, to prosper). The name is also used as a transliteration of the Norse god Thor in some Japanese contexts, though the spelling differs.

Cultural Ownership

Japan (primarily); also used in international media as a character name.

Disputed Identifications

The name is shared by multiple fictional characters. Without a specific source, it is unclear which character is intended. The most prominent are Tohru Honda from the manga 'Fruits Basket' and Tohru Kobayashi from 'Miss Kobayashi's Dragon Maid'.

Description

Appearance

Depends on the character. Tohru Honda: a teenage girl with brown hair and gentle features. Tohru Kobayashi: a dragon in her true form, but takes on a human form as a maid with long blonde hair, horns, and a tail.

Symbols & Iconography

Tohru Honda is often associated with the zodiac animals, particularly the dog, and with kindness. Tohru Kobayashi is associated with dragons, fire, and maid outfits.

Behaviour & Personality

Tohru Honda: kind, selfless, optimistic, and determined. Tohru Kobayashi: energetic, devoted, possessive, and occasionally mischievous.

Domains & Habitats

Tohru Honda: modern-day Japan, primarily her school and home. Tohru Kobayashi: the home of Kobayashi, her human companion, in modern Japan.

Version Differences

The two characters are entirely distinct, with no shared traits beyond the name.

Fruits Basket (Tohru Honda)

Source Manga by Natsuki TakayaCulture JapanesePeriod 1998-2006

Summary

Tohru Honda, an orphaned high school girl, lives in a tent after her mother's death. She is discovered by the Sohma family, who are cursed to transform into animals of the Chinese zodiac when hugged by the opposite sex. Tohru becomes their housekeeper and helps them overcome their trauma.
