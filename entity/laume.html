Laumė

The Field Fairy, The White Woman, The Child-Spirit

Leave bread by the bathhouse for Laumė, and she will bless your children; leave your house unclean, and she will leave a changeling in the cradle.

Who It Is

Laumė is a type of nature spirit in Baltic mythology, sometimes considered a single entity and sometimes a class of beings. She appears as a beautiful naked woman with long flowing hair, but her back may be hollow or her feet turned backwards. Laumės are connected to fertility, spinning, and the wilderness. They help women during childbirth (acting as midwives), but they are quick to anger and easily offended. If a woman leaves her spinning unfinished overnight, Laumė will finish it — but with such malicious perfection that the spinner will curse her own work. Laumės are known for swapping human babies with their own sickly changelings. They live in bathhouses, barns, and remote forest clearings. They bathe in lakes at night, and anyone who steals their clothing can force them to grant wishes. Laumės are neither wholly good nor evil — they enforce the rules of domestic order and treat kind humans with generosity but punish laziness, disrespect, and uncleanliness harshly. Offerings of bread, salt, and white cloth were left for Laumės at the edges of fields. In Christian times, they were often conflated with demons, but their essential protective and fertility-bringing nature ensured that rituals for Laumė persisted in rural Lithuania and Latvia well into the 20th century.
