Who It Is

Oshun (also spelled Osun, Ochún, or Oxum) is the Yoruba orisha of sweet water, rivers, love, beauty, fertility, diplomacy, and sensuality. She is the embodiment of the feminine principle of charm and grace, governing the life-giving waters that sustain agriculture and communities along West Africa's rivers. Her name derives from the Osun River in present-day Osun State, Nigeria, where her principal shrine is located in the sacred Osun-Osogbo Grove — a UNESCO World Heritage Site that hosts the annual Osun-Osogbo festival, drawing thousands of devotees and visitors each August.

Physically, Oshun is depicted as an extraordinarily beautiful woman, often dressed in yellow or gold — her sacred color — and adorned with brass jewelry, beads, and mirrors. She is associated with honey (her epithet is 'the owner of honey'), and her sweet, flowing nature mirrors the gentle rivers she governs. In Yoruba art and Afro-Caribbean traditions, she is shown with a hand mirror (representing self-reflection and beauty), a fan, and sometimes holding an infant, emphasizing her role as a mother and protector of children. She is the youngest of the orishas and, in some traditions, the only female among the original 17 orishas sent by Olodumare to create the world.

Oshun's most famous myth explains why she is essential to creation. When Olodumare (the supreme deity) sent the orishas to create and populate the world, they failed repeatedly. Displeased, Olodumare withheld the rains. The male orishas realized they could not succeed without Oshun, who had been excluded. They humbled themselves and begged her to join them. Oshun agreed, and her sweet waters restored fertility to the earth, allowing creation to proceed. This myth establishes her as not merely beautiful but indispensable — the force without which even the most powerful gods cannot function.

Beyond love and beauty, Oshun is a formidable diplomat and strategist. In several myths, she outwits or charms more powerful entities to achieve her goals. She is also known for her fierce protectiveness — she can become as destructive as a flood when wronged. In Santería, she is syncretized with Our Lady of Charity (La Virgen de la Caridad del Cobre), the patroness of Cuba. Her sacred symbols include the peacock, the vulture, and the river stone. Devotees offer her honey, pumpkins, oranges, cinnamon, and yellow flowers, and her sacred number is five.
