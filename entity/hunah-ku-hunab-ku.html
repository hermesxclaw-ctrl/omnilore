Who It Is

In the deep well of Maya cosmology, before the first dawn broke over the maize fields, there was a name whispered in the dark: Hunah Ku, the One God, the single heartbeat of creation. But was this a primordial deity of the ancient Maya, or a colonial invention—a native echo of the Spanish God forced onto the stones of Chichen Itza? The answer, like the god himself, remains half in shadow, half in light.

Described as a supreme creator, distinct from the many other gods. In colonial sources, he is portrayed as a single, all-powerful deity, but his personality is not elaborated. He is more a theological concept than a character.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is typically parsed from Yucatec Maya as 'hun' (one) and 'ku' (god), yielding 'One God' or 'Only God.' Alternative interpretations suggest 'hunab' may relate to 'hun' (one) and 'ab' (measure or unit), possibly meaning 'the measure of the cosmos' or 'the single source.'

Pronunciation — Approximately 'hoo-NAH koo' (Yucatec Maya: /huˈnaʔ ku/), with 'Hunab' sometimes pronounced 'hoo-NAHB'.

Aliases — Hunab Ku

Powers
Creation of the universe

According to Diego de Landa, Hunab Ku was the creator of all things. This is the only power explicitly attributed to him in colonial sources.

Limit: No specific limitations are recorded. The power is only mentioned in passing.

Supreme deity status

Landa describes him as 'the one living and true God,' implying omnipotence and omniscience, but these attributes are not elaborated.

Limit: This may be a projection of Christian concepts onto Maya beliefs.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No described appearance. Hunah Ku is generally considered a remote, abstract deity without iconography or physical form. No known depictions exist.

Symbols — None known. Unlike other Maya gods, Hunah Ku is not associated with any animal, object, or glyph.

Domains — Heavenly or celestial realm; not tied to any earthly location. He is a creator god, but his specific domains (e.g., creation, sky) are not clearly delineated.

Hunah Ku was the supreme god of the ancient Maya. — The ancient Maya were polytheistic, and there is no evidence that a single supreme god existed. Hunah Ku is only attested in colonial sources, and his portrayal as a 'one god' likely reflects Christian influence.

Hunah Ku is the same as Hunahpu, one of the Hero Twins. — Hunahpu is a hero in the Popol Vuh, with a distinct name and mythology. Hunah Ku is a creator deity mentioned only in colonial Yucatec sources. The similarity in spelling has led to confusion.

Hunab Ku is a symbol of cosmic unity in ancient Maya cosmology. — The concept of 'Hunab Ku' as a cosmic unity is a modern New Age interpretation, not supported by historical evidence. The term's meaning in colonial texts is debated, and it may simply mean 'one god' in a Christian context.
