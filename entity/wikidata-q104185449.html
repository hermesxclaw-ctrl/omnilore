Who It Is

In a quiet corner of the literary imagination, there exists a figure whose name has become synonymous with loyalty, service, and the blurred line between companionship and servitude. Friday, the faithful companion of Robinson Crusoe, steps out of the pages of Daniel Defoe's 1719 novel not merely as a supporting character, but as a mirror reflecting the complexities of colonialism, race, and human connection. His story, though often overshadowed by his master's, is a tale of survival, adaptation, and the enduring question of what it means to be 'civilized.'

Friday is depicted as loyal, grateful, and eager to learn. He quickly adopts Crusoe's customs and language, and he repeatedly expresses his devotion, saying 'O master! you die? no, no, I die, you no die.' He is also resourceful and brave, as shown when he helps Crusoe fight cannibals and later when he is sent to rescue others. He shows curiosity and a desire to understand Christianity, but he also retains some of his own beliefs, leading to theological discussions with Crusoe. Powers
Survival and hunting skills

Friday is skilled in hunting, fishing, and navigating the wilderness, as he demonstrates when he kills a bear and helps Crusoe hunt. He is also knowledgeable about local plants and animals.

Limit: None specified.

Language acquisition

Friday learns English quickly, and within a few years he can converse fluently. He also teaches Crusoe some of his own language, but these words are not recorded.

Limit: His English is initially broken and simplistic.

Loyalty and service

Friday's primary 'power' is his unwavering loyalty to Crusoe, which is so strong that he would risk his life for his master. This is depicted as a moral virtue, but also as a product of his 'savage' simplicity.

Limit: His loyalty is often seen as a form of subservience, limiting his autonomy.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Friday is described by Crusoe as 'a comely, handsome fellow, perfectly well made, with straight strong limbs, not too large, tall and well shap'd, and, as I reckon, about twenty six years of age.' He has a 'very good countenance, not a fierce and surly aspect, but seem'd to have something very manly in his face, and yet he had all the sweetness and softness of an European in his countenance too, especially when he smil'd.' Symbols — Friday is often depicted in illustrations and adaptations as a young, bare-chested man with a loincloth, carrying a spear or bow. He is frequently shown kneeling before Crusoe, symbolizing his submission. In modern iconography, he is sometimes portrayed as a native American or Pacific Islander, though the text is ambiguous.

Domains — Friday's original home is not explicitly named, but it is implied to be an island in the Caribbean or South America. After his rescue, he lives on Crusoe's island, then travels to England and later to the Pyrenees, where he helps fight off wolves.

Accounts I
The Rescue of FridayDaniel Defoe, 'Robinson Crusoe', 1719

17th century (set), 18th century (written)

Crusoe spots a group of cannibals on his island who are preparing to kill one of their captives. He shoots the pursuers and rescues the victim, who then kneels and shows gratitude. Crusoe names him Friday after the day of his rescue. Friday becomes Crusoe's servant and companion. Friday becomes Crusoe's loyal servant and helps him in various tasks. He also provides information about the mainland and the presence of other Europeans (the Spanish shipwreck survivors).

Accounts II
The Battle with the CannibalsDaniel Defoe, 'Robinson Crusoe', 1719

17th century (set), 18th century (written)

Crusoe and Friday discover that a group of cannibals has landed on the island with several captives. They attack, killing many cannibals and rescuing a Spaniard and Friday's father, who had been a captive. Friday is overjoyed to be reunited with his father. The group expands, and they plan to escape the island. Friday's father and the Spaniard are sent to the mainland to negotiate with other Spanish survivors.

The Journey to England and the PyreneesDaniel Defoe, 'Robinson Crusoe', 1719

17th century (set), 18th century (written)

After being rescued by an English ship, Crusoe and Friday travel to England. Later, they journey across the Pyrenees, where they are attacked by wolves. Friday shows bravery and skill in fighting off the wolves. They reach England safely, and Friday remains with Crusoe as his servant.
