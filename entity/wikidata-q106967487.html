The Approach

A name that drifts through mist and memory: Kasumi is at once a Japanese given name, a vanished coastal town, a cipher that guards mobile conversations, and a technique that unbalances an opponent in a single breath. This dossier traces the many faces of 'Kasumi' across geography, technology, martial arts, and fiction.

Content advisory. None of the subjects associated with 'Kasumi' contain disturbing material. Some fictional portrayals may involve combat or mild peril typical of their genres.

Etymology

The Japanese word 'kasumi' (霞) means 'mist' or 'haze'. It is composed of the radicals for 'rain' and 'false', evoking the fleeting, obscuring quality of mist. The name is also used for places and people, symbolizing beauty and transience.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily Japanese, with the name and its associated concepts rooted in Japanese language and culture. The village in Bosnia and the KASUMI cipher are unrelated to the Japanese etymology.

Disputed Identifications

There are multiple distinct subjects named 'Kasumi', and confusion can arise between them. The fictional character Kasumi from the Dead or Alive game series is not listed in the source material but is a well-known namesake. The manga series 'Kasumi' by Surt Lim and Hirofumi Sugimoto is separate from any other 'Kasumi' media.

Description

Appearance

No single appearance applies to all 'Kasumi' subjects. The town of Kasumi, Hyōgo, is a coastal area known for its scenic views. Kasumigaseki is a modern district with government buildings. The village in Bosnia has a rural appearance. The given name is used for people, and the destroyers were naval vessels. The cipher is abstract.

Symbols & Iconography

The kanji 霞 (mist) is a common symbol. For the town, the sea and mountains are iconic. Kasumigaseki is associated with the Japanese government. The destroyers bore the name as a symbol of the Imperial Japanese Navy.

Behaviour & Personality

Not applicable to most subjects. For the given name, it is often associated with grace and subtlety. The judo technique is a specific physical action.

Domains & Habitats

The town and district are in Japan; the village in Bosnia. The destroyers operated in naval theaters. The cipher exists in digital networks. The manga and other fictional works exist in their respective fictional worlds.

Version Differences

The various 'Kasumi' subjects are entirely distinct; there is no single entity to have versions. However, the name appears in multiple fictional contexts (e.g., Dead or Alive character, manga series) with different characteristics.

Powers & Abilities

KASUMI (block cipher)

Source Version 3GPP specification

Description

A symmetric key block cipher used in 3GPP mobile communications to encrypt and decrypt data. It operates on 64-bit blocks with a 128-bit key.

Limitations

Designed for specific mobile network protocols; not a general-purpose cipher.

Contradictions

None noted.

Kasumi (judo technique)

Source Version Traditional judo and Danzan-ryu

Description

A technique in Danzan-ryu and Kodokan judo that involves unbalancing an opponent with a twisting or misdirecting motion, akin to a 'mist' that obscures the opponent's perception.

Limitations

Requires precise timing and leverage; not a standalone throw but a setup.

Contradictions

Some sources may describe it differently across schools.

The Town of Kasumi, Hyōgo

Source Historical records and local historyCulture JapanesePeriod 20th century

Summary

Kasumi was a town in Hyōgo Prefecture, known for its fishing and scenic beauty. In 2005, it merged with other municipalities to form the city of Kami.

Participants

Consequences

The town ceased to exist as an independent entity, but its name lives on in the region.

The Imperial Japanese Destroyers Named Kasumi

Source Naval history recordsCulture JapanesePeriod Early 20th century

Summary

Two destroyers of the Imperial Japanese Navy bore the name 'Kasumi'. The first was an Asakaze-class destroyer from 1905, and the second was an Asashio-class destroyer from 1937 that served in World War II and was sunk in 1945.

Participants

Imperial Japanese Navy

Crews of the destroyers

Consequences

The second Kasumi played a role in the Battle of Leyte Gulf and was ultimately sunk.
