Name & Tongue

Etymology — The Japanese word 'kasumi' (霞) means 'mist' or 'haze'. It is composed of the radicals for 'rain' and 'false', evoking the fleeting, obscuring quality of mist. The name is also used for places and people, symbolizing beauty and transience.

Pronunciation — Kah-soo-mee (IPA: /käsɯmi/).

Aliases — Kasumigaseki (霞が関), KASUMI (cipher), Kasumi (given name), Kasumi (destroyer), Kasumi (manga), Kasumi (judo technique)

Ownership — Primarily Japanese, with the name and its associated concepts rooted in Japanese language and culture. The village in Bosnia and the KASUMI cipher are unrelated to the Japanese ety

Powers
KASUMI (block cipher)

A symmetric key block cipher used in 3GPP mobile communications to encrypt and decrypt data. It operates on 64-bit blocks with a 128-bit key.

Limit: Designed for specific mobile network protocols; not a general-purpose cipher.

Kasumi (judo technique)

A technique in Danzan-ryu and Kodokan judo that involves unbalancing an opponent with a twisting or misdirecting motion, akin to a 'mist' that obscures the opponent's perception.

Limit: Requires precise timing and leverage; not a standalone throw but a setup.

Accounts I
The Town of Kasumi, HyōgoHistorical records and local history

20th century

Kasumi was a town in Hyōgo Prefecture, known for its fishing and scenic beauty. In 2005, it merged with other municipalities to form the city of Kami. The town ceased to exist as an independent entity, but its name lives on in the region.

Accounts II
Kasumigaseki: The Gate of MistHistorical and modern records

Edo period to present

Kasumigaseki is a district in Tokyo that has become the center of Japanese government, housing many ministries. The name 'Gate of Mist' evokes an old gate in the area. It is now synonymous with the Japanese bureaucracy.

The Imperial Japanese Destroyers Named KasumiNaval history records

Early 20th century

Two destroyers of the Imperial Japanese Navy bore the name 'Kasumi'. The first was an Asakaze-class destroyer from 1905, and the second was an Asashio-class destroyer from 1937 that served in World War II and was sunk in 1945. The second Kasumi played a role in the Battle of Leyte Gulf and was ultimately sunk.
