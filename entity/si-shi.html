Who They Are

Si is the Moon Goddess, the supreme female deity of the Moche religion. The Moche worshipped the moon as more powerful than the sun because the moon controlled the ocean tides, storms, the seasons, and the cycles of agricultural fertility. Si was the mother of the pantheon and the ruler of the night, and her waxing and waning directly influenced Moche daily life.

Cultural Significance

Si's temple — Huaca de la Luna — is one of the largest adobe structures ever built in the Americas, standing over 30 meters (100 feet) tall and containing millions of mud bricks. The temple's extensive murals include depictions of Si, Ai Apaec, and the Sacrifice Ceremony. The Moche's lunar-centric cosmology was unique among major ancient civilizations, where sun gods typically held supremacy. The Moche observation of lunar cycles produced one of the most accurate lunar calendars in the ancient world. Her cult influenced later Andean moon goddesses, including the Inca Mama Quilla.
