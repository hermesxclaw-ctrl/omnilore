Who It Is

She arrives with a suitcase too big for her body, a smile too bright for a rainy town, and a single, earnest misunderstanding: she thought the Rabbit House was literally full of rabbits. Cocoa Hoto doesn't have magic powers or a tragic prophecy — she has something rarer in modern moe fiction: an inexhaustible, almost heroic optimism that slowly rewires the quiet, wood-scented world around her.

Attested personality: relentlessly optimistic, affectionate, slightly airheaded but socially intuitive. Self-identifies as Chino's older sister and embraces a caretaker role. Loves making bread (aspiring baker), often overestimates her skill. Cheerful to the point of obliviousness, quick to hug, prone to daydreams and malapropisms. Values found family, hard work, and making others smile. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Named after the beverage hot cocoa (Japanese: ココア, kokoa). All main characters in 'Is the Order a Rabbit?' are named after drinks: Chino (cappuccino), Rize (Thé des Alizés), Chiya (Uji tea), Syaro (Kilimanjaro coffee). Creator Koi confirmed the drink-naming motif in afterwords and interviews. 'Hoto'

Pronunciation — Japanese: /ko.ko.a ho.to/ (ココア・ホト), Hepburn: Kokoa Hoto. English fandom: /ˈkoʊkoʊ ˈhoʊtoʊ/ KOH-koh HOH-toh. Commonly shortened to 'Cocoa'.

Aliases — Cocoa, Kokoa Hoto, Hot Cocoa, Kokoa-chan, Nee-chan (by Chino Kafū)

Ownership — Japanese manga/anime franchise created by Koi (pen name), serialized in Houbunsha's Manga Time Kirara Max (seinen, 4-koma). Owned and published by Houbunsha; anime licensed by NBCU

Powers
Baking and Bread-Making (Aspiring Baker)

Cocoa's defining skill and ambition is baking bread, taught by her mother and older sister Mocha. She frequently bakes for Rabbit House, school events, and friends, though results vary from delicious to comically misshapen. Attested in manga Vol.1 Ch.3, Vol.2 Ch.8, anime S1 Ep.3, S2 Ep.7.

Limit: Not professionally trained at start; failures due to over-enthusiasm, misreading recipes, or distraction. Improves over time but still depicted as learning. No supernatural baking.

Emotional Support and Social Catalysis

Unusually high emotional intelligence for her age; able to befriend reserved characters (Chino, Rize, Syaro) quickly, defuse tension with humor, and act as 'older sister' confidante. Described in official guide as 'mood maker'.

Limit: Cannot solve serious trauma; her optimism sometimes annoys or overwhelms Chino. Effectiveness depends on recipient's willingness.

Physical Energy and Stamina

Depicted as highly energetic, able to work long café shifts, carry heavy luggage, run across town, and maintain enthusiasm. Comedic feats include carrying Chino piggyback and rapid bread-kneading.

Limit: Normal human limits; tires, gets sick (S1 Ep.8 cold episode), no superhuman strength.

Rabbit Handling (Tippy Affinity)

Unique rapport with Tippy, the angora rabbit who is also Chino's grandfather's spirit. Cocoa can balance Tippy on her head for extended periods and is one of few characters Tippy tolerates affectionately.

Limit: No control over Tippy's speech (only Chino and Rize's father can hear him clearly in early lore); Cocoa hears Tippy as a normal rabbit initially.

Academic and Practical Improvisation

Average student; excels at home economics and crafts, struggles with math. Shows improvisational skill in café service, latte art (attempted), and event planning.

Limit: Not academically gifted; relies on Rize and Chiya for study help.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Cocoa Hoto is depicted as a 15-16-year-old girl (first-year high school at start, later second-year) with shoulder-length, slightly wavy copper-auburn hair, often tied in a side ponytail with a large red bow, and hazel-brown eyes. Attested design by Koi: pale skin, average height, warm color palette (reds, creams, browns evoking cocoa). Outfit: Rabbit House uniform (white blouse, brown waistcoat, long red skirt, white apron in some art) and casua

Symbols — Motifs: hot cocoa cups, bread loaves, rabbits (especially Tippy), hearts and sparkles denoting enthusiasm. Color association: warm brown/red. Rabbit House signage and angora rabbit Tippy on her head are recurring iconography. In merchandise, she is paired with cocoa mugs and bakery imagery. No religious or folkloric symbolism; commercial moe iconography of 'genki older sister' archetype.

Domains — Primary habitat: Rabbit House (ラビットハウス), a combined café and boarding house in an unnamed European-inspired Japanese town (modeled loosely on Colmar, France in anime backgrounds). Lives on second floor as a boarder while attending high school in town. Secondary habitats: high school, Ama Usa An (Chiya's café), Fleur de Lapin (Syaro's café), town square, bakery workshops. Cosmology is mundane contemporary Japan with stylized European arc

Accounts I
Arrival at Rabbit House — 'The Girl Who Thought Rabbits Lived There'Koi, Gochuumon wa Usagi Desu ka? Vol.1 Ch.1; Anime S1 Ep.1

2011 (manga debut), 2014 (anime)

Cocoa arrives in a new town to attend high school and boards at Rabbit House, which she imagined would be literally full of rabbits. She meets the quiet, reserved Chino Kafū and the angora rabbit Tippy, who sits on Chino's head. Despite initial awkwardness, Cocoa's earnest declaration that she wants to be Chino's older sister breaks the ice. She is hired as a part-time waitress. Establishes premise, Cocoa's found-family role, and Rabbit House as central setting. Defines core dynamic: Cocoa as surrogate sister to Chino.

Becoming a Sister — The Hot Cocoa PromiseManga Vol.1 Ch.2-4; Anime S1 Ep.2

2011-2012

Cocoa struggles to be useful at the café, burning toast and misreading orders, but insists on learning. Chino, who initially finds Cocoa overbearing, gradually accepts her after Cocoa stays up to practice latte art and defends Chino from embarrassment at school. Cocoa formally becomes 'Nee-chan' to Chino. Solidifies sister bond that drives series; establishes Cocoa's perseverance despite lack of skill.

The Bread of the Day — Cocoa's Bakery DreamManga Vol.2 Ch.8, Vol.4 Ch.15; Anime S1 Ep.3, S2 Ep.7

2012-2015

Cocoa enters an informal bread-baking contest against Chiya and Syaro's cafés. Her signature cocoa-flavored bread is initially too sweet and dense, but with advice from Mocha (via letter) and Rize's discipline, she produces a well-received loaf for the town festival. She declares her future dream: to open her own bakery with Chino. Reveals long-term ambition beyond café work; deepens friendship group; recurring bakery motif in later volumes.

Accounts II
Sister Mocha Visits — The Older Sister Becomes the YoungerManga Vol.6 Ch.1-3; Anime S2 Ep.11-12, OVA 'Dear My Sister' (2017)

2015-2017

Cocoa's charismatic older sister Mocha Hoto visits town, revealing Cocoa's home life and why Cocoa idolizes her. Mocha is even more affectionate and teasing, instantly bonding with Chino and making Cocoa jealous. Cocoa briefly feels insecure about not being as reliable as Mocha, but Chino reassures her that Cocoa is her one and only sister. Provides backstory for Hoto family (bakery family), explains Cocoa's sister complex, and deepens Chino-Cocoa bond. Mocha becomes recurring character.

The Rabbit House Secret — Tippy's True IdentityManga Vol.3 Ch.18; Anime S1 Ep.6, S1 Ep.12

2013-2014

Cocoa learns that Tippy is not just a rabbit but houses the spirit of Chino's late grandfather, the founder of Rabbit House. While Chino and others treat this as normal, Cocoa is initially shocked, then accepts it with characteristic warmth, talking to Tippy as 'Grandpa' and helping Chino preserve his memory during the café's anniversary. Adds gentle supernatural element to otherwise mundane series; explains Rabbit House lore and Chino's reserved nature.

Graduation and the Future — Bloom ArcManga Vol.8-10; Anime Season 3: BLOOM Ep.10-12 (2020)

2019-2020

As Rize and Chiya prepare for graduation, Cocoa confronts her own future. She considers whether to return home or stay in town to pursue bakery school. In BLOOM's finale, the group stages a farewell festival, and Cocoa decides to continue working at Rabbit House while studying baking, affirming that the town is now her second home. Provides coming-of-age resolution; cements found-family theme; leaves door open for continuation.

Cocoa is named after the programming framework Cocoa (Apple API) — Character name derives from the drink hot cocoa, part of series-wide drink pun naming. No connection to Apple Cocoa API, which is named after the cocoa bean. Conflation arises from identical spelling.

Cocoa is Chino's actual blood sister — Cocoa is a boarder who self-appoints as older sister; no blood relation. Mocha is her only canonical sibling. The sister bond is found-family, not biological.

Is the Order a Rabbit? is set in Europe, so Cocoa is European — Setting is a fictional Japanese town with European-inspired architecture (modeled on Colmar for anime backgrounds) but characters are Japanese, attend Japanese school, and speak Japanese. Official materials list them as Japanese high school girls.

Cocoa has magical powers or is a rabbit herself — No supernatural abilities. Only Tippy has supernatural element. Cocoa is entirely human; rabbit motifs are thematic and comedic, not literal.
