Who It Is

When Phoenix Wright steps into a courtroom, the evidence is never as straightforward as it seems. With his spiky blue hair, his unwavering faith in his clients, and his habit of pointing dramatically while shouting 'Objection!', this defense attorney has become a symbol of justice in a world where the truth is often buried under layers of deceit. But behind the confident facade lies a man shaped by tragedy, rivalry, and an unshakable belief that every person deserves a fair trial.

Phoenix is characterized by his unwavering belief in his clients, his sharp intuition, and his ability to uncover contradictions in witness testimony. He is often laid-back and informal outside the courtroom, but becomes intensely focused during trials. He is also known for his occasional moments of panic or confusion, which add comedic relief. He values truth and justice above all, and is willing to take on seemingly hopeless cases. He is fiercely loyal to his friends and mentors, and he often acts as a father figure to his adopted daughter, Trucy.

Powers
Psycho-Lock (Psyche-Lock)

Phoenix can see magical locks that appear when a witness is hiding something. By presenting evidence and asking the right questions, he can break these locks and force the truth out.

Limit: Only works when Phoenix has the 'Magatama', a spiritual item. It does not reveal the exact nature of the secret, only that a secret exists.

Perceive (from Apollo Justice)

In later games, Phoenix can perceive nervous habits or 'tells' in witnesses, allowing him to detect lies. This ability is more associated with Apollo Justice, but Phoenix also demonstrates it in 'Dual Destinies'.

Limit: Requires careful observation and may be misled by red herrings.

Unwavering Dedication

Phoenix possesses an almost superhuman determination to defend his clients, even when the evidence seems overwhelmingly against them. This drives him to uncover the truth and achieve 'not guilty' verdicts.

Limit: Can lead to personal danger and emotional strain.

Objection!

Phoenix's signature exclamation, which he uses to halt proceedings and present crucial evidence or challenge testimony. It is often accompanied by a dramatic pose.

Limit: Only effective in a courtroom setting.

He typically wears a sharp blue suit with a white shirt and a red tie, though his tie color changes to a darker red or maroon in later games. He also wears a black 'attorney's badge' on his lapel. Symbols — Phoenix is symbolized by his spiky hair, his attorney's badge, and his iconic 'Objection!' pose, where he points his index finger forward with his other hand on his hip. His catchphrase 'Objection!' is often rendered in large, stylized text. In the courtroom, he is often shown with a determined expression, and his sprite animations include dramatic finger-points and table slams.

Accounts I
The Turnabout Begins (Case 1-1)Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney (2001)

Contemporary setting, early 2000s

Phoenix takes on his first case, defending his childhood friend Larry Butz, who is accused of murdering his girlfriend. Despite overwhelming evidence, Phoenix uncovers the true culprit, the prosecutor's assistant, and secures a 'not guilty' verdict. Phoenix gains confidence and begins his career as a defense attorney.

Turnabout Sisters (Case 1-2)Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney (2001)

Contemporary setting

Phoenix's mentor Mia Fey is murdered, and her younger sister Maya is accused. Phoenix takes the case, uncovering the involvement of the corrupt prosecutor Miles Edgeworth and the true culprit, Redd White. This case establishes Phoenix's rivalry with Edgeworth and his bond with Maya. Phoenix becomes Maya's legal guardian and continues to fight for justice.

Turnabout Goodbyes (Case 1-4)Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney (2001)

Contemporary setting

Miles Edgeworth is accused of murder, and Phoenix defends him despite their rivalry. Phoenix uncovers a 15-year-old secret involving a former prosecutor, Manfred von Karma, and a case that traumatized Edgeworth. Phoenix proves Edgeworth's innocence and helps him confront his past. Edgeworth begins to reform and becomes a more just prosecutor.

Accounts II
Farewell, My Turnabout (Case 2-4)Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney - Justice for All (2002)

Contemporary setting

Phoenix is forced to defend a client who is actually guilty, under threat of harm to Maya. He must find a way to secure a 'not guilty' verdict without letting a true criminal go free. This case tests his ethics and resolve. Phoenix manages to expose the truth and save Maya, but the experience leaves him shaken.

Bridge to the Turnabout (Case 3-5)Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney - Trials and Tribulations (2004)

Contemporary setting

Phoenix defends Maya Fey in a case involving a locked-room murder at a mountain temple. The case reveals the dark history of the Fey family and the identity of the true mastermind, Dahlia Hawthorne, who had previously manipulated Phoenix's mentor Mia. Phoenix's past romance with Dahlia is revealed, and he ultimately proves his worth as an attorney. Phoenix fully embraces his role as a defender of truth, and the Fey family's curse is broken.

The Disbarment and ReturnApollo Justice: Ace Attorney (2007) and subsequent games

Seven years after the original trilogy

In 'Apollo Justice', Phoenix is disbarred after presenting forged evidence in a case, forced to work as a pianist. He later returns as a mentor to Apollo Justice and eventually regains his attorney's badge. In 'Dual Destinies', he returns to the courtroom and confronts new threats, including a terrorist bombing and a corrupt legal system. Phoenix becomes a more seasoned lawyer, balancing his role as a father and mentor.

The Khura'in IncidentPhoenix Wright: Ace Attorney - Spirit of Justice (2016)

After the events of Dual Destinies

Phoenix travels to the Kingdom of Khura'in, where defense attorneys are reviled and trials are rigged. He defends a princess accused of murder and becomes embroiled in a political conspiracy. He uses his skills to bring justice to a country that has forgotten the meaning of a fair trial. Phoenix helps topple a corrupt regime and restores faith in the legal system.
