None of them are related — except by four letters that keep colliding on maps, letterheads, and laboratory gates.

Aeronautics and Wind-Tunnel Testing (NASA Ames)

Operates the world's largest wind tunnel complex (National Full-Scale Aerodynamic Complex, 80x120 ft and 40x80 ft), arc-jet facilities, and flight simulators. Historically developed blunt-body reentry theory, lifting-body designs, and rotorcraft research.

Limit: Facilities are ground-test and simulation; not a launch center. Research output depends on federal appropriations and inter-center competition. Many wind tunnels mothballed or demolished after 2000s due to costs.

Materials Science and Rare-Earth Separation (Ames National Laboratory)

Pioneered Frank Spedding's ion-exchange process for purifying uranium and rare-earth elements for the Manhattan Project. Today DOE leader in metallurgy, critical materials, and Ames Laboratory-developed lead-free solder.

Limit: Laboratory is research-only; does not produce weapons. Rare-earth expertise is scientific, not mining - U.S. still imports most rare earths. Work is unclassified now but originated in classified wartime program.

Industrial Blade and Tool Manufacture (Ames Manufacturing Company)

Mass-produced U.S. Model 1840 cavalry sabers, artillery swords, and later tools/cutlery. At peak, largest sword maker in the U.S., supplying Union Army in Civil War. Later Ames Sword Company and Ames True Temper lineage for shovels and garden tools.

Limit: Sword production ceased after 1865 demand collapse; company shifted to tools, then absorbed. Quality varied by contract period; early swords criticized for brittleness.

Mass-Market Discount Retailing (Ames Department Stores)

At peak operated ~700 discount stores in Northeast/Midwest, with distribution network and private-label brands. Known for acquiring Zayre (1988) and Hills (1998).

Limit: Business model failed against Walmart/Target logistics; filed Chapter 11 in 1990, 1992, and 2001; liquidated 2002. No stores remain.

Radar System Nomenclature and Early Warning (AMES Types)

AMES (Air Ministry Experimental Station) numbering system designated British WWII radar: AMES Type 1 (Chain Home), Type 7 (GCI), Type 80, etc. Basis for RAF radar deployment in Battle of Britain.

Limit: AMES was a designation system, not a single radar set. Performance limited by 1930s electronics, line-of-sight, and jamming vulnerability.

Accounts I
The Railroad Town Named for a Congressman: Founding of Ames, IowaHistory of Story County, Iowa (1884); William H. Farwell, History of Ames, Iowa (1890); City of Ames Historic Preservation records

1864-1870

In 1864 the Cedar Rapids and Missouri River Railroad, building west toward Council Bluffs, platted a station near the Skunk River at the 35th mile. The town was named Ames after Oakes Ames (1804-1873), Massachusetts congressman and major shareholder in the Union Pacific's Credit Mobilier, who had influence over railroad financing. The original plat was filed May 7, 1864. Early growth was slow until the Iowa Agricultural College (now Iowa State University) was located there in 1868-1869 after Story County offered land. The college and railroad together transformed a marshy siding into a permanent city, incorporated 1870. Established the most populous 'Ames' in the world (pop. ~66,000 in 2020 Census). Created permanent town-gown structure where university is largest employer. Name perpetuates Credit Mobilier figure, later censured by Congress in 1873.

Swords for the Union: Ames Manufacturing Company at ChicopeeU.S. War Department Ordnance Contracts 1832-1865; Springfield Armory Museum records; Charles W. Cooke, Ames Sword Company 1829-1935 (1989)

1774 (as Ames ironworks) / 1834-1865 (peak sword production)

Nathan Peabody Ames (1803-1847) expanded his family's cutlery works at Chicopee Falls, Massachusetts into a major arms contractor. In 1832 the company secured federal contracts for Model 1832 artillery swords and later Model 1840 cavalry sabers. During the Civil War, Ames produced over 300,000 swords, plus tools, cannon, and sewing machines. Each blade was stamped AMES MFG CO / CHICOPEE / MASS with date and U.S. inspector initials. The factory's water-powered trip hammers and skilled grinders made it the largest sword maker in the nation. Armed Union cavalry; Ames sabers became iconic Civil War artifacts now in museum collections. Company survived postwar collapse by shifting to tools and cutlery, later forming lineage to Ames True Temper. Chicopee factory buildings remain on National Register.

From Discount Aisles to Liquidation: Ames Department StoresSEC 10-K filings 1970-2002; FundingUniverse corporate history; New York Times business coverage 1988-2002

1958-2002

Ames Department Stores, Inc. was founded in 1958 in Southbridge, Massachusetts by Milton and Irving Gilman and Philip Feltman, opening its first store in Southbridge. Positioning as a rural/suburban discount alternative to department stores, it grew slowly then rapidly via acquisitions: Zayre (388 stores, 1988) and Hills Department Stores (155 stores, 1998). Overleveraged and facing Walmart and Target competition, Ames filed Chapter 11 in 1990 (emerged 1992), again in 2001, and announced liquidation August 14, 2002, closing its final 327 stores. At peak it was the fourth-largest discount chain in the U.S. Loss of ~21,000 jobs in 2002; left retail voids in small New England towns. Brand and intellectual property sold; no physical stores remain. Frequently cited in retail case studies as example of debt-fueled expansion failure.

Accounts II
Blunt Bodies and Wind Tunnels: Creation of Ames Aeronautical LaboratoryNASA History Office, 'Ames Research Center Origins' (Elizabeth Muenger, 1985); NACA Annual Reports 1939-1958; Moffett Field historical site records

1939-1958 (NACA) / 1958-present (NASA)

On 20 December 1939, the NACA established its second laboratory at Sunnyvale, California, adjacent to Moffett Field Naval Air Station, to handle aeronautical research overload. Named for physicist Joseph Sweetman Ames (1864-1943), NACA chairman, not for a place. Under director Smith J. DeFrance, the lab built massive wind tunnels including the 40x80 ft (1944) and later 80x120 ft (1982) circuits, the largest in the world. In 1958 it became NASA Ames Research Center. Key contributions: H. Julian Allen's blunt-body reentry theory (1951) enabling heat shields, lifting-body research leading to Space Shuttle, and later astrobiology and Kepler mission operations. Made Ames a core NASA research center (now ~2,500 employees, $1B budget). Enabled safe atmospheric reentry for Mercury through Apollo. Moffett Field now Moffett Federal Airfield operated by NASA. Wind tunnel complex designated National Historic Landmark.

The Ames Process: Purifying Uranium for the Manhattan ProjectFrank H. Spedding Papers, Iowa State University; Richard G. Hewlett and Oscar E. Anderson, The New World 1939-1946 (1962); U.S. Atomic Energy Commission History

1942-1947

In 1942, Iowa State College chemist Frank H. Spedding and his team in Ames, Iowa developed a low-cost method to purify uranium metal using the 'Ames Process' - a thermite-like reduction of uranium tetrafluoride with calcium. At the request of the Manhattan Project, a pilot plant in the basement of Wilhelm Hall produced over 2 million pounds of high-purity uranium for the first sustained chain reaction (Chicago Pile-1) and for Los Alamos. In 1947 the Atomic Energy Commission formally established Ames Laboratory as a national laboratory, operated by Iowa State. Spedding also pioneered ion-exchange separation of rare earths. Created the DOE Ames National Laboratory, now a leading materials science lab. The Ames Process solved the uranium supply bottleneck for atomic bombs. Postwar, rare-earth separation enabled modern electronics and magnets. Lab remains on Iowa State campus under DOE contract.

Air Ministry Experimental Station: How AMES Numbered Britain's RadarThe National Archives (UK) AIR 2/3150; Robert Watson-Watt, Three Steps to Victory (1957); R.V. Jones, Most Secret War (1978)

1936-1945

In 1936 the Air Ministry moved its radar research from Orfordness to Bawdsey Manor, Suffolk, cover-named Air Ministry Experimental Station (AMES). To obscure purpose, radar sets were designated by AMES Type numbers: Type 1 Chain Home (early warning), Type 2 Chain Home Low, Type 7 Ground Controlled Interception, etc. After Bawdsey, AMES became generic designation, not a place - e.g., AMES Type 80 was a 1950s long-range radar. The naming system persisted through WWII and into postwar RAF service, with over 50 AMES types. Standardized RAF radar deployment that won the Battle of Britain. AMES terminology survives in radar histories and museum displays (e.g., RAF Air Defence Radar Museum, Neatishead). The cover name itself became the official nomenclature.

Ames, Iowa was named for the famous orchid botanist Oakes Ames — Ames, Iowa (1864) was named for congressman Oakes Ames (1804-1873); the botanist Oakes Ames (1874-1950) was his great-nephew, born a decade after the town was platted.

NASA Ames Research Center is in Ames, Iowa — NASA Ames is in Mountain View, California at Moffett Field, 1,700 miles from Ames, Iowa. It is named for physicist Joseph Sweetman Ames, not the Iowa city. The DOE Ames National Laboratory is the one in Ames, Iowa.

Ames Department Stores, Ames True Temper, and Ames Manufacturing Company are the same company — Three distinct corporations with separate histories: Ames Manufacturing (swords/tools, 1834-1935, Chicopee), Ames True Temper (tools, lineage via Old Stone Forge 1774 to present, Camp Hill, PA), and Ames Department Stores (retail, 1958-2002, Southbridge). Only trademark lineage connects first and second partially.

AMES radar was named after the town of Ames or a person named Ames — AMES stood for Air Ministry Experimental Station, a cover name for the Bawdsey Manor research site. It later became a generic type designation (AMES Type 1, Type 7, etc.), not a place or person.

Ames is a rare or made-up name with one origin — Ames is a common English surname (rank ~1,200 in U.S. Census) with multiple independent origins, plus independent French and Galician toponyms (Ames, Pas-de-Calais and Ames, Galicia) that are not English transfers. There are 11+ U.S. places named Ames.
