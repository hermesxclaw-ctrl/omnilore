Who It Is

You hear it before you see it — a wet, labored wheezing that shakes the walls, then a mountain of blistered fat and rusted metal lumbers into view, raising two cannon-arms that were grafted where hands should be. The Mancubus is Doom's most grotesquely industrial demon: not sleek or satanic, but bloated, agonized, and engineered to be a walking artillery platform.

Attested behavior: Slow, lumbering movement (lowest walk speed among heavy demons in classic games), emits constant heavy breathing/wheezing audio cue. Territorial and aggressive, but not cunning - acts as artillery. In codex lore, described as slothful and driven by hunger, with low intelligence but high pain tolerance. Doom (2016) Codex notes they are 'laden with necrotic fat that acts as natural armor' and are 'docile when not provoked, but rage when their feeding is interrupted.' Doom Eternal describes them as 'gluttonous siege engines' that feed on corpses

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Invented pseudo-Latin coinage by id Software. Most plausibly from Latin 'mancus' (maimed, crippled, defective) + suffix '-cubus' as in incubus/succubus (from Latin 'cubare', to lie upon). No official id Software etymology statement has been published; community derivation is reconstructed, not attes

Pronunciation — /ˈmæŋkjʊbəs/ (MANK-yoo-bus), commonly anglicized as man-KYOO-bus; id Software developers and voice actors use first-syllable stress. Plural attested in manuals and fandom as 'Mancubi' (pseudo-Latin) and 'Mancubuses'.

Aliases — Cyber Mancubus, Mancubus (Doom II), Mancubus (Doom 2016/Doom Eternal variant)

Ownership — Wholly owned fictional creation of id Software LLC, originally published by GT Interactive (1994) and now under ZeniMax Media / Microsoft Gaming via Bethesda Softworks. No traditio

Powers
Dual Arm Cannons - Fireball Volley

Primary attack in all versions: simultaneous or alternating launch of large, slow-moving fireballs/plasma balls. Classic: 3 fireballs in a spread (one straight, two at ±~15 degrees). Doom (2016/Eternal): lobbed arcing fireballs that explode on impact, leaving residual flame.

Limit: Slow projectile speed allows dodging; long wind-up animation with audio cue; cannot fire while staggered. Classic Mancubus cannot aim vertically well.

Flamethrower Sweep

Sustained stream of fire from one or both cannons in a sweeping arc. Introduced in Doom 3 and retained in Doom (2016) and Doom Eternal. Deals continuous damage at close-medium range.

Limit: Short range (approx 15m), drains fuel (lore), leaves Mancubus stationary and vulnerable. Player can circle-strafe.

Acid/Flame Vomit (Cyber Mancubus / Doom Eternal)

Cyber Mancubus can detach stomach armor and spew pressurized acid bile / green flame in a wide cone. Doom Eternal Mancubus can do similar belly-mouth blast.

Limit: Requires breaking stomach armor first (player must shoot weak point). Long cooldown. Self-damaging if interrupted.

Necrotic Fat Armor / Damage Resistance

Thick layers of fat and grafted metal provide high hit points and resistance to explosive damage. Codex states fat is 'laced with Argent energy and acts as ablative armor.'

Limit: Weak points: fuel tanks on back/arms (2016) and exposed stomach/belly (Eternal). Fat is flammable - vulnerable to sustained fire? Not mechanically implemented. Slows movement dramatically.

Seismic Stomp / Ground Slam (Doom Eternal)

When player is close, Mancubus can slam ground creating shockwave and knocking back player. Rarely used.

Limit: Very short range, telegraphed, only triggers at melee distance where Mancubus is otherwise disadvantaged.

Infighting Resilience

High pain chance threshold means Mancubus rarely flinches from light damage, but will retaliate if damaged by other demons, causing demon infighting.

Limit: Low pain chance makes it vulnerable to stunlock by high-damage weapons (Super Shotgun, Ballista).

Form & Signs

Appearance — Classic (Doom II, 1994): Enormous humanoid, approximately 3-3.5 meters tall, morbidly obese with pale, mottled, blistered flesh covered in brownish lesions and boils. Head small relative to body, with a porcine snout, tiny eyes, and a wide mouth. Arms terminate not in hands but in massive, rust-colored metal flamethrower/cannon prosthetics grafted directly into flesh with visible rivets and tubing. Wears a soiled brown loincloth/diaper-like garme

Symbols — No traditional religious iconography; symbolism is industrial-military and body-horror. Represents id Software's design philosophy of 'Hell as industrial meat factory' - demons as perversions of technology and flesh. The grafted cannons symbolize dehumanization and weaponization of the body; the obese form contrasts with agile player to create a slow, tank-like threat. Accounts I
Doom II: Hell on Earth - Invasion of EarthDoom II: Hell on Earth (id Software, 1994) - Levels MAP07 Dead Simple, MAP08 Tricks and Traps

1994 - fictional near-future

After the Doom Marine returns from Hell to find Earth overrun, Mancubi appear as mid-to-late game heavy demons deployed by Hell's forces to hold urban and industrial zones. Their first canonical encounter is MAP07 Dead Simple, a deliberate arena designed to showcase them: the player is trapped with multiple Mancubi and Arachnotrons, forced to learn their spread-fire pattern. Lore in manual frames them as 'fat, fire-spewing monstrosities' among the legions that poured through the portal after the UAC's experiments. No individual Mancubus character; they are a species deployed en masse. Established Mancubus as iconic heavy demon archetype; MAP07 became one of the most referenced Doom levels. Species persists in all sequels.

Doom 3 - Delta Labs MutationDoom 3 (id Software, 2004) - PDA Entry, Levels Delta Labs, Hell

2004 - alternate continuity (2145 Mars)

Doom 3 reimagines Mancubus not as native Hell species but as former humans grotesquely mutated by Hell's influence and UAC teleportation experiments. Audio logs in Delta Labs describe UAC scientists observing subjects swelling with unnatural fat and having flamethrower apparatus fuse to their arms during possession. The player encounters them in the Delta Complex and later in Hell as guardians of the Soul Cube path. This version leans into body horror and industrial accident rather than fantasy demon. Created continuity split: Doom 3 Mancubus is explicitly human-derived, contradicting later Doom (2016) Hell-native lore. Fans debate whether Doom 3 is separate timeline.

Doom (2016) - UAC Augmentation Project: Cyber MancubusDoom (2016) - Codex Entries 'Mancubus' and 'Cyber Mancubus', UAC Report File 9H-6V-8

2016 - fictional 2149 Mars / Hell

The UAC, under Olivia Pierce and Samuel Hayden, captures Hell-native Mancubi and augments them cybernetically to study Argent energy weaponization. Codex states: 'The Mancubus is a lumbering behemoth... Its body is bloated with necrotic fat... The Cyber Mancubus is a UAC creation - a Mancubus fitted with Argent-powered arm cannons and stomach armor to increase survivability.' The player first meets a natural Mancubus in the Foundry, then Cyber Mancubi in Lazarus Labs as failed experiments that break containment. Lore emphasizes UAC hubris: they made a bad demon worse. Introduced the crucial distinction between natural and augmented Mancubus, explaining visual differences and adding weak-point gameplay. Became canonical explanation for 2016 design.

Accounts II
Doom Eternal - The Nekravol Soul FactoryDoom Eternal (id Software, 2020) - Codex 'Mancubus', Levels Nekravol, Super Gore Nest

2020 - fictional Earth invasion, Hell industrial lore

Doom Eternal expands Hell's ecology: Mancubi are revealed as a natural Hell fauna, bred in the Soul Factory of Nekravol where souls are rendered into Argent energy. They are described as 'gluttonous siege engines' that gorge on corpses and are deployed by the Hell Priests (Deag Grav, Deag Ranak) during the invasion of Earth. The Slayer encounters them early in Hell on Earth and repeatedly thereafter. New lore retcons the Cyber Mancubus augmentation as unnecessary - Eternal's Mancubus is naturally armored and needs no UAC tech, implying the 2016 Cyber variant was a dead-end experiment. Solidified Mancubus as Hell-native species, overriding Doom 3 human-mutation origin for main timeline. Introduced destructible armor mechanic tied to lore.

Doom 64 - The Forgotten HellDoom 64 (Midway/id Software, 1997) - Levels MAP03, MAP08

1997 - direct sequel to Doom II

Set after Doom II, Doom 64 follows the Marine remaining in Hell to prevent further invasions. Mancubi return with a redesigned, darker, more desaturated sprite to fit the game's horror atmosphere. No new lore text is provided in-game, but the manual and later Doom 64 Retold (2020 re-release) lore confirms they are survivors of the original invasion, now lurking in the remade Hell temples. Their role is identical to Doom II but with enhanced lighting making their wheeze more ominous. Maintained continuity between classic and modern designs; Doom 64's darker Mancubus influenced Doom 3's horror direction.

Doom RPG and Doom II RPG - Turn-Based IncursionDoom RPG (id Software / Fountainhead, 2005) and Doom II RPG (2009) - Mobile games, Bestiary entries

2005-2009 - non-canonical side continuity

In the turn-based mobile RPGs, Mancubus appears as a boss-class enemy with humorous bestiary text: 'So fat he can't see his own feet. So angry he doesn't need to.' The story has the UAC's Mars base overrun again; the player as a UAC marine must fight through Mancubi guarding the reactor. The RPGs introduce dialogue for other demons but Mancubus remains mute, only roaring. This continuity is considered non-canon but shows the Mancubus's adaptability to different genres. Demonstrated Mancubus's recognizability outside FPS; bestiary jokes entered fan culture.

Mancubus is based on a real demon from folklore or the Ars Goetia. — No pre-1994 folklore, demonology, or religious text contains a Mancubus. The name is a modern pseudo-Latin invention by id Software. It does not appear in Collin de Plancy's Dictionnaire Infernal, the Goetia, or medieval bestiaries. Any resemblance to incubus/succubus is etymological wordplay, not lineage.

Mancubus and Baron of Hell are the same demon or palette swaps. — They are distinct species with different roles, lore, and designs. Mancubus is obese with arm cannons and fireballs; Baron is muscular, horned, and melee-focused with green plasma. They share no codex entry and have separate internal IDs (MT_FATSO vs MT_BRUISER). Palette-swap confusion comes from Doom's limited palette and similar size.

Mancubus is slow and therefore not dangerous; you can ignore it. — While slow-moving, Mancubus deals extremely high burst damage via spread fireballs and flamethrower, and in higher difficulties (Ultra-Violence, Nightmare) can one-shot or trap players. Doom Eternal makes it a priority target because its death drops resources and its cannons block arenas. Speedrunners treat it as high-threat.

The Mancubus's cannons are removable weapons it picks up. — Lore and art in all versions show cannons are surgically grafted/fused to flesh, not held. Doom (2016) Codex explicitly says arms were removed and cannons fused to the ulna with fuel lines through the gut. The demon cannot drop or swap them.
