Who It Is

Yongwang (Yong-wang), the Dragon King, is the Korean deity who rules over all bodies of water—the seas, rivers, lakes, and even wells. He dwells in a magnificent underwater palace called Yonggung, attended by an extensive court of lesser dragon spirits, sea turtles, fish, and marine creatures. The concept of the Dragon King was partially imported from Chinese Buddhist mythology (where Dragon Kings rule the four seas) and partially indigenous to Korean shamanistic water worship, which long predates the arrival of Buddhism. In Korean folk religion, Yongwang is among the most actively worshipped deities. Fishermen, sailors, and coastal communities offer regular rituals (yongwangje) at seaside shrines to ensure safe voyages, bountiful catches, and protection from storms. These shrines, called yongwanggak or yongwangdang, are typically simple stone altars near the shore, often marked by a sacred tree, where offerings of rice, dried fish, rice wine, and paper boats are made. Yongwang also features prominently in Korean shamanic narrative songs (muga), where he appears as a powerful and sometimes temperamental deity who must be propitiated. In the shamanic epic 'The Abandoned Princess Bari,' the Dragon King assists the heroine in her quest to obtain the water of life. In Buddhist temple iconography, Yongwang is often depicted in murals alongside Sanshin and Dokseong (the Recluse) as part of a triad of indigenous deities absorbed into Korean Buddhist practice. He controls rainfall, storms, and marine life, and in agricultural communities he is invoked for irrigation water and protection from floods. The Dragon King also appears as a giver of magical treasures, including wish-fulfilling jewels, to heroes who visit his palace.

Attested in Korean shamanism; reinforced by Chinese and Buddhist dragon-king traditionsEra Pre-Three Kingdoms period; fully established by Goryeo dynasty
