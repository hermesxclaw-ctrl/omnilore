You smell it before you see it. It's like something died and then got left out in the swamp for a month. And then you see it -- big, hairy, watching you from the palmetto scrub with these dark, intelligent eyes.

Who It Is

The Skunk Ape, or Florida Bigfoot, is the most famous Southern variant of the Bigfoot legend -- a large, hairy hominid uniquely adapted to the subtropical swamps of Florida. Its name comes from its most distinctive characteristic: an overpowering, foul odor described as a combination of rotten eggs, methane, and wet dog, which reportedly can be smelled long before the creature itself is seen. Physically, the Skunk Ape is described as smaller than the Pacific Northwest Sasquatch -- about 6 to 7 feet tall rather than 8 to 10 -- with reddish-brown or dark hair that is thinner and more matted due to the humid swamp environment. It is said to be a solitary creature that feeds on wild hogs, deer, berries, and palmetto hearts. The Seminole people have long traditions of a 'hairy wild man' called Esti Capcaki inhabiting the Everglades. Sightings spiked in the 1960s and 1970s, with the most famous evidence being the 'Myakka photographs' from 2000 -- two photos sent anonymously to the Sarasota County Sheriff's Department showing what appears to be a large, hairy, orangutan-like creature in a backyard near the Myakka River. The photos have been analyzed extensively with no definitive evidence of hoaxing. Florida's unique environment, with its vast, inaccessible swamps, provides a plausible -- if improbable -- habitat for an undiscovered large primate. Skeptics propose misidentified black bears, escaped exotic pets (Florida has a significant problem with released exotic animals), or hoaxers in costume.
