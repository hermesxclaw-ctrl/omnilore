Who It Is

She died with an arrow in her chest and a jewel burning in her hands — and then she came back. Not alive, not dead, but made of grave-soil and bone ash, kept moving by a stolen sliver of her own reincarnated soul. Kikyo, the priestess of Inuyasha, is one of modern manga's most haunting tragic figures: powerful enough to purify a demon with a single arrow, human enough to be broken by jealousy, loneliness, and a lie.

In life, Kikyo is characterized as duty-bound, solitary, and preternaturally composed — the strongest priestess of her era, respected and feared. Villagers revere but keep distance; her younger sister Kaede notes Kikyo had no childhood. She is compassionate (healing the bandit Onigumo, caring for the wounded) but emotionally guarded, having been raised to suppress personal desire for the sake of the Jewel. Her love for Inuyasha is her first and only breach of that asceticism, and it terrifies her. After resurrection, her personality hardens into melancholy detachment, gallows humor, and sin

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese 桔梗 (kikyō), the Sino-Japanese reading for Platycodon grandiflorus, the Chinese or Japanese bellflower. The flower's hanakotoba (language of flowers) includes 'eternal love,' 'honesty,' and 'obedience' — all thematically resonant with the character's arc. The same reading kikyō also writes 帰

Pronunciation — Japanese [kʲi.kʲoː] — kee-KYOH (two morae, long final vowel). Hepburn romanization Kikyō; often simplified to Kikyo without macron in English editions. Viz Media English dub pronounces /ˈkɪkjoʊ/ KIK-yoh.

Aliases — Kikyo, Kikyou, Lady Kikyo, Kikyo-sama

Powers
Immense Reiryoku (Spiritual Power) and Purification

As the most powerful miko of her generation, Kikyo can generate purifying spiritual energy that destroys or neutralizes yokai, miasma, and curses. Touch or arrow strike can purify the Shikon Jewel itself, and she can sense jewel shards across great distances.

Limit: Power weakens when emotionally conflicted or when the Jewel is tainted; requires concentration and physical stamina. In resurrected body, power is slightly diminished and sustained by external souls — if soul collectors are destroyed or she expends too much energy (e.g., purifying Mount Hakurei's barrier), she collapses. Cannot purify Naraku completely while he holds the Jewel.

Sacred Archery (Hamaya)

Master archer; her arrows carry purification. The sealing arrow that pinned Inuyasha to the Sacred Tree held him in suspended animation for 50 years without aging. Can fire multiple arrows, create barrier-piercing shots, and in final arc fire an arrow that briefly wounds Naraku even through his defenses.

Limit: Requires bow and specially blessed arrows; not infinite. Sealing technique requires the target be immobilized and the arrow to strike the chest/heart area. Resurrected body has reduced draw strength over prolonged battle.

Barrier Creation (Kekkai)

Can erect spiritual barriers that repel yokai and miasma. Maintained the barrier around Mount Hakurei in concert with Saint Hakushin, and created personal barriers to hide from Naraku.

Limit: Barrier strength tied to her life force; large barriers drain her rapidly and require stillness. Cannot maintain while attacking.

Soul Manipulation and Soul Collectors (Shinidamachū)

After resurrection, commands small yokai shinidamachū that collect souls of deceased women and children and bring them to her to sustain her artificial body and supplement spiritual power. Can also see and extract souls, transfer a fragment of Kagome's soul back, and in death, lend remaining spiritual power to Kagome.

Limit: Souls are not hers; they dissipate if she is wounded or purified. She cannot create souls, only borrow. Taking souls is portrayed as morally ambiguous and physically necessary — without them she cannot move. She cannot take living souls without killing.

Healing and Medicinal Knowledge

Trained in herbal medicine and spiritual healing; healed Onigumo's burned body and treated villagers. Knowledge of poisons and antidotes.

Limit: Cannot resurrect the truly dead or heal wounds caused by Shikon Jewel corruption without purification. Her own artificial body cannot be healed conventionally.

Enhanced Perception and Jewel Sensing

Can sense Shikon Jewel shards, demonic auras, and the presence of Naraku's incarnations across regions. Can see through illusions and detect taint.

Limit: Naraku's barriers and the darkness of the Jewel can obscure her senses. Range limited when weakened.

Accounts I
The Priestess and the JewelTakahashi, Inuyasha Chapters 1-15; Anime Episode 1

1996-1997 (in-universe: Sengoku period, ~50 years before main story)

Kikyo is introduced in flashback as the young but unrivaled miko entrusted with guarding and purifying the Shikon no Tama, a jewel that grants power but amplifies desire. She lives alone at the edge of a village with her younger sister Kaede, respected and isolated. Her duty is to keep the Jewel from yokai and prevent its misuse, at cost of any normal life. This establishes her archetype: the solitary guardian whose power is also a curse. Establishes Kikyo's spiritual authority and loneliness, and the Jewel as plot engine. Her isolation makes her vulnerable to Inuyasha's companionship.

Love, Betrayal and the Sacred Tree — Naraku's DeceptionTakahashi, Inuyasha Chapters 16-19, 80-90, 140-148; Anime Episodes 1-2, 47-48

1996-1997 (in-universe: 50 years prior)

Kikyo meets the half-demon Inuyasha, who initially seeks the Jewel to become a full yokai. Instead of fighting, they talk; she sees his loneliness mirrors hers. They fall in love and plan for Inuyasha to use the Jewel to become human so they can live together, which would also purify and destroy the Jewel. The burned bandit Onigumo, whom Kikyo had nursed, lusts for Kikyo and offers his body to yokai to become Naraku. Naraku then impersonates both lovers: as Inuyasha he wounds Kikyo and steals the Jewel; as Kikyo he attacks Inuyasha. Believing each betrayed the other, Kikyo — mortally wounded — pins Inuyasha to the Sacred Tree with a sealing arrow, then has Kaede burn the Jewel with her body so no one can misuse it. Both lovers die/ are sealed; Jewel disappears for 50 years until Kagome's birth. Naraku is born from this malice. This is the central tragedy driving the entire series.

Resurrection by the Ogress UrasueTakahashi, Inuyasha Chapters 80-89 (Volume 9-10); Anime Episodes 23-24 'Kikyo's Stolen Ashes' / 'Kikyo and Kagome'

1999-2000 (in-universe: present, ~1550s)

Fifty years later, the ogress Urasue steals Kikyo's grave soil and bones and uses Kagome — Kikyo's reincarnation — to resurrect Kikyo as an undead clay vessel. Urasue intends to use Kikyo to find Jewel shards. Kikyo awakens, kills Urasue instantly with purification, and discovers she is animated by a fragment of Kagome's soul. She attempts to drag Kagome to hell to reclaim her full soul, but after seeing Kagome's compassion and Inuyasha's anguish, she relents and leaves, choosing to wander as an undead priestess sustained by soul collectors, seeking vengeance on Naraku. Kikyo returns as a liminal being, neither alive nor dead, setting up her uneasy alliance/antagonism with the main group. Establishes soul-collector mechanic and her borrowed-time status.

Accounts II
The Wandering Priestess — Kohaku, Mount Hakurei and the Purified Jewel FragmentTakahashi, Inuyasha Chapters 150-180, 200-210, 280-300; Anime Episodes 60-65, 90-95

2000-2003

As an undead, Kikyo travels Sengoku Japan hunting Naraku. She saves the young demon-slayer Kohaku (Rin's brother, killed and resurrected by Naraku with a Jewel shard in his back) by purifying his shard and preserving his life, telling him to live free. She later allies with Saint Hakushin on Mount Hakurei, helping maintain a purifying barrier that even Naraku cannot penetrate, and uses the mountain's sacred light to attempt to purify Naraku's miasma. She repeatedly saves Kagome and Inuyasha from afar while maintaining cold distance, and trades information with Sesshomaru. Her plan is to lure Naraku into purifying the Jewel from within by making him absorb her purified shard at the right moment. Demonstrates Kikyo's strategic, self-sacrificial role; Kohaku's survival becomes key to Naraku's defeat. Her barrier work shows her power still rivals Naraku's.

Final Death — Light in the DarknessTakahashi, Inuyasha Chapters 550-558 (Volume 56); Anime Inuyasha: The Final Act Episodes 20-21, 26

2008-2010 (final arc)

In the final battle, Naraku mortally wounds Kikyo by impaling her with his tentacle/miasma after she wounds him with a purified arrow and traps a fragment of light within him — a light that will later allow Kagome to see and purify the Jewel's darkness from inside Naraku. Dying for the second and final time, Kikyo dies in Inuyasha's arms, acknowledging her love and asking him to live. Her remaining spiritual power and light pass to Kagome, enabling Kagome to make the correct wish on the Jewel (to wish for it to disappear) and break its curse. Her soul finally departs to the afterlife, and her body crumbles to soil and bone. Kikyo's death is permanent; her plan succeeds posthumously. Resolves love triangle with grace — she frees Inuyasha to be with Kagome. The Jewel is destroyed/purified.

The Unfinished Life — Kaede's Memory and the Bone-Eater's WellTakahashi, Inuyasha Chapters 1, 15, 300, 558; Anime Episodes 1, 48, Final Act 26; Official Profiles

1996-2008 recurring

Through Kaede's perspective and village oral history, Kikyo's legacy persists: the 50-year seal on Inuyasha, the burned Jewel, and the warning that 'Kikyo's soul was too strong to be reborn cleanly.' Kaede, blinded in one eye by Kikyo's accidental arrow as a child, carries her sister's bow and teachings. The story repeatedly returns to the Sacred Tree as Kikyo's monument, where Inuyasha was pinned and where Kikyo and Inuyasha's promise was made and broken. Even after her final death, villagers and Kagome honor her shrine. Frames Kikyo not as antagonist but as ancestral guardian whose choices echo across generations; explains Kagome's spiritual inheritance.

Kikyo and Kagome are the same person / Kikyo is just Kagome in the past. — They share a soul due to reincarnation, but are distinct individuals with separate memories, personalities, and choices. Kikyo lived and died 50 years before Kagome was born; Kagome has her own modern family and agency. Takahashi explicitly distinguishes them.

Kikyo is the villain of Inuyasha and hates Kagome. — Kikyo never truly hates Kagome; after initial attempt to reclaim her soul, she protects Kagome, heals allies, and ultimately gives Kagome the light needed to defeat Naraku. Her seeming coldness is fatalism and focus on Naraku, not jealousy.

Kikyo's name means 'homecoming' and she represents returning home. — The priestess's name uses 桔梗 (bellflower), not 帰郷 (homecoming). The homophone causes confusion because many unrelated works titled 'Kikyō' do use 帰郷. Her name is floral, not thematic of return, though thematically her return from death ironically evokes homecoming.

Kikyo is a historical or folkloric figure. — Kikyo is a modern fictional creation (1996) by Rumiko Takahashi. She draws on the historical miko archetype but has no prior folklore source. No shrine or legend of 'Priestess Kikyo' predates Inuyasha.

Kikyo survived at the end or will return in Yashahime. — Kikyo dies permanently in Chapter 558 / Final Act Episode 21, her body returning to soil. Yashahime (2020) and official guides confirm she does not return; only flashbacks appear.
