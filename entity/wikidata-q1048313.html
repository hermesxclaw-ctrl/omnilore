Who It Is

Forged by the trickster god Loki, this enigmatic weapon appears only once in the surviving Norse corpus, hidden in a poem's riddle-like verses. Its very name is a scholarly reconstruction, its form a matter of fierce debate—dart, sword, or magic wand? Whatever it is, it is the only thing that can kill the rooster Viðofnir, who sits atop the World Tree. But is it also the mistletoe that slew Baldr? The answer lies buried in a tangle of manuscript emendations and poetic kennings.

As an inanimate object, it has no personality. In the poem, it is said to be kept in an iron vessel (or 'in Lægjarn's vessel') and is the only weapon that can kill Viðofnir.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is a scholarly emendation of the manuscript form Hævateinn. The first element is from Old Norse læ (genitive læva), meaning 'cunning, deception, injury, bane'. The second element teinn means 'twig, rod, wand, shaft'. Thus Lævateinn is often glossed as 'Wounding Wand' or 'Wand-of-Destruction

Pronunciation — Approximately /ˈlaɪvəteɪn/ or /ˈlɛːvəteɪn/ in modern English; Old Norse /ˈlɛːwaˌtɛinː/.

Aliases — Hævateinn, Hævatein, Lævatein, Wounding Wand, Wand-of-Destruction, Damage Twig

Ownership — Norse mythology, specifically the Poetic Edda. The weapon is associated with the god Loki and appears in the poem Fjölsvinnsmál.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The weapon's physical appearance is not described in the poem. Interpretations depend on the assumed type: if a dart, it is a projectile; if a sword, a bladed weapon; if a wand, a staff-like object. No visual attributes are attested.

Symbols — No iconography exists from the medieval period. In modern art, it is rarely depicted, and when it is, it is often shown as a sword or a wand.

Domains — It is located within the poem's narrative at the location of Mímameiðr (the World Tree), specifically in a vessel (possibly an iron chest) near the tree. It is not associated with a particular realm beyond that.

Accounts I
The Quest of Svipdagr (Fjölsvinnsmál)Poetic Edda, Fjölsvinnsmál

Possibly 10th-13th century, preserved in 17th century manuscripts

In the poem, the hero Svipdagr (or the seeker) asks the giant Fjölsviðr how to kill the rooster Viðofnir, who sits atop the tree Mímameiðr. Fjölsviðr replies that the only weapon is Lævateinn, which was forged by Loki (referred to as Lopt) and is kept in an iron vessel. The poem does not narrate the actual use of the weapon. The weapon's use is not depicted; the poem ends with Fjölsviðr's answer.
