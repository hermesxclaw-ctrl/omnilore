Who It Is

He arrived at the Xiaolin Temple with a soccer ball under his arm, a smirk, and no intention of bowing — the street kid from Rio who could summon a hurricane when he finally learned to trust the wind.

Attested personality (Showdown S01-S02): rebellious, impulsive, sarcastic, and fiercely independent — the 'street kid' archetype who resents authority and initially treats Xiaolin training as a game. He is competitive, especially with Omi, and struggles with self-doubt and desire for recognition, leading to key plot point where he is passed over for Apprentice rank in S01E13. Over S02-S03 he matures into a loyal, strategic team player and ultimately leader, learning humility and responsibility. He uses humor to deflect insecurity and shows strong protective instincts toward Kimiko

Powers
Xiaolin Wind Manipulation (Aerokinesis)

Innate elemental control over wind and air currents. Manifests as gusts, sustained flight/levitation, enhanced speed and agility, and concussive blasts. Signature move: Typhoon Boom — a focused tornado-like blast from hands or feet, often channeled through soccer-style kicks.

Limit: Requires emotional focus and Xiaolin training; initially unreliable when Raimundo is arrogant or distracted (S01E13 failure). Power scales with rank (Monk → Apprentice → Wudai Warrior → Shoku Warrior). Cannot create wind without air; ineffective in vacuum or when chi is blocked (e.g., by Hannibal Roy Bean's magic). Exhaustion after prolonged flight or large-scale storms.

Typhoon Boom and Advanced Techniques

Named attack: Typhoon Boom (standard), Wudai Star Wind, and later Shoku Wind. Wudai Star Wind creates massive spiraling vortex capable of lifting opponents and objects. Shoku-level allows riding wind currents for long-distance flight.

Limit: Wudai Star Wind requires Wudai Weapon as focus; without Blade of the Nebula, output is weaker. Shoku-level flight drains stamina quickly.

Wudai Weapon Mastery — Blade of the Nebula

Personal Wudai Weapon forged from Raimundo's own chi during the Wudai quest. A bladed polearm that amplifies wind into razor gusts and can summon localized cyclones. Only Raimundo can wield it at full power; others can hold it but not activate its wind property.

Limit: Bound to Raimundo's chi; if separated, power diminishes. Cannot be used by Heylin without corruption (seen when Jack Spicer attempts theft).

Shen Gong Wu Proficiency

Trained use of mystical artifacts: Sword of the Storm (flight + wind amplification, his most used Wu), Wings of Tinabi (flight), Ring of the Nine Dragons (cloning), etc. As Dragon of Wind, he has affinity for wind-aligned Wu but can use others.

Limit: Wu require Xiaolin Showdown (contest) rules; can be stolen/lost; over-reliance leads to vulnerability when Wu are captured by Heylin (Jack Spicer, Wuya, Chase Young).

Heylin Corruption Resistance and Vulnerability

Temporarily joined Heylin side under Hannibal Roy Bean's influence (S03E01-04 'Wu Got the Power'), gaining enhanced aggression and dark chi but losing Xiaolin restraint. Later resisted and returned, demonstrating willpower-based resistance.

Limit: While Heylin, wind powers become destructive and harder to control; redemption required external trust from teammates and Master Fung's guidance. Not immune to future manipulation (Chase Young attempts).

Accounts I
The Journey of a Thousand MilesXiaolin Showdown S01E01

2003

Master Fung and Dojo recruit four children with elemental potential to become Xiaolin Dragons and collect the Shen Gong Wu before the Heylin witch Wuya does. Raimundo, a circus kid from Rio, is the last to be recruited after showing off soccer tricks that unknowingly channel wind. He is cocky, questions temple discipline, and nearly loses the first Showdown for the Mantis Flip Coin to Jack Spicer. The episode establishes his outsider status and wind affinity, and his rivalry-friendship with Omi, Kimiko, and Clay. Formation of the Xiaolin team; Raimundo begins training as Dragon of Wind; establishes series premise of Wu hunting via Xiaolin Showdowns.

Citadel of DoomXiaolin Showdown S01E13

2004

After a season of Wu hunts, Master Fung elevates Omi, Kimiko, and Clay to Xiaolin Apprentice rank, passing over Raimundo due to his lack of discipline and selfish play during a Showdown against Katnappé. Humiliated, Raimundo quits and is manipulated by Wuya into leading her to the Shen Gong Wu vault. Realizing his mistake, he returns to help his friends defend the temple, proving loyalty over pride. Master Fung then promotes him to Apprentice. Defining character growth: Raimundo learns humility and teamwork; completes the core four's Apprentice status; foreshadows later Heylin temptation arc. Frequently cited by fans as his emotional peak.

The Shoku Warrior / Hannibal's Revenge — Time After TimeXiaolin Showdown S02E13-15 (Season 2 finale)

2005

Hannibal Roy Bean, a tiny demon in a bean form, manipulates Raimundo's lingering insecurities about leadership. In the two-part finale 'Time After Time,' Hannibal traps the monks in a time-bending Wu (Sands of Time) and offers Raimundo a deal: power and recognition in exchange for freeing him. Raimundo's choice to trust his friends and use Wudai Star Wind in concert with the others defeats Hannibal temporarily and earns him recognition as a Wudai Warrior. He also achieves Shoku Warrior status alongside the team. Raimundo solidifies as reliable team member; Hannibal Roy Bean becomes recurring Season 3 antagonist; team attains Wudai/Shoku rank, unlocking new powers and weapons.

Accounts II
Wu Got the Power — Heylin RaimundoXiaolin Showdown S03E01-04

2006

Now the official leader of the Xiaolin Dragons (Master Fung's decision), Raimundo struggles with the burden of leadership and feeling unappreciated when his decisions are questioned. Hannibal Roy Bean exploits this, convincing Raimundo that the Xiaolin side will never respect him. Raimundo defects, joins the Heylin, and fights his former friends using corrupted wind powers and stolen Wu. His friends refuse to give up on him; Kimiko's and Omi's faith, plus Dojo's intervention, help him break Hannibal's control. He returns, is forgiven, and leads the team to reclaim the Wu. Most controversial arc: establishes Raimundo as leader but shows fallibility; deepens theme of trust; leads to final battle against Chase Young and Hannibal. Often cited in reception as example of children's TV handling peer pressure and redemption.

Judging Omi and the Final Showdown with Chase YoungXiaolin Showdown S03E12-13 'Saving Omi' / 'Time After Time II'

2006

Omi's earlier Heylin turn (via Chase Young's influence) parallels Raimundo's arc. Raimundo, now secure as leader, is the one who argues for redeeming Omi rather than punishing him, showing his growth. In the series finale, Raimundo leads the combined Xiaolin forces, using Blade of the Nebula and Sword of the Storm together to create a massive wind barrier that seals Chase Young's portal and recovers the final Wu. He is confirmed as Shoku Warrior and future guardian. Closure of original series; Raimundo's leadership validated; sets up status quo inherited by Chronicles where he remains leader.

Xiaolin Chronicles — New Monk on the Block and The Fall of XiaolinXiaolin Chronicles S01E01 and S01E26

2013-2014

Set after Showdown, Chronicles reintroduces Raimundo as the confident teenage leader of the team, now mentoring new monk Ping Pong alongside Omi, Kimiko, and Clay. In the premiere, he demonstrates advanced wind mastery to impress Ping Pong. In the finale 'The Fall of Xiaolin,' Chase Young and Shadow (new villain) attempt to corrupt the temple's chi; Raimundo's wind barrier protects the younger monks, reaffirming his role as protector and strategist. Extends Raimundo's narrative beyond Showdown; shows aged-up design and stabilized leadership; reception was mixed, with fans debating recast and animation change.

Raimundo is Japanese or Chinese because Xiaolin Showdown is about Shaolin monks. — Raimundo is explicitly Brazilian (from Rio de Janeiro). The series is an American pastiche of Shaolin/Xiaolin mythology with a multicultural cast: Omi (Chinese), Kimiko (Japanese), Clay (American), Raimundo (Brazilian). He represents Wind, not an Asian nationality.

Raimundo's element is fire or his power is soccer magic. — His element is Wind. Soccer is a character motif linking his Brazilian background and footwork to wind attacks (e.g., Typhoon Boom via kicks), but not a separate magic system. Fire belongs to Kimiko.

Raimundo permanently turned evil and stayed Heylin. — He defected for four episodes in Season 3 (Wu Got the Power arc) under Hannibal Roy Bean's manipulation, then redeemed and returned to the Xiaolin side. He remains a hero through the series finale and Chronicles.

Raimundo is based on a Brazilian folklore figure or deity. — He is an original 2003 creation by Christy Hui for Warner Bros. with no folkloric predecessor. The name Raimundo is a common Iberian given name, not a mythological entity.
