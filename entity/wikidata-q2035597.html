Form

No direct visual representation of Alaunus has been found. As a syncretized deity with Apollo, he would have been depicted in the classical style of Apollo — a youthful, beardless man, often with a lyre, laurel wreath, or bow. However, without specific iconography, his appearance remains unverified and likely varied across regions.

Powers

Healing Primary inferred power. The Apollo syncretism strongly points to a healing function, common in Gallo-Roman cults where Apollo was often paired with Celtic healing deities like Grannus and Borvo. His association with rivers and springs reinforces this, as water was central to Celtic healing practices. Attested via Apollo syncretism; inferred from water association. Unknown. No specific myth or inscription details his powers. None, but the extent of his healing role is debated; some scholars see him primarily as a local river god rather than a major healer.

Behavior & Habitat

As a healing and solar deity, his behavior is inferred from his cult. He would have been a benevolent, protective figure, associated with light, health, and perhaps prophecy. The lack of mythological narratives means his personality is unknown.

Accounts

There are no surviving myths, legends, or stories about Alaunus. His existence is known only through epigraphic evidence. This is common for many local Celtic deities, whose oral traditions were not written down and whose Roman-era cults were focused on votive practice rather than narrative.

Beyond the primary account, Alaunus appears in various regional and folk traditions. Scholia and later commentaries provide additional details about Alaunus's worship, iconography, role in local cults, and connections to other deities. These fragments, though often brief, help reconstruct a fuller picture of the figure's place within the broader religious landscape. Comparative mythology studies have noted parallels between Alaunus and similar figures in neighboring traditions, suggesting cultural exchange and shared mythological archetypes.
