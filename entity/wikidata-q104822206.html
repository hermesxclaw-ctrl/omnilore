The Approach

In the shadowed world of Sanctuary, a name echoes through the ages—Diablo, the Prime Evil, the Lord of Terror. This is not merely a demon but a cosmic force of fear, a being whose very existence has shaped the fate of humanity and the eternal conflict between Heaven and Hell. From the depths of the Burning Hells to the hearts of mortals, Diablo's story is one of corruption, resurrection, and the relentless pursuit of ultimate power.

Content advisory. This dossier contains depictions of demonic entities, violence, horror, and themes of corruption and damnation. Reader discretion is advised.
Identity

Diablo

Pronunciation dee-AH-bloh

Known As

The Lord of TerrorThe Prime EvilThe Dark LordThe Butcher's MasterThe Lord of Hell

Etymology

The name 'Diablo' is the Spanish word for 'devil', derived from the Latin 'diabolus', which in turn comes from the Greek 'diabolos' meaning 'slanderer' or 'accuser'. In the context of the game, it is used as the name of the demonic antagonist, evoking the traditional Christian concept of the devil.

Cultural Ownership

Diablo is a fictional character owned by Blizzard Entertainment, a subsidiary of Activision Blizzard. The character is part of the Diablo franchise, which includes video games, novels, comics, and other media.

Disputed Identifications

There is no real-world dispute over the identity of Diablo as a fictional character. However, within the game's lore, there are debates among scholars within the Diablo universe about the nature of the Prime Evils and their origins. Some cults within the game's world worship Diablo as a god, while others see him as a malevolent force to be destroyed.

Description

Appearance

Diablo is typically depicted as a large, horned demon with a muscular, humanoid body. He has red or dark skin, glowing eyes, and a menacing presence. In Diablo II, his appearance is more monstrous, with a more beast-like form. In Diablo III, he appears in a more humanoid but still demonic form, with a crown of horns and a cloak of flames. In Diablo IV, his design is more subtle, with a more human-like appearance when possessing a mortal, but his true form is still terrifying.

Symbols & Iconography

Diablo's symbol is often a stylized demonic face with horns, which appears in the game's logo and throughout the series. He is associated with fire, terror, and darkness. His iconic imagery includes the black soulstone, which contains his essence, and the Prime Evil's aspect of terror.

Behaviour & Personality

Diablo is cunning, manipulative, and utterly malevolent. He seeks to corrupt and destroy, using fear as his primary weapon. He is patient and strategic, often working through minions and possession to achieve his goals. He is also arrogant, believing himself to be the ultimate power in the universe.

Domains & Habitats

Diablo's domain is the Burning Hells, a realm of fire and torment. He is also able to manifest in the mortal realm of Sanctuary, often by possessing a host. He has been imprisoned in the Black Soulstone and in a soulstone within a mortal's body, but he always seeks to break free.

Version Differences

In the original Diablo, Diablo is the final boss, a powerful demon with a humanoid form. In Diablo II, he is depicted as a more monstrous, four-legged creature. In Diablo III, he returns as a more humanoid figure, but with the combined power of all the Prime Evils. In Diablo IV, he is not the main antagonist, but his influence is still felt, and he appears in a vision. In Diablo Immortal, he is a looming threat, with the story set between Diablo II and III.

Diablo can possess mortal beings, using them as hosts to act in the mortal realm. This is a key aspect of his plan to return to Sanctuary.

Source Version All games

Diablo cannot be truly killed; he can only be banished or imprisoned. His essence persists and can be resurrected.

The Original Sin and the Birth of Diablo

Source Diablo III lore, Book of CainCulture Video game lorePeriod Before the creation of Sanctuary

Summary

Diablo is one of the Prime Evils, born from the conflict between Heaven and Hell. He and his brothers, Mephisto and Baal, were the first demons to rebel against the rules of the Burning Hells, seeking to dominate all of creation. They were defeated and banished to the Void, but they later returned and created the world of Sanctuary as a battleground.

The Darkening of Tristram

Source Diablo (video game)Culture Video game lorePeriod The first game

Summary

Diablo, after being defeated by a hero in the past, was imprisoned in a soulstone beneath the cathedral in Tristram. However, the soulstone was flawed, and Diablo slowly corrupted the town's inhabitants, including King Leoric and the Archbishop Lazarus. The hero of the game descends into the labyrinth, defeats Diablo, and absorbs his soul into his own body to contain him.

Contradictions & Disputes

Diablo's True Form

Position A

In Diablo and Diablo II, Diablo is depicted as a large, monstrous demon.

Position A — evidence

Game cinematics and artwork from Diablo and Diablo II.

Position B

In Diablo III, he appears in a more humanoid form, but with the combined power of all Prime Evils.

Position B — evidence

Game cinematics and artwork from Diablo III.

Scholarly Consensus

The change is attributed to his transformation into the Prime Evil, absorbing the power of his brothers.

Diablo's Death

Position A

In Diablo II, Diablo is defeated and his soulstone is destroyed, implying he is permanently dead.

Position A — evidence

The ending of Diablo II and the destruction of the Worldstone.

Position B

In Diablo III, Diablo returns, and his essence is shattered again, but in Diablo IV, his influence remains.

Position B — evidence

The events of Diablo III and Diablo IV, where Diablo's legacy persists.

Scholarly Consensus

Diablo is a demon of immense power, and his death is never permanent; he can be resurrected or his essence can persist.

The Nature of the Prime Evils

Position A

The Prime Evils are the original demons, born from the conflict between Heaven and Hell.

Position A — evidence

The Book of Cain and official lore.

Position B

Some sources suggest they were once angels who rebelled.

Position B — evidence

Some fan theories and early game lore that suggest a different origin.

Scholarly Consensus

The official lore states that the Prime Evils are demons, but their exact origin is somewhat mysterious.
