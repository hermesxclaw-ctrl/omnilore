Who It Is

In the shadow of Spartan legend, a prince whose name meant 'marshaller of chariots' met his end not in glorious battle, but as part of a family obliterated by Heracles' wrath—a cautionary tale of hubris, blood-feud, and the brutal logic of heroic vengeance.

Hippocorystes is portrayed as a member of a violent and ambitious family. He participated in the usurpation of the Spartan throne and later fought against Heracles, indicating a defiant and aggressive character. However, individual personality traits are not developed in the sources.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description of Hippocorystes survives in ancient sources. As a Spartan prince and warrior, he would conventionally be imagined as a strong, armed male, but this is not attested.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography are associated with Hippocorystes. He is not depicted in known ancient art.

Domains — Sparta (Lacedaemon) in the Peloponnese. He lived during the heroic age, before the Trojan War.

Accounts I
The Usurpation of TyndareusApollodorus, Diodorus, Pausanias

Heroic Age

Hippocorystes and his brothers helped their father Hippocoön overthrow the rightful king Tyndareus, who was Hippocoön's half-brother. They expelled Tyndareus, but later Heracles restored him after defeating the Hippocoöntids. The Hippocoöntids were killed; Tyndareus regained the throne.

Accounts II
The War with HeraclesApollodorus, Diodorus, Pausanias

Heroic Age

Heracles came to Sparta seeking purification for the murder of Iphitus, but Hippocoön and his sons refused. Additionally, the Hippocoöntids had joined King Neleus of Pylos in a war against Heracles. The final provocation was the murder of Oeonus, Heracles' cousin, by the brothers. Heracles initially attacked but was wounded and retreated. Later, with the aid of King Cepheus of Arcadia, he launched a full expedition and slew Hippocorystes, his brothers, and their father. The Hippocoöntids were annihilated; Heracles avenged Oeonus and restored Tyndareus.
