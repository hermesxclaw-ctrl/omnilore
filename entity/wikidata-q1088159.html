Who It Is

He stands motionless in the frozen palace of Valhalla as blizzard winds howl through white pillars — a towering warrior in a silver-white God Robe shaped like a slain dragon, his blonde hair unmoved by the cold. The strongest God Warrior of Asgard, Dubhe Alpha Siegfried was said to be invincible, his body bathed in dragon's blood, until a single leaf-sized weakness over his heart betrayed him. He is not from Masami Kurumada's original manga, yet for a generation of viewers he became the most memorable opponent the Bronze Saints ever faced.

Attested as the most loyal, honorable, and tragically self-aware of the God Warriors. Calm, formal speech, deeply devoted to Hilda of Polaris and to the ideal of protecting Asgard's people from the cold and from Sanctuary's perceived aggression. He questions orders internally but obeys until presented with proof of Hilda's possession. Shows respect for strong opponents, especially Dragon Shiryu and Phoenix Ikki, acknowledging their Cosmo. Powers
God Robe of Dubhe Alpha (Fafnir Dragon Robe)

White God Robe modeled on the dragon Fafnir, forged from Orichalcum and empowered by Odin's Cosmo. Provides extreme resistance to cold (functions at -100°C+), high defense against physical and Cosmo attacks, and limited self-repair via Cosmo. Its Object form is a winged dragon coiled around the Odin Sapphire pedestal.

Limit: Not as durable as Gold Cloths against god-level weapons; damaged by Ikki's Hōō Genma Ken-empowered strikes and Seiya's Sagittarius-boosted Cosmo. Requires Odin Sapphire energy; without Hilda's prayer, power wanes.

Odin Sword (Ōdin Sōdo)

Siegfried's signature technique: he raises Cosmo to summon a colossal spectral sword of light associated with Odin, then slashes with a high-speed hand-chop that projects a razor-sharp Cosmo blade capable of cutting through Cloths and freezing air. Animation shows a vertical energy arc extending many meters. Called out as 'Odin Sword!' in Japanese.

Limit: Requires full Cosmo charge and open stance; leaves back briefly exposed during wind-up. Can be evaded by high-speed movement (Ikki, Seiya). Drains Cosmo with repeated use.

Dragon's Blood Invulnerability (Siegfried's Body)

Legend-derived passive ability: having bathed in Fafnir's blood, his body is nearly invulnerable to attacks; Cosmo and physical strikes glance off except for a single leaf-shaped weak point on his back over the heart, where a linden leaf fell during the bath. In Saint Seiya this is portrayed as Cosmo-enhanced skin + God Robe synergy, not literal blood magic, but dialogue explicitly invokes the myth.

Limit: Weak point is fatal if struck with sufficient Cosmo; Ikki discovers it via Genma Ken illusion and Shun's chain; also vulnerable to internal attacks (e.g., Shiryu's Rozan Shō Ryū Ha aimed at back). Psychological burden — knowledge of weakness makes him guard his back obsessively.

Superhuman Cosmo and Physical Prowess

As strongest God Warrior, Siegfried exhibits Seventh Sense-level Cosmo, supersonic to lightspeed combat speed, strength to shatter Bronze and Silver Cloths with bare hands, and endurance to fight four Bronze Saints sequentially (Shiryu, Hyoga, Shun, Seiya) before falling to Ikki and Sorrento's combined assault. Can generate blizzard winds and withstand absolute zero attacks (Hyoga's Aurora Execution) with minor damage.

Limit: Not immortal; prolonged sequential battles exhaust Cosmo; susceptible to illusions (Phoenix Genma Ken) that reveal his past and Hilda's possession.

Accounts I
The Possession of Hilda and the Summoning of the God WarriorsSaint Seiya anime Ep. 74-78

1988 (in-universe 1987, between Sanctuary and Poseidon arcs)

In Asgard, the priestess Hilda of Polaris, earthly representative of Odin, prays to keep the polar ice from melting. While praying at the Odin statue, she is struck by the Nibelungen Ring (Nibelung Ring) secretly placed by Poseidon's General Sorrento of Siren, who exploits her compassion for her suffering people. The ring, cursed by Poseidon, corrupts Hilda into a tyrannical ruler who declares war on Sanctuary to obtain Athena's Cosmo to save Asgard. Siegfried, as Dubhe Alpha and Hilda's most trusted guardian, is first to pledge loyalty, believing Sanctuary are invaders. He gathers the other six God Warriors (Thor, Fenrir, Hagen, Alberich, Mime, Syd) and later Bud. Asgard mobilizes against Athena; Athena's Saints are lured north; Siegfried's oath locks him into tragic opposition until the truth is revealed. Establishes his role as Hilda's sword and moral center of Asgard.

Siegfried vs Dragon Shiryu - The Dragon's DuelSaint Seiya anime Ep. 87-88

1988

After Shiryu defeats Fenrir and Hagen, he confronts Siegfried in Valhalla Palace. Shiryu's Rozan Shō Ryū Ha (Rising Dragon) clashes with Odin Sword. Siegfried easily overwhelms Shiryu, whose Dragon Cloth is shattered and who is left near death, demonstrating Siegfried's superiority. Shiryu glimpses that Siegfried's Cosmo is not evil, planting doubt. Hyoga then intervenes, using Aurora Thunder Attack, but is also defeated and frozen. Siegfried spares them out of warrior respect, telling them to leave Asgard. Shows Siegfried's overwhelming power and merciful streak; Shiryu and Hyoga survive to inform Seiya; establishes that brute force alone cannot beat Siegfried.

Accounts II
The Invincible Warrior and the Leaf on the BackSaint Seiya anime Ep. 89 'The Invincible Siegfried! The Mark of Death on His Back'

1988

Andromeda Shun's Nebula Chain cannot bind Siegfried; his Cosmo repels it. Phoenix Ikki arrives, having been absent, and engages Siegfried. Ikki uses Phoenix Genma Ken (Phantom Demon Fist) to force Siegfried to relive his dragon-slaying past, revealing the mythic origin of his invulnerability. During the illusion, Ikki and Shun notice Siegfried instinctively guards his back. Ikki deduces the Nibelungen parallel: like the mythical Siegfried, this Siegfried has a single vulnerable point where a leaf fell. Shun's chain, acting autonomously, finds the spot and taps it, confirming the weakness over his heart. The secret of Siegfried's invulnerability is exposed, shifting the battle from power to precision. Directly quotes Nibelungenlied chapter on Siegfried's bath in dragon's blood.

Death of Siegfried and Entrusting the Future to Athena's SaintsSaint Seiya anime Ep. 90-91

1988

With his weakness known, Ikki and Seiya coordinate: Ikki strikes the back point with Hōō Gen Ken while Seiya, wearing the Sagittarius Gold Cloth, fires a Golden Arrow. Mortally wounded, Siegfried does not curse them; instead, recognizing their pure Cosmo, he realizes Hilda has been corrupted. As Sorrento of Siren reveals himself and tries to finish the weakened Saints, Siegfried, dying, grabs Sorrento and drags him into a suicide attack, using his last Odin Sword to blast both through the palace wall into the blizzard, sacrificing himself to remove Poseidon's agent. With his last breath he begs Seiya to save Hilda and Asgard, entrusting the Odin Sapphire to them. Siegfried dies heroically, redeeming his misguided loyalty; his sacrifice exposes Sorrento and Poseidon's manipulation, allowing Seiya to confront Hilda directly. His Odin Sapphire becomes key to awakening Odin's Robe later. Establishes his enduring popularity as a tragic noble antagonist.

Legacy - The Odin Robe and the Memory of DubheSaint Seiya anime Ep. 91-99, Saint Seiya: Soul of Gold Ep. 1-2 (reference)

1988 / 2015

After Siegfried's death, Seiya collects his Odin Sapphire along with those of the other God Warriors. The seven sapphires awaken the true Odin Robe (Odin God Robe) which Seiya dons to wield the sword Balmung against Hilda. In later media, Siegfried's spirit is invoked: in Soul of Gold, Lyfia and Hilda mourn the strongest God Warrior who fell before the Gold Saints' resurrection; in Saint Seiya Awakening mobile game, his memory appears in Asgard side stories where Dohko and others honor his loyalty. His design remains template for 'invincible northern warrior' in fan works. Cements Siegfried as the moral and power benchmark for Asgard; his sapphire enables Athena's victory over Hilda without killing her, breaking the Nibelungen Ring's curse. Explains why later Asgard stories treat Dubhe as vacant and sacred.

Siegfried is a Gold Saint or the Gold Saint of Ursa Major. — He is a God Warrior (Goddo Senshi) of Asgard serving Odin, not Athena. His God Robe is distinct from Gold Cloths, and his star Dubhe is not part of the 88 Athena constellations. He has no Gold Saint rank.

Siegfried appears in Masami Kurumada's original manga and fights Seiya there. — He does not appear in the manga at all. The entire Asgard arc was anime-original filler (1988) to pad time while Kurumada drew the Poseidon arc. Manga goes directly from Sanctuary (vol.13) to Poseidon (vol.14).

Siegfried's invulnerability means he cannot be hurt at all. — Like his mythic namesake, he is invulnerable everywhere except a single leaf-shaped point over his heart on his back, where dragon's blood did not cover. The anime explicitly shows Shun's chain and Ikki/Seiya exploiting this spot; without targeting it, attacks fail.

Siegfried is a villain who willingly betrays Athena. — He is a tragic antagonist, not a villain: he genuinely believes he protects Asgard's people from Sanctuary's aggression and is unaware Hilda is possessed. Once he learns the truth, he immediately sacrifices himself to atone and help Athena's Saints.
