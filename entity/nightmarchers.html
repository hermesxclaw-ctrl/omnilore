Who It Is

On certain dark nights of the Hawaiian moon calendar, the old trails fill again. First the sound: conch shells, far off, then drums, then chanting in old formal Hawaiian; then torchlight in a line where no road runs; then the smell of death on a wind that moves against the trade winds. These are the huaka'i po, the Nightmarchers — processions of dead ali'i (chiefs) and their warriors, armed and in rank, walking the routes they walked in life between the sacred places: heiau to heiau, birth-site to battlefield to the leaping-places of souls. The living have exactly one obligation, taught to every island child: do not look. Get off the trail, strip, lie face down on the ground, and do not look up whatever you hear pass — because to meet the eyes of the marching dead, or worse, to stand upright in their path in false pride, is to be killed and taken into the procession, marching forever at the back of the line. There is one exemption: if an ancestor of yours walks in that company, a voice will call na'u! — mine! — claiming you, and the ranks will step around your body in the dark. The marches are not history. Construction crews in the islands still reroute projects after nightmarcher encounters; hotels built across old trails keep doors that will not stay closed aligned with the procession's right of way. The dead of Hawaii kept their easements.

Origins

Hawaiian religious tradition of the ali'i dead, tied to the kapu system's rule that commoners must not look upon or stand above the highest chiefs — a protocol so absolute it outlived the bodies of everyone involved.

Attested in Hawaiian oral tradition, living practiceEra Precolonial to present

Form

Torches in file on trail-less ground, ranks of armed warriors and chiefs in feather cloak and helmet, sometimes floating a hand's breadth above the earth — heard and smelled more than safely seen.

Powers

Death at a glance for the disrespectful; passage through any obstacle built across their route; command of drums, conch, and contrary wind; the conscription of the slain into the eternal column.

Weaknesses & Wards

They harm only breaches of protocol: the prone, the respectful, and the ancestrally claimed are spared. Ti plants around a house, and doors aligned with the old trail, let the march pass without incident.

Field Notes

Cultural Significance

The nightmarchers keep pre-contact Hawaiian sacred geography enforced in the present tense — the reason islanders still know where the old trails run, and why some hotel hallways in Waikiki have inexplicable doors.
