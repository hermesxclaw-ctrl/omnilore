Who It Is

Khan Khuluk is the legendary horse of Genghis Khan, a supernatural steed that combined the attributes of an earthly horse with those of a celestial being. In the Secret History of the Mongols, the horse is described as an 'eight-legged' animal — a poetic reference to its incredible speed and endurance, capable of outrunning the wind and covering a day's journey in an hour. Khan Khuluk carried Genghis Khan through his greatest battles and was said to be instrumental in his rise to power. The horse was not just a mount but a divine companion — it whispered guidance to Genghis Khan, warned him of ambushes, and shared his joys and sorrows. When Genghis Khan died, his horse was buried with him according to Mongol tradition, its spirit accompanying its master to the next life. In Mongolian culture, the horse is the most revered animal — the companion of the nomad, the symbol of freedom, and the bridge between earth and sky. Khan Khuluk represents the ideal of the Mongol horse: small, hardy, intelligent, and incredibly loyal. The legend inspired the Mongolian national horse festival (the Naadam horse race) and is a symbol of Mongolian national pride. Statues of Khan Khuluk appear alongside Genghis Khan in modern Mongolian monuments. The horse's eight legs are sometimes interpreted as the four cardinal directions and four intercardinal directions, representing universal dominion.
