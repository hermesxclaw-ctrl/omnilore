The Approach

She crashes into Lupin III's life in a white wedding dress, diamonds glittering and sirens wailing — a Sammarinese heiress who can buy a country and then steal it for fun. Rebecca Rossellini is not a damsel waiting to be rescued or a rival waiting to be outwitted; she is Lupin's only wife, a thrill-seeking thief who married the world's most wanted criminal as a distraction for her own heist.

Content advisory. Mild sexualized character design and costuming; depictions of theft, fraud, and deceptive marriage; no explicit sexual violence or graphic gore in canonical appearances.

Known As

Etymology

Given name 'Rebecca' from Hebrew Rivqah (Rivka), via Latin and English, meaning 'to bind' or 'captivating' in traditional etymologies; chosen in-universe as a contemporary European given name. Surname 'Rossellini' is a prominent Italian surname, most famously associated with filmmaker Roberto Rossellini, evoking Italian high culture and cinema. In-universe, the name signals Sammarinese-Italian elite heritage. Japanese katakana rendering is レベッカ・ロッセリーニ (Rebekka Rosserīni).

Cultural Ownership

Anime-original character owned and managed by TMS Entertainment (Tokyo Movie Shinsha) under the Lupin III franchise licensed from the estate of Monkey Punch (Kazuhiko Katō, 1937-2019) and Futabasha. Created for Lupin III Part IV: The Italian Adventure (2015) by series writers and character designers at TMS/Telecom Animation Film, including character design by Hisao Horikoshi and Yoji Shimizu, series composition by Yuuya Takahashi. No folkloric or public-domain ownership; all rights reserved to TMS / Nippon Television Network.

Disputed Identifications

Not to be confused with real persons named Rebecca Rossellini or members of the Rossellini family (e.g., Isabella Rossellini). Not present in Monkey Punch's original manga, nor in Lupin III Part I-III. Fan wikis occasionally conflate her with Fujiko Mine or Clarisse d'Cagliostro due to shared 'Lupin bride' motif; they are distinct characters with separate narrative functions. Wikidata Q108081376 correctly identifies her as a fictional character from Lupin III Part IV.

Description

Appearance

Rebecca is depicted as a young adult woman in her early to mid-20s, tall and statuesque with long, wavy blonde hair (often depicted with a slight honey-gold tone), blue-green eyes, and fair skin. Official model sheets show her at approximately 170cm, with a fashion-model physique that is frequently emphasized by form-fitting haute couture. Her wardrobe is a core part of her iconography: she alternates between high-society attire (white wedding dress in debut, evening gowns, tailored business suits, equestrian and ski wear) and functional thief gear (black catsuit, goggles, gloves). She is often shown with luxury accessories — notably the Sammarinese crown jewels in her debut arc — and is drawn with expressive, theatrical facial acting that shifts rapidly from cool heiress poise to mischievous, adrenaline-lit grin. In thief mode, her hair is often tied back and her silhouette deliberately parallels Fujiko Mine while remaining distinct through lighter color palette and more athletic, less overtly femme-fatale styling.

Symbols & Iconography

Visual motifs tied to Rebecca include: the San Marino crown and signet-style jewels (her first target and symbol of her thrill-seeking motive); wedding imagery (veil, ring, marriage certificate) used ironically to signal deception and agency rather than romance; Italian national symbols (tricolore, San Marino's Three Towers, Renaissance art references) linking her to the Italy/San Marino setting of Part IV; luxury brands, sports cars, and equestrian imagery signifying inherited wealth and dilettante athleticism; and paired imagery with Lupin where she mirrors his pose or outpaces him, visually coding her as an equal rather than a prize. Her thief emblem is not a fixed logo but the act of theft itself performed in plain sight at galas and media events.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested personality across Part IV is consistently: brilliant, impulsive, thrill-seeking, and performatively confident. Rebecca is introduced as a Sammarinese businesswoman, heiress, model, actress and champion athlete who is publicly celebrated but privately bored by legitimacy. She explicitly states she steals 'for the excitement' rather than need, distinguishing her from Fujiko's mercenary pragmatism and Lupin's professional pride. She is cunning enough to trick Lupin himself — using their highly public marriage as a diversion while she steals the crown — and charismatic enough to manipulate media and police. She is also emotionally volatile and candid: she moves from using Lupin to genuinely falling for him, then asking to keep 'Mrs. Lupin' as a private code after their divorce. She is competitive, jealous of Fujiko, respectful of Zenigata's doggedness, and capable of both cruelty and loyalty. Japanese scripts and Yukiyo Fujii's performance emphasize playful, slightly spoiled ojō-sama (heiress) speech that drops into sharp, focused thief diction during heists. Limitations: her impulsivity and need for attention repeatedly expose her to danger; she underestimates conspiracies larger than herself (The Italian Game / Leonardo da Vinci plot).

Domains & Habitats

Primary habitat is the contemporary (2015) Italian Peninsula and the Republic of San Marino, presented as a cosmopolitan, media-saturated Europe of luxury hotels, auction houses, racetracks, ski resorts, and historic villas. She operates in high-society domains (boardrooms, fashion shows, film sets, sporting events) and criminal domains (museum heists, high-security vaults, rooftop chases). Her base is implied to be a Sammarinese estate and corporate headquarters of the Rossellini conglomerate, though no single lair is consistently depicted. Narratively, her domain is the intersection of legitimate elite culture and the underworld — she belongs to both and is fully at home in neither, which is the source of her restlessness. In Part 5 cameo, she appears in a digital-age Paris/France context, indicating mobility beyond Italy when the plot requires.

Version Differences

Rebecca is anime-original and has no manga, early anime, or film counterpart prior to 2015. Within anime canon: Part IV (2015) is her definitive version — 26 episodes with full character arc from marriage ruse to sincere affection to amicable divorce, retaining the name Mrs. Lupin as a secret. Italian Game (2016) is a condensed retelling with no substantive design or personality changes. Part 5 Episode 24 (2018) shows a slightly more mature, supportive version — less self-centered, acting as an ally who helps Lupin evade the Shake Hands surveillance state, with updated character design by the Part 5 team but same voice actor. English dub (Cassandra Lee Morris) renders her with a light continental accent and more overtly playful delivery than Fujii's cooler Japanese performance, but dialogue is not rewritten to alter biography. No video game, stage play, or live-action version exists as of 2026. Fan art often sexualizes her more heavily than official model sheets, which is an adaptation-specific distortion, not canonical.

Powers & Abilities

Elite Thief Skills (Burglary, Disguise, Escape)

Source VersionPart IV Episodes 1-2, 7, 12-13; Italian Game

Rebecca executes high-profile heists without superhuman assistance: lockpicking, safe-cracking, rooftop parkour, and social-engineering infiltration of galas and auctions. She successfully steals the Sammarinese crown in Episode 1 while Lupin and police are distracted, demonstrating planning and sleight-of-hand comparable to Fujiko Mine.

Limitations

Not at Lupin's level of improvisation against impossible security; she relies on wealth, access, and preparation rather than gadget mastery. She is captured or cornered multiple times and requires rescue by Lupin/Jigen/Goemon. No depiction of supernatural ability.

Contradictions

None within canon; fan wikis occasionally inflate her to 'rival of Fujiko' in all domains, but series shows Fujiko as more experienced and Rebecca as talented but less seasoned.

Athletic and Sporting Mastery

Source VersionPart IV official profile and Episodes 3, 9, 10 (sports and chase sequences)

Canonically established as a champion-level athlete: equestrian, skiing, motorsports, and general acrobatics. Uses physical prowess in chases and fights. Official profile lists model/actress/athlete as concurrent careers, implying professional-grade conditioning.

Limitations

Athleticism does not translate to hand-to-hand combat superiority over Goemon or Jigen; she is agile but not a trained assassin. Stamina and risk-taking lead to reckless exposure.

Contradictions

Some English promotional copy calls her 'Olympic-level' — Japanese profile says 'top athlete' without specifying Olympics; treat Olympic claim as marketing embellishment, not attested.

Business Acumen and Social Capital

Source VersionPart IV Episodes 1, 4, 8; TMS character bible

As heiress to the Rossellini conglomerate in San Marino, she commands vast financial resources, corporate infrastructure, media influence, and diplomatic access. She leverages press coverage, legal immunity, and high-society invitations as tools for heists — a form of social engineering unavailable to Lupin's gang.

Limitations

Wealth is a double-edged sword: her public profile makes anonymity impossible, and her corporation is vulnerable to hostile takeover plots within the Italian Game conspiracy. She cannot simply buy her way out of criminal consequences with Zenigata.

Contradictions

None; consistent across all appearances.

Acting, Deception, and Performance

Source VersionPart IV Episodes 1-2, 12, 24

Trained actress and media personality capable of sustained impersonation and emotional manipulation. Her marriage to Lupin is a long-con performance maintained under global media scrutiny. She convincingly plays the lovesick bride, the ruthless CEO, and the daring thief as needed.

Limitations

Deception fails against those who know her motive (Lupin quickly deduces the ruse) and against larger conspiracies that anticipate her moves. Emotional honesty later in the series undermines her ability to maintain the cold con-artist persona.

Contradictions

None.

Multilingual and Cultural Fluency

Source VersionPart IV setting and dialogue; Japanese voice track with Italian loanwords

Operates fluently in Italian, English, and Japanese contexts within the show's diegesis; navigates Sammarinese, Italian, and British elite circles. Implied fluency in French given San Marino/European setting, though not explicitly demonstrated in dialogue.

Limitations

No depiction of technical or cryptographic skill comparable to Ami Enan (Part 5) or Fujiko's gadget expertise; she is not a hacker or engineer.

Contradictions

Fan translations sometimes attribute polyglot claims not in original Japanese script; verified fluency is narrative, not explicitly listed as a 'power' in production notes.

The San Marino Marriage Con — Lupin III's Wedding (Part IV Episodes 1-2)

Source Lupin III Part IV: The Italian Adventure, Episodes 1 'Lupin III's Wedding' and 2 'Fake Fantasista', TMS/Telecom Animation Film, 2015Culture Japanese anime production with Italian/Sammarinese setting; contemporary crime caper genrePeriod 2015 (diegetic present)

Summary

At a high-society party in San Marino, Lupin is publicly announced as the husband of Rebecca Rossellini, the nation's most celebrated heiress. Lupin, who barely remembers the whirlwind courtship, is swept into a media frenzy and police pursuit led by Zenigata. The marriage is revealed to be Rebecca's calculated ruse: she used Lupin's notoriety and the massive police/media attention on him as a smokescreen to steal the historic Sammarinese crown — a jewel she does not need but craves for the thrill. Lupin, initially outplayed, comes to admire her audacity. The crown is recovered and the chase ends with Rebecca retaining both the loot's notoriety and Lupin's fascinated attention.

The episode foregrounds Rebecca's understanding that in modern Europe, attention is as valuable as gold, and she stages thefts as performances. Lupin and Rebecca alternate between alliance and one-upmanship, with Rebecca proving she can control the narrative even when she does not control the vault.

The Dream of Italy and the Leonardo Legacy (Part IV Episodes 5-6, 12-13)

Source Lupin III Part IV Episodes 5-6 'The Dream of Italy' Parts 1-2 and Episodes 12-13 'To Be a Lady' arc, 2015Culture Japanese anime with Italian Renaissance historical fictionPeriod 2015 diegetic, with flashbacks to Leonardo da Vinci era (15th-16th century)

Summary

Rebecca is drawn into the season's central mystery: a conspiracy surrounding Leonardo da Vinci's lost legacy and a shadow organization called The Italian Game that seeks to control Italy's cultural and financial heritage. As heiress to a Sammarinese conglomerate with deep ties to Italian art patronage, Rebecca possesses access and provenance knowledge Lupin's gang lacks. She and Lupin collaborate — uneasily — to steal and decode Leonardo-related artifacts, with Rebecca's high-society credentials opening doors that Lupin must otherwise break down. The arc reveals that Rebecca's family fortune is entangled with the same historical networks the conspiracy seeks to exploit, forcing her to choose between protecting her legacy and chasing the thrill.

With a Shot of Love — Jealousy and Partnership (Part IV Episode 10)

Source Lupin III Part IV Episode 10 'With a Shot of Love' (Ai o Komete Ikkiuchi), 2015Culture Japanese anime, romantic caperPeriod 2015

Summary

Rebecca's growing genuine affection for Lupin collides with her rivalry with Fujiko Mine. During a heist involving a heart-shaped jewel and a charity auction, Rebecca attempts to out-thief Fujiko to prove she is not merely Lupin's 'wife on paper.' The episode plays as a love-triangle caper where both women use Lupin as both prize and pawn. Rebecca's impulsivity nearly derails the job, but her athleticism and willingness to take physical risks save the crew. By the end, Lupin acknowledges Rebecca as a capable partner, while Fujiko wryly notes that Rebecca is still learning the difference between wanting Lupin and wanting to be Lupin.

Farewell, Mrs. Lupin — The Divorce Heist (Part IV Episodes 23-24)

Source Lupin III Part IV Episodes 23-24 'The End of Dreams' / 'Lupin III's Last Day' (finale arc), 2015Culture Japanese anime, season finalePeriod 2015

Summary

With the Italian Game conspiracy exposed — a plot by British and Italian elites to use Leonardo's legacy and financial manipulation to control Italy — Rebecca must decide what her marriage to Lupin means. Lupin offers her an out: a staged 'divorce heist' where they steal back her freedom and expose the conspirators in one final, highly public theft. Rebecca chooses to help Lupin dismantle the conspiracy even though it risks her corporate empire. In the aftermath, she and Lupin amicably divorce on paper but she asks to keep calling herself Mrs. Lupin as a private code between them — a sign that their bond, though not a conventional marriage, remains real on her terms.

Relationships
Rebecca Rossellini→Lupin III (Arsène Lupin III)romantic

Husband on paper, rival and partner in practice. Rebecca tricks Lupin into marriage in Episode 1 to use him as a distraction, but comes to genuinely love him. Lupin is initially bemused and outplayed, then fascinated and protective. Their relationship is consensually deceptive at first, then mutually affectionate but non-possessive; they divorce legally but keep 'Mrs. Lupin' as a private code. Lupin respects her skill and never treats her as a mere prize, which distinguishes him from other men in her elite circle.

Rebecca Rossellini→Fujiko Minehostile

Rival thief and romantic rival. Fujiko views Rebecca as a talented but naive newcomer who steals for fun rather than survival; Rebecca views Fujiko as the benchmark she must surpass and the woman who holds Lupin's long-term fascination. They compete directly in Episode 10 and collaborate warily in conspiracy arcs. Fujiko is more experienced and cynical; Rebecca is more daring and emotionally open. By the finale, rivalry softens into wary mutual respect.

Rebecca Rossellini→Daisuke Jigen and Goemon Ishikawa XIIIally

Lupin's core partners who treat Rebecca with bemused tolerance. Jigen is skeptical of the marriage ruse and protective of Lupin, often acting as the voice of caution when Rebecca's impulsivity endangers the job. Goemon is largely indifferent but respects her athleticism and willingness to take physical risks. Neither becomes a close confidant, but both accept her as a temporary crew member during Italian Game heists.

Rebecca Rossellini→Inspector Koichi Zenigatahostile

Pursuer who initially sees Rebecca as Lupin's accomplice and later as an independent criminal worth arresting in her own right. Rebecca, unlike Lupin, does not share Zenigata's grudging mutual respect; she sees him as an obstacle to be outwitted with media manipulation and legal privilege. Zenigata, in turn, is frustrated that her wealth and status shield her from consequences that Lupin cannot evade. Their dynamic highlights class differences in how crime is policed.

Rebecca Rossellini→Robson Zuccolially

Rebecca's elderly butler, bodyguard, and confidant — her Alfred Pennyworth figure. Robson manages her estate, provides logistical support for heists, and offers dry commentary on her recklessness. He is loyal to the Rossellini family and to Rebecca personally, often cleaning up after her impulsivity while enabling her double life. His presence grounds Rebecca's elite background and provides exposition about her family history.

Rebecca Rossellini→Agent Nyx (MI6)hostile

British intelligence officer assigned to monitor Lupin who becomes entangled with Rebecca's conspiracy arc. Nyx represents state power that views both Lupin and Rebecca as pawns in the larger Italian Game. Rebecca and Nyx are ideological opposites: she steals for personal thrill, he serves institutional control. Their confrontations force Rebecca to confront that her games have geopolitical consequences beyond her own adrenaline.

Rebecca Rossellini→Rossellini Family / Sammarinese Elitefamily

Rebecca is heiress to the Rossellini conglomerate, implying a powerful Sammarinese-Italian family with interests in business, culture, and possibly historical patronage networks linked to Leonardo da Vinci's legacy. Family members are not individually depicted; the family is present as an institution that gives Rebecca wealth, access, and expectations she rebels against by becoming a thief. Her rebellion is not against poverty but against boredom and predetermined elite destiny.

Deceptive Marriage and Fraud

Source Part IV Episodes 1-2

Description

Rebecca's debut involves tricking Lupin into a legally binding marriage under false pretenses to use him as a diversion for theft. While played comedically, the act is fraud and emotional manipulation. Series frames it as consensual caper mischief rather than coercion, and both parties are seasoned criminals who understand the game, but the power dynamic (wealthy heiress using a wanted thief's notoriety) is ethically ambiguous.

Scholarly Context

No scholarly critique specific to Rebecca exists; general Lupin III scholarship notes the franchise's recurring use of marriage as a heist device (cf. Clarisse, Fujiko) and its shift in the 2010s toward more agency for female characters who initiate rather than endure such plots.

Contradictions & Disputes

Is Rebecca a 'replacement' for Fujiko Mine?

Position A

Rebecca was created to replace or sideline Fujiko as Lupin's primary female counterpart for the Italian setting.

Position A — evidence

Marketing for Part IV heavily foregrounded Rebecca as 'Lupin's wife' and she receives more screen time than Fujiko in several episodes; some Western reviews (2015) described her as 'the new Fujiko.'

Position B

Rebecca was created as a complementary, season-specific heroine who coexists with Fujiko, not a replacement; Fujiko remains present and distinct throughout Part IV.

Position B — evidence

Fujiko appears in 18 of 26 Part IV episodes with independent heists and narrative agency; series composition by Yuuya Takahashi and interviews with staff explicitly describe Rebecca as a 'guest heroine' for the Italy arc, while Fujiko's role is retained. Part 5 restores Fujiko to central status while retaining Rebecca as cameo ally, proving coexistence.

Scholarly Consensus

Consensus among critics (ANN, Otaku USA) is position B: Rebecca is an addition, not a replacement, though fan debate persists. No official statement ever described her as a permanent replacement.

Nature of the marriage — legal vs. comedic ruse

Position A

The marriage is a fully legal, binding marriage in-universe that requires formal divorce.

Position A — evidence

Episode 1 presents a marriage certificate, media coverage, and Zenigata treating it as legally relevant; finale (Ep 24) explicitly uses the word 'divorce' (rikon) and Rebecca asks to keep the name as a code, implying legal dissolution.

Position B

The marriage is a comedic caper device with ambiguous legal status, quickly dissolved without legal proceedings shown.

Position B — evidence

No court or bureaucratic divorce process is depicted; Lupin III franchise routinely treats marriage, arrest, and identity documents as malleable caper props. The Italian Game film glosses over legal mechanics, treating the marriage as a narrative symbol.

Scholarly Consensus

Attested that series presents it as legally framed but resolves it symbolically rather than procedurally; exact legal mechanics are not depicted and should be treated as caper logic, not realistic law.

Rebecca's age and background — heiress vs. self-made

Position A

Rebecca is a self-made businesswoman who built her fortune.

Position A — evidence

Some English promotional copy calls her 'businesswoman and entrepreneur,' emphasizing active career as model/actress/athlete, leading to self-made reading.

Position B

Rebecca is an heiress who inherited the Rossellini conglomerate and then expanded it.

Position B — evidence

Japanese official profile (TMS) explicitly calls her 'ojō-sama' and 'heiress' (sekiyu), and Episode 1 introduces her as 'Sammarinese heiress'; her wealth is presented as inherited, with her thrill-seeking as rebellion against inherited privilege.

Scholarly Consensus

Position B is attested by primary Japanese sources; 'self-made' is a mistranslation/embellishment in some English marketing.
