Who It Is

She crashes into Lupin III's life in a white wedding dress, diamonds glittering and sirens wailing — a Sammarinese heiress who can buy a country and then steal it for fun. Rebecca Rossellini is not a damsel waiting to be rescued or a rival waiting to be outwitted; she is Lupin's only wife, a thrill-seeking thief who married the world's most wanted criminal as a distraction for her own heist.

Attested personality across Part IV is consistently: brilliant, impulsive, thrill-seeking, and performatively confident. Rebecca is introduced as a Sammarinese businesswoman, heiress, model, actress and champion athlete who is publicly celebrated but privately bored by legitimacy. She explicitly states she steals 'for the excitement' rather than need, distinguishing her from Fujiko's mercenary pragmatism and Lupin's professional pride. Powers
Elite Thief Skills (Burglary, Disguise, Escape)

Rebecca executes high-profile heists without superhuman assistance: lockpicking, safe-cracking, rooftop parkour, and social-engineering infiltration of galas and auctions. She successfully steals the Sammarinese crown in Episode 1 while Lupin and police are distracted, demonstrating planning and sleight-of-hand comparable to Fujiko Mine.

Limit: Not at Lupin's level of improvisation against impossible security; she relies on wealth, access, and preparation rather than gadget mastery. She is captured or cornered multiple times and requires rescue by Lupin/Jigen/Goemon. No depiction of supernatural ability.

Athletic and Sporting Mastery

Canonically established as a champion-level athlete: equestrian, skiing, motorsports, and general acrobatics. Uses physical prowess in chases and fights. Official profile lists model/actress/athlete as concurrent careers, implying professional-grade conditioning.

Limit: Athleticism does not translate to hand-to-hand combat superiority over Goemon or Jigen; she is agile but not a trained assassin. Stamina and risk-taking lead to reckless exposure.

Business Acumen and Social Capital

As heiress to the Rossellini conglomerate in San Marino, she commands vast financial resources, corporate infrastructure, media influence, and diplomatic access. She leverages press coverage, legal immunity, and high-society invitations as tools for heists — a form of social engineering unavailable to Lupin's gang.

Limit: Wealth is a double-edged sword: her public profile makes anonymity impossible, and her corporation is vulnerable to hostile takeover plots within the Italian Game conspiracy. She cannot simply buy her way out of criminal consequences with Zenigata.

Acting, Deception, and Performance

Trained actress and media personality capable of sustained impersonation and emotional manipulation. Her marriage to Lupin is a long-con performance maintained under global media scrutiny. She convincingly plays the lovesick bride, the ruthless CEO, and the daring thief as needed.

Limit: Deception fails against those who know her motive (Lupin quickly deduces the ruse) and against larger conspiracies that anticipate her moves. Emotional honesty later in the series undermines her ability to maintain the cold con-artist persona.

Multilingual and Cultural Fluency

Operates fluently in Italian, English, and Japanese contexts within the show's diegesis; navigates Sammarinese, Italian, and British elite circles. Implied fluency in French given San Marino/European setting, though not explicitly demonstrated in dialogue.

Limit: No depiction of technical or cryptographic skill comparable to Ami Enan (Part 5) or Fujiko's gadget expertise; she is not a hacker or engineer.

Accounts I
The San Marino Marriage Con — Lupin III's Wedding (Part IV Episodes 1-2)Lupin III Part IV: The Italian Adventure, Episodes 1 'Lupin III's Wedding' and 2 'Fake Fantasista', TMS/Telecom Animation Film, 2015

2015 (diegetic present)

At a high-society party in San Marino, Lupin is publicly announced as the husband of Rebecca Rossellini, the nation's most celebrated heiress. Lupin, who barely remembers the whirlwind courtship, is swept into a media frenzy and police pursuit led by Zenigata. The marriage is revealed to be Rebecca's calculated ruse: she used Lupin's notoriety and the massive police/media attention on him as a smokescreen to steal the historic Sammarinese crown — a jewel she does not need but craves for the thrill. Lupin, initially outplayed, comes to admire her audacity. The crown is recovered and the chase ends with Rebecca retaining both the loot's notoriety and Lupin's fascinated attention. Establishes Rebecca's core motive (theft for adrenaline), her willingness to manipulate Lupin, and the central romantic-comedic tension of the season. The 'Mrs. Lupin' title becomes a recurring motif. Sets up the Italian Game conspiracy by showing San Marino's treasures are targets for larger forces.

Zapping Operation and the Media Game (Part IV Episode 7)Lupin III Part IV Episode 7 'Zapping Operation' (Zappingu Operēshon), 2015

2015

Rebecca leverages her ownership of television and media interests to manipulate live coverage of Lupin's heists, turning a museum theft into a reality-TV spectacle. She competes with both Lupin and a British intelligence operative (Agent Nyx) who is monitoring Lupin for MI6. The episode foregrounds Rebecca's understanding that in modern Europe, attention is as valuable as gold, and she stages thefts as performances. Lupin and Rebecca alternate between alliance and one-upmanship, with Rebecca proving she can control the narrative even when she does not control the vault. Deepens Rebecca's characterization as a media-savvy thief distinct from Lupin's old-school craftsmanship. Introduces the season's theme of surveillance and information warfare that culminates in the Italian Game.

The Dream of Italy and the Leonardo Legacy (Part IV Episodes 5-6, 12-13)Lupin III Part IV Episodes 5-6 'The Dream of Italy' Parts 1-2 and Episodes 12-13 'To Be a Lady' arc, 2015

2015 diegetic, with flashbacks to Leonardo da Vinci era (15th-16th century)

Rebecca is drawn into the season's central mystery: a conspiracy surrounding Leonardo da Vinci's lost legacy and a shadow organization called The Italian Game that seeks to control Italy's cultural and financial heritage. As heiress to a Sammarinese conglomerate with deep ties to Italian art patronage, Rebecca possesses access and provenance knowledge Lupin's gang lacks. She and Lupin collaborate — uneasily — to steal and decode Leonardo-related artifacts, with Rebecca's high-society credentials opening doors that Lupin must otherwise break down. The arc reveals that Rebecca's family fortune is entangled with the same historical networks the conspiracy seeks to exploit, forcing her to choose between protecting her legacy and chasing the thrill. Moves Rebecca from episodic rival to season-long co-protagonist. Her personal stake in Italian cultural heritage gives her an emotional arc beyond thrill-seeking, and her alliance with Lupin becomes more genuine.

Accounts II
With a Shot of Love — Jealousy and Partnership (Part IV Episode 10)Lupin III Part IV Episode 10 'With a Shot of Love' (Ai o Komete Ikkiuchi), 2015

2015

Rebecca's growing genuine affection for Lupin collides with her rivalry with Fujiko Mine. During a heist involving a heart-shaped jewel and a charity auction, Rebecca attempts to out-thief Fujiko to prove she is not merely Lupin's 'wife on paper.' The episode plays as a love-triangle caper where both women use Lupin as both prize and pawn. Rebecca's impulsivity nearly derails the job, but her athleticism and willingness to take physical risks save the crew. By the end, Lupin acknowledges Rebecca as a capable partner, while Fujiko wryly notes that Rebecca is still learning the difference between wanting Lupin and wanting to be Lupin. Marks the pivot where Rebecca's feelings become sincere rather than performative. Establishes the respectful rivalry with Fujiko that persists through the finale, and clarifies that Rebecca's arc is about self-definition, not merely winning Lupin's heart.

Farewell, Mrs. Lupin — The Divorce Heist (Part IV Episodes 23-24)Lupin III Part IV Episodes 23-24 'The End of Dreams' / 'Lupin III's Last Day' (finale arc), 2015

2015

With the Italian Game conspiracy exposed — a plot by British and Italian elites to use Leonardo's legacy and financial manipulation to control Italy — Rebecca must decide what her marriage to Lupin means. Lupin offers her an out: a staged 'divorce heist' where they steal back her freedom and expose the conspirators in one final, highly public theft. Rebecca chooses to help Lupin dismantle the conspiracy even though it risks her corporate empire. In the aftermath, she and Lupin amicably divorce on paper but she asks to keep calling herself Mrs. Lupin as a private code between them — a sign that their bond, though not a conventional marriage, remains real on her terms. Resolves Rebecca's season arc: from thrill-seeking heiress using marriage as a tool to a self-aware partner who chooses loyalty over adrenaline. The 'secret Mrs. Lupin' code becomes her canonical epilogue status and explains her later cameo as an ally rather than a spouse.

Return in the Digital Age — Shake Hands Cameo (Part 5 Episode 24)Lupin III Part 5 Episode 24 'Viva Lupin III Forever' (Viva Rupan Sansei yo Eien ni), TMS, 2018

2018 diegetic (contemporary)

Two years after Part IV, Lupin is hunted by the global IT conglomerate Shake Hands, which weaponizes social media and the dark web (via the Marco Polo site) to crowdsource his capture. Rebecca reappears in the finale montage/coda, using her media influence and resources to help Lupin and his gang counter the digital manhunt. She is shown coordinating with other allies (including Ami Enan) to disrupt Shake Hands' narrative control. Her appearance is brief but narratively significant: it confirms she retained her network and her 'Mrs. Lupin' loyalty, and that she has matured from solo thrill-thief to collaborative ally in a larger fight against surveillance capitalism. Canonizes Rebecca as a recurring ally beyond her debut season, not a one-season gimmick. Bridges Part IV's Italy-centric conspiracy to Part 5's global digital conspiracy, showing thematic continuity in Lupin III's 2010s revival.

Rebecca Rossellini is from the original Monkey Punch manga. — She is anime-original, created in 2015 for Part IV, 48 years after the manga began. She has never appeared in any Monkey Punch manga chapter. The manga's female lead is Fujiko Mine (and earlier, unnamed heroines).

Rebecca is Lupin's permanent wife and the series ends with them married. — The marriage is a ruse that becomes sincere but is amicably dissolved by the end of Part IV. They divorce legally but keep 'Mrs. Lupin' as a private, affectionate code. Part 5 shows her as an ally, not a spouse. Lupin remains canonically unmarried and romantically entangled with Fujiko.

Rebecca has superhuman or gadget-based powers like many anime heroines. — She has no supernatural abilities, gadgets, or cybernetic enhancements. Her skills are realistic: athleticism, burglary, acting, business, and social engineering. She does not use Fujiko's firearms expertise at the same level, nor Ami Enan's hacking, nor Goemon's swordsmanship.

Rebecca Rossellini is Italian, not Sammarinese. — Canonically Sammarinese — from the Republic of San Marino, the microstate surrounded by Italy. The series deliberately chose San Marino to give her a distinct nationality tied to the Italian setting while making her a quasi-royal heiress (San Marino's historic institutions). She is culturally Italian-adjacent but legally Sammarinese.
