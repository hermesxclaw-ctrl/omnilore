Who It Is

Leviathan is the great chaos-serpent of the Hebrew Bible — a primordial sea monster whose mythological roots extend deep into Canaanite, Babylonian, and broader ancient Near Eastern combat myths. In the Ugaritic Baal Cycle (c. 14th-13th century BCE), the sea god Yam is accompanied by a seven-headed serpent called Lotan (Ltn), whom the storm god Baal defeats in cosmic battle — the direct predecessor of the Hebrew Leviathan. The Hebrew Bible preserves this myth in fragmentary references: Psalm 74 describes God breaking 'the heads of Leviathan' and 'the heads of the dragons in the waters'; Isaiah 27:1 prophesies the final day when the Lord 'with his sore and great and strong sword shall punish Leviathan the piercing serpent, even Leviathan that crooked serpent'; and Job 41 provides the most extensive description — a terrifying, fire-breathing sea creature with impenetrable scales, invulnerable to all weapons, ruling the deep as its undisputed king. Crucially, the biblical texts reframe the ancient combat myth: Leviathan is not an equal adversary but a created being under God's control, sometimes depicted as God's pet or plaything. In post-biblical Jewish tradition, the Talmud imagines the righteous feasting on Leviathan's flesh in the world to come, preserved especially for the messianic banquet. Christian demonology absorbed Leviathan into the infernal hierarchy, where medieval grimoires classify him as a demon of envy and a grand admiral of Hell's naval forces. The demonological Leviathan is associated with the sin of envy — an envious serpent gatekeeping forbidden knowledge. Thomas Hobbes' 1651 political treatise Leviathan appropriated the name for the sovereign state, drawing on the biblical monster's imagery of overwhelming power to describe the social contract's creation of an all-powerful authority. In the Book of Revelation, the 'beast from the sea' echoes Leviathan's imagery. Leviathan represents the raw, untamed power of chaos — the primordial deep that God alone can subdue — and its mythological DNA persists through dragon-slaying narratives from Tiamat to Marduk, Lotan to Baal, Python to Apollo, into modern fantasy's sea monsters and kaiju.
