Who It Is

In the grim darkness of the far future, there is only war—and decay. Nurgle, the Plague God, is the Chaos God of disease, death, and rebirth, a paradox of filth and fecundity, despair and joy. He is the Lord of Decay, the Grandfather of Plagues, whose garden teems with rot and renewal. His influence seeps through the Imperium of Man like a slow, inexorable plague, promising an end to suffering in the embrace of corruption.

Nurgle is paradoxically depicted as a jovial, grandfatherly figure who loves his followers and sees his plagues as gifts. He is patient, nurturing, and deeply caring for his daemons and mortal followers, offering them freedom from pain and despair. He is not malicious in the way other Chaos Gods are; he genuinely believes he is bringing relief from suffering. He is also extremely resilient and patient, willing to wait millennia for his plans to come to fruition.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Nurgle' is likely derived from the Old English 'hnur' (to grumble) or the German 'nörgeln' (to nag), fitting his role as a grumbling, paternal figure. It may also be a corruption of 'nurgle' from the works of Michael Moorcock, specifically the god 'Nur' or 'Nurgh'.

Powers
Disease and Plague Creation

Nurgle can create and control any form of disease, from simple infections to supernatural plagues that can affect even daemons and machines. These plagues can be tailored to affect specific targets or spread indiscriminately.

Limit: His plagues are not instantaneous; they take time to spread and take effect. Some powerful beings may be immune or resistant to his plagues.

Immortality and Regeneration

Nurgle himself is immortal and cannot be truly killed. His daemons are also extremely resilient, able to regenerate from wounds and even reform from destruction. Nurgle's followers often gain increased resilience and resistance to pain.

Limit: Daemons can be permanently banished if destroyed in the presence of a powerful enough psychic or holy force. Nurgle's power is diminished if his worship wanes or if he is distracted.

Corruption and Mutation

Nurgle's influence can corrupt living beings, causing them to mutate into plague-ridden monstrosities or become his daemonic servants. This corruption can affect both body and mind, making followers more resilient but also more decayed.

Limit: The corruption is gradual and may be resisted by strong will or psychic power. Some individuals may be corrupted against their will, but others embrace it willingly.

Psychic Power

As a Chaos God, Nurgle possesses immense psychic power, able to influence the Warp and manifest his will across the galaxy. He can grant his followers psychic powers related to disease and decay, such as the ability to spread plagues or sense weakness.

Limit: His power is limited in the material universe; he can only act through his followers and daemons. His influence is also challenged by other Chaos Gods.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Nurgle is described as a vast, bloated, decaying figure, covered in sores, boils, and open wounds. He is often depicted as a giant, corpulent humanoid with a grotesque, fly-like face, and his body is a breeding ground for countless diseases and parasites. He is sometimes shown with a massive, decaying antlered head or a fly's head. His presence is accompanied by clouds of flies, stench, and a sense of decay.

Symbols — Nurgle's symbols include the Nurgle symbol (a three-lobed shape resembling a fly or a plague heart), flies, maggots, and the number seven (sacred to him). His colors are typically green, brown, and yellow, representing decay and disease. His daemons often carry plagues, cauldrons, and other instruments of contagion.

Domains — Nurgle's domain in the Warp is the Garden of Nurgle, a vast, decaying landscape filled with stagnant pools, rotting trees, and all manner of disease and decay. Despite its repulsiveness, it is a place of life and renewal, where new diseases are born and nurtured. The garden is said to be the source of all plagues in the universe.

Accounts I
The Fall of the Death GuardHorus Heresy series, particularly 'The Buried Dagger' by James Swallow

During the Horus Heresy (30th millennium)

The Death Guard Legion, led by Primarch Mortarion, was trapped in the Warp during the Horus Heresy. They were subjected to Nurgle's plagues and eventually succumbed to his corruption. Mortarion, after a long and agonizing struggle, finally gave in to Nurgle to save his legion, becoming his Daemon Prince. This story establishes the Death Guard as Nurgle's primary mortal followers. The Death Guard became a Chaos Space Marine Legion dedicated to Nurgle, spreading plagues across the galaxy. Mortarion became a Daemon Prince of Nurgle, and Typhus became the herald of Nurgle's plagues.

Accounts II
The Plague WarsDark Imperium trilogy by Guy Haley

M42 (42nd millennium)

After the Great Rift opened, Nurgle launched a massive invasion of the Imperium, particularly focusing on Ultramar, the realm of Roboute Guilliman. The Plague Wars saw the Death Guard and Nurgle's daemons spread plagues across countless worlds. Guilliman fought back, eventually confronting Mortarion and banishing him, but the war left Ultramar devastated. Ultramar was heavily damaged, but Guilliman's leadership and the intervention of the Emperor's forces repelled the invasion. The war highlighted Nurgle's patient, grinding approach to conquest.

The Garden of NurgleVarious sources, including 'Codex: Chaos Daemons' and Black Library novels

Timeless

The Garden of Nurgle is Nurgle's realm in the Warp. It is a place of decay and rebirth, where Nurgle tends to his plagues and nurtures his daemons. The garden is described as both repulsive and strangely beautiful, with a sense of life blooming from decay. The garden is the source of all plagues and the home of Nurgle's daemons. It is a place of constant creation and destruction, reflecting Nurgle's nature.
