Who It Is

In the heart of Narnia, where the lamplight spills across a cozy dam, a small, bustling figure stirs the tea and measures the world in quiet wisdom. Mrs. Beaver, the first true hostess of the Pevensie children, is more than a talking animal—she is the anchor of hearth and home in a land under ice, whose practical kindness and unshakable loyalty prove that even the smallest paws can steady a kingdom.

Mrs. Beaver is practical, hospitable, and quietly wise. She is initially cautious about the children, but quickly becomes a nurturing figure, providing them with food and shelter. She is also brave, as she joins the journey to the Stone Table despite the danger. She is known for her excellent tea-making, but Mr. Beaver jokes that she is an indifferent cook, a playful jab that shows their affectionate relationship.

Powers
Hospitality and Domestic Skills

Mrs. Beaver is an expert at providing food, shelter, and comfort. She makes tea, prepares meals, and maintains a warm home.

Limit: No supernatural powers; her abilities are mundane but essential.

Practical Wisdom

She offers sensible advice and helps the children understand the situation in Narnia, such as explaining the White Witch's evil.

Limit: She is not omniscient and relies on common sense.

Courage and Loyalty

Despite the danger, she supports her husband and the children, joining them on the dangerous journey to meet Aslan.

Limit: She is a non-combatant and relies on others for protection.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Mrs. Beaver is described as a female beaver, likely with the characteristic flat tail, brown fur, and small, bright eyes. In the 2005 film, she is depicted as a plump, motherly beaver wearing a simple apron and cap, emphasizing her domestic role. In the book, her appearance is not detailed beyond being a beaver, but her actions suggest a neat and practical demeanor.

Symbols — She is associated with domesticity, hospitality, and maternal care. Her sewing machine and the act of making tea are iconic. In adaptations, she is often shown with a sewing needle or a teapot, symbolizing her role as a homemaker.

Domains — She lives in a dam on the Great River of Narnia, near the lamppost. The dam is a cozy, warm shelter with a fire, and it serves as a safe haven for the Pevensie children. She is deeply connected to the natural world of Narnia, particularly the river and the surrounding woods.

Accounts I
A Day with the BeaversThe Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, Chapter 7

1940s (writing), Narnian time: Winter of the White Witch

The Pevensie children meet Mr. and Mrs. Beaver, who take them to their dam. Mrs. Beaver provides them with a hearty meal, including fresh fish and potatoes, and they learn about Aslan and the prophecy. The Beavers agree to help the children find Aslan at the Stone Table. The children gain crucial allies and information, setting them on the path to fulfill the prophecy.

Accounts II
The Escape from the DamThe Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, Chapter 8

Narnian winter

After Edmund leaves to join the White Witch, the Beavers and the other children must flee immediately. Mrs. Beaver packs essentials, including a sewing machine, and they escape just as the Witch's wolves arrive. They travel through the snowy forest to the Stone Table. The group safely reaches Aslan, and the Beavers witness the beginning of the end of the Witch's reign.
