The Approach

In the heart of Narnia, where the lamplight spills across a cozy dam, a small, bustling figure stirs the tea and measures the world in quiet wisdom. Mrs. Beaver, the first true hostess of the Pevensie children, is more than a talking animal—she is the anchor of hearth and home in a land under ice, whose practical kindness and unshakable loyalty prove that even the smallest paws can steady a kingdom.

Content advisory. None. The character is a wholesome, maternal figure in a children's fantasy series.
Identity

Mrs. Beaver

Pronunciation /mɪsɪz ˈbiːvər/

Known As

Mrs. Beaver (wife of Mr. Beaver)Beaver's wife

Etymology

The name 'Beaver' is the species name; 'Mrs.' is a title of respect for a married woman, used in the English tradition.

Cultural Ownership

British literary tradition; created by C.S. Lewis as part of the Narnia series.

Disputed Identifications

None. She is a unique character in the Narnia canon, not conflated with other beaver characters.

Description

Appearance

Mrs. Beaver is described as a female beaver, likely with the characteristic flat tail, brown fur, and small, bright eyes. In the 2005 film, she is depicted as a plump, motherly beaver wearing a simple apron and cap, emphasizing her domestic role. In the book, her appearance is not detailed beyond being a beaver, but her actions suggest a neat and practical demeanor.

Symbols & Iconography

She is associated with domesticity, hospitality, and maternal care. Her sewing machine and the act of making tea are iconic. In adaptations, she is often shown with a sewing needle or a teapot, symbolizing her role as a homemaker.

Behaviour & Personality

Mrs. Beaver is practical, hospitable, and quietly wise. She is initially cautious about the children, but quickly becomes a nurturing figure, providing them with food and shelter. She is also brave, as she joins the journey to the Stone Table despite the danger. She is known for her excellent tea-making, but Mr. Beaver jokes that she is an indifferent cook, a playful jab that shows their affectionate relationship.

Domains & Habitats

She lives in a dam on the Great River of Narnia, near the lamppost. The dam is a cozy, warm shelter with a fire, and it serves as a safe haven for the Pevensie children. She is deeply connected to the natural world of Narnia, particularly the river and the surrounding woods.

Version Differences

In the 2005 film, Mrs. Beaver is given a more prominent role, with additional dialogue and scenes, such as helping the children escape the White Witch's wolves. Her character is visually more anthropomorphic, with clothing and a more expressive face. In the book, her role is smaller but equally significant. The BBC 1988 adaptation also includes her, but with less detail. Overall, her core traits remain consistent.

A Day with the Beavers

Source The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe, Chapter 7Culture British children's literaturePeriod 1940s (writing), Narnian time: Winter of the White Witch

Summary

The Pevensie children meet Mr. and Mrs. Beaver, who take them to their dam. Mrs. Beaver provides them with a hearty meal, including fresh fish and potatoes, and they learn about Aslan and the prophecy. The Beavers agree to help the children find Aslan at the Stone Table.
