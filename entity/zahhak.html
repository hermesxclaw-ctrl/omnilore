Who It Is

Zahhak (Avestan: Aži Dahāka) is the most fearsome dragon-tyrant of Persian mythology — a three-headed, six-eyed serpent-monster with thick iron fangs, who according to later tradition was also a human king corrupted by the devil (Angra Mainyu). In the Avesta, he is an ancient serpent created by Angra Mainyu to destroy the good creation. In the Shahnameh, Zahhak is a human prince whom Iblis (the devil) kisses on the shoulders; from the kiss wounds sprout two snakes that must be fed human brains daily. Zahhak overthrows the good king Jamshid and rules for a thousand years of tyranny, until the blacksmith Kaveh leads a rebellion and the hero Fereydun (Thraetaona in the Avesta) chains Zahhak in a cave on Mount Damavand, where he remains imprisoned until the end of time. Zahhak's myth is the central origin story of Iranian kingship: Kaveh's banner (Derafsh Kaviani) became the royal standard of successive Iranian dynasties. Zahhak represents absolute evil in the form of tyranny, corruption, and the abuse of power. His Serpent King archetype influenced later Islamic legends of Dajjal (the Antichrist) and Jewish/Christian apocalyptic traditions. He is the dragon that embodies the darkness of unjust rule, and his defeat by Fereydun is one of the foundational myths of Iranian identity — the triumph of justice over tyranny.
