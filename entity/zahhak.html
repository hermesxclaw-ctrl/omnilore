Who It Is

In the beginning, there was a serpent with three heads and six eyes, born of the primal lie, who would one day wear the crown of the world and feed on the brains of the innocent. His name is Zahhak, the tyrant king of Iranian myth, whose shadow stretches from the sacred fires of Zoroastrianism to the epic verses of Ferdowsi, and whose legacy still slithers through the languages and legends of an entire region.

In the Avesta, he is demonic and evil, the son of Ahriman, seeking to depopulate the world. In the Shāhnāmeh, he is handsome and clever but weak-willed, easily influenced by Ahriman, and becomes a cruel tyrant who feeds on human brains.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Zahhak derives from the Avestan 'Aži Dahāka'. 'Aži' means 'serpent' or 'dragon'. The meaning of 'Dahāka' is uncertain; suggestions include 'stinging', 'burning', 'manlike', 'huge', or 'foreign'. The Persian form 'Zahhāk' was influenced by the Arabic 'ḍaḥḥāk' meaning 'one who laughs'. In Zor

Pronunciation — Zah-HĀK (Persian: ضحاک). Avestan: Aži Dahāka (AH-zhi da-HAH-ka).

Aliases — Aži Dahāka, Dahāg, Zahhāk-e Tāzī, Zahāk, Azhdahāk

Ownership — Iranian (Persian) mythology, Zoroastrianism, and later Persian epic literature. The figure is also present in Armenian, Kurdish, and other regional traditions.

Powers
Serpent shoulders

Two black snakes grow from Zahhāk's shoulders after Ahriman kisses them. They require daily feeding of two human brains to keep them calm.

Limit: The snakes cannot be removed; cutting one results in another growing in its place.

Supernatural strength and longevity

Aži Dahāka is described as strong and cunning, ruling for a thousand years.

Limit: He is ultimately defeated by Thraetaona/Frēdōn.

Sorcery and demonic aid

Zahhāk rules with the aid of demons (daevas) and is described as a sorcerer.

Limit: His power is ultimately subservient to Ahriman.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Avesta, Aži Dahāka is described as a monstrous serpent with three mouths, six eyes, and three heads, cunning and strong. In the Shāhnāmeh, Zahhāk is human but has two black serpents growing from his shoulders, which cannot be removed without regrowth.

Symbols — Serpents/dragons, three heads, shoulders with snakes. In art, often depicted as a tyrant with snakes on his shoulders, or as a dragon.

Domains — The inaccessible fortress of Kuuiirinta in the land of Baβri (possibly Babylon). Later, his seat of power is in Iran, and he is imprisoned on Mount Damāvand.

Accounts I
The Avestan Myth of Aži DahākaAvesta, Dēnkard

Ancient (c. 1000 BCE - 1000 CE)

Aži Dahāka, the three-headed dragon, is the son of Ahriman. He attacks Yima (Jamshid) and cuts him in half with a saw, but is beaten back by the fire god Ātar. After Yima's death, Dahāg becomes king and rules for a thousand years, oppressing the world. The hero Thraetaona (Frēdōn) eventually defeats him, but cannot kill him because vermin emerge from his wounds. Instead, he chains him and imprisons him on Mount Damāvand. At the end of time, Dahāg will break free and ravage the world until the hero Kirsāsp kills him. Aži Dahāka is imprisoned until the end of the world, but his eventual release and destruction are prophesied.

Accounts II
Zahhāk in the ShāhnāmehShāhnāmeh by Ferdowsi

c. 1000 CE

Zahhāk, son of Merdās, is seduced by Ahriman, who convinces him to kill his father and take his throne. Ahriman then appears as a cook and, after feeding Zahhāk lavish meals, kisses his shoulders, causing two snakes to grow there. A physician (Ahriman in disguise) advises feeding the snakes with human brains. Zahhāk becomes emperor after defeating Jamshid, whom he saws in half. His daily tyranny involves killing two men to feed the snakes, until two cooks, Armayel and Garmayel, save one man each day by substituting sheep brains. These saved men become the ancestors of the Kurds. Zahhāk's reign ends when Fereydun, the son of Abtin, defeats him and chains him on Mount Damāvand. Zahhāk is overthrown and imprisoned, and Fereydun becomes the new king. The Kurds are said to descend from the saved men.

Zahhāk is purely a dragon in all traditions. — In the Avesta he is a dragon, but in the Shāhnāmeh he is human with snakes on his shoulders.

Zahhāk is the origin of all evil. — He is a personification of evil, but Ahriman is the ultimate source of evil in Zoroastrianism.
