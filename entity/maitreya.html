I sit in the Tushita heaven and watch the wheel of dharma slow below. When it stops, I will descend—not in anger, but in love, to turn it once more.

Who It Is

Maitreya is the Future Buddha, the successor to the historical Buddha Shakyamuni in the office of the supremely awakened one who turns the wheel of dharma in a new age. His name derives from the Sanskrit word 'maitri' (Pali: metta), meaning loving-kindness or benevolence—the quality he most perfectly embodies. According to Buddhist prophecy, Maitreya currently resides in Tushita Heaven, the fourth of the six heavens of the desire realm, where he awaits the moment when the dharma taught by Shakyamuni has been completely forgotten in the human world. At that time, human lifespan will have declined catastrophically and then rebounded to 80,000 years. Maitreya will descend to the earthly realm, be born in the city of Ketumati (identified by some with Varanasi), achieve enlightenment under a dragon-flower tree, and teach the dharma to a vast assembly, liberating countless beings. This messianic expectation has generated powerful movements throughout Buddhist history. In China during the turbulent period between the Sui and Tang dynasties, numerous rebellions invoked Maitreya's coming, most notably those associated with the White Lotus Society. In Korea, Maitreya (Mireuk) was syncretized with an indigenous creator deity. In Theravada countries like Thailand and Myanmar, Maitreya's future coming is a source of aspiration: devotees make offerings and perform good deeds with the prayer that they may be reborn in time to meet the Future Buddha. In Mahayana iconography, Maitreya is depicted both as a bodhisattva (adorned, seated in the 'European' posture with legs pendant, ready to descend) and as a Buddha. The colossal Maitreya Buddha of Leshan, China, carved into a cliff face in the 8th century, is the largest stone Buddha in the world.
