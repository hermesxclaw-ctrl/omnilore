Who It Is

Au Co (Âu Cơ) is the fairy mother of the Vietnamese nation—the mountain immortal whose union with the sea dragon Lac Long Quan produced the hundred sons from whom the Vietnamese people trace their descent. She is the embodiment of the mountain and bird clan lineage, representing the highlands, the forests, and the ethereal realm of the immortals (tiên), in dynamic complement to the watery, draconic nature of her husband. According to the traditional account preserved in the Linh Nam Chich Quai, Au Co was a fairy descended from the bird clan of the high mountains, a being of extraordinary beauty who could traverse the skies. She encountered Lac Long Quan during his travels and they fell deeply in love. Their marriage—the fusion of the dragon and the fairy—is the mythological foundation of Vietnamese identity. When Au Co gave birth, she did so in a manner befitting her supernatural nature: she laid not a single child but a sac containing one hundred eggs, from which hatched one hundred sons. Her sons required no nursing and grew to adulthood with remarkable speed and natural capability. The subsequent separation of the couple—Au Co taking fifty sons to the mountains, Lac Long Quan taking fifty to the sea—is not a tragedy in Vietnamese tradition but a foundational act that established the full geographic and cultural range of the Vietnamese people, from the highland forests to the coastal plains. Au Co taught her sons the ways of the mountains: planting, gathering, and the arts of the forest. Her legacy is commemorated across Vietnam, with temples dedicated to her worship. The 'Mother Au Co Temple' (Đền Mẫu Âu Cơ) in Phu Tho province is an important pilgrimage site. Together with Lac Long Quan, she stands at the head of the Vietnamese national genealogy, and the phrase 'con Rồng cháu Tiên' (children of the dragon, grandchildren of the fairy) remains the most potent and widely recognized expression of Vietnamese ethnic identity.
