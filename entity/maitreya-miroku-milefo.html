Who It Is

In the far future, when the memory of the Buddha's teachings has nearly faded from the world, a new Buddha will arise. His name is Maitreya, the 'Loving One,' and he already waits in the heaven of Tushita, watching over the cosmos until the time is right to descend and rekindle the Dharma. For over two millennia, Buddhists have looked to him as a beacon of hope, a promise that enlightenment and compassion will never be lost forever.

Maitreya is characterized as a bodhisattva of loving-kindness (maitrī). He is compassionate, patient, and devoted to the welfare of all beings. He is also a teacher, having revealed the Yogacara scriptures to Asanga.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Derived from Sanskrit 'maitrī' (friendliness, loving-kindness), which in turn comes from 'mitra' (friend). The Pali form is Metteyya. The name is also related to the Indo-Iranian deity Mitra.

Pronunciation — MY-tray-ya (Sanskrit), MET-tay-ya (Pali), MEE-ro-ku (Japanese), MEE-lay-fo (Chinese)

Aliases — Metteyya, Ajita, Miroku, Milefo, Jampa, Pakpa Jampa

Ownership — Primarily Buddhist, but also appears in Hindu prophecies as the king of Shambala and is associated with the Kalki Avatar.

Powers
Bodhisattva powers

As a bodhisattva, Maitreya possesses great spiritual powers, including the ability to visit numerous worlds, teach the Dharma, and perform miracles.

Limit: He has not yet attained Buddhahood, so his powers are not fully perfected.

Future Buddhahood

Maitreya will attain full enlightenment and become the next Buddha, teaching the Dharma to countless beings.

Limit: This will occur only after the current Dharma has been forgotten.

Revelation of Yogacara

Maitreya is credited with revealing the Yogacara scriptures to Asanga, including the Mahāyānasūtrālamkārakā and Madhyāntavibhāga.

Limit: This is a traditional belief, not historically verified.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Maitreya is often depicted as a standing or seated figure on a throne, dressed as a northern Indian nobleman or prince with a full head of hair, flowing robes, and jewels. In Gandharan art, he has a distinctive long hair loop folded at the top of the head. He often holds a vase or bottle (kamaṇḍalu), a symbol of immortality and abundance, which he shares with Brahma.

Symbols — Key symbols include the kamaṇḍalu (water vessel), the hair loop, and the throne. In Tibetan Buddhism, the vase is called a bumpa (wisdom urn). He may also be depicted in a heavenly realm, indicating his residence in Tushita.

Domains — Currently resides in Tushita heaven, where he awaits his final rebirth. He will descend to Earth when the time is right, and his future realm is described as a paradise where the Dharma flourishes.

Accounts I
The Prophecy of the Coming BuddhaCakkavatti-Sīhanāda Sutta

c. 5th-3rd century BCE

The Buddha Gautama prophesies that in the distant future, when the human lifespan has increased, a Buddha named Metteyya will arise. He will be a world teacher, and many beings will attain enlightenment. Establishes the hope of a future Buddha and the continuity of the Dharma.

Accounts II
Asanga's Vision of MaitreyaTibetan tradition

c. 4th century CE

The scholar Asanga meditated on Maitreya for years and finally had a vision of him. Maitreya took him to Tushita heaven and revealed the Yogacara teachings, which Asanga wrote down. Founding of the Yogacara school.

Sudhana's Visit to Maitreya's PalaceGaṇḍavyūha Sutra

c. 3rd century CE

In his pilgrimage, Sudhana meets Maitreya, who teaches him and allows him to enter his magnificent palace, symbolizing the entire bodhisattva path. Sudhana gains profound insight into the bodhisattva career.
