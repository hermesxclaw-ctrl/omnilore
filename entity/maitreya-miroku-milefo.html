Archive Dossier — Identity

Maitreya

Pronunciation MY-tray-ya (Sanskrit), MET-tay-ya (Pali), MEE-ro-ku (Japanese), MEE-lay-fo (Chinese)

Known As

MetteyyaAjitaMirokuMilefoJampaPakpa Jampa

Etymology

Derived from Sanskrit 'maitrī' (friendliness, loving-kindness), which in turn comes from 'mitra' (friend). The Pali form is Metteyya. The name is also related to the Indo-Iranian deity Mitra.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily Buddhist, but also appears in Hindu prophecies as the king of Shambala and is associated with the Kalki Avatar.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have speculated a connection to Indo-Iranian deities like Mitra and the Zoroastrian Saoshyant, but this is disputed. There is also debate over whether Maitreya is a historical figure or purely mythical.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the far future, when the memory of the Buddha's teachings has nearly faded from the world, a new Buddha will arise. His name is Maitreya, the 'Loving One,' and he already waits in the heaven of Tushita, watching over the cosmos until the time is right to descend and rekindle the Dharma. For over two millennia, Buddhists have looked to him as a beacon of hope, a promise that enlightenment and compassion will never be lost forever.

Maitreya is the bodhisattva who will become the next Buddha in this world cycle, succeeding Gautama Buddha. He currently resides in Tuṣita Heaven (the Heaven of Joy), where he awaits the proper time to take his final rebirth among humans.

Who It Is

Maitreya is the bodhisattva who will become the next Buddha in this world cycle, succeeding Gautama Buddha. He currently resides in Tuṣita Heaven (the Heaven of Joy), where he awaits the proper time to take his final rebirth among humans. His name means 'The Friendly One' or 'The Loving One,' reflecting the quality of loving-kindness (maitri/metta) that will define his dispensation. According to prophecy, Maitreya will be born during a golden age when the human lifespan is 80,000 years, when all beings are peaceful and prosperous. He will rediscover the path to enlightenment and teach it to a world ready to receive it. Maitreya is unique among bodhisattvas because his Buddhahood is prophesied and certain — he is the 'future Buddha' in the same way that Gautama was the 'present Buddha.' In art, Maitreya is often depicted seated on a throne with his feet on the ground (indicating his imminent descent), or as a royal figure with a stupa in his crown. In China, the 'Laughing Buddha' (Budai) became identified as an emanation of Maitreya. Maitreya is especially important in Theravada Buddhism as the only bodhisattva recognized in the Pali Canon, and in Mahayana as a symbol of hope for the future.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Maitreya is often depicted as a standing or seated figure on a throne, dressed as a northern Indian nobleman or prince with a full head of hair, flowing robes, and jewels. In Gandharan art, he has a distinctive long hair loop folded at the top of the head. He often holds a vase or bottle (kamaṇḍalu), a symbol of immortality and abundance, which he shares with Brahma.

Symbols & Iconography

Key symbols include the kamaṇḍalu (water vessel), the hair loop, and the throne. In Tibetan Buddhism, the vase is called a bumpa (wisdom urn). He may also be depicted in a heavenly realm, indicating his residence in Tushita.

Behaviour & Personality

Maitreya is characterized as a bodhisattva of loving-kindness (maitrī). He is compassionate, patient, and devoted to the welfare of all beings. He is also a teacher, having revealed the Yogacara scriptures to Asanga.

Domains & Habitats

Currently resides in Tushita heaven, where he awaits his final rebirth. He will descend to Earth when the time is right, and his future realm is described as a paradise where the Dharma flourishes.

Version Differences

In Theravada, he is a future Buddha but less emphasized. In Mahayana, he is a prominent bodhisattva and founder of Yogacara. In Vajrayana, he is a meditational deity and associated with the future. Some traditions view him as already enlightened, while others see him as still a bodhisattva.

Origins

Maitreya is mentioned in the Pali Canon (Dīgha Nikāya) as the next Buddha. In Mahayana, he is associated with the Buddhist sage Asanga, who is said to have visited Maitreya in Tuṣita Heaven and received the Five Treatises of Maitreya, which founded the Yogacara school.

Attested in Pali Canon (Dīgha Nikāya) / Mahayana SutrasEra c. 3rd century BCE onward

Archive Dossier — Powers

Bodhisattva powers

Source Version Mahayana

Description

As a bodhisattva, Maitreya possesses great spiritual powers, including the ability to visit numerous worlds, teach the Dharma, and perform miracles.

Limitations

He has not yet attained Buddhahood, so his powers are not fully perfected.

Contradictions

Some traditions consider him already enlightened, while others do not.

Future Buddhahood

Source Version All schools

Description

Maitreya will attain full enlightenment and become the next Buddha, teaching the Dharma to countless beings.

Limitations

This will occur only after the current Dharma has been forgotten.

Contradictions

None significant.

Revelation of Yogacara

Source Version Mahayana

Description

Maitreya is credited with revealing the Yogacara scriptures to Asanga, including the Mahāyānasūtrālamkārakā and Madhyāntavibhāga.

Limitations

This is a traditional belief, not historically verified.

Contradictions

Some scholars doubt the historical existence of a person named Maitreya who revealed these texts.
