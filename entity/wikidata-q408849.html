Form

No detailed physical description survives in the primary sources. She is a female deity of the Tuatha Dé Danann, likely depicted in later art with healing herbs or a well, but this is artistic interpretation. Her name is often associated with a 'green' or 'herbal' aspect.

Powers

Herbal Healing Airmed is a master herbalist. She is described as gathering herbs and using them to heal the wounded after the Second Battle of Mag Tuired. Her knowledge is so profound that she can cure nearly any ailment, though the specifics of her methods are not detailed. Cath Maige Tuired Her power, while great, is not absolute. She cannot restore life to the truly dead, as her brother Miach's death demonstrates. Her healing is a practical art, not a resurrection spell. The Dindshenchas tradition presents her as a well-goddess, where healing is a property of the well itself, not her personal craft. This suggests two distinct traditions or a later evolution of her myth.

Herbal Knowledge (The 'Cloak' of 365 Herbs) Airmed is said to have collected 365 herbs to heal her slain brother. This number is symbolic, possibly representing all the herbs in the world or the days of the year, suggesting a total or complete knowledge of the herbal realm. Cath Maige Tuired This knowledge is incomplete in the narrative, as her father destroys her collection, leaving her with only a fraction of the knowledge. None recorded.

Behavior & Habitat

Her behavior is defined by her role as a healer. She is methodical, intelligent, and compassionate, working alongside her father and brother to restore the wounded after battle. Her actions are focused and practical, driven by a desire to preserve life.

Accounts

After the Second Battle of Mag Tuired, Airmed, along with her father Dian Cecht and brother Miach, works to heal the wounded warriors of the Tuatha Dé Danann. She uses her extensive knowledge of herbs to treat injuries and restore the soldiers to health.

After Miach, Airmed's brother, surpasses their father Dian Cecht in healing skill (by successfully replacing a severed arm), Dian Cecht, in a fit of jealous rage, kills Miach. Airmed, grieving, collects 365 herbs from her brother's grave, which are said to be the herbs that could restore him. Dian Cecht, enraged again, scatters the herbs, destroying the knowledge of how to use them.

Scholarly Debates
Nature of her Healing
Airmed is a personal healer who uses her herbal knowledge as a skill. ⇌ Airmed is a guardian of a healing well, and the healing is a property of the well, not her own power.
Evidence for A: Cath Maige Tuired (her active role in healing and gathering herbs).
Evidence for B: Metrical Dindshenchas (poem on 'Airmed's Well', which describes the well's healing properties).
What We Don't Know

The primary sources are fragmentary. There is no surviving description of her appearance or personality beyond her actions. The Dindshenchas tradition is under-researched in relation to the Cath Maige Tuired. Her potential connection to other Celtic healing deities (like Sirona or Nantosuelta) is a matter of speculation. No archaeological evidence directly links to her worship.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Airmed as the 'lost healer' — a brilliant medical researcher whose work was destroyed by a jealous superior. She's the mythological embodiment of 'what could have been' in the field of medicine. She's also a powerful figure for anyone who has ever felt their potential was thwarted by the pettiness of others. In the modern world, she'd be the brilliant scientist whose groundbreaking cure is buried by a rival's ego.
