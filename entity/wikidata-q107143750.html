Who It Is

In the mists of Arthurian legend, where swords are forged in myth and kings are crowned by fate, there exists a spear whose name echoes through the halls of Camelot—Rhongomyniad, the lance of King Arthur. Unlike the famed Excalibur, this weapon is a shadow in the chronicles, mentioned in passing by medieval poets yet never given a full saga. Its very obscurity invites wonder: What power did it hold? Why did bards sing of it? And what became of it after the fall of the Round Table?

As an object, it has no personality, but it is consistently associated with Arthur's prowess in battle. It is a weapon of war, not a magical artifact with a will of its own.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is Welsh, derived from 'rhon' (spear, lance, pike) and 'gomynt' or 'gwmyn' (related to 'gwm' meaning 'battle' or 'conflict'). Thus, Rhongomyniad broadly translates as 'the battle-spear' or 'the lance of conflict.' Some scholars suggest a connection to 'rhong' (piercing) and 'myniad' (going,

Pronunciation — Approximately 'hron-go-MIN-yad' (Welsh: [r̥ɔŋɡɔˈmɨnjad])

Aliases — Rhongomyn, Ron, Rhongomyniad (spear), Rhongomyniad (lance)

Ownership — Welsh and broader Arthurian tradition. The spear is named in Welsh texts and later appears in English and French Arthurian romances, though its primary identity is Celtic.

Powers
Battle spear

Rhongomyniad is a spear used by King Arthur in combat. It is described as a 'spear of war' that strikes fear into enemies. There is no evidence of magical properties.

Limit: None described; it is a mundane weapon, though of high quality.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The only direct description comes from 'The Dream of Rhonabwy,' where the spear is described as having a 'red tip' and being 'long and sharp.' The shaft is not described, but it is implied to be sturdy and fit for a king. No other physical details are given in any source.

Symbols — In the Dream of Rhonabwy, the spear is one of the few named items of Arthur, symbolizing his martial power and sovereignty. Its red tip may evoke bloodshed and the violence of kingship.

Domains — It exists solely within the Arthurian world, appearing in battle scenes and as part of Arthur's armory. No specific realm or habitat is attached to it.

Accounts I
The Dream of RhonabwyThe Mabinogion

13th century (story set in Arthur's time)

In this dream-vision, Rhonabwy, a warrior of a later era, falls asleep and enters Arthur's world. He sees Arthur and his knights preparing for battle against the Saxons at the Battle of Badon. Arthur is depicted playing a game of gwyddbwyll (a board game) with his knight Owain, while his army waits. Rhongomyniad is mentioned as Arthur's spear, but it plays no active role in the story. The story ends with Arthur's army defeating the Saxons, but Rhongomyniad's specific role is not detailed.

Accounts II
Culhwch and OlwenWelsh prose

11th–12th century

In this tale, Arthur and his warriors help Culhwch win Olwen. Arthur's spear is mentioned among his weapons, but the name Rhongomyniad is not in the earliest text. Some later manuscripts add the name. Arthur's assistance is crucial, but the spear itself is a background detail.

Alliterative Morte ArthureMiddle English poem

c. 1400

In this poem, Arthur fights a giant and later battles the Roman emperor Lucius. His spear is called 'Rhongomyniad' (spelled 'Roun' or 'Rone') and is used in the thick of battle, but it is not given a special role. Arthur wins his battles, and the spear is part of his armament.
