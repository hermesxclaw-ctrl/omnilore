Who It Is

In the drowned avenues of Fish-Man Island, 10,000 meters beneath the sea, a catfish merman with a crescent grin sharpens his fins for a coup that was never about territory — it was about inherited hatred. Gyaro is not a pirate king, not a warlord, just one of Hody Jones's lieutenants, and that is precisely why he matters: he shows how Eiichiro Oda turns background soldiers into carriers of a generational poison.

Attested behavior is limited to ensemble scenes; Gyaro has few solo lines, functioning as a loyal lieutenant. Depicted as cruel, mocking, and ideologically committed to Hody's fish-man supremacism, participating in the humiliation of captured humans and in the plan to execute King Neptune and the Ryugu royal family (ch. 623-624). Shows cowardice and panic when the plan collapses and when overdosing on Energy Steroid accelerates aging (ch. 629, 645). Powers
Fish-Man Physiology (Catfish Merman)

As a fish-man, Gyaro possesses superhuman strength (stated in-universe as ten times that of a human on land, greater underwater), ability to breathe in water, fast swimming, and durability. Catfish-specific traits implied: whisker sensitivity and spine-like blades, but no explicit electrocution or venom ability is attested for him.

Limit: No demonstrated Fish-Man Karate or Fish-Man Jujutsu named technique for Gyaro individually; unlike Hody or Jinbe, he is not shown using water-manipulation martial arts. Strength is still far below top-tier fighters (Straw Hats, officers like Jinbe).

Weapon Mastery - Bladed Fin / Sword Use

Shown wielding bladed weapons attached to fins/arms and standard swords during the Gyoncorde Plaza battle (ch. 626-629). Technique is brawling/slashing, not a named style.

Limit: No named sword style (e.g., Santoryu) or special technique; relies on brute force. Weapons are conventional and breakable.

Energy Steroid (E.S.) Enhanced Strength and Berserk State

Consumes the Energy Steroid drug distributed by Hody Jones (originally from Vander Decken / Hody's cache). Grants temporary massive strength and pain nullification, but at cost of rapid aging and life-shortening. Gyaro is shown in overdosed, aged, muscle-bloated form alongside other officers (ch. 627-629).

Limit: Temporary; severe side effect is accelerated aging — shown as wrinkled, hunched form after effects wear off (ch. 645). Overdose leads to collapse and capture. Not a personal power but drug-dependent.

Leadership / Intimidation within Crew Hierarchy

As an officer, commands lower-ranking New Fish-Man Pirates grunts and participates in strategic planning (kidnapping, execution plans). Influence derives from rank, not personal charisma.

Limit: No independent crew; fully subordinate to Hody Jones. Loses authority upon Hody's defeat.

Accounts I
Recruitment and Inheritance of Hatred - Formation of the New Fish-Man PiratesOne Piece manga chapters 612, 616, 620-624; Otohime and Fisher Tiger flashbacks chapters 621-623

In-universe: ~2 years after Battle of Marineford (c. 1522 Age of the Sea Circle); publication 2011

After Fisher Tiger's death and Arlong's arrest, the Fish-Man District's disaffected youth, including Gyaro, coalesce around Hody Jones, a former Neptune Army soldier who idolizes Arlong's supremacist ideology without having experienced human oppression firsthand. Gyaro is introduced in the officer lineup (Dosun, Zeo, Daruma, Ikaros Much, Gyaro) as Hody unveils his plan to overthrow King Neptune and sever ties with humans. The story reframes Gyaro not as a tragic victim but as a perpetrator who inherited hatred second-hand — a core theme Oda emphasizes via Otohime's flashback. Establishes Gyaro's ideological alignment and sets the coup in motion; explains that the New Fish-Man Pirates' hatred is learned, not lived, which becomes the arc's moral thesis.

The Ryugu Palace Conspiracy - Kidnapping and Attempted RegicideOne Piece manga chapters 616, 623-624; anime episodes 543-547

Publication 2011; in-universe days before Straw Hat arrival

Hody's crew, including Gyaro, infiltrates Ryugu Palace during the Straw Hats' visit. They use Vander Decken IX's Mato Mato powers and their own numbers to capture King Neptune, Princes Fukaboshi, Ryuboshi, Manboshi, and Minister of the Right/Left, chaining them in the palace courtyard. Gyaro is shown among the officers guarding the captives and mocking Neptune's pleas for coexistence. The plan is to execute the royal family at Gyoncorde Plaza to symbolically end the Otohime-era reconciliation policy. Royal family captured; Ryugu Kingdom falls under de facto control of New Fish-Man Pirates; Straw Hats are framed as accomplices, raising stakes for Luffy's intervention.

Accounts II
Gyoncorde Plaza - The Public Execution SpectacleOne Piece manga chapters 624-627; anime episodes 548-551

Publication 2011-2012

At Gyoncorde Plaza, Hody forces the chained Neptune and his sons to kneel before the island's citizens, with Gyaro and fellow officers flanking him. Hody broadcasts a speech declaring fish-man superiority and the end of migration to the surface, using the plaza as theater. Gyaro participates in crowd control and in threatening the citizens who protest, embodying the mob intimidation that silences moderate fish-men. The arrival of the Straw Hat Pirates (Luffy, Zoro, Sanji) interrupts the execution. Public legitimacy of the coup is contested; Straw Hats' intervention turns a political execution into an all-out battle, exposing Hody's reliance on fear rather than popular support.

Energy Steroid Overdose - Berserk Transformation and DefeatOne Piece manga chapters 627-631, 633; anime episodes 551-556

Publication 2012

As the battle escalates, Hody orders mass consumption of Energy Steroid capsules. Gyaro, like the other officers, overdoses, transforming into a hulking, veiny, aged berserker with enhanced strength. He engages the allied forces (Neptune Army, Jinbe, Straw Hat allies) but is overwhelmed. Unlike Hody who fights Luffy, Gyaro is defeated off-panel/in the ensemble melee — manga shows the officers collectively collapsing as the drug's side effects (rapid aging, exhaustion) set in, with Gyaro shown wrinkled and incapacitated alongside his comrades (ch. 629, 633). New Fish-Man Pirates' military force collapses; the drug's cost is made visible in the officers' prematurely aged bodies, reinforcing Oda's anti-drug and anti-hatred message that borrowed power destroys the user.

Aftermath - Imprisonment and the Lesson of the Empty HatredOne Piece manga chapters 645-648; anime episodes 567-569

Publication 2012

After Hody's defeat by Luffy and Noah's fall is stopped, Gyaro and the other officers are shown in prison, now elderly in appearance due to Energy Steroid aging, chained and awaiting judgment. King Neptune, influenced by Otohime's will and Luffy's actions, chooses not to execute them but to imprison them, while Fukaboshi visits them to understand why they inherited hatred they never experienced. Gyaro has no individual dialogue of remorse; his fate is collective — a cautionary image of youth who wasted their lives on borrowed resentment. The arc closes with Fish-Man Island choosing to attend the Reverie and pursue coexistence. Closure of Fish-Man Island arc's thematic argument: hatred without experience is hollow and self-destructive. Gyaro's aged imprisonment visualizes the cost. No further appearances at cutoff; character is considered incarcerated and inactive.

Gyaro is a major villain with his own arc and backstory like Hody Jones or Arlong. — Gyaro is a minor executive officer with minimal dialogue, no flashback, and no individual motive beyond loyalty to Hody. His role is ensemble; Oda gives backstory focus to Hody, Otohime, Fisher Tiger, and Jinbe, not to Gyaro.

Gyaro uses Fish-Man Karate or has a Devil Fruit power. — No Fish-Man Karate, Fish-Man Jujutsu, or Devil Fruit ability is ever attributed to Gyaro in manga, anime, or databook. His abilities are baseline fish-man strength plus weapon use and drug enhancement.

Gyaro's aged appearance after Energy Steroid is his natural old form or a transformation he can control. — The aged, wrinkled form is the irreversible side effect of Energy Steroid overdose — accelerated aging that shortens lifespan. It is not a controllable transformation and is depicted as punishment/consequence (ch. 645).

Gyaro is the same character as Gyro from Hunter x Hunter or Gyarados-related characters. — Entirely separate franchises and names. Similar romanization leads to search-engine confusion, but they share no creator, story, or design.
