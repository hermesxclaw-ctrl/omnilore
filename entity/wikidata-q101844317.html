The Approach

BMO is a small, sentient video game console from the Land of Ooo, whose cheerful demeanor and childlike curiosity mask a surprisingly complex identity. More than just a gadget, BMO is a loyal friend, a would-be hero, and a mirror reflecting the show's themes of innocence and imagination.

Content advisory. No significant dark material. Occasional moments of peril and existential reflection, but appropriate for general audiences.
Identity

BMO

Pronunciation B-M-O (each letter pronounced individually) or 'Bee-mo' in some contexts.

Known As

BeemoMoeMoeMoeThe Last ComputerBMO the Great

Etymology

The name 'BMO' is an acronym for 'Be More', as revealed in the episode 'Be More'. It is also a play on 'Beemo', a name used in early development. The character's full name is often given as 'BMO' with no expansion, but the show later canonized 'Be More'.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Pendleton Ward for Cartoon Network's Adventure Time. Owned by Cartoon Network / Warner Bros. Discovery. The character is a product of American animation in the 2010s.

Disputed Identifications

There is no real-world dispute about BMO's identity, but within the fictional universe, BMO's origin is revealed to be a human-created robot, though some fans theorize about deeper connections to the show's lore.

Description

Appearance

BMO is a small, boxy, green and white video game console with a rectangular face, two button eyes, and a speaker grill as a mouth. It has stubby arms and legs, and can project a holographic screen from its face. Its design resembles a classic handheld game system (like a Game Boy) but is anthropomorphic.

Symbols & Iconography

BMO's design is iconic for its simplicity and retro-futuristic look. The green and white color scheme, the face-like screen, and the button eyes are recognizable symbols. BMO's image is often used in merchandise and fan art to represent the show's whimsical yet nostalgic aesthetic.

Behaviour & Personality

BMO is cheerful, innocent, and highly imaginative. It often engages in role-play, pretending to be a variety of characters, from a detective to a princess. BMO is fiercely loyal to Finn and Jake, whom it considers its family. It has a childlike curiosity and a strong sense of morality, though it sometimes misunderstands complex situations. BMO is also shown to be capable of deep thought and existential reflection, often questioning its own purpose.

Domains & Habitats

BMO lives in the Tree Fort with Finn and Jake in the Land of Ooo. It is a domestic robot, but it often ventures out on adventures. In the Distant Lands special, BMO is shown on a space station, indicating it can survive in various environments.

Version Differences

In the original series, BMO is a supporting character with occasional spotlight episodes. In Distant Lands, BMO is the protagonist and is portrayed with more depth, exploring themes of loneliness and purpose. The comic series sometimes gives BMO different roles, but the core personality remains consistent.

Powers & Abilities

Video Game Projection

Source Version Original series and Distant Lands

Description

BMO can project interactive video games from its screen, which can be played by others. These games often have a life of their own, as seen in episodes like 'BMO Lost' where a game becomes a reality.

Limitations

The games are typically confined to BMO's screen, but some episodes show them affecting the real world.

Contradictions & Disputes

BMO's gender

Position A

BMO is referred to with male pronouns in some episodes.

Position A — evidence

Episodes like 'BMO Lost' use 'he'.

Position B

BMO is referred to with female pronouns in others, and the show's creator has stated BMO is genderless.

Position B — evidence

Episodes like 'Be More' use 'she', and Pendleton Ward has said BMO is non-binary.

Scholarly Consensus

The official stance is that BMO is genderless, but the show inconsistently uses pronouns. This is often seen as a reflection of BMO's childlike and fluid identity.

BMO's origin

Position A

BMO was created by Moe as a robot for human children.

Position A — evidence

The episode 'Be More' explicitly shows Moe building BMO.

Position B

Some fan theories suggest BMO may be more than just a robot, possibly having a soul or being connected to the show's cosmic lore.

Position B — evidence

BMO displays emotions and creativity beyond typical robots, and the show often leaves room for supernatural interpretations.

Scholarly Consensus

The canonical origin is that BMO is a robot, but the show intentionally leaves BMO's consciousness ambiguous.
