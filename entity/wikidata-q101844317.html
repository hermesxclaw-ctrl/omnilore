Who It Is

BMO is a small, sentient video game console from the Land of Ooo, whose cheerful demeanor and childlike curiosity mask a surprisingly complex identity. More than just a gadget, BMO is a loyal friend, a would-be hero, and a mirror reflecting the show's themes of innocence and imagination.

BMO is cheerful, innocent, and highly imaginative. It often engages in role-play, pretending to be a variety of characters, from a detective to a princess. BMO is fiercely loyal to Finn and Jake, whom it considers its family. It has a childlike curiosity and a strong sense of morality, though it sometimes misunderstands complex situations. BMO is also shown to be capable of deep thought and existential reflection, often questioning its own purpose.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'BMO' is an acronym for 'Be More', as revealed in the episode 'Be More'. It is also a play on 'Beemo', a name used in early development. The character's full name is often given as 'BMO' with no expansion, but the show later canonized 'Be More'.

Pronunciation — B-M-O (each letter pronounced individually) or 'Bee-mo' in some contexts.

Aliases — Beemo, Moe, MoeMoe, The Last Computer, BMO the Great

Ownership — Created by Pendleton Ward for Cartoon Network's Adventure Time. Owned by Cartoon Network / Warner Bros. Discovery. The character is a product of American animation in the 2010s.

Powers
Video Game Projection

BMO can project interactive video games from its screen, which can be played by others. These games often have a life of their own, as seen in episodes like 'BMO Lost' where a game becomes a reality.

Limit: The games are typically confined to BMO's screen, but some episodes show them affecting the real world.

Multifunctional Robotics

BMO has various built-in functions such as a flashlight, a camera, a music player, and a compass. It can also function as a calculator, a clock, and even a toaster (as seen in one episode).

Limit: These functions are often used for comedic effect and are not always practical.

Self-Sustenance and Survival

BMO can survive in space and other harsh environments, as shown in Distant Lands. It does not require food or water, but it does need battery power or charging.

Limit: BMO's battery can run low, and it needs to recharge.

Imagination and Role-Play

BMO has an incredibly vivid imagination, allowing it to create elaborate fantasies and personas. This is not a physical power but a mental one, enabling BMO to cope with challenges and entertain itself.

Limit: Its imagination can sometimes lead it to misread reality.

Form & Signs

Appearance — BMO is a small, boxy, green and white video game console with a rectangular face, two button eyes, and a speaker grill as a mouth. It has stubby arms and legs, and can project a holographic screen from its face. Its design resembles a classic handheld game system (like a Game Boy) but is anthropomorphic.

Symbols — BMO's design is iconic for its simplicity and retro-futuristic look. The green and white color scheme, the face-like screen, and the button eyes are recognizable symbols. BMO's image is often used in merchandise and fan art to represent the show's whimsical yet nostalgic aesthetic.

Domains — BMO lives in the Tree Fort with Finn and Jake in the Land of Ooo. It is a domestic robot, but it often ventures out on adventures. In the Distant Lands special, BMO is shown on a space station, indicating it can survive in various environments.

Accounts I
BMO's First AppearanceAdventure Time, Season 1, Episode 12: 'Evicted!'

2010

Finn and Jake are evicted from their tree house and move into a new home, where they discover BMO in the basement. BMO quickly becomes part of their household. BMO becomes a permanent member of the Tree Fort.

BMO LostAdventure Time, Season 5, Episode 17

2013

BMO gets lost in the woods and must find its way home, encountering various creatures and having a series of misadventures. It also deals with a malfunctioning game that creates a simulated reality. BMO learns about independence and the value of home.

Accounts II
Be MoreAdventure Time, Season 5, Episode 34

2013

BMO's battery is low, and Finn and Jake take it to be repaired. They discover BMO's origin: it was created by a human scientist named Moe as part of a series of robots. BMO is revealed to be the last of its kind, as the other robots were destroyed. BMO learns about its past and its creator. This episode establishes BMO's origin as a robot.

BMO (Distant Lands)Adventure Time: Distant Lands – 'BMO'

2020

BMO is aboard a space station with a new friend, a small alien named Olive. When the station is attacked, BMO must save the day. The story explores BMO's loneliness and its desire for purpose, and it features a heartwarming conclusion where BMO finds a new family. BMO gains a new companion and a sense of belonging.
