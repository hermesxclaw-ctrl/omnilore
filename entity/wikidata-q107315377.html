The Approach

In the shadowy corners of Greek religious life, where the familiar gods of Olympus gave way to a host of lesser-known spirits and powers, the Prodomeis stand as a puzzling group. Known only through a handful of inscriptions and a single literary mention, these divine beings—whose name suggests 'those who go before' or 'forerunners'—offer a tantalizing glimpse into the rich, local, and often undocumented world of ancient Greek cults. Who were they, and what role did they play in the religious landscape of the ancient Mediterranean? The evidence is sparse, but the questions they raise are as intriguing as the gods themselves.

Content advisory. No dark or disturbing content is known or suspected for this subject. The scarcity of evidence means that much is uncertain, and readers should be aware that the reconstruction of these deities is highly speculative.
Identity

Prodomeis

Pronunciation pro-DOH-mays (English approximation); in Greek: Προδομεῖς (singular: Προδομεύς).

Etymology

The name 'Prodomeis' (Προδομεῖς) is derived from the Greek preposition 'pro' (before) and the root 'dom-' (to build or house), suggesting 'those who build before' or 'those who go before the building'. It may also be related to 'prodromos', meaning 'forerunner' or 'precursor'.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Greek religion, likely a local or regional cult, possibly in the Peloponnese or central Greece, but no specific location is confirmed.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have suggested a connection to the 'Prodomoi', a group of heroes or deities associated with beginnings or foundations, but this is speculative. The name may also be a variant or misspelling of another deity, such as 'Prodromos' (a title of Apollo or Hecate). No consensus exists.

Behaviour & Personality

As deities, they would have been objects of cult worship, but no myths or personality traits are recorded. Their role as 'forerunners' might imply they were invoked at the start of building projects or other enterprises, but this is conjecture.

Domains & Habitats

Likely worshipped in Athens, based on Hesychius's note. Their domain might have been related to building, beginnings, or the protection of foundations, but this is uncertain.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

The evidence for the Prodomeis is extremely limited: a single lexicographical entry and a possible inscription. No archaeological finds, no literary narratives, and no scholarly monograph exist. The nature of their cult, their iconography, their powers, and their relationships remain unknown. The etymology is speculative. The connection to Athens is based on Hesychius alone, and the inscription's reading is uncertain. Further research would require a comprehensive search of epigraphic databases and archaeological reports, but the likelihood of new evidence is low.

No modern scholarly monograph exists, and much of the information is derived from epigraphic corpora and general works on Greek religion.
