Befana

The Christmas Witch, La Vecchia, The Old Woman of Epiphany

I missed the Child once, and I will never miss Him again. Every house I visit, every stocking I fill — perhaps this one is His.

Who It Is

La Befana is the beloved Italian Christmas witch who flies on her broomstick on the night of January 5 (Epiphany Eve), descending chimneys to fill children's stockings with candy and gifts if they've been good, or coal (actually black sugar candy) if they've been bad. The name 'Befana' is a corruption of 'Epifania' (Epiphany). According to the most popular legend, the Three Magi stopped at her house on their way to Bethlehem and invited her to join them. She refused, too busy with housework, but later regretted it. She gathered gifts and tried to follow, but could not find them. Now she flies every year on Epiphany Eve, giving gifts to all children in the hope that one of them is the Christ Child. This syncretizes Christian nativity with pre-Christian Roman traditions — particularly the goddess Strenia (associated with gift-giving at the new year) and the figure of the 'old woman' burned in effigy at winter's end to banish the old year. Befana is depicted as an old, soot-covered woman in rags, flying on a broom, carrying a sack — a benevolent witch contrasted with the more malevolent witch archetype. Children leave wine and snacks for her as offerings. In some regions, she is accompanied by her husband Befano or leaves extra gifts as an apology. The Befana tradition is strongest in central and southern Italy and remains a major part of Italian Christmas celebrations, distinct from Santa Claus (Babbo Natale) who is a more recent import.
