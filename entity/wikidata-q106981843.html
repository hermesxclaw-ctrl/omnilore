The Approach

In the grand, wine-soaked epic of Nonnus, a satyr named Gemon marches east with Dionysus's army—a name that literally means 'load' or 'burden,' hinting at a figure who carried weight in more ways than one, yet remains a shadowy presence in the vast Dionysiaca.

Content advisory. None; ancient mythological content with no explicit dark material, though it includes typical martial themes of conquest.

Gemon

Etymology

From Ancient Greek Γέμων (Gémōn), meaning 'load' or 'burden' (from γέμω, 'to be full').

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology (specifically in the literary tradition of Nonnus, a Greek poet of Late Antiquity)

Source Nonnus, Dionysiaca, Book 14Culture Greek (Late Antiquity)Period 5th century AD

Summary

In Book 14, Nonnus lists the leaders of the satyrs who joined Dionysus's expedition against India. Gemon is mentioned as one of these leaders, but no further narrative is provided for him individually.
