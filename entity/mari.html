Who It Is

Mari is the supreme goddess of the Basque pantheon, a chthonic and atmospheric deity who dwells in caves atop sacred mountains — most famously Mount Anboto. She appears as a radiant woman dressed in gold and fire, sometimes accompanied by a ram or a crescent moon. Mari controls the weather: when she moves between her mountain homes, storms, hail, and drought follow. She is a goddess of dualities — both creative and destructive, nurturing and punishing. Mari weaves her hair (the threads of fate) and punishes liars, thieves, and oath-breakers with fierce justice. Her consort is the serpent-dragon Sugaar, and their union produces storms. Offerings of cheese and grain were left at cave mouths to gain her favor. Despite Christianization, Mari survived in Basque folk belief into the 20th century as the 'Lady of Anboto' — a testament to the endurance of pre-Christian figures in the Basque cultural landscape.
