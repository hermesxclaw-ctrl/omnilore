Who It Is

Imagine a god whose entire existence is known from a single stone. Anextiomarus is a whisper from Roman Britain — a name carved into an altar in South Shields, the only surviving trace of a deity who was once worshipped, prayed to, and feared. He is a ghost in the historical record, a 'Great Protector' whose protection we can no longer see, whose stories have crumbled to dust. He is a reminder that for every god we know, there are hundreds more we have lost.

Unknown. As a protective deity, he would have been invoked for safety in battle, protection of a community, or perhaps protection of a specific place or person. The votive altar suggests a personal or communal act of thanksgiving or petition.

Protection

The core domain implied by the name. Anextiomarus was likely a protective deity, capable of shielding individuals, warriors, or communities from harm. The specific scope of this protection — battle, travel, disease, or general misfortune — is unknown.

Limit: Unknown. No myths or stories survive to define the limits of his power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No visual representation of Anextiomarus survives. There is no statue, relief, or coin image. Any description of his appearance is therefore entirely speculative. In the context of Romano-British religion, a god worshipped under the name of Mars might have been depicted in a Roman military style — helmeted, armored, perhaps with a spear — but this is inference from the Roman name, not evidence about the native god.

Symbols — No symbols are attested. If he was depicted with the attributes of Mars, he might have had a spear, shield, or helmet, but this is pure inference.

Domains — Protection (specifically martial protection). The etymology strongly suggests a god of protective power, likely invoked in a military context. He may have been a local war-god, a guardian of a specific tribe or settlement, or a deity of personal safety.

This is the critical gap in the record. We know the god was worshipped — the altar proves it — but we have absolutely no idea what stories were told about him, what his mythology was, or what specific deeds he was believed to have performed. The narratives of Anextiomarus are entirely lost to history. The god is reduced to a name and an epithet. His personality, his myths, and his specific functions are unknowable with current evidence.
