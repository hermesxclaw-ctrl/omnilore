Who It Is

In the black heart of the Maya underworld, Xibalba, there lurks a creature whose very name means 'death bat'—Camazotz, the bat god who serves the lords of the dead. With wings like obsidian blades and a thirst for blood, this demon of the night has haunted Mesoamerican imagination for centuries, and its legend still echoes in modern horror and fantasy.

Camazotz is a fierce and deadly predator, serving the lords of Xibalba. It is not a benevolent deity but a malevolent force that brings death to those who encounter it. In the Popol Vuh, it acts as an obstacle for the Hero Twins, demonstrating its role as a guardian or executioner.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From K'iche' Maya: 'kama' (death) + 'sotz'' (bat), literally 'death bat'.

Pronunciation — kah-mah-SOTS

Aliases — Kamazotz, The Death Bat, Zotz

Ownership — Maya civilization, particularly the K'iche' Maya of highland Guatemala. Also present in Zapotec and possibly other Mesoamerican cultures.

Powers
Flight

As a bat, Camazotz can fly with great speed and agility, allowing it to attack from above.

Limit: None attested; flight is natural to its form.

Superhuman Strength and Claws

Camazotz possesses powerful claws and fangs capable of decapitating a human, as seen when it kills Hunahpu.

Limit: None attested.

Bloodthirst and Vampirism

In later interpretations, Camazotz is associated with blood-drinking, similar to vampire bats, though this is not explicit in the Popol Vuh.

Limit: Not attested in ancient sources; may be a modern invention.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Camazotz is typically depicted as a giant bat with sharp fangs and claws, often with wings spread. In some representations, it may have anthropomorphic features, such as a human body with a bat head. Its eyes are often glowing, and it is associated with darkness and the night.

Symbols — Bats in Maya iconography are often associated with darkness, death, and the underworld. Camazotz may be depicted with a sacrificial knife or a torch, linking it to ritual sacrifice. The bat itself is a symbol of the night and the transition between life and death.

Domains — Camazotz dwells in the House of Bats (Zotzaha) in Xibalba, the Maya underworld. It may also inhabit caves, which are seen as entrances to the underworld.

Accounts I
The Ordeal of the Hero Twins in the House of BatsPopol Vuh

Pre-Columbian (original myth), recorded in 16th century

The Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque, after defeating the lords of Xibalba, are subjected to a series of trials. In one, they are placed in the House of Bats, where they must survive the night. To avoid the bats, the twins hide inside their blowguns. Hunahpu, however, sticks his head out to check the dawn and is decapitated by Camazotz. Xbalanque later restores his brother's head using a squash as a substitute. Hunahpu's temporary death and resurrection; the twins ultimately triumph over the lords of Xibalba.

Camazotz is a vampire bat god. — In ancient Maya mythology, Camazotz is a death bat, but there is no evidence that it drank blood. The vampire association is a modern invention.

Camazotz is the same as the Maya god of death, Ah-Puch. — Ah-Puch is a separate deity. Camazotz is a bat demon, not the primary death god.

Camazotz was worshipped as a god. — There is no evidence of a cult dedicated to Camazotz. It appears as a demon in the Popol Vuh, not a deity.
