Who It Is

Camazotz is the death bat of Maya myth — and the monster responsible for one of the most shocking scenes in any world scripture. In the Popol Vuh, the hero twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque descend into Xibalba, the underworld, to face its lords in a gauntlet of trial-houses: the House of Cold, the House of Jaguars, the House of Fire — and then Zotzi-ha, the House of Bats, where shrieking snatch-bats with snouts like flint knives swarm the darkness. The twins hide inside their own blowguns and survive the night — until dawn approaches and Hunahpu, checking whether the sky has lightened, pokes his head out. Camazotz takes it off in one pass. The lords of Xibalba hang the hero's head over their ballcourt as the game ball, and the myth barely pauses: his brother borrows a squash to serve as a replacement head, tricks the lords during the ballgame, and reattaches the original. Camazotz distills everything the Maya night held: the bat as pollinator and vampire, cave-dweller of the underworld's literal mouths, the flicker at the torch's edge. Classic-era pottery paints him with a sacrificial knife in one hand and a heart in the other — death on wings, punctual at dusk.

Origins & Fall

The Kʼicheʼ Popol Vuh preserves the House of Bats episode; bat-demon iconography runs far earlier through Classic Maya vase painting and the Zapotec bat-god tradition of Oaxaca, where a great jade bat-mask presided over tombs.

Attested in Popol VuhEra Classic Maya onward

Form

A man-sized bat with flint-knife snout and claws, or a bat-headed warrior bearing knife and severed heart; his glyph marks the bat-month of the Maya calendar.

Powers

Decapitating flight, dominion over the House of Bats and its shrieking swarms, service as Xibalba's executioner — the underworld's one-strike enforcer of impatience.

Weaknesses & Wards

Patience defeats him: the twins survive by staying hidden the whole night. Hunahpu's head is lost to a single moment of checking too soon — Camazotz punishes exactly one mistake, and only that one.

Field Notes

⛧ The bat (zotz) was a Maya calendar month and a royal emblem — the Kaqchikel kings styled themselves Ahpo-Zotz, Lords of the Bat.

⛧ Common vampire bats really do inhabit Mesoamerican caves and really do drink blood — the myth needed almost no exaggeration.

⛧ The squash-head ballgame trick that avenges Hunahpu is the Popol Vuh's most audacious con.

Cultural Significance

The House of Bats is Mesoamerica's definitive darkness-trial, and Camazotz its apex predator. He haunts modern culture from A Wrinkle in Time (as the name of the dark planet) to persistent internet claims that he inspired Batman's darker incarnations.
