Form & Signs

Appearance — Champ is often described as a serpent-like creature, but with more fish-like characteristics. It has a long, slender body, typically estimated to be between 15 and 30 feet in length. Some accounts describe it as having a horse-like head or a large, flat head. The coloration of Champ is often reported as dark brown or black, with a lighter underside.

Symbols — Champ is often depicted in folklore and popular culture as a large, serpentine creature, similar to the Loch Ness Monster. It is sometimes shown with a horse-like head or a large, flat head.

Domains — Champ is said to inhabit Lake Champlain, a 125-mile-long body of fresh water shared by New York, Vermont, and Quebec.

Accounts I
The First Sighting of ChampWikipedia

19th century

The first reported sighting of Champ was in 1819, when a group of Native American tribesmen claimed to have seen a large, serpent-like creature in Lake Champlain. The sighting of Champ sparked widespread interest in the creature, leading to numerous reports and sightings over the years.
