Who It Is

In the vast, echoing halls of Greek mythology, most gods and heroes are loud, dramatic, and impossible to miss. But some figures are barely a whisper—a single name etched on a broken shard of pottery, a footnote in a forgotten lexicon. Anthracia is one of these whispers. Her name, which crackles with the memory of charcoal and embers, hints at a fiery presence in the cool, mountainous landscape of Arcadia. Yet, no story of hers survives. She is a ghost, a tantalizing gap in our knowledge, a reminder that for every Zeus or Athena, there were countless local spirits whose tales have crumbled to dust, leaving behind only a name to spark our imagination.

Unknown. The name suggests a chthonic or fiery nature, but no myths or behaviors are attested.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Anthracia' (Ἀνθρακία) is derived from the Ancient Greek word ἄνθραξ (ánthrax), meaning 'charcoal' or 'coal'. The name thus suggests a connection to fire, burning embers, or a place or being associated with coal.

Pronunciation — an-THRA-kee-ah (English approximation); an-thra-KEE-ah (Ancient Greek reconstructed)

Aliases — Anthracia

Ownership — Ancient Greek mythology and religion, specifically Arcadian tradition
