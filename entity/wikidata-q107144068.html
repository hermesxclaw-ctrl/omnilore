Who It Is

In the tangled genealogies of Greek myth, where heroes sired countless children across the mortal and divine, Lyse is one of the many daughters of Thespius—a woman whose name means 'release' or 'loosening,' yet whose own story is nearly lost to time. Her sole claim to fame is as a mother of Eumedes, one of the fifty sons Heracles fathered in a single night, a tale that raises questions about agency, desire, and the erasure of women in ancient storytelling.

No information exists about Lyse's personality or behavior. The myth only records that she was a daughter of Thespius and bore a son to Heracles.

Accounts I
Lyse and the Sons of HeraclesPseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.7.8

Classical antiquity (mythological setting); text from 1st-2nd century CE

Thespius, king of Thespiae, sought to protect his city from a lion by enlisting the hero Heracles. As a reward, Thespius offered his fifty daughters to Heracles, hoping to obtain many strong grandchildren. Heracles slept with all fifty daughters in one night (or over several nights, depending on the version). Each daughter bore a son, and Lyse gave birth to Eumedes. The sons later became the founders of Thespiae's noble families. Lyse became the mother of Eumedes, who, according to some traditions, was one of the sons who accompanied Heracles or later led a colony. The story explains the heroic lineage of Thespian families.
