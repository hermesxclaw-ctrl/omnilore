The Approach

In the neon-lit underworld of Southtown, where gang loyalties are etched in blood and steel, Soiree Meira burns with a fire his cool-headed twin brother Alba keeps banked. Younger by minutes, brash where Alba is calculating, Soiree is the impulsive heart of the Meira brothers' quest to avenge their fallen mentor Fate and tear down Duke's Mephistopheles empire—even if it means walking into the lion's den with fists first and questions never.

Content advisory. Contains themes of gang violence, murder, revenge, and organized crime. Mild language and suggestive character designs typical of fighting games.
Identity

Soiree Meira

Pronunciation SWAH-ray MAY-rah

Etymology

The name 'Soiree' is French for 'evening' or 'evening party,' possibly chosen to contrast with his brother Alba, which means 'dawn' in Italian and Spanish. This suggests a thematic duality of light/dark or beginning/end.

Cultural Ownership

Created by SNK Playmore for the King of Fighters franchise, specifically for KOF: Maximum Impact. He is a Japanese video game character, though his name and design draw from Western influences.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans initially speculated that the Meira brothers were intended as counterparts to Terry and Andy Bogard from Fatal Fury, but this is confirmed by developer commentary. There is no dispute about Soiree's identity as a distinct character.

Description

Appearance

Soiree has a lean, athletic build with spiky, medium-length dark hair with a reddish tint. He wears a black tank top, dark pants with a chain wallet, and fingerless gloves. His outfit is more casual and rebellious compared to Alba's formal vest. He has a confident, cocky smirk.

Symbols & Iconography

His color scheme is typically red/black, contrasting with Alba's blue/white. He is associated with fire motifs, reflecting his brash personality.

Behaviour & Personality

Soiree is brash, impulsive, and hot-headed, often acting before thinking. He is fiercely loyal to his brother Alba and determined to avenge Fate by any means necessary. He shows a soft spot for Chae Lim, suggesting a romantic interest. He is more straightforward and less calculating than Alba.

Domains & Habitats

Southtown, a fictional city inspired by American urban environments, particularly the gang-controlled districts.

Version Differences

No significant differences between the PS2 and Maniax versions. In the Maniax version, players can choose Japanese or English voices, but Soiree's character and story remain the same.

Powers & Abilities

Martial Arts (Meira-style Kickboxing)

Source Version KOF: Maximum Impact (all versions)

Description

Soiree employs a hybrid of kickboxing and street fighting, emphasizing fast, powerful kicks and aggressive rushdown tactics. His moveset includes rapid punch combinations, spinning kicks, and a striking style that reflects his hot-headed nature.

Limitations

As a fighter in a 3D arena, his moves are balanced with startup frames and recovery, making him vulnerable to counterattacks if overextended.

Stories & Myths

Main Story of KOF: Maximum Impact

Source KOF: Maximum Impact (game)Culture Japanese video gamePeriod 2004 (in-game: 2 years after N.E.S.T.S. saga)

Summary

Soiree and his twin brother Alba are adopted sons of Fate, the benevolent leader of the Addis gang. After Fate is murdered by Duke, the brothers enter the Mephistopheles Fighting Tournament to confront Duke and dismantle his criminal empire. Soiree fights through the tournament, defeating Duke's cronies and ultimately facing Duke himself. In his ending, he and Alba successfully defeat Duke and restore order to Southtown, with Soiree vowing to protect the city alongside his brother.

Gang Violence and Murder

Source KOF: Maximum Impact

Description

The story revolves around the murder of Fate and the violent power struggle between gangs. Soiree participates in a tournament where fighters are pitted against each other, and the final confrontation with Duke is presumably lethal or at least brutal.

Scholarly Context

The game is a fighting game, and violence is stylized and non-graphic. The narrative treats murder as a serious crime, driving the protagonists' quest for justice.
