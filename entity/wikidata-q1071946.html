Who It Is

A healer who survived the fall of Troy, only to be shipwrecked, married to a princess he saved from a deadly fall, and granted a kingdom by her grateful father—Podalirius is the Greek hero whose post-war wanderings turned him into a founder of cities and a god of healing in Caria.

In Homer, he is a healer and a warrior, leading ships and fighting. Dares later portrays him as haughty and moody, which may reflect a negative stereotype of physicians in late antiquity. His post-war actions show resourcefulness and piety, as he consults the Delphic oracle and founds cities.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is likely derived from Greek ποδ- (pod-, 'foot') and ἀλείτης (aleitēs, 'wanderer' or 'sinner'), possibly meaning 'foot-wanderer' or 'one who wanders on foot', fitting his post-war travels. Alternatively, it may be connected to ἀλέξω (alexō, 'to ward off'), suggesting 'foot-defender'.

Pronunciation — po-da-LEER-ee-us (English approximation); Greek: Ποδαλείριος, po-da-LEE-ree-os

Aliases — Podaleirius, Podaleirios

Ownership — Greek mythology, specifically the Homeric and post-Homeric epic tradition; later cult and foundation legends in Caria (Anatolia) and Daunia (Italy).

Powers
Healing

As a son of Asclepius, Podalirius was a skilled healer. He healed Philoctetes' wound, which was necessary for the Greeks to win the Trojan War. The stream near his heroön in Daunia was believed to cure animals of any disease.

Limit: No explicit limitations are given, but as a mortal healer, he could not always prevent death, as seen in the war.

Military Leadership

Podalirius led thirty ships from Tricca to Troy, indicating his status as a commander.

Limit: None specified.

Divination (possibly)

Some traditions associate the sons of Asclepius with divination, but for Podalirius, this is not explicitly attested.

Limit: Not attested.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No detailed physical description is given in the ancient sources except for Dares Phrygius, who characterizes him as 'sturdy, strong, haughty, and moody.' This is a late Latin source and may reflect Roman ideals rather than earlier Greek depictions.

Symbols — As a healer, he may be associated with the rod of Asclepius (a staff with a serpent), though no specific iconographic attributes are attested for him individually. In art, he is sometimes depicted in scenes of the Trojan War, but no distinct symbols are recorded.

Domains — Healing, medicine, and possibly divination (as a son of Asclepius). His cultic presence in Caria and Daunia suggests he was venerated as a local hero and healer, with a stream believed to cure animals.

Accounts I
The Trojan WarHomer, Iliad; Apollodorus, Epitome

Late Bronze Age (mythical)

Podalirius, along with his brother Machaon, led thirty ships from Tricca, Thessaly, to Troy. He served as a healer for the Greek forces. He healed Philoctetes, who possessed the bow and arrows of Heracles, essential for the fall of Troy. He was one of the warriors who entered the Trojan Horse. His healing of Philoctetes enabled the Greeks to win the war. After the war, he traveled with Amphilochus, Calchas, Leonteus, and Polypoetes to Colophon, where Calchas died.

Accounts II
Settlement in CariaApollodorus, Epitome 5.8; Strabo, Geography; Stephanus of Byzantium

Post-Trojan War (mythical)

After the war, Podalirius settled in Caria. According to one version, he returned to Argos and then consulted the Delphic oracle, which told him to settle where the sky would not fall on him; he chose a mountain-surrounded peninsula. In another, his ship was blown off course to Syrnus, Caria. In a third, he was shipwrecked and rescued by a shepherd named Bybassus. He healed the daughter of King Damaethus, Syrna, who had fallen from a roof, and was rewarded with her hand in marriage and the kingdom. He founded two cities, Syrnus and Bybassus, named after his wife and the shepherd. Podalirius became a founder-hero in Caria, with cities bearing his and his associates' names. His cult may have been established there.

Heroön in ItalyStrabo, Geography 14.2.10; Lycophron, Alexandra

Hellenistic and Roman

Strabo reports a heroön of Podalirius and Calchas in Daunia, Italy, on a hill called Drium. A stream there was believed to cure animals. Lycophron places his burial in Italy near Calchas's cenotaph, but Tzetzes disputes this. This suggests a cult of Podalirius in Magna Graecia, possibly brought by Greek colonists.

Podalirius was a god. — He was a mortal hero, son of the god Asclepius, but not a deity himself.

Podalirius and Machaon were twins. — They are brothers, but not specifically twins in most sources.

Podalirius was the only healer among the Greeks at Troy. — Machaon was equally a healer, and other healers like Patroclus learned medicine from Achilles.
