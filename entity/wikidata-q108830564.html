ATMOS - The Sontaran Stratagem (Doctor Who) - HOMONYM NARRATIVE, NOT DC CANONDoctor Who Series 4 Episode 4 'The Sontaran Stratagem' and Episode 5 'The Poison Sky', written by Helen Raynor, BBC One 2008

2008 (in-universe: late 2000s Earth)

ATMOS (Atmospheric Omission System) is a fictional automotive technology marketed as a satellite navigation system that also eliminates CO2 emissions. Invented by young genius Luke Rattigan and secretly funded by the clone warrior race the Sontarans, ATMOS is installed in 50% of the world's cars. The Sontarans use it to choke Earth's atmosphere with clone-feed gas. The Doctor, Donna Noble and UNIT thwart the plan by disabling ATMOS. This is a completely separate fiction from DC's Atmos and is included only to document the most prominent narrative use of the identical name. ATMOS devices are deactivated; Sontaran invasion fails; establishes 'ATMOS' in popular culture as villainous terraforming tech, often returned in fan searches for 'Atmos'.

The Perpetual Clock - Invention of the Atmos Clock (Horological Narrative)Jean-Léon Reutter patent CH 127406 (1928) and Jaeger-LeCoultre heritage archives; biography of Reutter and LeCoultre

1928-present

Engineer Jean-Léon Reutter invents a clock powered by minute temperature and atmospheric pressure changes acting on an ethyl chloride-filled bellows that winds the mainspring. Production taken over by Compagnie Générale Radio (1930) then Jaeger-LeCoultre (1935). Marketed as 'Atmos' - the clock that runs on air, requiring no manual winding. Becomes a diplomatic gift of the Swiss state. This is a real-world technological history, not a fictional story, but functions as the dominant historical narrative for the word. Creates enduring luxury object; establishes 'Atmos' as synonym for perpetual motion in horology; source of frequent confusion with fictional Atmos.

Dolby Atmos Launch - From Channel-Based to Object-Based Audio (Technological Narrative)Dolby Laboratories press release and white paper 'Dolby Atmos Cinema Technical Guidelines' (2012); film Brave (Pixar, 2012) as first Dolby Atmos release

2012-present

Dolby introduces object-based surround sound allowing sound designers to place up to 128 audio objects in 3D space, including overhead channels, rather than fixed 5.1/7.1 channels. Premiered in theaters with Pixar's Brave, then expanded to home theater, headphones, music (Dolby Atmos Music), and live events. Becomes industry standard for immersive audio. Redefines film sound mixing; 'Atmos' becomes generic shorthand for immersive audio; overwhelms search results for any other Atmos, including the DC character.
