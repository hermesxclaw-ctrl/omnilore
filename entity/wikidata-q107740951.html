The Approach

She does not punch, shoot, or summon fire. Miss Goldenweek sits on a rock in the middle of a prehistoric jungle, palette in hand, and paints a yellow circle at your feet — and suddenly you cannot stop laughing long enough to fight. In a world of Devil Fruits and sea kings, Eiichiro Oda's most disarming Baroque Works officer wins by weaponizing color itself, turning art into hypnosis and a sixteen-year-old girl into one of the most conceptually dangerous people on Little Garden.

Content advisory. Character is depicted as a minor (stated age 16) employed by a criminal organization; themes of child exploitation within organized crime, hypnotic coercion and loss of autonomy, cartoon violence. No sexual violence depicted for this character.
Identity

Miss Goldenweek

Pronunciation English: MISS GOLD-en-week /mɪs ˈɡoʊldən wiːk/; Japanese: Misu Gōruden Wīku [misɯ ɡoːɾɯdeɴ ɰiːkɯ]

Etymology

Codename 'Miss Goldenweek' derives from Golden Week (Gōruden Wīku), the major Japanese holiday period in late April-early May when many businesses close. Baroque Works female agents are codenamed for holidays; male partners for numbers. 'Golden Week' continues the holiday theme alongside Miss Valentine, Miss Merry Christmas, etc. Her real name Marianne is revealed in the Vivre Card databook and SBS; likely from French name Marianne, national symbol of France, fitting Oda's art-history naming. Her epithet 'Colors Trap' (Karāzu Torappu) describes her paint-based hypnosis. Japanese pronunciation: misu gōruden wīku [mi.sɯ ɡoːɾɯ.deɴ ɰiːkɯ].

Cultural Ownership

Original character created by Eiichiro Oda, serialized in Weekly Shonen Jump (Shueisha) from 1999. Intellectual property owned by Shueisha / Shueisha-Shogakukan Productions and licensed internationally by Toei Animation, Viz Media. Character design, story and ability are Oda's authorial creation within Japanese manga culture; no traditional folklore ownership.

Disputed Identifications

No major scholarly dispute over identity, but common fan conflation: (1) Miss Goldenweek vs. Marianne — same individual, codename vs. real name, not separate characters; (2) Confusion with other Baroque Works Officer Agents due to similar color/holiday naming; (3) Incorrectly identified as a Devil Fruit user in early English fandom (1999-2002) before Oda clarified Colors Trap is a skill, not a fruit; (4) Not to be confused with Charlotte Angel (also artist-themed) or other One Piece paint users like Kanjuro.

Description

Appearance

Miss Goldenweek is depicted as a young girl, stated age 16 at debut, notably short (145 cm per Vivre Card) with a childlike, chibi-proportioned design that contrasts with adult Baroque Works agents. She has large dark eyes, freckles across nose and cheeks, and long dark hair (black in manga, dark brown/black in anime) worn loose with a small yellow beret tilted to the side. Outfit: long-sleeved white shirt with orange-yellow trim, dark green or teal overalls/shorts, red scarf or neckerchief, and simple shoes. She carries an oversized artist's palette and paintbrush disproportionate to her size, often with a small easel or paint set slung over her shoulder. In cover story she wears a patched traveling cloak. Her design emphasizes innocence and artistic whimsy — deliberately non-threatening — which makes her ability more unsettling. Anime adds slightly more saturated colors. No Devil Fruit transformation; appearance remains constant.

Symbols & Iconography

Core symbols are painter's palette, brush, and color circles/symbols painted on ground, clothing, or skin. Each color carries a kanji/English label in manga: Red (Betrayal), Yellow (Laughter), Blue (Sadness), Green (Relaxation/Calm), Black (Betrayal reversal? Actually 'Treachery'), etc. Oda draws hypnotized victims with altered facial expressions matching color emotion. Holiday symbolism: Golden Week evokes leisure, travel, art — ironic for an assassin. Art-historical allusion: beret and palette evoke stereotypical French painter, linking to name Marianne. In cover story, her art literally reshapes reality by painting dream futures on canvas that inspire Baroque Works fugitives.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested personality is calm, detached, laconic, and almost sleepy, speaking rarely and in short sentences. She shows complete loyalty to her partner Mr.3 (Galdino) rather than to Crocodile's ideology, and displays quiet compassion in the cover story by helping former Baroque Works comrades escape and find new dreams. She is not sadistic; she treats combat as painting, sitting on the sidelines while others are hypnotized. She is observant, intelligent, and has strong artistic vision — able to perceive people's 'dreams' and paint them. Reconstructed from cover story: empathetic, idealistic, pacifistic, prefers to free colleagues rather than fight. Disputed: whether her silence indicates shyness or supreme confidence; Vivre Card lists hobby as painting and dream as 'becoming a real artist' (interpreted as genuine artistic ambition vs. criminal tool). No evidence of ambition to become Pirate King or similar grand goal.

Domains & Habitats

Primary habitat is Baroque Works as an Officer Agent (rank just below the 5 senior agents), operating under Crocodile's secret criminal organization based in Arabasta. Field assignment: Little Garden (Rikujima), a preserved prehistoric island on the Grand Line populated by giants Dorry and Brogy and dinosaurs. Later habitat during cover story: fugitive journey across desert and sea, culminating in 'Spiders Cafe' (Kumo no Café), a reformed Baroque Works hideout/cake shop on an unnamed island where former agents live in hiding after Arabasta's fall. She does not have a personal territory or kingdom; her domain is conceptual — the psychological space created by color.

Version Differences

Manga (canonical): debut Ch.117 silhouette, full appearance Ch.128; Colors Trap effects explained with text labels; cover story provides extensive post-defeat character development (359-413). Anime (Toei): adds filler dialogue and slightly extended hypnosis scenes Ep.70-71; color palette more vivid; voice performance by Akiko Nakagawa (Japanese) adds soft, childlike timbre; Funimation dub by Cherami Leigh. Vivre Card adds biographical data (age 16, height, birthday April 4, favorite food is senbei rice crackers). Video games (Pirate Warriors, Grand Battle) simplify Colors Trap to area-of-effect debuffs and omit nuanced hypnosis rules. Live-action Netflix series (Season 1, 2023) does not include Miss Goldenweek; Little Garden not yet adapted as of 2026, so no live-action version exists. No significant contradictory redesigns.

Powers & Abilities

Colors Trap (Karāzu Torappu)

Source VersionManga Ch.128-129, SBS Vol.15, Vivre Card #0520

A non-Devil Fruit hypnotic technique using specially mixed paints. By painting a colored symbol or circle and having a target see it, Miss Goldenweek induces a powerful suggestive state corresponding to the color's emotional concept. It is explicitly stated to be hypnosis via art and color psychology, not magic or fruit power. Requires the victim to see the color; effect breaks if victim is distracted or color is removed/overpainted.

Limitations

Requires line of sight to painted color; blind persons or those who close eyes are immune. Effect is psychological suggestion, not physical control — victim retains abilities but motivation altered. Can be countered by painting over with another color or by strong will/awareness (Luffy initially resists yellow by sheer stupidity/anger). Paint must be prepared; not instantaneous without palette. Does not work on those who already strongly embody opposite emotion. No effect on inanimate objects.

Contradictions

Early English fan translations (2000-2001) incorrectly labeled it a Devil Fruit; Oda SBS explicitly corrected this. Some databooks translations list 'Colors Trap' as 'ability' without clarifying non-fruit nature, causing continued misconception.

Red - Colors Trap: Betrayal (Uragiri no Aka)

Source VersionManga Ch.128 pp.50-54

Painting a red circle causes anyone who sees it to betray allies and attack friends. Used on Little Garden to make Brogy and Dorry fight each other indirectly and to turn Luffy and Karoo against each other.

Limitations

Victim must see red mark; if they realize they are being manipulated, they can resist partially. Effect ends when red is covered or victim is knocked unconscious.

Contradictions

None; consistently described.

Yellow - Colors Trap: Laughter (Warai no Kiiro)

Source VersionManga Ch.128-129

Yellow circle induces uncontrollable laughter, incapacitating target with mirth and preventing serious combat. Used on Luffy.

Limitations

Those with strong focus or anger can power through; Luffy eventually overcomes by getting angry enough to ignore laughter.

Contradictions

None.

Blue - Colors Trap: Sadness (Kanashimi no Ao)

Source VersionManga Ch.128

Blue induces deep melancholy and tears, sapping will to fight.

Limitations

Same line-of-sight requirement; can be washed off.

Contradictions

None.

Green - Colors Trap: Calm / Friendship (Yasuragi no Midori / Nakama no Midori)

Source VersionManga Ch.128; Cover Story Ch.391-395

Green induces relaxation and sense of friendship/comradeship. In cover story, she uses green-adjacent dream painting to inspire hope.

Limitations

Effect is subtle; not combat-dominant.

Contradictions

Some translations label green as 'Relaxation' vs 'Friendship'; both attested as same color with nuanced description.

Black - Colors Trap: Betrayal Reversal / Treachery (Uragiri no Kuro)

Source VersionManga Ch.128

Black is described as color of betrayal that can undo other colors or cause ultimate betrayal. Mr.3 notes it is the most dangerous.

Limitations

Rarely used; requires precise painting.

Contradictions

Fandom debate whether black cancels other colors or intensifies betrayal; manga suggests it is 'color of betrayal' that makes allies betray each other completely.

Rainbow - Colors Trap: Dream (Yume no Nanairo)

Source VersionCover Story Ch.359-413, especially Ch.382, 391, 400

In cover story, Miss Goldenweek paints people's ideal futures/dreams on canvas using rainbow palette, which psychologically motivates former Baroque Works agents to pursue new lives (e.g., painting Mr.2's dream, Mr.4 and Miss Merry Christmas's new shop). Not combat hypnosis but inspirational art that reveals inner desire.

Limitations

Requires subject's cooperation and reflection; not mind control, but therapeutic visualization. Effect is long-term motivation, not instant hypnosis.

Contradictions

Disputed whether this is same Colors Trap mechanism or separate artistic talent; Vivre Card treats it as extension of same skill.

Artistic Perception and Tactical Support

Source VersionManga Ch.128-129; Cover Story

Keen artistic eye that can read people's hidden dreams and personalities, plus tactical support for Mr.3's wax strategies by controlling battlefield emotions. Non-superhuman but notable intelligence for age.

Limitations

Physically weak — no superhuman strength, speed, or durability; relies entirely on partner for protection. Stated bounty not issued; no combat feats beyond hypnosis.

Contradictions

None.

Stories & Myths

Baroque Works Assignment: Little Garden Infiltration

Source One Piece manga Chapters 117, 122-125Culture Japanese manga / Grand Line fictionPeriod 1999-2000 (publication); in-universe c. 1522 Age of Sea

Summary

As Officer Agents of Baroque Works, Miss Goldenweek and her partner Mr.3 (Galdino) are dispatched by Mr.0 (Crocodile) to Little Garden to eliminate the giants Dorry and Brogy and capture the Straw Hats. They arrive posing as castaways, using Mr.3's wax powers and Goldenweek's Colors Trap to manipulate the giants' 100-year duel. She is first silhouetted in Ch.117 among Baroque Works agents, then fully revealed on Little Garden observing the Straw Hats' arrival. She remains largely silent, painting traps while Mr.3 monologues.

Sets up Little Garden as Baroque Works trap; demonstrates Colors Trap as psychological threat distinct from Devil Fruits; establishes her partnership dynamic with Mr.3 where she is the calm strategist to his flamboyant scheming.

Battle of Little Garden: Colors Trap vs. Straw Hats

Source One Piece manga Chapters 128-129Culture Japanese mangaPeriod 2000 publication; in-universe Little Garden incident

Summary

When Luffy, Vivi and Karoo confront Mr.3's wax house, Miss Goldenweek activates her traps. She paints a yellow circle that forces Luffy into helpless laughter and a red circle that makes Karoo and Vivi attack each other. She also uses blue to sadden Usopp and others. The Straw Hats are incapacitated not by force but by emotion. Only when Usopp, who had earlier been marked, cleverly paints over her traps and Luffy's anger overrides the yellow laughter do they break free. Luffy then defeats Mr.3, and Goldenweek is left defeated but unharmed, her paints scattered.

Relationships
Miss Goldenweek→Mr.3 (Galdino)ally

Official Baroque Works partner pairing: Officer Agent duo Mr.3 and Miss Goldenweek. He is the flamboyant, scheming wax-man (Doru Doru no Mi) who relies on her calm Colors Trap to control battlefields; she is his silent, loyal support who rarely speaks but obeys his plans. Post-defeat, their dynamic reverses — she becomes the emotional leader who rescues him from prison and guides the fugitive group, while he follows her artistic vision. Deeply codependent, not romantic in canon, but fandom often interprets as sibling-like or mentor-student. Vivre Card notes they have high compatibility.

Miss Goldenweek→Crocodile (Mr.0)creator

Boss of Baroque Works and secret leader of the criminal organization. As Mr.0, he recruited and assigned Goldenweek as an Officer Agent, though they never meet on-panel. She serves his plan to take over Arabasta, but shows no personal loyalty to his ideology; after his defeat and arrest (Ch.212), she does not attempt to rescue him, instead choosing to save fellow low-ranking agents. Represents exploitative employer of a minor.

Miss Goldenweek→Monkey D. Luffyhostile

Primary enemy during Little Garden. Luffy is victim of her Yellow and Red traps, but ultimately breaks free through willpower and defeats her partner, indirectly defeating her. No personal grudge; Luffy barely remembers her, typical of his attitude to minor Baroque Works agents. She does not re-encounter him post-timeskip.

Miss Goldenweek→Nefertari Vivi and Karoohostile

Victims of Colors Trap on Little Garden — Vivi and her duck Karoo are forced to attack each other under Red betrayal paint. Vivi later forgives Baroque Works agents as part of Arabasta reconciliation theme, but no direct later interaction with Goldenweek.

Miss Goldenweek→Mr.2 Bon Kurei (Bentham)ally

Fellow Baroque Works Officer Agent, not present on Little Garden but central to Goldenweek's cover story. She paints his dream of true friendship and Okama Way, helping him find purpose after Arabasta. In cover story, she frees him from Marine capture (with Mr.3's help) and inspires him before his later heroic sacrifice in Impel Down (Ch.535-538). Their bond is artistic and empathetic.

Miss Goldenweek→Miss Valentine (Mikita) and Mr.5 (Gem)ally

Fellow Officer Agent duo (Mr.5/Miss Valentine) defeated by Luffy and Zoro on Little Garden earlier. In cover story, Goldenweek, Mr.3 and Mr.5 escape prison together, then rescue Miss Valentine from Marines. They become core members of the traveling fugitive group and later Spiders Cafe staff. Shared experience as low-rank Baroque Works outcasts creates found-family dynamic.

Miss Goldenweek→Mr.4 and Miss Merry Christmasally

Officer Agent duo assigned to Arabasta. After Crocodile's fall, they are fugitives. Goldenweek paints their dream of opening a bakery/cake shop, which becomes the new Spiders Cafe. She acts as their artistic counselor, visualizing a peaceful future they had not imagined for themselves.

Miss Goldenweek→Hina (Marine Captain)hostile

Marine officer who pursues the Baroque Works fugitives during the cover story. Hina's fleet nearly captures Goldenweek's group, but they evade via art-based disguises and wax tricks. Represents World Government pursuit that Goldenweek must outwit without violence.

Child Employment in Baroque Works Criminal Syndicate

Source Vivre Card #0520 (age 16), Manga Ch.117-129

Description

Miss Goldenweek is canonically 16 years old at debut (Vivre Card) and employed as an Officer Agent — a ranked assassin/enforcer — for Crocodile's Baroque Works, which functions as a mercenary, bounty hunter and assassination organization. The narrative does not comment on the ethics of a minor in organized crime; she is treated as a competent professional. This reflects Oda's broader pattern of youthful agents (e.g., Miss Valentine age 24, but Goldenweek notably youngest officer) and raises real-world concerns about child exploitation and normalization of minors in violent labor, though presented in a comedic, non-graphic shonen context.

Scholarly Context

One Piece scholars and critics (e.g., analyses in One Piece Magazine Vol.9) note Oda often uses childlike designs to contrast innocence with criminality for comedic and thematic effect, but rarely addresses the in-universe child welfare implications. No in-story condemnation of Crocodile's recruitment of a minor is given.

Hypnotic Coercion and Loss of Autonomy

Source Manga Ch.128-129, SBS Vol.15

Description

Colors Trap is explicitly non-consensual mind control via color suggestion, forcing victims to betray friends, laugh uncontrollably, or fall into despair. Victims are aware but unable to resist without external help. The ability is used to make allies attack each other (Red betrayal), which is a form of psychological violence. While presented comically, it depicts coercive control and removal of agency.

Scholarly Context

In-universe, the Straw Hats treat it as a trick to be overcome by willpower; no lasting trauma is depicted. Fan and academic readings note it as an early One Piece exploration of non-physical combat and manipulation, precursor to later hypnotic fruits like Jango's hypnosis.

Warning: Sanitization of Baroque Works Violence — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Sanitization of Baroque Works Violence

Source Manga Ch.160-212 (Arabasta), Cover Story 359-413

Description

Baroque Works as a whole is responsible for assassination, extortion, and attempted mass murder in Arabasta (dance powder, civil war). Miss Goldenweek's role is sanitized — she is never shown killing, only incapacitating — and her cover story redeems her without legal accountability. The narrative pivots from criminal to sympathetic artist without addressing victims' justice, reflecting shonen tendency to redeem youthful villains via found family rather than punishment.

Scholarly Context

Critics note this as part of One Piece's broader theme of chosen family and second chances, but also as narrative sanitization where former assassins become bakers without atonement arc.

Contradictions & Disputes

Is Colors Trap a Devil Fruit ability?

Position A

Colors Trap is a Devil Fruit power (early fan belief, some early English guidebooks).

Position A — evidence

Prior to SBS Vol.15, no explicit denial in manga; fans assumed any superhuman ability must be Devil Fruit; some unofficial guides listed it as fruit.

Position B

Colors Trap is a learned hypnotic skill using paint, not a Devil Fruit (Oda's canonical clarification).

Position B — evidence

Oda SBS Vol.15 explicitly states: 'It's not a Devil Fruit, it's hypnosis via colors. She's just good at painting.' Vivre Card #0520 lists 'Ability: Colors Trap (not Devil Fruit)' and lists no fruit name. She is never shown with fruit weakness to seawater.

Scholarly Consensus

Consensus is position B is canonical. Early guides and wikis have been corrected. Still recurs as misconception in casual fandom.

Age and physical maturity depiction

Position A

She is a child (12-14) based on chibi design and height 145cm.

Position A — evidence

Visual design is highly childlike; anime voice is childlike; some fans infer younger age.

Position B

She is 16 at debut (Vivre Card canonical), 18 post-timeskip, legally a minor but older teen.

Position B — evidence

Vivre Card databook (2018) explicitly gives age 16 (debut), birthday April 4, height 145cm. Oda's SBS and databooks are primary canon for ages.

Scholarly Consensus

Canonical age is 16; visual design is stylized chibi, not literal age indicator, common in Oda's art for petite characters.

Effect of Green and Rainbow paints

Position A

Green is 'friendship' and Rainbow is separate 'dream' ability unrelated to hypnosis.

Position A — evidence

Cover story rainbow paintings do not cause instant forced behavior but long-term motivation, suggesting different mechanism (art therapy vs hypnosis).

Position B

Green and Rainbow are extensions of same Colors Trap hypnosis, just inspirational rather than coercive.

Position B — evidence

Vivre Card and Magazine describe all paints as part of Colors Trap; Oda draws rainbow effects with same palette and brush iconography.

Scholarly Consensus

Most official sources treat them as same skill family — Colors Trap has both coercive and inspirational applications; distinction is narrative function, not separate power.
