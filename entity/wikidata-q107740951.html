Who It Is

She does not punch, shoot, or summon fire. Miss Goldenweek sits on a rock in the middle of a prehistoric jungle, palette in hand, and paints a yellow circle at your feet — and suddenly you cannot stop laughing long enough to fight. In a world of Devil Fruits and sea kings, Eiichiro Oda's most disarming Baroque Works officer wins by weaponizing color itself, turning art into hypnosis and a sixteen-year-old girl into one of the most conceptually dangerous people on Little Garden.

Attested personality is calm, detached, laconic, and almost sleepy, speaking rarely and in short sentences. She shows complete loyalty to her partner Mr.3 (Galdino) rather than to Crocodile's ideology, and displays quiet compassion in the cover story by helping former Baroque Works comrades escape and find new dreams. She is not sadistic; she treats combat as painting, sitting on the sidelines while others are hypnotized. She is observant, intelligent, and has strong artistic vision — able to perceive people's 'dreams' and paint them. Reconstructed from cover story: empa

Powers
Colors Trap (Karāzu Torappu)

A non-Devil Fruit hypnotic technique using specially mixed paints. By painting a colored symbol or circle and having a target see it, Miss Goldenweek induces a powerful suggestive state corresponding to the color's emotional concept. It is explicitly stated to be hypnosis via art and color psychology, not magic or fruit power. Requires the victim to see the color; effect breaks if victim is distracted or color is removed/overpainted.

Limit: Requires line of sight to painted color; blind persons or those who close eyes are immune. Effect is psychological suggestion, not physical control — victim retains abilities but motivation altered. Can be countered by painting over with another color or by strong will/awareness (Luffy initially resists yellow by sheer stupidity/anger). Paint must be prepared; not instantaneous without palette. Does not work on those who already strongly embody opposite emotion. No effect on inanimate objects.

Red - Colors Trap: Betrayal (Uragiri no Aka)

Painting a red circle causes anyone who sees it to betray allies and attack friends. Used on Little Garden to make Brogy and Dorry fight each other indirectly and to turn Luffy and Karoo against each other.

Limit: Victim must see red mark; if they realize they are being manipulated, they can resist partially. Effect ends when red is covered or victim is knocked unconscious.

Yellow - Colors Trap: Laughter (Warai no Kiiro)

Yellow circle induces uncontrollable laughter, incapacitating target with mirth and preventing serious combat. Used on Luffy.

Limit: Those with strong focus or anger can power through; Luffy eventually overcomes by getting angry enough to ignore laughter.

Blue - Colors Trap: Sadness (Kanashimi no Ao)

Blue induces deep melancholy and tears, sapping will to fight.

Limit: Same line-of-sight requirement; can be washed off.

Green - Colors Trap: Calm / Friendship (Yasuragi no Midori / Nakama no Midori)

Green induces relaxation and sense of friendship/comradeship. In cover story, she uses green-adjacent dream painting to inspire hope.

Limit: Effect is subtle; not combat-dominant.

Black - Colors Trap: Betrayal Reversal / Treachery (Uragiri no Kuro)

Black is described as color of betrayal that can undo other colors or cause ultimate betrayal. Mr.3 notes it is the most dangerous.

Limit: Rarely used; requires precise painting.

Rainbow - Colors Trap: Dream (Yume no Nanairo)

In cover story, Miss Goldenweek paints people's ideal futures/dreams on canvas using rainbow palette, which psychologically motivates former Baroque Works agents to pursue new lives (e.g., painting Mr.2's dream, Mr.4 and Miss Merry Christmas's new shop). Not combat hypnosis but inspirational art that reveals inner desire.

Limit: Requires subject's cooperation and reflection; not mind control, but therapeutic visualization. Effect is long-term motivation, not instant hypnosis.

Artistic Perception and Tactical Support

Keen artistic eye that can read people's hidden dreams and personalities, plus tactical support for Mr.3's wax strategies by controlling battlefield emotions. Non-superhuman but notable intelligence for age.

Limit: Physically weak — no superhuman strength, speed, or durability; relies entirely on partner for protection. Stated bounty not issued; no combat feats beyond hypnosis.

Accounts I
Baroque Works Assignment: Little Garden InfiltrationOne Piece manga Chapters 117, 122-125

1999-2000 (publication); in-universe c. 1522 Age of Sea

As Officer Agents of Baroque Works, Miss Goldenweek and her partner Mr.3 (Galdino) are dispatched by Mr.0 (Crocodile) to Little Garden to eliminate the giants Dorry and Brogy and capture the Straw Hats. They arrive posing as castaways, using Mr.3's wax powers and Goldenweek's Colors Trap to manipulate the giants' 100-year duel. She is first silhouetted in Ch.117 among Baroque Works agents, then fully revealed on Little Garden observing the Straw Hats' arrival. She remains largely silent, painting traps while Mr.3 monologues. Sets up Little Garden as Baroque Works trap; demonstrates Colors Trap as psychological threat distinct from Devil Fruits; establishes her partnership dynamic with Mr.3 where she is the calm strategist to his flamboyant scheming.

Battle of Little Garden: Colors Trap vs. Straw HatsOne Piece manga Chapters 128-129

2000 publication; in-universe Little Garden incident

When Luffy, Vivi and Karoo confront Mr.3's wax house, Miss Goldenweek activates her traps. She paints a yellow circle that forces Luffy into helpless laughter and a red circle that makes Karoo and Vivi attack each other. She also uses blue to sadden Usopp and others. The Straw Hats are incapacitated not by force but by emotion. Only when Usopp, who had earlier been marked, cleverly paints over her traps and Luffy's anger overrides the yellow laughter do they break free. Luffy then defeats Mr.3, and Goldenweek is left defeated but unharmed, her paints scattered. First and only canonical combat defeat; shows limitation of hypnosis against willpower and creativity. After defeat, she and Mr.3 are left on Little Garden as Marines approach. Establishes that Colors Trap is counterable by painting over or strong emotion.

Capture and Imprisonment After Little GardenOne Piece manga Chapter 130-132 (implied), Cover Story prologue Chapter 359

2000-2002 publication

Following Mr.3's defeat, Miss Goldenweek and Mr.3 are captured (implied) by the Marines or left stranded; Chapter 130 shows Marines heading to Little Garden. The cover story later reveals they were imprisoned but escaped or were transferred. Chapter 359 cover shows Miss Goldenweek, Mr.3 and Mr.5 in a Marine prison cell, establishing they were incarcerated after Arabasta's exposure. This bridges Little Garden defeat to fugitive arc. Explains why Baroque Works agents disappear after Little Garden and sets up redemption/fugitive storyline; confirms World Government treats even minor agents as criminals despite her age.

Accounts II
Miss Goldenweek's Operation: Meet Baroque Works — The Great Escape and JourneyOne Piece manga Cover Story Serial Chapters 359-382

2002-2003 publication; in-universe weeks after Arabasta fall

The 42-part cover serial starring Miss Goldenweek as protagonist. She, Mr.3 and Mr.5 escape prison (using Mr.3's wax key) and decide to rescue other Baroque Works agents. Traveling across desert and sea, she uses her paints not for combat but to inspire: she paints portraits of each agent's dream future. She frees Miss Valentine, Mr.5, and others from Marine pursuit, evades Hina's Marine fleet, and reunites the scattered Baroque Works fugitives. Her art becomes therapeutic — she paints Mr.2 Bon Kurei's dream of friendship, Miss Merry Christmas and Mr.4's dream of a new life. The serial is largely wordless, told through Oda's cover art with minimal dialogue. Major character development: transforms Goldenweek from silent assassin to compassionate leader and artist who redeems former villains through art. Establishes her pacifist, nurturing side and artistic dream. Considered canonical by Oda and referenced in later databooks. Provides rare shonen example of villain rehabilitation via creativity rather than battle.

The New Spiders Cafe: A New LifeOne Piece manga Cover Story Chapters 383-413 (final arc)

2003 publication; in-universe post-Arabasta

The fugitives establish the new Spiders Cafe (formerly Spiders Café in Arabasta run by Paula/Miss Doublefinger) in a new location, reimagined as a cake shop and hideout. Miss Goldenweek paints a large sign and uses her rainbow dream painting to give each former agent a new purpose: Mr.4 and Miss Merry Christmas open a bakery, Miss Valentine works as attendant, etc. Final covers show them living peacefully, hiding from Marines but free, with Goldenweek as the quiet center, painting their futures. Last panel shows them celebrating, with Goldenweek smiling — rare expressive moment. Provides closure for Baroque Works saga; shows alternative to World Government justice — self-determined rehabilitation. Goldenweek's dream of being an artist is realized as she literally paints new lives. As of Elbaf/Wano arcs (2026), no further canonical reappearance, but Vivre Card confirms they remain at large, living quietly.

Legacy Cameo: Baroque Works Reunion Hints and Vivre Card UpdateOne Piece Vivre Card Databook (2018), One Piece Magazine Vol.8 (2019), Chapter 1058+ world update

2018-2026

While Miss Goldenweek has not reappeared in main storyline post-timeskip, supplementary materials keep her alive in canon. Vivre Card confirms her status as free, age 20 post-timeskip (extrapolated), and reiterates Colors Trap is not a Devil Fruit. One Piece Magazine and SBS mention that Oda considered bringing Baroque Works back during Dressrosa/Cross Guild but chose to keep Goldenweek's group in peaceful retirement. Her influence is cited in discussions of non-Devil Fruit abilities and color psychology in One Piece. Maintains canonical existence without requiring new arc; leaves door open for future return, especially as Cross Guild (Crocodile's new organization) rises. Demonstrates Oda's pattern of keeping cover story characters alive off-panel.

Miss Goldenweek ate a Devil Fruit to get her color powers. — Oda explicitly confirmed in SBS Vol.15 and Vivre Card that Colors Trap is not a Devil Fruit but a hypnotic technique using specially mixed paints and color psychology. She can swim and is not affected by seawater or Seastone.

Miss Goldenweek is a weak, filler character with no importance after Little Garden. — She is the protagonist of a 42-chapter canonical cover story (359-413) that provides the only canonical rehabilitation arc for Baroque Works and reveals her as the emotional core who saves and redeems multiple agents. Oda gave her more post-defeat development than most Officer Agents.

Her real name is Goldenweek and she is an adult woman. — Her codename is Miss Goldenweek; her real name is Marianne (revealed in Vivre Card). She is 16 at debut, 145cm tall, and designed to look younger than other Officer Agents. She is a minor, not an adult.

Colors Trap works like magic and cannot be resisted. — Manga explicitly shows limitations: victim must see the color, effect can be broken by painting over the color, by strong emotion/willpower (Luffy's anger), or by closing eyes. Usopp defeats her by using his own painting to counter hers.
