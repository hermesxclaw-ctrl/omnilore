Who It Is

On the slopes of a mountain that bore his name, Tmolus—a king, a god, and a judge—presided over one of the most famous musical contests in Greek mythology, only to be outplayed by a satyr and punished by a god for his honesty.

In the myth, Tmolus is portrayed as a wise and fair judge, though his judgment in the musical contest between Apollo and Pan is based on his appreciation of refined music over rustic tunes. He is not a major character in mythology, and his personality is only sketchily defined. He appears to be a local deity or hero, respected for his connection to the land.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Tmolus is of uncertain etymology, possibly derived from a Lydian or Anatolian root. Some ancient sources connect it to the mountain's physical characteristics, but a definitive linguistic origin is not attested.

Powers
Mountain god powers

As a mountain god, Tmolus would have powers over the natural features of his domain, including earthquakes, vegetation, and wildlife. However, these powers are not explicitly described in surviving sources.

Limit: No specific limitations are recorded.

Judgment and wisdom

In Ovid, Tmolus is chosen to judge a musical contest between Apollo and Pan, indicating his wisdom and authority. He judges in favor of Apollo, which leads to King Midas's punishment for preferring Pan.

Limit: His judgment is subjective and not binding on the gods, as seen when Apollo punishes Midas for disagreeing.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In Ovid's Metamorphoses, Tmolus is described as a mountain god with a rustic appearance. He is depicted as an old man with pine trees on his head, symbolizing his connection to the mountain. His ears are described as 'rough' or 'unpolished,' fitting his rustic nature. However, this description is specific to Ovid's account and may not reflect earlier Greek traditions.

Symbols — The mountain itself is the primary symbol, often depicted in ancient art with a personification of a mountain god. Pine trees and rocky terrain are associated with Tmolus due to his mountain identity. In Roman art, he might be shown as a reclining figure with a cornucopia or holding a staff.

Domains — Tmolus is associated with Mount Tmolus in Lydia (modern-day Bozdağ in Turkey). The mountain was known for its vineyards and was sacred to Dionysus. Tmolus, as the personification of the mountain, would inhabit this region. The mountain range is also the location of the city of Sardis, the capital of Lydia.

Accounts I
The Musical Contest of Apollo and PanOvid, Metamorphoses 11.146-171

Augustan era

Tmolus, the mountain god, is asked to judge a musical contest between Apollo, playing the lyre, and Pan, playing the pipes. Tmolus, after listening, awards the victory to Apollo, praising his refined music. King Midas, who is present, disagrees and prefers Pan's rustic tunes. Apollo then punishes Midas by giving him donkey ears. Tmolus's role is brief but pivotal. Midas receives donkey ears, and Tmolus's judgment establishes Apollo's superiority in music.

Accounts II
Tmolus as King of LydiaVarious Greek mythographers (e.g., Hyginus, Fabulae)

Hellenistic and Roman

Tmolus is sometimes depicted as a mortal king of Lydia, the father of Tantalus (in some versions). His reign is not well-documented, and he may have been a legendary founder or early ruler. This version is less prominent than the mountain god figure. His kingship connects the mountain and region to royal lineage.
