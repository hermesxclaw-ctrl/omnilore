Who It Is

Cartoon Cat is another creation of Trevor Henderson, and arguably his most terrifying design. It appears as an enormous, rubber-hose-style cartoon cat — the kind that would have appeared in 1930s black-and-white animation — but wrong in every possible way. It stands over 15 feet tall, with a body that is simultaneously cartoonish and grotesquely realistic. Its permanent smile is too wide, its eyes are glossy and dead rather than expressive, and its movements are described as fluid and impossible, like a cartoon character that exists in three-dimensional space. Cartoon Cat is capable of extreme shape-shifting — stretching its limbs, enlarging its mouth to swallow prey whole, flattening itself to slide under doors, and generally defying physics in the way only animated characters can. This makes it one of the most dangerous entities in the Henderson mythos, as conventional barriers are meaningless against it. It inhabits abandoned buildings, particularly old malls, arcades, and theaters — places associated with childhood entertainment that have been left to rot. The horror of Cartoon Cat draws on multiple sources: the uncanny valley of old rubber-hose animation viewed through a modern lens, the childhood fear of mascots and costumed characters, and the corruption of nostalgia. It is a thing that was supposed to be funny and safe but is instead predatory and malevolent. The character's design — a towering black-and-white cartoon creature with white gloves and an ear-to-ear grin — has become iconic in internet horror circles, frequently appearing in fan art, game mods, and YouTube horror series alongside Siren Head. Cartoon Cat represents the apex of the modern trend of corrupting childhood iconography into adult horror.
