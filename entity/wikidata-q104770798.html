Who It Is

Bartolomeo is a name that echoes through five centuries of Italian history, from the battlefields of condottieri to the quiet workshops of Renaissance painters, from the Vatican kitchens to the invention of the piano. It is a name worn by saints and sinners, by popes and murderers, by the man who first struck a hammer on strings and the man whose trial shook the world. To say 'Bartolomeo' is to summon a gallery of lives—each one a thread in the rich tapestry of Italian culture and its global reach.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From the Aramaic bar-Tolmai, meaning 'son of Tolmai' or 'son of the furrow', referring to a farmer or ploughman. The name entered Italian via the Greek Bartholomaios and Latin Bartholomaeus.

Accounts I
The Life of Bartolomeo ColleoniHistorical records, biographies

c. 1400–1475

Bartolomeo Colleoni was a renowned condottiero (mercenary captain) who served various Italian city-states, including Venice and Milan. He is famous for his military prowess and for the equestrian statue by Verrocchio in Venice. He left a legacy of military leadership and a famous monument.

The Invention of the Piano by Bartolomeo CristoforiHistorical records, museum archives

c. 1655–1731

Bartolomeo Cristofori, a harpsichord maker, invented the piano (gravicembalo col piano e forte) around 1700, which allowed for dynamic expression. He worked at the Medici court in Florence. Revolutionized music and became the foundation of modern keyboard instruments.

Accounts II
The Martyrdom of Saint BartholomewChristian hagiography

1st century AD (traditional)

Saint Bartholomew, one of the twelve apostles, is traditionally said to have been flayed alive and beheaded in Armenia. His relics are venerated in various churches. He became a martyr and patron saint of tanners, leatherworkers, and bookbinders.

The Trial of Sacco and VanzettiCourt records, historical accounts

1920–1927

Bartolomeo Vanzetti, an Italian anarchist, was convicted and executed for murder in Massachusetts along with Nicola Sacco. Their trial was controversial, with many believing they were convicted for their political beliefs and immigrant status. Their execution sparked worldwide protests and remains a symbol of injustice.
