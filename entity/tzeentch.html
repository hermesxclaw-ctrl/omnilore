Archive Dossier — Identity

Tzeentch

Pronunciation The official Games Workshop pronunciation is 'Zeench', with a soft 't' that is nearly silent. Some pronounce it as 'Tzeench' with a hard 't'.

Known As

The Changer of WaysThe Architect of FateThe Great ConspiratorThe Weaver of DestiniesThe God of ChangeThe Lord of SorceryThe Master of FortuneThe SchemerThe Raven God

Etymology

The name 'Tzeentch' is likely derived from the German word 'Zins' or 'Zin', meaning 'tribute' or 'rent', but more probably from the Slavic word 'tsar' or 'tzar' (emperor), reflecting his role as a ruler of fate. Some fan theories suggest a connection to the word 'trickster' or the Lovecraftian entity 'Tsathoggua', but these are unofficial. In Warhammer lore, the name is often associated with the sound of change and manipulation.

Cultural Ownership

Tzeentch is a fictional entity created by Games Workshop for their Warhammer Fantasy and Warhammer 40,000 settings. He is part of the Chaos Gods pantheon, which draws inspiration from various mythological and literary sources, but Tzeentch himself is an original creation. He is owned by Games Workshop and has appeared in their publications, games, and licensed media since the 1980s.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans have drawn parallels between Tzeentch and real-world trickster deities like Loki or Coyote, but these are speculative and not official. Within the Warhammer community, there is debate over whether Tzeentch's nature is truly unknowable or if he has a hidden agenda, as his motivations are intentionally left ambiguous by writers. There is also discussion about the extent to which Tzeentch's power is limited by his own schemes, as he is often said to be his own greatest enemy.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the grim darkness of the 41st millennium, there is only war—and behind that war, a scheming god of change who plots the fate of galaxies. Tzeentch, the Changer of Ways, is the Chaos God of ambition, sorcery, and hope, a being of infinite plots and ever-shifting forms, whose ultimate goal is a mystery even to himself.

There is a plan. There is always a plan. But the plan changes. It must change. Change is the only constant. And I am the only god who understands that even my own plans are lies I tell myself.

Who It Is

Tzeentch is the Chaos God of change, knowledge, sorcery, ambition, and scheming — the most intelligent and subtle of the four Great Chaos Gods, and arguably the most dangerous. While Khorne offers brute force, Tzeentch offers knowledge and power — but always at a terrible price. Tzeentch is a being of infinite cunning and infinite deceit. Every event in the universe is part of his grand, incomprehensible plan — except that he may be lying about having a plan at all. The truth about Tzeentch is that even he may not know his own goals, because change (his fundamental nature) means that his plans are constantly evolving and contradicting themselves. His followers — the Thousand Sons Chaos Space Marines and Tzeentchian daemons — are powerful sorcerers and schemers who believe they are manipulating events toward their own goals, unaware that they are puppets in Tzeentch's ever-shifting design. Tzeentch's realm in the Warp is the Impossible Fortress, a labyrinthine structure that constantly rearranges itself, filled with portals, traps, and horrors. His daemons include Pink Horrors (which split into Blue Horrors when killed), Flamers (living fire), and the Lords of Change — his Greater Daemons, massive avian entities of immense psychic power and ancient knowledge. Tzeentch's greatest weapon is hope — because hope inspires ambition, and ambition leads to deals with the Changer of Ways. Every wizard who seeks forbidden knowledge, every revolutionary who wants to overturn the order, every soul that wishes for things to be different — all of them are Tzeentch's prey. He represents the terrifying truth that the pursuit of knowledge may corrupt you absolutely, that the desire for change may destroy everything you love, and that the greatest conspiracies are the ones where even the conspirators do not know they are pawns. Tzeentch is the god who offers you exactly what you want — and makes you grateful for the disaster that follows.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

Tzeentch is a shapeshifter and has no fixed form. He is often depicted as a towering, ethereal being composed of shimmering, ever-shifting flesh, with multiple faces, limbs, and eyes. Common depictions include a large, feathered body reminiscent of a bird or a serpent, with a beak-like mouth and glowing, piercing eyes. His form is said to be constantly changing, making it impossible to look at him for long without feeling disoriented. In art, he is often shown surrounded by swirling magical energies, floating in a void of stars and arcane symbols.

Symbols & Iconography

Tzeentch's primary symbol is the 'Sigil of Tzeentch', a complex, nine-pointed star or a labyrinthine pattern representing the nine circles of magic and the ever-turning wheel of fate. His colors are blue and pink, often with gold accents. His daemons are often decorated with eyes, feathers, and swirling patterns. The number nine is sacred to him, and his daemons often have nine eyes, nine horns, or other multiples of nine.

Behaviour & Personality

Tzeentch is the master of schemes and plots. He is intelligent, cunning, and manipulative, viewing all beings as pawns in his grand game. He is not malicious in a petty sense, but rather sees everything as a means to an end. He is also a god of hope and ambition, encouraging mortals to strive for change and betterment, but always twisting their desires to serve his own inscrutable plans. He is often described as having a dark sense of humor and a love of irony. He is also notoriously unreliable, even to his own followers, as he may aid them one moment and betray them the next, all part of his grand design.

Domains & Habitats

Tzeentch dwells in the Realm of Tzeentch, a domain within the Warp known as the 'Crystal Labyrinth'. This realm is a vast, ever-shifting maze of crystalline structures, impossible geometry, and floating platforms, filled with rivers of pure magic and skies of swirling colors. It is a place of constant change and intellectual pursuit, where daemons and sorcerers ponder the mysteries of the universe. Tzeentch's influence extends throughout the Warp and into the material universe, particularly on worlds where sorcery is practiced or where ambition and change are prevalent.

Version Differences

In Warhammer Fantasy Battles, Tzeentch is depicted similarly to his 40k counterpart, but with a focus on magic and mutation. In the Total War: Warhammer video games, his appearance and abilities are adapted for gameplay. In the Horus Heresy series, Tzeentch's influence on Magnus the Red and the Thousand Sons is explored in detail, providing a more nuanced view of his manipulation. In the Age of Sigmar setting, Tzeentch is a major antagonist, with his own battletome and expanded lore, including the concept of 'Fateweavers' and the 'Tzeentchian Cults'.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Shapeshifting

Source Version All versions

Description

Tzeentch can alter his form at will, appearing as anything he desires, from a beautiful mortal to a monstrous horror. This is a fundamental aspect of his nature as the god of change.

Limitations

It is implied that his true form is unknowable and that any form he takes is just a projection.

Contradictions

None known.

Sorcery and Magic

Source Version Warhammer 40,000 and Warhammer Fantasy

Description

Tzeentch is the ultimate master of sorcery, capable of manipulating reality itself. He can grant magical powers to his followers and is the source of all psychic powers in the Warp, though other gods have their own domains.

Limitations

His power is often limited by his own schemes; he may not act directly but through manipulation.

Contradictions

Some sources suggest that Tzeentch is not the sole source of psychic power, but the most powerful practitioner.

Fate Manipulation

Source Version Warhammer 40,000

Description

Tzeentch can see and manipulate the threads of fate, altering the future to suit his plans. He is often called the 'Architect of Fate'.

Limitations

He is not omniscient; there are limits to his foresight, and his plans often backfire.

Contradictions

Some lore suggests that Tzeentch is trapped by his own prophecies, unable to escape the fate he has woven.

Immortality and Godhood

Source Version All versions

Description

As a Chaos God, Tzeentch is immortal and exists in the Warp, beyond the constraints of time and space. He cannot be permanently killed, though his power can be diminished.

Limitations

He is dependent on the emotions of mortals, particularly ambition, hope, and the desire for change, to sustain himself.

Contradictions

None known.

Daemonic Legions

Source Version Warhammer 40,000 and Warhammer Fantasy

Description

Tzeentch commands vast legions of daemons, including Pink Horrors, Blue Horrors, Flamers, Screamers, and Lords of Change. These daemons are extensions of his will and can be summoned to the material realm.

Limitations

Daemons require a connection to the Warp or a ritual to manifest in the material realm.

Contradictions

None known.

Mutation

Source Version Warhammer 40,000 and Warhammer Fantasy

Description

Tzeentch can mutate living beings, altering their bodies and minds in chaotic and often grotesque ways. This is both a gift and a curse, as his blessings are often unpredictable.

Limitations

Mutations may be unstable or lead to madness.

Contradictions

None known.

Archive Dossier — Relationships

Khorne

Type rivalSource Codex: Chaos Daemons

Description

Khorne, the Blood God, despises Tzeentch for his reliance on sorcery and manipulation, which Khorne sees as cowardly. Tzeentch, in turn, views Khorne as a mindless brute. Their rivalry is the most intense among the Chaos Gods, and their followers often clash.

Version Note

This rivalry is consistent across all editions.

Conflicting Accounts

None.

Nurgle

Type rivalSource Codex: Chaos Daemons

Description

Nurgle, the Plague God, represents stagnation and despair, the antithesis of Tzeentch's change and hope. They are enemies, as Tzeentch's schemes often disrupt Nurgle's plans for decay.

Version Note

Consistent across editions.

Conflicting Accounts

None.

Slaanesh

Type rivalSource Codex: Chaos Daemons

Description

Slaanesh, the Prince of Pleasure, is a rival to Tzeentch, as both seek to corrupt mortals through different means. Slaanesh's excess is a form of change, but Tzeentch sees it as wasteful and undisciplined.

Version Note

Consistent.

Conflicting Accounts

None.

Magnus the Red

Type worshipper/pawnSource A Thousand Sons, Codex: Thousand Sons

Description

Magnus is one of Tzeentch's most powerful servants, a Daemon Prince who leads the Thousand Sons. Tzeentch often uses Magnus as a tool, but Magnus retains some independence and occasionally defies his patron.

Version Note

In the Horus Heresy, Magnus was manipulated into serving Tzeentch; later he became a Daemon Prince.

Conflicting Accounts

Some sources suggest Magnus may have willingly embraced Tzeentch, while others imply he was tricked.

Ahriman

Type worshipper/pawnSource Ahriman novels

Description

Ahriman is a powerful sorcerer of the Thousand Sons who seeks to undo the Rubric. Tzeentch often thwarts his plans, but also grants him power. Their relationship is complex, with Ahriman sometimes rebelling against Tzeentch's will.

Version Note

This relationship is primarily explored in the Ahriman trilogy.
