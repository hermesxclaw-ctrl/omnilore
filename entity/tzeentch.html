Who It Is

In the grim darkness of the 41st millennium, there is only war—and behind that war, a scheming god of change who plots the fate of galaxies. Tzeentch, the Changer of Ways, is the Chaos God of ambition, sorcery, and hope, a being of infinite plots and ever-shifting forms, whose ultimate goal is a mystery even to himself.

Tzeentch is the master of schemes and plots. He is intelligent, cunning, and manipulative, viewing all beings as pawns in his grand game. He is not malicious in a petty sense, but rather sees everything as a means to an end. He is also a god of hope and ambition, encouraging mortals to strive for change and betterment, but always twisting their desires to serve his own inscrutable plans. He is often described as having a dark sense of humor and a love of irony. Powers
Shapeshifting

Tzeentch can alter his form at will, appearing as anything he desires, from a beautiful mortal to a monstrous horror. This is a fundamental aspect of his nature as the god of change.

Limit: It is implied that his true form is unknowable and that any form he takes is just a projection.

Sorcery and Magic

Tzeentch is the ultimate master of sorcery, capable of manipulating reality itself. He can grant magical powers to his followers and is the source of all psychic powers in the Warp, though other gods have their own domains.

Limit: His power is often limited by his own schemes; he may not act directly but through manipulation.

Fate Manipulation

Tzeentch can see and manipulate the threads of fate, altering the future to suit his plans. He is often called the 'Architect of Fate'.

Limit: He is not omniscient; there are limits to his foresight, and his plans often backfire.

Immortality and Godhood

As a Chaos God, Tzeentch is immortal and exists in the Warp, beyond the constraints of time and space. He cannot be permanently killed, though his power can be diminished.

Limit: He is dependent on the emotions of mortals, particularly ambition, hope, and the desire for change, to sustain himself.

Daemonic Legions

Tzeentch commands vast legions of daemons, including Pink Horrors, Blue Horrors, Flamers, Screamers, and Lords of Change. These daemons are extensions of his will and can be summoned to the material realm.

Limit: Daemons require a connection to the Warp or a ritual to manifest in the material realm.

Mutation

Tzeentch can mutate living beings, altering their bodies and minds in chaotic and often grotesque ways. This is both a gift and a curse, as his blessings are often unpredictable.

Limit: Mutations may be unstable or lead to madness.

Common depictions include a large, feathered body reminiscent of a bird or a serpent, with a beak-like mouth and glowing, piercing eyes. Accounts I
The Horus Heresy and the Fall of the Thousand SonsA Thousand Sons by Graham McNeill, The Burning of Prospero by Dan Abnett, and other Horus Heresy novels

30th Millennium (M31)

Tzeentch manipulated Magnus the Red, Primarch of the Thousand Sons Legion, offering him knowledge and power in exchange for his loyalty. Magnus, seeking to warn the Emperor of Horus's betrayal, used sorcery to project his soul to Terra, but inadvertently breached the Emperor's wards, allowing daemons to enter the Imperial Palace. The Emperor, enraged, ordered the Space Wolves to attack Prospero, leading to the destruction of the Thousand Sons' homeworld. Magnus and his surviving sons fled into the Warp, eventually becoming daemons and servants of Tzeentch. The Thousand Sons were corrupted and became Chaos Space Marines dedicated to Tzeentch. Magnus became a Daemon Prince of Tzeentch. The event contributed to the Horus Heresy and the eventual fall of the Imperium.

Accounts II
The Ahriman TrilogyAhriman: Exile, Ahriman: Sorcerer, Ahriman: Unchanged by John French

41st Millennium (M41)

Ahriman, a powerful sorcerer of the Thousand Sons, sought to reverse the mutations caused by the Rubric of Ahriman, a ritual he himself had cast to save his legion from mutation. The ritual failed, turning most of his brothers into mindless automatons. Ahriman's quest to undo this has led him to delve deeper into Tzeentch's power, often coming into conflict with his daemons and even Tzeentch himself. The trilogy explores the nature of fate, ambition, and the price of sorcery. Ahriman remains a powerful but tormented figure, constantly seeking to restore his legion but always falling short. Tzeentch uses him as a tool, often thwarting his plans for his own amusement.

The Crystal LabyrinthVarious codexes and novels

Timeless

The Crystal Labyrinth is Tzeentch's domain in the Warp, a vast, ever-shifting maze of crystal and magic. It is said that those who enter are lost forever, trapped in a maze of their own desires and fears. Tzeentch himself resides at the center, plotting the fates of all. The labyrinth is also a place of knowledge, where secrets are kept and bargains are made. The labyrinth is a symbol of Tzeentch's nature, representing the complexity and inescapability of his schemes.
