Who It Is

Phi Pop is a terrifying cannibal ghost in the folklore of northeastern Thailand (Isan) and Laos. Unlike phi krasue whose appearance is distinct, phi pop is an invisible or rarely-seen entity that possesses living humans — usually women — turning them into cannibals with an insatiable hunger for raw meat and human entrails. A person possessed by phi pop becomes pale, secretive, and gluttonous; they are said to crawl at night to consume the internal organs of sleeping relatives. The condition is believed to be hereditary — phi pop 'runs in families,' passed from mother to daughter. Entire Isan villages have been known to expel families suspected of carrying the phi pop curse. The spirit can also be summoned by a practitioner of black magic (sorcerer) to attack enemies. The diagnosis of phi pop possession was historically associated with mental illness, pellagra (niacin deficiency), or other medical conditions that caused strange behavior. Unlike the tragic phi krasue, phi pop is purely malevolent. Exorcism by a powerful monk (often using the practice of suat mon or chanting) is the only cure. The belief remains strong in rural Isan and has been explored in Thai horror cinema as a metaphor for social ostracism and the fear of hidden evil within the community.
