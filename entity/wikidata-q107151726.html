Who It Is

In the spring of 1993, a chestnut filly from the regional tracks of Iwate charged onto the central racing scene, her mane braided like a ribbon, and finished a nose behind the great Vega in two of Japan's most prestigious filly races. She never won a classic, but her beauty and tenacity captured hearts, and her legacy endures in a popular anime and a memorial at Yushun Memorial Park.

She was described as a 'good-looking horse' and was popular among female fans. On the track, she displayed front-running tendencies, leading from start to finish in her Central debut.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Yukino Bijin' (ユキノビジン) translates to 'Snow Beauty' or 'Beauty of the Snow' in Japanese. It is a combination of 'yuki' (snow) and 'bijin' (beautiful woman). The name reflects her pure white mane and her elegant appearance.

Pronunciation — Yoo-kee-no Bee-jeen

Racing ability

Yukino Bijin was a talented racehorse, winning the Queen Stakes and finishing second in two classic races (Oka Sho and Yushun Himba) behind Vega.

Limit: She suffered from a hip joint condition and a pastern fracture, which limited her racing career.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Yukino Bijin was a chestnut mare with a coat resembling that of her sire, Sakura Yutaka O. She was known for a pure white, ribbon-like braid in her mane, which contributed to her 'beautiful girl' image.

Symbols — Her white braid became her signature symbol, often depicted in fan art and in her anthropomorphic representation in Umamusume.

Domains — She raced on both dirt and turf. Her early career was at Morioka Racecourse (dirt), and later she competed on turf at central tracks like Hanshin and Tokyo.

Accounts I
Debut at MoriokaSource material

1992

Yukino Bijin made her debut at Morioka Racecourse on dirt, winning her first race. She then won two more races, making it three consecutive victories, before finishing fifth in the Nambu Koma Sho. Her regional background later became a notable aspect of her story.

Central Debut VictorySource material

1993

Transferred to the central circuit, Yukino Bijin was a longshot in the Crocus Stakes but led from start to finish, winning by three lengths. She proved her ability on the central circuit.

Accounts II
Classic Battles with VegaSource material

1993

In the Oka Sho and Yushun Himba, Yukino Bijin finished second to Vega in both races, with the Oka Sho finish being a dead heat for second. She was unable to win a classic but gained recognition for her close fights.

Queen Stakes Victory and Later CareerSource material

1993

After a rest, Yukino Bijin won the Queen Stakes by two lengths. She then finished 10th in the Queen Elizabeth II Cup but won the Turquoise Stakes at the end of the year. Her victory in the Queen Stakes was her biggest win. She retired due to a pastern fracture.
