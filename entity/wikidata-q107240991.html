The Approach

From a 17th-century pharmacy in Hanau to a global technology group, Heraeus's story is one of relentless innovation in precious metals and quartz glass. Its platinum smelting revolution, pioneering vacuum melting, and the quartz glass that still reflects laser beams on the Moon demonstrate a legacy of scientific and industrial achievement. Yet this history also includes a darker chapter of forced labor during the Nazi era, a past the company has since confronted through independent research and compensation funds.

Content advisory. Mentions of forced labor during World War II and the company's involvement in the Third Reich. No graphic or violent content.

The name 'Heraeus' is derived from the family surname of the founder, Wilhelm Carl Heraeus. The family name itself has roots in the German-speaking region, but the exact origin is not documented in the provided sources.

Heraeus is a German company, deeply rooted in the Hanau region, with a long history of German industrial and scientific innovation. It is also a global entity with operations worldwide.

Powers & Abilities

Platinum Smelting

Source Version Historical - 1856

Description

Heraeus pioneered the large-scale melting of platinum using an oxyhydrogen blowpipe, enabling the production of pure platinum in large quantities. This innovation was crucial for the jewelry, dental, and chemical industries.

Limitations

The initial process required significant technical expertise and specialized equipment, and was limited by the availability of oxyhydrogen gas.

Contradictions

None noted.

Quartz Glass Production

Source Version Historical - late 19th century

Description

Heraeus developed a process to melt rock crystal at around 2000°C to produce high-purity quartz glass, which is resistant to heat and acids and used in medicine and manufacturing.

Limitations

The high temperatures required special ovens and careful control to avoid defects.

Stories & Myths

The Founding of the Platinum Smelter

Source Heraeus official historyCulture German industrialPeriod 1851-1888

Summary

Wilhelm Carl Heraeus, a pharmacist, took over the Einhorn Pharmacy and began experimenting with melting platinum to help local goldsmiths. In 1856, he succeeded using an oxyhydrogen blowpipe, leading to the founding of the W.C. Heraeus First German Platinum Smelters. The company grew rapidly, supplying platinum to various industries.

Participants

Established Heraeus as a leader in platinum processing, paving the way for future innovations.

Source NASA and HeraeusCulture Space explorationPeriod 1969

Summary

Forced Labor During World War II

Source Independent scientific study and EVZ FoundationCulture German wartime historyPeriod 1939-1945

Summary

During World War II, Heraeus employed 1,500 forced laborers from the Hanau civilian labor camp. The company produced goods for the armament industry, including catalytic converters for anti-aircraft searchlights. After the war, Heraeus commissioned an independent study of its role in the Third Reich and contributed to the compensation fund for forced laborers.
