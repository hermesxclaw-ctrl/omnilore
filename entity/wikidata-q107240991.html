Who It Is

From a 17th-century pharmacy in Hanau to a global technology group, Heraeus's story is one of relentless innovation in precious metals and quartz glass. Its platinum smelting revolution, pioneering vacuum melting, and the quartz glass that still reflects laser beams on the Moon demonstrate a legacy of scientific and industrial achievement. Yet this history also includes a darker chapter of forced labor during the Nazi era, a past the company has since confronted through independent research and compensation funds.

Heraeus is characterized by a culture of innovation, family ownership, and long-term thinking. The company values its heritage and scientific excellence, while also adapting to modern market needs. It is known for its commitment to sustainability and responsible business practices, though its wartime history is a complex part of its past.

Powers
Platinum Smelting

Heraeus pioneered the large-scale melting of platinum using an oxyhydrogen blowpipe, enabling the production of pure platinum in large quantities. This innovation was crucial for the jewelry, dental, and chemical industries.

Limit: The initial process required significant technical expertise and specialized equipment, and was limited by the availability of oxyhydrogen gas.

Quartz Glass Production

Heraeus developed a process to melt rock crystal at around 2000°C to produce high-purity quartz glass, which is resistant to heat and acids and used in medicine and manufacturing.

Limit: The high temperatures required special ovens and careful control to avoid defects.

Vacuum Melting

Heraeus patented the process of melting metals under vacuum, which was a significant innovation in metallurgy, allowing for the production of high-purity metals and alloys.

Limit: Requires vacuum chambers and energy-intensive processes.

UV High-Pressure Lamp

In 1904, Heraeus and Richard Küch developed the tanning lamp, pioneering body irradiation with artificial light for medical light therapy.

Limit: Early versions were cumbersome and had limited lifespan.

Platinum Resistance Thermometer

Developed in 1906, this standardized thermometer uses platinum's predictable resistance changes with temperature, a principle still used in cars and ovens today.

Limit: Requires high-purity platinum and careful calibration.

Accounts I
The Founding of the Platinum SmelterHeraeus official history

1851-1888

Wilhelm Carl Heraeus, a pharmacist, took over the Einhorn Pharmacy and began experimenting with melting platinum to help local goldsmiths. In 1856, he succeeded using an oxyhydrogen blowpipe, leading to the founding of the W.C. Heraeus First German Platinum Smelters. The company grew rapidly, supplying platinum to various industries. Established Heraeus as a leader in platinum processing, paving the way for future innovations.

Accounts II
The Apollo 11 Laser ReflectorNASA and Heraeus

1969

Heraeus supplied the quartz glass for the laser reflector left on the Moon by Apollo 11. This reflector is still used to measure the exact distance between Earth and the Moon. Demonstrated Heraeus's technological prowess and contributed to lunar science.

Forced Labor During World War IIIndependent scientific study and EVZ Foundation

1939-1945

During World War II, Heraeus employed 1,500 forced laborers from the Hanau civilian labor camp. The company produced goods for the armament industry, including catalytic converters for anti-aircraft searchlights. After the war, Heraeus commissioned an independent study of its role in the Third Reich and contributed to the compensation fund for forced laborers. Heraeus has acknowledged its wartime actions and participated in reconciliation efforts.
