Who It Is

Thagyamin is the king of the nats, the celestial spirits in Burmese Buddhist cosmology. He is derived from the Hindu god Indra (Sakka in Pali) but has been thoroughly adapted into Burmese culture as the chief of the Thirty-Seven Nats — the official pantheon of spirits recognized across Myanmar. Thagyamin is the ruler of Tavatimsa, the second heaven atop Mount Meru, where he presides over a court of lesser nats and devas. He is responsible for recording the deeds of all beings, keeping a ledger of good and evil actions, and reporting to the Buddha. In Burmese folk belief, Thagyamin descends to earth during the Burmese New Year (Thingyan, the Water Festival) to judge human behavior for the past year — the festival's water-throwing traditionally represents cleansing for the new year. Thagyamin is married to Queen Sujata (a celestial queen) and is depicted in Burmese iconography as a crowned king, often holding a fly whisk (a symbol of authority) and wearing regalia similar to that of Burmese monarchs. His face is typically green, reflecting his celestial nature. Unlike many nats who were historical figures deified after violent deaths, Thagyamin is purely celestial in origin. He represents the Buddhist integration of pre-existing spirit worship into a hierarchical, moral framework.
