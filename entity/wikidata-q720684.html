Who It Is

Before Marduk was the King of the Gods, before he split Tiamat's corpse to make the sky and sea, there was a humbler, more focused deity: Asalluhi, the divine exorcist, the god you called when the demon was in the house and the sickness was in the bones. He was the original 'priest on call,' the one who ran to his father Enki, not for power, but for knowledge—the precise, potent words that could banish the darkness and restore a life.

Asalluhi is characterized as the archetypal exorcist-priest (āšipu). He is the mediator between the divine realm and humanity, diagnosing the cause of illness or misfortune (often divine punishment or demonic attack) and then performing the necessary rituals to cure it. He is dutiful, wise, and powerful, but also subordinate to his father Enki, to whom he turns for instruction in the most difficult cases.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is of Sumerian origin, likely meaning 'the exorcist' or 'the incantation priest.' The element 'Asal' may relate to a divine or exalted figure, while 'luḫi' connects to cleansing or purification, reflecting the god's role in magical rites.

Pronunciation — Ah-sahl-LOO-hee

Aliases — Asarluhi, Asaruludu, Asalluḫi, Asalluhhi, Marduk (later syncretized)

Ownership — Mesopotamian (Sumerian and Akkadian); originally a local god of Ku'ara, later syncretized with the Babylonian national god Marduk.

Powers
Divine Exorcism (Āšipūtu)

Asalluhi is the supreme exorcist. He has the power to identify, confront, and banish demons, ghosts, and malevolent spirits that cause illness, madness, and misfortune. His rituals involve purification with water, fire, and incense, as well as the recitation of powerful incantations.

Limit: Asalluhi often requires the knowledge and authorization of his father Enki to perform the most powerful exorcisms. He is not omnipotent; he works within a cosmic framework where demons are a reality and must be countered through specific, correct rituals.

Healing Magic

Closely linked to exorcism, Asalluhi's powers are used to heal diseases that were believed to be caused by supernatural agents. He restores health by removing the offending demon or curse and by purifying the patient.

Limit: His healing is conditional on the correct diagnosis of the ailment and the proper performance of the ritual. If the patient's suffering was a punishment from a major god, Asalluhi's power might be insufficient without that god's pardon.

Purification and Cleansing

Asalluhi has the power to cleanse people, objects, temples, and homes from ritual impurity, taboo violations, and the lingering effects of witchcraft. This purification is essential for restoring order and allowing normal life to resume.

Limit: The purification is effective only when the correct rituals and materials (water, fire, tamarisk, etc.) are used, as dictated by the god.

Intercession with Enki

A unique power/role: Asalluhi acts as the intermediary between humanity and the god of wisdom, Enki. He reports the problem to Enki, who then provides the solution, which Asalluhi then implements. This establishes him as a crucial link in the divine chain of help.

Limit: This power is one of delegation; he cannot act on the highest-level knowledge without Enki's input.

Kingship and Cosmic Order (as Marduk)

After the syncretism, Asalluhi/Marduk gains the powers of kingship over the gods, creation (defeating Tiamat), and the maintenance of cosmic order (me). He becomes the creator of humanity and the chief deity of the pantheon.

Limit: These powers are a later development, not part of Asalluhi's original independent portfolio.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No definitive visual depiction of Asalluhi as an independent deity survives. In later syncretized form as Marduk, he is depicted as a bearded man in royal robes, often holding a sickle-sword or a spade, and frequently shown standing on or battling the dragon Mušḫuššu. As Asalluhi, his iconography was likely simpler, possibly a local god figure, but this is reconstructed from his role as a divine exorcist.

Symbols — Asalluhi's symbols, as an independent god, are not clearly attested. In his syncretized form as Marduk, his symbols include the spade (a digging tool, symbolizing his role in creation and purification), the sickle-sword, and the dragon Mušḫuššu. As a god of incantations, the ritual equipment of the exorcist—such as the water-sprinkler and censer—would be symbolically associated with him.

Domains — Asalluhi's primary domain is exorcism, magic, and healing. He is the god of incantations and purification rituals, specifically those that cleanse individuals and households from demonic influence, witchcraft, and the effects of sin or taboo. He is also a god of wisdom, as his knowledge of rituals is the key to restoring cosmic and personal order. He is closely associated with the city of Ku'ara, his cult center, and later with Babylon

Accounts I
The Divine Exorcist at WorkSumerian incantation texts (e.g., 'The Demon Lament')

c. 2100–1600 BCE

A typical story pattern: A person is afflicted by a demon (such as the 'Lil' or 'Asag') causing sickness or misfortune. The sufferer cries out to the god Asalluhi. Asalluhi, seeing the problem, goes to his father Enki and says, 'What I do not know, what I do not know, what shall I do?' Enki then reveals the secret ritual and incantation to his son. Asalluhi returns and performs the exorcism, banishing the demon and restoring the person to health. The demon is banished, the person is healed, and the cosmic order is restored. The story also explains the origin and efficacy of the incantation itself, as it is now known to be the very words of Enki, transmitted through Asalluhi.

Accounts II
The Syncretism with MardukOld Babylonian god lists, later theological texts

c. 1800 BCE onwards

As the city of Babylon rose to political prominence, its patron god Marduk absorbed the identities of several local deities to consolidate power. Asalluhi, the god of Ku'ara, was one of the most significant. The god list 'An = Anum' explicitly equates the two: 'Asalluhi is Marduk.' This was not a simple name change but a theological merger, where Marduk took on Asalluhi's role as the divine exorcist, and Asalluhi's cult and mythology were folded into Marduk's. Asalluhi largely disappeared as an independent deity, surviving primarily as an epithet or alternate name for Marduk. His role as the exorcist god, however, became a major part of Marduk's identity, making the king of the gods also the great healer and purifier.
