Who It Is

A universal remedy, solvent, and spiritual principle in alchemy — the 'universal medicine' sought alongside the Philosopher's Stone. The term Azoth is a linguistic artifact: it represents the letters A (alpha), Z (omega), O (omicron), and TH (theta/tau) — the first and last letters of the Greek, Latin, and Hebrew alphabets — symbolizing the beginning and end of all things, the Alpha and Omega of the alchemical work. Azoth was also identified with the alkahest (the universal solvent) and the anima mundi (world soul). The Caduceus (Hermes' wand) became its emblem: the central staff representing the axis of transformation, and the two intertwined serpents representing the union of opposites (solve et coagula). Azoth is the mercurial principle — fluid, transformative, and containing all possibilities within itself.
