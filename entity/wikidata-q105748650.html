Who It Is

In the vast unseen hierarchy of heaven, one archangel stands apart: Azrael, the Angel of Death. He is not a grim reaper of terror, but a servant of divine will, tasked with the solemn duty of separating soul from body at the appointed hour. In Islamic tradition, he is one of the four great archangels, a being of immense power and compassion, who holds the scroll of every mortal's fate.

Benevolent and obedient to God's will. He does not act independently but only when commanded. He is compassionate to saints and prophets, appearing to them in beautiful forms. He is impartial and follows divine decree.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Hebrew עֲזַרְאֵל (ʿǍzarʾēl), meaning 'God has helped' or 'Help from God'. In Arabic, عزرائيل (ʿAzrāʾīl) is the common form. The name suggests a theophoric element meaning 'the one whom God helps'.

Pronunciation — AZ-rah-el (English), Az-ra-EEL (Arabic)

Aliases — ʿAzrāʾīl, ʿIzrāʾīl, Ezrā’ël, Malak al-Mawt, Angel of Death, Azriel (possible variant)

Ownership — Primarily Islamic, with roots in Jewish angelology and later Christian Ethiopian apocrypha.

Names are cult layers, not one fixed label.

Powers
Soul Taking

The primary ability to remove souls from bodies at death, as commanded by God.

Limit: Cannot take a soul until God gives permission and the appointed time arrives.

Immense Size and Form

He can appear in various forms, including a blue ram, a giant with many eyes and wings, or a handsome human to saints.

Limit: Form may be limited by the perception of the soul he is approaching.

Knowledge of Death Times

He knows the names of those who will die, written on a leaf from a tree near God's throne, 40 days before death.

Limit: Knowledge is given by God; he does not know independently.

Psychopomp

He guides souls to the afterlife.

Limit: His role is to transport, not judge.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Descriptions vary: some say he has 4 faces, 4000 wings (wings of grace and punishment), and a body covered with eyes and tongues. A blue ram-like form is mentioned. His size is immense; the Earth on his shoulder is like a bean. He has 70,000 feet. Saints may see him in a beautiful human form.

Symbols — Scroll of fate, flaming whip, book of life and death, sometimes depicted with a scythe (later Western influence).

Domains — Resides in the Third Heaven (according to some traditions). His domain is the transition between life and death, and the afterlife.

Accounts I
The Creation of AdamQisas al-Anbiya (Stories of the Prophets) by Ibn Kathir, Tabari, etc.

Pre-Islamic/early Islamic

God ordered the archangels to collect dust from the earth for creating Adam. Only Azrael succeeded, descending to the earth and taking dust from all corners. For this, he was appointed the angel of death. Azrael became the angel of death, linking life and death.

Death and AzraelVarious exegesis, including Al-‘Umairi

Medieval

When God created Death, the angels swooned. Death submitted to Azrael. In some accounts, Death and Azrael are one entity; in others, Death is a tool. Establishes Azrael's authority over death.

Accounts II
Meeting with RumiSufi tradition

13th century

Rumi met Azrael in human shape before his death, indicating a peaceful transition for saints. Reinforces the belief that saints meet Azrael in a beautiful form.

Azrael in the Apocalypse of PeterEthiopic Apocalypse of Peter

16th century (text may be older)

Azrael appears as an angel of hell who avenges those wronged in life. Shows a different role in a non-Islamic context.

Azrael is a fallen angel or demon. — In Islam, Azrael is a loyal archangel, not fallen.

Azrael kills people arbitrarily. — He only acts on God's command and at the appointed time.

Azrael is a figure in Judaism. — The Hebrew Bible and rabbinic literature do not mention Azrael; Azriel appears in Kabbalah but not as angel of death.

The Grim Reaper is based on Azrael. — The Grim Reaper is a separate personification of death, not directly derived from Azrael.
