The Approach

In the vast unseen hierarchy of heaven, one archangel stands apart: Azrael, the Angel of Death. He is not a grim reaper of terror, but a servant of divine will, tasked with the solemn duty of separating soul from body at the appointed hour. In Islamic tradition, he is one of the four great archangels, a being of immense power and compassion, who holds the scroll of every mortal's fate.

Content advisory. Contains religious themes and descriptions of death and the afterlife.
Identity

Azrael

Pronunciation AZ-rah-el (English), Az-ra-EEL (Arabic)

Known As

ʿAzrāʾīlʿIzrāʾīlEzrā’ëlMalak al-MawtAngel of DeathAzriel (possible variant)

Etymology

From Hebrew עֲזַרְאֵל (ʿǍzarʾēl), meaning 'God has helped' or 'Help from God'. In Arabic, عزرائيل (ʿAzrāʾīl) is the common form. The name suggests a theophoric element meaning 'the one whom God helps'.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily Islamic, with roots in Jewish angelology and later Christian Ethiopian apocrypha.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars identify Azrael with the biblical 'destroyer' or the Angel of Death in Jewish tradition, but this is not canonical in Judaism. In Islam, he is identified with the Quranic Malak al-Mawt. Some traditions distinguish between Death as a separate entity and Azrael as its agent.

Description

Appearance

Descriptions vary: some say he has 4 faces, 4000 wings (wings of grace and punishment), and a body covered with eyes and tongues. A blue ram-like form is mentioned. His size is immense; the Earth on his shoulder is like a bean. He has 70,000 feet. Saints may see him in a beautiful human form.

Symbols & Iconography

Scroll of fate, flaming whip, book of life and death, sometimes depicted with a scythe (later Western influence).

Behaviour & Personality

Benevolent and obedient to God's will. He does not act independently but only when commanded. He is compassionate to saints and prophets, appearing to them in beautiful forms. He is impartial and follows divine decree.

Domains & Habitats

Resides in the Third Heaven (according to some traditions). His domain is the transition between life and death, and the afterlife.

Version Differences

In Islam, he is a major archangel. In Jewish tradition, no canonical angel named Azrael; Azriel appears in Kabbalistic texts. In Ethiopian Christian apocrypha, he is an avenging angel of hell. Western popular culture often portrays him as a grim reaper or a neutral figure.

Powers & Abilities

Soul Taking

Source Version Quran 32:11, Islamic tradition

Description

The primary ability to remove souls from bodies at death, as commanded by God.

Limitations

Cannot take a soul until God gives permission and the appointed time arrives.

Contradictions

Some traditions say he has assistants, others do not.

Immense Size and Form

Source Version Al-Qurtubi, Muqatil ibn Sulayman, Sufi traditions

Description

He can appear in various forms, including a blue ram, a giant with many eyes and wings, or a handsome human to saints.

Limitations

Form may be limited by the perception of the soul he is approaching.

Contradictions

Descriptions vary significantly across sources.

Knowledge of Death Times

Source Version Hadith tradition

Description

Limitations

Knowledge is given by God; he does not know independently.

None noted.

The Creation of Adam

Source Qisas al-Anbiya (Stories of the Prophets) by Ibn Kathir, Tabari, etc.Culture IslamicPeriod Pre-Islamic/early Islamic

Summary

God ordered the archangels to collect dust from the earth for creating Adam. Only Azrael succeeded, descending to the earth and taking dust from all corners. For this, he was appointed the angel of death.

Angel of Hell in Apocalypse of Peter

Source Apocalypse of Peter (Ethiopic)

Description

In the Ethiopic Apocalypse of Peter, Azrael is an angel of hell who avenges wrongs, implying punitive actions.

Scholarly Context

This is a non-canonical Christian text, and the portrayal is distinct from the Islamic Azrael.

Punitive Wings

Source Al-Qurtubi

Description

Azrael has wings of punishment made of iron rods, hooks, and scissors, suggesting a fearsome aspect.

Scholarly Context

These descriptions emphasize his power and the seriousness of death.

Fear and Resistance

Source Sufi tradition

Description

The soul may resist Azrael, accusing him of arbitrariness, but he returns with proof from heaven.

Scholarly Context

Shows the psychological struggle of death.

Contradictions & Disputes

Azrael and Death as one entity

Position A

Azrael and Death are the same being

Position A — evidence

Some exegesis and Hadith interpretations

Position B

Death is a separate entity, a tool used by Azrael

Position B — evidence

Other scholars, including Al-‘Umairi, argue they are distinct

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus; both views exist in Islamic thought.

Azrael's appearance

Position A

He has a specific form with many eyes, wings, etc.

Position A — evidence

Descriptions in Al-Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir

Position B

He appears in different forms depending on the context

Position B — evidence

Sufi traditions say he appears in forms appropriate to the soul

Scholarly Consensus

Both are accepted; the form may be adaptable.

Identification with Malak al-Mawt

Position A

Azrael is the same as the Quranic Angel of Death

Position A — evidence

Mainstream Islamic tradition

Position B

Position B — evidence

Some modern scholars question the identification

Scholarly Consensus

Traditional view identifies them; academic debate exists.
