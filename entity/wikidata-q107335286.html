The Approach

In the moonlit forests of Manipur, a man hears a woman's laughter—sweet, alluring, and utterly inhuman. She is a Helloi, a spirit of the wild, whose beauty masks a power that can unmake a mind. To meet her is to risk madness; to survive her is to carry a story that will haunt you forever.

Identity

Helloi

Pronunciation Not recorded in available sources; likely pronounced 'hel-loy'.

Known As

Heloi

Etymology

The term 'Helloi' is of Meitei origin, but its precise etymological roots are not documented in the available sources. It may be related to words for 'spirit' or 'female', but this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Meitei culture, Manipur, India

Disputed Identifications

Some sources may conflate Helloi with other female spirits like Hingchabi, but they are distinct entities. The relationship between Helloi and the goddess Lairembi is not clarified.

Description

Appearance

Hellois are depicted as beautiful young women, often with charming beauty that is both alluring and dangerous. They are sometimes seen as evil spirits in the form of beautiful maidens.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are documented in the available sources.

Behaviour & Personality

Hellois are wandering spirits associated with seduction of men. They can cause diseases, especially sexually transmitted infections, and mental disorders. They are known to attack men, causing disorder of mind, and may demand something in return to leave a victim. They are considered the most powerful among female spirits.

Domains & Habitats

Hellois inhabit wilderness areas such as thick bushes, dark dense forests, rivers, and meadows. The seven sisters (Helloi Taret) are specifically associated with such places.

Version Differences

The provided source does not elaborate on version differences, but it distinguishes Hellois from Hingchabis: Hellois are wandering spirits, while Hingchabis live inside women.
