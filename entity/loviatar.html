Loviatar

Mother of Diseases, Blind Daughter of Tuoni, Plague-Bringer

Loviatar, blind daughter of Tuoni, gave birth to nine sons in the dark fields of the dead — and every son a different death.

Who It Is

Loviatar is one of the most terrifying figures in Finnish mythology — the mother of diseases, a blind daughter of Tuoni and Tuonetar, and the source of all human suffering in the form of plague, pain, and ague. In the Kalevala (Runo 45), she is impregnated by the east wind while lying on a meadow in the realm of the dead. She returns to Tuonela to give birth to nine sons — the Nine Diseases (sometimes ten, including a daughter) — whom Louhi later unleashes upon the people of Kalevala as revenge for the theft of the Sampo.

Loviatar's name is cognate with the Estonian pain-spirit Lovi and may be connected to the word lovi ('cleft, crevasse, gap') — suggesting an opening between worlds through which sickness enters. She is blind, a detail that emphasizes the mindless, indiscriminate nature of disease: it does not see its victims, does not distinguish between the guilty and the innocent. Her blindness is not a disability in the human sense but a cosmic horror — disease comes for everyone, without reason, without justice.

The Nine Diseases (Yhdeksän sairautta) born of Loviatar are individually named in the Kalevala's incantations: Pistos (Stitch), Ähky (Colic), Luuvalo (Gout), Rutto (Plague), Rupi (Scab), Syöjä (Cancer), Koi (Consumption/Tuberculosis), Pusu (Pustule), and Riisi (Rickets — a relatively modern addition suggesting the ongoing elaboration of the list). Each is a personified affliction with its own origin story, its own favorite organ or tissue, and its own incantation for expulsion. Finnish folk medicine was essentially a system of verbal combat against Loviatar's children — the healer's task was to identify which disease-spirit had entered the patient and to sing it back to its mother in Tuonela.

Loviatar's role in the Kalevala is primarily as the source of epidemic disease. When Louhi, enraged by the theft of the Sampo, decides to destroy Kalevala, she releases the Nine Diseases from their mountain prison (Kipuvuori, the 'Pain-Mountain,' or Kivutar, the 'Pain-Hag') and sends them to ravage the land. Väinämöinen, the master of healing songs, combats them with his incantations, driving each disease back to its source. The healing runos of the Kalevala are among the oldest and most authentically shamanic elements of the epic, preserving pre-Christian medical practices in which the healer (tietäjä, 'knower') entered an altered state of consciousness to do battle with disease-spirits.

Loviatar persists in Finnish folk memory. Her name was used as a curse: 'Loviatar take you!' She represents the darker aspect of Tuonela's power — not just death itself, but the slow, painful approach of death through illness.
