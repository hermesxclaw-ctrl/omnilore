Who It Is

Vishnu is the Preserver and Protector of the universe in the Hindu Trimurti (trinity). As the supreme deity of Vaishnavism—one of the largest traditions within Hinduism—he maintains cosmic order (dharma) through his periodic descents (avatars) into the world whenever evil threatens to overwhelm righteousness. In his cosmic form, Vishnu reclines upon the thousand-headed serpent Ananta Shesha, floating on the primordial ocean of causality, dreaming the universe into existence, with the goddess Lakshmi at his feet and a lotus growing from his navel from which Brahma emerges. His iconography consists of four arms holding four attributes: the conch (shankha), whose sound represents the primordial vibration of creation; the discus (sudarshana chakra), the weapon of divine justice that cuts through ignorance; the mace (gada), representing strength and the destruction of evil; and the lotus (padma), symbolizing purity and liberation. His vehicle is Garuda, the divine eagle. The most essential theological concept of Vaishnavism is the doctrine of avatars—Vishnu's deliberate incarnations at pivotal moments in cosmic history. The ten principal avatars (Dashavatara) are: Matsya (the fish who saved the Vedas from the flood), Kurma (the turtle who supported the churning of the ocean), Varaha (the boar who rescued the earth), Narasimha (the man-lion who destroyed the demon Hiranyakashipu), Vamana (the dwarf who reclaimed the three worlds), Parashurama (the axe-wielding Brahmin), Rama (the ideal king of the Ramayana), Krishna (the divine teacher of the Bhagavad Gita), Buddha (in some traditions), and Kalki (the future avatar who will appear at the end of the Kali Yuga). The Bhagavad Gita, spoken by Krishna as an avatar of Vishnu on the battlefield of Kurukshetra, is the most widely read and influential scripture in all of Hinduism. Vaishnavism has produced some of the most vibrant devotional movements in India's history, from the Alvars of Tamil Nadu to the Chaitanya tradition of Bengal and the Pushtimarg of Gujarat.

Form

Vishnu is typically depicted as a four-armed male deity with a dark blue or blue-black complexion, symbolizing the infinite and the all-pervading nature of the cosmos. He is often shown standing or reclining on the serpent Shesha (Ananta), the cosmic serpent, who floats on the causal ocean. His four hands hold a conch (shankha), a discus (chakra), a mace (gada), and a lotus (padma). He wears yellow silk garments (pitambara), a crown (mukuta), and various jewels, including the Kaustubha gem on his chest. His consort, Lakshmi, is often depicted near his feet, and he is frequently shown with his mount, the eagle Garuda.

Powers

Cosmic Pervasion Vishnu pervades the entire universe, existing in all things as the inner controller (Antaryami). He is the source of all creation and the ultimate reality. Vedic and Puranic None known. None.

Avatara (Descent) Vishnu has the power to incarnate in various forms (avatars) to restore dharma and protect the righteous. The standard list of ten avatars (Dashavatara) includes Matsya (fish), Kurma (tortoise), Varaha (boar), Narasimha (man-lion), Vamana (dwarf), Parashurama (warrior with axe), Rama (prince), Krishna (divine statesman), Buddha (teacher), and Kalki (future warrior). Some traditions include Balarama instead of Buddha. Puranic, with the list varying slightly between texts The avatar is bound by the rules of the form it takes, though it retains divine knowledge. The inclusion of Buddha or Balarama as the ninth avatar is a point of contention between different Vaishnava traditions.

Panchajanya Conch A divine conch that produces the primordial sound 'Om' and is used to announce the beginning of cosmic cycles and to frighten demons. Puranic None. None.

Yoga Nidra (Cosmic Sleep) Vishnu's ability to enter a deep, creative sleep on the serpent Shesha, during which the universe is created and dissolved. This state is a metaphor for his transcendence and his role as the dreamer of the cosmos. Puranic This is not a state of unconsciousness but of supreme awareness. None.

Behavior & Habitat

Vishnu is the Preserver, the sustainer of cosmic order (dharma). He is characterized by his benevolence, compassion, and willingness to descend into the material world to restore balance whenever it is threatened. He is a loving father and protector, yet also a stern judge who can wield his discus to vanquish evil. He is seen as a calm, serene, and majestic presence, embodying grace and tranquility, but his avatars (especially Narasimha and Rama) display fierce and heroic qualities.

Accounts

In the Rigveda, Vishnu, as a solar deity, takes three strides, covering the earth, the atmosphere, and the heavens. These strides establish the cosmos and provide space for all beings to dwell. The third stride is in a realm beyond mortal comprehension, the highest abode.

The devas (gods) and asuras (demons) churn the cosmic ocean to obtain the nectar of immortality (amrita). They use Mount Mandara as the churning rod and the serpent Vasuki as the rope. Vishnu, in the form of Kurma (tortoise), supports the mountain on his back. During the churning, many treasures emerge, including Lakshmi, the goddess of fortune, who becomes Vishnu's consort. A terrible poison (halahala) also emerges, which Shiva drinks to save the universe.

Vishnu descends to earth in various forms to restore dharma. The most famous avatars include: Matsya (fish) who saves the Vedas from a flood; Kurma (tortoise) who supports Mount Mandara during the churning; Varaha (boar) who rescues the earth from the demon Hiranyaksha; Narasimha (man-lion) who kills the demon Hiranyakasipu; Vamana (dwarf) who humbles the king Bali; Parashurama (warrior) who rid the world of corrupt Kshatriyas; Rama (prince) who defeats the demon Ravana; Krishna (divine statesman) who delivers the Bhagavad Gita; Buddha (teacher) who teaches compassion; and Kalki (future warrior) who will end the Kali Yuga.

Krishna, the eighth avatar of Vishnu, is born to Devaki and Vasudeva in a prison cell in Mathura, where his uncle, the tyrant King Kamsa, plans to kill him. By divine intervention, Vasudeva transports the infant Krishna across the Yamuna river to Gokula, where he is raised by the cowherd couple Nanda and Yashoda. Krishna's childhood is filled with miracles, including the killing of demons sent by Kamsa, and his youth is marked by his playful and romantic exploits with the gopis (cowherd girls).

Scholarly Debates
Supremacy of Vishnu vs. Krishna
Vishnu is the supreme deity, and Krishna is his avatar. ⇌ Krishna is the supreme deity, and Vishnu is an expansion of Krishna.
Evidence for A: Puranas like the Vishnu Purana present Vishnu as the ultimate reality.
Evidence for B: The Bhagavata Purana and the Bhagavad Gita, where Krishna speaks as the ultimate Godhead, support this view.
The Ninth Avatar: Buddha vs. Balarama
The ninth avatar is Buddha. ⇌ The ninth avatar is Balarama (Krishna's brother).
Evidence for A: The Agni Purana and some other texts list Buddha as the ninth.
Evidence for B: The Bhagavata Purana and other texts list Balarama.
Historical Origins
Vishnu was a minor Vedic deity who was later elevated to supreme status. ⇌ Vishnu was a major deity from the start, with his Vedic role being one of many.
Evidence for A: The Rigveda gives him a relatively minor role compared to Indra.
Evidence for B: Some scholars argue that his three strides are a major cosmological act, and his prominence grew naturally.
What We Don't Know

Further research could explore regional variations in Vishnu worship, the role of Vishnu in specific folk traditions, and the reception of Vishnu in Western art and literature. A deeper dive into the philosophical commentaries on the Bhagavad Gita and the Vishnu Sahasranama would also be valuable. The exact historical evolution from Vedic Vishnu to Puranic Vishnu remains a subject of ongoing scholarly debate.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Vishnu as the ultimate system administrator of the universe. He doesn't write the code or delete the files (that's Brahma and Shiva), but he ensures everything stays online, runs smoothly, and gets patched when a major bug (a demon) threatens to crash the whole system. His avatars are like emergency patches, each one deployed to fix a specific critical error. He's the calm, reliable presence in the server room, keeping the cosmos from blue-screening.
