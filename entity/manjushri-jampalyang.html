Who It Is

Manjushri (Tibetan: Jampalyang, 'Gentle Glory') is the bodhisattva of transcendent wisdom (prajna) in Mahayana and Tibetan Buddhism. He is the personification of the enlightened wisdom of all Buddhas, wielding the flaming sword of discriminating awareness that cuts through ignorance, confusion, and dualistic thinking. He holds a Prajnaparamita Sutra (the Perfection of Wisdom texts) in his left hand, representing the primacy of wisdom over all other spiritual accomplishments. Manjushri is depicted as a youth (symbolizing the fresh, direct nature of wisdom) with golden skin, wearing the ornaments of a bodhisattva, seated in meditation posture. In Tibetan tradition, Manjushri was instrumental in the establishment of Buddhism in Tibet — it was he who inspired King Trisong Detsen to invite Padmasambhava and Shantarakshita to Tibet. The great debate at Samye Monastery between the Indian master Kamalashila and the Chinese master Heshang Moheyan (8th century) was presided over by the spirit of Manjushri (through the king), and Indian gradualist Buddhism won the day. This debate shaped the future of Tibetan Buddhism. Manjushri's five-peaked mountain abode, Wu Tai Shan in China, is a major pilgrimage site for Tibetan and Chinese Buddhists alike. In Tibetan Buddhism, Manjushri is the special yidam (meditation deity) associated with textual study, debate, and philosophical insight. He is often paired with Avalokiteshvara and Vajrapani as the three 'Protectors of the Lineage' (Rigsum Gonpo).
