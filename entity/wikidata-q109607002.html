Who It Is

She has died a hundred times and remembered every one of them — an ancient Egyptian princess murdered with a cursed knife, reborn in 1940s America as a society girl who picks up wings and a mace to fight beside the man she has loved across millennia. If you search for her as "Shierra," you will find almost nothing; if you search as "Shiera," you find one of DC Comics' oldest heroic legends.

Attested (Golden Age and JSA retcons): Shiera is brave, outspoken, and independently heroic — she initially rescues Hawkman rather than being rescued, and chooses to become Hawkgirl of her own volition (All-Star Comics #5). She is compassionate, witty, and often the more pragmatic partner to Carter Hall's archaeologist-obsessive. As Chay-Ara, she is a princess (later retconned as lover of Prince Khufu) whose murder catalyzes the cycle. Powers
Flight via Nth Metal Wings/Harness

Artificial wings and Nth metal belt/harness grant self-propelled flight, gliding, and aerial combat maneuverability. Early stories show large feathered wings; later stories use Nth metal belt with smaller wings. Speed is superhuman but not Flash-level; highly agile.

Limit: Requires Nth metal and intact harness/wings; damaged wings prevent flight; Nth metal is rare and vulnerable to specific energy attacks in later continuity; cannot achieve space flight without sealed suit. Early stories show fatigue over long distances.

Reincarnation and Past-Life Memory

Cursed/blessed to reincarnate across centuries alongside Carter Hall (Khufu) and their murderer Hath-Set. Upon maturity or trauma, memories of past lives (especially Chay-Ara) resurface, granting skills and recognition of allies/enemies.

Limit: Not voluntary; cycle is tied to Nth metal knife murder; memories are fragmentary and can be suppressed (Kendra Saunders initially has no Shiera memories); Hath-Set also reincarnates and hunts them. Does not grant immortality within a single life.

Enhanced Strength, Durability, and Healing via Nth Metal

Nth metal enhances physical strength (able to wield heavy mace, carry adults in flight), durability against falls and blows, and accelerated healing; also repels cold and regulates body temperature at altitude.

Limit: Not invulnerable; can be killed by conventional weapons (notably Hath-Set's Nth metal knife, which disrupts reincarnation); healing is not instantaneous; strength is peak-human to low superhuman, not Superman-tier.

Mastery of Ancient and Modern Weaponry

Proficient with mace, flail, spear, sword, crossbow, and later modern firearms; as Chay-Ara and subsequent lives retains combat training across incarnations. Signature weapon is Nth metal mace that can disrupt magic and affect intangible foes.

Limit: Skill is learned, not innate in each new life until memories return; Nth metal mace can be lost or broken.

Enhanced Vision and Aerial Combat Tactics

Hawk-like visual acuity and tactical sense in aerial combat; often depicted as able to spot targets from great height and coordinate with Hawkman in paired maneuvers.

Limit: Not telescopic or x-ray; still human eye enhanced by Nth metal, not true hawk vision; can be blinded or disoriented.

Curse Link to Hath-Set

Metaphysical link to Hath-Set, the priest who murdered Chay-Ara and Khufu with an Nth metal knife; ensures all three reincarnate and converge, with Hath-Set driven to kill them again each life.

Limit: Link is a vulnerability — Hath-Set can track them; knife wounds with Nth metal can prevent or corrupt reincarnation (as with Shiera's final death).

Accounts I
The Origin of Hawkman and Shiera Sanders — Flash Comics #1Flash Comics #1 (DC Comics, Jan 1940) by Gardner Fox and Dennis Neville

1940

Archaeologist Carter Hall dreams of past life as Prince Khufu of Egypt, murdered with his lover Princess Chay-Ara by the priest Hath-Set using an Nth metal knife. He discovers the same Nth metal in a tomb, builds a gravity-defying belt and wings, and becomes Hawkman. He meets Shiera Sanders, who is the reincarnation of Chay-Ara; she gradually recalls her past life and becomes his confidante and later partner. The story establishes the reincarnation curse that defines both characters. Creates the Hawkman/Hawkgirl mythos and the reincarnation engine used for 80+ years; establishes Shiera as central, not ancillary, to the curse.

Hawkgirl Dons the Mask — All-Star Comics #5All-Star Comics #5 (June 1941) by Gardner Fox and Sheldon Moldoff

1941

When Carter Hall is captured, Shiera Sanders fashions her own costume — hawk mask, wings, and Nth metal belt — and rescues him as Hawkgirl. She insists on fighting alongside him thereafter, becoming the first costumed female partner in the JSA orbit and a member of the All-Star Squadron in retroactive continuity. First canonical Hawkgirl appearance; establishes her as self-appointed hero, not sidekick; template for all later Hawkgirls (Shayera, Kendra).

The Justice Society at War — All-Star Comics #8-24 and Wartime All-Star SquadronAll-Star Comics #8 (Dec 1941) onward; retroactively All-Star Squadron #1-67 (1981-1987) by Roy Thomas

1941-1945 (stories set); 1981-1987 (retroactive expansion)

As Hawkgirl, Shiera fights Axis spies, sorcerers like Ian Karkull, and supernatural threats alongside the JSA. Roy Thomas's All-Star Squadron later fills gaps, showing Hawkgirl on missions in 1942-1944, balancing wartime duties with her relationship to Hawkman and her civilian identity as Shiera Sanders. Solidifies Hawkgirl as Golden Age team hero; provides most of her wartime feats; later used to justify JSA's post-war retirement.

Accounts II
The Hath-Set Cycle and the Nth Metal KnifeFlash Comics #1 origin expanded in Hawkman (vol.2) #1-4 (1986) by Tony Isabella and Richard Howell; JSA #10-11 (2000) by Geoff Johns & David Goyer

1986; 2000 (retelling)

Every life, Hath-Set — reincarnated as various men (e.g., Dr. Anton Hastor) — hunts Khufu/Carter and Chay-Ara/Shiera to murder them with the same Nth metal knife, perpetuating the curse. Shiera experiences vivid past-life flashbacks (Egypt, medieval England, Old West) and learns that Nth metal both enables flight and binds their souls. The cycle explains why she and Carter always find each other. Provides metaphysical backbone for reincarnation; explains Shiera's trauma and déjà vu; sets up that only destruction of Hath-Set or the knife can end cycle.

The Death of Shiera Hall and the Birth of Kendra Saunders — JSA #10-11 'The Kendra Conundrum'JSA #10-11 (2000) by Geoff Johns, David S. Goyer, Stephen Sadowski

2000

Hath-Set, as the serial killer Hasen, finally succeeds in murdering Shiera Hall with the Nth metal knife under circumstances that corrupt her reincarnation. Her soul is prevented from cleanly reincarnating; instead, her grandniece Kendra Saunders, who has committed suicide, is resurrected with Shiera's soul incomplete — Kendra becomes the new Hawkgirl but initially has no memories of Shiera, and Shiera as an individual is declared dead. Carter Hall, recently returned from the dead himself, must confront losing her. Ends Shiera's active continuity; transfers Hawkgirl mantle to Kendra Saunders (1999-); establishes that Shiera's soul is fragmented, explaining Kendra's amnesia; Shiera appears thereafter only in flashbacks, spirit visions, and Blackest Night.

Blackest Night: The Return of the Hawks — Blackest Night #1-8 and JSA #39-40Blackest Night #5-6 (2009) by Geoff Johns and Ivan Reis; JSA #39-40 (2010)

2009-2010

During the Blackest Night, Shiera and Carter are resurrected as Black Lanterns — undead revenants — and attack the JSA and Kendra. Their Black Lantern forms force Kendra and Carter to confront the unresolved curse. At the climax, they are truly resurrected as living beings by the White Light, briefly reunited, before Carter and Shiera are again killed and later reincarnated in Brightest Day. The story treats Shiera's death as reversible only by cosmic forces. Temporarily brings Shiera back to active continuity; confirms her death was not final in DC metaphysics; leads to Brightest Day reincarnation as new bodies.

Shierra/Shiera and Shayera are the same person, just different spellings. — They are distinct characters in DC canon. Shiera Sanders Hall (1940, human, Earth-Two, reincarnated Egyptian) and Shayera Hol (1961, Thanagarian alien police officer) have different origins, homeworlds, and timelines. They share the Hawkgirl/Hawkwoman mantle and hawk motif, but are not the same individual. Kendra Saunders (1999) is a third distinct Hawkgirl. The double-r 'Shierra' is most often a typo for Shiera, not Shayera.

Hawkgirl is just Hawkman's girlfriend/sidekick who got powers from him. — Shiera becomes Hawkgirl by her own choice to rescue Hawkman (All-Star Comics #5), builds/fashions her own costume, and operates independently. Early stories emphasize her agency; she is Carter's equal partner and often more pragmatic. The 'sidekick' framing is a later pop-culture simplification.

Hawkgirl's powers come from being Thanagarian or having actual hawk DNA. — For Shiera Hall, powers come from Nth metal belt/wings (technology/magic), not alien biology. She is human. Thanagarian biology applies only to Shayera Hol. No version has hawk DNA.

Shiera is immortal and cannot be killed because she always reincarnates. — She can be killed within a lifetime, and Nth metal knife wounds can corrupt or delay reincarnation. Her death in JSA #10 is treated as real and lasting — her soul does not cleanly return as Shiera, but fragments into Kendra. Reincarnation is a curse, not reliable immortality.

The Egyptian past-life story is historically accurate or based on a real myth. — Chay-Ara/Khufu/Hath-Set are invented pulp characters, not figures from Egyptian mythology. No Egyptian text attests them. The story uses Orientalist Hollywood Egypt (1930s mummy-film aesthetic), not historical Egyptology. Nth metal is fictional.
