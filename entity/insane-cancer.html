Who It Is

In the shadowy corners of folklore and the darker recesses of the human imagination, there exists a concept so terrifying it defies easy definition: the Insane Cancer. Not merely a disease, but a monstrous entity—a beast that feeds on sanity, growing and spreading through the minds of its victims. This dossier delves into the scant evidence, the whispered tales, and the modern interpretations of this chilling archetype.

The Insane Cancer is said to be intelligent, malevolent, and parasitic. It feeds on fear and sanity, growing stronger with each victim's despair. It may communicate through whispers or visions, driving its host to commit horrific acts.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'Insane Cancer' combines 'insane' (from Latin 'insanus', meaning mad or unhealthy) and 'cancer' (from Latin 'cancer', meaning crab, used for tumors). The name suggests a malignant growth that also affects the mind.

Powers
Psychic Infection

The Insane Cancer can infect minds, causing hallucinations, paranoia, and madness.

Limit: Requires physical contact or ingestion of contaminated matter.

Cellular Manipulation

It can cause rapid, uncontrolled growth of cells, leading to tumors and deformities.

Limit: Unknown; possibly limited to organic matter.

Madness Inducement

Prolonged exposure drives victims to insanity, making them violent or catatonic.

Limit: Victims with strong will may resist.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Descriptions vary: some depict a pulsating, tumorous mass with eyes and teeth; others describe an invisible force that causes physical deformities and madness in its host. Often associated with a sickly green or purple glow.

Symbols — Symbols include spirals, distorted faces, and images of cellular growth. In internet art, it is often depicted as a grotesque, organic entity.

Domains — It is said to dwell in places of decay, such as abandoned hospitals, asylums, and graveyards, but can also spread through contaminated water or air.

A patient in a mental hospital begins speaking in tongues and drawing spirals. An autopsy reveals a tumor with a face that appears to be laughing. The tumor is preserved, but those who handle it begin to show signs of madness.
