Who It Is

Vajrayogini is the supreme female deity (yidam) of the Highest Yoga Tantra class in Vajrayana Buddhism—a fully enlightened female Buddha whose practice embodies the direct realization of emptiness and bliss. Her name means 'the Diamond Female Yogi,' signifying the indestructible (vajra) nature of her awakened mind. She appears in several primary forms, the most famous being Vajravarahi (the Diamond Sow, identifiable by a small sow's head emerging from her crown) and Naro Khechari (the form transmitted by the Indian mahasiddha Naropa). Her iconography is among the most dramatic and symbolically dense in all of Buddhist art: she is depicted as a naked, sixteen-year-old dakini, fiery red or deep ruby in color, dancing in the archer's pose upon the inert bodies of Bhairava (representing ignorance) and Kalaratri (representing dualistic concepts). She wears a garland of freshly severed heads (representing the transformation of the 51 mental factors), bone ornaments (the six paramitas or perfections), and a crown of five skulls (the five Buddha wisdoms). She holds a curved flaying knife (kartika) in her right hand—the weapon that severs ego-clinging, discursive thought, and all conceptual obstructions—and a skull-cup (kapala) filled with blood in her left hand—the blood of samsara transformed into the nectar of bliss and wisdom. A khatvanga staff rests on her left shoulder. Her practice was transmitted from the mahasiddha Naropa to the Tibetan translator Marpa and became one of the central yidam practices of the Kagyu school, though all Tibetan schools practice some form of Vajrayogini. She is considered especially effective in the degenerate age (Kali Yuga) for achieving swift realization, and her practice is classified among the 'swift path' (nyen dro) methods. Vajrayogini represents the union of wisdom, bliss, power, and the fearless confrontation with all that is impure, transforming samsara and nirvana into the non-dual ground of liberation.
