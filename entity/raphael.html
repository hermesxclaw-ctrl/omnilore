Who It Is

Raphael is the archangel of healing, guidance, and protection — the divine physician whose name means 'God heals.' Unlike Michael and Gabriel, Raphael does not appear in the canonical Hebrew Bible but is a central figure in the deuterocanonical Book of Tobit, one of the most charming and narrative-rich angelic stories in scripture. The Book of Tobit recounts how the pious but blinded Tobit and the tormented Sarah both pray for death, and God sends Raphael — in human disguise as 'Azarias' — to heal them both. Raphael guides Tobit's son Tobias on a journey to collect a debt, teaches him to use fish organs for healing (the gall to cure blindness, the heart and liver to drive away the demon Asmodeus), arranges Tobias's marriage to Sarah, and ultimately reveals his true identity: 'I am Raphael, one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord.' His departure, ascending into heaven, leaves both families in awe. This narrative established Raphael as the patron of travelers, the blind, the sick, and young people seeking marriage partners. In the Book of Enoch, Raphael is one of the four archangels alongside Michael, Gabriel, and Uriel; he binds the fallen angel Azazel hand and foot and casts him into darkness as punishment for corrupting humanity. Catholic and Orthodox traditions venerate Raphael as the patron of healing, happy meetings, and travelers; his feast day (September 29) is shared with Michael and Gabriel. In Islamic tradition, Israfil is sometimes identified with Raphael, though Israfil's primary function is blowing the trumpet on Judgment Day. Raphael's iconography typically shows him with a staff (pilgrim's), a fish (from the Tobit story), and a small vessel of healing ointment. Compared to Michael's warrior aspect and Gabriel's messenger role, Raphael embodies a gentler, more intimately involved angelic presence — the divine hand that guides, heals, and restores without fanfare.
