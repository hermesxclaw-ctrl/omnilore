Who It Is

When the sun rose over the Maya world, it was not a ball of fire but a living lord—K'inich Ahau, the Sun-Facing Lord, whose burning eye marked the passage of time, the ascent of kings, and the fate of the world itself. From the stucco masks of ancient pyramids to the whispered stories of twentieth-century Lacandon Maya, this deity has endured as a force of both life and destruction, a cosmic king whose daily journey through the underworld echoed the cycle of human existence.

As a solar deity, he follows a daily cycle: rising in the east, crossing the sky, descending into the underworld, and being reborn. He is a patron of kings and is associated with warfare, as seen in year-bearer rituals. Among the Lacandon, he is an elder brother of the upper god and will bring about the end of the world.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name contains the element kʼinich, usually interpreted as 'sun-eyed' or 'sun-facing', derived from k'in, meaning 'sun' or 'day'. The term ahau or ajaw means 'lord', yielding 'Sun-Facing Lord'.

Pronunciation — [kʼiː.nitʃ a'haw] (Yucatec Maya)

Aliases — God G (in codices), K'inich Ajaw, Kinich Ahau, Sun-Facing Lord, Sun-Eyed Lord

Ownership — Maya civilization (Classic, Postclassic, and modern Lacandon Maya)

Solar Journey

The sun god travels across the sky from east to west, then through the underworld at night, to be reborn each morning.

Limit: He must traverse the underworld, where he may be threatened by underworld deities.

Patronage of Kings

The Maya king is often assimilated to the sun god, suggesting apotheosis and life after death.

Limit: This is a symbolic association, not a direct power.

Year-Bearer Patron

K'inich Ahau is the patron of one of the four years in the 52-year cycle, with associated war dances.

Limit: Specific rituals are not well-documented.

End-of-World Destruction

According to the Lacandon, K'inich Ahau will descend from the sky and have his jaguars devour mankind, ending the world.

Limit: This is an eschatological event, not a personal power.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Classic period, God G (K'inich Ahau) is depicted as a middle-aged man with an aquiline nose, large square eyes, cross-eyed, and a filed incisor in the upper row of teeth. He often has the k'in glyph infixed on his body. In some contexts, he may be shown as a young man paddling a canoe or as a chimerical water bird.

Symbols — The k'in (sun) glyph is often infixed on his body. He is associated with the day-unit k'in, the month Yaxk'in, and the number Four. He is also shown with attributes shared with other deities, such as a large eye (like Itzamna, Chaak, and jaguar gods).

Domains — He resides in an aquatic eastern paradise, the 'Land of the Sunrise'. He also travels through the underworld during the night.

Accounts I
The Sun God's Daily JourneyClassic Maya iconography and hieroglyphic texts

c. 250-900 CE

The sun god travels across the sky, then descends into the underworld, where he may take the form of the Jaguar God of the Underworld (Night Sun). He is reborn each dawn. The daily cycle of day and night.

The Rabbit and the Theft of God L's RegaliaClassic Maya ceramics (e.g., the 'Rabbit Vase' from Naranjo)

c. 250-900 CE

A scene shows the lunar rabbit hiding behind K'inich Ahau, in a myth involving the theft of the regalia of God L, an underworld deity. Usually, the rabbit is associated with the Moon Goddess, but here it is with the sun god, suggesting a relationship. The myth explains the rabbit's presence in the moon and its connection to solar deities.

The Sun God as Ancestral KingClassic Maya monuments (e.g., lintels, stelae)

c. 250-900 CE

The sun god is shown as a king seated on a throne or carrying the ceremonial bar. Maya kings are assimilated to the sun god, suggesting royal apotheosis. The divine legitimacy of Maya rulership.

The End of the WorldLacandon oral tradition (Boremanse 1986)

20th century

K'inich Ahau, elder brother of the upper god, will put an end to the world by descending from the sky and having his jaguars devour mankind. The destruction of the world.
