Who It Is

Cthulhu is a cosmic entity and Great Old One created by H.P. Lovecraft. A gigantic hybrid of octopus, dragon, and human, he lies dreaming in the sunken city of R'lyeh. His cult worships him across the world, and he is the central figure of Lovecraft's 1928 story "The Call of Cthulhu," which launched the Cthulhu Mythos.

Cthulhu is a cosmic entity created by H.P. Lovecraft, first appearing in the 1928 short story "The Call of Cthulhu." He is one of the Great Old Ones, a race of immensely powerful alien deities that predate humanity. Cthulhu is described as a gigantic, hybrid creature with an octopus-like head, a massive scaly body, claws, wings, and tentacles. He lies dead but dreaming in the sunken city of R'lyeh in the Pacific Ocean, awaiting the stars to align so he can rise and reclaim the Earth. His cult, spread across the world, worships him as a god and chants his name: "Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn." Cthulhu represents Lovecraft's theme of cosmic insignificance: humanity is a tiny, ephemeral species in a universe ruled by uncaring, incomprehensible gods.

Emergence

Cthulhu is one of the Great Old Ones, cosmic beings who came to Earth from the stars in prehistoric times. He and his kin ruled the Earth when the stars were right, building the sunken city of R'lyeh. When the stars shifted, they fell into a death-like sleep, buried beneath the Pacific. Cthulhu's cult spread across the world in ancient times, with priests in every civilization preparing for his return. The 1925 earthquake raised R'lyeh from the depths, though it remains inaccessible to most. Cthulhu waits, dead but dreaming, for the stars to align once more.

Form

Cthulhu is described as a gigantic hybrid creature, roughly humanoid but of immense scale. His head is octopus-like, with a mass of feelers or tentacles. His body is vast, scaly, and winged—great clawed wings folded on his back. His lower body is a thick, grotesque trunk that tapers into a tail. He has tentacles around his mouth instead of a jaw. He is so large that he towers over humans like a mountain. Artists typically depict him as a greenish, slimy, tentacled monster with a vaguely human torso, dragon-like wings, and octopus head. The exact proportions are deliberately left vague by Lovecraft, emphasizing the incomprehensibility of the entity.

Powers

Cthulhu possesses immense physical strength, telepathy (can communicate with sensitive humans through dreams), psychic domination (can inspire madness and worship in humans), immortality (exists in a state between life and death, cannot be killed by conventional means), and reality-warping (his presence warps natural laws). He is connected to the other Great Old Ones and can presumably call upon their aid. His cultists believe he is immortal and indestructible. When the stars are right, he can physically rise from R'lyeh and reclaim the Earth. His psychic influence extends across the globe, allowing him to communicate with worshippers and drive others mad.

Behavior & Habitat

Cthulhu is currently in a state of death-like sleep or hibernation. His cultists worship him through human and animal sacrifice, chanting the phrase that means "In his house at R'lyeh dead Cthulhu waits dreaming." When he rises, he is expected to slaughter humanity and establish his dominion over Earth. He is not a character with emotions or motivations humans can understand; he is a force of nature, a cosmic leveler. His worshipers are driven mad by dreams of him and his octopus-like visage. He does not negotiate or communicate in any comprehensible way.

Weaknesses & Wards

Cthulhu is currently in a death-like sleep and cannot move until the stars are right (i.e., astronomical alignments align with the configuration when he originally came to Earth). He can be temporarily driven back by physical force, but cannot be permanently destroyed. The Elder Sign and R'lyehian magical barriers can keep him at bay. His cultists are vulnerable to ordinary weapons and investigation.

Accounts

The central narrative in which Cthulhu is discovered. After his great-granduncle's death, Francis Wayland Thurston discovers a bas-relief depicting Cthulhu and learns of a worldwide cult worshipping the entity. Inspector Legrasse of the New Orleans police raided a cult in the Louisiana swamps in 1908, capturing a statuette of Cthulhu. Norwegian sailor Gustaf Johansen discovered R'lyeh in 1925 when his ship was blown off course; his crew found the sunken city, and his surviving mate later died after being attacked by a "chosen" cultist. Johansen returned to R'lyeh with a second ship, rammed Cthulhu with his bowsprit, and was driven mad by the encounter. Cthulhu regenerated and retreated into the city. The story concludes that humanity is "peaceful and prosperous" only because Cthulhu sleeps.

The central event awaited by Cthulhu's cult: when the stars are right, the sunken city of R'lyeh will rise from the Pacific, and Cthulhu will awaken to reclaim Earth. The 1925 earthquake raised R'lyeh but it remains inaccessible, surrounded by mirages and magical barriers. The cult believes that when the astronomical configuration matches the pre-human era, Cthulhu will rise, the seas will boil, and humanity will be destroyed or enslaved. This apocalyptic scenario is the central eschatology of the Cthulhu Mythos.

Cthulhu's cult is depicted as the oldest and most widespread religion on Earth, with cells in every continent and ancient roots. The cult worships Cthulhu, the star-spawn, and the Great Old Ones. Members engage in human sacrifice, cannibalism, and ritual cannibalism. They chant the sacred phrase and await the stars' alignment. The cult is led by the "Grandfather" or "High Priest" who claims direct dream-communication with Cthulhu. Cultists include degenerate humans, half-fish hybrids (Deep Ones), and other monstrous races. The cult's spread across time and space makes it the most enduring conspiracy in horror literature.

Cthulhu is associated with the Deep Ones, an amphibious race that worships him and his father Dagon. The Deep Ones interbreed with humans, producing hybrid descendants who eventually transform into Deep Ones. Innsmouth, Massachusetts, is the site of a Deep One colony and cult. This connection to marine biology and hybridity expands Cthulhu's mythology beyond the single story of "The Call of Cthulhu."

Cultural Significance

Cthulhu is the most iconic figure of the Cthulhu Mythos, one of the most influential shared fictional universes in literature. He represents Lovecraft's core theme of cosmic horror: the universe is vast, uncaring, and ruled by entities whose motives are beyond human comprehension. The character has become a cultural phenomenon, spawning countless adaptations, games, films, and internet memes. He is a central figure in horror fandom, goth culture, and tabletop gaming. The Cthulhu Mythos is now considered one of the foundational texts of modern horror.

Modern Interpretations

Cthulhu has appeared in films (e.g., "The Call of Cthulhu" 2005 silent film, "Cthulhu" 2007), games (Call of Cthulhu RPG, Arkham Horror), comics (Marvel, IDW), anime, music, and internet culture. He is a popular meme and icon. August Derleth expanded the Mythos, giving Cthulhu a "good vs evil" structure that Lovecraft did not intend. The character is now in the public domain and has been reimagined in countless ways, from comedic to terrifying.

Content Notes

When Gustaf Johansen and his crew discovered R'lyeh, Cthulhu killed all but Johansen and one mate. The mate later died from injuries and exposure after escaping the city.

Lovecraft, "The Call of Cthulhu"

Cthulhu's cult is implicated in human sacrifice, cannibalism, and mass murder across all continents. The 1925 riots in New York and other cities are implied to have been cult-inspired.

Lovecraft, "The Call of Cthulhu"

The 1925 earthquake that raised R'lyeh from the Pacific was caused by astronomical alignments that also signal Cthulhu's impending awakening. The event killed thousands and devastated Pacific islands.

Scholarly Debates
Cthulhu as individual vs. species
Cthulhu is a unique individual, the high priest of the Great Old Ones. ⇌ Cthulhu may be a species or a title, with multiple Cthulhu-like entities existing.
Evidence for A: Lovecraft refers to "Cthulhu" as a singular being, the "great priest of the Old Ones."
Evidence for B: Some expansions suggest there are many Star-Spawn of Cthulhu, and the name may refer to an office or lineage.
Cthulhu's parentage
Cthulhu is the offspring of Yog-Sothoth and Shub-Niggurath (August Derleth's cosmology). ⇌ Cthulhu is the offspring of Nug and Yeb, or simply an independent entity.
Evidence for A: Derleth's cosmology made Yog-Sothoth and Shub-Niggurath the parents of Cthulhu.
Evidence for B: Lovecraft's original text gives no parentage; Derleth's cosmology was added later.
What We Don't Know

Lovecraft's original text provides almost no biological or cosmological detail about Cthulhu beyond what is in "The Call of Cthulhu." The expanded Mythos, especially Derleth's additions, created elaborate genealogies and cosmologies that Lovecraft never intended. The exact astronomical configuration that will allow Cthulhu to rise is not described in detail.
