Who It Is

Xochiquetzal ('Precious Flower' or 'Flower Quetzal Feather') is the goddess of flowers, beauty, love, pregnancy, and the feminine arts — one of the most lyrical and beloved figures of the Aztec pantheon. She is the patroness of painters, weavers, embroiderers, and all makers of fine objects, as well as the protectress of prostitutes and lovers. In myth, she was originally the wife of the rain-god Tlaloc, but the trickster-god Tezcatlipoca abducted her, making her his consort — a myth of the transition from spring rains to summer drought. Xochiquetzal presides over the domestic garden and the 'flower-world' (xochitlalpan), the paradise where those who died in childbirth or in war go, depicted in Aztec poetry as a realm of song, butterflies, and unfading blossoms. Her festival, Xochilhuitl, involved offerings of flowers, the wearing of flower-crowns, and the sacrifice of a woman embodying the goddess.
