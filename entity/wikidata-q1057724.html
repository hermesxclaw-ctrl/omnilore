Who It Is

In the shadow of Balor of the Evil Eye, there stood a prophetess whose warnings were as sharp as her javelin—Cethlenn of the Crooked Teeth, a Fomorian queen who foresaw her people's doom and fought to the last.

She is portrayed as a prophetess and a warrior. She actively participates in battle, wounding the Dagda. Her prophecy to Balor shows her as a voice of warning.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Cethlenn is of uncertain etymology. It appears in Old Irish as Cethlenn, Cethleann, Ceithlenn, or Ceithlionn. The nickname 'Chaisfhiaclach' means 'crooked-toothed' from cas 'twisted' and fhiaclach 'toothed'. The variant 'Chraos-Fhiaclach' may mean 'ravening tooth' or 'gaping tooth'.

Pronunciation — Modern Irish Ceithlinn is pronounced approximately 'Kehlen'.

Aliases — Caitlín, Cethleann, Ceithlenn, Ceithlionn, Cethlenn of the Crooked Teeth, Ceithlion Chaisfhiaclach

Ownership — Irish mythology, specifically the Fomorian tradition.

Names are cult layers, not one fixed label.

Javelin throwing

She wounded the Dagda with a javelin, causing a wound that took 120 years to kill him.

Limit: The wound was not immediately fatal.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Cethlenn is described as having crooked or twisted teeth, earning her the nickname 'of the Crooked Teeth'. No other physical description is given in the sources.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography are associated with Cethlenn in the sources.

Domains — As a Fomorian, she is associated with the sea and the otherworldly realm of the Fomorians. She is also linked to the area around Enniskillen in local legend.

Accounts I
The Second Battle of Mag TuiredLebor Gabála Érenn

Mythological

Cethlenn fought against the Tuatha Dé Danann and wounded the Dagda with a javelin. The wound eventually caused his death after 120 years. The Dagda's death was delayed, but the Fomorians were defeated.

Accounts II
The Prophecy of Lug's ArrivalOidheadh Chloinne Tuireann

Early modern

Cethlenn identifies Lug as her grandson and warns Balor that the Fomorians' days are numbered once Lug comes to Ireland. The prophecy is fulfilled when Lug kills Balor.

The Legend of EnniskillenLocal tradition, Annals of Clonmacnoise

Early modern to modern

According to local lore, Cethlenn was injured and swam to Enniskillen (Innis Cethlenn) where she died. The town is said to be named after her. The naming of Enniskillen and a prophecy about the island flooding.
