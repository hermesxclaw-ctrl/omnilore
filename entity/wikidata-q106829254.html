The Approach

In the sun-scorched expanses of Kutch, where the desert meets the sea, a goddess rides a camel, her four arms bearing sword, trident, lotus, and armor. She is Momai, the fierce protector of nomadic herders and warrior clans, whose presence is as vital as the monsoon rains they pray for.

Identity

Momai

Pronunciation Mo-mai

Known As

Momai MataDashamaMammai

Etymology

The name 'Momai' is of Gujarati origin, but its precise etymology is not documented. 'Mata' means 'mother' in Gujarati and Hindi. 'Dashama' possibly relates to the Dashama Vrata, a ten-day festival associated with her.

Cultural Ownership

Gujarat, India, particularly the Kutch region; associated with the Rabari tribe and various Kshatriya clans.

Disputed Identifications

Momai is also known as Dashama, but the relationship between these names is not fully clarified. She is closely associated with Goddess Ashapura, but the exact nature of this association is undefined.

Description

Appearance

Depicted as mounted on a camel, with four hands. Upper right holds a sword, upper left a trident, lower right a lotus, lower left armor.

Symbols & Iconography

Camel mount; sword, trident, lotus, armor in four hands. These symbolize martial prowess, protection, purity, and defense.

Behaviour & Personality

Not described in the source.

Domains & Habitats

Goddess of the desert region of Kutch; her primary temple is at Momai Mora near Rapar in Kutch.
