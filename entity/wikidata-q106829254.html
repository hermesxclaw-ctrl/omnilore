Who It Is

In the sun-scorched expanses of Kutch, where the desert meets the sea, a goddess rides a camel, her four arms bearing sword, trident, lotus, and armor. She is Momai, the fierce protector of nomadic herders and warrior clans, whose presence is as vital as the monsoon rains they pray for.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Momai' is of Gujarati origin, but its precise etymology is not documented. 'Mata' means 'mother' in Gujarati and Hindi. 'Dashama' possibly relates to the Dashama Vrata, a ten-day festival associated with her.

Pronunciation — Mo-mai

Aliases — Momai Mata, Dashama, Mammai

Ownership — Gujarat, India, particularly the Kutch region; associated with the Rabari tribe and various Kshatriya clans.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Depicted as mounted on a camel, with four hands. Upper right holds a sword, upper left a trident, lower right a lotus, lower left armor.

Symbols — Camel mount; sword, trident, lotus, armor in four hands. These symbolize martial prowess, protection, purity, and defense.

Domains — Goddess of the desert region of Kutch; her primary temple is at Momai Mora near Rapar in Kutch.
