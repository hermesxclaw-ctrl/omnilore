The Approach

The surname Luong, a Vietnamese variant of the ancient Chinese Liang, carries a legacy that spans from the battlefields of the Zhou dynasty to the halls of modern academia and politics. Its bearers have shaped nations, from a Vietnamese mathematician to a Hong Kong chief executive, yet its earliest origins lie in the feudal divisions of a king's reward. This is the story of a name that has crossed borders and cultures, evolving with each pronunciation and adaptation.

Identity

Luong

Pronunciation In Vietnamese, it is pronounced as 'Lương' with a rising tone on the 'ương' part. In Mandarin, it is 'Liáng' (second tone).

Known As

LươngLiangLeungLeongNeoNioLiongYangRyang

Etymology

The Vietnamese surname Luong (Lương) is derived from the Chinese surname Liang (梁), which originally referred to a bridge or a beam. The character 梁 also denotes a state during the Zhou dynasty.

Cultural Ownership

The surname originates from Chinese culture, with significant diaspora presence in Vietnam, Hong Kong, Macau, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, and the Philippines. It is also found in Korea as Yang or Ryang.

Disputed Identifications

There are no major disputed identifications for the surname itself, but the various romanizations (Leung, Leong, Neo, etc.) are often considered distinct surnames by outsiders, though they share the same Chinese character.

Stories & Myths

Origin of the Liang surname

Source Historical recordsCulture ChinesePeriod Zhou dynasty (827–782 BC)

Summary

During the reign of King Xuan of Zhou, Qin Zhong led an expedition to subdue western peoples. After his death, the king divided the area of Shang among his sons. The second son received the area around Liangshan County, and his descendants adopted the surname Liang.
