Who It Is

In the beginning, from the very body of the creator god Sanamahi, a divine horse burst forth—not to serve, but to challenge the serpent lord Pakhangba. This is Samaton, the winged steed of Meitei mythology, whose hoofbeats echo through the rituals of the Lai Haraoba and whose lineage is said to run in the veins of every Manipuri pony. Here is his story, as told in ancient chronicles and sung in living tradition.

Initially created to attack Pakhangba, Samaton is depicted as a powerful and formidable creature. Later, he becomes the mount of God Marjing, indicating a shift to a more noble and servile role. He is revered as an ancestor of the Manipuri pony, implying a protective or generative nature.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Samaton is described as a winged divine horse, though specific physical details (color, size, wing structure) are not provided in available sources. He is likely depicted as majestic and supernatural.

Symbols — Samaton is a symbol of royalty and divine power. He is associated with the Manipuri pony, and his image may be used in heraldry or royal insignia, though specific examples are not documented in this dossier.

Domains — Samaton is a celestial or divine being, associated with the cosmos in Meitei cosmology. He is not tied to a specific earthly habitat, but his descendants are the Manipuri ponies of the Manipur region.

Accounts I
Creation of SamatonLeithak Leikharol

Primordial (cosmological)

Samaton is created by the god Sanamahi from his own body to attack Pakhangba, a serpent deity. This act is part of a larger cosmological conflict. Samaton later becomes the mount of God Marjing, and his creation story establishes his divine origin and importance.

Accounts II
Samaton as Royal InsigniaMeitei tradition

Historical (during deification of Meitei kings)

Because Samaton is a divine being, he becomes associated with Meitei kings and is adopted as a symbol of royalty. Samaton becomes an insignia of royalty, reinforcing his cultural significance.
