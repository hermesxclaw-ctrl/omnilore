The Approach

In the sprawling, genre-defying universe of the manga and anime series 'Kyousougiga', Ryouko Kusakabe stands as a figure of profound paradox—a mother who is also a god, a creator who is also a destroyer, and a mortal who transcends the boundaries of life and death. Her story weaves through themes of family, identity, and the nature of reality, leaving an indelible mark on the narrative and its characters.

Content advisory. Contains themes of death, grief, family separation, and existential questions about identity and reality. Some scenes may be emotionally intense for younger viewers.
Identity

Ryouko Kusakabe

Pronunciation REE-oh-koh KOO-sah-kah-beh

Etymology

The name 'Ryouko' (涼子) can be translated as 'cool child' or 'refreshing child', possibly reflecting her composed yet mysterious nature. 'Kusakabe' (日下部) is a surname that may have historical roots in Japan.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese pop culture, specifically the 'Kyousougiga' franchise created by Rie Matsumoto and Toei Animation.

Disputed Identifications

No known disputes regarding her identity, but some fans speculate about her true nature and origins, which are intentionally ambiguous in the series.

Description

Appearance

Ryouko Kusakabe is depicted as a young woman with long, flowing dark hair and expressive eyes. She often wears a traditional Japanese kimono, sometimes with a modern twist, reflecting her connection to both the past and the present. In her more divine or 'god' form, her appearance may become more ethereal, with details that hint at her otherworldly nature.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with the 'Kyousougiga' universe's central motifs: the mirror, the labyrinthine city of Kyoto, and the concept of 'koto' (the word/heart). She is often linked to the imagery of the 'Maze' and the 'Divine' family.

Behaviour & Personality

Ryouko is portrayed as gentle, compassionate, and deeply loving, especially towards her children. She possesses a quiet strength and a sense of melancholy, as she is aware of the sacrifices her role demands. She is also shown to be playful and mischievous at times, especially in her interactions with her husband and children. Her actions are driven by a desire to protect her family and to create a world where they can be happy.

Domains & Habitats

She resides in the 'Kyoto' of the alternate dimension, a city that mirrors the real Kyoto but is imbued with magical and surreal elements. She is also connected to the concept of the 'Maze', a space that reflects the hearts of those who enter it.

Version Differences

In the ONA (original net animation) and the TV series, her role is consistent, but the TV series expands her backstory and her relationship with her children. The manga adaptation may offer slight variations in her portrayal, but the core characteristics remain the same.

Powers & Abilities

Creation and Reality Manipulation

Source Version TV series and ONA

Description

As a being connected to the divine, Ryouko has the ability to shape reality within the alternate Kyoto. She can create or alter spaces, and her will can influence the fabric of her world.

Limitations

Her powers seem tied to her emotional state and her connection to her family. When she is distant or in despair, the world she created begins to decay.

Contradictions

The exact extent of her powers is not fully explained, and it is unclear whether she is truly omnipotent within her domain or simply possesses advanced abilities.

Immortality / Resurrection

Source Version TV series

Description

Ryouko has died and been resurrected, and she appears to exist outside the normal flow of time. She can return from death, though the mechanism is not fully explained.

Limitations

Her resurrection may be tied to the will of her family or to the nature of the alternate Kyoto.

Contradictions

The series does not clarify if her immortality is inherent or a result of the 'Maze's' influence.

Empathic Connection

Source Version TV series

Description

She shares a deep emotional bond with her children, allowing them to sense her presence and intentions even across different planes of existence.

Limitations

This connection can be strained by distance and emotional turmoil.

Contradictions

None noted.

Stories & Myths

The Tale of the Kusakabe Family

Source Kyousougiga (TV series)Culture Japanese anime/mangaPeriod 2013

Summary

Ryouko is the mother of the three Kusakabe children: Koto, Yase, and Kurama. She married the eccentric priest Myoue and together they created a family in a surreal version of Kyoto. However, Ryouko's true nature as a being from another realm causes complications. She eventually disappears, leaving her children to search for her. The series follows Koto's quest to reunite her family and uncover the truth about her mother's past.

Consequences

Ryouko's actions set the stage for the central conflict of the series, as her absence drives the plot. Her eventual return and reconciliation with her family bring closure to the emotional arc.

Variants

The ONA version presents a shorter, more condensed version of the story, while the TV series expands on the backstory and character development.

Contradictions & Disputes

Ryouko's true nature

Position A

She is a divine being, a 'god' of the alternate Kyoto.

Position A — evidence

She is able to create and manipulate reality, and she is referred to as a 'god' by other characters.

Position B

She is a human who gained powers through her connection to the Maze.

Position B — evidence

She is shown to have human emotions and vulnerabilities, and her powers are not always absolute.

Scholarly Consensus

The series intentionally leaves this ambiguous, and no official source confirms either interpretation.

Ryouko's death

Position A

She died and was resurrected by the power of the Maze.

Position A — evidence

Koto finds her mother's grave and later Ryouko appears alive.

Position B

She never truly died, but was transported to another plane.

Position B — evidence

The Maze can create illusions and alternate realities, so her death may have been an illusion.

Scholarly Consensus

The series does not provide a clear explanation, and both interpretations are possible.
