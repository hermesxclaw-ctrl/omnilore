Who They Are

Viracocha is the supreme creator god of Inca mythology, the primordial being who fashioned the earth, the sky, the sun, the moon, the stars, and all living creatures. After creating the world, he walked among humanity as a wandering teacher before departing across the Pacific Ocean, promising to return. He is the most remote and abstract figure in the Inca pantheon, representing the ultimate source of all existence.

Attested in Oral Tradition / Spanish Colonial Chronicles (Garcilaso de la Vega, Cieza de León)Era Inca Empire (c. 1200-1572 CE)

Cultural Significance

Viracocha was the supreme god in the official Inca state religion, though his cult was more abstract and philosophical than the popular worship of Inti (the sun god). The Inca emperor (Sapa Inca) claimed descent from Viracocha, and the great Temple of Viracocha at Racchi (in the Cusco region) was one of the most important religious structures in the empire. When Spanish conquistadors arrived in the 16th century, some Incas initially believed that the bearded white man Viracocha had returned — a tragic misunderstanding that facilitated the Spanish conquest. Viracocha's name means 'Foam of the Sea' or 'Lake of Fat,' which may reference his origins from the water.
