Who It Is

Before the sun crossed the sky, before the first stone of Tiwanaku was laid, there was only darkness and the still waters of Lake Titicaca. From that lake rose a being of immense power, a creator who breathed life into stone and then wept at the flaws of his own creation. He was Viracocha, the Sea Foam, the Teacher of the World, who walked across the Pacific and vanished, promising to return in times of trouble. His story is a tapestry of creation, destruction, and a mysterious white god whose image has been shaped by both ancient belief and colonial imagination.

Viracocha is a creator deity, but also a teacher and a wanderer. He is depicted as compassionate, weeping for his creations, but also as a punisher, sending a flood to destroy the first, flawed creations. He is all-knowing and powerful, yet he chooses to walk among humans in disguise.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Viracocha is derived from Quechua 'wira' (fat) and 'qucha' (lake, sea, reservoir), often interpreted as 'Sea Foam' or 'Fat of the Sea'. Alternative etymologies include Aymara 'Wila Quta' (blood lake), and Max Uhle's suggestion of 'huyra' (end of all things), yielding 'lake of origin and end

Pronunciation — Wee-rah-KOH-chah (Spanish-influenced); in Quechua: Wiraqucha (wee-rah-KOO-cha).

Aliases — Wiracocha, Wiraqocha, Huiracocha, Ticsi Viracocha, Tiqsi Wiraqocha, Contiti Viracocha

Ownership — Pre-Inca and Inca cultures of the Andes, particularly the region around Lake Titicaca (modern Peru/Bolivia). The cult was central to the Inca state, though its origins are earlier.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Viracocha is described in Spanish chronicles as a man of medium height, white-skinned, and bearded, wearing a white robe secured at the waist, carrying a staff and a book. However, native accounts often do not mention these features; the 'white god' description is likely a post-conquest addition. In some legends, he appears as a beggar wandering the earth.

Symbols — Viracocha is associated with the sun, storms, and the sea. He is often depicted holding a staff, and in Tiwanaku art, the Staff God holds two staffs. He is also linked to the concept of time and creation.

Domains — He rose from Lake Titicaca, and is associated with the sea, the sky, and the earth. He is the creator of the universe, sun, moon, stars, and civilization. After creating the world, he walked across the Pacific Ocean and disappeared.

Accounts I
The Creation and the FloodJuan de Betanzos

Post-conquest recording of pre-Columbian myth

Viracocha rose from Lake Titicaca during darkness and brought light. He created the sun, moon, and stars. He made mankind by breathing into stones, but the first creation were brainless giants. He destroyed them with a flood and made better humans from smaller stones, scattering them across the world. The world was populated with humans who had the potential for civilization.

Accounts II
The Wanderings of ViracochaBetanzos, Sarmiento de Gamboa

Post-conquest recording

After creation, Viracocha wandered the earth as a beggar, teaching humans civilization and performing miracles. Many refused his teachings and fell into warfare. He wept at their plight. Eventually, he walked across the Pacific and disappeared, promising to return in times of trouble. He left but his teachings remained, and his return is anticipated.

The Founding of the Inca CivilizationSarmiento de Gamboa, other chroniclers

Post-conquest recording

After the flood, Viracocha saved Manco Capac and Mama Uqllu (sometimes his children) to repopulate the world. They founded the Inca civilization, carrying a golden staff called 'tapac-yauri'. The Inca civilization was established.
