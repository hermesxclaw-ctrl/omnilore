Who It Is

The recurring modern folklore of individuals claiming to be time travelers from the future — or discovered 'evidence' of time travelers in historical photographs and films. John Titor (2000-2001) is the most elaborate example: a figure who posted on internet forums claiming to be a U.S. soldier from 2036, describing a future civil war and providing technical specifications for his time machine (a C204 'gravity distortion unit' installed in a 1967 Chevrolet Corvette).

Cultural Significance

Time traveler folklore reflects anxieties about technological acceleration and the desire to escape present crises by 'knowing' they're part of a larger narrative arc. The 'cell phone in 1928 Chaplin film' meme demonstrates how digital culture repurposes historical artifacts as evidence for contemporary myths.
