Accounts I
The Birth of the Tournament Universe - The King of Fighters '94SNK. The King of Fighters '94 (arcade/Neo Geo, 1994) and developer prototype documentation (Survivor concept)

1994 - Orochi Saga prologue

SNK abandons the side-scrolling beat-'em-up prototype 'Survivor' (intended to star Terry Bogard and Robert Garcia) and creates a cross-over fighting game that unites Fatal Fury, Art of Fighting, Ikari Warriors, and Psycho Soldier casts under a new invitation-based 3-on-3 tournament hosted by Rugal Bernstein. The concept of three-man teams is retained from the beat-'em-up. The Kusanagi clan's pyrokinetic heritage and the Yamata no Orochi legend are established as the mythic backbone, with Kyo Kusanagi (originally Syo Kirishima) as the 'snazzy' new hero designed to fight SNK veterans. This establishes the cosmology, rules, and invitation-letter trope that later justifies every newcomer's entry, including Moe Habana's generation. Creates the persistent KOF tournament structure, sacred treasure mythology, and design philosophy ('Kyo-ify', fan-service collaboration) that governs all later character creation. Without this, Moe Habana's later inclusion has no narrative framework.

The Shun'ei Saga Prologue - Ash's Paradox and the New GenerationSNK. The King of Fighters XIV (2016) and The King of Fighters XV Official Story Prologue (2021-2022)

2016-2022 - Shun'ei Saga

Following the Tales of Ash Saga (Ash Crimson steals the sacred treasures and erases himself from history), a time-travel paradox leaves Shun'ei, a young Chinese fighter raised by Tung Fu Rue, imbued with unknown supernatural powers connected to Ash. KOF XIV introduces Shun'ei as protagonist; KOF XV continues his story as he confronts his powers and the resurgence of Orochi-related forces. SNK art director Kuroki notes Shun'ei was designed to contrast previous brooding leads, and newcomer designer Ogura creates Sylvie and other stylized fighters to diversify the cast. This generational shift explicitly opens the roster to new youth-oriented fighters with contemporary fashion and varied nationalities, avoiding stereotypes like sumo wrestlers (Oda). Moe Habana is introduced within this creative window as part of the expanded, diverse cast. Establishes the narrative and aesthetic era into which Moe Habana debuts. Her invitation to KOF is diegetically justified by the ongoing tournament that now accommodates post-Ash supernatural anomalies.
