Who It Is

In Hopi stories of the beginning, the universe does not simply appear—it is built. Out of Taiowa’s infinite mind steps Sotuknang, a named edge to the boundless, tasked with gathering water into seas, air into winds, and worlds into a ladder of moral tests. He is not a thunderous war god or a trickster coyote, but the quiet architect who burns, freezes, and floods entire worlds when their inhabitants forget why they were made. To follow Sotuknang through the Four Worlds is to watch creation itself learn, fail, and begin again.

In Hopi cosmology, Sotuknang behaves as a lawful, purposeful world-shaper. He follows Taiowa’s plan, creates worlds in harmony, and then destroys them when humans fall into moral and spiritual corruption. His personality, as inferred from narratives, is firm but not capricious: he warns humans to remember their Creator, observes their behavior, and acts decisively when balance is lost. He is neither a trickster nor a purely wrathful destroyer; rather, he enforces cosmic law and resets creation to give faithful beings another chance. Powers
Creation of nine universes/worlds

Sotuknang is tasked by Taiowa/Tawa to create nine universes or worlds according to the Creator’s plan. He organizes the elements, gathers water to form the sea, and air to form the winds, and establishes the First World (Tokpela) and subsequent worlds as stages for human spiritual development.

Limit: He acts under Taiowa’s authority and does not originate the plan; his creative power is delegated rather than absolute.

Delegation of life-creation to Spider Woman

Sotuknang creates Spider Woman (Spider Grandmother, Kokyangwuti) and grants her the power to create life. She sings the Creation Song and brings forth twins and other beings, populating the worlds according to Sotuknang’s instructions.

Limit: Spider Woman acts as intermediary; Sotuknang does not personally shape every being but oversees the process.

Destruction of corrupted worlds by fire, ice, and flood

When humans in each world forget their Creator, close the kopavi (soft spot at the crown of the head), and become materialistic, violent, or morally corrupt, Sotuknang destroys the world: the First World by fire, the Second by ice, the Third by flood. He preserves a faithful remnant by sending them to shelter with the ants or underground, then leads them into the next world.

Limit: His destructive acts are conditional on moral failure; he does not destroy arbitrarily. Faithful beings are always given a path to survival and renewal.

Moral oversight and enforcement of cosmic law

Sotuknang monitors human behavior across worlds, reminding them to remember their Creator and live in balance. He judges when corruption has become complete and initiates destruction and renewal. His power is thus both cosmological and ethical.

Limit: He operates within a framework set by Taiowa and does not appear as a personal judge in everyday human affairs; his interventions are world-scale rather than individual.

Granting languages and dispersal of peoples

In some accounts, Sotuknang gives four different languages to the first people created by Spider Woman, sending them to spread across the globe. This dispersal sets up later migrations and the eventual emergence of the Hopi in the Fourth World.

Limit: This is a mythic explanation of linguistic and cultural diversity, not a literal historical account.

Accounts I
Creation of the First World (Tokpela) and the role of SotuknangOraibi elders’ account recorded by Voth (1905) and summarized by later writers (Railsback; Grunge article).

Late 19th–early 20th century ethnographic recording of older oral tradition

Taiowa, the infinite Creator, brings forth Sotuknang, calling him his nephew and manifestation of the finite. Taiowa instructs Sotuknang to create nine universes according to his plan. Sotuknang gathers water to make the sea and air to make the winds, shaping the First World, Tokpela (Endless Space), in perfect harmony. He then creates Spider Woman, giving her the power to sing the Creation Song and bring life into being. Spider Woman fashions twins and other beings, and the first people live in balance, with kopavi (soft spots) on their heads through which they receive wisdom directly from their Creators. The First World establishes the pattern of harmony and direct spiritual connection. The creation of Spider Woman and the twins sets up the balance of the earth and the possibility of human spiritual growth.

Destruction of the First World by fire and emergence into the Second WorldEmergence myth summarized by Lockett (1933), Railsback, Nicole Lau’s blog, and Grunge article.

Ethnographic recordings late 19th–early 20th century; modern retellings 20th–21st century.

Over time, people in the First World become obsessed with material things, close their kopavi, and forget their Creator. Division and conflict arise. Seeing that the world has become corrupted, Sotuknang destroys it with fire, burning away the wickedness. He saves the faithful by sending them to shelter underground with Spider Woman and the ants. After the cleansing, Sotuknang creates the Second World, Tokpa (Dark Midnight), and leads the faithful to emerge into it. The destruction by fire marks the first major reset of creation and establishes the pattern that worlds end when humans fail to maintain balance. The faithful learn that survival depends on remembering the Creator and following guidance.

Accounts II
Destruction of the Second and Third Worlds and emergence into the Fourth WorldEmergence myth as in Lockett (1933) and modern summaries.

Ethnographic and modern retellings.

In the Second World, people develop villages, agriculture, and trade. Initially they remember the lessons of the First World, but gradually they become obsessed with possessions and hoarding. When corruption becomes complete, Sotuknang destroys the Second World with ice, freezing hearts and lands. Again, Spider Woman leads the faithful underground. In the Third World, people develop complex societies but descend into war and violence. Sotuknang destroys this world with a great flood. The survivors rise from the earth and are sent migrating across the land, eventually settling between the Colorado River and the Rio Grande, becoming the Hopi in the harsh landscape of the Fourth World, designed to remind them of their dependence on the Creator. The sequence of destructions teaches that spiritual neglect, materialism, and violence all lead to cosmic reset. The Fourth World is both a gift and a test, where humans must live in balance or risk another destruction.

Granting of languages and dispersal of peoplesOraibi creation story as summarized in Railsback and derivative articles.

Ethnographic recording; modern synthesis.

After Spider Woman creates the first people, Sotuknang gives them four different languages and sends them to spread across the world. Each group travels to different regions, carrying the memory of the Creator. Over time, many forget, but a remnant maintains the teachings and eventually becomes the Hopi after emergence into the Fourth World. This story explains the diversity of languages and cultures and frames the Hopi as one group that retained the Creator’s teachings more faithfully than others.
