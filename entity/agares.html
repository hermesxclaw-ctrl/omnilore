Who It Is

In the shadowed hierarchy of the Ars Goetia, Agares waits—a weathered old man perched on a crocodile, a hawk on his fist, commanding thirty-one legions of demons. He is the master of languages, the hunter of runaways, the shaker of the earth, and the grantor of titles. Yet beneath this medieval portrait lies a stranger, more complex history, one that shifts across grimoires and centuries, from a noble duke of the east to a servant of Lucifuge Rofocale, from a handler of treasure-guarding spirits to a sleepy student in an anime. To know Agares is to trace the evolution of demonology itself—a mirror of human fears, ambitions, and the enduring fascination with the infernal.

Agares is typically depicted as a noble and powerful duke who commands legions of demons. He is said to be willing to teach languages and grant titles, suggesting a relatively cooperative or even benevolent demeanor toward those who summon him properly. However, his ability to cause earthquakes and retrieve runaways implies a more menacing aspect, capable of forceful action.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The etymology of Agares is uncertain. Some scholars suggest a possible derivation from the Greek 'agathos' (good), but this is speculative. Others propose a connection to the Latin 'agere' (to drive), but no consensus exists.

Pronunciation — AH-gah-rees or ah-GAH-rees; variant spellings suggest alternative pronunciations such as AH-gah-rus or AH-gah-ros.

Aliases — Agarus, Agarat, Agaros, Aguares, Agreas

Form & Signs

Appearance — Agares is consistently described as an old man, but his mount varies: in the Pseudomonarchia Daemonum and Lesser Key of Solomon, he rides a crocodile and carries a hawk on his fist; in the Book of the Office of Spirits, he rides a cockatrice and does not carry a hawk; in the Livre des Esperitz, he is simply an old man with no mount mentioned. The Grimoire of Pope Honorius omits any physical description.

Symbols — The crocodile and hawk are the primary symbols associated with Agares in the most influential grimoires. The crocodile may symbolize the primordial chaos or the Nile, while the hawk is often associated with solar deities and kingship. In the Book of the Office of Spirits, the cockatrice represents a chimeric beast, perhaps indicating a different lineage of spirit.

Domains — Agares is associated with the east, as he is 'under the power of the east.' He is a terrestrial demon, often invoked in ritual contexts, particularly for treasure hunting, as seen in Sloane MS 3824. His domain includes the ability to control runaways and cause earthquakes, indicating influence over movement and the earth.
