The Approach

In the shadowed hierarchy of the Ars Goetia, Agares waits—a weathered old man perched on a crocodile, a hawk on his fist, commanding thirty-one legions of demons. He is the master of languages, the hunter of runaways, the shaker of the earth, and the grantor of titles. Yet beneath this medieval portrait lies a stranger, more complex history, one that shifts across grimoires and centuries, from a noble duke of the east to a servant of Lucifuge Rofocale, from a handler of treasure-guarding spirits to a sleepy student in an anime. To know Agares is to trace the evolution of demonology itself—a mirror of human fears, ambitions, and the enduring fascination with the infernal.

Etymology

The etymology of Agares is uncertain. Some scholars suggest a possible derivation from the Greek 'agathos' (good), but this is speculative. Others propose a connection to the Latin 'agere' (to drive), but no consensus exists.

Cultural Ownership

Agares belongs to the Western esoteric tradition, specifically the demonology of medieval and early modern grimoires. He is not a figure from ancient mythology but a product of Christian demonology, shaped by the synthesis of Solomonic lore, Kabbalistic angelology, and folk beliefs. His cultural ownership is thus primarily European, with later global dissemination through occult literature and popular culture.

Disputed Identifications

Some authors have conflated Agares with other demons, such as Agreas or Aguares, but these are likely orthographic variants rather than distinct entities. There is no evidence linking Agares to any pre-Christian deity, despite occasional speculative claims. His identification as the second spirit in the Ars Goetia is consistent across major grimoires, but his attributes vary.

Description

Appearance

Agares is consistently described as an old man, but his mount varies: in the Pseudomonarchia Daemonum and Lesser Key of Solomon, he rides a crocodile and carries a hawk on his fist; in the Book of the Office of Spirits, he rides a cockatrice and does not carry a hawk; in the Livre des Esperitz, he is simply an old man with no mount mentioned. The Grimoire of Pope Honorius omits any physical description.

Symbols & Iconography

The crocodile and hawk are the primary symbols associated with Agares in the most influential grimoires. The crocodile may symbolize the primordial chaos or the Nile, while the hawk is often associated with solar deities and kingship. In the Book of the Office of Spirits, the cockatrice represents a chimeric beast, perhaps indicating a different lineage of spirit.

Behaviour & Personality

Agares is typically depicted as a noble and powerful duke who commands legions of demons. He is said to be willing to teach languages and grant titles, suggesting a relatively cooperative or even benevolent demeanor toward those who summon him properly. However, his ability to cause earthquakes and retrieve runaways implies a more menacing aspect, capable of forceful action.

Domains & Habitats

Agares is associated with the east, as he is 'under the power of the east.' He is a terrestrial demon, often invoked in ritual contexts, particularly for treasure hunting, as seen in Sloane MS 3824. His domain includes the ability to control runaways and cause earthquakes, indicating influence over movement and the earth.

Version Differences

The primary differences across versions include the number of legions (31 in Lemegeton and Pseudomonarchia, 36 in Livre des Esperitz), the mount (crocodile vs. cockatrice vs. none), the presence of the hawk, and the specific functions emphasized. The Grimoire of Pope Honorius omits most functions, while the Grand Grimoire subordinates him to Lucifuge Rofocale. The Book of the Office of Spirits places him under Oriens, whereas other grimoires list him as second in the hierarchy.

Demon Possession

Source The Crucifixion (2017); news reports on the Tanacu exorcism

Description

In the 2017 film The Crucifixion, Agares is depicted as possessing a nun, leading to her death. This is a dramatization of the Tanacu exorcism case, which involved the death of Sister Maricica Irina Cornici.

Scholarly Context

The Tanacu exorcism was a real event in 2005 in Romania, where a nun died during an exorcism performed by a priest. The case raised questions about religious extremism and mental health.
