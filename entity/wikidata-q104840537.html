Who It Is

In Vietnam, the zodiac is not merely a calendar but a living tapestry of folklore, where the celestial rabbit yields to the cat and the sheep gives way to the goat, reflecting a culture that has reimagined the heavens in its own image. This is the story of a system that has shaped festivals, marriages, and daily life for millennia, and yet remains little known beyond its borders.

The zodiac is not a sentient entity but a cultural construct. It influences personality traits attributed to individuals born in each year, and it guides auspicious activities such as marriages, business openings, and travel.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'Vietnamese zodiac' derives from the French 'zodiaque vietnamien', but in Vietnamese it is 'mười hai con giáp' (twelve earthly branches) or simply 'con giáp' (earthly branches). The word 'giáp' refers to the stems and branches system of the calendar.

Pronunciation — mɨəj haːi kɔn zaːp (approximate)

Aliases — Tử vi, Lịch âm, Mười hai con giáp

Ownership — Vietnam, with roots in the broader East Asian cultural sphere, particularly Chinese calendrical systems.

Astrological influence

Each zodiac sign is believed to influence the personality, fortune, and compatibility of individuals born under it.

Limit: Belief in astrological influence is not universal; it is considered cultural rather than scientifically proven.

Calendrical timekeeping

The zodiac is used to mark years in the lunar calendar, which determines the dates of Tết and other festivals.

Limit: The lunar calendar is no longer the official calendar, but it remains culturally significant.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Vietnamese zodiac is typically represented as a cycle of twelve animals, each associated with a year. In art, these animals are depicted in various styles, from traditional woodblock prints to modern illustrations. The animals are: Rat, Water Buffalo, Tiger, Cat, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig.

Symbols — Each animal has symbolic meanings: Rat (cleverness), Buffalo (diligence), Tiger (courage), Cat (gentleness), Dragon (power), Snake (wisdom), Horse (vitality), Goat (creativity), Monkey (intelligence), Rooster (confidence), Dog (loyalty), Pig (prosperity). These symbols appear in statues, paintings, and festive decorations.

Domains — The zodiac permeates Vietnamese life: it is used in the lunar calendar, Tết (New Year) celebrations, and astrology. It is also present in literature, art, and everyday language.

Accounts I
The Legend of the Jade Emperor's RaceVietnamese folk tradition

Unknown (mythological)

The Jade Emperor invited all animals to a race to determine the order of the zodiac. The rat won by riding on the buffalo's back and jumping off at the finish line. The cat, who had been tricked by the rat into missing the race, was replaced by the rabbit in the Chinese version, but in Vietnam, the cat was included, and the rabbit was omitted. The order of the zodiac was established, and each animal was granted a year of influence.

Accounts II
The Cat's RevengeVietnamese folk tale

Unknown

After the race, the cat swore revenge on the rat, explaining why cats chase rats. This tale is used to explain the natural enmity between the two animals. Cats and rats are enemies in Vietnamese folklore.

Cat vs. RabbitThe cat replaced the rabbit because the Vietnamese word for rabbit (mão) sounds like 'cat' (mèo).The cat was already part of the native Vietnamese zodiac before Chinese influence.
Goat vs. SheepThe Vietnamese zodiac uses the goat because the Chinese character for 'sheep' (羊) is ambiguous and can mean both sheep and goat.The Vietnamese deliberately chose the goat because it is more common in Vietnam than sheep.

The Vietnamese zodiac is identical to the Chinese zodiac. — It differs in two animals: cat instead of rabbit, and goat instead of sheep.

The zodiac is a form of fortune-telling with predictive power. — It is a cultural tradition, not a scientifically validated system.
