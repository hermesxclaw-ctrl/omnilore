Who It Is

Mawu-Lisa is the supreme dual creator deity of the Fon people of Dahomey (modern Benin) — two deities in one, representing the inseparable creative union of female and male principles. Mawu, the female aspect, is associated with the moon, night, coolness, wisdom, and mercy. Lisa, the male aspect, is associated with the sun, day, heat, strength, and justice. Together (or alternately, in some versions), they created the universe and all the lesser vodun (deities). Mawu is said to be the eldest and most powerful, and in some traditions she created Lisa as her male counterpart to bring balance. Their children include the vodun Dan (the rainbow serpent), Gu (the god of iron and war), and various other deities who manage specific domains. Mawu-Lisa's dual-gendered nature is not considered a contradiction but the highest expression of completeness — only the union of opposites can create and sustain the cosmos. During the slave trade, the Fon people's vodun traditions were transported to Haiti, where they merged with other African traditions and Catholic elements to form Haitian Vodou. Mawu-Lisa's dual nature is one of the most sophisticated theological expressions of non-binary divinity in world religion, predating and independently paralleling concepts found in Hindu Ardhanarishvara.
