Who It Is

Before there were Shards, before Honor was Honor and Ruin was Ruin, there was a single, impossibly vast power that held all Investiture in the Cosmere — and then sixteen people decided to kill it. Adonalsium is Brandon Sanderson's murdered god, a being whose death explains everything and whose life is almost entirely unknown.

Attested: Adonalsium is described by Hoid and Frost as having been opposed to its own Shattering, implying volition. Beyond that, personality is disputed/unknown. Hoid's letter (Way of Kings epigraphs) implies Adonalsium was a known entity to Yolen scholars, possibly a guiding or protective presence. WoB: Sanderson has deliberately refused to answer whether Adonalsium was sentient in the human sense, stating 'RAFO' (Read And Find Out) and that revealing its intent would spoil the eventual Dragonsteel novels. Powers
Infinite Investiture / Source of All Investiture

In Cosmere metaphysics, Investiture is a fundamental force. Adonalsium held effectively all Investiture in the Cosmere prior to the Shattering. This manifested as ability to create worlds, invest planets with magic systems, and sustain Realmatic balance. Post-Shattering, this power is divided among 16 Shards, each infinite within its Intent but finite relative to the whole.

Limit: Attested limitation: Adonalsium could be killed / Shattered by concerted action of mortals wielding specific tools (a weapon / method still unrevealed in text, RAFO). This implies not omnipotent or invulnerable. No other limits described; speculation about inability to act against its nature is extrapolated from Shardic Intent, not confirmed for Adonalsium.

Creation and World-Forging

Associated with creation of the Cosmere's planets and the establishment of its physical and Cognitive Realm structure. Khriss notes that many Shardworlds existed before the Shattering (Yolen, Roshar), implying Adonalsium either created them or invested them heavily. Exact creative acts are unattested.

Limit: No direct creation narrative survives in published text. Whether Adonalsium created the universe ex nihilo or shaped a pre-existing cosmos is unknown.

Sentience and Intent (Disputed)

If sentient, Adonalsium would have possessed an Intent (like Shards have Honor, Odium, Preservation). The nature of that Intent is unknown and heavily theorized (Unity, Perfection, Change). WoB: Sanderson has hinted its Intent was broader than any single Shard.

Limit: Intent would constrain action, as it does for Shards (e.g., Ruin must ruin). If Adonalsium had an Intent, it is not named in text. This is hypothetical until Dragonsteel publication.

Posthumous Influence - Echoes and Splinters

Even after death, Adonalsium's power persists as Shards, Splinters (e.g., spren on Roshar, Seons/Skaze on Sel, divine Breaths on Nalthis), and the Dor. Characters sometimes access 'tones' or powers that predate Shards, suggesting residual Adonalsium Investiture.

Limit: Residual power is fragmented and filtered through Shardic Intent; no character can access 'pure' Adonalsium Investiture directly in published text.

Accounts I
The Shattering of AdonalsiumThe Way of Kings, Words of Radiance, Oathbringer epigraphs (Letters between Hoid and Frost); Arcanum Unbounded; WoB compilation

In-world: >10,000 years before Stormlight Archive (c. 10,000-7,000 years pre-canon); Publication: 2010-2017 reveals

The central narrative of Adonalsium: sixteen individuals from the planet Yolen, including future Shard Vessels (Ati, Leras, Rayse, Tanavast, Koravellium, Edgli, etc.) and witnesses Hoid and Frost, conspired to kill and Shatter Adonalsium. Using an unknown weapon/method (Sanderson has said he knows what it was but will not reveal until Dragonsteel), they succeeded. Adonalsium was killed and its infinite power divided into sixteen Shards, each embodying an aspect/Intent of the whole (Honor, Odium, Ruin, Preservation, Cultivation, Devotion, Dominion, etc.). Hoid was offered a Shard and refused. Frost also declined. The act was described by Hoid as a murder and by others as necessary. The Shards then dispersed across the Cosmere. Creation of the Shardic system that drives all Cosmere plots; establishment of 16 magic systems; millennia of Shardic conflicts (Odium's rampage, Ruin vs Preservation, Devotion/Dominion's Splintering). The Shattering is the Cosmere's foundational trauma and the ultimate mystery for future novels.

Adonalsium on Yolen - The Pre-Shattering WorldWoB; Arcanum Unbounded (Yolen references); Dragonsteel Prime summary via WoB

Pre-Shattering era; publication via WoB 2012-2024

Before the Shattering, Adonalsium was centered on Yolen, the oldest human planet. Yolen humans had early access to Investiture (Lightweaving, etc.) under Adonalsium. Dragons (like Frost) and other pre-human species coexisted. Adonalsium's presence made Yolen highly Invested, allowing proto-magic. The planet's scholars, including Hoid's original people, studied Adonalsium. This period is almost entirely withheld from published novels; only fragments appear in Hoid's recollections and Khriss's essays. Sanderson has stated Yolen will be the setting for the Dragonsteel sequence, which will depict Adonalsium directly for the first time. Established the cultural and magical baseline that led to the Shattering conspiracy; explains why Yolen humans became cosmere-aware worldhoppers.

Echoes on Roshar - The Almighty and the VisionsThe Way of Kings (2010) Ch. 19, 57; Words of Radiance Interludes; Oathbringer Ch. 38-57

In-world: 2010-2017 publication; in-story c. 1173-1174 Roshar

On Roshar, the Shard Honor (Tanavast) posed as 'the Almighty' to the Vorin peoples. After Honor's death (Splintering by Odium), Dalinar Kholin receives visions from Honor's Cognitive Shadow. Characters repeatedly ask whether the Almighty/God Beyond is Adonalsium. Hoid's letter to Frost explicitly distinguishes Adonalsium from the God Beyond, suggesting Adonalsium is dead and not answering prayers. This story shows how memory of Adonalsium has been lost and replaced by Shard-worship. Demonstrates post-Shattering religious distortion; clarifies that Roshar's religion is based on a Shard, not Adonalsium; sets up Wind and Truth's exploration of Honor's remnants.

Accounts II
The Splintering on Sel - Devotion and DominionElantris (2005); Elantris - Postscript (2006); Arcanum Unbounded - Sel essay (2016)

In-world: centuries before Stormlight; Publication 2005-2016

After the Shattering, two Shards — Devotion (Aona) and Dominion (Skai) — settled on Sel and created humans there. Later, Odium (Rayse) arrived, killed both Vessels, and Splintered their Shards, creating the Dor — a trapped, mindless Investiture that powers Sel's diverse magic systems (AonDor, Dakhor, Forgery, etc.). Khriss's essay explicitly frames the Dor as 'the corpse of Adonalsium's power filtered through broken Shards,' showing how Adonalsium's original Investiture becomes dangerous when Shards are destroyed. Illustrates that Adonalsium's power, once divided, can be further broken; explains Sel's unique perverted Investiture; provides the clearest example of Adonalsium -> Shard -> Splinter chain.

Ruin and Preservation - The Scadrian BargainMistborn: The Final Empire (2006); The Well of Ascension (2007); The Hero of Ages (2008); Mistborn: Secret History (2016)

Publication 2006-2016; in-world ~300 years pre-Stormlight

On Scadrial, Shards Ruin (Ati) and Preservation (Leras) — both fragments of Adonalsium — made a pact to create humanity together. Later they conflicted: Ruin sought to destroy, Preservation to protect. Preservation sacrificed its Vessel to imprison Ruin. Eventually both Shards were held by new Vessels (Vin, then Sazed), who combined them into Harmony. Khriss and Hoid's notes in Secret History explicitly trace Ruin and Preservation back to Adonalsium, calling them 'two halves of a murder.' Shows Adonalsium's Intents in opposition; demonstrates that Shards can be recombined but not restored to Adonalsium; sets precedent for possibility of reconstituting Adonalsium (a major fandom theory).

The Future Reconstitution - Will Adonalsium Return?Rhythm of War (2020) Ch. 75-76; Wind and Truth (2024) epilogue hints; WoB - Dragonsteel 2022-2024 panels

Publication 2020-2024; in-world future

In Rhythm of War, Dalinar and Navani research anti-Investiture and the possibility that Shards can be combined. Hoid and Frost's letters debate whether the Shattering can or should be undone. Sanderson has stated in WoB that the overarching Cosmere arc (culminating after Stormlight 10 and Mistborn Era 4) will address whether Adonalsium can be reformed, and whether doing so would be desirable. No published text shows Adonalsium reformed; this is a foreshadowed future narrative, not an attested story. It is included to document authorial intent and reader expectation. Frames Adonalsium as the Cosmere's endgame; drives scholarly and fan speculation; Sanderson has warned that reforming Adonalsium would not simply resurrect the original being.

Adonalsium is just Brandon Sanderson's version of the Christian God and the books are allegory. — Sanderson, a Latter-day Saint, has stated Adonalsium is fictional and not intended as allegory for any real deity. While inspired by the idea of a singular divine power, the narrative treats Adonalsium as a killable, fragmentable entity within a fantasy physics system, distinct from theological concepts. The God Beyond is closer to an unknowable deity, but also fictional.

Hoid Shattered Adonalsium himself / Hoid is the main killer. — Text and WoB are clear: Hoid was present at the Shattering, was offered a Shard and refused, and did not take part in the killing. He is a witness and perhaps facilitator, but not a Vessel. The 16 Vessels are the killers.

Adonalsium appears on-page as a character in Stormlight or Mistborn. — Adonalsium has never appeared directly in any published Cosmere novel. All mentions are second-hand (epigraphs, scholarly essays, character speculation). Any artwork showing Adonalsium is fanon. First direct appearance is planned for the unpublished/forthcoming Dragonsteel novels.

Recombining the 16 Shards would automatically resurrect Adonalsium as the same person. — Sanderson has said reforming Adonalsium would create something 'similar but not identical' — like reassembling a shattered plate, the person would be new. Sazed as Harmony (Ruin+Preservation) is explicitly not Adonalsium, but a new being with two Intents. Whether true resurrection is possible is RAFO.
