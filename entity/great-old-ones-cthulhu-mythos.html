Who It Is

The Great Old Ones are a pantheon of ancient, immensely powerful cosmic entities in the Cthulhu Mythos — a shared fictional universe that originated with H.P. Lovecraft's stories and was expanded by a circle of writers he corresponded with and encouraged to borrow from his world-building. Unlike traditional gods, the Great Old Ones are alien beings from distant stars and dimensions who came to Earth eons ago, ruled or slept here, and will return when celestial alignments permit. The key entities include: Azathoth, the 'Blind Idiot God' at the center of the universe, a mindless nuclear chaos surrounded by insane flute-playing servitors whose awakening would end reality; Yog-Sothoth, the 'All-in-One and One-in-All,' a being coterminous with all time and space who can grant access to any point in existence; Shub-Niggurath, the 'Black Goat of the Woods with a Thousand Young,' a fertility deity of monstrous fecundity whose worship involves obscene rites; Nyarlathotep, the 'Crawling Chaos,' unique among the Great Old Ones in having a conscious will and taking pleasure in manipulating and tormenting humanity, walking the Earth in a thousand forms; and Cthulhu himself, the high priest of the Old Ones, sleeping in sunken R'lyeh. The Mythos's philosophical horror is that these beings are not evil in any comprehensible sense — they are simply vast and indifferent, and human morality, civilization, and existence are entirely meaningless to them. The ultimate horror is knowledge itself: learning the truth about the cosmos leads inevitably to madness. This is the core of 'cosmic horror' or 'cosmic indifferentism' — the terror not of death but of realizing our absolute insignificance. The Mythos is remarkable for being one of the earliest open-source fictional universes, with Lovecraft actively encouraging other writers to contribute. After his death, August Derleth codified and systematized the Mythos (controversially imposing a good-vs-evil framework), and the universe has continued to grow through novels, the Call of Cthulhu tabletop RPG (which has done more than any other single work to popularize and systematize the Mythos), video games, films, and countless homages in popular culture. The Mythos has effectively created a new 'mythology' that functions like ancient pantheons in modern fiction.

Cultural Significance

A genuinely new 20th-century mythology that functions like classical pantheons in modern fiction. Cosmic horror challenges the anthropocentric worldview. The collaborative/open-source nature of the Mythos was decades ahead of its time for shared universes.
