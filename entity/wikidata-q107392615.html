The Approach

Talus is a name that echoes through myth, literature, and modern games—a bronze giant, a stone guardian, a champion, and even a planet. But who is the true Talus? This dossier untangles the many identities that share this ancient name, from the automatons of Greek legend to the animated boulders of Hyrule.

Content advisory. None of the Talus entities contain explicit dark material; however, some depictions involve combat and violence typical of mythological and fantasy contexts.

Etymology

The name 'Talus' likely derives from the Greek 'Talos' (Τάλως), possibly related to the Greek word for 'sun' or 'young man'. In Latin, 'talus' means 'ankle' or 'die', and in geology it refers to a slope of rock debris. The connection between the mythological figure and the anatomical/geological terms is uncertain.

Cultural Ownership

The name spans multiple cultures and domains: Greek mythology, English literature, modern gaming, astronomy (fictional), geology, anatomy, and military architecture. No single culture owns the name; it is a shared term with diverse uses.

Disputed Identifications

The mythological Talus/Talos is sometimes conflated with other automatons or giants, such as the bronze giant in the Argonautica. In gaming, Talus in Paladins is a distinct character, while the Zelda Talus is a generic enemy type. The Star Wars planet Talus is separate from the mythological figure.

Description

Appearance

Varies by entity: In Greek myth, Talos is a giant bronze man. In Spenser, Talus is an 'yron man' (iron man). In Paladins, Talus is a humanoid with a futuristic appearance. In Zelda, Talus is a massive stone creature with a crystalline core.

Symbols & Iconography

Bronze or iron material, flail (Spenser), greatsword (Paladins), ore deposit (Zelda).

Behaviour & Personality

In myth, Talos is a guardian, aggressive towards intruders. In Spenser, Talus is an unfeeling enforcer of justice, relentless and mechanical. In Paladins, Talus is youthful and energetic. In Zelda, Talus is a mindless enemy that attacks on sight.

Domains & Habitats

Greek myth: island of Crete. Spenser: Faerie Land. Paladins: fictional realm of the Realm. Zelda: various mountainous regions of Hyrule.

Version Differences

The mythological Talos has a single vein and a vulnerable ankle; Spenser's Talus has no such vulnerability. Paladins Talus has teleportation powers; Zelda Talus has a rock-like body and can be defeated by targeting its weak point.

Talos and the Argonauts

Source Argonautica by Apollonius of RhodesCulture Ancient GreekPeriod 3rd century BCE

Summary

Talos, a bronze giant, guards Crete by throwing rocks at approaching ships. When the Argonauts arrive, Medea uses her magic to remove the nail sealing his vein, causing him to bleed out and die.

Participants

Consequences

Variants

In some versions, Talos is killed by a wound to the ankle; in others, he is driven mad by Medea's enchantments.

Contradictions & Disputes

Vulnerability of Talos

Position A

Talos is vulnerable at the ankle where the vein is sealed.

Position A — evidence

Argonautica describes the nail sealing the vein.

Position B

Talos is invulnerable except for the ankle.

Position B — evidence

Other sources suggest he was killed by Medea's magic.

Scholarly Consensus

The most common account is the vulnerability at the ankle, but variations exist.

Spelling: Talus vs Talos

Position A

Talus is the correct spelling for the mythological figure.

Position A — evidence

Some English translations use Talus.

Position B

Talos is the correct spelling.

Position B — evidence

The original Greek is Talos.

Scholarly Consensus

Talos is the standard transliteration, but Talus appears in some English works.
