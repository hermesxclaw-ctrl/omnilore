Who It Is

Rübezahl is the capricious spirit-lord of the Riesengebirge (Giant Mountains) between Silesia and Bohemia. His name — literally 'turnip-counter' — comes from a legend in which he kidnapped a human princess, and when she grew bored, he created a field of magical turnips that transformed into all the people and things of her homeland. She tricked him into counting the turnips, and while he was distracted, escaped. Rübezahl's appearance is variable: sometimes a giant, sometimes a monk in grey robes, sometimes a nobleman, sometimes a rustic with a staff — reflecting his shapeshifting, unpredictable nature. He controls the mountain weather — wind, fog, storms, avalanches — and tests travellers. If treated with respect, he rewards hikers with guidance, healing herbs, and riches; if mocked or challenged, he unleashes devastating storms and sends the offender wandering lost until they die of exposure. He is neither good nor evil but mercurial: a protector of the mountains and those who respect them, a terror to the arrogant. Rübezahl stories were collected and literary-ized by Johannes Praetorius in the 1660s and later polished by Johann Karl August Musäus. He remains a cultural icon of the Silesian-Bohemian border region, appearing in postage stamps, children's books, and tourism campaigns.
