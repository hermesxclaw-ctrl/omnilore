Who They Are

Xochiquetzal was the Toltec goddess of love, beauty, flowers, art, and weaving — the patroness of all that is beautiful and refined. In the Toltec tradition, she was the wife of the culture hero Quetzalcoatl at Tollan, and her presence in the palace made the city the center of artistic creation. She represents the Toltec ideal of beauty and craftsmanship.

Cultural Significance

Xochiquetzal's role in Toltec myth reinforces the understanding of Tollan as a golden age of art and refinement. The 'Toltec' ideal in Aztec culture — as the original masters of every craft — was personally embodied by Xochiquetzal. She represents the feminine principle of beauty and creation that balanced the masculine power of Quetzalcoatl's rule. The Toltec reverence for the arts, which the Aztecs later inherited and expanded, was directly connected to the cult of Xochiquetzal. The tradition of flower festivals and poetry contests (the 'flower songs' of Nahuatl literature) has its origins in Toltec Xochiquetzal worship.
