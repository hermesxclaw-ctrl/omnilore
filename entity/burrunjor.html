The cattle were ripped apart -- not like dingoes do it, not like crocs. Something big, something that walked on two legs and stood taller than a man. The old people call it Burrunjor.

Who It Is

Burrunjor is Australia's 'living dinosaur' cryptid -- a creature reported from the remote outback regions of the Northern Territory and Queensland that is described as a large, bipedal reptile resembling a theropod dinosaur, particularly Tyrannosaurus rex or Allosaurus. Reports describe a creature 20 to 30 feet long, walking on two powerful hind legs, with small forelimbs, a long tail, a lizard-like head with sharp teeth, and a loud, bellowing roar. Aboriginal oral traditions in Arnhem Land speak of 'Burrunjor' as a giant reptilian monster that inhabits the bush. European-Australian encounters emerged in the 1950s, most notably from cattle station owners Charles Bryan and Rex Aslin, who reported mysterious livestock killings (cattle torn apart, enormous tracks, and strange roaring) in the Gulf Country of northern Australia. In the 1970s and 1980s, cryptozoologist Rex Gilroy documented multiple accounts of large reptilian creatures from the outback, suggesting a surviving population of a megalania-like monitor lizard or a relict theropod dinosaur. Skeptics suggest misidentified crocodiles (saltwater crocs can travel surprisingly far inland), feral camels seen in poor light, or large goannas (monitor lizards, which can reach 6+ feet). The vast, sparsely populated expanses of the Australian outback provide a theoretical -- if highly improbable -- habitat for an undiscovered large reptile.
