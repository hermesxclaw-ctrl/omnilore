Who It Is

Preah Pisnukar is the Cambodian version of the Hindu divine architect Vishvakarma, the celestial craftsman who built the palaces of the gods. In Khmer mythology, Preah Pisnukar is credited with designing and building the great monuments of the Angkorian empire — particularly Angkor Wat itself. According to legend, when King Suryavarman II commissioned the temple now known as Angkor Wat, he dreamed of a celestial palace and called upon Preah Pisnukar to replicate it on earth. The divine architect descended from the heavens and drew the plans for the temple complex, which mirrors the heavenly abode of Vishnu. Preah Pisnukar is depicted as a bearded man carrying a carpenter's square, measuring rod, and mallet. He is the patron of architects, engineers, masons, and all craftsmen who work with their hands. The precision of Angkorian stonework — the interlocking sandstone blocks cut to within millimeters of tolerance, the perfectly aligned causeways, the astronomical orientation of the temples — was attributed to Preah Pisnukar's divine guidance. The deity also appears in Lao mythology as Phra Phisnu. In modern Cambodia, construction workers and architects still make offerings to Preah Pisnukar before beginning major projects, especially temple restorations at the Angkor Archaeological Park.

Attested in Hindu tradition transmitted to Cambodia, Khmer adaptation in inscriptions and temple reliefsEra Pre-Angkorian to Angkorian period (6th-15th century)
