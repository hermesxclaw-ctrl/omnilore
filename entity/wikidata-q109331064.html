Who It Is

She was not a Newtype, not a Cyber-Newtype, and not a zealot — which is exactly why Mouar Pharaoh stands out in the blood-soaked Gryps Conflict. A young Titans officer who flew because she believed in order and loved one flawed man, she chose to put her Gabthley between Jerid Messa and a killing beam, turning a minor supporting pilot into one of Zeta Gundam's most remembered tragedies.

Attested personality (TV dialogue and official profiles): earnest, dutiful, emotionally open, and devoted. Described in Entertainment Bible as 'bright and straightforward' (akaruku sunao). Unlike many Titans officers who display cruelty or cynicism (Bask Om, Yazan Gable), Mouar is consistently courteous, concerned for comrades, and openly affectionate toward Jerid Messa. She shows professional competence without arrogance, and naivete about the Titans' political corruption. Powers
Mobile Suit Piloting (Normal Human, Non-Newtype)

Competent Titans mobile suit pilot. Initially pilots RMS-106 Hizack, later assigned the high-performance transformable prototype RX-110 Gabthley developed by Paptimus Scirocco. Demonstrates ability to handle transformable MA/MS hybrid in combat against AEUG pilots including Kamille Bidan and Quattro Bajeena. Not rated as an ace on the level of Char, Amuro, or Yazan, but considered above-average within Titans junior officer corps.

Limit: No Newtype pressure sensing, precognition, or psycommu ability. No Cyber-Newtype augmentation. Relies entirely on standard sensors and human reflexes. Gabthley's performance often exceeds her ability to fully exploit it compared to Scirocco himself.

Transformable Mobile Armor Operation (RX-110 Gabthley)

Trained to operate Gabthley's dual forms: Mobile Suit mode and Mobile Armor (jet) mode with large claw-like shoulder binders functioning as atmospheric/space flight surfaces. Uses Gabthley's beam rifle and diffuse beam cannon mounted in binders. Capable of coordinated pair tactics with Jerid Messa's Gabthley.

Limit: Gabthley is a prototype with high thrust but complex transformation; requires skilled handling. No psycommu weapons. Vulnerable to beam rifle fire in MS mode as demonstrated in her death.

Interpersonal Support and Morale Function

Narrative ability rather than superhuman power: provides emotional stability to Jerid Messa, whose performance had degraded due to repeated losses and rivalry with Kamille. Her encouragement temporarily restores his combat effectiveness. This is explicitly noted in dialogue (Jerid: 'Mouar...' after her death, losing will to fight momentarily).

Limit: No tactical command authority; does not influence broader Titans strategy. Effect is personal and ultimately tragic, as her death accelerates Jerid's reckless final charge.

Accounts I
Arrival on the Jupitris and Pairing with Jerid MessaMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 26-32 (TV broadcast)

UC 0087 (in-universe); 1985 broadcast

Mouar Pharaoh is introduced as a young Titans ensign assigned to Paptimus Scirocco's contingent aboard the Jupitris. She is paired with fellow junior officer Jerid Messa, who has been demoralized after the deaths of Kacricon Cacooler and Lila Milla and his ongoing humiliations against Kamille Bidan. Mouar's open affection and uncomplicated loyalty quickly become Jerid's emotional anchor. She is first seen in Hizack sorties, then selected alongside Jerid to test Scirocco's new RX-110 Gabthley prototypes, marking her elevation to Scirocco's favored pilots. Establishes Mouar-Jerid romance as central emotional subplot; explains why two relatively junior pilots receive prototype transformable units. Sets up Scirocco's practice of using personal loyalty as control.

First Gabthley Sortie - Pursuit of the ArgamaMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 34 'The Call of Darkness' / Episode 36 'Four's Ghost'

UC 0087

Mouar and Jerid sortie together in their Gabthleys against the AEUG flagship Argama and its mobile suits. Mouar demonstrates competent handling of the transformable machine, engaging in high-speed MA mode pursuits and providing cover for Jerid. The pair's coordinated attacks pressure Kamille's Zeta Gundam and Quattro's Hyaku Shiki, though they are forced to retreat by superior Newtype performance and the Argama's defenses. Mouar shows concern for Jerid's recklessness, urging caution. Demonstrates Titans' technological edge via Scirocco's designs; reinforces Mouar as capable wingman rather than liability. Deepens audience investment before tragedy.

Shore Leave and Confession - Humanizing the TitansMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 36-38 interludes

UC 0087

Between battles, Mouar and Jerid share quiet moments aboard ship. In contrast to the Titans' brutal reputation (Bask Om's colony gassing, torture of civilians), their scenes depict normal young love: Mouar teases Jerid, worries about his obsession with Kamille, and promises to survive together after the war. These scenes are deliberately placed after the deaths of Four Murasame and Emma Sheen's defection to contrast AEUG and Titans humanity. Tomino uses Mouar to show that not all Titans are monsters — some are ordinary youths swept into fascism through loyalty and ambition. Makes eventual death more impactful; complicates moral binary of Gryps Conflict. Frequently cited by critics as example of Tomino's 'kill them after you make the audience love them' structure.

Accounts II
Battle of Gryps - Final Defense of JeridMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 44 'The Jupitris Infiltration' / Episode 45 'Coming from Heaven' (TV)

UC 0087

During the chaotic three-way battle between AEUG, Titans, and Axis near Gryps 2, Jerid and Mouar sortie together against Kamille Bidan in the Zeta Gundam. Kamille, enraged and tapping into heightened Newtype pressure, fires a beam rifle shot intended to kill Jerid. Mouar instantly interposes her Gabthley between the beam and Jerid's machine, taking the full blast through the cockpit. Her Gabthley explodes. Jerid screams her name as she dies. Kamille is briefly shaken, but the battle continues. Mouar's death is immediate and irreversible in TV canon. Jerid is devastated, then consumed by grief-fueled rage, leading to his own death shortly after in a reckless charge against Kamille. The sequence cements Mouar as the series' most explicit 'shielding sacrifice' and completes Jerid's tragic arc from arrogant cadet to bereaved lover.

Aftermath - Jerid's Last ChargeMobile Suit Zeta Gundam Episode 45-46

UC 0087

Though Mouar is dead, her death drives the next narrative beat. Jerid, now with nothing left, abandons tactical caution and attacks Kamille with suicidal fury, shouting that Kamille took everything from him. Kamille, channeling the spirits/pressure of Four, Emma, and others, defeats and kills Jerid. In the novelization, Jerid's internal monologue explicitly links Mouar's loss to his final despair. Mouar is referenced posthumously by Scirocco's faction as an example of expendable loyalty. Closes Mouar-Jerid arc; demonstrates Tomino's theme that war devours even apolitical love. Her death has no strategic effect on Gryps Conflict outcome, emphasizing senselessness.

Legacy in Official Side Materials - Gabthley Pair TacticsGundam Perfect File, MS Encyclopedia, and Game Appearances (1986-ongoing)

UC 0087 side stories; publication 1986-2010s

Outside the anime, Mouar appears in mechanical and character guides detailing Gabthley development: Scirocco designed the Gabthley as a two-machine team, requiring paired pilots with high mutual trust — Mouar and Jerid were selected for their bond. Data books note her as 'Scirocco's girl' in the sense of being under his patronage, not romantic. No new canonical stories expand her life before Titans; no prequel manga gives her hometown or family. Solidifies her mechanical legacy; explains why Gabthley is almost always depicted as a pair in model kits and games. No retcon of survival.

Mouar Pharaoh was a Cyber-Newtype like Four Murasame and Rosamia Badam. — She was explicitly a normal human with no Newtype or Cyber-Newtype abilities. Scirocco employed both augmented and non-augmented pilots; Mouar's value was loyalty and teamwork with Jerid, not psychic power.

Mouar and Jerid were married or engaged. — Official materials describe them as lovers (koibito). No marriage, engagement, or formal betrothal is ever mentioned in dialogue, scripts, or data books. Their relationship is that of young officers dating during wartime.

Mouar survived the Gryps Conflict in an alternate official ending. — In TV and film canon she definitively dies shielding Jerid. Survival only occurs in non-canonical game branching paths (Super Robot Wars secret flags) where player intervention can prevent her death. These are game mechanics, not official timeline.
