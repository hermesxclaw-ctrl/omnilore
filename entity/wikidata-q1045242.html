The Approach

In the quiet mountain village of Hinamizawa, a cheerful girl with a cleaver in her bag and a smile that never quite reaches her eyes greets you as a friend. Rena Ryūgū is the heart and the horror of Higurashi When They Cry—a girl desperate to be normal in a world that won't allow it, whose love can turn to violence in the blink of an eye. Her story is a tragedy of broken trust, a curse that may be real, and a mind pushed to the brink by forces both supernatural and all too human.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses self-harm, mental illness, violence, murder, child abuse, and psychological horror. It includes spoilers for Higurashi When They Cry. Reader discretion is advised.
Identity

Rena Ryūgū

Pronunciation REH-nah RYOO-goo (Japanese: りゅうぐう れな)

Known As

Reina RyūgūRenaRyūgū Rena竜宮レナ竜宮礼奈

Etymology

The surname Ryūgū (竜宮) means 'Dragon Palace', referencing the mythical underwater palace of Ryūgū-jō. The given name Rena (レナ) is a shortened, more childlike form of her birth name Reina (礼奈), which combines the characters for 'thanks' and 'apple tree' or 'nara'. The change from Reina to Rena symbolizes her rejection of her past and her desire to reinvent herself.

Cultural Ownership

Created by 07th Expansion (Ryukishi07 and Yatazakura) for the Japanese visual novel series Higurashi no Naku Koro ni. Rena is a Japanese fictional character and is part of the broader otaku and moe culture.

Disputed Identifications

Some fans have mistakenly conflated Rena with other 'yandere' characters or assumed her mental illness is a simple case of split personality. The official canon clarifies that her condition is Hinamizawa Syndrome, a fictional disease, not dissociative identity disorder. Additionally, her name change from Reina to Rena is sometimes misinterpreted as a legal name change, but it is a personal choice.

Description

Appearance

Rena is a teenage girl with long, light brown hair and large amber-brown eyes. She typically wears her school uniform, a white shirt with a red bow, a green plaid skirt, and black thigh-high socks. Her most iconic accessory is her cleaver (a nata), which she carries in a bag or on her back. In later appearances, she is shown with a more mature appearance, such as in Higurashi Gou where she is a university student.

Symbols & Iconography

Rena is often associated with the cleaver, which has become a symbol of her potential for violence. Her 'cute' items, like her stuffed animals and her habit of 'treasure hunting' at the garbage dump, represent her desire for innocence and normalcy. The 'cloudy eyes' effect, where her eyes lose their light, is a visual shorthand for her descent into madness or her serious moments.

Behaviour & Personality

On the surface, Rena is cheerful, friendly, and somewhat naive, often using the verbal tic 'kana?' (かな?) and exclaiming 'Uso da!' when shocked. She is kind and caring, especially toward her friends, and has a strong maternal instinct. However, she is also highly perceptive and suspicious of lies, and when she feels betrayed or when the curse of Oyashiro is questioned, she can become hostile and violent. Her mental instability is rooted in her traumatic past, and she is prone to paranoia and delusions, especially when suffering from Hinamizawa Syndrome.

Domains & Habitats

Rena lives in Hinamizawa, a rural village in Japan, and attends the local school. She is often seen at the village's garbage dump, where she searches for discarded items she finds cute. She is also associated with the Sonozaki family's shrine and the village's festivals. In alternate timelines, she may live in Ibaraki Prefecture or attend university.

Version Differences

In the original visual novels, Rena is the protagonist of Tsumihoroboshi-hen and is more clearly depicted as suffering from Hinamizawa Syndrome. In the anime, her character is somewhat streamlined, and her 'yandere' traits are emphasized. In Higurashi Gou, she is older and more mature, but still retains her core personality. In crossover appearances, she is often portrayed as a comedic or fanservice character, downplaying her darker side.

Powers & Abilities

Hinamizawa Syndrome

Source Version Original visual novels, anime, manga

Description

A fictional disease that causes paranoia, hallucinations, and delusions, often leading to violence. It is triggered by stress and is linked to the curse of Oyashiro. Rena's case is particularly severe, manifesting as delusional parasitosis (the belief that maggots are in her blood).

Limitations

The syndrome is not supernatural in origin; it is a disease with a parasitic cause. Its effects can be mitigated by stress reduction and medication.

Contradictions

Some adaptations imply the syndrome is supernatural, while others treat it as a purely medical condition.

Physical Strength and Agility

Source Version Various

Description

Rena is shown to be physically capable, able to wield a cleaver effectively and overpower opponents in moments of extreme emotion. She is also agile and quick.

Limitations

Her strength is not superhuman; it is augmented by adrenaline and desperation.

Contradictions

None noted.

Perception of Alternate Realities

Source Version Tsumihoroboshi-hen

Description

In Tsumihoroboshi-hen, Rena is able to vaguely perceive that she is in an alternate world, remembering fragments from previous timelines. This is a rare ability among Higurashi characters, who are usually unaware of the looping.

Limitations

This ability is vague and not fully controllable. It is not a power she can use at will.

Contradictions

Some fans interpret this as a result of her mental illness rather than a genuine ability.

Cleaver Proficiency

Source Version Various

Description

Rena is often depicted wielding a cleaver (nata), which she uses as a weapon in violent outbursts. She is skilled enough to use it effectively in combat.

Limitations

She only uses it in extreme situations, and it is not her preferred tool.

Contradictions

None noted.

Stories & Myths

Onikakushi-hen (Demon-Deleting Chapter)

Source Higurashi no Naku Koro niCulture Japanese otaku culturePeriod 2002

Summary

Rena is introduced as Keiichi's friend. As Keiichi becomes paranoid about the village's secrets, Rena acts as both a comfort and a threat. The arc ends with Keiichi attacking Rena, and in the bad end, Rena kills him with her cleaver. This is the first appearance of her 'Uso da!' line.

Participants

Keiichi Maebara

Rena Ryūgū

Mion Sonozaki

Satoko Hojo

Rika Furude

Consequences

Establishes Rena as a potential antagonist and sets the tone for the series' horror.

Variants

The anime adaptation expands some scenes, and the manga provides more internal monologue.

Tsumihoroboshi-hen (Atonement Chapter)

Source Higurashi no Naku Koro niCulture Japanese otaku culturePeriod 2003

Summary

Rena is the protagonist. She begins to remember fragments of previous timelines and becomes determined to stop the tragedy. She uncovers the truth about Hinamizawa Syndrome and confronts the police, leading to a standoff. In the end, she is saved by Keiichi and others, and the arc provides a turning point in the series.

Participants

Rena Ryūgū

Keiichi Maebara

Mion Sonozaki

Rika Furude

Tomitake Jirō

Consequences

Reveals the existence of alternate timelines and the nature of the syndrome. Rena's character is fully developed, showing her strength and vulnerability.

Variants

The anime adaptation condenses the arc, and the manga provides more detail.

Source Higurashi no Naku Koro ni ReiCulture Japanese otaku culturePeriod 2006

A comedic arc where Rena and the others are in a beach setting. Rena is depicted as overly attached to Keiichi, leading to uncomfortable situations. It is notable for its fanservice and comedic tone.

Self-harm and suicide attempt

Source Tsumihoroboshi-hen

Description

After her parents' divorce, Rena became despondent and attacked three male classmates with a baseball bat, smashed school windows, and later attempted to slit her wrists while hospitalized.

Scholarly Context

This is portrayed as a result of Hinamizawa Syndrome and her mental breakdown, and is handled with sensitivity in the narrative.

Violence and murder

Source Onikakushi-hen, Tsumihoroboshi-hen

Description

In several arcs, Rena kills or attempts to kill characters, often while under the influence of the syndrome or when she believes it is necessary.

Scholarly Context

Her violence is depicted as tragic and driven by delusion, not as glorified.

Family dysfunction

Source Tsumihoroboshi-hen

Description

Rena's mother had an affair and became pregnant, leading to the divorce. Rena's father was abusive or neglectful, and Rena internalized the blame.

Scholarly Context

The story explores the impact of parental betrayal on a child's psyche.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nature of Hinamizawa Syndrome

Position A

It is a supernatural curse from Oyashiro-sama.

Position A — evidence

Characters in the story believe it is a curse, and some events seem to support this.

Position B

It is a parasitic disease with no supernatural element.

Position B — evidence

The true cause is revealed in Matsuribayashi-hen to be a parasite.

Scholarly Consensus

The official canon states it is a disease, but the curse is a red herring.

Rena's mental state

Position A

Rena has dissociative identity disorder (split personality).

Position A — evidence

Her behavior changes drastically, and some fans interpret this as multiple personalities.

Position B

Rena suffers from Hinamizawa Syndrome, which causes paranoia and delusions, not DID.

Position B — evidence

The narrative explicitly states she has the syndrome, and her behavior is consistent with paranoia.

Scholarly Consensus

The official canon does not support DID; it is a misreading.

Rena's role in Gou

Position A

Rena is a supporting character in Gou.

Position A — evidence

She appears less frequently than in the original.

Position B

Rena is a key plot point in Gou due to her memories.

Position B — evidence

Her memories of the loops are crucial to the plot.

Scholarly Consensus

She is a supporting character but with a significant role.
