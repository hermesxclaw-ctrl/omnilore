Who It Is

In the quiet mountain village of Hinamizawa, a cheerful girl with a cleaver in her bag and a smile that never quite reaches her eyes greets you as a friend. Rena Ryūgū is the heart and the horror of Higurashi When They Cry—a girl desperate to be normal in a world that won't allow it, whose love can turn to violence in the blink of an eye. Her story is a tragedy of broken trust, a curse that may be real, and a mind pushed to the brink by forces both supernatural and all too human.

On the surface, Rena is cheerful, friendly, and somewhat naive, often using the verbal tic 'kana?' (かな?) and exclaiming 'Uso da!' when shocked. She is kind and caring, especially toward her friends, and has a strong maternal instinct. However, she is also highly perceptive and suspicious of lies, and when she feels betrayed or when the curse of Oyashiro is questioned, she can become hostile and violent. Her mental instability is rooted in her traumatic past, and she is prone to paranoia and delusions, especially when suffering from Hinamizawa Syndrome.

Powers
Hinamizawa Syndrome

A fictional disease that causes paranoia, hallucinations, and delusions, often leading to violence. It is triggered by stress and is linked to the curse of Oyashiro. Rena's case is particularly severe, manifesting as delusional parasitosis (the belief that maggots are in her blood).

Limit: The syndrome is not supernatural in origin; it is a disease with a parasitic cause. Its effects can be mitigated by stress reduction and medication.

Physical Strength and Agility

Rena is shown to be physically capable, able to wield a cleaver effectively and overpower opponents in moments of extreme emotion. She is also agile and quick.

Limit: Her strength is not superhuman; it is augmented by adrenaline and desperation.

Perception of Alternate Realities

In Tsumihoroboshi-hen, Rena is able to vaguely perceive that she is in an alternate world, remembering fragments from previous timelines. This is a rare ability among Higurashi characters, who are usually unaware of the looping.

Limit: This ability is vague and not fully controllable. It is not a power she can use at will.

Cleaver Proficiency

Rena is often depicted wielding a cleaver (nata), which she uses as a weapon in violent outbursts. She is skilled enough to use it effectively in combat.

Limit: She only uses it in extreme situations, and it is not her preferred tool.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Rena is a teenage girl with long, light brown hair and large amber-brown eyes. She typically wears her school uniform, a white shirt with a red bow, a green plaid skirt, and black thigh-high socks. Her most iconic accessory is her cleaver (a nata), which she carries in a bag or on her back. In later appearances, she is shown with a more mature appearance, such as in Higurashi Gou where she is a university student.

Symbols — Rena is often associated with the cleaver, which has become a symbol of her potential for violence. Her 'cute' items, like her stuffed animals and her habit of 'treasure hunting' at the garbage dump, represent her desire for innocence and normalcy. The 'cloudy eyes' effect, where her eyes lose their light, is a visual shorthand for her descent into madness or her serious moments.

Domains — Rena lives in Hinamizawa, a rural village in Japan, and attends the local school. She is often seen at the village's garbage dump, where she searches for discarded items she finds cute. She is also associated with the Sonozaki family's shrine and the village's festivals. In alternate timelines, she may live in Ibaraki Prefecture or attend university.

Accounts I
Onikakushi-hen (Demon-Deleting Chapter)Higurashi no Naku Koro ni

2002

Rena is introduced as Keiichi's friend. As Keiichi becomes paranoid about the village's secrets, Rena acts as both a comfort and a threat. The arc ends with Keiichi attacking Rena, and in the bad end, Rena kills him with her cleaver. This is the first appearance of her 'Uso da!' line. Establishes Rena as a potential antagonist and sets the tone for the series' horror.

Tsumihoroboshi-hen (Atonement Chapter)Higurashi no Naku Koro ni

2003

Rena is the protagonist. She begins to remember fragments of previous timelines and becomes determined to stop the tragedy. She uncovers the truth about Hinamizawa Syndrome and confronts the police, leading to a standoff. In the end, she is saved by Keiichi and others, and the arc provides a turning point in the series. Reveals the existence of alternate timelines and the nature of the syndrome. Rena's character is fully developed, showing her strength and vulnerability.

Accounts II
Hirukowashi-hen (Daybreak Chapter)Higurashi no Naku Koro ni Rei

2006

A comedic arc where Rena and the others are in a beach setting. Rena is depicted as overly attached to Keiichi, leading to uncomfortable situations. It is notable for its fanservice and comedic tone. Provides a lighter take on the characters, but has been criticized for its handling of Rena's character.

Higurashi GouHigurashi: When They Cry – Gou (anime)

2020-2021

A sequel series that reboots the timeline. Rena is initially a supporting character, but it is revealed that she is one of the few who remembers the previous loops. She plays a crucial role in the mystery, and her character is shown to have grown. Expands the lore of the series and recontextualizes Rena's character.

Rena is a typical 'yandere' character who is crazy for love. — Rena's violence is not primarily motivated by romantic obsession, but by her mental illness and her belief in the curse. Her love for Keiichi is a factor, but it is not the main driver.

Rena's name change from Reina to Rena is a legal name change. — It is a personal choice to distance herself from her past; she is still legally Reina Ryūgū.

Rena is a 'menhera' character, meaning she has a mental illness that is romanticized. — Rena's mental illness is portrayed realistically as a tragic and debilitating condition, not something to be romanticized. The term 'menhera' is often used in Japanese pop culture to describe characters with mental health issues, but it can be problematic.

Rena is responsible for all the murders in Higurashi. — Rena is only responsible for some of the murders in specific arcs, and the overall mystery involves multiple characters and causes.
