I wait at the bottom of the deepest billabong. I hold the monsoon in my coils. Come to me with the right song, and the land will bloom. Come to me empty, and I will swallow you whole.

Who It Is

Julunggul is the Yolngu manifestation of the Rainbow Serpent — a great python-like being, specifically gendered as female and grandmotherly in this Arnhem Land tradition. She is intimately associated with the Arafura Swamp and the great water systems of northeastern Arnhem Land, where she dwells in the deepest waterholes and controls the monsoon rains, the fertility of the land, and the reproductive cycles of all living things. In Yolngu mythology, Julunggul is particularly associated with the Gunabibi and Djungguan ceremonies — major ritual cycles that involve both male and female initiates and are among the most sacred and restricted ceremonies in Yolngu culture. During these ceremonies, Julunggul is said to swallow the initiates whole and then release them, symbolizing death and rebirth into a new spiritual status. Her association with the moon and the menstrual cycle also connects her to women's ritual life. Julunggul's presence is not abstract — she is a living reality in the landscape, and specific waterholes, billabongs, and stretches of the Glyde River and Arafura Swamp are identified as her body or her resting places. To approach these sites without proper ritual protocol is to risk her wrath, which manifests as drowning, disease, or meteorological disaster. Yolngu art, renowned globally, frequently depicts Julunggul in the cross-hatched (rarrk) style characteristic of Arnhem Land bark painting, with her serpentine body stretching across the painting in patterns that encode specific sacred sites. As with all manifestations of the Rainbow Serpent, Julunggul represents the fundamental Aboriginal principle that water, life, fertility, and correct ritual behavior are inseparable.
