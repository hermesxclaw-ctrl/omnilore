Who It Is

The Ropen ('demon flyer') is a cryptid reported from Papua New Guinea that, like the Kongamato, is often described in terms strongly reminiscent of a living pterosaur. Eyewitnesses describe a large flying creature with a wingspan of 3 to 4 feet (some reports claim up to 15-20 feet for a larger variant called the Duah), a long tail ending in a diamond-shaped flange, a beak-like mouth with teeth, leathery wings, and -- most strikingly -- a bioluminescent glow. Local islanders report that the Ropen emits a bright light while flying over the sea at night, possibly to attract fish. In 1944, American soldier Duane Hodgkinson claimed to have startled a large flying creature with a pterosaur-like appearance during WWII in a jungle clearing. More recently, American creationist-cryptozoologist expeditions led by explorers like Paul Nation, Jonathan Whitcomb, and Carl Baugh have interviewed eyewitnesses and captured night footage of unexplained flying lights off Umboi Island. The bioluminescence claim has drawn particular attention, as no known bird or bat produces such light. Skeptics propose the Ropen is a misidentified frigatebird (which has a forked tail and large wingspan) or flying fox bat, while the glowing lights are attributed to the bioluminescent fungus on rotting trees, meteors, or distant campfires. The Ropen has become a fixture of both cryptozoological and creationist literature, the latter using the potential survival of pterosaurs as evidence against evolutionary timescales. Indigenous Papuans consistently describe the Ropen as a real, living animal, not a spirit.
