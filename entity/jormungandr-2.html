Who It Is

Imagine the horizon. Not as a line where sky meets sea, but as the curve of a serpent's back, scales the size of mountains, silently circling the entire world. This is Jörmungandr, the Midgard Serpent, a being so vast he has become the very edge of reality. He is not a monster that lives in the world; he is a monster that defines it. And when he finally lets go of his own tail, the universe as the Norse knew it will end.

His primary behavior is to lie in the ocean, encircling Midgard, biting his own tail. This is a static, cosmic role. He is not described as actively malevolent in his daily existence, but rather as a force of nature. He is, however, fiercely antagonistic towards Thor and the gods, and his release of his tail is a sign of the coming apocalypse.

Powers
World-Encircling Size

Jörmungandr's defining power is his immense size. He is so large that he surrounds the entire disk of Midgard, forming a physical boundary for the world. This makes him a cosmic entity on par with the world itself.

Limit: His size also binds him; he is locked in a cycle of biting his own tail, unable to act beyond his encircling role until Ragnarök.

Venomous Bite

While not described in detail in his daily state, his venom is lethal. During Ragnarök, he poisons Thor, and Thor's final act is to kill him. The venom is potent enough to kill the greatest of the gods.

Limit: He must be able to bite or spit venom; this is a close-range or breath-based attack.

Prophecy-Bound Existence

His existence is tied to fate. His release of his tail is not a choice but a sign of the onset of Ragnarök. His death is also foretold, making him a being whose actions are predetermined by prophecy.

Limit: He is not a free agent; his life is a script he must follow.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Jörmungandr is described as an unfathomably large serpent or dragon. The Prose Edda states that he grew so large that he could encircle the entire world of Midgard and grasp his own tail. This makes him a being of cosmic scale, his body forming the very boundary of the mortal realm. He is not described in detail beyond his immense size, but as a serpent, he is imagined with scales, a long, coiling body, and likely a venomous bite.

Symbols — The primary symbol is the ouroboros, the serpent biting its own tail, representing cyclicality, eternity, and the self-contained nature of the world. He is also a symbol of the untamable, chaotic power of the sea and the forces that exist beyond the ordered world of the gods and humans.

Domains — His domain is the world-encircling ocean (the 'world sea' or 'Miðgarðsormr's realm'), the depths of the sea, and the boundary between the mortal world and the unknown. He is a chthonic and cosmic being, tied to the primordial waters.

Accounts I
The Binding and Casting OutProse Edda (Gylfaginning, chapter 34)

Medieval (c. 1220 CE)

Odin, learning of the monstrous children of Loki and Angrboða, has them brought to him. He casts Jörmungandr into the great ocean that surrounds Midgard. There, the serpent grows until he encircles the world and bites his own tail. Jörmungandr becomes a permanent fixture of the world, a boundary and a threat. This act establishes his role and sets the stage for his feud with Thor.

Accounts II
Thor's Fishing TripPoetic Edda (Hymiskviða) and Prose Edda (Gylfaginning, chapter 46)

c. 10th-13th century CE

Thor, the thunder god, goes fishing with the giant Hymir. Thor uses a giant ox head as bait and rows far out to sea. He hooks Jörmungandr, who thrashes so violently that Thor's feet punch through the bottom of the boat. Thor pulls the serpent up to the surface, and the two stare at each other, with Thor preparing to strike with his hammer, Mjölnir. Hymir, terrified, cuts the line, and Jörmungandr sinks back into the depths. In the Prose Edda, Thor throws his hammer and strikes the serpent's head, but it survives. This strengthens the enmity between Thor and Jörmungandr. It demonstrates the serpent's immense power and Thor's courage. The act of cutting the line by Hymir is seen as a cowardly act that robs Thor of his victory.

The Death of Thor and Jörmungandr at RagnarökPoetic Edda (Völuspá, stanzas 50-53) and Prose Edda (Gylfaginning, chapter 51)

c. 10th-13th century CE

At Ragnarök, the world serpent releases his tail and emerges from the sea, flooding the land with venomous waves. He joins the forces of the giants and the dead against the gods. Thor battles him. The two fight a ferocious duel. Thor kills Jörmungandr with his hammer, but as the serpent dies, it spews its venom over Thor. Thor takes nine steps before succumbing to the poison and dying. The mutual destruction of Thor and Jörmungandr is a central event of Ragnarök. It signifies the death of a major god and the end of an era. The world is destroyed and reborn, and the serpent is gone.

Jörmungandr is a 'dragon' in the modern, fire-breathing, winged sense. — In Old Norse, he is a 'ormr' (worm or serpent). He is depicted as a giant snake, not a winged dragon. The term 'dragon' is a modern translation that can be misleading.

Jörmungandr is a servant of Loki or a willing agent of chaos. — While he is Loki's son, there is no evidence he serves his father. He is a force of nature, and his role in Ragnarök is part of prophecy, not a plot.

Jörmungandr is 'evil' in a moral sense. — He is a destructive force, but Norse mythology does not frame him as 'evil' in a Christian sense. He is a monster, a threat, and a part of the cosmic order. His destruction is a part of the cycle of death and rebirth.
