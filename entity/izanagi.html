Who It Is

Izanagi is the initial Persona of Yu Narukami, protagonist of Persona 4 — a manifestation of the Fool Arcana representing infinite potential. A lightning-wielding warrior based on the Shinto creator god, Izanagi's arc mirrors the game's search for truth in a town shrouded in fog. His mythological connection to Izanami — and the revelation that the true antagonist IS a corrupted Izanami — makes this Persona the narrative center of Persona 4.

Origins

In the Kojiki (712 CE), Izanagi and Izanami stirred the primordial ocean with the jeweled spear Ame-no-Nuhoko. The brine dripping from the spear formed the first island. They created Japan's islands and numerous kami. Izanami died giving birth to Kagutsuchi (fire god). Izanagi descended into Yomi to retrieve her — but seeing her maggot-ridden body, fled in horror. Izanami pursued him until Izanagi sealed the entrance. He purified himself in a river, and from this ritual, Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo were born.

Form

Tall, imposing figure in flowing black coat with high collar — Japanese school uniform reimagined as divine armor. Metallic, featureless mask obscures face. Wields Ame-no-Nuhoko — the jeweled spear — as a massive glaive. Lightning arcs crackle from his body. Izanagi-no-Okami: radiant white and gold figure, flowing cape, serene divine face, mask removed — visual representation of enlightenment.

Powers

Land-creation (Kuniumi) via the heavenly spear Standing on the Floating Bridge of Heaven (Ame-no-ukihashi), Izanagi churns the primordial, jellyfish-like ocean with the jewelled spear Ame-no-Nuboko; the brine that drips from the spear's tip congeals into the first land, Onogoro Island. He and Izanami then descend, plant the heavenly pillar, and beget the rest of the Japanese archipelago (the Ōyashima and six further islands). Kojiki and Nihon Shoki (main accounts); ritual-fertility reading well established in scholarship (the penis-and-vagina churning of the sea is a phallic symbolism). Requires the tool (the spear) given by the older gods; the first attempt at the marriage rite fails and produces deformed offspring (Hiruko), showing that ritual protocol governs the power's success. Nihon Shoki has the couple volunteer to consolidate the earth and casts them as 'god of yang' (male) and 'goddess of yin' (female) under Chinese cosmology, omitting the Kotoamatsukami delegation and the last six islands.

Deity-begetting (Kamiumi) through his own body After escaping Yomi, Izanagi cleanses himself (misogi) at the river-mouth in Himuka; deities spring from his discarded clothing, the water he splashes, and each immersion. Washing his left eye births the sun goddess Amaterasu-Ōmikami; washing his right eye births the moon deity Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; washing his nose births the storm god Susanoo-no-Mikoto — the 'Three Precious Children' (mihashira no uzu no miko). Kojiki (eye/nose misogi origin); a Nihon Shoki variant instead births them from two bronze mirrors held in his left and right hands plus a sideways glance. This is a reactive, purgative power: he only generates deities after being polluted by contact with the dead, and only while ritually washing away that kegare (pollution). Other Shoki versions make the Three Precious Children (and Hiruko, and even Susanoo) the joint offspring of Izanagi AND Izanami through procreation, not of Izanagi alone.

Slaying of the fire god (Ame-no-ohabari sword) In grief and rage over the death of Izanami (burned by the newborn fire god Kagutsuchi's birth), Izanagi decapitates Kagutsuchi with his ten-grasp sword (Ame-no-ohabari; the 'heavenly' blade also called Ame-no-Habakiri), cutting the corpse into eight pieces that become eight volcanoes; more deities issue from the blood and remains. Kojiki; Nihon Shoki; Engishiki (which adds that in her death throes Izanami bears the water goddess Mizuhanome to quench the fire god). The slaying, while a 'power', is an act of retributive violence with vast collateral consequence (spawning volcanoes and a host of lesser kami) rather than a clean victory. Different texts catalogue the deities born from Kagutsuchi's corpse differently (Watatsumi, Kuraokami, Takemikazuchi, Futsunushi, Amatsu-Mikaboshi, Ōyamatsumi are among those commonly named).

Descent into Yomi and the power to walk between life and death Izanagi descends to the land of the dead (Yomi) in a doomed attempt to retrieve Izanami, demonstrating a unique, if limited, ability of a living deity to cross into the underworld — and to return, by fleeing, deceiving pursuers with thrown vegetation, and finally sealing the entrance with a great boulder (Chigaeshi no Ōkami). Kojiki (full descent); the Nihon Shoki main narrative omits the descent and instead has Izanagi retire to Awaji. Death can be seen but not undone: because Izanami has eaten food cooked at the furnace of Yomi she cannot return; Izanagi's own return pollutes him and forces the misogi that births the high pantheon. The descent is central in Kojiki but absent entirely in the Shoki main text — a major internal inconsistency between the two canonical chronicles.

Divine expulsion and world-division among the Three Precious Children Izanagi formally apportions the cosmos to his children (Amaterasu: Takamagahara/high heaven; Tsukuyomi: the night or the sea-tides depending on version; Susanoo: the seas), and — when the weeping Susanoo refuses his appointed task and begs to go to his mother's land Ne-no-Katasu-Kuni — expels him 'with a divine expulsion', the ritual banishing that becomes a template for punishment in the Age of the Gods. Kojiki (Amaterasu high heaven / Tsukuyomi night / Susanoo sea; banishment for weeping); Nihon Shoki variants vary the assignments and the trigger. His authority over the children is real but not absolute: Susanoo later returns, wrecks havoc in Takamagahara, and drives Amaterasu into the heavenly rock-cave — episodes in which Izanagi plays no further saving role. The realms each child is given differ text to text (Tsukuyomi: 'night' vs 'govern heaven alongside the sun' vs 'the eightfold tides of the sea plain'), and scholars suspect Susanoo was added to myths originally centered on Amaterasu and Tsukuyomi.

Apotropaic command over the peach Fleeing Yomi, Izanagi throws three peaches at the hags of Yomi (Yomotsu-shikome) to drive them back, declares the peach divine, and bids it grow in the land of the living to aid people in need — instituting the peach as a sacred charm-fruit (a motif of Chinese origin, where peaches are apotropaic). Kojiki (the 'Great Divine Fruit' / Ōkamu-no-Mi); the peach's sacredness is further attested in Chinese Hualü tradition influencing Japan. Close-range obstruction, not a ranged weapon of destruction; its permanent value is talismanic (warding death-taint), set for the living rather than used in combat. The peach's Chinese lineage is a documented borrowing, yet the Kojiki presents it as an indigenous divine declaration — scholars flag this as a naturalised foreign motif, not a native institution.

Cosmogonic Creation Izanagi and Izanami together possess the power to create land and deities through ritual procreation. By stirring the primordial ocean and speaking the proper ritual words, they bring the Japanese archipelago into existence. After Izanami's death, Izanagi's creative power continues through purification, generating Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo from his own body. Kojiki, Nihon Shoki Creation requires the complementary female principle (Izanami); after her death, he creates only through purification (auto-generation), not through further procreation. His creative power is effectively exhausted after the birth of the Three Precious Children. The Nihon Shoki variants differ on what exactly Izanagi creates alone vs. with Izanami.

Ritual Speech (Kotodama) Izanagi's creative acts are explicitly tied to correct ritual speech. When he and Izanami first circle the pillar, Izanami speaks first, resulting in a malformed child (Hiruko, the Leech Child). Only when Izanagi (the male) speaks first does the ritual succeed, producing the islands. This establishes the Shinto principle that correctly spoken ritual words have world-shaping power. Kojiki The power of speech is tied to ritual context, hierarchy, and correctness. Incorrect speech produces deformity rather than creation. Some later commentaries de-emphasize the gender hierarchy of the pillar-circling ritual.

Death-Dealing / Divine Violence In grief over Izanami's death, Izanagi draws his Totsuka-no-Tsurugi and decapitates the infant fire god Kagutsuchi, whose birth killed his mother. This act of filicide produces eight new kami from the blood and body parts of the slain fire god, demonstrating that even violence, in the divine economy, generates new life. Kojiki This is a singular, grief-driven act, not a general power of combat or war. Izanagi is not a warrior god. Some Nihon Shoki variants soften or omit the decapitation, having Kagutsuchi merely 'cut' rather than killed.

Purification (Misogi / Harae) After fleeing the polluting realm of Yomi, Izanagi performs the first misogi (ritual purification) by bathing in a river, washing away the death-impurity from his body. Each item of clothing he removes and each body part he washes generates a new kami. This establishes Shinto purification as both cleansing and creative. Kojiki The purification must be performed in flowing water (river or sea), and certain impurities (like the Yomi pollution) require full-body immersion. Some traditions specify the river Tachibana-no-Odo in Himuka; others give different locations.

Sealing / Boundary-Making Izanagi seals the entrance to Yomi (the Even Pass of Yomi, Yomotsu Hirasaka) with a massive boulder that cannot be moved by human or divine force. This act irrevocably separates the realm of the living from the realm of the dead, establishing the fundamental boundary of Shinto cosmology. Kojiki The separation is absolute and permanent. Izanagi cannot undo it, nor can he visit Izanami again — the boundary he creates also imprisons him from his wife. Some folk traditions suggest the boulder is not completely impermeable, allowing spirits to cross under certain ritual conditions.

Behavior & Habitat

Embodies the Fool Arcana — the beginning of the journey, infinite potential. Yu's relationship with Izanagi reflects his growing acceptance of truth: initially a combat tool, by the end Izanagi-no-Okami represents enlightenment — seeing through the fog of lies. The Persona does not speak but acts through Yu, embodying the protagonist's will.

Weaknesses & Wards

Electricity specialization effective against early-game Shadows but needs fusion for later content. Izanagi-no-Okami requires completing the true ending path — confronting Izanami on the final day after the game seems to have ended.

Accounts

Standing on the Floating Bridge of Heaven, Izanagi and Izanami stir the formless, oil-like primordial ocean with the jeweled spear Ame-no-Nuboko. When they lift the spear, brine drips from its tip and congeals into the island Onogoro ('Self-Coagulating Island'). They descend to Onogoro, erect a heavenly pillar (Ame-no-Mihashira) and a spacious palace. They perform a marriage ritual: circling the pillar in opposite directions, Izanagi from the left and Izanami from the right. When they meet, Izanami speaks first ('What a beautiful youth!'), but this female-first speech produces a malformed child, Hiruko ('Leech Child'), whom they set adrift in a reed boat. Consulting the primordial kami, they learn the error and repeat the ritual with Izanagi speaking first ('What a beautiful maiden!'). This time the union is fruitful, and they proceed to create the eight main islands of Japan (the Great Eight-Island Country, Ōyashima) and numerous other islands.

After creating the islands, Izanagi and Izanami give birth to numerous kami — of the sea, wind, trees, mountains, plains, and other natural phenomena. However, when Izanami gives birth to Kagutsuchi (the fire god), her genitals are severely burned, and she falls deathly ill. From her vomit, urine, and feces, additional kami are born — metal-working gods and water gods. Izanami eventually dies and descends to Yomi-no-Kuni (the Land of Darkness). Izanagi, overcome with grief and rage, draws his Totsuka-no-Tsurugi and decapitates the infant Kagutsuchi. From the blood dripping from the sword onto the rocks, eight kami of thunder and mountains are born. From Kagutsuchi's dismembered body parts, more kami emerge — including mountain gods and the god of the vital breath.

Grieving and determined, Izanagi descends to Yomi-no-Kuni to retrieve Izanami. He finds her in the darkness and begs her to return, saying the creation of the land is not yet complete. Izanami replies that it is too late — she has already eaten the food of Yomi (the hearth of the underworld), which binds her there. She agrees to consult with the gods of Yomi but makes Izanagi promise not to look at her. He waits but grows impatient. Breaking off a tooth from his comb (kushi), he lights it as a torch and enters the palace. There he sees Izanami — her body rotting, infested with maggots, and surrounded by the eight Thunders of Yomi. Horrified, Izanagi flees. Izanami, enraged at the betrayal, sends the hags of Yomi (Yomotsu-Shikome), the eight Thunders, and finally herself in pursuit. Izanagi blocks the Even Pass of Yomi (Yomotsu Hirasaka) with a massive boulder — the Chigaeshi-no-Ōkami. Standing on opposite sides of the boulder, they exchange divorce vows. Izanami curses him, vowing to kill a thousand people per day; Izanagi counters that he will cause fifteen hundred to be born each day — thus establishing the eternal balance of death and birth.

After escaping Yomi, Izanagi declares: 'I have been to a most unpleasant, horrible, unclean land. I must purify myself.' He goes to a river mouth in Himuka (Hyūga, modern Miyazaki Prefecture) and performs the first misogi (purification ritual). As he removes his clothing and bathes, each discarded item and each body part he washes generates a new kami. When he washes his left eye, Amaterasu Ōmikami (the Sun Goddess) is born. From his right eye comes Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto (the Moon God). From his nose comes Takehaya-Susanoo-no-Mikoto (the Storm God). Izanagi rejoices, removing his magatama necklace and giving it to Amaterasu, assigning her rule over Takamagahara (the High Plain of Heaven). He assigns Tsukuyomi the night and Susanoo the sea.

Cultural Significance

The most narratively integrated initial Persona in the series — his mythological story directly parallels the game's plot. Themes of truth vs. fog, facing ugly realities, and the courage to pursue answers are encoded in the Izanagi/Izanami myth. Myriad Truths is the ultimate 'I win' button. Izanagi-no-Okami as DLC in Persona 5 lets players carry enlightenment between stories.

What We Don't Know

The pre-literate worship of Izanagi is entirely unknown — no archaeological remains definitively identify an Izanagi cult before the Kojiki. The relationship between the Izanagi-Izanami myth and earlier continental (Chinese/Korean) creator-pair myths needs more systematic comparative study. The emotional and psychological dimensions of the myth — particularly Izanagi's grief, horror, and eventual withdrawal — are underexplored in English-language scholarship, which tends to focus on the political and ritual dimensions. The variant accounts in the Nihon Shoki need more thorough comparative analysis to reconstruct regional traditions that may have been suppressed by the Kojiki's Yamato-centric synthesis. The role of kotodama (word-soul, the spiritual power of speech) in the creation myth — particularly why male-first speech is cosmologically correct — deserves deeper philosophical investigation. The transformation of Hiruko from abandoned child to Ebisu (Lucky God) is a fascinating medieval development that requires more research into the interaction between the Kojiki myth and folk religion.

A way to hold it in your mind

You created a world and then went to the underworld to retrieve your dead wife. You saw her rotting body and ran — because some truths are too horrible to face. But you returned, purified yourself, and from the water, new gods were born: the sun, the moon, the storm. In Persona 4, you're the power a boy awakens when he first accepts that the fog can be pierced. You are lightning. You are clarity. That's Izanagi: the god who learned the only thing worse than knowing the truth is living in the fog.
