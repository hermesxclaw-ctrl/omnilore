Who It Is

Izanagi is the initial Persona of Yu Narukami, protagonist of Persona 4 — a manifestation of the Fool Arcana representing infinite potential. A lightning-wielding warrior based on the Shinto creator god, Izanagi's arc mirrors the game's search for truth in a town shrouded in fog. His mythological connection to Izanami — and the revelation that the true antagonist IS a corrupted Izanami — makes this Persona the narrative center of Persona 4.

Origins

In the Kojiki (712 CE), Izanagi and Izanami stirred the primordial ocean with the jeweled spear Ame-no-Nuhoko. The brine dripping from the spear formed the first island. They created Japan's islands and numerous kami. Izanami died giving birth to Kagutsuchi (fire god). Izanagi descended into Yomi to retrieve her — but seeing her maggot-ridden body, fled in horror. Izanami pursued him until Izanagi sealed the entrance. He purified himself in a river, and from this ritual, Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo were born.

Form

Tall, imposing figure in flowing black coat with high collar — Japanese school uniform reimagined as divine armor. Metallic, featureless mask obscures face. Wields Ame-no-Nuhoko — the jeweled spear — as a massive glaive. Lightning arcs crackle from his body. Izanagi-no-Okami: radiant white and gold figure, flowing cape, serene divine face, mask removed — visual representation of enlightenment.

Powers

Zio skills (Zionga, Ziodyne, Maziodyne): Electricity attacks. Cross Slash: signature physical diagonal slash. Myriad Truths (Izanagi-no-Okami): ultimate Almighty attack — one of the most powerful skills in Persona 4. In Persona 5 DLC, Myriad Truths is arguably the most broken ability in the game.Land-creation (Kuniumi) via the heavenly spear Standing on the Floating Bridge of Heaven (Ame-no-ukihashi), Izanagi churns the primordial, jellyfish-like ocean with the jewelled spear Ame-no-Nuboko; the brine that drips from the spear's tip congeals into the first land, Onogoro Island. He and Izanami then descend, plant the heavenly pillar, and beget the rest of the Japanese archipelago (the Ōyashima and six further islands). Kojiki and Nihon Shoki (main accounts); ritual-fertility reading well established in scholarship (the penis-and-vagina churning of the sea is a phallic symbolism). Requires the tool (the spear) given by the older gods; the first attempt at the marriage rite fails and produces deformed offspring (Hiruko), showing that ritual protocol governs the power's success. Nihon Shoki has the couple volunteer to consolidate the earth and casts them as 'god of yang' (male) and 'goddess of yin' (female) under Chinese cosmology, omitting the Kotoamatsukami delegation and the last six islands.Deity-begetting (Kamiumi) through his own body After escaping Yomi, Izanagi cleanses himself (misogi) at the river-mouth in Himuka; deities spring from his discarded clothing, the water he splashes, and each immersion. Washing his left eye births the sun goddess Amaterasu-Ōmikami; washing his right eye births the moon deity Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; washing his nose births the storm god Susanoo-no-Mikoto — the 'Three Precious Children' (mihashira no uzu no miko). Kojiki (eye/nose misogi origin); a Nihon Shoki variant instead births them from two bronze mirrors held in his left and right hands plus a sideways glance. This is a reactive, purgative power: he only generates deities after being polluted by contact with the dead, and only while ritually washing away that kegare (pollution). Other Shoki versions make the Three Precious Children (and Hiruko, and even Susanoo) the joint offspring of Izanagi AND Izanami through procreation, not of Izanagi alone.Slaying of the fire god (Ame-no-ohabari sword) In grief and rage over the death of Izanami (burned by the newborn fire god Kagutsuchi's birth), Izanagi decapitates Kagutsuchi with his ten-grasp sword (Ame-no-ohabari; the 'heavenly' blade also called Ame-no-Habakiri), cutting the corpse into eight pieces that become eight volcanoes; more deities issue from the blood and remains. Kojiki; Nihon Shoki; Engishiki (which adds that in her death throes Izanami bears the water goddess Mizuhanome to quench the fire god). The slaying, while a 'power', is an act of retributive violence with vast collateral consequence (spawning volcanoes and a host of lesser kami) rather than a clean victory. Different texts catalogue the deities born from Kagutsuchi's corpse differently (Watatsumi, Kuraokami, Takemikazuchi, Futsunushi, Amatsu-Mikaboshi, Ōyamatsumi are among those commonly named).Descent into Yomi and the power to walk between life and death Izanagi descends to the land of the dead (Yomi) in a doomed attempt to retrieve Izanami, demonstrating a unique, if limited, ability of a living deity to cross into the underworld — and to return, by fleeing, deceiving pursuers with thrown vegetation, and finally sealing the entrance with a great boulder (Chigaeshi no Ōkami). Kojiki (full descent); the Nihon Shoki main narrative omits the descent and instead has Izanagi retire to Awaji. Death can be seen but not undone: because Izanami has eaten food cooked at the furnace of Yomi she cannot return; Izanagi's own return pollutes him and forces the misogi that births the high pantheon. The descent is central in Kojiki but absent entirely in the Shoki main text — a major internal inconsistency between the two canonical chronicles.Divine expulsion and world-division among the Three Precious Children Izanagi formally apportions the cosmos to his children (Amaterasu: Takamagahara/high heaven; Tsukuyomi: the night or the sea-tides depending on version; Susanoo: the seas), and — when the weeping Susanoo refuses his appointed task and begs to go to his mother's land Ne-no-Katasu-Kuni — expels him 'with a divine expulsion', the ritual banishing that becomes a template for punishment in the Age of the Gods. Kojiki (Amaterasu high heaven / Tsukuyomi night / Susanoo sea; banishment for weeping); Nihon Shoki variants vary the assignments and the trigger. His authority over the children is real but not absolute: Susanoo later returns, wrecks havoc in Takamagahara, and drives Amaterasu into the heavenly rock-cave — episodes in which Izanagi plays no further saving role. The realms each child is given differ text to text (Tsukuyomi: 'night' vs 'govern heaven alongside the sun' vs 'the eightfold tides of the sea plain'), and scholars suspect Susanoo was added to myths originally centered on Amaterasu and Tsukuyomi.Apotropaic command over the peach Fleeing Yomi, Izanagi throws three peaches at the hags of Yomi (Yomotsu-shikome) to drive them back, declares the peach divine, and bids it grow in the land of the living to aid people in need — instituting the peach as a sacred charm-fruit (a motif of Chinese origin, where peaches are apotropaic). Kojiki (the 'Great Divine Fruit' / Ōkamu-no-Mi); the peach's sacredness is further attested in Chinese Hualü tradition influencing Japan. Close-range obstruction, not a ranged weapon of destruction; its permanent value is talismanic (warding death-taint), set for the living rather than used in combat. The peach's Chinese lineage is a documented borrowing, yet the Kojiki presents it as an indigenous divine declaration — scholars flag this as a naturalised foreign motif, not a native institution.

Behavior & Habitat

Embodies the Fool Arcana — the beginning of the journey, infinite potential. Yu's relationship with Izanagi reflects his growing acceptance of truth: initially a combat tool, by the end Izanagi-no-Okami represents enlightenment — seeing through the fog of lies. The Persona does not speak but acts through Yu, embodying the protagonist's will.

Weaknesses & Wards

Electricity specialization effective against early-game Shadows but needs fusion for later content. Izanagi-no-Okami requires completing the true ending path — confronting Izanami on the final day after the game seems to have ended.

Accounts

From a silent cosmic egg, order condenses into the High Plain of Heaven (Takamagahara). Five lone, sexless Kotoamatsukami (Two-in-One lords of the center of heaven and generative forces) appear first, then the Kamiyonanayo — seven generations: first two lone deities (Kuni-no-Tokotachi, Toyokumono), then five brother-sister married pairs (Uhijini/Suhijini, Tsunugui/Ikugui, Ōtonoji/Ōtonobe, Omodaru/Ayakashikone, and finally Izanagi and Izanami). The older gods charge the youngest couple to solidify the drifting, oil-like earth.

With the jewel-tipped Ame-no-Nuboko, Izanagi churns the sea from the Floating Bridge of Heaven; the salt drips form Onogoro Island, their first home. They erect the heavenly pillar and palace (Yahiro-dono, the eight-hiro hall), then devise a marriage rite: circling the pillar in opposite directions and greeting one another. When Izanami speaks first, Izanagi objects (the wife must not speak first); their first union produces the deformed 'leech-child' Hiruko and the island of foam Awashima, set adrift unacknowledged. They consult the older gods, learn the protocol error, repeat the rite with Izanagi speaking first, and the union becomes blessed: the eight great islands (Ōyashima) and six further islands, then the deities, are born in order.

Izanami, giving birth to the fire deity Kagutsuchi (Hi-no-Kagutsuchi / Homusubi), is fatally burned. In grief and rage Izanagi beheads the newborn fire-god with his ten-grasp sword Ame-no-ohabari; Kagutsuchi's dismembered body becomes eight volcanoes and spawns dozens of deities, while further kami issue from Izanami's excreta, Kagutsuchi's blood, and Izanagi's tears. Roughly a dozen more gods appear as their firstborn/'death-throes' children. Izanami is buried on Mount Hiba at the border of Izumo and Hōki (in the Kojiki). The Engishiki records that Izanami, in dying, also births the water-goddess Mizuhanome to pacify the fire-god.

Refusing to accept Izanami's death, Izanagi descends to Yomi, the land of the dead, to retrieve her. She warns that she has already eaten food cooked at Yomi's furnace and cannot return. Breaking his promise not to look at her, Izanagi lights a torch from his comb and sees Izanami's body rotting, swarming with maggots. He flees in terror. She, in shame and fury, sends the thunder-gods (Yakusanoikazuchi), the hags of Yomi (Yomotsu-shikome), and a host to catch him. Izanagi throws his vine and right comb (which become grapes and bamboo shoots the hags pause to eat), then hurls three peaches to drive them off, and finally seals the mouth of Yomi's slope (Yomotsu Hirasaka) with a great boulder. Izanami vows to kill a thousand a day; Izanagi vows to beget fifteen hundred — mortality begins to be countered by fecundity.

Shaken by his pollution from Yomi, Izanagi purifies himself by bathing at 'the plain of Awagihara at the river-mouth of Tachibana in Himuka on Tsukushi'. As he removes his clothing and washes, countless kami are born. Washing his left eye births the sun-goddess Amaterasu-Ōmikami; his right eye births the moon-deity Tsukuyomi-no-Mikoto; his nose births the storm-god Susanoo-no-Mikoto — the Three Precious Children. This misogi is the mythological charter for the purification rituals (harae/temizuya) at Shinto shrines. Izanagi then divides the realms among the three.

Izanagi apportions Takamagahara to Amaterasu, the night to Tsukuyomi, and the seas to Susanoo. Susanoo, however, 'kept crying and howling until his beard eight hands long hung over his chest,' withering the mountains and drying the rivers. He begs to be allowed to go to his mother's land, Ne-no-Katasu-Kuni. Furious, Izanagi expels him 'with a divine expulsion,' and from this point Izanagi largely disappears from the myth — the narrative torch passes to Susanoo's later havoc in Takamagahara and his slaying of Yamata no Orochi.

Cultural Significance

The most narratively integrated initial Persona in the series — his mythological story directly parallels the game's plot. Themes of truth vs. fog, facing ugly realities, and the courage to pursue answers are encoded in the Izanagi/Izanami myth. Myriad Truths is the ultimate 'I win' button. Izanagi-no-Okami as DLC in Persona 5 lets players carry enlightenment between stories.

Modern Interpretations

In Persona 4 Golden, Izanagi-no-Okami is the true ending reward. In Persona 5 Royal DLC, returns as the most overpowered Persona. Copyright: Atlus / Sega Corporation.

A way to hold it in your mind

You created a world and then went to the underworld to retrieve your dead wife. You saw her rotting body and ran — because some truths are too horrible to face. But you returned, purified yourself, and from the water, new gods were born: the sun, the moon, the storm. In Persona 4, you're the power a boy awakens when he first accepts that the fog can be pierced. You are lightning. You are clarity. That's Izanagi: the god who learned the only thing worse than knowing the truth is living in the fog.
