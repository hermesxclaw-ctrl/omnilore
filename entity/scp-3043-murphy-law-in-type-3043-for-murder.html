Who It Is

A manual typewriter that compels anyone who sits before it to type a confession to a murder they did not commit, always in 1940s noir pastiche. Once complete, the real murderer — someone the typist knows — is compelled to turn themselves in. The typewriter enforces narrative justice: innocent confesses in fiction, guilty confesses in reality. The Foundation has used it to solve 14 cold cases.

Form

A 1942 Underwood typewriter, black enamel, slightly worn. The keys are warm to the touch. When in use, the room smells faintly of cigarette smoke and cheap whiskey.

Powers

Compulsive typing of false confession

Style imposition (noir pastiche)

Real perpetrator confession compulsion

Cold case solving
