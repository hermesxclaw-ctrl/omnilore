Who It Is

Before there were worlds, waters, or even light, Zuni storytellers say that only one presence existed—Awonawilona, the ‘All Container,’ a being so encompassing that everything that would ever be was already held within. From that solitary awareness came a thought that pushed outward into the dark, thickening into mist, then water, then a blazing sun whose light turned green scum on a primordial sea into Earth and Sky themselves. In this cosmology, creation is not a distant act by a human-shaped god but a slow unfolding of light and water from a consciousness that becomes the sun, births the world, and then steps back, letting twin war gods and womb-like worlds deliver humanity into daylight. Awonawilona is portrayed as a self-knowing, self-transforming, and generative principle. In Cushing’s account, the deity ‘conceived within himself and thought outward in space,’ suggesting introspective cognition and deliberate creation. Awonawilona’s behavior is characterized by orderly, purposeful cosmogenesis: evolving mists, becoming the Sun, generating waters, and forming Earth Mother and Sky Father from green scum on the sea. Powers
Cosmic creation and containment

Awonawilona is explicitly described as the ‘Maker and Container of All’ and ‘All Container,’ indicating the power to originate, encompass, and sustain the entire cosmos. This includes the ability to conceive creation within the self, to think outward into space, and to evolve mists, steams, waters, and the Sun. The deity’s containment is not merely spatial but ontological: all beings and worlds arise within and are held by Awonawilona.

Limit: No explicit limitations are stated in the myths; however, Awonawilona delegates specific functions to other beings (Earth Mother, Sky Father, Twin War Gods), suggesting a distributed cosmological order rather than direct micromanagement of all events.

Self-transformation into the Sun Father

Awonawilona conceives mists and steams and then ‘made himself in person and form of the Sun,’ becoming the Sun Father. This power of self-transformation establishes the deity as the source of light and solar vitality. Through this transformation, Awonawilona brightens the spaces, thickens mist-clouds, and initiates the evolution of the world-holding sea.

Limit: The transformation is presented as a singular, foundational act rather than a repeated or reversible ability; the myths do not describe Awonawilona shifting back and forth between forms in later narrative episodes.

Generation of waters and world-holding sea

By thinking outward and becoming the Sun, Awonawilona causes mist-clouds to thicken and fall, evolving ‘water in water’ and the world-holding sea. The deity’s power encompasses the creation and configuration of all forms of water—mists, steams, clouds, seas—which serve as the medium for further creation.

Limit: Some versions suggest that fog and steam pre-exist Awonawilona, which would limit the deity’s role to ordering and transforming rather than absolute creation of these elements.

Formation of Earth Mother and Sky Father from green scum

From the Sun Father’s substance of flesh drawn from his person, seed-stuff of twain worlds is formed and impregnates the great waters. In the heat of his light, the waters grow green and scums rise upon them, waxing wide and weighty until they become Earth Mother (Áwitelin Tsíta, ‘Four-fold Containing Mother-earth’) and Sky Father (Ápoyan Tä'chu, ‘All-covering Father-sky’). Awonawilona, as Sun Father, thus possesses the power to generate major cosmic beings from the interaction of light and water.

Limit: Once Earth Mother and Sky Father are formed, they act with considerable autonomy, conceiving terrestrial life and organizing the world; Awonawilona’s direct creative role shifts to a more foundational, background function.

Conception of terrestrial life in the four-fold womb of the world

From the lying together of Earth Mother and Sky Father upon the great world-waters, terrestrial life—men and creatures—is conceived in the four-fold womb of the world. While Earth Mother and Sky Father are the immediate parents, Awonawilona’s prior actions (becoming the Sun, forming waters, generating these beings) make the deity the ultimate source of terrestrial life.

Limit: The myths attribute gestation and birth to Earth Mother and Sky Father; Awonawilona’s role is more remote at this stage, emphasizing cosmic hierarchy.

Sending or empowering the Twin War Gods (Twain Deliverers)

In Cushing’s narrative, the Twin Children of the Sun (Ko'wituma and Wats'usi) are endowed with sacred knowledge, caps, bows, arrows, and shields to have dominion over men and creatures and to deliver them from the underworlds. In Benedict’s emergence tale and modern summaries, Awonawilona (as Sun and creator) sends the twin gods of war (Ahayuta) to bring people from the womb-like darkness into the light and to establish ritual relations (offerings and prayer sticks). Awonawilona thus exercises power through delegated agents, shaping human emergence and social order.

Limit: The twins act with their own agency and sacred equipment; Awonawilona’s involvement is primarily commissioning and empowering rather than direct physical intervention.

Essence of life and vegetative growth

Modern summaries note that the combination of light, clouds, and air created by Awonawilona allows for vegetation and makes Awonawilona the ‘essence of life.’ This power is less a discrete miracle than a continuous function: Awonawilona’s solar and atmospheric actions sustain plant growth and, by extension, all living beings.

Limit: The myths do not describe specific interventions in agriculture or plant species; the association is cosmological rather than agrarian in detail.

Accounts I
The genesis of the worlds, or the beginning of newnessFrank Hamilton Cushing, Outlines of Zuñi Creation Myths, Thirteenth Annual Report of the Bureau of Ethnology, 1896.

Recorded 1879–1881; published 1896 (reflecting late-19th-century Zuni oral tradition as mediated by an outsider ethnographer).

Before the beginning of new-making, Áwonawílona alone has being in a vast darkness and desolation. Conceiving within himself and thinking outward in space, he evolves mists and steams potent of growth. By innate knowledge, he makes himself in person and form of the Sun, the Sun Father, whose appearance brightens the spaces. The mist-clouds thicken and fall, evolving water and the world-holding sea. From the Sun Father’s substance, seed-stuff of twain worlds impregnates the waters; in the heat of his light, the waters grow green and scums rise, becoming Earth Mother (Áwitelin Tsíta) and Sky Father (Ápoyan Tä'chu). From their lying together upon the waters, terrestrial life is conceived in the four-fold womb of the world. This story establishes Awonawilona as the sole pre-existent being, the self-transforming Sun, and the ultimate source of Earth, Sky, and life. The cosmos is created, with light, waters, Earth Mother, Sky Father, and the four-fold womb of the world. The genesis of men and the creaturesFrank Hamilton Cushing, Outlines of Zuñi Creation Myths, 1896.

Recorded 1879–1881; published 1896.

From the lying together of Earth Mother and Sky Father upon the great world-waters, terrestrial life—men and creatures—is conceived in the four-fold womb of the world. Earth Mother, foreboding for her first-born, withholds birth and seeks counsel with Sky Father on how their children will know places by the light of the Sun Father. Through their changeable, mask-like manifestations, they envision terraced mountains and world-countries that will differentiate space. Eventually, the foremost of men and creatures are brought forth from the earth, and the Twain Deliverers (Twin War Gods) are born from the sea. These twins, endowed with sacred knowledge and weapons, deliver men and creatures through successive cave-worlds (Underworld, Moss World, Mud World, Wing World) into the Daylight World, where they learn the ways of life and seek the Sun Father. Humans and creatures emerge into the Daylight World, acquire knowledge, clothing, and social organization, and establish relations with the Sun Father and other divine beings. Accounts II
The Emergence (people brought from the fourth world to the daylight world)Ruth Benedict, The Emergence and Other Kachina Tales.

Early 20th century (fieldwork and publication).

People dwell crowded together in total darkness in the fourth world, deep in the earth. The daylight world above has hills and streams but no people to live there or to present prayer sticks to Awonawilona, the Sun and creator. Awonawilona takes pity on the people, and his two sons are stirred to lead them to the daylight world. The sons, with human features, locate the opening to the fourth world in the southwest and pass through progressively dimming first, second, and third worlds before reaching the overcrowded fourth world. The people, blinded by darkness, identify the brothers by touch and call them bow priests. The brothers lead the people upward, eventually bringing them into the daylight world, where they can live properly and offer prayers and ritual objects to Awonawilona. Humans are relocated from a cramped, dark existence to the daylight world, establishing a proper ritual relationship with Awonawilona through offerings and prayer sticks. The story reinforces Awonawilona’s compassion and the importance of ritual reciprocity.

Poshaiyankya and the four-fold womb of Awitelin TsitaModern summaries based on Cushing and later ethnographic retellings.

Late 19th–20th century.

In one version of the Zuni creation story, the creature Poshaiyankya escapes from the four-fold womb of Awitelin Tsita (Earth Mother) and convinces the Sun Father, Awonawilona, to send the Ahayuta, the twin gods of war, to bring the rest of the womb dwellers into the light. The twins act as deliverers, guiding beings from the enclosed womb-world into the open daylight world. In other versions, Awonawilona himself sends the twin gods of war to bring forth people who will give him offerings and prayers, emphasizing the deity’s desire for ritual reciprocity. The escape of Poshaiyankya and the subsequent mission of the twin gods of war result in the emergence of beings from the womb-world into the daylight world, reinforcing the motif of deliverance and the establishment of ritual relations with Awonawilona.
