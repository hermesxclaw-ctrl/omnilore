Who It Is

Before the first mind conceived of time, before the gods stirred in the dark between stars, the aboleths swam in oceans older than memory. They are the perfect memory of evil, the eldest rememberers, creatures whose vast intellects have catalogued every secret, every sin, and every forgotten horror of the multiverse. To encounter an aboleth is to face a mind that has outlived empires and will outlive yours, a being that does not merely hate you but remembers the exact moment your ancestors first crawled from the slime—and has been planning your extinction ever since.

Aboleths are highly intelligent, scheming, and utterly malevolent. They are obsessed with the past, particularly their own lost empire, and seek to reclaim their former glory by enslaving other races. They are patient and calculating, preferring to manipulate events from the shadows rather than engage in direct confrontation. They have no concept of morality as understood by humanoids, viewing all other beings as either tools or threats. They are also deeply xenophobic, hating all non-aboleth life, and they remember every slight, real or imagined, for millennia.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'aboleth' is a neologism created by Dungeons & Dragons co-creator Gary Gygax, likely derived from the Greek 'aboleth' (meaning 'destroyer' or 'one who obliterates'), though no direct linguistic source is confirmed. It may also be a play on 'abolish' or 'abyss'.

Pronunciation — AB-oh-leth

Aliases — The Eldest Rememberer, The Mind Before Minds, The Perfect Memory of Evil

Ownership — Dungeons & Dragons (TSR, Inc. / Wizards of the Coast). The aboleth is an original creation of Gary Gygax, first appearing in the 1st edition Fiend Folio (1981).

Powers
Enslave

Once per day, an aboleth can attempt to enslave a creature within 30 feet. The target must succeed on a Will save or be permanently enslaved, becoming a loyal servant. The enslavement can be broken by a dispel magic spell, but otherwise lasts until the aboleth dies or releases the target.

Limit: The target must be able to understand the aboleth's telepathic commands. The aboleth can only have a limited number of enslaved creatures at once (typically 1 per Hit Die).

Mucus Cloud

An aboleth is surrounded by a 1-foot-radius cloud of mucus. Any creature that comes into contact with it must save or begin to suffocate, as the mucus coats their gills or lungs. The affected creature must make a new save each round to avoid taking damage.

Limit: The cloud is ineffective against creatures that do not breathe, such as undead or constructs.

Psychic Memory Drain

An aboleth can touch a creature and drain its memories, causing the target to lose all knowledge of the past 24 hours. This ability is used to erase its own existence from the victim's mind, making the aboleth virtually untraceable.

Limit: The target must be touched, and the effect can be reversed by a greater restoration spell.

Phantasmal Attack

An aboleth can project a phantasmal image of itself, which can attack a creature's mind, causing it to believe it is being attacked by the aboleth. This can cause the victim to become frightened or even die of fright.

Limit: The phantasm can be disbelieved if the victim succeeds on a Will save.

Telepathy

Aboleths can communicate telepathically with any creature within 100 feet that has a language.

Limit: The target must have a language; creatures without language are immune.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The aboleth is a massive, eel-like amphibian, typically 20 to 30 feet long, with a slimy, scaly body that shimmers with iridescent hues. It has three glowing, expressive eyes set in a fish-like head, and three long tentacles on each side of its body, which are used for manipulation and combat. Its mouth is filled with rows of sharp teeth, and it constantly exudes a thick, mucus-like slime that is both protective and dangerous.

Symbols — Aboleths are often depicted in D&D art as ancient, malevolent sea gods, with stylized tentacles and glowing eyes. They are associated with the sea, memory, and psychic domination. In-game, they are sometimes worshiped by aquatic races, and their lairs are often adorned with murals depicting their ancient history.

Domains — Aboleths dwell in the deepest oceans and underground seas, often in sunken ruins or vast caverns. They are common in the Underdark, particularly in areas with large bodies of water. They are also found on the Elemental Plane of Water and, in some settings, in the depths of the Astral Sea. They build sprawling cities, often enslaving aquatic races to serve them.

Pre-history of the D&D multiverse

In the dawn of time, the aboleths were the dominant race of the prime material plane, ruling vast empires beneath the seas. They were the first intelligent beings to achieve civilization, and they built cities that rivaled the gods themselves. However, their hubris led them to challenge the gods, and in a cataclysmic war, they were overthrown and their empire shattered. Since then, they have been plotting to regain their former glory, but they are now a shadow of their former selves, lurking in the deep places of the world. The aboleths were cast down and their civilization destroyed. They now harbor an eternal hatred for the gods and all other races, and they seek to reclaim their lost power.

The Aboleth That Ate a GodLords of Madness (2005)

Unknown, possibly ancient

One aboleth, known as the 'God-Eater,' is said to have consumed a minor deity. This act gave the aboleth immense power, but also cursed it with a fragment of the god's divine essence, driving it mad. The God-Eater now dwells in a sunken temple, guarded by its insane followers. The God-Eater gained god-like powers but became insane. Its cult seeks to feed it more gods, hoping to restore its sanity or bring about a new age of aboleth dominance.

Accounts II
The Aboleth of the Sunless SeaDungeon Magazine #72 (1999), adventure 'The Sunless Sea'

Contemporary

In this adventure, a group of adventurers discovers an aboleth that has established a lair in an underground sea. The aboleth has enslaved a tribe of kuo-toa and is using them to build a temple to an ancient evil. The adventurers must infiltrate the temple and defeat the aboleth before it can complete its dark ritual. The adventurers defeat the aboleth and free the kuo-toa, but they learn that the aboleth was only a servant of a more powerful entity.

The Aboleth's BargainDragon Magazine #126 (1987)

Contemporary

A powerful aboleth named S'vadra makes a deal with a human wizard, offering him forbidden knowledge in exchange for his service. The wizard becomes S'vadra's agent, but he eventually betrays the aboleth, leading to a deadly confrontation. The wizard is ultimately destroyed, but he manages to weaken S'vadra, allowing a group of adventurers to finish her off. S'vadra is killed, and the wizard's soul is trapped in an artifact. The wizard's betrayal shows that even aboleth plans can fail.

The Aboleth of the Deep (video game)Dungeons & Dragons: Eye of the Beholder II (1991)

1991

In this video game, an aboleth named 'The Deep' is one of the major antagonists. It has corrupted a group of drow and is using them to open a portal to the Abyss. The player must descend into the Underdark and defeat the aboleth to close the portal. The player defeats the aboleth and closes the portal, saving the surface world from an invasion of demons.
