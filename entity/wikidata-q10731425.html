Who It Is

In the shadowed margins of Japanese divine genealogy, where the great celestial and terrestrial deities take their places in the chronicles of the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, there stands a figure whose very name evokes the mystery of the sacred vessel: Tori no Iwakusufune no Kami, the kami of the bird-like rock boat. Little known beyond the oldest textual layers, this deity embodies the ancient Japanese imagination of divine conveyance—a vessel hewn not of wood but of primordial stone, guided by the wings of birds across the waters of creation.
