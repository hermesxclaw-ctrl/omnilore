Who It Is

In the deep forests of medieval Poitou, a mysterious woman rose from a sacred spring and promised a grieving count wealth, power, and a dynasty that would rule kingdoms. But her price was absolute: one day each week, she must be left utterly alone. For ten years, Raymondin kept his vow, and Melusine built castles, bore him sons, and made his name feared across France—until curiosity shattered their pact, and the fairy queen transformed into a winged dragon, vanishing into the night, leaving behind a legacy of haunted towers and royal bloodlines forever marked by her curse.

Melusine is intelligent, resourceful, and powerful. She is a builder and a benefactor, using her magic to construct castles and grant wealth. She is bound by oaths and punishes betrayal severely. She is also a devoted mother, returning to nurse her infants after her departure. She is both a bringer of prosperity and a harbinger of doom for her descendants.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The French Dictionnaire de la langue française suggests Latin 'melus' meaning 'melodious, pleasant'. Another theory connects the name to a Poitevin legend of 'Mère Lusine', a fairy leader who built Roman structures.

Pronunciation — French: [melyzin]

Aliases — Melusina, Melusine, Mère Lusine, Merlusse, Merluisaine

Ownership — Primarily French (Poitevin and Breton), also claimed by Luxembourg and the Low Countries. The legend is associated with the noble houses of Lusignan, Luxembourg, and Plantagenet.

Powers
Shapeshifting

Melusine can transform from a human woman to a half-serpent or half-fish form, and eventually into a dragon. This transformation is often tied to her curse or her own choice.

Limit: On Saturdays, she must retain her serpent form; if her husband sees her, the transformation becomes permanent.

Supernatural Construction

She can magically build castles, fortresses, and other structures overnight. She is credited with building the Castle of Lusignan and the Castle of Luxembourg.

Limit: The construction is tied to her oath and her presence; if the oath is broken, she can no longer continue.

Prophecy and Omen

After her departure, she appears as a harbinger of death or change for her descendants, wailing around the castle before a death or a change of ownership.

Limit: She appears only in spirit form, not physically.

Magic Rings

Before leaving, she gives Raymondin two magic rings that protect him and ensure his prosperity, as long as they are not used to harm others.

Limit: The rings lose their power if misused.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Melusine is typically depicted as a beautiful woman from the waist up, but from the waist down she has a serpent's or fish's tail (often two tails). Some illustrations show wings, and in her dragon form she has bat-like wings and a long tail. In the bathing scene, she is described as half-serpent.

Symbols — Two-tailed mermaid or serpent, often with wings; associated with wells, springs, and water; her image appears in heraldry of the Lusignan family and on the Luxembourg coat of arms. Sometimes depicted with a mirror or comb, typical of mermaids.

Domains — Fresh water: holy wells, springs, rivers. She is associated with the forest of Coulombiers near Poitiers, and later with the castle of Lusignan. In Luxembourg, she is linked to the Bock rock and the Alzette river.

Accounts I
The Tale of Melusine and RaymondinJean d'Arras

14th century

After accidentally killing his uncle, Count Raymondin meets Melusine at a fountain. They marry, with the condition that he never see her on Saturdays. She bears him ten sons, many deformed, and builds castles. After ten years, Raymondin breaks his vow, sees her in serpent form, and later publicly calls her a serpent after their son commits murder. She transforms into a dragon, gives him magic rings, and flies away, but returns at night to nurse her infants. Melusine departs, the Lusignan line is cursed with misfortune, and she appears as a harbinger of death. Raymondin is left with grief and guilt.

Accounts II
The Origin of Melusine and Her SistersJean d'Arras

14th century

Pressine, a fairy, marries King Elinas of Albany on condition he never see her during childbirth. He breaks the vow, and Pressine leaves with her triplet daughters, Melusine, Melior, and Palatine. They grow up on Avalon. On their fifteenth birthday, Melusine learns of her father's broken promise and, with her sisters, locks him in a mountain. Pressine punishes them: Palatine is imprisoned, Melior is sealed in a castle, and Melusine is cursed to become a serpent every Saturday unless a husband respects her privacy. Melusine's curse is established, setting the stage for her own story.

The Luxembourg MelusineLocal legend

Medieval

Count Siegfried of the Ardennes buys the land where he founds Luxembourg. He marries Melusine, who builds the castle on the Bock rock. She demands one day of privacy each week. Siegfried spies on her and sees she is a mermaid. She sinks into the earth, and she is said to return every seven years as a woman or a serpent. Melusine becomes trapped in the rock, and the Luxembourg dynasty claims descent from her.

Melusine is a mermaid. — She is more commonly depicted as a serpent-woman, though mermaid versions exist, especially in Luxembourg.

Melusine is a demon or succubus. — She is a fairy or water spirit, not a demon. The demonic association is a later Christian interpretation.

Melusine was a historical person. — She is a legendary figure, not historical. The noble houses claimed descent for prestige.
