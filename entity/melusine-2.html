Who It Is

Melusine is a legendary fairy-woman of French folklore, most famously associated with the noble House of Lusignan. According to the romance by Jean d'Arras, Melusine was the daughter of the fairy Pressina and King Elinas of Albany. When Elinas broke a promise not to witness Pressina's childbirth, Pressina vanished, and Melusine and her sisters were raised in secrecy on the lost Isle of Avalon. After imprisoning their father for his betrayal, Melusine was cursed by her mother to transform into a half-serpent creature every Saturday — a secret she must keep from any mortal husband. Melusine later met Count Raymond of Poitou in a forest. They married, and she built the magnificent Castle of Lusignan through supernatural means, using stones carried by spirits. Her only condition was that Raymond must never see her on a Saturday. She bore him ten sons, each marked by some strange feature — oversized ears, a single eye, or a lion's claw. When Raymond, goaded by his brother, spied upon her through a door on a Saturday, he saw her bathing in a tub with her lower half transformed into a great serpent's tail. Melusine's secret exposed, she transformed fully into a winged serpent and flew from Lusignan Castle, cursed to roam the night until Judgment Day. Her descendants became the powerful Lusignan dynasty, which ruled Cyprus and Jerusalem. Melusine is considered a foundational figure for the concept of the mermaid in later folklore.
