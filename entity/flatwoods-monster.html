Who It Is

The Flatwoods Monster is one of the most distinctive and eerie cryptid encounters in American history. On the evening of September 12, 1952, a group of seven people -- including two adults and five children -- witnessed a fiery object streak across the sky and apparently crash-land on a hilltop farm near Flatwoods, West Virginia. Climbing the hill to investigate, they encountered a pulsating red light and a terrifying entity: a 10-foot-tall figure with a bright red face, glowing green eyes, and a body that appeared to be clad in dark, metallic clothing resembling a mechanical skirt or 'ace of spades' shape. The creature emitted a hissing, shrieking sound and a thick, pungent mist that caused burning sensations in the witnesses' eyes and throats. The group fled in terror. Subsequent investigation by local authorities and the Air Force (as part of Project Blue Book) found no physical evidence, though skid marks and an oily residue were reported at the site. The official explanation attributed the sighting to a meteor and the 'monster' to a barn owl perched on a tree branch. However, witnesses -- especially the children, who later in life maintained their accounts -- insisted what they saw was no owl. The Flatwoods Monster has become one of West Virginia's most famous cultural icons, inspiring a museum, an annual festival, and appearances in popular media.
