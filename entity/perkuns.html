Who It Is

Perkūns (Lithuanian Perkūnas) is the Baltic thunder god and one of the most prominent and active figures in the pantheon. He is the wielder of the thunderbolt, riding a two-wheeled chariot drawn by fiery horses, or riding the thundercloud itself. Perkūns is the enforcer of cosmic justice — he hunts down Velnias (the devil), strikes evil spirits, and purifies the earth with lightning. His symbols are the oak tree (sacred to him), the axe, and the hammer. The strike of lightning was sacred ground: a place struck by Perkūns was considered a holy boundary that could not be crossed. In Lithuanian tradition, the 'stone of Perkūnas' — a thunderstone (neolithic axe head) — was a powerful protective amulet kept in homes to ward off evil and disease. Perkūns also has a dual role as a fertility god: his rains bring growth, and his thunder drives away the sterile forces of winter. He is in constant conflict with Velnias, who steals cattle and hoards the waters. In Christian syncretism, Perkūns was sometimes identified with the prophet Elijah (which gave him a reprieve from demonization). The Lithuanian month of June is named birželis (from beržas, birch), associated with Perkūnas's season; the Latvian month of the same period is liepa (linden), when thunderstorms are most frequent.

Attested in Latvian dainas; Lithuanian chronicles (Malala, Daukša)Era Pre-Christian (Bronze Age through medieval Baltic paganism)
