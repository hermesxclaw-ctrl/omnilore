Identity

Taoroinai

Pronunciation Uncertain; likely /tao-roi-nai/ with stress on the first syllable.

Etymology

The name 'Taoroinai' is of Meitei origin, but its precise meaning is uncertain. It may be composed of elements related to 'tao' (possibly 'python' or 'serpent') and 'roinai' (possibly 'coil' or 'encircle'). Without access to linguistic resources, this remains speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Meitei (Manipuri) culture, Northeast India

Disputed Identifications

Some sources may conflate Taoroinai with other serpent deities in Meitei mythology, such as Pakhangba, the dragon-god. The relationship between these figures is not clearly defined in available summaries.

Description

Appearance

Taoroinai is described as a colossal python or serpent, often depicted as a cosmic entity whose body encircles the earth or the universe. Its scales are said to be the rocks and soil, and its movements cause earthquakes.

Symbols & Iconography

In Meitei art and ritual, serpents symbolize cosmic power, fertility, and the underworld. Taoroinai may be depicted in temple carvings or ritual objects, but specific iconography is not documented in available sources.

Behaviour & Personality

As a cosmic serpent, Taoroinai is likely a primordial being, existing before creation and participating in the ordering of the cosmos. It may be both benevolent and destructive, depending on the myth.

Domains & Habitats

Taoroinai is associated with the earth, the underworld, and the waters. It may live in the cosmic ocean or beneath the earth, coiling around the world.
