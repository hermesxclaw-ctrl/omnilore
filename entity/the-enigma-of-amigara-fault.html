Who It Is

The Enigma of Amigara Fault is a manga by Japanese horror master Junji Ito, later adopted into the creepypasta canon through internet discussion and adaptation. The story concerns a mysterious fault line that appears after an earthquake on Mount Amigara, exposing a cliff face covered in thousands of human-shaped holes. Each hole is a unique silhouette — a specific person's exact outline. People from across Japan are inexplicably drawn to the fault, each finding 'their' hole — a silhouette that perfectly matches their body. The compulsion to enter their hole is irresistible. Those who enter cannot be retrieved, and the holes wind deep into the mountain, stretching and deforming the human body in impossible ways. At the story's climax, a character emerges from the other side of the mountain — but the process of passing through the fault has stretched them into a grotesque, serpentine form, their body elongated over hundreds of meters, their face frozen in an expression of indescribable suffering. The final panel, showing the distorted, elongated creature emerging from a hole on the far side of the mountain with the words 'This is my hole!' is one of the most famous images in horror manga. The story functions as an allegory for fatalism, the compulsion toward self-destruction, and the horror of destiny — the idea that there is a shape cut into the universe specifically for you, and no matter how terrible it is, you will eventually enter it. 'The Enigma of Amigara Fault' has been widely translated, memed, and referenced across the internet, and its iconic phrase 'This hole was made for me' has entered the lexicon of internet horror as shorthand for inescapable, self-destructive compulsion.

Form

The story is set in a stark, realistic Japanese mountain landscape. The central visual is a massive, sheer rock face of the Amigara Fault, riddled with thousands of human-shaped holes. The holes are perfectly smooth, dark, and each is a unique silhouette of a specific person. The protagonists are ordinary people in casual clothing, drawn in Ito's distinctive, clean-yet-unsettling style. The horror escalates as characters are seen contorting their bodies to fit into the holes, with grimaces of pain and obsession on their faces.

Powers

Psychological Compulsion The fault emits a field that creates an obsessive desire in a person to find and enter their specific hole. This compulsion is so strong that it overrides fear, logic, and social bonds. Manga The compulsion only affects people whose hole exists on the fault. It seems to be a deeply personal, individual call. None, the compulsion is consistent and absolute within the story.

Physical Transformation The holes are not just cavities; they actively reshape the bodies of those who enter, contorting them into new, elongated, and alien forms. Manga The process is irreversible and destructive to the person's original form. The end result is unknown, but implied to be a loss of humanity. None, the transformation is shown to be horrifying and permanent.

Precognitive Matching The holes are perfectly tailored to the exact dimensions of a specific individual, indicating a foreknowledge of the person and their body. Manga This power is passive; the fault does not actively seek people out, it simply presents the matching hole when they arrive. The source of this knowledge is never explained, adding to the cosmic horror.

Behavior & Habitat

The primary 'antagonist' is the fault itself, which exerts a powerful, inexplicable psychological compulsion on people to enter their corresponding hole. This compulsion is irresistible, overriding self-preservation and rational thought. The holes are also physically transforming, reshaping the people who enter them into a new, unknown form.

Incident Log

After a massive earthquake, a fault line is exposed on a mountain, revealing thousands of human-shaped holes. People flock to the site in morbid curiosity. The protagonist, Owaki, and a woman he meets, Yoshida, discover that there are holes matching their exact silhouettes. They are horrified, but feel an overwhelming pull to enter. Despite their resistance, they eventually give in. Owaki enters his hole and is contorted and squeezed into the rock. Yoshida follows, and the story ends with them seeing a strange, new world and realizing they are being transformed into long, worm-like creatures, crawling endlessly towards an unknown destination. They see others who have been there longer, now completely alien, and realize they will never return to their human forms.

Although Amigara Fault is a short manga by Junji Ito, its most famous panel—a man pressing himself against the mouth of his hole—and the line 'This is the hole for me!' detached from the original context and traveled across 4chan, Reddit, Tumblr, and later TikTok and YouTube. The image became shorthand online for a feeling of being personally 'called' by something inevitable or doomed, often deployed humorously. This second life turned a niche horror chapter into one of the most recognizable images in internet horror, sometimes circulating as a standalone creepypasta with the manga's provenance stripped away.

The Meme That Outlived the Manga

Although Amigara Fault is a short manga by Junji Ito, its most famous panel—a man pressing himself against the mouth of his hole—and the line 'This is the hole for me!' detached from the original context and traveled across 4chan, Reddit, Tumblr, and later TikTok and YouTube. The image became shorthand online for a feeling of being personally 'called' by something inevitable or doomed, often deployed humorously. This second life turned a niche horror chapter into one of the most recognizable images in internet horror, sometimes circulating as a standalone creepypasta with the manga's provenance stripped away.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of it as the ultimate 'destiny' argument taken to its horrifying extreme. It's not a prophecy you can try to avoid; it's a physical, personalized invitation you cannot refuse. The mountain isn't a villain with a plan; it's a force of nature that has simply decided that you, specifically, will become something else. It's the horror of having no choice, no agency, and no one to blame.
