Who It Is

The Enigma of Amigara Fault is a manga by Japanese horror master Junji Ito, later adopted into the creepypasta canon through internet discussion and adaptation. The story concerns a mysterious fault line that appears after an earthquake on Mount Amigara, exposing a cliff face covered in thousands of human-shaped holes. Each hole is a unique silhouette — a specific person's exact outline. People from across Japan are inexplicably drawn to the fault, each finding 'their' hole — a silhouette that perfectly matches their body. The compulsion to enter their hole is irresistible. Those who enter cannot be retrieved, and the holes wind deep into the mountain, stretching and deforming the human body in impossible ways. At the story's climax, a character emerges from the other side of the mountain — but the process of passing through the fault has stretched them into a grotesque, serpentine form, their body elongated over hundreds of meters, their face frozen in an expression of indescribable suffering. The final panel, showing the distorted, elongated creature emerging from a hole on the far side of the mountain with the words 'This is my hole!' is one of the most famous images in horror manga. The story functions as an allegory for fatalism, the compulsion toward self-destruction, and the horror of destiny — the idea that there is a shape cut into the universe specifically for you, and no matter how terrible it is, you will eventually enter it. 'The Enigma of Amigara Fault' has been widely translated, memed, and referenced across the internet, and its iconic phrase 'This hole was made for me' has entered the lexicon of internet horror as shorthand for inescapable, self-destructive compulsion.
