Who It Is

Atar is the yazata of fire — not fire as mere physical combustion, but as the radiant, purifying presence of Asha (cosmic truth/order) in the material world. In Zoroastrianism, fire is the 'son of Ahura Mazda' (the first and greatest creation of the Wise Lord), and the 'fire in the house' is the center of all ritual life. The five fires identified in the Bundahishn — the Berezisavah (fire before Ahura Mazda), the Vohufryana (fire in living bodies), the Urvazisht (fire in plants), the Vazisht (lightning), and the Spenishta (the fire that will purify at the end of time) — represent the ubiquity of Atar's presence across all creation. Atar judges the souls: at the Chinvat Bridge, the ordeal of molten metal will be a 'just fire' that burns the wicked but feels like warm milk to the righteous. No Zoroastrian ritual can be performed without the presence of Atar, who is tended by priests in the fire-temples (ateshgahs) that have burned continuously for centuries in Iran and India.
