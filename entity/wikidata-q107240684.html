The Approach

In the highlands of ancient Arcadia, where the mountains touched the sky, there was a town named Hypsus, and the Greeks said it was founded by a man of the same name, a son of the wolf-king Lycaon. But who was Hypsus? The ancient sources are silent on his deeds, leaving only a name carved into the landscape and a question that echoes through the ages.

Identity

Hypsus

Pronunciation Hip-soos (English approximation of the Greek Ὑψοῦς).

Known As

HypsousHypsuntusHypsountos

Etymology

The name Hypsus (Ὑψοῦς) is derived from the Greek word ὕψος (hypsos), meaning 'height' or 'summit', reflecting the town's location on a mountain.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Greek (Arcadia)

Disputed Identifications

There is no clear scholarly dispute over the identification of Hypsus as a character, but the town's name and its mythological founder are sometimes confused with other Hypsus figures in Greek mythology, such as a possible river god or a different eponym.

Description

Appearance

No physical description of Hypsus survives. As a mythological figure, he is presumed to have been a mortal man, possibly of heroic stature, but no sources provide details.

Behaviour & Personality

Domains & Habitats

Hypsus is associated with the town of Hypsus in Arcadia, which was located on a mountain of the same name, near the modern town of Stemnitsa.

Version Differences

William Smith, Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography

Type textDate 1854-1857Locator Entry on HypsusLanguage English

Translation

The entry states: 'Hypsus or Hypsous (Ὑψοῦς), also known as Hypsuntus or Hypsountos (Ὑψοῦντος), was a town of ancient Arcadia, in the district Cynuria, situated upon a mountain of the same name. According to Greek mythology, it was said to have been founded by Hypsus, a son of Lycaon.'
