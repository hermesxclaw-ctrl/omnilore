The Approach

He never hurries. While his partner Miss Merry Christmas shrieks and tunnels through solid rock, Mr. 4 lumbers forward, bat over his shoulder, speaking one syllable at a time — and then swings a four-ton iron bat so hard his exploding baseballs level palaces. In Eiichiro Oda's One Piece, the slowest talker in Baroque Works is also its heaviest hitter.

Content advisory. Cartoon violence, terrorism and attempted mass casualty (civil war manipulation), animal weaponization (dog-gun hybrid), comic beatings
Identity

Mr. 4

Pronunciation Japanese: Misutā Fō [mi.sɯ.taː ɸoː]; English dub: MISTER FOUR /ˈmɪstər fɔːr/

Known As

Misutā Fō (ミスター・フォー)Mr. FourMister 4

Etymology

Baroque Works codename system: male agents receive 'Mr.' + number, with lower numbers indicating higher rank and Officer Agent status. '4' places him as the fourth-highest male officer under Mr. 0 (Crocodile). The number is thematically tied to baseball: his primary weapon is a bat weighing 4 tons, his partner weapon fires exploding baseballs, and 'four-bagger' is slang for a home run. Oda's SBS and databooks confirm the baseball motif links to American baseball culture, though no in-universe etymology beyond Crocodile's numbering is given.

Cultural Ownership

Original fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda for One Piece, serialized by Shueisha in Weekly Shōnen Jump (Japan) since July 22, 1997. Copyright held by Eiichiro Oda / Shueisha. International manga licensing by Viz Media (English), anime adaptation by Toei Animation. No public-domain or traditional cultural ownership; stewardship is corporate/authorial.

Disputed Identifications

No canonical real name has been revealed in manga chapters 1-1115, SBS columns, Vivre Card databook, or One Piece Blue/ Yellow databooks. The name 'Babe' widely circulated on fan wikis and forums (derived from Babe Ruth baseball association) is fanon and unattested in primary sources. Must not be conflated with Lassoo (his dachshund-gun partner, sometimes mislisted as a separate Baroque Works agent) or with unrelated 'Mr. 4' characters in other franchises. Distinct from Mr. 4's anime voice actors vs manga depiction.

Description

Appearance

Mr. 4 is an exceptionally large, broad-shouldered man listed at 218 cm (7'2") in Vivre Card, with a soft, overweight build rather than muscular definition. He has pale skin, small round black sunglasses that hide his eyes, short dark brown hair with a center part, and a faint stubble. His standard outfit is a baseball-inspired uniform: a long-sleeved pale green button-up shirt with dark green pinstripes, a yellow tie, dark green pants, and a baseball cap rarely worn. He perpetually carries a massive black iron baseball bat stated to weigh 4 tons, dragging it or resting it on his shoulder. His most distinctive visual companion is Lassoo — a dachshund-shaped gun with a long snout, floppy ears, and a fuse-like tail that sneezes exploding baseballs. Attested in manga Vol. 19-21 and anime Ep. 103+. No Devil Fruit transformation alters his appearance.

Symbols & Iconography

Core motifs are baseball and the number 4. The 4-ton bat, baseballs, and pitching stance code him as a batter/pitcher hybrid. Lassoo's baseball-shaped explosive projectiles reinforce the theme; the dog-gun's sneezing mechanism parodies a pitcher releasing a ball. Baroque Works iconography: the cross-like Baroque Works insignia does not appear on his clothing, but his codename itself is the symbol of rank. Color palette (greens and yellows) aligns with Baroque Works Officer Agents' varied but coordinated designs by Oda. No traditional religious or folkloric symbols are associated.

Behaviour & Personality

Canonically laconic to the point of comic extreme. Mr. 4 speaks and moves at an exaggeratedly slow tempo — his dialogue is drawn with elongated pauses and his actions are deliberately delayed, contrasting sharply with his hyperactive partner Miss Merry Christmas, who constantly berates him for his slowness. He is obedient, stoic, and unflappable; he rarely shows anger, fear, or excitement, responding to both orders and insults with a slow '...yeah.' He is loyal to Crocodile's chain of command and cooperative with Miss Merry Christmas despite her abuse, showing no ambition to betray or lead. He is not depicted as cruel for its own sake but as a professional enforcer. His slowness is a gag that also functions as a combat trait: enemies underestimate his swing speed until the bat connects.

Domains & Habitats

Primary domain is Baroque Works as a clandestine criminal organization operating in the Paradise half of the Grand Line, with headquarters at Rain Dinners in Rainbase, Alabasta. His operational habitat during the Alabasta Saga is the Alabasta Kingdom desert — specifically Spiders Cafe (oasis meeting point), the desert transit to Alubarna, and the Royal Palace grounds where the final battle occurs. Post-defeat, his domain shifts to Marine captivity (shown in Ch. 211) and later to the reformed Baroque Works hideout on Kyuka Island in the Miss Goldenweek cover story (a non-canonical but official Oda-drawn serial). He has no known hometown, ship, or territory outside Baroque Works assignments.

Version Differences

Manga (primary canon): Minimal dialogue, slow speech rendered via elongated katakana and panel timing; Lassoo's mechanics explained in a single exposition box. Anime (Toei): Adds filler dialogue, extended sneezing sound effects for Lassoo, and a slightly deeper, more drawn-out vocal performance by Masaya Takatsuka; battle in Ep. 114-116 is extended with additional dodges by Usopp/Chopper not in manga. English dub (Funimation): Scott Hinze performs the slow speech with Southern-tinged drawl; name consistently 'Mr. 4.' Live-action Netflix series (2023-present) has not yet adapted Alabasta; no live-action version exists as of 2026. Video games (Pirate Warriors 4, Grand Battle) simplify his moveset to bat swings and Lassoo shots, sometimes increasing his movement speed for gameplay balance — an adaptation-specific change. Vivre Card adds height/weight stats not stated in manga dialogue.

Powers & Abilities

4-Ton Baseball Bat Swing

Source VersionManga Ch. 184-188, Vivre Card #0514; Anime Ep. 114-116

Wields a black iron baseball bat explicitly stated to weigh 4 tons (4t batto). Despite its weight, Mr. 4 swings it with one hand at high velocity, generating enormous blunt-force impact capable of launching opponents and shattering stone. He uses it to bat Lassoo's exploding baseballs like a batter, combining pitching and hitting into a single artillery system.

Limitations

Requires Lassoo to supply ammunition; bat itself is not explosive. Swing is powerful but telegraphed — Usopp is able to predict trajectory once he understands the baseball mechanic. Slow movement means he is vulnerable if disarmed or if Lassoo is incapacitated. No ranged attack without Lassoo.

Contradictions

No contradiction on weight; databooks and manga agree on 4 tons. Some fan translations rendered it as '4-ton bat' vs '4000 kg bat' — same value.

Lassoo Artillery System (Inu Inu no Mi, Model: Dachshund Gun-Dog)

Source VersionManga Ch. 184-185, Vivre Card #0515; SBS Vol. 25 explanation of object Devil Fruits

Lassoo is a bazooka/gun that ate the Inu Inu no Mi, Model: Dachshund, becoming a living dog-gun. Lassoo sneezes or coughs up heavy baseballs that are timed explosives; Mr. 4 then bats them at targets. The balls explode on impact or after a short fuse, with blast radius sufficient to destroy palace walls. Lassoo can also move independently, bite, and bark ('wan!'). The Devil Fruit was fed to the gun via Oda's established weapon-fruit technology (also used for Funkfreed and Smiley).

Limitations

Lassoo has canine instincts and low intelligence; can be confused, frightened, or distracted (e.g., by Chopper's tricks). Explosive baseballs have a timed fuse — Usopp exploits this by using a dial-like timing. Lassoo must be loaded/fed; cannot fire indefinitely without Mr. 4's batting. As a living gun, Lassoo is vulnerable to blunt damage.

Contradictions

None in canon; anime adds extra sneezing fits for comedy but does not alter mechanics. Fan misconception that Mr. 4 himself ate a Devil Fruit is false — he is explicitly non-Devil Fruit user.

Superhuman Strength and Durability

Source VersionManga Ch. 187-188

Even without a Devil Fruit, Mr. 4 displays superhuman physical strength to lift and swing the 4-ton bat casually and superhuman durability to withstand Usopp's attacks and Chopper's Horn Point strikes before being defeated by a combined Usopp-Chopper finisher (Chopper's Heavy Gong + Usopp's hammer). He continues fighting after taking direct hits that would incapacitate normal humans.

Limitations

Not invulnerable; defeated by coordinated attack from two Straw Hats (Usopp's Usopp Hammer and Chopper's attack that sends him flying). Slow reflexes mean he cannot easily evade agile opponents. No Haki is ever shown or stated.

Contradictions

Power scaling debates: Some fans argue his strength should rank higher given bat weight; Oda never assigns a Doriki or bounty, so comparative strength remains unquantified.

Slow-Tempo Combat Style (Tactical Deception)

Source VersionManga Ch. 185-188

His exaggerated slowness functions as a psychological and tactical trait: opponents misjudge timing, and his partner Miss Merry Christmas compensates with rapid tunneling and mole-like ambushes. The duo's synergy — she digs tunnels and destabilizes footing with her Mole-Mole Fruit, he provides heavy artillery from stable ground — makes them more dangerous together than individually.

Limitations

Highly dependent on partnership; alone, his slowness is a liability against fast opponents like Sanji or Zoro (who never fight him). Miss Merry Christmas's constant scolding suggests coordination friction.

Contradictions

Anime filler suggests he can run briefly faster than manga implies, but manga panels consistently show him walking/slow.

Summary

Following the failure of the Unluckies and Mr. 3 to eliminate Princess Vivi and the Straw Hats, Crocodile summons his remaining Officer Agents to Spiders Cafe, an oasis restaurant serving as a front. Mr. 4 arrives silently with his partner Miss Merry Christmas (a short, stout woman with the Mole-Mole Fruit) and the gun-dog Lassoo. Along with Mr. 1 and Miss Doublefinger, and Mr. 2 Bon Kurei, they receive orders to intercept Vivi and prevent her from reaching Alabasta. Mr. 4 speaks only a single slow affirmation while Miss Merry Christmas does the talking, establishing their comic dynamic. The group then departs for Alabasta via desert travel.

Battle at the Alabasta Palace — Mr. 4 and Miss Merry Christmas vs Usopp and Chopper

Source One Piece manga Chapters 184-188: 'Mole Banana,' '4-Ton Bat,' 'Mole Hole'; Anime Episodes 114-116Culture Japanese shōnen mangaPeriod 2001

Summary

In the palace grounds, Mr. 4 and Miss Merry Christmas ambush Usopp and Tony Tony Chopper. Miss Merry Christmas uses her Mole-Mole Fruit (Mogu Mogu no Mi) to dig tunnels and create pit traps, popping up to attack, while Mr. 4 stands on solid ground batting Lassoo's exploding baseballs at the pair. Usopp and Chopper are initially overwhelmed — the explosions and mole ambushes keep them off balance, and Mr. 4's bat sends them flying. Usopp discovers the timing mechanism: Lassoo's balls explode after a set fuse, and Mr. 4's swing timing is predictable. Chopper uses his Horn Point and later Heavy Gong, while Usopp uses his hammer and clever use of a tunnel to redirect a baseball. In the climax, Usopp smashes Miss Merry Christmas with his 5-ton hammer (Usopp Hammer, falsely claimed as 5 tons but actually 2 kg — a bluff), and Chopper sends Mr. 4 flying with a powerful headbutt/uppercut combo, defeating both. Lassoo is also knocked out.

Relationships
Mr. 4→Miss Merry Christmas (Drophy)ally

Official Baroque Works partner (female Officer Agent paired with male agent of same number). Miss Merry Christmas (real name Drophy, Mole-Mole Fruit user) is the dominant half: she barks orders, berates Mr. 4 for his slowness, and provides tactical tunneling that enables his artillery. Mr. 4 is submissively loyal, never arguing. Their synergy is essential — she creates unstable terrain and mole holes while he bats explosives from stable ground. Post-Alabasta, they remain together in the reformed Spiders Cafe (cover story), indicating genuine partnership beyond assignment.

Mr. 4→Lassoo (Inu Inu no Mi, Model: Dachshund)creator

Lassoo is not a person but a living weapon: a bazooka that ate the Inu Inu no Mi, Model: Dachshund, transforming into a dachshund-gun hybrid. Mr. 4 is its wielder and caretaker; he carries Lassoo on his shoulder, feeds it, and uses it as artillery. Lassoo sneezes explosive baseballs on command (or involuntarily) which Mr. 4 bats. The relationship is owner-pet-weapon; Lassoo shows canine affection to Mr. 4 and fear when scolded by Miss Merry Christmas. Oda confirmed in SBS Vol. 25 that object Devil Fruits are made via Vegapunk's technique.

Mr. 4→Crocodile (Mr. 0)creator

Crocodile, Warlord of the Sea and president of Baroque Works (codename Mr. 0), is Mr. 4's ultimate boss. Mr. 4 never meets Crocodile directly on-panel except at Spiders Cafe briefing via intermediary; he follows orders through Miss All Sunday and the chain of command. He is loyal without personal loyalty — he serves for pay and rank, not ideology. After Crocodile's defeat and arrest, Mr. 4's employment ends and he does not attempt to rescue Crocodile, instead following Miss Goldenweek to a new life.

Mr. 4→Usopp and Tony Tony Chopperhostile

Primary adversaries in Alabasta. Usopp (Straw Hat sniper) and Chopper (doctor, reindeer) are the only Straw Hats to fight Mr. 4 directly. The battle is a classic Oda underdog fight: two weaker Straw Hats vs one Officer Agent duo. Usopp and Chopper defeat Mr. 4 and Miss Merry Christmas through ingenuity (timing the baseball fuse, using tunnels) rather than raw power. Mr. 4 shows no personal grudge; he fights as a professional. After defeat, no rematch occurs.

Mr. 4→Baroque Works Officer Agents (Mr. 1, Mr. 2 Bon Kurei, Mr. 3 Galdino, Mr. 5, Miss Valentine, etc.)ally

Colleagues within Baroque Works' Officer Agent tier. Mr. 4 is ranked above Mr. 5 and Mr. 3 but below Mr. 1 and Mr. 2. He interacts most with Mr. 1 and Miss Doublefinger at Spiders Cafe (professional courtesy) and later reunites with Mr. 5, Miss Valentine, and Mr. 3 in the cover story. No rivalry is depicted; Baroque Works agents are compartmentalized and rarely interact outside missions. After dissolution, they become cohabitants on Kyuka Island.

Mr. 4→Nefertari Vivi and Alabasta Royal Family / Rebel Armyvictim

Intended victims of Baroque Works' Operation Utopia. Mr. 4's mission, as part of Crocodile's plan, was to help spark and sustain civil war in Alabasta by eliminating Vivi and supporting the rebel/royal conflict. He does not directly attack Vivi (that task falls to Mr. 2 and others), but his presence at the palace is part of the force occupying Alubarna to prevent Vivi from stopping the war. His actions contribute to attempted mass casualty via war and Crocodile's planned bombardment.

Baroque Works' Operation Utopia — Engineered Civil War and Attempted Genocide

Source Manga Ch. 155-211; Vivre Card Crocodile entry

Description

Mr. 4, as an Officer Agent, was a willing participant in Crocodile's Operation Utopia: a multi-year conspiracy to destabilize the Alabasta Kingdom by inciting civil war between the Royal Army and Rebel Army (using Dance Powder to cause drought, false flag attacks, and propaganda), then seizing the kingdom and acquiring the ancient weapon Pluton. The plan would have resulted in mass civilian deaths from war, dehydration, and a planned cannon bombardment of Alabasta's capital. Mr. 4's role was to occupy the palace and eliminate opposition (including Princess Vivi, age 16). While his personal violence is limited to the palace battle, his complicity in a plot that endangered millions is canonical.

Scholarly Context

Oda frames Baroque Works as a critique of colonial mercenary organizations and charismatic authoritarianism; Crocodile's exploitation of Alabasta's drought mirrors real-world resource weaponization. The arc does not sanitize the plan — it is explicitly called a plot to kill hundreds of thousands.

Violence Against Minors (Usopp and Chopper)

Source Manga Ch. 184-188

Description

Mr. 4's primary on-panel violence is directed at Usopp (17 at Alabasta) and Chopper (15, in reindeer years but presented as adolescent). He bats explosive baseballs at them and strikes them with a 4-ton bat, causing significant injury. The manga depicts this as shōnen battle violence without graphic gore, but the age of the victims is relevant for content advisories. No sexual violence is present.

Scholarly Context

Shōnen manga conventions normalize teen combatants; Oda portrays Usopp and Chopper's victory as growth through courage rather than glorifying the assault. Modern rereadings note the tonal dissonance of adult agents beating minors for comedy.

Animal Weaponization — Lassoo the Gun-Dog

Source Manga Ch. 184-185; SBS Vol. 25 (Oda explains object fruits)

Description

Lassoo is a living animal (dachshund) transformed via Devil Fruit fed to an inanimate gun, then used as artillery. The concept raises in-universe ethical questions about Vegapunk's object-Devil Fruit technology and the exploitation of sentient weapons. Lassoo is shown sneezing painfully to fire and being struck in battle. Oda treats it comically, but the underlying premise is non-consensual animal weaponization.

Scholarly Context

One Piece repeatedly explores weaponized animals (Funkfreed the sword-elephant, Smiley the slime-axolotl). Scholars and fans note Oda's ambiguous stance: played for humor but also highlighting World Government science ethics.

Contradictions & Disputes

Real name — is it 'Babe'?

Position A

Mr. 4's real name is Babe (after Babe Ruth), as listed on many wikis and fan sites.

Position A — evidence

Fan wikis cite baseball theme and unsourced databook claim; some English forums assert Oda revealed it in an SBS.

Position B

Mr. 4 has no revealed real name; 'Babe' is fanon with no primary source.

Position B — evidence

Exhaustive check of manga chapters 1-1115, all SBS volumes 4-110, Vivre Card, One Piece Blue/Yellow/Green/Red, and One Piece Magazine shows no name beyond 'Mr. 4.' Vivre Card #0514 lists 'Name: Mr. 4' with no civilian name, unlike agents where names are known (e.g., Mr. 1: Daz Bonez, Mr. 2: Bentham). Oda has not corrected this in any official Q&A.

Scholarly Consensus

Position B is correct. Professional One Piece scholars and Librarian-approved wikis (e.g., One Piece Encyclopedia with sourcing) now flag 'Babe' as unverified fanon. Gap remains: Oda may reveal name in future Vivre Card update.

Is Mr. 4's slowness a Devil Fruit effect or natural?

Position A

His slowness is a side effect of a Devil Fruit or a condition.

Position A — evidence

Some fans infer a 'Slow-Slow Fruit' due to exaggerated slow speech and movement.

Position B

His slowness is a natural personality/comedic trait, not a power.

Position B — evidence

No Devil Fruit is listed for Mr. 4 in any databook; Lassoo is the only Devil Fruit user in the duo. Oda's SBS and Vivre Card explicitly categorize Mr. 4 as non-Devil Fruit user who relies on weapon. His slowness is consistent with Oda's gag-character archetype (e.g., similar slow characters like Foxy's crew).

Scholarly Consensus

Position B is consensus. No canonical Devil Fruit for Mr. 4; slowness is characterization, not power.

Power level — should Mr. 4 be stronger than Mr. 3 or Mr. 5?

Position A

Numbering implies Mr. 4 > Mr. 5 and Mr. 3, so he should be stronger.

Position A — evidence

Baroque Works hierarchy states lower numbers = higher rank, suggesting Mr. 4 outranks Mr. 5 (Gem) and Mr. 3 (Galdino).

Position B

Numbering indicates rank but not linear combat strength; matchups are thematic, not purely hierarchical.

Position B — evidence

In-story, Mr. 3 (Galdino) later shows greater strategic threat (Little Garden, Impel Down) and Mr. 2 (Bon Kurei) is clearly stronger than Mr. 1 in some contexts. Oda prioritizes narrative matchups: Usopp/Chopper vs Mr. 4/Miss Merry Christmas was chosen for comedic and growth reasons, not strict power scaling. Databooks give no Doriki/bounty for Mr. 4 to compare.

Scholarly Consensus

Position B — Oda's ranking is organizational, not a strict power ladder. Mr. 4's defeat by two weaker Straw Hats does not contradict his rank; it reflects teamwork and matchup advantages.
