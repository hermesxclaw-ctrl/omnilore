You'll hear her before you see her -- a scream that sounds almost human, almost animal, like something in terrible pain. Some say she's looking for her husband. Some say she's just looking for company. Either way, you don't want to meet her.

Who It Is

The Wampus Cat is a creature of Cherokee mythology that became firmly embedded in Appalachian cryptid and folk tradition. In the Cherokee legend, a woman named Running Deer was suspicious of her husband's nighttime activities and followed him to a secret ceremonial gathering. She was discovered, and as punishment, a medicine man transformed her into a terrifying half-woman, half-mountain lion creature -- the Wampus Cat -- condemned to wander the mountains forever. The creature is described as a large, cat-like being that walks on its hind legs (or sometimes on all fours), with the face of a woman, glowing yellow eyes, and a terrifying, screaming cry that echoes through the mountains at night. Different Appalachian variants describe the Wampus Cat as: a large, predatory feline with supernatural stealth; a spirit-beast that drinks livestock blood; or a guardian that protects the forest by frightening intruders. American football teams in the South (including the University of Tennessee's unofficial mascot history, and several high schools) have adopted the Wampus Cat as a mascot. Cryptozoologists have sometimes linked Wampus Cat reports to sightings of large, unidentified felines in the Appalachian region, connecting it to the broader 'Alien Big Cat' phenomenon in North America. Skeptics attribute sightings to mountain lions, bobcats, or feral dogs seen at a distance. The Wampus Cat endures as one of the most iconic Appalachian folkloric creatures.
