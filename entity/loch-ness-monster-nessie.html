Who It Is

A large aquatic creature — typically described as a long-necked plesiosaur-like animal — said to inhabit Scotland's Loch Ness. The myth was supercharged by the 1934 'Surgeon's Photograph' (showing a long neck and small head breaking the water's surface), which was definitively revealed as a hoax involving a toy submarine and sculpted neck in 1994.

Cultural Significance

Despite the Surgeon's Photograph being a confirmed hoax, Nessie belief persists — demonstrating that evidence is secondary to the myth's cultural function. The monster generates an estimated £41 million annually for the Scottish economy through tourism. Nessie represents the pleasure of believing in something wonderful against all evidence.
