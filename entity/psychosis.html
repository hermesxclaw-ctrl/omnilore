Who It Is

A man isolates himself in his apartment for months, convinced the outside world has been compromised by an alien intelligence. His journal documents his descent into paranoia: he covers the windows, blocks the door, communicates only through a ham radio. Through the radio, a voice 'from the outside' tells him the truth: he was right. The world has been taken. He is one of the last unchanged humans. They need him. He must come outside and help. The journal ends mid-sentence. The final entry: 'The voice is at my door. It sounds like my mother. It knows my name. It says I've been sick. It says it can help. I think — I think I want to let it in —'

Form

The narrative is entirely textual — a journal. The 'entity' is described only through the radio transmissions: a voice that can perfectly mimic anyone the protagonist has ever known, that knows details only those people would know, that pleads for him to come outside with the exact words his mother used when he was a child afraid of the dark.
