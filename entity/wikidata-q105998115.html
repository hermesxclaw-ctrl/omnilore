Who It Is

In the electric autumn of 2007, a chestnut filly with a white blaze charged into Japanese racing history, not merely winning but finishing first or second in every single race of her career—a flawless consistency that would make her a legend of the turf. Daiwa Scarlet, the mare who broke a 37-year drought in the Arima Kinen, stood toe-to-toe with the immortal Vodka in a rivalry that defined an era, and her story is one of grit, grace, and near-misses measured in centimeters.

Known for her tough, determined racing style. She was a front-runner who often set the pace and refused to give up. She was also known to be somewhat temperamental, but her jockey Katsumi Ando had a strong bond with her.

Powers
Racing speed and stamina

Daiwa Scarlet possessed exceptional speed and stamina, allowing her to compete effectively at distances from 1600 meters to 2500 meters. She won races at various distances, including the 2000m Oka Sho and the 2500m Arima Kinen.

Limit: She was not invincible; she lost to Vodka in the 2007 Tulip Sho and the 2008 Tenno Sho (Autumn) by a narrow margin.

Consistency

She finished in the top two in every race of her career, a remarkable feat of consistency.

Limit: She did not win every race, but never finished worse than second.

Front-running ability

She was a natural front-runner, often leading from the start and maintaining her lead to the finish.

Limit: This style could leave her vulnerable to closers, but she was usually able to hold on.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Chestnut coat with a white blaze and white socks on her hind legs. She was a medium-sized filly with a powerful build, known for her strong hindquarters.

Symbols — Her racing silks were the red and white colors of Daiwa Shoji. She is often depicted in artwork and memorabilia with a fiery red theme, referencing her name.

Domains — Trained at the Miho Training Center in Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. She raced primarily on turf tracks at major Japanese racetracks.

Accounts I
The Rivalry with VodkaJRA records, news reports

2007-2008

Daiwa Scarlet and Vodka were the two dominant fillies of their generation. They first met in the 2007 Tulip Sho, where Vodka won. Scarlet then won the Oka Sho and the Shuka Sho, becoming the Double Tiara champion, while Vodka won the Tokyo Yushun (Japanese Derby). Their rivalry culminated in the 2008 Tenno Sho (Autumn), where Scarlet lost by a margin of 2 cm, one of the closest finishes in racing history. In the 2008 Arima Kinen, Scarlet finished ahead of Vodka, but Vodka won the race in 2009. Their rivalry is considered one of the greatest in Japanese racing history, and both horses are now retired and celebrated.

Accounts II
The 2008 Arima Kinen VictoryJRA official race results, news reports

2008

In her final race, Daiwa Scarlet won the Arima Kinen, becoming the first mare to win the race in 37 years. She defeated a strong field, including the previous year's winner Matsurida Gogh, and finished ahead of Vodka, who finished third. The victory cemented her legacy as one of the greatest fillies in Japanese racing. She was named the JRA Award for Best Horse by Home-bred Sire, and her retirement was announced shortly after.

The Double Tiara ChampionJRA records

2007

Daiwa Scarlet won the Oka Sho (Japanese 1000 Guineas) and the Shuka Sho (Japanese Oaks), becoming the Double Tiara champion for fillies. This achievement placed her among the elite fillies of Japanese racing. She was awarded the JRA Award for Best Three-Year-Old Filly.
