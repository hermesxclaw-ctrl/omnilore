The Approach

In the electric autumn of 2007, a chestnut filly with a white blaze charged into Japanese racing history, not merely winning but finishing first or second in every single race of her career—a flawless consistency that would make her a legend of the turf. Daiwa Scarlet, the mare who broke a 37-year drought in the Arima Kinen, stood toe-to-toe with the immortal Vodka in a rivalry that defined an era, and her story is one of grit, grace, and near-misses measured in centimeters.

The name 'Daiwa' comes from the owner's prefix, Daiwa Shoji Co., Ltd., and 'Scarlet' refers to her chestnut coat color, which is a shade of red.

Description

Appearance

Chestnut coat with a white blaze and white socks on her hind legs. She was a medium-sized filly with a powerful build, known for her strong hindquarters.

Symbols & Iconography

Her racing silks were the red and white colors of Daiwa Shoji. She is often depicted in artwork and memorabilia with a fiery red theme, referencing her name.

Behaviour & Personality

Known for her tough, determined racing style. She was a front-runner who often set the pace and refused to give up. She was also known to be somewhat temperamental, but her jockey Katsumi Ando had a strong bond with her.

Domains & Habitats

Trained at the Miho Training Center in Ibaraki Prefecture, Japan. She raced primarily on turf tracks at major Japanese racetracks.

Stories & Myths

The Rivalry with Vodka

Source JRA records, news reportsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2007-2008

Summary

Daiwa Scarlet and Vodka were the two dominant fillies of their generation. They first met in the 2007 Tulip Sho, where Vodka won. Scarlet then won the Oka Sho and the Shuka Sho, becoming the Double Tiara champion, while Vodka won the Tokyo Yushun (Japanese Derby). Their rivalry culminated in the 2008 Tenno Sho (Autumn), where Scarlet lost by a margin of 2 cm, one of the closest finishes in racing history. In the 2008 Arima Kinen, Scarlet finished ahead of Vodka, but Vodka won the race in 2009.

Consequences

Their rivalry is considered one of the greatest in Japanese racing history, and both horses are now retired and celebrated.

Some accounts emphasize the 2 cm loss as a defining moment, while others highlight Scarlet's Arima Kinen victory.

The 2008 Arima Kinen Victory

Source JRA official race results, news reportsCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2008

Summary

In her final race, Daiwa Scarlet won the Arima Kinen, becoming the first mare to win the race in 37 years. She defeated a strong field, including the previous year's winner Matsurida Gogh, and finished ahead of Vodka, who finished third. The victory cemented her legacy as one of the greatest fillies in Japanese racing.
