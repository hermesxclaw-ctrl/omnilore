Form

No physical description survives. As an abstract personification in the Orphic tradition, she would have been imagined as a female figure, but no iconographic evidence exists. She is best understood as a conceptual entity rather than an anthropomorphic goddess.

Powers

Personification of Simplicity As a daimon, she embodies the abstract quality of simplicity, frankness, and honesty. Orphic Hymn 73 She is a personified concept, not an active agent in myths. Her 'power' is symbolic and ethical, not narrative. None recorded.

Accounts

The hymn is a short invocation, typical of the collection. It calls on Apheleia to come favorably, to bestow a life of simplicity and honesty, and to nurture the soul, freeing it from the 'rough' and 'harsh' aspects of existence. This is her only known narrative context.

Scholarly Debates
Conflation with Apatheia
Some casual sources treat Apheleia and Apatheia as the same concept, or as interchangeable personifications of 'calm'. ⇌ The Orphic Hymn and the philosophical tradition are distinct. Apheleia is simplicity and frankness; Apatheia (from the Stoic tradition) is freedom from passion or disturbance.
Evidence for A: Internet summaries and some popular synopses of Greek mythology.

What We Don't Know

No iconographic evidence. No other literary references. The exact date and authorship of the Orphic Hymns are debated. The full text of Hymn 73 in the original Greek should be consulted for a more precise translation.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Apheleia as the ancient Greek version of a Marie Kondo philosophy for the soul: she's not about grand, dramatic power, but about clearing away the 'rough' and 'harsh' psychic clutter to live a simple, honest, and direct life.
