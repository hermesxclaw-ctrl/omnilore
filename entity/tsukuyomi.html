Who It Is

In the moonlit sky of Japanese myth, a god watches from afar, separated from his sister by an act of disgust that split day from night forever. Tsukuyomi, the moon reader, remains one of Shinto's most enigmatic figures.

In mythology, he is calm and quiet, but also capable of disgust and violence, as shown by his killing of Ukemochi. He is often portrayed as a solitary figure, separated from Amaterasu.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Not described in detail in ancient texts. He is a moon deity, often depicted in art as a male figure, sometimes with a bow or mirror. No canonical physical description exists.

Symbols — Moon, night, bow (from 'Tsukuyumi'), mirror (white bronze mirror in some accounts). In art, he may be shown with a crescent moon or as a hunter.

Domains — Moon, night, and in some accounts the sea ('eightfold tides of the sea plain') or 'Yoru no Oshikuni' (Realm of Night Food). In the Nihon Shoki, he is to assist the sun and manage heavenly affairs.

Accounts I
Birth from Izanagi's right eyeKojiki; Nihon Shoki (variant 6)

Mythic age

After Izanagi escapes from Yomi and performs purification, he washes his right eye, and Tsukuyomi is born. He is one of the three noble children, with Amaterasu from the left eye and Susanoo from the nose. He is assigned to rule the night or the sea.

Accounts II
The Killing of UkemochiNihon Shoki (variant 11)

Mythic age

Amaterasu sends Tsukuyomi to represent her at a feast given by Ukemochi. Ukemochi produces food by spitting out fish, game, and rice. Disgusted, Tsukuyomi kills her. Amaterasu is so angry she refuses to look at Tsukuyomi again, separating day and night forever.

Tsukuyomi is an evil deity. — He is not inherently evil; his actions are contextual. He is a moon god with a calm nature.

Tsukuyomi and Susanoo are the same. — They are distinct deities in the Kiki, though some scholars speculate on a possible connection.

Tsukuyomi is a minor deity. — He is one of the three noble children, but his role in mythology is limited.
