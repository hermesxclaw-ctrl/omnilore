Who It Is

Mwari (also known as Musikavanhu, Nyadenga, or Dzivaguru) is the supreme creator deity of the Shona people of Zimbabwe. Unlike many African supreme beings who are remote and inaccessible, Mwari communicates directly with humanity through oracles housed in sacred caves in the Matopos (Matobo) Hills, a practice that may date back to the Great Zimbabwe civilization. The most famous of these is the Mwari cult oracle at Njelele, where the voice of God (ascribed to a ventriloquist priest or interpreted through natural acoustic phenomena) gave oracular pronouncements on matters of war, drought, and political succession. The Rozvi and later Ndebele kings would consult the Mwari oracle before major undertakings. In Shona theology, Mwari is the giver of rain, the guardian of the land, and the ultimate source of moral authority. He is strongly associated with water, fertility, and caves — and the name 'Mwari' may be etymologically connected to concepts of 'pool' or 'being in water.' During the First Chimurenga (1896–97), the Shona and Ndebele uprising against British colonial rule, Mwari's oracle played a crucial role in coordinating resistance, demonstrating the deity's political as well as spiritual significance. Today, Mwari's worship has syncretized with Christian practice among many Shona, but traditional cult centers continue to receive pilgrims.
