Who It Is

On a broken house half-sunk off the coast of Jaya, a bare-chested man with a chestnut-shaped head dives ten thousand meters into darkness every day — not for treasure, but to prove his ancestor wasn't a liar. Mont Blanc Cricket is One Piece's monument to inherited shame and stubborn faith, the man who waited for a sky island to fall.

Attested: Initially presented as eccentric, loud, and obsessive — shouting about gold and sky islands while diving compulsively. Quickly revealed as deeply principled, melancholic, and self-sacrificial. Cricket endured 12 years of mockery in Mock Town without retaliation, internalizing family shame but never abandoning Noland's logbook. He is fiercely loyal to his found family (Masira, Shoujou) and immediately risks his life to help strangers (Straw Hats) once they show faith. Powers
Master Free-Diver / Salvage Diver

Attested elite breath-hold diving to extreme depths without equipment. Dives daily to the Jaya seabed (estimated 100m+ depth) to search for evidence of the lost City of Gold. Can hold breath for extended periods and withstand pressure that incapacitates normal humans. Uses no Devil Fruit; purely physiological training.

Limit: Human limits still apply — requires surface intervals, suffers decompression stress, chest pain, and exhaustion. Cannot reach Skypiea depths; relies on Knock Up Stream for vertical transport. Age and cumulative barotrauma are implied costs.

Superhuman Durability (Chestnut Head)

Gag/attested durability: Cricket's chestnut-shaped head is extremely hard. He demonstrates by having Masira and Shoujou strike it, and by headbutting. Luffy remarks it feels like a real chestnut. Used comically but also to show toughness from years of diving pressure.

Limit: Comedic trait, not combat-relevant against serious fighters (Bellamy defeats him off-screen in Mock Town brawl; Cricket does not resist). No Haki, no armament.

Navigation & Historical Research (Noland Studies)

Expert knowledge of Jaya's geography, Knock Up Stream mechanics, South Bird ecology, and Noland's logbook. Can predict Stream timing via cloud reading and seabed topography. Taught Straw Hats how to capture South Bird (which always faces south) to navigate to Skypiea.

Limit: Knowledge is specialized to Jaya/Skypiea; not a general navigator like Nami. Relies on Noland's 400-year-old records which were incomplete.

Leadership / Saruyama Alliance Command

Commands loyalty of two pirate salvage crews (Masira's and Shoujou's) totaling ~50 men, who call him 'Cricket-san' / 'Aniki' and follow his orders to repair the Going Merry and prepare for Skypiea launch. Charisma based on shared dreamer ethos, not strength.

Limit: Alliance is non-combatant salvage group; not comparable to Yonko or Warlord crews. Cricket explicitly avoids pirate infamy and has no bounty.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested: Extremely tall (242 cm), heavily muscled, bare-chested man with a distinctive chestnut-shaped head — bulbous, vertically ridged, dark brown with a stem-like tuft of black hair on top. The shape is literal: Oda draws suture lines like a chestnut husk. Wears tattered green cargo pants, barefoot, often with a cigarette. Chest is scarred and tanned from years of free-diving. Posture is stooped from diving pressure. In early Jaya appearances

Symbols — Chestnut (kuri) is central visual pun and symbol of inherited burden — hard outer shell, hidden value. His house is a stilt hut built around the half-submerged carcass of Noland's ship, symbolizing living inside ancestral wreckage. Motifs: diving goggles, salvage ropes, South Bird (as compass), golden bell (as proof of truth). The Saruyama Alliance flag (monkey + chestnut) merges Masira/Shoujou's monkey salvage motif with Cricket's

Domains — Habitat: Stilt house on the southern coast of Jaya (Grand Line, Paradise), near Mock Town but deliberately isolated. The house straddles the sea where Jaya was torn apart by the Knock Up Stream 400 years ago; the seabed below is his research domain. Accounts I
The Lie of Mont Blanc Noland (400 Years Ago)One Piece Vol.25 Ch.224, Vol.32 Ch.286-302 (Kalgara/Noland flashback); recounted by Cricket in Ch.228-230

One Piece timeline: ~400 years before present (c.1122 in One Piece calendar); published 2002-2003

Explorer and botanist Mont Blanc Noland, Cricket's ancestor, befriends warrior Kalgara of Jaya's Shandians and discovers the City of Gold (Shandora). He returns to Lvneel Kingdom and reports gold to King, but when the King sails to Jaya, the city has vanished — torn into the sky by the Knock Up Stream. Noland is executed as 'Noland the Liar,' and his descendants bear the stigma for centuries. Cricket recites this as family history, insisting Noland told the truth. Establishes Cricket's inherited shame and life mission; explains why Jaya's seabed contains only ruins. Later vindicated when Straw Hats ring Shandora's Golden Bell in Skypiea, proving gold existed. Cricket hears the bell from Jaya, weeping.

Childhood Ostracism and the Vow on JayaOne Piece Ch.228-230 (Cricket monologue), Vivre Card biography

Cricket age ~12-24 (c.12 years before present, ~1990-2002 timeline)

Born into the Mont Blanc family in North Blue, Cricket is bullied from childhood as 'descendant of the liar.' At age ~24 he leaves North Blue, sails to Jaya — the island from Noland's log — and vows to find proof. He builds a stilt house over Noland's sunken ship and begins daily dives. Initially alone, he is mocked by Mock Town pirates as 'the idiot who talks about dreams.' After 6-8 years, salvage brothers Masira and Shoujou, moved by his sincerity, swear brotherhood and form the Saruyama Alliance to support his search. Alliance provides manpower, ships, and emotional support; Cricket gains surrogate family. Establishes Jaya as his permanent home and research base. Explains his isolation and refusal to fight back against Bellamy's crew — he accepts mockery as price of faith.

Humiliation in Mock Town and Encounter with BellamyOne Piece Ch.222-226 (Bellamy vs. Cricket's allies), Ch.228

Present timeline: Jaya Arc, May 1522 (One Piece calendar)

Bellamy the Hyena, a Doflamingo-affiliated pirate who preaches the 'New Era' where dreams are dead, beats Cricket's allies and mocks Cricket's dream of sky islands and gold. Cricket, returning from a dive, finds his friends injured but refuses to retaliate, telling them dreams are not to be forced on others. He is derided as weak. This scene establishes the Jaya Arc's thematic core: cynicism vs. faith. Luffy and Zoro, who also refused to fight Bellamy earlier, witness Cricket's dignity and are moved. Cricket's nonviolent endurance contrasts with Luffy's later violent vindication (Luffy one-punches Bellamy). Cricket's philosophy influences Luffy's declaration to go to Skypiea despite mockery. Bellamy's defeat later validates Cricket's worldview.

Accounts II
The Straw Hat Alliance: South Bird and the Knock Up StreamOne Piece Ch.229-239, Anime Ep.149-152

Jaya Arc climax, 1522

After Luffy declares he believes in Skypiea, Cricket allies with the Straw Hats. He explains the Knock Up Stream — a massive geyser that erupts five times per month south of Jaya — and that they must ride it to the sky. To find its location, they must capture a South Bird, which always faces south. Cricket, Masira, Shoujou and the Straw Hats hunt the bird in Jaya's jungle, fend off Bellamy's lingering crew, and then the Saruyama Alliance repairs and upgrades the Going Merry (adding wings, reinforcing hull) for sky voyage. Cricket provides Noland's logbook copy, eternal pose to Skypiea, and emotional farewell. Straw Hats reach Skypiea; Cricket's 12-year research directly enables the Skypiea Arc. Going Merry's modifications save the crew. Cricket remains on Jaya, waiting to hear the bell.

The Bell Rings: Vindication Across the SkyOne Piece Ch.301 'The Guidepost of the Bell', Ch.302, Anime Ep.195

Skypiea Arc climax, 1522

On Skypiea, Luffy rings the Golden Bell of Shandora (the bell Noland and Kalgara promised to ring) to prove to Cricket that gold existed. The sound, amplified by the sky, carries down to Jaya. Cricket, Masira and Shoujou, still on Jaya, hear the bell's chime through the clouds. Cricket collapses weeping, shouting that Noland was not a liar. The 400-year stigma is lifted. He declares his dream fulfilled and that he will continue diving not for proof, but for joy. Narrative closure for Jaya/Skypiea saga; Cricket's arc resolves without him physically going to sky. Establishes One Piece theme that dreams outlive the dreamer. Later cover stories show Cricket and alliance still together, now respected.

After the Dream: From the Decks of the World (Post-Timeskip)One Piece Cover Story 'From the Decks of the World' Ch.631, Vol.63-64; One Piece Vol.82 Ch.824 (brief cameo)

1524 (two years later)

In the two-year timeskip cover serial, Cricket, Masira and Shoujou are shown on Jaya, now celebrated rather than mocked, building a new ship and still salvaging. They have grown slightly older, Cricket with a calmer smile. A later cameo in the Reverie/Whole Cake buildup shows Jaya news reaching them. They do not join the Straw Hat Grand Fleet, but remain allies in spirit. No further major plot role as of 2026. Shows lasting impact of vindication; Cricket's found family endures. Confirms he did not pursue piracy or bounty, remaining a salvage explorer.

Mont Blanc Cricket is Mont Blanc Noland himself or a reincarnation. — Cricket is Noland's descendant 400 years later, not Noland. They share surname and chestnut motif but are distinct generations. Noland died executed; Cricket lives on Jaya.

Cricket is a Devil Fruit user or has chestnut powers. — Cricket has no Devil Fruit. His chestnut head is a visual gag and natural durability from diving, not a fruit ability. Vivre Card explicitly lists no fruit.

Cricket is a pirate captain with a bounty. — Cricket has no bounty and does not identify as a pirate. He leads the Saruyama Alliance, a salvage alliance, not a pirate crew. Masira and Shoujou are captains; Cricket is their 'big brother' but not a wanted pirate.

The Saruyama Alliance joined the Straw Hat Grand Fleet. — The Saruyama Alliance never joined the Grand Fleet (Dressrosa). They are Jaya allies who helped the Straw Hats but are not part of the 5600-member fleet. Grand Fleet members are Cavendish, Bartolomeo, etc.

Cricket went to Skypiea with the Straw Hats. — Cricket stayed on Jaya and never went to Skypiea. He heard the bell from below. His dream was vindicated remotely, which is thematically intentional — faith without needing to see.
