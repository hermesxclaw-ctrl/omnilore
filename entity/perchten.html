Who It Is

Die Perchten are the wild, masked figures of Alpine folklore who appear during the Rauhnächte (Twelve Days of Christmas) and the Perchtenlauf (Perchten Run) tradition, particularly in Austria (Salzburg, Tyrol, Upper Austria) and Bavaria. The name 'Perchten' derives from Frau Perchta (also Berchta), the winter goddess, and her retinue. The Perchten are divided into two main types: the 'Schöne Perchten' (Beautiful Perchten), who wear elaborately carved and painted wooden masks representing beautiful-faced women with crowns, mirrors, and intricate headdresses — they bring good luck, prosperity, and blessings to the homes they visit; and the 'Schiach Perchten' (Ugly Perchten), who wear monstrous masks with bulging eyes, fangs, horns, and distorted features — they represent the punishing, chaotic forces of winter and chase away evil spirits. The Perchtenlauf is a ritual procession in which men dressed as Perchten parade through the streets, their heavy wooden masks (Larven) clattering, their cowbells (Glocken) ringing, and their bodies wrapped in furs and straw. The cacophony of bells, chains, and screams is believed to drive away evil spirits and ensure a prosperous new year. The tradition has roots stretching back to pre-Christian Germanic solstice rituals, where masked processions marked the transition from the old year to the new, the death of winter and the rebirth of spring. After being suppressed by the Catholic Church in the 17th–19th centuries, the Perchten tradition experienced a major revival in the late 20th century and is now a celebrated part of Alpine cultural heritage, with major events in Salzburg, the Gastein Valley, and the Pongau region of Austria featuring hundreds of participants. The Perchten are distinct from Krampus in timing (Krampus appears December 5–6; Perchten run during the entire Rauhnächte, December 21 through January 6) and in their broader symbolism of both blessing and banishing.
