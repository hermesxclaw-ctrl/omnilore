Who It Is

Yog-Sothoth is the cosmic entity of boundless knowledge and space-time, described by Lovecraft as 'the Key and the Gate' — an Outer God who exists simultaneously at every point in space and time, and who knows all things. Unlike the mindless Azathoth or the indifferent Cthulhu, Yog-Sothoth is conscious and can communicate with those who summon it, offering forbidden knowledge at terrible cost. Its most famous appearance is in The Dunwich Horror (1929), where the sorcerer Old Whateley summons Yog-Sothoth to impregnate his daughter Lavinia, producing the monstrous half-human twins Wilbur Whateley and the invisible, world-devouring Horror that terrorizes Dunwich. Old Whateley's description of Yog-Sothoth is one of Lovecraft's most quoted passages: 'Yog-Sothoth knows the gate. Yog-Sothoth is the gate. Yog-Sothoth is the key and guardian of the gate. Past, present, future, all are one in Yog-Sothoth.' This formulation — Yog-Sothoth as simultaneously the means of access to cosmic knowledge and the knowledge itself — established the entity as the ultimate cipher of Lovecraft's metaphysics. Through the Gates of the Silver Key (a collaboration with E. Hoffmann Price) expands Yog-Sothoth's nature dramatically: the entity is coextensive with all of space-time, containing within itself all minds and all moments. When Randolph Carter passes through the Silver Key and confronts Yog-Sothoth, he experiences the 'All-in-One and One-in-All' — the realization that all consciousnesses are facets of a single cosmic awareness. Yog-Sothoth reveals that Carter has always been a facet of itself and always will be. This Gnostic revelation is simultaneously enlightenment and horror: individuality is an illusion, and the truth of existence is dissolution into the cosmic whole. In The Case of Charles Dexter Ward, the necromancer Joseph Curwen summons Yog-Sothoth through rituals that involve 'the essential Saltes of Animals,' using the entity's inter-temporal nature to resurrect the dead and acquire knowledge from across the ages. Yog-Sothoth has been depicted in art as a conglomeration of glowing, iridescent spheres — a mass of soap-bubble-like orbs that shift and merge, each containing a different moment or dimension. The entity's offspring (the Dunwich Horror) is invisible to normal sight, its true form only revealed through a special powder, and is described as an octopus-centipede-spider hybrid of titanic scale. In later Mythos expansions (especially August Derleth), Yog-Sothoth was positioned in a cosmic dualism with Azathoth — Yog-Sothoth as the principle of knowledge and order, Azathoth as chaos — though this schema is largely Derleth's invention and not firmly Lovecraftian. Yog-Sothoth remains the most mind-bending of Lovecraft's creations: a being that is everywhere and everywhen, that knows everything because it is everything, and to whom we are all ultimately just temporary facets of an eternal, alien consciousness.
