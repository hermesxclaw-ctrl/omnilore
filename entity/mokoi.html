I am what happens when the dead are not sent home. I am the hunger that has no Dreaming place — and I am always hungry.

Who It Is

Mokoi (also Morkul-kau-lu or similar variants) is a feared malevolent spirit in Yolngu mythology from Arnhem Land in northern Australia. Described as a monstrous being that hunts at night, Mokoi is said to be the spirit of a person who died an unnatural or violent death and now roams the darkness seeking to harm the living. It is particularly associated with sorcery and is believed to enter the body of sleeping victims, consuming their internal organs or stealing their souls. The only defense against Mokoi is the presence of a powerful marrngitj (healer/shaman) who can diagnose Mokoi attacks through ritual procedures and drive the spirit out. Some accounts describe Mokoi as having a voracious, insatiable hunger — it is an eater, a consumer, a being that has crossed the boundary between the living and the dead but refuses to accept the obligations of either state. In Yolngu cosmology, Mokoi represents the chaos that results when proper funeral rites are not conducted: the spirit does not return to its Dreaming place but becomes lost, hungry, and dangerous. This belief system functions as a powerful social mechanism that ensures the dead are treated with exhaustive ritual care. Anthropological accounts from Arnhem Land in the early to mid-20th century document the genuine terror that Mokoi inspired, with Yolngu people taking extreme precautions at night. Mokoi also serves a disciplinary function: the threat of being taken by Mokoi encourages adherence to social norms and the avoidance of dangerous, isolated places after dark. It is a distinctly Yolngu expression of a near-universal human motif — the monster that lurks in the dark and preys on those who stray from the protection of community and ritual.
