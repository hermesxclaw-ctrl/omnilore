Who It Is
Form

A is an exceptionally tall and massively built adult male shinobi, standing among the tallest human characters in the Naruto series. His physique is defined by extreme musculature—broad shoulders, a thick neck, and heavily developed arms and legs—conveying both his status as the strongest physical combatant in Kumogakure and his reliance on taijutsu-centric fighting techniques. His skin is dark brown, and his hair is blond (depicted as a pale yellow-gold), worn short and swept upward in a style that emphasizes his imposing stature. His eyes are sharp and slightly downward-slanted, conveying his perpetual intensity and readiness for combat. A bears a distinctive scar across his chest, a mark resulting from a training exercise with Killer Bee in which both performed the Double Lariat technique on each other to test their limits—a scar he wears without shame as evidence of his bond with his brother. His standard attire consists of a sleeveless, dark-colored Kumogakure flak jacket worn over a long-sleeved black shirt, paired with dark pants tucked into shinobi sandals and forearm wrappings. During his appearance at the Kage Summit, he wears the formal Raikage robes: a white cloak bearing the kanji for 'Fourth Raikage' over his standard battle attire. Following his battle with Sasuke Uchiha, A loses his left forearm (severed just below the elbow when Amaterasu ignites it and he has Darui cut it off to prevent the flames from consuming his body), and he subsequently wears his left sleeve pinned up or empty. In flashback sequences depicting his youth, A is shown with his blond hair in a shorter style and wearing standard Kumogakure training gear. By the time of the Boruto series, A is older, his hair having gone white, and he has fully adapted to fighting with one arm.

Powers

Lightning Release Chakra Mode (雷遁チャクラモード, Raiton Chākura Mōdo) A's signature and most formidable technique, in which he wraps his entire body in a cloak of lightning-natured chakra. This armor dramatically amplifies his already-exceptional physical speed and strength, and additionally provides a degree of damage negation—opponents who strike him while the armor is active receive an electrical shock that can paralyze their nervous system, damaging their ability to move. In this mode, A is considered one of the fastest shinobi alive, capable of moving so quickly that even the Sharingan has difficulty tracking him. The armor's generation consumes significant chakra but A's enormous chakra reserves make sustained use viable. Its visual manifestation—a crackling yellow-white corona of electricity—is one of the most iconic technique aesthetics in the series.

Double Lariat (ダブルラリアット, Daburu Rariatto) A combination technique performed in tandem with Killer Bee, in which both brothers extend one arm and charge at an opponent from opposite sides at high speed, striking the target simultaneously with clothesline-style blows of tremendous force. The combined impact is sufficient to decapitate opponents. A and Killer Bee are the only practitioners of this technique, and its execution requires deep mutual trust and coordination. The brothers used this technique to decapitate what they believed to be Kisame Hoshigaki, later revealed to be a White Zetsu impersonator. The scar on A's chest originated from an early training exercise in which both brothers performed this technique on each other to test their limits.

Liger Bomb (ライガーボム, Raigā Bomu) A high-speed grappling technique in which A seizes an opponent—typically grabbing them by the legs—lifts them overhead while running at maximum velocity using his Lightning Armor, then drives them headfirst into the ground with devastating force. The combination of A's raw physical strength, his lightning-enhanced speed, and the resulting impact creates an attack of extreme destructive potential. When used against Sasuke Uchiha during the Kage Summit arc, the jutsu was powerful enough to create a massive crater on impact, and only the intervention of Suigetsu Hōzuki (partially absorbing the impact with his water-body) prevented Sasuke's death.

Guillotine Drop (ギロチンドロップ, Girochin Doroppu) An aerial variant of A's grappling arsenal in which he grabs an opponent by the face or head and drives them vertically downward from height, slamming their skull into the ground. The technique exploits A's aerial mobility and the gravitational force added to his already prodigious strength. It has been used in conjunction with his Lightning Armor to maximize impact.

Lightning Release: Four Pillar Bind (雷遁・四柱しばり, Raiton: Shichū Shibari) A collaborative technique performed with his subordinates C and Darui, in which four enormous lightning-natured chakra pillars are raised to immobilize a target. Though A himself is not the primary caster, his chakra contribution amplifies the technique's potency, and the restraint technique is used to set up decisive physical follow-ups.

Extreme physical speed and strength (base) Entirely independent of any ninjutsu technique, A possesses physical speed and strength that vastly exceed human norms even without chakra enhancement. His databook statistics rate both strength and speed at their maximum value of 5. His base speed is sufficient to keep pace with the Eight-Tails jinchūriki (Killer Bee) in partial-transformation, and his strikes can shatter rock and leave craters without any chakra amplification. This baseline physicality means that even when chakra-depleted, A remains one of the most dangerous taijutsu-focused fighters in the shinobi world.

One-Armed Combat Adaptation Following the loss of his left forearm, A adapted his entire combat style to function with a single arm, a process that required significant physical and psychological adjustment. In post-war appearances (Boruto era), A is shown to have fully integrated the limitation into his fighting style, demonstrating that his effectiveness as a combatant is not fundamentally diminished. This adaptation is portrayed as a mark of his indomitable will and physical mastery.

Accounts

Years before the main narrative, the Third Raikage—biological father to A—adopted a young child who would become Killer Bee and assigned him to be A's partner and future jinchūriki candidate for the Eight-Tails. The two trained together intensively. In a definitive test of their bond, A and Killer Bee performed the Double Lariat on each other simultaneously—each taking the full force of the other's blow across the chest. Both survived and bore the resulting scars as permanent marks of their brotherhood. This event cemented the relationship that would define both characters throughout the series and established the Double Lariat as their signature combined technique.

A arrives at the Five Kage Summit in the Land of Iron with his subordinates Darui and C, convened to address the rising threat of Akatsuki. When Sasuke Uchiha and his team Taka infiltrate the summit to assassinate Danzo Shimura, A immediately engages Sasuke in combat—seeking to kill him both for the threat he poses and for his affiliation with the Akatsuki who had attacked Killer Bee. During their battle, A utilizes his Lightning Armor and full physical power, landing devastating strikes on Sasuke including the Liger Bomb. Sasuke survives due to the intervention of Suigetsu and subsequently uses Amaterasu (black flames) against A. To prevent the inextinguishable flames from consuming his body, A orders Darui to sever his left forearm, sacrificing the limb to escape. Itachi Uchiha's crow genjutsu intervenes to redirect Sasuke. A later cooperates with the other Kage to form a unified anti-Akatsuki alliance.

During the Fourth Great Ninja War, A—serving as Supreme Commander of the Allied Shinobi Forces—learns that Naruto Uzumaki has escaped from the island where he was being kept safe and is attempting to reach the battlefield. A personally moves to intercept Naruto, believing that allowing Naruto to fight would place the Nine-Tails' chakra at risk of capture by Madara. Their encounter becomes a contest of speed and conviction: A moves to stop Naruto physically while Naruto, in his Nine-Tails Chakra Mode, counters each attack. The confrontation reaches its emotional climax when Naruto demonstrates that he possesses Killer Bee's acknowledgment and when Gaara articulates Naruto's role as the emotional core of the shinobi world. A is ultimately persuaded—not defeated—to allow Naruto to proceed, representing a fundamental shift in his assessment of Naruto's worth.

During the Kage Summit arc, Killer Bee and A jointly perform the Double Lariat on Kisame Hoshigaki after Kisame loses possession of Samehada. The technique appears to decapitate Kisame cleanly. However, it is later revealed that the 'decapitated' Kisame was in fact a White Zetsu duplicate using shapeshifting, while the real Kisame had concealed himself inside Samehada, which Killer Bee had taken as his own sword. This story beat serves to demonstrate both the technique's lethal efficacy and the deceptive intelligence of the Akatsuki's operatives.

Following the formation of the Allied Shinobi Forces, A is appointed Supreme Commander—a role that places him in authority over all five hidden villages' combined military forces for the first time in shinobi history. The position requires him to suppress his instinct for direct combat and instead exercise strategic leadership, coordinating division commanders (including Darui, Shikamaru, and others), managing intelligence (via Inoichi Yamanaka's long-range communication), and responding to crises across multiple simultaneous battlefield theaters. A's performance in this role is mixed: he demonstrates genuine strategic acumen in initial deployment and resource allocation, but his protectiveness toward Killer Bee and his personal hatred of Madara Uchiha periodically threaten to override his judgment. The war ultimately ends not through A's strategy but through Naruto and Sasuke's individual power, though A's organizational role in preventing total collapse during the Ten-Tails' rampage is acknowledged.

Modern Interpretations

A's introduction coincides with one of the manga's highest-stakes story arcs. Kishimoto uses A to embody the 'might makes right' philosophy that Naruto's values must overcome, while simultaneously giving him genuine emotional depth via his relationship with Killer Bee. Published in Weekly Shōnen Jump, then collected in tankōbon volumes by Shueisha. Scholarly Debates
A's speed ranking relative to other shinobi
A, using his Lightning Release Chakra Mode, is portrayed within the narrative as one of the fastest shinobi alive—faster than the Sharingan-enhanced perception of Sasuke Uchiha during the Kage Summit, and requiring Naruto's Nine-Tails Chakra Mode to match. Official databook entries give him maximum (5/5) speed scores. The Third Raikage is described as 'the fastest' of the Raikage, but A's Lightning Armor pushes him beyond his father's documented speed. ⇌ Fan and critical analysis frequently contests this ranking, noting that multiple other characters—including Minato Namikaze (the Fourth Hokage, whose Flying Thunder God technique enables near-instantaneous teleportation), Tobirama Senju (also a Flying Thunder God user), and later Naruto and Sasuke in their final power states—demonstrably exceed or match A's speed by different metrics. The series never resolves a clean speed hierarchy, and A's positioning as 'among the fastest' is qualified by the availability of space-time ninjutsu that render physical speed comparisons somewhat moot.
The racial coding of A and Kumogakure characters
Several scholars and fans of color have argued that Kishimoto's depiction of A and other dark-skinned Kumogakure characters (including Killer Bee) reproduces stereotypical associations between dark skin tone and extreme physical prowess, musical talent (Killer Bee's rap hobby), and aggressive/combative temperament. From this perspective, A exemplifies a pattern in which dark-skinned characters in shōnen manga are positioned as physical threats or obstacles rather than psychologically complex leads, their emotional depth secondary to their role as tests of the protagonist's development. ⇌ Other readers and scholars argue that A is among the most thoroughly developed secondary characters in Naruto—his emotional arc, relationship with Killer Bee, and role as a moral authority who must reckon with Naruto's values give him more interiority than many lighter-skinned supporting characters. From this perspective, the critique conflates visual design choices with narrative substance, and A's physical characterization is consistent with the shōnen genre's broader emphasis on embodied power for all major warriors regardless of depicted ethnicity. Kishimoto has not publicly addressed the racial dimensions of his character designs.
A's effectiveness as Supreme Commander of the Allied Shinobi Forces
Within the narrative, A's appointment as Supreme Commander is presented as appropriate and his leadership is depicted as broadly effective: he manages to unify historically antagonistic villages, distribute forces across multiple battlefields, and prevent the Allied Forces from collapsing entirely under the Ten-Tails' assault. Characters within the story treat his authority as legitimate and his tactical decisions as sound. ⇌ Critical readers and fans argue that A's command during the Fourth Great Ninja War is largely reactive and that the war's actual turning points are driven by individual protagonists (Naruto, Sasuke, Itachi, Gaara) operating outside the chain of command. A's attempt to stop Naruto from reaching the battlefield—the action of a man prioritizing personal protectiveness over strategic need—is frequently cited as evidence that his emotional volatility undermined the war effort, and that his eventual concession to Naruto was a capitulation rather than a command decision.
The ethics of A's jinchūriki policy toward Killer Bee
A's treatment of Killer Bee as his brother first and a jinchūriki second is repeatedly positioned by the narrative as morally superior to the treatment jinchūriki receive in other villages (notably Naruto's own ostracism in Konoha). A's genuinely loving relationship with Killer Bee is presented as the primary reason Killer Bee achieved stable control of the Eight-Tails, and A is shown granting Killer Bee significant personal freedom as a reward for this achievement. ⇌ The same relationship can be read critically as A using emotional bonds to bind Killer Bee more effectively to his role as a living weapon—making the jinchūriki system palatable through familial love rather than questioning the system itself. Killer Bee's status as a 'guardian of the village' is never truly optional; his departure from the village requires A's explicit approval. The affection, however genuine, functions within a structure of control that Killer Bee cannot fully exit.

A way to hold it in your mind

The Fourth Raikage 'A' is one of Naruto's most striking studies in the relationship between power and love—a man who built the entirety of his identity around being the strongest, and whose greatest vulnerability is a single person: his adopted brother. Would you like to explore the ethical dimensions of the jinchūriki system through A's relationship with Killer Bee, or trace how his arc mirrors Naruto's own central theme of protecting people you love?
