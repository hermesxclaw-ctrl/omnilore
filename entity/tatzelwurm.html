Who It Is

The Tatzelwurm (literally 'clawed worm') is a cryptid of the Alpine regions — a dragon-like creature said to inhabit crevices and caves in the Bavarian, Austrian, Swiss, and Italian Alps. Descriptions vary but generally converge on a creature 2-5 feet long, resembling a serpent or lizard with a cat-like head, large luminous eyes, and two short clawed forelegs (but no hind legs). It is said to be extremely aggressive, leaping at intruders from hiding and emitting a high-pitched hissing whistle. Its breath is allegedly poisonous or pestilential, causing swelling and death. Reports surged in the 18th and 19th centuries, appearing in almanacs and scientific journals — some naturalists attempted to classify it as a real (but elusive) reptile. A famous incident in 1929 involved a Swiss photographer who claimed to have photographed a Tatzelwurm but 'lost' the plate in a panic. The creature parallels other European 'worm' legends (lindwurm, wyrm) but is smaller and specifically alpine. Cryptozoologists propose it could be a surviving species of giant salamander (such as Andrias scheuchzeri, named for the Swiss naturalist who documented the 'Homo diluvii testis' fossil — actually a giant salamander, not a human), an unknown lizard, or a cultural memory of prehistoric reptiles. The Tatzelwurm embodies the alpine fear of the inaccessible crevice — the mountain's interior as a living, hostile entity with teeth and claws.
