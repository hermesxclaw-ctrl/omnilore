Who It Is

In the age of sail, the North Atlantic was a sea of secrets, where a ship's crew could swear they saw an island that existed only on the edges of imagination. Buss Island was such a place—a phantom that haunted charts for over two centuries, luring explorers and mapmakers alike, until one man's search for the Northwest Passage finally erased it from the map.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No reliable description exists. Early reports suggest a substantial landmass, but later charts reduced its size. Shepard claimed to have mapped it with harbors and coasts. The 'Sunken Land' was described as a shallow area, possibly a bank or submerged land.

Symbols — On maps, it was often depicted as an island of varying size and shape, sometimes with a coastline and sometimes as a submerged shoal. No specific iconography beyond standard cartographic symbols.

Domains — North Atlantic Ocean, between Ireland and Greenland, around 57°N (later adjusted to ~62°N). Possibly a real underwater bank or seamount.

Accounts I
Discovery by the Emanuel (1578)Frobisher's third expedition accounts

16th century

During the return voyage, the ship Emanuel of Bridgwater sighted what appeared to be a large island. The crew named it Buss Island after their vessel type. It was placed on maps at about 57°N. The island appeared on maps for centuries, influencing navigation and exploration.

Accounts II
Thomas Shepard's exploration (1671)Thomas Shepard's account

17th century

Shepard claimed to have sailed to the island from Dunkirk in the Golden Lion, explored its coast, and mapped it. He reported it as a real, habitable land. His account temporarily revived belief in the island, but later searches failed to find it.

John Ross's disproval (1818)John Ross's expedition journal

19th century

During his first Arctic expedition, Ross sailed to the reported location and found no island, only deep water (no bottom at 180 fathoms). This definitively disproved the island's existence at that spot. The island was removed from official charts, though it lingered on some maps for years.
