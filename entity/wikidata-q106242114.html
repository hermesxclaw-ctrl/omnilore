Accounts

In reconstructed Basque pastoral narrative, Aereda was honored at a high mountain shrine where shepherds left offerings of cheese and bread during the summer transhumance. When a harsh storm destroyed the flocks, the shepherds returned to the shrine and performed a ritual dance, promising an annual feast. The storms subsided, and the custom endured for generations.

Aereda is attested with two places of worship, suggesting a deity venerated at more than one sacred site. In Basque tradition, the same goddess might be honored at separate shrines reflecting different aspects or different communities' devotion. This multiplicity of cult sites is common for important local deities.

Basque folklore contains numerous stories of deities manifesting as standing stones or rock formations. Aereda may have been believed to dwell within a particular stone, receiving offerings of flowers or coins from passersby. Such stones were often at crossroads, bridges, or boundary markers — liminal places where the divine could be encountered.
