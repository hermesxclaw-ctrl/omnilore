The Olmec Dragon (also called the Witz Monster or Earth Monster) is a major supernatural being in Olmec religion, a composite creature with reptilian, avian, and feline features that represents the sky, the earth, and the underworld. Often depicted with a distinctive cleft head, crossed bands (an Olmec symbol of kingship and cosmic order), and flame eyebrows, this creature is the most complex and powerful of all Olmec supernatural beings.

Cultural Significance

The Olmec Dragon is the single most important iconographic complex in Olmec art, appearing on nearly every major Olmec monument. La Venta Altar 4 (or Altar 5) — showing a ruler emerging from the cave-mouth of the Earth Monster — is one of the most famous works of Olmec sculpture and established the artistic convention of the 'ruler in the cave-mouth' that would influence Maya and Aztec art for centuries. The dragon's crossed-band motif became one of the most enduring symbols in Mesoamerican art, used extensively by the Maya and Aztecs to denote preciousness and royal authority.
