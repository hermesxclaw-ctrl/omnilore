Who It Is

The Tarasque is a fearsome dragon-like monster from Provençal folklore that dwelled in the Rhône River near the town of Tarascon. Described as a hybrid creature with the head of a lion, six short bear-like legs, the body of an ox covered in a turtle's shell, and a serpent's tail ending in a scorpion's stinger. Its breath was said to be poisonous, and it emerged from the river to devour livestock, children, and unwary travelers. According to the Golden Legend, the monster was tamed by Saint Martha in the 1st century CE. When the terrified townspeople begged her for help, she went into the forest where the Tarasque lived, found it devouring a man, and calmly sprinkled holy water upon it while holding up a cross. The beast became docile as a lamb at her feet. However, the townspeople, still fearing it, attacked and killed the now-gentle creature. Saint Martha wept and rebuked them for their faithlessness. The city of Tarascon renamed itself after the monster, and a festival called the Fête de la Tarasque has been celebrated there since at least the 14th century, featuring a large effigy of the monster paraded through the streets. The Tarasque is classified as a type of guivre or dragon in French medieval bestiary tradition.
