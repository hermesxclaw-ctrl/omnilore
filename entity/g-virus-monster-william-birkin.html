Who It Is

In the bowels of Raccoon City, a brilliant scientist who sought to perfect humanity's evolution instead became its most grotesque monument. Dr. William Birkin, creator of the G-Virus, was consumed by his own creation, transforming into a shambling, ever-mutating horror that defied death itself. This is the chronicle of that fall, pieced together from Umbrella Corporation files, survivor accounts, and the remnants of a man who traded his humanity for a glimpse of godhood.

Initially, Birkin retains some semblance of his human intellect, pleading for his work and showing signs of desperation. However, as the mutation progresses, he becomes increasingly animalistic, attacking anything in his path with savage ferocity. He exhibits a strong drive to reproduce and spread the G-Virus, as seen in his attempts to implant embryos into victims.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name derives from the G-Virus, a viral agent engineered by Dr. William Birkin. 'G' stands for 'Golgo', a term Birkin used to denote the ultimate evolutionary form. The monster is the result of Birkin's self-infection with the virus.

Powers
Superhuman Strength

The G-Virus Monster possesses immense physical strength, capable of crushing steel, tearing through walls, and overpowering heavily armed opponents.

Limit: Strength is limited by the monster's physical form; it can be temporarily incapacitated by heavy firepower.

Rapid Regeneration

The G-Virus grants the host incredible regenerative abilities, allowing it to recover from gunshot wounds, explosions, and even severe dismemberment.

Limit: Regeneration requires energy; the monster can be killed if its core is destroyed or if it undergoes complete cellular collapse.

Mutation and Evolution

The G-Virus causes the host to mutate and evolve rapidly, producing new limbs, eyes, and other appendages. Each transformation stage grants new abilities and increased size.

Limit: Mutation is uncontrollable and eventually leads to the host's complete loss of humanity and sanity.

G-Embryo Implantation

The monster can implant a G-embryo into a living host, which will gestate and eventually burst out, creating a new G-creature. This is a primary method of spreading the G-Virus.

Limit: The implantation requires close contact and a vulnerable victim; the process takes time and leaves the host incapacitated.

Durability

The G-Virus Monster can withstand extreme trauma, including explosions, falls, and sustained gunfire, without being killed.

Limit: Durability is not absolute; the monster can be defeated with sufficient firepower, especially when targeting its exposed weak points.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In its initial stage, the G-Virus Monster appears as a heavily mutated William Birkin, with a grotesquely swollen right arm and a large eye emerging from his right shoulder. As the infection progresses, Birkin's human features deteriorate, his body grows larger, and additional eyes and limbs sprout. In later stages, he becomes a massive, shambling beast with multiple eyes, exposed muscle, and a gaping maw, losing all human resemblance.

Symbols — The G-Virus Monster is often associated with themes of mutation, evolution, and the loss of humanity. The prominent eye motif symbolizes Birkin's obsession with vision and evolution, as well as the all-seeing nature of the virus. The monster is also iconic for its ever-changing form, representing the virus's relentless drive to evolve.

Domains — The G-Virus Monster primarily haunts the sewers, laboratories, and industrial areas of Raccoon City, particularly the Umbrella Corporation's underground facility. It is a creature of darkness and confinement, thriving in the claustrophobic environments it inhabits.

Accounts I
The Fall of William BirkinResident Evil 2 (both versions), Resident Evil 0 (cameo), various files

1998 (fictional timeline: September-October 1998)

Dr. William Birkin, a leading virologist at Umbrella Corporation, develops the G-Virus, a revolutionary bioweapon. When Umbrella attempts to seize his research, Birkin injects himself with the virus to protect his work. The infection transforms him into a monstrous creature, and he rampages through Raccoon City, attacking soldiers, police, and civilians. He is ultimately killed by Leon S. Kennedy and Claire Redfield, but not before spreading chaos and contributing to the city's destruction. Birkin's transformation leads to the release of the G-Virus in Raccoon City, accelerating the city's infection and demise. His death marks the end of the immediate threat, but the G-Virus samples are later recovered by other parties, leading to further outbreaks.

Accounts II
The Pursuit of Sherry BirkinResident Evil 2 (both versions)

1998

Throughout the game, the G-Virus Monster repeatedly encounters his daughter, Sherry, and attempts to implant a G-embryo into her, seeing her as a suitable host to propagate the virus. This pursuit drives much of the game's tension, as Leon and Claire must protect Sherry while evading the monster. Sherry is infected with the G-Virus but is later cured using a vaccine developed by her mother, Annette. The monster's obsession with Sherry highlights its twisted remnant of paternal instinct.

The Final BattleResident Evil 2 (both versions)

1998

In the climactic confrontation, Leon and Claire face the G-Virus Monster in its most advanced stage, a massive, multi-eyed beast. They manage to destroy it using heavy weaponry and a prototype weapon, finally ending Birkin's rampage. The monster is destroyed, and its body is recovered by the Umbrella Corporation, which later uses its data to further bioweapon research.
