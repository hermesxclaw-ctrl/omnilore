Who It Is

Perchta (also Berchta, Bertha) is a complex Alpine winter goddess who emerges during the Twelve Nights of Christmas (Raunächte, December 25 to January 6). She has a dual nature: to the good and industrious, she appears as a beautiful, radiant woman (die Schöne Perchta) who rewards them with silver coins and blessings for finishing their spinning and housework. To the lazy and dishonest, she manifests as a hag with a long nose and iron teeth (die Wilde Perchta), who slits open the bellies of those who haven't finished their year-end chores, stuffing them with straw and stones. Perchta is closely associated with spinning, women's work, and the fateful turning of the year. She leads a procession of unbaptized children and restless souls known as the 'Perchten.' The Perchtenlauf, a ritual procession of masked runners in elaborate wooden or fur costumes, survives today in the Austrian Alps (especially Salzburg and Tyrol). Perchta may be a survival of an ancient Germanic goddess associated with the hearth, spinning, and the winter solstice — possibly linked to the goddess Frigg or Holda. Her name likely means 'the bright one,' connected to the Old High German 'beraht' (bright).
