Who It Is

In the highlands of Guatemala, long before the Spanish came, there was a goddess of the night who was so vital that a whole ruling house took her name. She was Awilix, the moon, the patroness of weavers and warriors, a figure who rode the darkness on a jaguar's back. Her story is a ghost in the archives—fragments of a lost epic, a name carved in stone and written in colonial manuscripts. But those fragments are enough to see her: a queen of the night, a fierce mother, a political power wrapped in lunar light.

As a lunar deity, Awilix governs the rhythms of the night, fertility, childbirth, and the cycles of women. She is a protector of women, particularly those involved in weaving and other domestic arts. She is also a patroness of the 'Avilix' lineage, a powerful K'iche' ruling house, and thus a bestower of political legitimacy and martial prowess. Her behavior is not explicitly described in surviving myths, but her association with the moon implies a cyclical, predictable, yet mysterious nature.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name's root is uncertain. Some scholars suggest a connection to the K'iche' word for 'moon' or 'night'. The prefix 'Hun-' (as in Hun-Awilix) means 'one' in K'iche', potentially indicating a numbering or ranking among deities.

Pronunciation — ah-WEE-leesh

Aliases — Awilix, Avilix, Auilix, Hun-Awilix, Awilix the Moon, Lady of the Moon

Ownership — K'iche' Maya civilization, highlands of Guatemala (pre-Columbian and contemporary Maya culture)

Form & Signs

Appearance — Awilix is typically depicted as a young, beautiful woman. Her iconography is associated with the moon, often shown with a crescent moon headdress or a rabbit, which in Maya mythology is a lunar symbol. She is also associated with the night sky, jaguars (a nocturnal predator), and water, possibly due to the moon's influence on tides and its connection to wells and cenotes. Accounts I
The Patroness of the Avilix LineagePopol Vuh, Título de Totonicapán

Pre-Columbian (oral tradition), recorded in the 16th century

Awilix is identified as a patron deity of the Avilix lineage, one of the principal ruling houses of the K'iche' kingdom. This lineage, named after her, held significant political and religious power. Her role was to legitimize their rule, grant them martial prowess, and ensure their prosperity. The lineage's name and her worship were intertwined, making her a central figure in the political cosmology of the K'iche' state. The Avilix lineage's political power was divinely sanctioned by their patroness, reinforcing their authority over other lineages and the general population.

Awilix is the same as the Aztec goddess Coyolxauhqui. — They are both lunar deities from different Mesoamerican cultures, but they are distinct figures with different myths and iconographies. Coyolxauhqui was dismembered by her brother Huitzilopochtli, a myth not associated with Awilix.

Awilix is a minor or obscure goddess. — She was a major deity, as evidenced by her role as patron of a powerful lineage and her inclusion in the Popol Vuh. Her apparent obscurity is due to the loss of most K'iche' mythology.

Awilix was only a moon goddess. — She was a multifaceted deity, also associated with fertility, lineage, political power, and warfare. Her lunar aspect was one part of a complex whole.
