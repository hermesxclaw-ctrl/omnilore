Who It Is

Manjushri (Mañjuśrī) is the bodhisattva of transcendent wisdom (prajna), the embodiment of the insight that cuts through ignorance and delusion like a sword. He is one of the oldest and most important bodhisattvas in Mahayana Buddhism, appearing in some of the earliest Mahayana sutras. His name means 'Gentle Glory' or 'Sweet Splendor,' reflecting the union of compassionate gentleness with awe-inspiring wisdom. In iconography, Manjushri is most commonly depicted as a beautiful youth of sixteen years (symbolizing the ever-fresh, ever-new quality of awakened wisdom), seated in lotus posture, his right hand holding a flaming sword of wisdom that cuts through ignorance and duality, and his left hand holding a lotus flower upon which rests the Prajnaparamita Sutra (the Perfection of Wisdom text), the supreme scripture of Mahayana philosophy. His youthful form also carries a deeper meaning: wisdom is not something that ages, decays, or becomes obsolete. In the Vimalakirti Sutra, Manjushri is the only bodhisattva brave enough to visit the layman Vimalakirti, who is feigning illness, and engage him in a profound dialogue on the nature of non-duality—demonstrating that ultimate wisdom transcends even the distinction between monastic and lay life. Manjushri's earthly abode is Mount Wutaishan (Five Terrace Mountain) in Shanxi province, China, one of the four sacred mountains of Chinese Buddhism, where he is believed to manifest to sincere pilgrims, sometimes as an ordinary person, a child, or a beggar. In Tibetan Buddhism, Manjushri is closely associated with the primordial Buddha Vajradhara and is the patron of scholars and debaters. The great Tibetan master Tsongkhapa, founder of the Gelug school, was considered an emanation of Manjushri. The mantra 'Om A Ra Pa Ca Na Dhih' is his seed syllable invocation, used to enhance wisdom, memory, and intellectual clarity.

Attested in Mahayana sutras (Vimalakirti Sutra, Lotus Sutra, Prajnaparamita literature) / c. 1st–3rd century CEEra Early Mahayana; major cult from Gupta period onward; particularly venerated in China and Tibet
