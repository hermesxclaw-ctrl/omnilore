Etymology

The name 'Aim' may derive from Hebrew or Arabic roots associated with fire or burning, though scholarly consensus is lacking. 'Haborym' is an alternate name whose etymology is uncertain, possibly connected to Hebrew 'habor' (to burn) or other Semitic roots.

Cultural Ownership

Western occult tradition, specifically the Ars Goetia, a 17th-century grimoire within the Lesser Key of Solomon. The demon is part of the Christian demonological framework, though its origins may draw on earlier folklore and mythology.

Disputed Identifications

Some occultists and scholars have equated Aim with other fire-related demons, such as Agreas or Flauros, but these identifications are speculative and not supported by primary sources.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the shadowed pages of the Lesser Key of Solomon, there is a demon who arrives not in fire or storm, but in the guise of a beautiful serpent—Aim, the Great Duke of Hell, who carries a torch that sets cities ablaze and grants mortal minds the cunning to outwit kings.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

In the Ars Goetia, Aim appears as a beautiful serpent. When commanded, he transforms into a human shape, but speaks with a hoarse voice. Later illustrations, such as those in the Dictionnaire Infernal, depict him with three heads (serpent, human, and toad) riding a viper, but this is not attested in the primary textual sources.

Symbols & Iconography

The torch is a recurring symbol associated with Aim, representing his ability to set cities and castles ablaze. The serpent form symbolizes cunning and transformation. In later iconography, the three heads and viper mount are artistic additions.

Behaviour & Personality

Aim is described as a Great Duke who is obedient to the summoner's commands. He grants wit and intelligence, but his destructive power over fire suggests a malevolent nature. His hoarse voice in human form may indicate a connection to serpents or a bestial aspect.

Domains & Habitats

As a demon of hell, Aim is associated with the infernal realms. He is invoked in ritual contexts, often within a triangle or circle, and is said to rule over 26 legions of spirits. His ability to set places on fire links him to destruction and chaos.

Version Differences

The Ars Goetia and Pseudomonarchia Daemonum agree on his rank and abilities, but the number of legions varies (26 in both, though some editions say 26 or 28). Later illustrations add visual details not in the texts. Modern occult interpretations may emphasize his role as a bestower of intelligence.

Form

Aim (Haborym) is described as a powerful and strong demon, ruling over 26 legions of demons. His appearance is not explicitly stated in available sources.

Powers

Fire-starting Aim (Haborym) is said to have the ability to set cities, castles, and great places on fire.

Wit and intelligence Aim (Haborym) is said to make men witty in all ways.

Divination Aim (Haborym) is said to give true answers concerning private matters.
