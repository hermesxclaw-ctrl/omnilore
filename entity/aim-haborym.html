Who It Is

In the shadowed pages of the Lesser Key of Solomon, there is a demon who arrives not in fire or storm, but in the guise of a beautiful serpent—Aim, the Great Duke of Hell, who carries a torch that sets cities ablaze and grants mortal minds the cunning to outwit kings.

Aim is described as a Great Duke who is obedient to the summoner's commands. He grants wit and intelligence, but his destructive power over fire suggests a malevolent nature. His hoarse voice in human form may indicate a connection to serpents or a bestial aspect.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Aim' may derive from Hebrew or Arabic roots associated with fire or burning, though scholarly consensus is lacking. 'Haborym' is an alternate name whose etymology is uncertain, possibly connected to Hebrew 'habor' (to burn) or other Semitic roots.

Pronunciation — Aim (AYM); Haborym (ha-BOR-im)

Aliases — Haborym, Aym, Aimoy, Aimor

Powers
Fire Setting

Aim can set cities, castles, and great places on fire, causing destruction and chaos.

Limit: The texts do not specify any limitations, but it is implied that he acts under the command of the summoner.

Granting Wit

Aim makes men witty in all manner of ways, enhancing their intelligence and cunning.

Limit: The texts do not specify any limitations, but this ability is likely invoked for personal gain or problem-solving.

Shape-shifting

Aim can appear as a serpent or take human form, though his human voice is hoarse.

Limit: He only takes human form when commanded, suggesting a degree of control by the summoner.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Ars Goetia, Aim appears as a beautiful serpent. When commanded, he transforms into a human shape, but speaks with a hoarse voice. Later illustrations, such as those in the Dictionnaire Infernal, depict him with three heads (serpent, human, and toad) riding a viper, but this is not attested in the primary textual sources.

Symbols — The torch is a recurring symbol associated with Aim, representing his ability to set cities and castles ablaze. The serpent form symbolizes cunning and transformation. In later iconography, the three heads and viper mount are artistic additions.

Domains — As a demon of hell, Aim is associated with the infernal realms. He is invoked in ritual contexts, often within a triangle or circle, and is said to rule over 26 legions of spirits. His ability to set places on fire links him to destruction and chaos.

Summoning of AimArs Goetia

17th century

The grimoire provides instructions for summoning Aim, including the use of seals and rituals. The demon is said to appear as a serpent and can be commanded to perform tasks. The summoner gains the demon's services, but must be cautious of the demon's malevolent nature.
