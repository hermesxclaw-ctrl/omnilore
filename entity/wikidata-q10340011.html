Who It Is

In the moonlit woods of Shakespeare's imagination, a king of shadows and flowers wages a war of wills with his queen—his jealousy stirring storms that shake the mortal world. But this fairy monarch did not begin as a character in a play; he emerged from medieval French romance, a dwarf with a magic cup, son of a sorceress and a Roman emperor. Oberon is a shape-shifter of legend, a figure who has worn many faces across centuries, from chanson de geste to opera, from gacha games to anime, each retelling adding a new layer to his enigmatic reign.

In Huon, he is courteous and helpful after being befriended, but can be dangerous if ignored. In Shakespeare, he is manipulative, jealous, and prone to using magic for personal ends, but ultimately reconciles and blesses the couples. In later works, he is often a powerful, sometimes capricious fairy monarch.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Variant spelling of Auberon, earlier Alberon. Origin uncertain; possibly connected to Alberich (Germanic dwarf king) or Aubrey. May derive from Old High German 'adal' (noble) + 'ber(n)' (bear). Fanciful Gaelic etymology proposed by Charles Mackay, not accepted.

Pronunciation — /ˈoʊbərɒn/ (OH-bə-ron)

Aliases — Auberon, Alberon, Obreon, King of the Fairies, Faery Prince

Ownership — Primarily French and English literature; later European and global popular culture. Not tied to a specific living tradition.

Powers
Dwarfish stature with beauty

In Huon, Oberon is cursed to remain three years old in height but given great beauty.

Limit: Height cannot be changed.

Magic cup of plenty

A cup that provides wine and food endlessly, similar to the Holy Grail or cornucopia.

Limit: Only works for those he favors?

Love potion (flower juice)

Juice from a flower struck by Cupid's arrow causes the user to fall in love with the next living thing seen.

Limit: Must be applied to the eyes; effect can be reversed with an herb.

Control over nature

His quarrel with Titania disrupts weather and seasons, causing floods, droughts, and diseases.

Limit: Not directly controlled; a side effect of their conflict.

Fairy magic

General magical abilities typical of fairy kings, including shapeshifting (implied), enchantments, and blessing.

Limit: Varies by version.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In Huon de Bordeaux, Oberon is dwarfish (height of three years old) but very handsome. Shakespeare does not specify his appearance, but he is a fairy king and often depicted as regal and otherworldly. In later adaptations, he varies from tall and ethereal to small and impish.

Symbols — Magic cup (ever-full, like a cornucopia), flower with love potion (in Shakespeare), fairy realm, sometimes a crown or scepter. In Dadd's painting, he appears with a butterfly and other fairy figures.

Domains — Forests, fairy realms; in Shakespeare, he and Titania's feud affects nature (weather, crops). In Huon, he appears in a forest. In later versions, he is associated with the fairy kingdom.

Accounts I
Huon de BordeauxAnonymous chanson de geste

13th century

Huon encounters Oberon in a forest. Despite a hermit's warning not to speak, Huon courteously answers Oberon, gaining his friendship and magical aid. Oberon helps Huon on his quests, including to Babylon. Huon succeeds with Oberon's help.

A Midsummer Night's DreamWilliam Shakespeare

1595/96

Oberon quarrels with Titania over a changeling boy. He uses a love potion to make her fall in love with Bottom (transformed into an ass), while also meddling in the loves of four Athenian youths. Eventually he relents, reverses the spell, and blesses the couples. The couples are united, and the fairy royals reconcile.

NimphidiaMichael Drayton

1627

Oberon is jealous of Pigwiggin's love for Queen Mab, leading to a fairy battle. Comic resolution.

Oberon is a Celtic fairy king. — His origins are in French medieval romance, not Celtic mythology.

Oberon is the same as the Germanic Alberich. — The names may be related, but they are distinct characters in different traditions.

Oberon's magic cup is the Holy Grail. — It is similar but not the same; a magical vessel of plenty.
