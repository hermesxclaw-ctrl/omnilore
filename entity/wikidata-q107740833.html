The Approach

He flicks a booger at you — and it detonates like dynamite. In a world of swordsmen, giants, and living dinosaurs, Mr. 5 of Baroque Works weaponizes the most undignified bodily function imaginable, turning a picked nose into a bomb with a deadpan swagger that makes his inevitable humiliation all the more satisfying.

Content advisory. Cartoon explosive violence; comedic gross-out bodily humor (nasal mucus/breath used as explosives); criminal organization themes (assassination, bounty hunting). No sexual violence depicted for this character.
Identity

Mr. 5

Pronunciation Japanese: Misutā Faibu (ミスター・ファイブ) / Jemu (ジェム); English dub: MISTER FIVE / Gem (pronounced 'jem')

Known As

Gem (Jemu) - real name revealed in Vivre Card databookBomu Bomu no Mi no GemMr. Five (English typographic variant)

Etymology

Codename within Baroque Works' numerical hierarchy created by Sir Crocodile (Mr. 0). Male Officer Agents are numbered 0-12 with lower numbers indicating higher rank/strength; Mr. 5 is the second-lowest ranked Officer Agent above only Mr. 6 and below Mr. 4. His real name 'Gem' is English for a precious stone, following Baroque Works' pattern of real names being overlooked while codenames dominate. Devil Fruit name Bomu Bomu no Mi from English 'bomb' + Japanese mi (fruit).

Cultural Ownership

Fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda (Japanese mangaka) for One Piece, serialized in Weekly Shōnen Jump (Shueisha, 1997-present) and produced by Toei Animation. Owned and managed by Shueisha / Shueisha's licensors (VIZ Media in North America). No traditional folklore ownership; entirely modern commercial manga franchise creation.

Disputed Identifications

Not to be confused with Mr. 5's partner Miss Valentine (Mikita) or other Baroque Works numbered agents. Fan confusion occasionally merges Mr. 5's Bomu Bomu no Mi with Mr. 4's gun Lassoo (a gun that ate the Inu Inu no Mi, Model: Dachshund) or with Gladius's Pamu Pamu no Mi (similar explosion ability in Dressrosa). Vivre Card confirms his real name spelling as 'Gem' (ジェム); older fan translations rendered it 'Jem' or 'Gem' interchangeably - both transliterations of same katakana.

Description

Appearance

Mr. 5 is a tall (205 cm), broad-shouldered adult man with light-brown skin, a distinctive large curled mustache and small goatee, and dark sunglasses that hide his eyes. In his Baroque Works Officer Agent uniform he wears a beige/tan military-style long coat with epaulettes and a high collar over a dark shirt marked with a large '5', a matching peaked cap with a '5' badge, light trousers and boots. His hair is short and dark, curled at the sides. His most recognizable mannerism is picking his nose to produce explosive mucus projectiles, often with a deadpan expression. Post-defeat cover story shows him in casual prisoner/escapee clothes, still with sunglasses and mustache. Anime colors him with a slightly darker coat and olive trousers; manga color spreads show variations. No cyborg or non-human features - fully human male. Attested in manga and anime; height and facial hair consistent across sources.

Symbols & Iconography

Numeral '5' as personal sigil (cap, shirt, coat). Explosions and smoke as visual shorthand. Paired visually with Miss Valentine (Mikita) - tall/short, bomb/weight contrast. Baroque Works cross-bones symbol (skull with tilted hat) appears on their transport. In Little Garden, his bombs are drawn with classic manga 'BOM!!' effects and soot-covered victims. His nose-picking gesture itself becomes iconographic, parodying serious bomb-thrower tropes by making the source comically undignified.

Behaviour & Personality

Arrogant, laconic, and dismissive - typical of lower-ranked Officer Agents who overestimate their Devil Fruit. He speaks in a cool, almost bored tone, shows open contempt for non-Devil Fruit users, and believes his Bomu Bomu no Mi makes him invincible. He is loyal to Baroque Works hierarchy and follows orders from Crocodile via Miss All Sunday (Nico Robin) without question, but shows no particular strategic brilliance. He is quick to mock opponents (especially Usopp and Karoo) and slow to recognize danger, continuing to underestimate Luffy and Zoro even after his explosions fail. After defeat, cover story depicts him as listless and later as part of the reformed 'New Baroque Works' running the Spiders Cafe with other former agents, suggesting a more laid-back, almost reformed demeanor. No evidence of sadism beyond professional assassination; his cruelty is casual rather than psychopathic. Attested: manga dialogue Ch. 112-129.

Domains & Habitats

Active as a roaming Officer Agent of Baroque Works, a clandestine criminal organization based in Alabasta (Whisky Peak as forward base, Rainbase as headquarters). Operates primarily in Paradise (first half of Grand Line), specifically deployed to Little Garden (prehistoric island with Dorry and Brogy) to eliminate Princess Nefertari Vivi and the Straw Hats. No fixed homeland revealed; Vivre Card lists origin as Grand Line. After Baroque Works' collapse (Ch. 212), he is imprisoned then escapes, later seen inhabiting the Spiders Cafe (former Baroque Works holiday base) with Miss Valentine, Miss Goldenweek, Mr. 3 and others in the cover story - a desert oasis turned impromptu commune. No supernatural realm association.

Version Differences

Manga vs Anime: Anime adds filler extension to Little Garden fight (Ep. 68-70) with additional explosion gags and extended Usopp/Karoo chase; core outcome identical. Censorship: 4Kids English dub (2004-2007) heavily edited his nose-picking (digitally obscured/removed) and renamed/recolored explosions to reduce gross-out humor; Funimation uncut dub restores original. Color palette varies between manga color pages (lighter coat) and anime (darker khaki). Vivre Card adds canonical real name 'Gem' and height/blood type not stated in manga dialogue. No significant personality or power differences between versions. Live-action Netflix series (2023-present) has not yet adapted Little Garden; Mr. 5 has no live-action appearance as of 2026.

Powers & Abilities

Bomu Bomu no Mi (Bomb-Bomb Fruit)

Source VersionManga Ch. 112, 117-118, 126; Vivre Card; One Piece Blue Deep; Anime Ep. 65-68

Paramecia-type Devil Fruit that turns the user's entire body into a bomb. Any part of the body, including mucus, saliva, breath, and even picked boogers, can be made to explode at will, with the user immune to his own explosions. Mr. 5 typically picks his nose and flicks explosive boogers (Bomu Bomu no Nosekuso / Nose Fancy Cannon), or exhales explosive breath. He can also make his whole body detonate on contact and cause delayed explosions. Explosions are powerful enough to destroy rock and incapacitate normal humans, but shown to be ineffective against high-durability fighters like Luffy and Zoro.

Limitations

Standard Devil Fruit weaknesses: cannot swim, seawater and Seastone (Kairoseki) drain power and prevent explosion; vulnerable to Haki (though not relevant at his introduction, pre-Haki system). Explosions require conscious activation and contact/proximity; can be dodged, blocked, or endured by durable opponents. Does not grant enhanced speed or durability beyond explosion immunity. Booger projectiles are slow and short-range compared to firearms. Later power scaling renders his explosions trivial against New World-level fighters.

Contradictions

Fan misconception that it is Logia (it is not - he does not become explosion itself, he creates explosions); Oda and databooks explicitly classify as Paramecia. Anime filler briefly suggests he can make his eyeballs explode - not in manga and considered non-canonical.

Assassination and Hand-to-Hand Combat

Source VersionManga Ch. 112-129

As an Officer Agent, trained in Baroque Works' covert operations, infiltration, and murder. Shown using basic brawling and kicking, but relies almost entirely on Devil Fruit. No formal martial art or weapon mastery depicted; carries no gun or blade, unlike partner Miss Valentine who uses umbrella/gun. Physical strength is above average (can throw Vivi and create craters with kicks) but far below Straw Hat combatants.

Limitations

No Haki, no Rokushiki, no notable weapon skill. Defeated in one solid hit from Luffy's Gum-Gum Bazooka and Zoro's sword pommel. Ranked low in Baroque Works for a reason.

Contradictions

None - consistently portrayed as physically weaker than higher-ranked agents like Mr. 3 and Mr. 2 Bon Kurei.

Revolver Leg / Kick Bomb (Anime naming)

Source VersionAnime Ep. 68; Manga Ch. 118 (unnamed)

Anime-only naming for a technique where he kicks and causes explosion on impact with his leg. In manga this is simply his body exploding on contact without named attack. Demonstrates that any limb can be detonated.

Limitations

Requires close range; leaves him open to counterattack.

Contradictions

Named only in anime and games; manga does not name individual bomb attacks beyond descriptive dialogue.

Stories & Myths

Debut: Baroque Works Moves - Whisky Peak Aftermath

Source One Piece manga Ch. 110 'Willpower' - Ch. 112 'Little Garden'Culture Japanese manga - One Piece / Baroque Works Saga (Arabasta Saga)Period 1999-2000 (publication); in-universe 1522 Age of the Sea

Summary

After the Straw Hats defeat the bounty hunters of Whisky Peak and learn that their 'benefactors' were actually Baroque Works Frontier Agents, Baroque Works leadership dispatches Officer Agents to eliminate the witnesses: Princess Vivi (Miss Wednesday), her guardian Igaram, and the Straw Hats. Mr. 5 and his partner Miss Valentine (Mikita, the Kilo Kilo no Mi user) are introduced via Den Den Mushi receiving orders from Mr. 0's partner Miss All Sunday. They are shown flying over the sea on Miss Valentine's umbrella, heading for Little Garden where Vivi's group is expected. This establishes Baroque Works' hierarchical response and Mr. 5's role as a low-tier but superhuman enforcer.

Little Garden: Arrival and Provocation

Source One Piece manga Ch. 112-117Culture Japanese manga - Arabasta SagaPeriod 2000

Summary

Mr. 5 and Miss Valentine arrive on Little Garden, a prehistoric island where giants Dorry and Brogy have dueled for 100 years. They encounter Mr. 3 (Galdino) and Miss Goldenweek (Marianne) who are already executing a plan to kill the giants and capture the Straw Hats. Mr. 5 arrogantly interrupts, declaring his Bomb-Bomb power superior, and demonstrates by flicking an explosive booger at a giant dinosaur, obliterating it. He and Miss Valentine then ambush Vivi, Karoo (her duck), and Usopp, while Mr. 3 deals with Luffy and Zoro. Mr. 5 captures Vivi and uses her as bait, mocking Usopp's cowardice.

Little Garden: Bomb vs. Bravery - Usopp and Karoo's Counterattack

Source One Piece manga Ch. 118-126Culture Japanese mangaPeriod 2000

Summary

Mr. 5 confronts Usopp, Vivi, and Karoo in the jungle. He repeatedly flicks explosive nose-mucus and exhales a massive 'Breeze Breath Bomb' that levels trees, while Miss Valentine floats above and drops her 10,000kg press on foes. Usopp, despite terror, uses his ingenuity: he uses Karoo's speed, his own Usopp Hammer (a fake giant hammer that is actually two frying pans), and ultimately his trademark lying/strategy to outwit them. Mr. 5 underestimates Usopp, allowing Usopp to hit him with a surprise attack. Meanwhile Luffy and Zoro, freed from Mr. 3's wax, intervene. Mr. 5 attempts to detonate his entire body against Luffy, but Luffy is immune to blunt explosive force due to rubber body and simply swallows the explosion? Actually Luffy inhales? In manga Luffy takes explosion head-on and is unharmed, then counters.

Defeat and Humiliation on Little Garden

Source One Piece manga Ch. 126-129Culture Japanese mangaPeriod 2000

Summary

After Usopp and Vivi outmaneuver Miss Valentine, Luffy and Zoro arrive. Mr. 5 tries his ultimate move - making his whole body explode upon punching Luffy - but Luffy's Gum-Gum body absorbs the blast. Luffy then delivers a Gum-Gum Bazooka that sends Mr. 5 flying, while Zoro knocks out Miss Valentine. They are left unconscious and soot-covered as the Straw Hats reunite, free Dorry and Brogy from Mr. 3's wax, and escape Little Garden as the giants' duel resumes and the island's volcano erupts. Mr. 5 and Miss Valentine are last seen unconscious in the jungle, later recovered by Mr. 3's group.

Miss Goldenweek's Operation: Meet Baroque Works - Aftermath and New Baroque Works

Source One Piece manga Cover Story Ch. 359-413 (inter-chapter cover serial)Culture Japanese manga - Cover Story serialPeriod 2001-2002 (publication); in-universe shortly after Alabasta's fall (Ch. 212)

Summary

After Baroque Works' collapse and Crocodile's arrest by the Marines, most Officer Agents are imprisoned. Miss Goldenweek (Marianne) uses her Colors Trap art to free Mr. 5, Miss Valentine, and Mr. 3 from the Marine prison ship, but they choose not to rescue Crocodile. Instead, the group - now including Mr. 5, Miss Valentine, Miss Goldenweek, Mr. 3, and later Mr. 2 Bon Kurei's absence noted - travels to an abandoned holiday island, reopens the Spiders Cafe, and starts a new, peaceful life. Mr. 5 is shown working at the cafe, fishing, and living communally, no longer as an assassin. The final cover (Ch. 413) shows them as 'New Baroque Works' running a cafe, with Mr. 5 in casual clothes serving customers, indicating rehabilitation rather than continued villainy.

Legacy Cameo: Baroque Works Reunion Hints

Source One Piece manga Ch. 631 (cover), Vivre Card, and author SBSCulture Japanese manga - Post-TimeskipPeriod 2011-present

Summary

Mr. 5 makes no direct main-story return after the timeskip, but Oda periodically references Baroque Works veterans. In cover requests and Vivre Card updates, Mr. 5 is listed as affiliated with New Spiders Cafe, not with Cross Guild (Crocodile's new organization). This distinguishes him from Crocodile, Daz Bonez (Mr. 1), and Galdino (Mr. 3) who reappear in later arcs. His absence from Impel Down (where Mr. 1, Mr. 2, Mr. 3 and Crocodile were imprisoned) and from Marineford confirms he escaped earlier via Miss Goldenweek. His legacy is primarily as the archetypal 'weak Officer Agent' used to measure Straw Hat growth.

Relationships
Mr. 5→Miss Valentine (Mikita)ally

Official Baroque Works partner - Officer Agent pairs are always male (number) + female (holiday name). Mr. 5 and Miss Valentine operate as a duo, travel together via her Kilo Kilo no Mi umbrella flight, and coordinate attacks (his bombs + her 10,000kg press). Their dynamic is comedic: he is stoic/arrogant, she is vain and giggly, both overconfident. They are defeated together on Little Garden and later escape and live together at Spiders Cafe, suggesting genuine camaraderie beyond professional pairing. No romantic relationship attested.

Mr. 5→Sir Crocodile (Mr. 0)creator

Boss and creator of Baroque Works. Mr. 5 never meets Crocodile face-to-face in depicted story; receives orders via Miss All Sunday. Crocodile views Officer Agents as expendable tools; after Mr. 5's failure, Crocodile shows no concern and moves to deploy stronger agents. Mr. 5 shows absolute obedience and fear/respect for Mr. 0, typical of Baroque Works' compartmentalized cell structure where lower agents do not know the boss's identity.

Mr. 5→Miss Goldenweek (Marianne) and Mr. 3 (Galdino)ally

Fellow Officer Agents of similar low rank. On Little Garden they cooperate (Mr. 3's wax + Mr. 5's bombs) but also compete and bicker, with Mr. 5 dismissing Mr. 3's cunning as cowardice. After defeat, Miss Goldenweek frees Mr. 5 from prison and they become co-founders of New Spiders Cafe. This post-defeat alliance shows a shift from hierarchical colleagues to chosen family. Mr. 3 is the de facto leader of the escape group due to his intelligence.

Mr. 5→Nefertari Vivi (Miss Wednesday)victim

Target of assassination. As Princess of Alabasta who infiltrated Baroque Works, Vivi is marked for death by Crocodile. Mr. 5 is explicitly ordered to kill her on Little Garden. He captures her, mocks her weakness, and attempts to execute her with a point-blank explosion. Vivi is rescued by Usopp and later Luffy. Her survival and eventual defection from Baroque Works directly causes Mr. 5's mission failure.

Mr. 5→Monkey D. Luffy, Roronoa Zoro, Usopphostile

Primary adversaries. Mr. 5 underestimates all three: he considers Usopp a weakling (ironic, as Usopp outsmarts him), believes his bombs can kill Luffy (Luffy's rubber body nullifies explosive blunt force), and ignores Zoro until defeated. Luffy defeats him with a single Gum-Gum Bazooka; Zoro defeats Miss Valentine. This trio's victory establishes the Straw Hats' ability to defeat Devil Fruit users through creativity and durability. No personal vendetta beyond mission.

Mr. 5→Dorry and Brogy (Giants of Little Garden)hostile

Collateral targets. Mr. 5 and other Baroque Works agents exploit the giants' 100-year duel by sabotaging their weapons (Mr. 3's wax, explosives). Mr. 5 demonstrates his power by blowing up a dinosaur near them and later tries to use explosions to kill them, but giants' immense durability renders his bombs ineffective. Dorry and Brogy ultimately ignore him after Straw Hats defeat him, focusing on their honorable duel.

Mr. 5→Karoo (Vivi's Spot-Billed Duck)hostile

Unusual adversarial relationship. Karoo, Vivi's loyal duck, repeatedly carries Usopp and Vivi to safety while being targeted by Mr. 5 and Miss Valentine. Miss Valentine tries to crush Karoo with 10,000kg, Mr. 5 tries to bomb him. Karoo's bravery and speed allow Usopp's plan to succeed. The duck's survival and comedic resilience make Mr. 5 look incompetent.

Baroque Works as State-Subverting Criminal Syndicate

Source Manga Ch. 160-212 (Alabasta arc reveals); Vivre Card

Description

Mr. 5 is an active member of Baroque Works, a secret criminal organization that infiltrated the Kingdom of Alabasta, incited civil war, caused mass starvation and deaths through Dance Powder drought manipulation, and planned to seize the ancient weapon Pluton. As an Officer Agent, Mr. 5 is complicit in assassination, bounty hunting, and terrorism, even if his depicted actions are limited to Little Garden. The organization is modeled on real-world intelligence/mafia structures with compartmentalization to hide culpability.

Scholarly Context

Oda uses Baroque Works to critique mercenary capitalism and colonial exploitation; Mr. 5's low rank illustrates how ordinary operatives enable systemic violence without seeing the full plan.

Attempted Murder of a Minor

Source Manga Ch. 118-123

Description

His explicit mission on Little Garden is to murder Nefertari Vivi, who is 16 years old at the time, along with her allies. He captures her, holds her at point-blank range, and attempts to detonate her. The scene is played with shōnen action tension but depicts attempted femicide/assassination of a child royal.

Scholarly Context

Common in shōnen manga where teenage protagonists face lethal adult assassins; Vivi's age is canonical (Vivre Card). Content is not sanitized - Oda shows Vivi's fear clearly.

Warning: Comedic Gross-Out as Violence — Show unsanitized · Archive policy & legal

Comedic Gross-Out as Violence

Source Manga Ch. 117-118

Description

Mr. 5's power deliberately conflates disgusting bodily humor (nose-picking, mucus) with lethal force. While comedic, it normalizes the idea that even the most undignified human waste can be weaponized, and his casual flicking of explosive mucus at living beings (including animals) is a form of dehumanizing violence played for laughs.

Scholarly Context

Oda frequently uses scatological humor to undercut power fantasy; scholars note this as a way to make a genuinely dangerous ability (bomb creation) appear ridiculous, thereby making his defeat more palatable for young readers.

Contradictions & Disputes

Real Name Spelling: Gem vs Jem

Position A

Real name is 'Gem' (English word for jewel)

Position A — evidence

Vivre Card katakana ジェム is standard transliteration of English 'Gem'; Shueisha English Vivre Card translations and VIZ use 'Gem'; fits Baroque Works gem/jewel naming? Actually Baroque Works real names are varied, but 'Gem' matches English word.

Position B

Real name is 'Jem' (variant transliteration)

Position B — evidence

Older fan scanlations (Aoi Manga, early One Piece Wikia) wrote 'Jem' because ジェム can be romanized either way; no official Japanese romaji given in manga itself.

Scholarly Consensus

Official English sources (Vivre Card, One Piece Card Game) now standardize as 'Gem'; 'Jem' considered outdated fan romanization. Both refer to same katakana.

Devil Fruit Classification

Position A

Bomu Bomu no Mi is Paramecia

Position A — evidence

Databooks (Blue Deep, Vivre Card) and SBS explicitly list Bomu Bomu no Mi as Paramecia; user creates explosions, does not turn body into natural element; he is immune to own explosions but can still be hit normally.

Position B

Fan claim it is Logia because he 'becomes explosion'

Position B — evidence

Anime visual where his body seems to turn into smoke/explosion and fan wikis pre-2010 mislabeled it Logia due to explosion being 'elemental'.

Scholarly Consensus

Unanimous official classification is Paramecia. Misconception persists in casual fandom but not in scholarly/official sources.

Power Source: Nose Only vs Whole Body

Position A

Only his nose mucus/breath explodes

Position A — evidence

Early anime dub and 4Kids edit focused only on nose-picking, leading viewers to think nose is sole source.

Position B

Any part of his body can explode

Position B — evidence

Manga Ch. 118-126 shows he can detonate his entire body, legs, breath, and even hair; Vivre Card states 'whole body is a bomb, including mucus, breath, etc.'

Scholarly Consensus

Whole-body bomb is canonical; nose-picking is just his preferred, most humiliating delivery method.
