Who It Is

Before zero, the void was a philosophical puzzle, a grammatical absence, a blank space on the counting board. Then a humble dot in India began to move, to calculate, to hold place, and in doing so, it transformed mathematics from a tool of counting into a language of the infinite. This is the story of the most revolutionary number ever conceived—a number that represents nothing, yet makes everything possible.

As a mathematical entity, zero behaves according to specific rules: it is the additive identity (adding zero to any number leaves it unchanged), the absorbing element for multiplication (multiplying any number by zero yields zero), and it has no multiplicative inverse (division by zero is undefined). It is neither positive nor negative, and it is an even number. It is the smallest non-negative integer and the largest non-positive integer.

Powers
Additive Identity

Adding zero to any number leaves that number unchanged: x + 0 = x. This is a fundamental property in arithmetic and algebra.

Limit: None

Multiplicative Absorbing Element

Multiplying any number by zero results in zero: x · 0 = 0. This property leads to the zero-product property: if xy = 0, then x = 0 or y = 0.

Limit: None

Division by Zero (Undefined)

Division by zero is undefined in standard arithmetic because zero has no multiplicative inverse. The expression x/0 has no meaning, and 0/0 is an indeterminate form in calculus.

Limit: In some contexts, such as extended real numbers or wheel theory, division by zero is defined differently, but these are non-standard.

Placeholder in Positional Notation

Zero is used as a digit in positional numeral systems to indicate that a certain power of the base is absent. For example, in 205, the zero indicates no tens.

Limit: None

Exponentiation Rules

Any nonzero number raised to the power of zero equals one: x^0 = 1. Zero raised to a positive power is zero: 0^x = 0. The expression 0^0 is indeterminate or defined as 1 in some contexts.

Limit: 0^0 is undefined in some contexts, but often defined as 1 in combinatorics and set theory.

Empty Set Cardinality

In set theory, zero is the cardinality of the empty set. It is also the smallest ordinal number.

Limit: None

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a numeral, zero is typically written as a circle or an oval (0). In many typefaces, it is distinguished from the letter O by being narrower or having a slash. In ancient India, it was often a dot (bindu). The Mayan zero was a shell-like glyph. In Babylonian cuneiform, it was represented by a double wedge or a slanted wedge.

Symbols — The symbol '0' is universal in modern mathematics. The concept of zero is often associated with emptiness, void, nothingness, and the circle as a symbol of eternity or wholeness. In some cultures, zero is symbolized by a dot or a shell.

Domains — Zero exists in the realm of mathematics, specifically in number systems such as integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers. It is the origin on number lines and coordinate systems. It appears in algebra, calculus, set theory, and many other branches.

Accounts I
The Invention of Zero in IndiaHistorical accounts, Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta

c. 5th-7th century CE

Indian mathematicians developed the concept of zero as a number with its own arithmetic rules. Brahmagupta in 628 CE provided rules for operations with zero, including that zero plus a positive number is that number, zero minus a positive number is the negative of that number, and zero times any number is zero. This was a crucial step in the development of the decimal place-value system. The concept of zero as a number enabled the development of algebra and calculus, and the place-value system revolutionized arithmetic.

Accounts II
Transmission to the Islamic World and EuropeAl-Khwarizmi's works, Fibonacci's Liber Abaci

9th-13th century CE

Islamic scholars like Al-Khwarizmi adopted Indian numerals, including zero, and wrote treatises that spread the system. In the 13th century, Fibonacci introduced these numerals to Europe in his book Liber Abaci, which was influential in replacing Roman numerals. The Hindu-Arabic numeral system, with zero, became standard in Europe, facilitating commerce, science, and mathematics.

Mayan Independent Invention of ZeroMayan inscriptions and codices

c. 36 BCE onward

The Maya independently developed a concept of zero, using a shell glyph for zero in their vigesimal (base-20) numeral system. They used it in their calendar and astronomical calculations. The Mayan zero allowed for precise astronomical calculations and a sophisticated calendar system.

Zero was invented by the Arabs. — The concept of zero as a number was developed in India, and the numeral was transmitted to Europe via Islamic mathematicians. The word 'zero' comes from Arabic, but the concept is Indian.

Zero is a positive number. — Zero is neither positive nor negative.
