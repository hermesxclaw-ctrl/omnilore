Who It Is

Galahad's story is a fascinating example of the complexities and nuances of the Arthurian legend, with its rich symbolism and moral themes.

Galahad is characterized as a chivalrous, noble, and heroic figure, who embodies the ideals of knighthood. He is often depicted as a devout Christian, who seeks to fulfill his duty to God and King Arthur.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Derived from the Old French name 'Galeas', possibly related to the Latin 'gallus', meaning 'rooster' or 'cock'.

Aliases — Galeas, Galath, The Perfect Knight, Grail Achiever, Son of Lancelot

Form & Signs

Appearance — Galahad is often depicted as a young, handsome knight, dressed in armor and carrying a sword. He is sometimes shown with a halo or surrounded by a radiant aura, symbolizing his connection to the divine.

Symbols — The Grail, a cup or dish said to have been used by Jesus at the Last Supper, is a central symbol associated with Galahad. He is also linked to the White Knight, a figure from the Arthurian legend.

Domains — Galahad's stories take place in the realm of King Arthur, specifically in Camelot and the surrounding countryside.

Accounts I
The Quest for the Holy GrailLancelot–Grail prose cycle

13th century

Galahad, along with Sir Percival and Sir Bors, embarks on a quest to find the Holy Grail, a sacred cup or dish said to have been used by Jesus at the Last Supper. Galahad achieves the Grail, fulfilling his duty to God and King Arthur.

Accounts II
The Story of Galahad's BirthWikipedia

13th century

Galahad is born to Elaine of Corbenic, who is deceived into believing that Lancelot is her husband. This story explores the circumstances of Galahad's conception and birth. Galahad's illegitimacy is revealed, and he is recognized as the son of Lancelot and Elaine.
