Etymology

The word 'world' derives from Old English 'weorold', meaning 'age of man', from Proto-Germanic '*werald-', from '*wer' (man) + '*ald' (age). It originally referred to the lifetime of a person, later expanding to mean the whole of human existence and the physical universe.

Cultural Ownership

Global; the term is used across all cultures and languages, each with its own equivalent (e.g., 'monde' in French, 'Welt' in German, 'mundo' in Spanish).

Disputed Identifications

The term is inherently ambiguous, referring to either the physical planet Earth, the abstract concept of human civilization, or any of dozens of specific entities that share the name. There is no single canonical definition.

Description

Appearance

As a concept, the world has no physical appearance; as a planet, it is Earth, a blue and green sphere with continents, oceans, and clouds.

Symbols & Iconography

Globes, maps, the planet Earth as seen from space, the 'World' tarot card (which depicts a dancing figure within a wreath), and various logos of organizations using the term.

Behaviour & Personality

The world as a concept is the totality of human experience, history, and the human condition. It is dynamic, complex, and ever-changing.

Domains & Habitats

The world encompasses all of Earth's biomes, from oceans to deserts, and all human societies and cultures.

Version Differences

Different contexts yield different definitions: in astronomy, the world may refer to any planet; in philosophy, it may be a metaphysical construct; in media, it is a title for various works.
