Who It Is

In the shadowy mountains of Arcadia, fifty brothers once dared to test the king of the gods—and paid for it with their lives. Among them was Eumon, a name that survives only as a whisper in an ancient catalogue of impiety.

Eumon is described as 'nefarious and carefree' (in the source material) along with his siblings, indicating a lack of moral restraint. Their impiety led to Zeus's test and their destruction.

Accounts I
The Wickedness of Lycaon's SonsApollodorus, Bibliotheca 3.8.1; Pausanias, Description of Greece 8.2.4-6

Mythic (pre-Trojan War)

Lycaon, king of Arcadia, had fifty sons, including Eumon. They were notorious for their impiety and wickedness. To test them, Zeus visited in the guise of a peasant. The brothers killed a child (often identified as Nyctimus, Lycaon's own son or a hostage) and mixed its entrails into the meal offered to Zeus. The god, disgusted, overturned the table and struck the brothers and their father with lightning, killing them all. Lycaon was transformed into a wolf, but his sons were simply slain. Death of Eumon and his brothers; Lycaon's transformation into a wolf (in some versions); establishment of Zeus Lycaeus worship in Arcadia.
