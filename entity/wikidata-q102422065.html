Who It Is

In the shadowed glens of Greek myth, where monsters breed and heroes are tested, there lurks a creature half-woman, half-serpent—Echidna, the 'She-Viper,' mother of the most fearsome beasts ever to plague the ancient world. Yet this dread mother is not merely a figure of terror; she is a foundational force, a primordial matriarch whose offspring shaped the very landscape of heroism and challenge.

Attested: Dwells in a cave, preys on travelers. Reconstructed: as a chthonic deity, she embodies raw, untamed nature and the dangers of the wild.

Powers
Immortality

As a primordial being, she is ageless and immortal.

Limit: None attested; she is never killed in myth.

Monstrous Progeny

She can give birth to fearsome monsters, each with unique powers.

Limit: Only with Typhon as the father; the offspring are specific to that union.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested: Upper body of a beautiful woman, lower body of a serpent. Often depicted with a speckled or viper-like tail. Reconstructed: some vases show her with a full serpent body and a woman's head.

Symbols — Serpent imagery, caves, and associations with the underworld. In art, she appears on vases as a fearsome creature alongside her offspring.

Domains — A cave in the land of the Arimi (often identified with Cilicia or Anatolia), or in Scythia according to Herodotus.

Accounts I
The Mother of MonstersHesiod, Theogony

Archaic

Echidna, born to Phorcys and Ceto, dwells in a cave. She mates with Typhon, and together they produce Orthrus, Cerberus, the Hydra, the Chimera, and other monsters. Their offspring become the adversaries of heroes, populating Greek mythology with challenges.

Accounts II
Heracles and EchidnaHerodotus, Histories

Classical

In Scythia, Heracles encounters Echidna, who returns his lost horses in exchange for a tryst. They have three sons, and the one who can string Heracles' bow becomes the ancestor of the Scythians. This story explains the origin of the Scythian people.
