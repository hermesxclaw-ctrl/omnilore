The Approach

In the sprawling tapestry of Greek myth, the name Euippe—'good mare'—echoes across eight distinct women, each a thread of tragedy, lineage, and metamorphosis. From Danaids who slew their husbands to a mother of the Pierides who dared challenge the Muses, these figures embody the diverse fates of women in ancient legend.

Content advisory. Contains themes of murder, divine punishment, and tragic death.
Identity

Euippe

Pronunciation yoo-IP-ee (English approximation)

Known As

Evippe

Etymology

From Ancient Greek Εὐίππη (Euíppē), meaning 'good mare' (eu 'good' + hippos 'horse').

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology

Disputed Identifications

The name is shared by eight distinct mythological women; some are possibly identical in different traditions, but ancient sources treat them as separate individuals.

Description

Appearance

No physical descriptions of any Euippe survive in ancient sources.

Symbols & Iconography

None attested.

Behaviour & Personality

The various Euippes are defined by their actions: the Danaid murders her husband; the Pierides' mother is implied to share in her daughters' hubris; others are passive figures in genealogies.

Domains & Habitats

Various locations: Argos, Thessaly, Paionia, Ithaca, Orchomenus, Italy, Crete.

Version Differences

Each Euippe is a separate individual; no single narrative unites them.

Stories & Myths

The Danaid Euippe

Source Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.1.5Culture GreekPeriod Heroic Age

Summary

Euippe, daughter of Danaus and Polyxo, was one of the fifty Danaids who married the fifty sons of Aegyptus. She wed Imbrus and murdered him on their wedding night, as commanded by her father.

Participants

Euippe

Imbrus

Danaus

Polyxo

Consequences

The Danaids were punished in Tartarus for their crimes, though some sources say they were eventually purified.

Variants

Another Euippe, also a Danaid, married either Argius or Agenor.

Mother of the Pierides

Source Ovid, Metamorphoses 5; Pausanias 9.29.1Culture GreekPeriod Heroic Age

Summary

Euippe of Paionia, by Pierus, bore nine daughters who challenged the Muses to a singing contest. Defeated, they were transformed into magpies.

Participants

Consequences

The Pierides were turned into magpies, forever chattering.

Variants

Pausanias names the mother as Euippe, but other traditions omit her.

Mother of Euryalus

Source Parthenius, Love Romances 3Culture GreekPeriod Heroic Age

Summary

Euippe, daughter of Tyrimmas, bore Odysseus a son, Euryalus, who was later mistakenly slain by his father.

Participants

Consequences

Tragic death of Euryalus; Odysseus unknowingly killed his own son.

Variants

This story is not in Homer; it appears in later sources.

Contradictions & Disputes

Number of Danaids named Euippe

Position A

Apollodorus lists two distinct Danaids named Euippe.

Position A — evidence

Apollodorus 2.1.5 lists both with different mothers.

Position B

Some scholars conflate them.

Position B — evidence

Lack of distinguishing details in other sources.

Scholarly Consensus

The majority view is that they are separate individuals.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

No primary sources for the Euippe of Daunus and Alaenus. No physical descriptions. The exact lineage of the Pierides is uncertain. The corrupted name in Hyginus for Meriones' mother is unresolved. Further research into scholia and lesser-known mythographers may yield additional details.

Research Scope

This dossier compiles the eight mythological figures named Euippe (or Evippe) as attested in ancient Greek and Roman sources, primarily Apollodorus, Hyginus, Parthenius, Pausanias, and Ovid. It synthesizes available evidence, notes variant traditions, and identifies gaps in the historical record.
