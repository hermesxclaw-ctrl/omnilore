Who It Is

In the sprawling tapestry of Greek myth, the name Euippe—'good mare'—echoes across eight distinct women, each a thread of tragedy, lineage, and metamorphosis. From Danaids who slew their husbands to a mother of the Pierides who dared challenge the Muses, these figures embody the diverse fates of women in ancient legend.

The various Euippes are defined by their actions: the Danaid murders her husband; the Pierides' mother is implied to share in her daughters' hubris; others are passive figures in genealogies.

Accounts I
The Danaid EuippeApollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.1.5

Heroic Age

Euippe, daughter of Danaus and Polyxo, was one of the fifty Danaids who married the fifty sons of Aegyptus. She wed Imbrus and murdered him on their wedding night, as commanded by her father. The Danaids were punished in Tartarus for their crimes, though some sources say they were eventually purified.

Mother of the PieridesOvid, Metamorphoses 5; Pausanias 9.29.1

Heroic Age

Euippe of Paionia, by Pierus, bore nine daughters who challenged the Muses to a singing contest. Defeated, they were transformed into magpies. The Pierides were turned into magpies, forever chattering.

Accounts II
Mother of EuryalusParthenius, Love Romances 3

Heroic Age

Euippe, daughter of Tyrimmas, bore Odysseus a son, Euryalus, who was later mistakenly slain by his father. Tragic death of Euryalus; Odysseus unknowingly killed his own son.

Other EuippesVarious

Heroic Age

Euippe, daughter of Leucon, bore Andreus a son Eteocles, king of Orchomenus. Euippe, daughter of Daunus, was loved by Alaenus. Euippe, mother of Meriones by Molus, is mentioned by Hyginus. Genealogical connections to notable heroes.
