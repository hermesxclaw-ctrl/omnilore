Who It Is

Uriel is the archangel of wisdom, prophecy, and illumination — the 'Fire of God' whose name signifies divine light and understanding. Though absent from the canonical Hebrew Bible and New Testament, Uriel appears prominently in Second Temple Jewish apocalyptic literature and early Christian texts. In the Book of Enoch, Uriel is one of the four principal archangels alongside Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael; he guides Enoch through the heavens, explaining the celestial mechanics of the sun, moon, and stars, and interpreting the visions of cosmic judgment. His pedagogical role — patiently explaining the mysteries of creation — established him as the angel of wisdom and knowledge. In 2 Esdras (4 Ezra), Uriel engages the prophet Ezra in an extended theological dialogue, answering profound questions about divine justice, the fate of the righteous and wicked, and the signs of the end times. When Ezra despairs over the destruction of Jerusalem and questions God's ways, Uriel responds with the famous challenge: 'You cannot understand the things with which you have grown up; how then can your mind comprehend the way of the Most High?' In later Christian tradition, Uriel acquired additional roles: he is the angel who warned Noah of the Flood, the cherub who guards the gates of Eden with a flaming sword (Genesis 3:24), and in some Renaissance texts, the angel who brought the knowledge of alchemy and Kabbalah to humanity. Eastern Orthodox tradition honors Uriel as one of the seven archangels, his feast falling on November 8. He is the patron of the arts, sciences, and confirmation (the sacrament of wisdom). Milton's Paradise Lost describes Uriel as the regent of the sun, the sharpest-sighted spirit in heaven, whom Satan deceives by assuming the form of a lesser angel to gain directions to Eden. This episode ambiguously presents Uriel as supremely perceptive yet capable of being outwitted — an angel of profound knowledge whose wisdom has limits. Uriel embodies the celestial intellect: the divine mind sharing its understanding with humanity, illuminating the darkness of ignorance with the fire of God's truth.
