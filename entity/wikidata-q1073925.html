The Approach

In the beginning, when the islands of Japan had just been shaped from the brine of the primordial sea, a divine breath swept away the morning mists that veiled the new land. That breath, according to the Nihon Shoki, became Shinatsuhiko, the Long Blowing Lad, the wind god who still whispers through the pines of Ise and howls across the rice fields of Shizuoka.

Etymology

The Kojiki writes the name as 志那都比古神, which is interpreted as 'Long Blowing Lad' (or 'Long Wind Boy'). The Nihon Shoki uses 級長津彦命, with a similar meaning. 'Shina' may relate to 'shinatsu' (to blow) and 'hiko' means 'prince' or 'lad'. The counterpart 'Shinatobe' uses 'tobe' which may mean 'maiden' or 'lady'.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese, Shinto

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars suggest that Shinatobe may have originally been a separate goddess of wind, later paired with Shinatsuhiko as his feminine counterpart. The names Ame no Mihashira and Kuni no Mihashira are also used to refer to wind deities, but it is unclear if these are earlier names for the same deities or separate entities.

Description

Appearance

As a wind god, Shinatsuhiko is typically depicted as a male figure, often carrying a bag of winds or blowing air. In art, he may be shown with flowing hair and robes, representing the wind's movement. However, the provided source does not specify a physical appearance.

Symbols & Iconography

Wind, breath, morning mists, pillars (as in Ame no Mihashira, 'pillar of Heaven'), and possibly the sky.

Behaviour & Personality

As a personification of wind, he is associated with the force and movement of air. He is a benevolent deity who clears mists and supports the sky. No specific personality traits are given in the source.

Domains & Habitats

Wind, sky, the boundary between heaven and earth. He is enshrined at wind shrines (Kaze-no-Miya) at Ise Grand Shrine and at Oki-jinja in Shizuoka.

Version Differences

In the Nihon Shoki, he is born from Izanagi's breath. In the Kojiki, his name is given as Shinatsuhiko. The norito texts mention both Shinatsuhiko and Shinatobe, suggesting a male and female pair. Earlier names, Ame no Mihashira and Kuni no Mihashira, are also used for wind deities, but it is uncertain if these are exactly the same entities.

Stories & Myths

Birth from Izanagi's breath

Source Nihon ShokiCulture JapanesePeriod Mythic

Summary

After Izanagi and Izanami created the islands of Japan, Izanagi blew at the morning mists that obscured the new land. From that breath, Shinatsuhiko was born.

Participants

Izanagi

Shinatsuhiko

Consequences

Shinatsuhiko became the god of wind, and the mists were cleared, revealing the islands.

Variants

The Kojiki gives a different name and does not explicitly describe this birth, but the name 'Long Blowing Lad' suggests a similar origin.
