Who It Is

The Monaciello (Little Monk) is a small supernatural being of Neapolitan folklore, appearing as a tiny figure in a monk's habit with a shrunken, wizened face. He inhabits the underground world of Naples — its ancient Greco-Roman tunnels, catacombs, and the cellars of old palazzi. The Monaciello is associated with hidden treasure: many Neapolitan legends tell of families who treated him kindly and were rewarded with bags of gold, while those who mocked or tried to trap him found their money turned to dust or stones. He is not inherently malicious but deeply capricious — one must never thank him directly (which would break the spell) but instead leave offerings of wine, bread, and cheese. His cowl is particularly important; if you manage to snatch the Monaciello's cap from his head, he must reveal the location of hidden treasure. The Monaciello's origin is debated: he may be a degraded memory of the lares (Roman household gods), the spirits of monks who administered underground cemeteries, or simply a folk-psychological explanation for the underground of a densely layered city where ancient wealth was constantly being unearthed. The Neapolitan writer Matilde Serao collected extensive Monaciello lore, and the figure remains a beloved part of Neapolitan cultural identity — a reminder that beneath the city's noisy streets lies another Naples, ancient and full of secrets.
