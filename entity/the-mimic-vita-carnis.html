Who It Is

A species of humanoid predators that evolved to imitate humans. Mimics pass through four life stages: Crawl (spider-like infant attaching to hosts), Meat Snake (serpentine adolescent that strangles prey), Elder Mimic (gaunt 2.5m adult imperfectly mimicking human speech and appearance), and Harvester (stationary tree-like organism producing new Crawls). Elder Mimics are the most dangerous: they lure victims with cries for help before paralyzing them with venomous spines.

Form

Elder Mimic: tall, gaunt humanoid with gray leathery skin, elongated limbs, and a face that is a crude approximation of human features — two sunken eye sockets, a slit for a nose, a mouth that cannot fully close revealing needle teeth. Movements are jerky. Voice is distorted parody of human speech. Harvester: massive immobile trunk rooted in the ground, with the Elder Mimic's face frozen mid-scream in the bark.

Cultural Significance

Vita Carnis's 'fictional nature documentary' format — presenting monsters as real animals — distinguishes it from other analog horror. The Mimic's life cycle feels biologically plausible and therefore more terrifying.
