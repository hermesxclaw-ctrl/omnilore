Who It Is

Excalibur is the sword every later magic sword is quoting — and the first thing to know is that it is not the sword in the stone. Malory keeps them distinct: the stone-sword proves Arthur's right; Excalibur, received later from a hand rising out of a lake, sustains his reign — sovereignty as a loan from the otherworld, not a possession. Its blade blazed like thirty torches in battle, but the romances are emphatic that the scabbard was worth ten of the sword: while Arthur wore it he could not bleed, and its theft by Morgan le Fay is the quiet beginning of his end. The sword's greatest scene is its last. Arthur, dying at Camlann, orders Bedivere to throw Excalibur back into the water. Twice the knight hides it instead — the treasure is too great to waste — and twice Arthur knows he lies, because a man who has thrown Excalibur away has seen something no liar can describe. The third time: an arm rises from the lake, catches the sword, brandishes it thrice, and draws it under. That image — the gift returned to the giver, the kingdom's power going home — is the whole Arthurian theology in one gesture: you do not own sovereignty; you borrow it, and the lake keeps the receipts.

Provenance

Caledfwlch in Welsh tradition (Culhwch and Olwen); Latinized by Geoffrey of Monmouth as Caliburnus, forged — he says — in Avalon; the lake-gift and lake-return canonized by the French romances and Malory.

Attested in Geoffrey of Monmouth, Malory's Le Morte d'ArthurEra 12th century onward

Form

A great cross-hilted blade too bright to look at in anger; its scabbard chased with gold; in the oldest Welsh telling its name means hard-breach or battle-breach.

Powers

Battle-radiance that blinds enemies; cuts iron like wood; the scabbard's bleeding-ward — the true strategic asset; and the sword's own homing instinct toward the water it came from.

Weaknesses & Wards

Separation of sword and scabbard; the mortality of the man who bears it — Excalibur survives every battle; its kings do not.

Field Notes

⚱ Malory occasionally confuses the two swords himself — eight centuries of readers have politely covered for him.

⚱ The scabbard-beats-sword point is medieval literature's best insurance lesson.

⚱ A 7-year-old girl pulled a sword from the lake at Dozmary Pool — Cornwall's claimed Excalibur lake — in 2017; the sword proved to be a film prop, the story proved the legend still breathes.

Cultural Significance

The template for every named blade in fantasy from Anduril to Master Swords; the throw-it-back ending remains among the most retold scenes in Western literature.
