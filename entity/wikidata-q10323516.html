Who It Is

In the shadowed forests of Philippine folklore, where the boughs creak and the night wind carries whispers, there lurks a creature of immense size and terrifying power—the Manaul. Known to some as a giant bird of prey, to others as a spirit of the wild, this enigmatic beast has haunted the stories of the archipelago for centuries, its image shifting with each telling, yet always commanding awe and fear.

Manaul is a predator, feared for carrying off livestock and even humans. It is solitary and territorial, often associated with remote mountains or forests. Some tales describe it as a guardian of sacred places, attacking those who trespass.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Manaul is typically described as a colossal bird of prey, with wingspans that darken the sky and talons like iron hooks. Its feathers are often dark, sometimes iridescent, and its eyes glow with an eerie light. Some accounts give it a human-like face or the ability to shape-shift, but these are inconsistent.

Symbols — In art, Manaul is sometimes depicted as a stylized bird with exaggerated wings, symbolizing power and the untamed wild. It may appear in carvings or textiles, but no standardized iconography is recorded.

Domains — High mountains, dense forests, and isolated cliffs across the Philippines.

A child is snatched from a village by a Manaul and carried to its mountain nest. A brave hunter climbs the cliff, slays the bird, and rescues the child. The village is saved; the hunter becomes a hero.
