Who It Is

The Trollkatt (literally 'troll-cat' or 'magic-cat,' also called Bjära in Swedish, Tilberi in Icelandic) is a supernatural creature created by a witch to steal milk and other resources from neighbouring farms. In Norwegian tradition, the witch creates the Trollkatt by shaping it from various materials (old rags, human hair, wood shavings, stolen communion bread) and breathing life into it with a drop of her own blood (often from her left ring finger). The Trollkatt then rolls across the countryside as a ball of yarn or a cat-like creature, entering barns to suck milk from cows and goats, returning to the witch to vomit the stolen milk into her churn. The milk is tainted and yields only thin, bluish butter — a sign of witchcraft known as 'trollsmør' (troll butter). In some traditions, the Trollkatt is a living creature that must be fed constantly; it grows insatiable, eventually turning on and devouring its creator. In Icelandic folklore, the Tilberi is created from a human rib stolen from a graveyard and a stolen communion wafer, wrapped in wool and kept between a woman's breasts — it lives by sucking her blood from the inside of her thigh and stealing sheep's milk. The Trollkatt is documented extensively in Scandinavian witch-trial records (16th-17th centuries), where accused witches confessed to creating such creatures. It represents the deep anxiety of pre-industrial Scandinavian rural life: milk was wealth, and a neighbour's inexplicable dairy prosperity implied supernatural theft. The Trollkatt is the economic terror of the smallholder — the supernatural explanation for the milk that was there yesterday and gone today.
