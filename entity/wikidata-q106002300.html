The Approach

In the autumn of 2016, a long-striding bay colt with a diamond on his forehead swept through Japan's most storied races, beating the nation's best older horses as a three-year-old and announcing himself as a classic winner of rare promise. Satono Diamond, a son of the legendary Deep Impact, was bred for greatness and delivered it in a season that still echoes in Japanese racing lore.

Content advisory. None. This is a factual account of a racehorse's career.
Identity

Satono Diamond

Pronunciation sah-TOH-noh DYE-uh-mond (Japanese: サトノダイヤモンド, Hepburn: Satono Daiyamondo)

Etymology

The name combines the 'Satono' prefix used by owner Hajime Satomi with 'Diamond', referencing the horse's distinctive diamond-shaped star marking.

Cultural Ownership

Japan (Thoroughbred racing)

Disputed Identifications

None. The name is unique in racing records.

Description

Appearance

Bay horse with a diamond-shaped white star. No other markings are recorded in the source material.

Symbols & Iconography

The diamond star is the namesake and a natural symbol.

Behaviour & Personality

Described by trainer Yasutoshi Ikee as 'very stable mentally' and able to handle any kind of race. Jockey Christophe Lemaire praised him as 'such an easy ride' and noted his big heart. He was known for his long stride and late acceleration, though he could be keen in the early stages.

Domains & Habitats

Trained at Ritto Training Center in Japan, with a European campaign in France in 2017.

Version Differences

Not applicable; there is only one Satono Diamond.

Powers & Abilities

Racing ability

Top-class middle-distance and long-distance turf runner, with a strong finishing burst. Won from 1800m to 2500m, placed in 3000m and 3200m races.

Limitations

Failed to perform on soft ground in France (Prix Foy) and against elite older horses in 2017-2018.

Stories & Myths

2016 Classic Season

Source Wikipedia (provided)Culture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2016

Summary

After winning the Kisaragi Sho, Satono Diamond finished third in the Satsuki Sho and second in the Tokyo Yushun. In autumn, he won the Kobe Shimbun Hai, Kikuka Sho, and Arima Kinen, defeating older horses and securing the JRA Award for Best Three-Year-Old Colt.
