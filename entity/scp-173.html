Who It Is

You're a D-Class personnel, which means you're expendable. They lock you in a concrete room with a statue — a lumpy, spray-painted thing made of rebar and concrete. 'Don't blink,' they told you. You laughed. You're not laughing now. It's been four hours. Your eyes are burning. Every time you blink — even for a split second — the statue has moved closer. It's not a statue. It was never a statue. And you just heard a wet, scraping sound directly behind you.

SCP-173 is the first SCP (Special Containment Procedures) entry ever written, posted to 4chan's /x/ board in 2007 by an anonymous user who kicked off what would become the SCP Foundation — a vast collaborative fiction project documenting a secret organization that Secures, Contains, and Protects the world from anomalous objects, entities, and phenomena. SCP-173 is 'The Sculpture' — a concrete and rebar statue that moves at incredible speed when not observed. It cannot move while within a direct line of sight, but the instant you blink, look away, or the lights go out, it snaps to a new position with an audible scraping sound. It kills by snapping the neck at the base of the skull, leaving no other marks.

SCP-173 is contained at an undisclosed SCP Foundation site in a locked chamber that is cleaned weekly by D-Class (Disposable Class) personnel — convicts who have been assigned to SCP duty. At least three personnel must enter during cleaning, and two are instructed to alert each other before blinking. Despite these precautions, SCP-173 has killed hundreds of personnel. The entry notes that the chamber floor is covered in a reddish-brown mixture of blood and feces — the material the statue appears to produce as waste after it kills. This grotesque detail cemented SCP-173's reputation as one of the Foundation's most disturbing entities.

Origin Post

SCP-173 was posted to 4chan's /x/ (paranormal) board on June 22, 2007, by an anonymous user later identified as Moto42 (Wesley Williams). The post, titled 'SCP-173 - The Sculpture,' followed a simple containment-procedure format and included a photograph of the sculpture 'Untitled 2004' by Japanese artist Izumi Kato. The post went viral on /x/, inspiring a wave of imitation SCP entries that eventually coalesced into the SCP Foundation wiki (established 2008). The original post is preserved as the wiki's first entry and has become the foundation (literally) of one of the internet's most ambitious collaborative fiction projects, with over 7,000 SCP entries as of 2024.

Form

SCP-173 is a humanoid statue standing approximately 2 meters tall, constructed from concrete, rebar, and Krylon brand spray paint. Its body is lumpy, asymmetrical, and crudely sculpted — stubby legs, thick arms that end in three-fingered hands, and a bulbous, distorted head. Its face is a crude, featureless lump. The statue is painted in abstract patterns — primary colors over grey concrete — that give it an almost whimsical appearance that makes its violence more shocking. After killing, the floor beneath it becomes coated in a reddish-brown substance (blood and fecal matter) that appears to be a byproduct of some unknown biological process. The rebar in its construction is occasionally visible, protruding through the concrete like bone through skin.

Powers

Superhuman speed and strength (capable of snapping necks instantly). The ability to move only when not in direct line of sight — it is 'quantum-locked' when observed, becoming a completely immobile statue. This effect works through cameras, mirrors, and any form of direct visual observation. It produces a scraping sound when it moves, caused by its concrete body dragging across the floor. It appears to be animate and hostile despite being composed entirely of inorganic materials. It generates biological waste (blood and feces) despite having no digestive system, implying some form of anomalous metabolism.Anomalous Speed and Mobility When not directly observed, SCP-173 can move at an incredible, seemingly instantaneous speed. It can traverse a room, cross a hallway, or close a distance of several meters in the time it takes a human to blink. This speed is not a form of teleportation but rather an extreme, superhuman acceleration. Original SCP-173 article and all major adaptations. It cannot move when it is within the direct line of sight of any living being. This is an absolute, unbreakable rule of its existence. The exact nature of its movement is debated. Some tales suggest it is a form of teleportation, while others imply it is simply incredibly fast. The original article is ambiguous, but the 'blink and it moves' mechanic is universally consistent.Lethal Neck Snap SCP-173's preferred and most iconic method of killing is to break a human's neck at the base of the skull. It is a fast, brutal, and efficient kill, leaving its victims with a horrifically snapped neck. Original article, video games, and tales. It appears to be a purely physical attack. It has not demonstrated any other methods of harming or killing, though its speed makes it nearly impossible to defend against. None. The neck snap is the established and consistent method of execution.Invulnerability to Conventional Harm The original article notes that SCP-173 is 'extremely hostile' and that its construction is durable. It is implied that it is resistant to physical damage, though it is not explicitly indestructible. Containment procedures focus on observation, not on damaging it. Original article, where it is described as being made of concrete and rebar. Its durability is not absolute. It is not described as being immune to all forms of harm, but its primary threat is its movement, not its toughness. Some tales and adaptations depict SCP-173 being damaged or destroyed, while others treat it as effectively invulnerable. This is a point of inconsistency within the broader canon.

Behavior & Habitat

SCP-173 is a simple, single-minded killer. It remains completely still when observed and moves — violently and swiftly — the instant observation breaks. Its only known method of attack is snapping the neck at the base of the skull, which it does with apparently practiced precision. It does not communicate, does not display strategy beyond exploiting the blink reflex, and does not appear to have higher intelligence. It attacks indiscriminately — D-Class, researchers, anyone in the room. It does not appear to feed, sleep, or have any needs. Its only observable drive is to kill anything it can reach when unobserved.

Weaknesses & Wards

SCP-173 can be contained by continuous observation — multiple observers who stagger their blinks can keep it immobilized indefinitely. It cannot move through doors or walls (it must be able to scrape along the floor to its target). Its complete immobility when observed is absolute — even a single maintained line of sight from any source neutralizes it. It has no known ranged attack. The containment procedure (clean by teams of three, stagger blinks, maintain visual contact) is simple and effective when properly followed. It cannot move in complete darkness if there is no physical path to a target (though this is a dangerous assumption to test).

Incident Log

The foundational story is not a narrative but a set of procedures. It describes how SCP-173 was found, its initial containment, and the absolute necessity of maintaining eye contact. The most famous detail is the incident where a subject was killed, and the only clue was a smear of red paint on the floor, indicating that SCP-173 had been active. The story is told through the cold, clinical language of a containment protocol, which makes the horror all the more effective.

The game puts the player in the role of a D-class test subject during a massive containment breach at Site-19. SCP-173 is one of the first and most persistent threats the player encounters. The game's core mechanic is managing your blinking; you must constantly look at SCP-173 to keep it frozen, but you also need to look away to navigate and avoid other threats. This creates a constant, nerve-wracking tension that made the game a viral sensation.

In some canons, SCP-173 is connected to a larger mythology involving a 'Broken God' or a war between ancient factions. These tales give SCP-173 a deeper, more mysterious origin, suggesting it may be a fragment of a destroyed deity or a weapon from a forgotten conflict. These stories are not universally accepted but add layers of meaning to the simple statue.

Field Notes

█ SCP-173 was posted only three months after Doctor Who's Weeping Angels first appeared — both independently developing the 'don't blink' concept.

█ The photograph in the original SCP-173 post is of 'Untitled 2004' by Japanese artist Izumi Kato — a legitimate artwork, not a prop.

█ The sound SCP-173 makes when moving is described in some tales as 'stone scraping on stone' and in others as 'wet concrete dragging across bone.'

█ SCP-173 can be stopped by closing your eyes — it can't move if you can't see it, but it also can't move if someone else IS watching.

█ The Foundation's 'D-Class' designation is explicitly dehumanizing: 'Disposable' — personnel who are expected to die in the line of duty.

Cultural Significance

SCP-173 is the ur-text of the SCP Foundation, the collaborative fiction project that has become one of the internet's largest and most enduring creative communities. With over 7,000 entries, spin-off games (SCP: Containment Breach, SCP: Secret Laboratory), films, podcasts, and merchandise, the SCP universe is a cultural phenomenon. SCP-173's 'don't blink' mechanic entered popular consciousness alongside Doctor Who's Weeping Angels (interestingly, a parallel evolution — the Angels first appeared three months before SCP-173 was posted). The containment-procedure format has been imitated across the internet, and SCP-173's iconic photograph (the Izumi Kato sculpture) is instantly recognizable to millions.

Modern Interpretations

SCP-173 has been featured in multiple video games, most notably 'SCP: Containment Breach' (2012), where it is a primary antagonist that forces the player to manage the blink mechanic in real-time. The SCP Foundation wiki continues to expand, with SCP-173 frequently referenced in cross-testing logs, tales, and other entries. The statue's design was formally separated from Izumi Kato's 'Untitled 2004' in 2022 due to licensing concerns — the wiki now uses an original design — but the original image remains iconic. SCP-173's 'quantum lock' concept has been analyzed in philosophical discussions of observation and reality. The containment-procedure format has influenced real-world emergency planning documents and tabletop RPG design.

What We Don't Know

The true origin of SCP-173 is intentionally left a mystery. There is no canonical answer to who created it, why it exists, or what its ultimate purpose is. The identity of the original author, 'Moto42', is known, but his personal inspiration for the creature is not publicly documented. The exact nature of its movement—whether it is super-speed or a form of teleportation—is also left ambiguous in the original text. The full scope of its powers and weaknesses is not exhaustively documented, leaving room for fan interpretation and speculation.

A way to hold it in your mind

SCP-173 is not a monster you can fight or outrun. It is a test of attention, a puzzle where the only winning move is to never stop looking. It is the physical embodiment of the fear that the world is not as stable as it seems, that there are gaps in our perception where anything could be hiding. Think of it as a predator that has evolved to exploit the most fundamental flaw in human biology: the need to blink. It is a reminder that reality is a fragile thing, held together by the thin thread of our own attention.
