Who It Is

Don't blink. That is the only rule that keeps you alive. In the world of the SCP Foundation, a secret organization that locks away the unnatural, there is a creature that is less a monster and more a law of physics. It is a crude statue of concrete and rebar, a piece of brutalist art that would be at home in any modern gallery. But when you look away, it moves. It doesn't lunge, it doesn't roar, it simply is there, right in front of you, its featureless head tilted. And then, with a crack, you are gone. This is SCP-173, the 'Peanut,' the statue that taught the internet to be afraid of the dark. It is a monster born not of flesh and blood, but of a single, terrifying idea: that the world is full of things that only exist when you aren't looking.

SCP-173 is animate and extremely hostile. Its defining characteristic is that it is incapable of moving while within a direct line of sight. When unobserved, it moves with incredible speed, closing distances in the blink of an eye. Its primary method of attack is to snap the neck of its victim at the base of the skull. It is relentless and will pursue its target with a single-minded purpose. It does not communicate, show emotion, or exhibit any signs of intelligence beyond a predatory cunning. It is a perfect, patient predator that exploits the fundamental limitation of human perception.

Powers
Anomalous Speed and Mobility

When not directly observed, SCP-173 can move at an incredible, seemingly instantaneous speed. It can traverse a room, cross a hallway, or close a distance of several meters in the time it takes a human to blink. This speed is not a form of teleportation but rather an extreme, superhuman acceleration.

Limit: It cannot move when it is within the direct line of sight of any living being. This is an absolute, unbreakable rule of its existence.

Lethal Neck Snap

SCP-173's preferred and most iconic method of killing is to break a human's neck at the base of the skull. It is a fast, brutal, and efficient kill, leaving its victims with a horrifically snapped neck.

Limit: It appears to be a purely physical attack. It has not demonstrated any other methods of harming or killing, though its speed makes it nearly impossible to defend against.

Invulnerability to Conventional Harm

The original article notes that SCP-173 is 'extremely hostile' and that its construction is durable. It is implied that it is resistant to physical damage, though it is not explicitly indestructible. Containment procedures focus on observation, not on damaging it.

Limit: Its durability is not absolute. It is not described as being immune to all forms of harm, but its primary threat is its movement, not its toughness.

Form & Signs

Appearance — SCP-173 is a humanoid sculpture constructed from concrete and rebar, with traces of Krylon brand spray paint. It stands approximately 2 meters (6.5 feet) tall. Its form is crude and blocky, with a single, featureless head that has been described as resembling a peanut or a lumpy potato. It is a grotesque, unsettling figure, appearing as a piece of modern art that has gone horribly wrong. The paint is chipped and faded, giving it an ancient, weath

Symbols — The crude, expressionless face of SCP-173 has become a symbol of the SCP Foundation itself, often used as a logo or mascot. It represents the Foundation's core mission: to contain the unknown, no matter how terrifying. The image of a concrete statue with a peanut-shaped head is instantly recognizable within internet culture as a shorthand for 'SCP' and 'containment breach'.

Domains — SCP-173 is currently contained within a standard secure containment cell at Site-19 of the SCP Foundation. Its domain is wherever it is kept, and its 'hunting ground' is any space where it can be unobserved. In a broader sense, its domain is the liminal space between perception and reality, the fraction of a second when you blink.

Accounts I
The Original Containment BreachSCP Foundation Wiki, SCP-173 article

2007

The foundational story is not a narrative but a set of procedures. It describes how SCP-173 was found, its initial containment, and the absolute necessity of maintaining eye contact. The most famous detail is the incident where a subject was killed, and the only clue was a smear of red paint on the floor, indicating that SCP-173 had been active. The story is told through the cold, clinical language of a containment protocol, which makes the horror all the more effective. Established the template for all future SCP articles and created the iconic 'blink and it moves' horror mechanic.

Accounts II
SCP – Containment Breach (Video Game)Undertow Games

2012

The game puts the player in the role of a D-class test subject during a massive containment breach at Site-19. SCP-173 is one of the first and most persistent threats the player encounters. The game's core mechanic is managing your blinking; you must constantly look at SCP-173 to keep it frozen, but you also need to look away to navigate and avoid other threats. This creates a constant, nerve-wracking tension that made the game a viral sensation. The game's popularity exploded, bringing SCP-173 and the SCP Foundation to a massive mainstream audience and cementing its status as a horror icon.

The Tale of the Broken GodVarious SCP Foundation tales

Various

In some canons, SCP-173 is connected to a larger mythology involving a 'Broken God' or a war between ancient factions. These tales give SCP-173 a deeper, more mysterious origin, suggesting it may be a fragment of a destroyed deity or a weapon from a forgotten conflict. These stories are not universally accepted but add layers of meaning to the simple statue. These tales expand the SCP universe's lore, connecting SCP-173 to a grand cosmic narrative.
