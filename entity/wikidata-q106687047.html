Who It Is

He is the greatest detective in England, a man of iron logic and unshakable method — yet in Paris he meets his match in a gentleman burglar who toys with him as a cat plays with a mouse. Herlock Sholmes is not the Sherlock Holmes you know; he is the parody that became a legend in his own right, a character born of legal necessity and literary mischief. In the pages of Maurice Leblanc, he is the worthy adversary of Arsène Lupin, the only man who can match wits with the master thief — and the only one who consistently loses, yet never loses his dignity.

Sholmes is methodical, logical, and supremely confident in his deductive abilities. He is often portrayed as arrogant and somewhat disdainful of French methods, but he is also honorable and persistent. He shows a dry wit and a sense of superiority that is often punctured by Lupin's tricks.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is a deliberate near-anagram of 'Sherlock Holmes', altered to avoid copyright issues with Arthur Conan Doyle. 'Herlock' may be a playful corruption of 'Sherlock', and 'Sholmes' a variation of 'Holmes'. The French spelling 'Sholmès' adds an accent for pronunciation.

Pronunciation — HER-lock SHOHMZ (English); in French, roughly 'AIR-lock SHOL-mess'.

Aliases — Herlock Sholmès (French), Holmlock Shears (British translation), Sherlock Holmes (original French chapters and some adaptations)

Powers
Deductive Reasoning

Sholmes possesses extraordinary observational and deductive skills, allowing him to solve complex crimes from minute clues.

Limit: He is not infallible; Lupin often outwits him, and he sometimes jumps to conclusions that prove wrong.

Physical Combat

He is capable in physical altercations, though not superhuman. He can hold his own in a fight but is not a martial artist.

Limit: He is often overpowered or trapped by Lupin's schemes.

Disguise

Sholmes occasionally uses disguises to investigate, though not as extensively as Lupin.

Limit: His disguises are less elaborate and may be seen through.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Herlock Sholmes is described as a tall, thin, sharp-featured Englishman with a penetrating gaze. He typically wears a deerstalker cap and a cloak, similar to Sherlock Holmes's iconic attire. His appearance is that of a quintessential British detective, contrasting with the French elegance of Lupin.

Symbols — Associated with the magnifying glass, pipe, and deerstalker hat—symbols borrowed from Sherlock Holmes but now also linked to Herlock Sholmes in the Lupin series. His name itself is a symbol of the legal and creative constraints that shaped him.

Domains — Primarily operates in London and Paris. He is a private detective in England, but is frequently called to France to deal with Lupin's cases. He is at home in the foggy streets of London and the elegant salons of Paris.

Holmes (later Sholmes) is called to investigate a theft by Lupin, but arrives only to find Lupin has escaped. This story establishes the rivalry. Sets the stage for future confrontations.

The Blonde LadyArsène Lupin versus Herlock Sholmes

Early 20th century

Sholmes is brought to Paris to solve a series of crimes linked to a mysterious blonde woman. He matches wits with Lupin, is tricked, escapes, and eventually captures Lupin, only for Lupin to escape again. Sholmes earns a reputation as Lupin's nemesis, though Lupin ultimately outsmarts him.

The Jewish LampArsène Lupin versus Herlock Sholmes

Early 20th century

Sholmes is asked to recover a valuable Jewish lamp. Lupin warns him not to interfere, but Sholmes defies the warning and investigates, uncovering a deeper scheme. Another round in their ongoing duel; Sholmes is again bested.
