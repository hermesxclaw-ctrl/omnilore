The Approach

He is the greatest detective in England, a man of iron logic and unshakable method — yet in Paris he meets his match in a gentleman burglar who toys with him as a cat plays with a mouse. Herlock Sholmes is not the Sherlock Holmes you know; he is the parody that became a legend in his own right, a character born of legal necessity and literary mischief. In the pages of Maurice Leblanc, he is the worthy adversary of Arsène Lupin, the only man who can match wits with the master thief — and the only one who consistently loses, yet never loses his dignity.

Content advisory. None for the character itself; the stories contain mild violence, deception, and criminal activity typical of detective fiction of the era. No explicit dark material.
Identity

Herlock Sholmes

Pronunciation HER-lock SHOHMZ (English); in French, roughly 'AIR-lock SHOL-mess'.

Known As

Herlock Sholmès (French)Holmlock Shears (British translation)Sherlock Holmes (original French chapters and some adaptations)

Etymology

The name is a deliberate near-anagram of 'Sherlock Holmes', altered to avoid copyright issues with Arthur Conan Doyle. 'Herlock' may be a playful corruption of 'Sherlock', and 'Sholmes' a variation of 'Holmes'. The French spelling 'Sholmès' adds an accent for pronunciation.

Cultural Ownership

Created by French author Maurice Leblanc; the character is a parody/adaptation of Arthur Conan Doyle's Sherlock Holmes. While the original Holmes is British, Herlock Sholmes is a French literary creation and has been used in various French and international adaptations.

Disputed Identifications

Some readers and scholars initially mistook Herlock Sholmes for a simple pseudonym of Sherlock Holmes, but they are distinct characters in the Lupin universe. Later adaptations sometimes use the real name 'Sherlock Holmes' when legally possible, causing confusion between the two.

Description

Appearance

Herlock Sholmes is described as a tall, thin, sharp-featured Englishman with a penetrating gaze. He typically wears a deerstalker cap and a cloak, similar to Sherlock Holmes's iconic attire. His appearance is that of a quintessential British detective, contrasting with the French elegance of Lupin.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with the magnifying glass, pipe, and deerstalker hat—symbols borrowed from Sherlock Holmes but now also linked to Herlock Sholmes in the Lupin series. His name itself is a symbol of the legal and creative constraints that shaped him.

Behaviour & Personality

Sholmes is methodical, logical, and supremely confident in his deductive abilities. He is often portrayed as arrogant and somewhat disdainful of French methods, but he is also honorable and persistent. He shows a dry wit and a sense of superiority that is often punctured by Lupin's tricks.

Domains & Habitats

Primarily operates in London and Paris. He is a private detective in England, but is frequently called to France to deal with Lupin's cases. He is at home in the foggy streets of London and the elegant salons of Paris.

Version Differences

The original French version uses 'Herlock Sholmès' (with accent). The American translation by Morehead uses 'Herlock Sholmes' (no accent). The British translation by Teixeira de Mattos uses 'Holmlock Shears'. In the 1910 film serial, he is called 'Sherlock Holmes'. In the 2015 video game, he is 'Herlock Sholmes'.

Contradictions & Disputes

Name discrepancy

Position A

The character is named Herlock Sholmes in the official French text and most translations.

Position A — evidence

Original French publications and the 1910 American translation by Morehead.

Position B

Some editions and adaptations use 'Sherlock Holmes' or 'Holmlock Shears'.

Position B — evidence

The original serialized chapters used 'Sherlock Holmes'; the British translation used 'Holmlock Shears'; the 1910 film used 'Sherlock Holmes'.

Scholarly Consensus

The character is distinct from Doyle's Holmes, but the name variation is due to legal and editorial choices.

Research Scope

This dossier focuses on the fictional character Herlock Sholmes as created by Maurice Leblanc, primarily from the two collections 'Arsène Lupin, Gentleman Burglar' and 'Arsène Lupin versus Herlock Sholmes'. It covers his origins, appearances, relationships, adaptations, and the legal and literary context that shaped him.
