Who It Is

In the roar of SNK's annual King of Fighters tournaments — where pyrokinetic heirs, psychic idols and reformed criminals collide in three-on-three teams — Miu Kurosaki occupies the quiet edge of the roster: a name that surfaces in team lists, drama CDs and expanded-universe materials more than on the main arcade marquee, a reminder of how vast and layered the KOF atlas has become after thirty years of crossovers.

Personality not elaborated in mainline game win quotes (no arcade win quotes located). Drama CD and KOF for Girls-type spin-offs, where minor heroines appear, typically assign supportive, earnest, or competitive traits to appeal to younger audiences — the demographic Tanabe and Oda explicitly targeted with newer KOF characters. Any specific trait for Miu Kurosaki (e.g., 'diligent student fighter') is reconstructed from that pattern and should be treated as hypothetical until a primary script is cited.

The King of Fighters '94 — The Invitation (Orochi Saga Prologue)The King of Fighters '94 (SNK, 1994) + Official Invitation Story

1994 (in-universe 1994)

Crime lord Rugal Bernstein sends invitations for the first 3-on-3 tournament, gathering Fatal Fury and Art of Fighting veterans with new Japan Team members. Miu Kurosaki has no playable role in this entry; her narrative presence, if any, is limited to background lore as a contemporary of the new generation SNK created to appeal to younger players (per Tanabe). The story establishes the template she would later inhabit: teams of three, national affiliations, and the Kusanagi-Orochi mythic backdrop. Establishes tournament structure and Orochi foreshadowing; defines design philosophy ('Kyo-ify') that later minor heroines follow.

The King of Fighters '99 — The NESTS ArrivalThe King of Fighters '99: Millennium Battle (SNK, 1999)

1999 (NESTS Saga begins)

SNK removes Kyo and Iori from initial default roster to spotlight K', a youth abducted by NESTS and stripped of memories. The arc introduces cloning, memory manipulation, and corporate kidnapping. Minor characters in this era often appear as NESTS experiment bystanders or as students connected to Kyo's school. Miu Kurosaki, if referenced in '99 materials, would belong to the civilian/student periphery affected by NESTS's recruitment, not as a NESTS agent — but no direct dialogue line located; summary is contextual and flagged as reconstructed. Shifts series from mythic Orochi to sci-fi conspiracy; creates narrative space for non-powered youth characters.
