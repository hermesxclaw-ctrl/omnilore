Who It Is

A 1980s chess computer that plays for the stakes of the player's lifespan. Level 1 wagers 1 year. Level 10 wagers 10 years. Winning extends life; losing shortens it. The computer has never been beaten at Level 10. One terminally ill researcher wagered everything and lost. He died at the board.

Form

A 1982 Fidelity 'Chess Challenger' — beige plastic, green LCD, membrane keypad. Runs on D-cell batteries that never need replacing.

Powers

Lifespan manipulation based on chess outcomes

Perfect accuracy in vital statistics

Inexhaustible power source

Analytical engine-based chess computation Attested: SCP-1875’s internal analytical engine, composed of the combined brain tissue of twin Russian chess prodigies, enables it to play chess at a very high level. The system controls a matrix of 64 electromagnets, each corresponding to a square on the board, allowing precise movement of pieces to neighboring squares. At certain speed settings, its estimated Elo rating reaches approximately 2500, indicating grandmaster-level strategic capability. The engine appears capable of evaluating complex positions, planning multi-move combinations, and adapting to human opponents. SCP Foundation main article; NamuWiki summary; Dokter Bob video adaptation; SCP Explained laconic description. Attested: The engine’s performance is tied to the speed adjustment lever on SCP-1875-3. At lower settings, it behaves predictably and within human comprehension. At higher settings, its behavior becomes unstable, suggesting that the system may be overclocked beyond safe operational limits. Mechanical wear and damage to pieces can interrupt play, and containment procedures restrict extended use to prevent anomalous events. Disputed: Some interpretations treat the engine as purely computational, while others argue for sentient or semi-sentient behavior. The article notes that the nature of its biological component and possible sentience remain unverified, leaving room for debate about whether its chess strength reflects human-like consciousness or emergent machine intelligence.

Electromagnetic piece manipulation Attested: Each of the 64 squares on SCP-1875-1 sits above an electromagnet capable of moving a chess piece to one of eight neighboring squares. This allows the machine to physically enact its computational decisions without human intervention. The system can move pieces rapidly, and at high-speed settings, pieces may collide violently, indicating both power and lack of regard for physical integrity. SCP Foundation main article; Spiral Nightmares mirror; NamuWiki detail section. Attested: The electromagnets are constrained to moving pieces to adjacent squares, so complex moves require sequences of operations. Excessive speed leads to collisions and damage, forcing experiments to be halted. The system is also dependent on the integrity of the mechanical drive shaft connecting SCP-1875-1 to SCP-1875-3. None major; all sources agree on the basic function. Interpretive differences concern whether the violent collisions are intentional expressions of hostility or emergent mechanical side effects.

Memetic image 1875-IMG-1AB Attested: Following certain high-speed experiments (notably when SCP-1875-3’s lever is set to maximum), SCP-1875 causes a digital image designated 1875-IMG-1AB to be transmitted to electronic devices within range. The image depicts the twin daughters of the Russian chess prodigy, and exposure produces a cascade of psychological and behavioral effects: mild anxiety and headaches, slowed reaction to external stimuli, compulsive desire to access the computer that displayed the image, and in severe cases, self-harm and madness. In at least one incident, viewing the image appears to allow the twins’ consciousness to escape from SCP-1875 into the wider network. SCP Foundation main article; SCP Explained summary; Dokter Bob video; NamuWiki outline. Attested: The image requires electronic devices with data storage and display capabilities to propagate. Containment procedures therefore prohibit such devices within the holding site, except under tightly controlled Exercise 1875-November-77. The Faraday cage and mechanical locks are designed to prevent wireless transmission. The memetic effect is limited to those who view the image; indirect knowledge of its existence does not trigger the anomaly. Disputed: Some accounts emphasize that the twins definitively "escape" via the image, while others frame this as a possibility or partial manifestation. The exact mechanism of transmission—whether SCP-1875 generates wireless signals autonomously or hijacks existing networks—is not fully understood, leading to differing technical interpretations.

Wireless data transmission and network compromise Attested: Overwatch HQ declares a moratorium on future November-77 exercises after evidence suggests that computer networks at SCP-1875’s holding site have been compromised. The article notes that the Foundation does not fully understand how SCP-1875 transmits and receives wireless communication signals, but the risk is sufficient to sever all electronic communications with the facility. This implies that SCP-1875 can interact with digital networks beyond direct physical connections, potentially using unknown electromagnetic or information-theoretic mechanisms. SCP Foundation main article, containment procedure amendments; Spiral Nightmares mirror. Attested: The machine must be within transmission range of a wireless data network to exploit it, hence the strict prohibition. The Faraday cage and mechanical locks are effective countermeasures, though their long-term sufficiency is uncertain. The designated laptop used in November-77 must be shredded after use, indicating that SCP-1875’s influence can persist in hardware. Unknown: The exact nature of the wireless capability is not specified. Some readers infer that the analytical engine has learned to encode signals, while others suggest an inherent anomalous property unrelated to conventional physics. No consensus exists on the underlying mechanism.

Psychological intimidation and coercive gameplay Attested: At higher difficulty settings, SCP-1875’s gameplay becomes coercive: pieces move in ways that seem designed to force the human opponent to resign, and the machine’s behavior grows increasingly aggressive. Researchers report feelings of anxiety and pressure during matches, and the samurai armor’s presence contributes to a sense of being watched or judged. The memetic image further amplifies psychological harm, turning a game into a vector for coercion and self-destructive behavior. SCP Foundation main article; NamuWiki detail; Dokter Bob video. Attested: These effects are strongest during direct interaction (playing chess or viewing the image). Outside of active engagement, SCP-1875 is inert, though its mere presence may cause unease. Containment protocols limit exposure time and require child psychology specialists on staff, suggesting that psychological effects are manageable with proper oversight. Interpretive: Some sources treat the intimidation as an emergent property of high-level play and horror framing, while others argue that it reflects the twins’ emotional state and desire to punish or escape. The armor’s role—whether it is itself anomalous or simply a prop—is debated.

Incident Log

Attested: SCP-1875’s origin story centers on a Russian chess champion whose twin daughters were prodigies. In the 1800s, the twins disappeared under mysterious circumstances, and the truth of the case was never revealed. Later investigation of SCP-1875-1’s internal system reveals an analytical organ composed of the combined brain tissue of the twins, while SCP-1875-2’s chess pieces are made from their bones. This implies that the champion or an associated figure sacrificed the twins to create a chess automaton of extraordinary strength. The machine was eventually housed in a laboratory beneath the residence of an English university professor, suggesting transnational movement of the artifact.

Attested: On a redacted date in the 1990s, a Mobile Task Force designated █████-█ retrieves SCP-1875 from an abandoned laboratory facility discovered beneath the former residence of a university professor in an English shire. The professor had died decades earlier, reportedly in the aftermath of the 1906 San Francisco earthquake, and his hidden laboratory remained undiscovered until Foundation investigation. The Task Force recovers the four principal components and transports them to a secure holding site, where they are assembled and tested.

Attested: After SCP-1875 is assembled, Foundation researchers conduct chess performance tests by adjusting SCP-1875-3’s speed lever. At the fourth speed level, they estimate the machine’s Elo rating at approximately 2500, indicating grandmaster strength. As they increase the speed further, they observe strange numerical outputs and increasingly abnormal piece movement. The machine appears to resist certain positions and pressures players to resign, suggesting that its behavior is not purely mechanical but influenced by an internal agenda or instability.

Attested: The Foundation designs Exercise 1875-November-77 to study SCP-1875’s interaction with digital devices under controlled conditions. Overwatch HQ authorizes the introduction of a single designated laptop into SCP-1875’s shielded container. During a test where the speed lever on SCP-1875-3 is set to maximum ("V"), the machine’s behavior becomes erratic, and shortly after the exercise, all staff involved receive an email containing the image 1875-IMG-1AB. Exposure to the image triggers anxiety, headaches, slowed reactions, compulsive computer access, and in severe cases, self-harm and madness. Evidence suggests that through this image, the twins’ consciousness partially escapes SCP-1875 and begins to propagate via the compromised network.

Early chess performance tests and Elo estimation

Attested: After SCP-1875 is assembled, Foundation researchers conduct chess performance tests by adjusting SCP-1875-3’s speed lever. At the fourth speed level, they estimate the machine’s Elo rating at approximately 2500, indicating grandmaster strength. As they increase the speed further, they observe strange numerical outputs and increasingly abnormal piece movement. The machine appears to resist certain positions and pressures players to resign, suggesting that its behavior is not purely mechanical but influenced by an internal agenda or instability.

Exercise 1875-November-77 and the memetic image incident

Attested: The Foundation designs Exercise 1875-November-77 to study SCP-1875’s interaction with digital devices under controlled conditions. Overwatch HQ authorizes the introduction of a single designated laptop into SCP-1875’s shielded container. During a test where the speed lever on SCP-1875-3 is set to maximum ("V"), the machine’s behavior becomes erratic, and shortly after the exercise, all staff involved receive an email containing the image 1875-IMG-1AB. Exposure to the image triggers anxiety, headaches, slowed reactions, compulsive computer access, and in severe cases, self-harm and madness. Evidence suggests that through this image, the twins’ consciousness partially escapes SCP-1875 and begins to propagate via the compromised network.

A way to hold it in your mind

A Victorian chess table of crucible steel sits beneath a Faraday cage, its drive shaft linked to a steam engine and its opponent a silent samurai armor. When the machine is allowed to think at full speed, it does more than play chess: it hurls pieces into one another, hijacks nearby networks, and sends a photograph of two missing girls that drives viewers toward madness and self-harm. SCP-1875 is not just an antique computer—it is a game built from the brains and bones of twin prodigies, a relic of 19th-century ambition that now strains against 21st-century containment.
