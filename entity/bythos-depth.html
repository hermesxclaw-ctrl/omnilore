Who It Is

Bythos (Greek βυθός, 'Depth' or 'Abyss') is the supreme, ineffable first principle of the Valentinian Pleroma — the unfathomable source from which the entire divine realm of aeons proceeds. Bythos is utterly transcendent: no predicates can be truly asserted of him; he is 'unbegotten, invisible, eternal, and unbegun' (Iren. AH 1.11.1). His entire being is a potentiality that must be 'differentiated' into the aeons to achieve self-knowledge. Bythos is not a creator in the ordinary sense — he does not will the material world into being. Rather, he silently contains the fullness of divine attributes within himself, and these attributes are 'projected' or 'emanated' as distinct but consubstantial aeons, beginning with his first pairing: Nous (Mind) and Aletheia (Truth). The limit (Horos) that later separates the Pleroma from the kenoma (the 'void' outside) derives ultimately from Bythos's self-limitation.
