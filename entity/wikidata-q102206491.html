Who It Is

In the shadow of the Hawraman mountains, where the waters of Lake Dukan lap against ancient shores, lies a town that has become a living symbol of Kurdish resilience. Ranya is not merely a place on a map; it is a name etched into the memory of a people who, in the spring of 1991, rose against an empire and won, if only for a moment, their freedom.

The town is characterized by its strong Kurdish cultural identity and historical involvement in political movements. The inhabitants are described as being well-known for their participation in the 1991 uprising, indicating a spirit of resilience and resistance. The town also hosts cultural events celebrating Kurdish traditions, suggesting a vibrant community life.

Accounts I
The 1991 Kurdish Uprising in RanyaWikipedia article and general knowledge of Kurdish history

1991

On May 5, 1991, after a two-week confrontation, local civilians and Peshmerga forces successfully expelled the Iraqi army of Saddam Hussein from Ranya. This event was part of the larger 1991 Kurdish uprising (Raperîn) against Ba'athist Iraq. The anniversary is regularly celebrated with guests from all over Kurdistan. The expulsion of the Iraqi army from Ranya was a symbolic victory for Kurdish resistance. It contributed to the broader Kurdish autonomy movement, eventually leading to the establishment of the Kurdistan Region's autonomy.
