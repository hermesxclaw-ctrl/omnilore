Who It Is

A miniature, fully formed artificial human created through alchemical means. The concept was popularized by the Swiss physician-alchemist Paracelsus (1493-1541) in his work 'De Natura Rerum,' where he described a recipe: place human semen in a sealed vessel (alembic) with horse dung for warmth, and after 40 days it would form a transparent human-like shape. Fed with human blood and kept at the constant temperature of a horse's womb for 40 weeks, it would grow into a miniature human — a homunculus. Paracelsus claimed these beings possessed all the knowledge of the arts and could reveal hidden truths. The concept has roots in preformationism (the theory that organisms develop from miniature versions of themselves) and earlier Arabic and Greco-Egyptian alchemical traditions. In Goethe's 'Faust,' Wagner creates a homunculus that lives in a glass vial.
