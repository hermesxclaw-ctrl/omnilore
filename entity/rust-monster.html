Who It Is

The Rust Monster is one of D&D's most infamous monsters — not for its lethality (it's physically harmless to flesh), but for its ability to reduce a fully-armored knight to a naked, defenseless commoner in seconds. Standing about 3 feet tall at the shoulder with a humped, insectoid body, four spindly legs, and a long tail ending in a propeller-shaped protrusion, the Rust Monster's most distinctive features are its two long, feathery antennae that constantly twitch and probe the air, sensing the presence of ferrous metal from up to 90 feet away. When it detects metal — particularly the iron and steel of weapons, armor, and treasure — it scurries forward with alarming speed and begins feeding.

The Rust Monster's origin is one of D&D's most charming creation stories. Gary Gygax, D&D's co-creator, acquired a bag of cheap plastic monster toys from a Hong Kong dollar store to use as miniatures for the game. One of these figures — a bizarre, rust-colored creature with feathery antennae — had no obvious mythological counterpart, so Gygax invented one: the Rust Monster, a creature that corrodes and consumes metal. The original toy became part of D&D's first boxed set, and the monster has been a staple ever since.

In game terms, the Rust Monster's antennae instantly rust, corrode, and destroy any ferrous metal object they touch. A magic sword struck by the antenna takes a permanent penalty; non-magical plate armor can be reduced to brittle flakes in a single round. For adventurers who have invested their entire wealth in their equipment, a Rust Monster encounter is more terrifying than any dragon — the dragon might kill you, but the Rust Monster takes everything you own and leaves you alive to contemplate your poverty. The creature's non-aggressive nature toward unarmored characters makes it a uniquely tactical encounter: do you strip off your armor and fight naked, flee and lose face, or risk losing your prized +2 longsword?
