Who It Is

In the pantheon of Japanese horse racing, few names resonate as powerfully as Kitasan Black, a bay colossus whose thunderous strides and indomitable will carried him to an unprecedented seven Grade 1 victories and a record-breaking prize money haul. Bred from the same bloodline as the legendary Deep Impact, Kitasan Black was not merely a racehorse; he was a cultural phenomenon, beloved by a nation for his tenacity, his connection to the enka singer Saburo Kitajima, and his ability to transform the racetrack into a stage for sheer drama.

Kitasan Black was renowned for his tenacity and fighting spirit, often engaging in fierce duels down the stretch. He was described as a horse who 'doesn’t give in easily' by his trainer, and he displayed a remarkable ability to sustain his speed over long distances.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Kitasan' derives from the owner's prefix 'Kitasan' (北山), referencing the owner's company, Ōno Shōji, and the singer Saburo Kitajima's stage name. 'Black' refers to his dark bay coat.

Pronunciation — KEE-tah-sahn BLACK

Aliases — キタサンブラック, Kitasan Burakku

Powers
Stamina

Exceptional ability to maintain speed over long distances, particularly 2400m and beyond. He won multiple Grade 1 races at 2500m, 3000m, and 3200m.

Limit: Less effective at shorter distances, as seen in his poor showing in the 2015 Tokyo Yushun over 2400m, where he finished 14th.

Racing Tactics

Often employed a front-running or prominent style, using his stamina to control the pace and then holding off challengers in the straight.

Limit: This tactic sometimes left him vulnerable to faster finishers, as in the 2016 Osaka Hai and Takarazuka Kinen.

Consistency

Won 12 of 20 races, with 7 Grade 1 victories, and was placed in most other starts, demonstrating remarkable consistency at the highest level.

Limit: None significant

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kitasan Black was a large bay Thoroughbred with a narrow white blaze. He weighed up to 540 kg during his racing career, making him a substantial and imposing presence.

Symbols — His racing colours were owned by Ōno Shōji, associated with Saburo Kitajima. He was known for his distinctive late-running style, often coming from behind to win dramatic races.

Domains — He raced primarily on turf courses in Japan, including Tokyo, Nakayama, Kyoto, and Hanshin. He excelled at distances of 2400 meters and beyond, particularly in the spring and autumn Tenno Sho and the Arima Kinen.

Accounts I
2015 Classic CampaignJRA records, Racing Post

2015

After two wins in minor races, Kitasan Black won the Grade II Spring Stakes, finished third in the Satsuki Sho, and then disappointed in the Tokyo Yushun. He bounced back to win the St Lite Kinen and then claimed his first Grade 1 in the Kikuka Sho over 3000m, holding off Real Steel. He finished third in the Arima Kinen. Established himself as a top stayer and a contender for future Grade 1 races.

Accounts II
2016 Horse of the Year CampaignJRA records, Racing Post

2016

After a narrow loss in the Osaka Hai, Kitasan Black won the spring Tenno Sho in a thrilling duel with Curren Mirotic. He finished third in the Takarazuka Kinen, then won the Kyoto Daishoten and the Japan Cup in impressive fashion, beating a strong international field. He was subsequently voted Japanese Horse of the Year. Solidified his status as the leading stayer in Japan and earned international recognition.

2017 Record-Breaking SeasonJRA records, Racing Post

2017

Kitasan Black added four more Grade 1 wins: the spring Tenno Sho, the Takarazuka Kinen, the autumn Tenno Sho, and the Arima Kinen. These victories set a record for prize money earned in Japan and earned him a second Horse of the Year award. Became the highest-earning racehorse in Japanese history and a national hero.
