The Approach

In the pantheon of Japanese horse racing, few names resonate as powerfully as Kitasan Black, a bay colossus whose thunderous strides and indomitable will carried him to an unprecedented seven Grade 1 victories and a record-breaking prize money haul. Bred from the same bloodline as the legendary Deep Impact, Kitasan Black was not merely a racehorse; he was a cultural phenomenon, beloved by a nation for his tenacity, his connection to the enka singer Saburo Kitajima, and his ability to transform the racetrack into a stage for sheer drama.

Etymology

The name 'Kitasan' derives from the owner's prefix 'Kitasan' (北山), referencing the owner's company, Ōno Shōji, and the singer Saburo Kitajima's stage name. 'Black' refers to his dark bay coat.

Description

Appearance

Kitasan Black was a large bay Thoroughbred with a narrow white blaze. He weighed up to 540 kg during his racing career, making him a substantial and imposing presence.

Symbols & Iconography

His racing colours were owned by Ōno Shōji, associated with Saburo Kitajima. He was known for his distinctive late-running style, often coming from behind to win dramatic races.

Behaviour & Personality

Kitasan Black was renowned for his tenacity and fighting spirit, often engaging in fierce duels down the stretch. He was described as a horse who 'doesn’t give in easily' by his trainer, and he displayed a remarkable ability to sustain his speed over long distances.

Domains & Habitats

He raced primarily on turf courses in Japan, including Tokyo, Nakayama, Kyoto, and Hanshin. He excelled at distances of 2400 meters and beyond, particularly in the spring and autumn Tenno Sho and the Arima Kinen.

Stories & Myths

2015 Classic Campaign

Source JRA records, Racing PostCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2015

Summary

After two wins in minor races, Kitasan Black won the Grade II Spring Stakes, finished third in the Satsuki Sho, and then disappointed in the Tokyo Yushun. He bounced back to win the St Lite Kinen and then claimed his first Grade 1 in the Kikuka Sho over 3000m, holding off Real Steel. He finished third in the Arima Kinen.

2016 Horse of the Year Campaign

Source JRA records, Racing PostCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 2016

Summary

After a narrow loss in the Osaka Hai, Kitasan Black won the spring Tenno Sho in a thrilling duel with Curren Mirotic. He finished third in the Takarazuka Kinen, then won the Kyoto Daishoten and the Japan Cup in impressive fashion, beating a strong international field. He was subsequently voted Japanese Horse of the Year.

Kitasan Black added four more Grade 1 wins: the spring Tenno Sho, the Takarazuka Kinen, the autumn Tenno Sho, and the Arima Kinen. These victories set a record for prize money earned in Japan and earned him a second Horse of the Year award.
