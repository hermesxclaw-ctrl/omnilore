Mayahuel (the Maguey / Pulque-Goddess)

also known asthe Maguey / Pulque-Goddess
Typegoddess (maguey plant, intoxication, fertility)
Who It Is
Form

Mayahuel is often depicted as a woman surrounded by maguey plants, with a crown or headdress made of the plant's leaves. She may be shown with a child or a serpent, symbolizing fertility and rebirth.

Powers

Fertility and Abundance Mayahuel's powers are associated with the cycles of nature, particularly the growth and harvest of the maguey plant. She is said to have the ability to bring fertility and abundance to the land and its people.

Accounts

Mayahuel is said to have been created by the gods to provide sustenance and nourishment to humanity. She is often depicted as a woman surrounded by maguey plants, with a crown or headdress made of the plant's leaves.

Mayahuel is often depicted with a serpent, symbolizing her connection to the cycles of nature and the underworld. The serpent is said to represent the power of rebirth and regeneration.

Scholarly Debates
Mayahuel's association with the underworld
Mayahuel is often depicted as a goddess of the underworld, associated with the cycles of death and rebirth. ⇌ Mayahuel is also depicted as a goddess of the sun, associated with the cycles of growth and harvest.

Further research is needed to fully understand Mayahuel's role in Aztec mythology and her association with the maguey plant.
