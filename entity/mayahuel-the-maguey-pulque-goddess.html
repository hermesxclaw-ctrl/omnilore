Who It Is

Mayahuel is the goddess of the maguey (agave) plant — the 'tree of wonders' that provided the Aztecs with thread, needles, paper, roofing, food, and most importantly, the sacred fermented drink pulque (octli). In myth, the wind-god Ehecatl (an aspect of Quetzalcoatl) found the beautiful Mayahuel and carried her to earth; when her grandmother tried to destroy her, she was transformed into the maguey — a plant whose heart (the quiote) could be tapped for the life-giving sap (aguamiel) that ferments into pulque. Mayahuel's 400 children, the Centzon Totochtin ('400 Rabbits'), represent the endless variations of intoxication: some drunkenness is silly, some dangerous, some wise — each rabbit-god presides over a different aspect of inebriation. The pulque ritual was a controlled entry into the realm of the gods, and Mayahuel was its gatekeeper.
