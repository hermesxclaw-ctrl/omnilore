The Approach

In the year 2000, as the new millennium dawned, a Swedish band called In Flames released an album that would become a watershed moment in melodic death metal. 'Clayman' captured a band at a crossroads, blending the ferocity of their death metal roots with a newfound melodic sensibility that would propel them to global fame. Its iconic cover, a twisted homage to Leonardo da Vinci's Vitruvian Man, signaled a transformation—both for the band and for the genre itself.

Content advisory. The album's lyrics deal with themes of depression and internal struggles. Some listeners may find these topics emotionally challenging. The album cover features a distorted human figure, which may be unsettling to some.
Identity

Clayman

Pronunciation KLAY-man

Etymology

The album title 'Clayman' is likely a reference to the human figure constructed from clay, echoing the biblical creation of Adam and the idea of humanity as a malleable creation. It also reflects the album's themes of internal struggle and transformation.

Cultural Ownership

Swedish heavy metal music; specifically, the melodic death metal subgenre.

Disputed Identifications

There is no significant dispute over the album's identity, but some fans debate whether it is the last true melodic death metal album by In Flames or a transitional work.

Description

Appearance

The album cover art is based on Leonardo da Vinci's Vitruvian Man, but with a distorted, industrial, or metallic interpretation. The Jester Head, a recurring In Flames symbol, appears in the background. The booklet contains similar imagery.

Symbols & Iconography

The cover art is a direct reference to the Vitruvian Man, symbolizing the ideal human proportions, but the distortion suggests a flawed or altered humanity. The Jester Head is a band mascot representing chaos or mockery.

Behaviour & Personality

The album's music is characterized by aggressive guitar riffs, melodic solos, and a blend of harsh and clean vocals. The lyrics often deal with personal struggles, depression, and internal conflict.

Domains & Habitats

The album exists in the realm of heavy metal music, specifically the Gothenburg melodic death metal scene. It was recorded and produced in Sweden.

Version Differences

The original 2000 release contains 11 tracks. The 20th Anniversary Edition (2020) includes re-recorded versions of 'Bullet Ride', 'Pinball Map', 'Only for the Weak', and the title track, as well as remastered versions of the original songs.

Stories & Myths

Creation of Clayman

Source Band interviews and album creditsCulture Swedish metal scenePeriod Late 1990s to 2000

Summary

In Flames, consisting of Anders Fridén, Björn Gelotte, Jesper Strömblad, Peter Iwers, and Daniel Svensson, recorded Clayman in Sweden. The album was produced by Fredrik Nordström and the band. It was the last album to feature their original melodic death metal sound before they shifted towards a more commercial style.

Reception and Legacy

Source Music reviews and magazinesCulture Heavy metal communityPeriod 2000 to present

Summary

Clayman received positive reviews, with AllMusic praising its songwriting. It was ranked in Rock Hard's book of greatest metal albums and named one of the best metal albums of 2000 by Metal Hammer. The 20th Anniversary Edition re-recordings received mixed fan responses.
