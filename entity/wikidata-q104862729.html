Who It Is

In the year 2000, as the new millennium dawned, a Swedish band called In Flames released an album that would become a watershed moment in melodic death metal. 'Clayman' captured a band at a crossroads, blending the ferocity of their death metal roots with a newfound melodic sensibility that would propel them to global fame. Its iconic cover, a twisted homage to Leonardo da Vinci's Vitruvian Man, signaled a transformation—both for the band and for the genre itself.

The album's music is characterized by aggressive guitar riffs, melodic solos, and a blend of harsh and clean vocals. The lyrics often deal with personal struggles, depression, and internal conflict.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The album cover art is based on Leonardo da Vinci's Vitruvian Man, but with a distorted, industrial, or metallic interpretation. The Jester Head, a recurring In Flames symbol, appears in the background. The booklet contains similar imagery.

Symbols — The cover art is a direct reference to the Vitruvian Man, symbolizing the ideal human proportions, but the distortion suggests a flawed or altered humanity. The Jester Head is a band mascot representing chaos or mockery.

Domains — The album exists in the realm of heavy metal music, specifically the Gothenburg melodic death metal scene. It was recorded and produced in Sweden.

Accounts I
Creation of ClaymanBand interviews and album credits

Late 1990s to 2000

In Flames, consisting of Anders Fridén, Björn Gelotte, Jesper Strömblad, Peter Iwers, and Daniel Svensson, recorded Clayman in Sweden. The album was produced by Fredrik Nordström and the band. It was the last album to feature their original melodic death metal sound before they shifted towards a more commercial style. The album was a commercial success and helped In Flames gain international recognition. It also marked a turning point in their musical evolution.

Accounts II
Reception and LegacyMusic reviews and magazines

2000 to present

Clayman received positive reviews, with AllMusic praising its songwriting. It was ranked in Rock Hard's book of greatest metal albums and named one of the best metal albums of 2000 by Metal Hammer. The 20th Anniversary Edition re-recordings received mixed fan responses. The album's legacy has endured, and it is considered a classic of melodic death metal.
