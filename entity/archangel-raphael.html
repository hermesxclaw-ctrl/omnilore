Who It Is

The 'sociable spirit' sent by God to warn Adam about Satan and to answer Adam's questions about the cosmos. Raphael shares a meal with Adam and Eve (angels can eat, he explains, through a process of transubstantiation), then narrates the War in Heaven and the Creation.

Attested in Paradise Lost (1667)

Cultural Significance

Raphael's lengthy answer to Adam's question about astronomy ('Whether the sun predominant in heaven / Rise on the earth…') reflects Milton's engagement with the Copernican debate. His famous line 'Be lowly wise' counsels intellectual humility before divine mysteries.
