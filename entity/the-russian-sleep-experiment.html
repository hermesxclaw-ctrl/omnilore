Who It Is

The Russian Sleep Experiment is one of the most famous and widely believed creepypasta stories on the internet. The narrative, presented in the style of a leaked classified report, describes a Soviet-era experiment conducted in the late 1940s in which five political prisoners were sealed in a sealed gas chamber and exposed to an experimental stimulant gas designed to prevent sleep. The subjects were kept awake for 15 days under constant observation through microphones and porthole windows. For the first five days, everything was normal — the subjects talked, read, and showed no ill effects. By day nine, however, things had begun to deteriorate. The subjects screamed continuously, tearing at themselves and the walls. On day 15, when the gas was finally turned off and the chamber opened, researchers found a scene of absolute horror: the subjects had mutilated themselves, tearing out their own organs and consuming their own flesh. When the researchers attempted to sedate the survivors, the subjects violently resisted, screaming that they must remain awake — that sleep would bring something worse. When one subject was operated on to repair his injuries, he was found to have bones broken and regenerated multiple times, and his body continued functioning despite catastrophic damage. The story ends with the subjects demanding to be returned to the gas chamber, with the implication that whatever they had become needed the stimulant to survive. The text is masterfully constructed in a clinical, report-style tone that lent it a credibility many readers accepted as truth. Widespread circulation on early social media platforms led many to believe the experiment was historical fact. The story has been thoroughly debunked — no such experiment ever occurred — but the power of its imagery and its critique of dehumanizing state power has made it an enduring classic. It has since been adapted into short films, animations, and countless YouTube narrations.
