Who It Is

In August 2010 an anonymous user posted a story about five Soviet prisoners kept awake for fifteen days by an experimental gas. Three years later, Snopes felt obliged to publish a formal fact-check — because enough people believed it had actually happened. The photograph that convinced them was of a Halloween prop.

Considered as a single narrative agent, the entity behaves as an escalating engine of dehumanisation operating on a strict fifteen-day schedule, and the text's structure is essentially a calendar. Etymology — The title is descriptive rather than nominal, and its construction is the single most consequential rhetorical decision in the text. Each of the three lexical elements performs a distinct authenticating function. 'Russian' supplies a geopolitical frame that, for an Anglophone audience, carries pre-l

Pronunciation — /ˈrʌʃən sliːp ɪkˈspɛrɪmənt/, pronounced as ordinary English with no specialised or traditional articulation. As with internet-native folklore generally, the text propagates in written form and requires no oral competence to transmit; the widely circulated YouTube narrations that carry it to non-reading audiences are read in standard English by narrators who make no attempt at Russian pronunciation, and no Russian-language proper nouns appear in the story at all. The absence of any Russian names, places, or terminology within a story whose entire premise depends on Russianness is itself a diagnostic feature of the text's Anglophone authorship.

Powers
Sustained wakefulness under stimulant gas

The story's premise-level mechanism: an unnamed experimental gas-based stimulant, toxic at high concentration and therefore administered in a sealed chamber with monitored oxygen intake, that abolishes the need for sleep. It is never chemically identified, has no stated developer, and produces no side effects of the sort a real stimulant would; it functions purely as a narrative solvent that dissolves the boundary between waking and something else. Its efficacy is total and its withdrawal is intolerable.

Limit:

Refusal of death by ordinary means

The subjects sustain injuries that the text explicitly frames as incompatible with life and continue to act. One subject's heart is described as beating for a full two minutes after he has bled out to the point of having more air than blood in his vascular system, and he continues to scream, struggle, and repeat the word 'MORE' for three minutes after it stops. Another survives a six-hour abdominal reconstruction without anaesthetic. The surgeon is made to state repeatedly that it should not be medically possible for the patient to be alive — a line whose function is to pre-empt the reader's own objection.

Limit:

Immunity to sedation and anaesthesia

Chemical suppression fails comprehensively. One subject receives more than ten times a human dose of a morphine derivative and continues to fight, breaking a doctor's ribs and arm. Another proves effectively immune to pre-surgical sedative. A paralytic administered for surgery clears the subjects' systems in an abnormally short period. This ability is thematically precise rather than arbitrary: every drug that fails is one that induces unconsciousness, and the entity's single defining trait is that it cannot be made unconscious.

Limit:

Anomalous strength and oxygen metabolism

Oxygen consumption in the sealed chamber during the three silent days registers at the level of five people undertaking heavy strenuous exercise. A restrained subject tears most of the way through a four-inch leather strap while a two-hundred-pound soldier holds the same wrist. Post-mortem examination finds triple the normal level of oxygen in one subject's blood, and nine broken bones, most of them fractured by the force of the subject's own musculature acting against his skeleton.

Limit:

Self-evisceration as deliberate technique

The mutilation is presented not as frenzy but as method. Forensic detail is supplied to establish intent — the destruction of flesh and bone at the fingertips proves the wounds were made by hand rather than by teeth, and the angles indicate that most or all were self-inflicted. The organs are not torn out and discarded but removed intact and fanned out around the living body. The subjects consume their own excised flesh over a period of days while leaving untouched the food rations supplied to them. The purpose, as they later state, is to generate sufficient pain and stimulus to prevent sleep.

Limit:

Fatal sleep

The story's closing rule and its cleanest horror. Once the transformation is complete, sleep is death: a subject monitored on EEG shows normal brainwaves punctuated by inexplicable flatlines resembling repeated brain death, and at the instant his eyes close and his head touches the pillow his trace converts to deep sleep and then flatlines permanently as his heart stops. This retroactively reframes every prior act of self-harm as rational, and converts the subjects from monsters into something closer to the condemned.

The Chamber (the primary narrative, days 1-15)Creepypasta Wiki (attributed to 'OrangeSoda', 2010-08-10); Creepypasta.com

Set in the late 1940s; composed 2009-2010

At a covert Soviet facility, researchers seal five political prisoners, described as enemies of the state during the Second World War, into a chamber flooded with an experimental sleep-inhibiting gas, having falsely promised them freedom if they remain awake for thirty days. The first five days pass with little complaint, though the subjects' conversation turns steadily toward traumatic material from their pasts. After day five paranoia sets in, the subjects cease speaking to one another, and each begins whispering denunciations of the others into the microphones. On day nine the first subject begins screaming, running the length of the chamber and shouting for three hours until his vocal cords tear; the others ignore him entirely. When a second begins to scream, the two remaining subjects calmly dismantle the books provided to them, smear the pages with their own faeces, and paste them over the portholes. Accounts II
The SurgeriesCreepypasta.com, 'The Russian Sleep Experiment'

Narrative days 15 onward

Attempts to surgically restore the subjects' organs founder on their immunity to anaesthesia. The most severely injured subject fights the anaesthetic gas, tearing nearly through a four-inch leather restraint, and dies the instant he loses consciousness; his autopsy finds triple normal blood oxygen and nine bones broken by his own muscular force. The mute subject, unable to object, nods assent to surgery without anaesthetic and lies unreacting through six hours of abdominal reconstruction, with an assisting nurse reporting that his mouth curled into a smile whenever their eyes met. Afterwards he asks for pen and paper and writes two words: 'Keep cutting.' The remaining two undergo the same procedure without anaesthetic, requiring a paralytic only because the surgeon cannot operate through their continuous laughter. Asked why they mutilated themselves and why they want the gas, they give one answer: 'I must remain awake.' The subjects are returned to reinforced restraints pending a decision on their disposal.

The Final ExchangeCreepypasta.com, 'The Russian Sleep Experiment'

Narrative conclusion

The researchers, having failed the project's stated goals, consider euthanasia, but the commanding officer — a former KGB agent — overrules them and orders the subjects returned to the gas. All three cease struggling at the news and visibly labour to stay awake: one hums continuously, the mute subject strains his legs alternately against his bonds, and the third holds his head off the pillow and blinks rapidly while his EEG shows normal activity punctuated by inexplicable flatlines. A nurse alone sees his eyes close; his trace converts to deep sleep and then flatlines permanently as his heart stops. The commander orders the chamber sealed with the remaining subjects and three researchers inside. One of the named researchers draws his weapon, shoots the commander between the eyes, kills the mute subject, and turns the gun on the last survivor, demanding to know what he is. The subject smiles and answers: 'We are you. We are the madness that lurks within you all, begging to be free at every moment in your deepest animal mind. Date and authorship of first publicationThe story was first posted to the Creepypasta Wiki on 10 August 2010 by a user with the handle 'OrangeSoda'. This is the attribution given by Snopes, news.com.au, LiveAbout, and English Wikipedia, and it is supported by a dated wiki page created on 16 August 2010.The story predates that posting by a year, appearing on the Rip747 WordPress blog on 8 August 2009 and on the Bodybuilding.com Misc forum on 20 August 2009 under the handle 'Falconpunch'. If correct, OrangeSoda propagated rather than authored the text. The Rip747 post further claimed the story had been passed on by the poster's brother, which is either evidence of still earlier circulation or a conventional distancing device of the kind the genre routinely employs.
Historical period of the fictional eventsThe text states the experiment took place 'in the late 1940s', which places it after the end of the Second World War, in the early Cold War period of Soviet consolidation.The subjects are described as political prisoners deemed enemies of the state 'during World War II', and Tosha R. Taylor's scholarly treatment describes the story as revealing 'a top-secret effort by Russian scientists in World War II'. Wikipedia categorises it under Works set in the 1940s while also tagging it as an Internet meme introduced in 2009 — itself in tension with the article's own 2010 attribution. The internal inconsistency is unresolved in the source text and has propagated into the secondary literature.
Whether the closing supernatural revelation belongs to the workThe final speech — 'We are you. We are the madness that lurks within you all' — is present in the standard circulating text on both the Creepypasta Wiki and Creepypasta.com, and constitutes the story's thematic resolution, converting a pharmacological horror into a statement about human nature.A substantial body of retellings, including several high-view narrations, omit or truncate the passage, and reader commentary frequently identifies it as the story's weakest element on the grounds that it abandons the clinical register that made the preceding text persuasive. On this reading the ending is a late or inferior accretion and the story is stronger without explanation.
Genre classification: urban legend or authored fictionThe story is an urban legend. Wikipedia's opening sentence states it 'has become the basis of an urban legend'; Snopes, LiveAbout, and Dread Central all use the term, and it is justified by the story's circulation as claimed fact, its anonymous transmission, and the persistent public belief in its truth.The story is authored short fiction misread as legend. Genuine urban legends mutate substantially in retelling, whereas this text is reproduced near-verbatim across a decade and thousands of reposts — a stability characteristic of a fixed authored work. Wikipedia's own category assignments reflect the tension, filing the story simultaneously under 'Horror short stories', '2010 short stories', 'Russian urban legends', and 'Fakelore'.
The evidentiary status of the associated photographThe image is definitively a photograph of 'Spazm', an animatronic Halloween prop manufactured by Morbid Enterprises in 2005, as identified by The13thFloor in 2016 and noted directly in Wikipedia's image caption. It postdates neither the story nor depicts anything the story describes.A persistent lay reading treats the photograph as documentary evidence of the experiment, with its monochrome presentation and apparent period quality taken as corroboration. This belief survives the debunking and, in practice, drives more of the story's circulation than the text does — an inversion in which the fabricated illustration authenticates the fiction it was never part of.
