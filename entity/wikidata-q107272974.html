Who It Is

In the sprawling family trees of Greek myth, one name appears again and again—Clymenus—worn by kings, heroes, and even the god of the dead himself. But behind this single name lies a web of distinct lives, each with its own story, from a father's incestuous tragedy to a king's dying curse that sparked a war.

The various Clymeni exhibit different traits: the son of Phoroneus is a religious founder; the king of Orchomenus is vengeful; the Arcadian king is incestuous and cruel; the suitor is a typical suitor. No unified personality can be reconstructed.

Kingship

Several Clymeni are kings: of Orchomenus, of Arcadia, of Olympia, and possibly of Boeotia. This implies political power and authority.

Limit: Mortal limitations; subject to death and defeat.

Divine lineage (son of Helios)

As a son of Helios, Clymenus would have divine ancestry, but no special powers are attributed to him.

Limit: No powers are described.

Title of Hades

Clymenus is used as a title or epithet for Hades, the god of the underworld, possibly meaning 'the renowned one'.

Limit: Not a separate power, but an attribute of a deity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description is given for any of the figures named Clymenus in ancient sources. As a mortal king, he would be depicted in regal attire; as a son of Helios, he might have a radiant appearance, but this is not attested.

Symbols — No distinctive symbols or iconographic attributes are associated with the name Clymenus. In art, he might be shown in scenes of his myths (e.g., the incest with Harpalyce), but no specific vase paintings or sculptures are identified.

Domains — Clymenus is associated with several Greek regions: Boeotia (Orchomenus, Onchestus), Arcadia, Elis (Olympia, Phrixa), Calydon, Dulichium, and Pylos (as father of Eurydice). As a title of Hades, he is a chthonic deity.

Accounts I
Clymenus, son of Phoroneus, founds sanctuary of DemeterPausanias, Description of Greece 2.35.4

Mythical

Clymenus, son of Phoroneus, and his sister Chthonia founded a sanctuary of Demeter at Hermione. This act establishes a cult site. The sanctuary becomes a local religious center.

Clymenus as father of PhaethonHyginus, Fabulae 154

Mythical

In a variant genealogy, Clymenus, son of Helios, is the father of Phaethon and the Heliades by Merope. This is an alternative to the usual account where Helios and Clymene are the parents. Phaethon's disastrous ride in the sun chariot occurs in other sources, but here the parentage is altered.

Clymenus kills Hodites in the fight between Phineus and PerseusApollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.5

Mythical

During the battle at the wedding of Perseus and Andromeda, Clymenus kills Hodites. This is a minor episode in the larger myth. Clymenus participates in the battle, but his fate is not recorded.

Clymenus, king of Olympia, founder of temple of Athena CydoniaPausanias, Description of Greece 5.8.1

Mythical

Clymenus, son of Cardys, a descendant of Heracles of Ida, became king of Olympia but was deposed by Endymion. He founded the temple of Athena Cydonia at Phrixa in Elis. He establishes a cult to Athena; his deposition by Endymion is part of the mythological history of Olympia.

Clymenus, Calydonian prince, dies in warApollodorus, Bibliotheca 1.8.2

Mythical

Clymenus, son of Oeneus and Althaea, along with his brothers, including Meleager, falls in the war between the Curetes and the Calydonians. His death is part of the tragedy of the Calydonian royal family.

Clymenus, king of Orchomenus, killed by a stonePausanias, Description of Greece 9.37.1

Mythical

Clymenus, king of Orchomenus, quarreled with Thebans at Onchestus and was mortally wounded by a stone thrown by Perieres. He instructed his son Erginus to avenge him, leading to a war against Thebes. Erginus leads the Minyans against Thebes, resulting in the imposition of tribute on Thebes, later ended by Heracles.

Accounts II
Clymenus, king of Arcadia, incest with HarpalyceParthenius, Love Romances 13; Antoninus Liberalis, Metamorphoses 13

Mythical

Clymenus, king of Arcadia, committed incest with his daughter Harpalyce. When she bore a son, he later killed the child and served it to her as food. Upon discovering this, Harpalyce killed the child and was transformed into a bird. Clymenus committed suicide. The myth explains the origin of the bird called 'chalkis' or 'harpalyce' and ends in tragedy.

Clymenus, ArgonautGaius Valerius Flaccus, Argonautica 1.367

Mythical

Clymenus, brother of Iphiclus, is listed as one of the Argonauts. His parentage is given as Phylacus and Clymene. He participates in the quest for the Golden Fleece.

Clymenus, son of AeolusVarious (scholia)

Mythical

Clymenus is one of the sons of Aeolus of Lipara. Aeolus married his sons and daughters to each other to preserve concord. Part of the Aeolus family saga.

Clymenus, suitor of PenelopeApollodorus, Bibliotheca, Epitome 7.26-27

Mythical

Clymenus from Dulichium is one of the suitors of Penelope. He is killed by Odysseus. His death is part of the slaughter of the suitors.

Clymenus, father of EurydiceHomer, Odyssey 3.452

Mythical

Clymenus is the father of Eurydice, wife of Nestor. This is a brief mention. Establishes a family connection to Nestor's household.

Clymenus, father of NyctimeneHyginus, Fabulae 204

Mythical

In one version, Clymenus is the father of Nyctimene, who was raped by him and transformed into an owl. Nyctimene becomes an owl, a myth explaining the owl's nocturnal habits.

Clymenus as a title of HadesVarious (e.g., Orphic Hymns)

Classical to Hellenistic

Clymenus is used as an epithet for Hades, meaning 'the famous one' or 'the renowned'. It is a cultic title.
