Who It Is

In the vast tapestry of Hindu mythology, few figures embody the paradox of immense strength and unwavering duty as vividly as Balarama, the elder brother of Krishna. Wielding a mighty plow as his weapon, he is the god of agriculture, intoxication, and the unyielding force that upholds dharma itself.

Balarama is known for his immense physical strength, straightforwardness, and a tendency to be impulsive. He is loyal to his family, especially Krishna, and is a staunch upholder of dharma. He enjoys agriculture, music, and drinking.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Balarama means 'Rama of strength' (bala = strength, Rama = a name meaning 'pleasing' or 'dark'). It may also be derived from 'Bala' (strength) and 'Rama' (the deity).

Pronunciation — Buh-luh-RAH-muh

Aliases — Baladeva, Balabhadra, Halayudha, Sankarshana, Rama, Rauhineya

Ownership — Hinduism, particularly Vaishnavism; also significant in Jainism and Buddhism as a figure in their narratives.

Balarama possesses enormous physical strength, capable of uprooting trees, crushing demons, and defeating entire armies.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Balarama is typically depicted as fair-skinned (or white), in contrast to Krishna's dark complexion. He is tall and muscular, often wearing blue or yellow garments. His attributes are a plow (hala) and a pestle (musala), and he is often shown with a snake-hood canopy over his head, symbolizing his association with Shesha.

Symbols — Plow (hala), pestle (musala), snake hood, earrings, and a garland of wild flowers. He is often depicted with a drinking cup or in a state of intoxication.

Domains — Agriculture, strength, intoxication, and the protection of devotees. He is associated with the region of Braj (Mathura/Vrindavan) and the sacred river Yamuna.

Accounts I
Birth and ChildhoodVishnu Purana, Bhagavata Purana

Mythic

Balarama was conceived as the seventh child of Devaki, but was transferred to the womb of Rohini to escape Kamsa's infanticide. He was born as Sankarshana, the one who was drawn away. He grew up in Gokula with Krishna, performing many childhood feats, including killing the demon Dhenuka and the bull demon Arishtasura. Established his role as Krishna's protector and ally.

The Killing of PralambasuraBhagavata Purana

Mythic

A demon named Pralambasura disguised himself as a cowherd boy and carried Balarama away, but Balarama killed him by striking him with his fist. Demonstrated Balarama's strength and courage.

Accounts II
The Duel with DuryodhanaMahabharata

Epic

Balarama taught both Bhima and Duryodhana the art of mace-fighting. When the Kurukshetra war was about to begin, he went on a pilgrimage to avoid taking sides. Later, he witnessed the final mace duel between Bhima and Duryodhana and was furious when Bhima struck Duryodhana below the belt, a foul blow. He threatened to kill Bhima, but Krishna intervened. Balarama remained neutral in the war but showed his adherence to fair combat.

The Curse on the YadavasMahabharata, Mausala Parva

Epic

After the destruction of the Yadava dynasty, Balarama, in a state of grief and meditation, gave up his body. Some accounts say he cursed the Yadavas for mocking him, leading to their destruction. His death marks the end of the Yadava era and the beginning of the Kali Yuga.

The Taming of the YamunaBhagavata Purana

Mythic

Balarama, in a fit of intoxication, called the Yamuna river to come to him. When she refused, he dragged her with his plow, forcing her to follow his will. Demonstrated his power over nature and his temper.
