Who It Is

Marmoo is the spirit of evil in Wiradjuri and Kamilaroi mythology — the primary antagonist to Baiame, the All-Father creator. While Baiame creates the world, establishes the Law, and gives life, Marmoo works to undo creation, introducing poison into the land, disease into bodies, and discord into communities. Marmoo is described as a dark, underground being — associated with snakes, particularly venomous serpents, and with the barren, infertile places of the landscape. In one of the most significant Wiradjuri myths, Marmoo, envious of the beautiful world Baiame had created, rallied an army of venomous insects, snakes, and evil spirits to destroy it. Baiame's wife, Birranooloo (also called Gunnanbeely or Emu Woman), witnessed the attack and warned Baiame, who came down from the sky and defeated Marmoo's forces. In another myth, Marmoo created the first dingo to harass and kill the animals Baiame had made for human sustenance — making the dingo an ambiguous creature, simultaneously a companion to humans and a threat to their food supply. Marmoo embodies the Aboriginal understanding that creation is not a one-time event but an ongoing struggle between constructive and destructive forces. Evil, in this worldview, is not a fallen state or a punishment but an active, hostile principle that must be continuously resisted. The Wiradjuri were, before colonization, one of the largest Aboriginal nations in Australia, and their theological sophistication — including a supreme creator, a primordial antagonist, a ritual system, and a moral code — deeply impressed early European observers and challenged racist assumptions about the intellectual capacity of Aboriginal peoples.
