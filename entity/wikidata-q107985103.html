Comparative: The Bloodstone (Heliotrope) Lore

Source Pliny the Elder, Naturalis Historia Book 37.165; Kunz 1913 pp. 104-110 summarizing classical and medieval lapidariesCulture Greco-Roman / Medieval European lapidary traditionPeriod 1st century CE (Pliny) through medieval lapidaries (13th-17th century)

Summary

Heliotrope, called bloodstone (green jasper with red spots), was believed to staunch bleeding, render the wearer invisible, and confer courage when smeared with blood. Medieval lapidaries claimed the red spots were Christ's blood. The stone acts upon blood, rather than blood enchanting the stone. No sorcery system name is given.
