The Approach

In the green hills of Manipur, a tribal chief's daughter loved a king across worlds—and when death tore them apart, she became a goddess of heartbreak, disease, and the restless dead. Haoreima, the 'tribal lady,' is no distant myth; she is a woman's grief turned divine, a spirit of separation whose story still echoes in Meitei rituals.

Content advisory. Contains themes of tragic love, death, and separation. No explicit violent or graphic content is described in available sources.

Etymology

Haoreima (Meitei: ꯍꯥꯎꯔꯩꯃ) literally means 'tribal lady' (from 'hao' meaning tribal, 'leima' meaning lady). The variant 'Haoleima' is the Old Manipuri form.

Cultural Ownership

Meitei people of Manipur, India; also venerated in Sanamahism, the indigenous religion of Manipur. Originated from the Tangkhul people, a Naga ethnic group.

Disputed Identifications

Some traditions identify Haoreima as an incarnation of the goddess Panthoibi, while others associate her with Nongthang Leima. There is debate over whether she is a historical figure or purely mythological.

Stories & Myths

The Tragic Love of Haoreima

Source Meitei mythology (as summarized in the provided source)Culture Meitei / TangkhulPeriod Historical period around 1359-1394 CE (reign of King Meidingu Tabungba)

Summary

Haoreima was the daughter of Khelemba, a Tangkhul chief of Chingdai village. She was married to Khamlangba, a Tangkhul chief of Chingshong village. Despite her marriage, she had a secret love affair with King Meidingu Tabungba (also known as Tabung Saphaba). The king died an untimely death, and Haoreima, traumatized, went to the Kanglei Pungmayol (modern Imphal). After her own death, she was deified.
