Who It Is

The Enfield Horror is one of the most bizarre cryptid encounters on record -- a brief but widely reported flap centered on the small town of Enfield, Illinois in April 1973. The sightings began when a man named Henry McDaniel returned home to find something scratching at his door. He grabbed a flashlight and a pistol and went outside, where he encountered a creature unlike anything he had ever seen: 4 to 5 feet tall, grayish in color, with three legs, no visible arms, two pink eyes on short stalks, and the ability to leap 50 feet or more in a single bound. McDaniel fired four shots at the creature, striking it but apparently not killing it -- it let out a hissing, screeching cry and fled into the woods. The following night, McDaniel's neighbors also heard strange sounds and reported seeing a similar creature. A local radio station crew investigated and claimed to have recorded the creature's screeches. Police and wildlife officials searched the area but found no trace, though they did discover strange three-toed tracks. Cryptozoologist Loren Coleman investigated the case and documented the accounts. The creature's description -- three-legged, stalk-eyed, leaping -- is so bizarre that it defies easy categorization. Some theories suggest a misidentified escaped kangaroo or wallaby (though this doesn't explain the stalk-like eyes), a deformed animal, or a hoax. The Enfield Horror was never reported again after the April 1973 flap, making it another cryptid one-off. Its oddity has made it a staple of cryptid lists and paranormal literature.
