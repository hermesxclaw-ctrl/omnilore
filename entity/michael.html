Who It Is

Michael is the supreme archangel in the Abrahamic traditions, recognized as the commander of the heavenly armies and the principal defender against the forces of evil. His name in Hebrew, Mi-ka-El, poses the rhetorical question 'Who is like God?' — a declaration of divine incomparability and a rejection of pride (most significantly Lucifer's). In the Hebrew Bible, Michael appears in the Book of Daniel as 'one of the chief princes' and the 'great prince who protects your people,' serving as the special guardian of Israel. In Christian tradition, his role expands dramatically: the Book of Revelation describes the war in heaven where Michael and his angels defeat the dragon (Satan), casting him down to earth. The Epistle of Jude references Michael contending with the devil over Moses' body, demonstrating his role even in disputes over the dead. Catholic and Orthodox traditions venerate Michael as the patron of soldiers, police officers, and the dying — the angel who conducts souls to judgment and weighs them on the scales. In Islam, Mikail (Michael) is one of the four archangels alongside Jibril (Gabriel), Israfil (Raphael), and Azrail, tasked with providing nourishment to bodies and souls and controlling the forces of nature. Medieval angelology, particularly in Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite's Celestial Hierarchy, placed Michael at the highest levels of the angelic orders. Artistic depictions consistently show him in armor, wielding a sword or spear, often with scales of justice, standing triumphant over a dragon or serpent beneath his feet. Mount Saint-Michel in Normandy and Mont Saint-Michel in Cornwall both claim Michael's apparition, while numerous churches, shrines, and feast days (Michaelmas, September 29) attest to his enduring significance in Western religious culture.
