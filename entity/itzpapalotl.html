Who They Are

Itzpapalotl's name translates to Obsidian Butterfly, and the image should be held exactly as written: a butterfly whose wings are edged with blades of volcanic glass. She is the queen of the tzitzimimeh — the star demons, the skeletal female powers who are the stars themselves, waiting at the edges of the sky to descend and devour humanity if the sun ever fails — and she rules Tamoanchan, the lost paradise where the gods created the current race of humans from ground bones and divine blood. She is a goddess of magnificent contradiction: a skeletal death-figure with jaguar claws and knife-tipped wings who is simultaneously a patroness of birth, of the souls of women who died in childbirth (the mocihuaquetzque, Mesoamerica's warrior dead of the delivery room), and of the fire-drill that begins all warmth. In the Legend of the Suns, two cloud serpents witness her fall from heaven; one, Mixcoatl, eventually shoots her, and her burned body yields flint and obsidian blades — the goddess literally becoming the sacrificial knives of the religion that told her story. Terror, motherhood, and the cutting edge, all one insect.

Origins & Fall

Central Mexican tradition with Chichimec roots — she was the divine patroness of the northern hunting peoples the Aztecs claimed as ancestors, carried south and enthroned over paradise.

Attested in Aztec codices, Legend of the SunsEra Postclassic Mesoamerica

Form

A skeletal woman with butterfly wings edged in obsidian knives, jaguar claws on hands and feet, sometimes lovely and sometimes fleshless — the codices alternate without apology.

Powers

Queenship over the star demons; dominion over Tamoanchan; patronage of women dead in childbirth; the yielding of flint and obsidian — every sacrificial blade a fragment of her body.

Weaknesses & Wards

The arrow of Mixcoatl brought her down; fire consumed her — but her burning was a distribution, not a defeat: the blades struck from her corpse armed the altars of an empire.

Field Notes

The Approach

In the shimmering expanse of the Mesoamerican cosmos, where the vibrant colors of creation clash with the shadows of destruction, Itzpapalotl reigns supreme as the Obsidian Butterfly. With wings edged in volcanic glass, she embodies the very duality of existence—beauty intertwined with peril. As the queen of the tzitzimimeh, her presence looms over humanity like a celestial specter, reminding us of the ever-present balance between life and death.

Content advisory. This entry contains themes of violence, death, and ritualistic practices that may be unsettling to some readers.
Identity

Itzpapalotl

Etymology

The name Itzpapalotl derives from the Nahuatl words 'itz' (obsidian) and 'papalotl' (butterfly), reflecting her dual nature as both beautiful and deadly.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily associated with the Aztec (Mexica) pantheon, she occupies a significant role in Mesoamerican mythology, particularly in relation to the tzitzimimeh.

Disputed Identifications

Some sources conflate Itzpapalotl with other deities such as Xochiquetzal due to overlapping attributes, though this is debated among scholars.

Description

Appearance

Itzpapalotl is often depicted as a skeletal figure adorned with obsidian wings, which are edged with sharp, glass-like blades. She embodies a juxtaposition of beauty and terror, often shown with a fierce expression and surrounded by stars or celestial motifs.

Symbols & Iconography

Common symbols associated with Itzpapalotl include obsidian, butterflies, and skeletal imagery. Her iconography emphasizes her role as a deity of the stars and death, often represented in codices with vibrant colors and intricate patterns that signify her celestial domain.

Behaviour & Personality

As a chaotic neutral entity, Itzpapalotl exhibits unpredictable behavior, embodying both nurturing and destructive forces. While she is a creator figure in the genesis of humanity, she also represents a threat, ready to consume souls if the sun falters.

Domains & Habitats

Her realm is Tamoanchan, described as a paradise where the gods created humanity. This is a cosmic domain, often associated with the stars and celestial phenomena. Itzpapalotl's influence extends to the underworld and the heavens, linking life and death in her dominion.

Version Differences

In various adaptations, Itzpapalotl's attributes may shift; in some texts, she appears more benevolent, reflecting fertility and creation, while in others, her darker aspects are emphasized, portraying her as a malevolent force threatening humanity.

Powers & Abilities

Star Manipulation

Description

Itzpapalotl has the ability to manipulate stars, depicted as the embodiment of the tzitzimimeh, who are thought to descend from the sky to devour humanity if the sun fails.

Limitations

Her powers are contingent upon the sun's existence; without the sun, her strength wanes, and she becomes more malevolent.

Contradictions

Some sources argue that her powers are not solely punitive but also protective, depending on the context of worship.

Death and Rebirth
