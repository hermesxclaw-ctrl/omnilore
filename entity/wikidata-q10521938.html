Who It Is

In the shadowed borderlands of Chinese folk religion, two brothers stand eternal guard over the gates of the afterlife—Heng and Ha, their names whispered as a charm against evil. But 'Heng' is also a letter, a stroke, a mountain, a star, and a surname scattered across Southeast Asia. This dossier untangles the threads of a name that spans alphabets and afterlives.

As deities, Heng and Ha are guardians, fierce and vigilant. They are said to emit a snort that can kill or ward off demons. As a letter, it has no personality. As a surname, it is carried by people across Southeast Asia.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Heng' has multiple independent etymologies depending on context. As a Chinese surname, it is written with various characters (e.g., 衡, 亨) and its meaning varies accordingly. As part of the deity pair, 'Heng' (哼) is an ideophone representing a snort or hum, while 'Ha' (哈) represents a laugh

Pronunciation — In Mandarin: 'Hēng' (first tone) for the deity and stroke; for the surname, pronunciation depends on the specific character (e.g., 'Héng' second tone for 衡). In Norwegian: /hɛŋ/. In English, the letter is typically pronounced /hɛŋ/.

Symbols — The deity pair Heng and Ha symbolize protection and warding off evil; they are often painted on temple doors. Their open mouths (Ha) and closed mouths (Heng) represent the cosmic sounds 'Om' and 'Ah' in some interpretations. The Latin letter Heng is used in phonetic transcription. The stroke 'heng' is one of the basic strokes in Chinese calligraphy.

The Legend of Heng and HaVarious Chinese folk traditions; not a single canonical text.

Unknown; likely ancient, but popularized in Ming-Qing era novels and opera.

Heng and Ha are two brothers (or generals) who serve as door gods. In one version, they were generals under King Zhou of the Shang dynasty, but after death they were deified. In another, they are the reincarnations of two disciples of the Buddha. They are often depicted on temple doors, with Heng's mouth closed (emitting a snort) and Ha's mouth open (emitting a breath). Their combined power protects against evil. They became standard door gods in Chinese temples and homes, particularly in the south.

Accounts II
The Naming of Mount HengGeographic and historical records

Ancient

Mount Heng (Hunan) is one of the Five Great Mountains of China, associated with the south. It has been a site of Taoist and Buddhist worship for centuries. Mount Heng (Shanxi) is the northern sacred mountain, known for its Hanging Temple. Both mountains are major pilgrimage sites.

The Star HengChinese astronomical records

Ancient

The star Nu Centauri was part of a Chinese asterism called 'Heng', possibly meaning 'balance' or 'constant'. The exact naming is obscure. The name is now used in modern astronomy as a proper name for the star.
