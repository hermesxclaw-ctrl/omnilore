Who It Is

In the quiet corners of Japanese folklore, where the mundane meets the mysterious, there lives a creature born from the most humble of objects: the sake jar. Known as Kameosa, this yōkai is said to be a rice wine jar that has gained a life of its own, embodying the spirit of the drink it once held. But is it a mischievous trickster, a guardian of the brew, or a cautionary tale about excess? The stories are as varied as the jars themselves, and the truth is as elusive as the scent of aged sake.

Kameosa is generally portrayed as a jovial, mischievous entity. It may appear at night, wandering or rolling around, and is known to play pranks on humans, such as stealing sake or leading drunks astray. However, it is not malevolent; it is more of a trickster that revels in the pleasure of drinking. Some stories suggest that Kameosa protects the household's sake supply, ensuring it is not wasted, while others say it consumes the sake itself, leaving the jar empty.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Kameosa' (甕長) is composed of 'kame' (甕), meaning 'earthenware jar' or 'pot', and 'osa' (長), meaning 'chief' or 'elder'. Thus, it can be interpreted as 'elder of jars' or 'jar chief', suggesting a venerated or aged jar that has acquired spiritual significance.

Pronunciation — kah-meh-oh-sah

Aliases — Kame-osa, Sake jar yōkai, Sake spirit

Ownership — Japanese folklore, specifically within the category of tsukumogami (付喪神), household objects that come to life after a hundred years. Kameosa is a yōkai (妖怪), a class of supernatura

Powers
Animation

Kameosa is an inanimate sake jar that has come to life, able to move, speak, and interact with the world. This is a result of its age, as per tsukumogami lore.

Limit: It is bound to its physical form as a jar; if the jar is destroyed, the spirit may perish.

Sake Manipulation

Kameosa is often said to have control over sake, able to refill its jar or cause sake to appear or disappear. It may also influence the quality of the brew.

Limit: This ability is not consistently attributed; some stories do not mention it.

Illusion and Trickery

Like many yōkai, Kameosa can create illusions to confuse or amuse humans, such as making them see double or leading them on a wild chase.

Limit: Its tricks are usually harmless and short-lived.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kameosa is typically depicted as an old, round sake jar made of earthenware or ceramic, often with a lid. It is anthropomorphized with a face, usually featuring a cheerful or drunken expression, and sometimes with small arms and legs. The jar may have cracks or moss, indicating its age. In some illustrations, it is shown with a sake cup or gourd, emphasizing its connection to alcohol.

Symbols — The jar itself is a symbol of sake brewing and consumption. In Japanese culture, sake jars are associated with celebration, hospitality, and the harvest. Kameosa embodies the spirit of the drink, representing both the joy of intoxication and the potential for chaos. Its anthropomorphic form suggests that even inanimate objects can possess a soul, a common theme in tsukumogami lore.

Domains — Kameosa is found in households, breweries, and taverns where sake is stored. It is said to inhabit old jars that have been kept for a long time, particularly those that have reached the age of one hundred years, as per tsukumogami tradition. It is most active at night, when the household is asleep.

Accounts I
The Hundred-Year JarGeneral tsukumogami folklore

Edo period to present

An old sake jar in a merchant's home reaches its hundredth birthday and comes to life. It begins to roll around the house at night, making noise and occasionally tipping over to spill sake. The merchant, annoyed, tries to discard the jar, but it always returns. Eventually, the merchant learns to appreciate the jar's antics and offers it sake as an offering, after which the jar becomes a guardian of the household, warding off evil spirits. The jar is accepted as a household spirit, and the merchant gains good fortune.

Accounts II
The Drunken LaughterOral tradition, regional tales

Unknown

A farmer hears strange laughter coming from his storage shed at night. Upon investigating, he finds his old sake jar rolling on the floor, giggling. The jar speaks, asking for a drink. The farmer, amused, pours sake into the jar, and it drinks it all, becoming even merrier. From then on, the jar becomes a companion, and the farmer's sake never runs out, as the jar refills itself. The farmer enjoys endless sake and friendship with the spirit.
