Who It Is

In the dead of night, a demon lurks in the shadows of a warrior's home, plotting revenge against the legendary slayer of Shuten-dōji. This is Kidōmaru, a creature of vengeance and cunning, whose tale weaves through Japanese folklore, capturing the imagination across centuries.

Kidōmaru is portrayed as vengeful and cunning, lying in wait to ambush Yorimitsu. He is also shown as a skilled fighter, capable of tearing chains and defeating deer and boars as a child.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Kidōmaru (鬼童丸) combines 'ki' (oni/demon), 'dō' (child), and 'maru' (a common suffix for male names, often used for young boys). It can be interpreted as 'demon child' or 'oni child'.

Pronunciation — Kee-doh-mah-roo

Aliases — Kidō, 鬼童丸, 鬼同丸

Ownership — Japanese folklore, specifically from the Kamakura period setsuwa collection Kokon Chomonjū and later yōkai illustrations and warrior tales.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In the Kokon Chomonjū, Kidōmaru is described as an oni, but his physical appearance is not detailed. In Toriyama Sekien's illustration, he is depicted in the snow wearing the skin of a cattle, suggesting a monstrous or beastly form. In legends, as a child of Shuten-dōji, he is said to have had teeth at birth and grew to be a fearsome oni.

Symbols — Kidōmaru is often depicted in relation to cattle skin, as in Sekien's illustration, and in ukiyo-e prints showing him in combat or in the contest with Hakamadare.

Domains — According to the Zentaiheiki, he lived in a cave in the mountains after being expelled from Mount Hiei. In the Kokon Chomonjū, he is encountered at the home of Minamoto no Yorinobu and later hides in a cattle at Ichihara field.

Accounts I
The Ambush at the ToiletKokon Chomonjū

Kamakura period

Minamoto no Yorimitsu, known for slaying Shuten-dōji, visited his brother Yorinobu's home. Kidōmaru was caught at the toilet. Yorimitsu advised restraining him with chains, but Kidōmaru tore them off and, bearing a grudge, watched Yorimitsu's bed. Yorimitsu announced he would visit Kurama temple, and Kidōmaru went ahead, killed a cow, hid inside it, and waited. Yorimitsu saw through the ruse and ordered Watanabe no Tsuna to shoot the cow. Kidōmaru emerged and tried to slash Yorimitsu, but Yorimitsu struck him down with one blow. Kidōmaru was killed.

Accounts II
The Child of Shuten-dōjiOral tradition from Kumohara, Fukuchiyama

Unknown (recorded in modern times)

After Yorimitsu slew Shuten-dōji, a captured girl became mentally ill and gave birth to Shuten-dōji's child at Kumohara. The child had teeth at birth and by age 7 or 8 killed deer and boars with rocks. He grew up to become Kidōmaru and sought revenge on Yorimitsu. Kidōmaru dedicated his life to avenging his father.

The Contest with HakamadareShitennō Shōtō Iroku by Takizawa Bakin

Edo period

In a cave, Kidōmaru met Hakamadare, a bandit from the Konjaku Monogatarishū, and they engaged in a contest of skills. Unknown.
