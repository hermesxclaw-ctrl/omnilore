Who It Is

Imagine walking into a familiar IKEA—fluorescent lights, flat-pack furniture, the promise of meatballs—and realizing, only hours later, that the exit has vanished and the store stretches on for miles. Night falls when the lights click off, and faceless staff in yellow and blue uniforms begin to hunt, politely insisting that the store is closed as they tear through barricades of sofas and bookshelves. SCP-3008 turns the most mundane weekend errand into an endless, lethal labyrinth, where corporate signage becomes the names of fragile towns and meatball stations are the only proof that the universe still remembers you exist.

Attested: SCP-3008 itself behaves as a spatial anomaly: individuals who enter through the main entrance and pass out of sight of the doors are translocated into SCP-3008-1 without perceivable transition. They typically only realize the anomaly when attempting to return to the entrance and finding it absent. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) SCP-3008-1 exhibits a day–night cycle controlled by overhead lighting, corresponding to the opening and closing times of the original store. During "day" periods, SCP-3008-2 entities wander passively, ignoring humans unless attacked

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The designation "SCP-3008" follows the internal numbering system of the collaborative fiction project SCP Foundation, where anomalous entities and objects are catalogued as "Secure, Contain, Protect" (SCP) followed by a numeric identifier. "A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old IKEA" is an ironic subtitle

Pronunciation — Commonly pronounced as the initialism "ess-see-pee three-thousand-eight" or "ess-see-pee three-zero-zero-eight"; the subtitle is pronounced as standard English: "A perfectly normal, regular old eye-KEE-uh."

Powers
Spatial translocation at entrance

Attested: Individuals who enter SCP-3008 through the main entrance and pass out of sight of the doors are translocated into SCP-3008-1, a spatially impossible interior resembling an IKEA store. The transition is imperceptible; victims typically remain unaware until they attempt to leave and cannot find the entrance. This functions as a one-way portal into a pocket dimension or non-Euclidean space. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008)

Limit: Attested: The translocation appears tied specifically to the main entrance and the condition of passing out of sight of the doors. Attempts to breach walls or create alternative entrances reveal only non-anomalous sections of the store, suggesting that the anomaly is threshold-bound rather than building-wide. [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008)

Seemingly infinite interior (SCP-3008-1)

Attested: SCP-3008-1 is described as extending far beyond the physical limits of the retail unit, with measurements indicating at least 10 km² and no visible external terminators. Laser rangefinder tests yield inconclusive results, leading to speculation that the space may be infinite. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[3]](https://scp-db.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008-1)

Limit: Attested: The Foundation has not conclusively proven true infinity; the article explicitly frames infinity as speculative. Exploration is limited by danger from SCP-3008-2 and logistical constraints.

Autonomous resource replenishment (meatballs and supplies)

Attested: Food courts within SCP-3008-1 serve meatballs and other IKEA-style food despite the absence of visible kitchen staff or supply chains. Meatball stations appear to replenish themselves, providing a continuous food source for trapped humans. [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) [[3]](https://scp-db.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008-1)

Limit: Unknown: The mechanism of replenishment is not described; it may be an emergent property of the pocket dimension or an unobserved staff process.

Day–night cycle controlled by lighting

Attested: SCP-3008-1 exhibits a day–night cycle determined by the activation and deactivation of overhead lighting, corresponding to the opening and closing times of the original store. During "day" the environment is brightly lit and SCP-3008-2 entities are passive; during "night" the lights switch off and the entities become aggressive hunters. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008)

Limit: Attested: The cycle appears regular but is not fully mapped; trapped civilians rely on it for survival planning. The Foundation has not demonstrated external control over the cycle.

SCP-3008-2 humanoid entities (The Staff)

Attested: SCP-3008-2 are humanoid entities inhabiting SCP-3008-1. They resemble humans but possess exaggerated and inconsistent bodily proportions (too tall, too short, elongated limbs) and lack facial features. They wear yellow shirts and blue trousers matching IKEA staff uniforms, which appear integrated with their bodies. During the day they wander passively; at night they become violently hostile, attacking humans and repeating customer-service phrases about store closing. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008)

Limit: Attested: SCP-3008-2 can be killed by sufficient force; the Foundation has terminated at least one instance outside the building. Their senses and exact vulnerabilities are not fully documented, but fortifications and distance can reduce risk.

Randomized exits from SCP-3008-1

Attested: SCP-3008-1 contains exits that allow humans to return to baseline reality, but these exits are rare, difficult to locate, and appear to move or manifest unpredictably. Only a small number of individuals (14 in one fan count) have successfully exited since containment began. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008)

Limit: Attested: The Foundation has not established a reliable method for locating or stabilizing exits. Disorientation, vast scale, and hostile entities severely limit escape attempts.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Attested: SCP-3008 appears externally as a large, modern retail unit branded as an IKEA furniture store, located within a retail park. The building’s exterior is non-anomalous in appearance, matching typical IKEA architecture: a box-like structure, large signage, and surrounding parking. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) Inside, SCP-3008-1 manifests as a vast interior resembling an IKEA showroom and warehouse, with aisles of furniture

Symbols — Attested: SCP-3008’s iconography is dominated by IKEA’s corporate branding—blue and yellow color schemes, section signs (e.g., "Living Room", "Exchange", "Checkouts"), and staff uniforms. SCP-3008-2 entities wear yellow shirts and blue trousers consistent with IKEA employee uniforms, but these garments appear integrated into their bodies rather than removable clothing. SCP-3008 — A Perfectly Normal, Regular Old IKEA (main containment narrative)SCP Foundation official article

2017 (publication of SCP-3008 article) to present

Attested: The main SCP-3008 article presents a quasi-technical containment report describing an anomalous IKEA store whose interior (SCP-3008-1) is a vast, possibly infinite pocket dimension populated by faceless humanoid staff (SCP-3008-2) and numerous trapped civilians. The SCP Foundation purchases the retail park, redirects roads, and establishes Site-██ to monitor and restrict access. Special Containment Procedures mandate constant surveillance of the entrance, prohibition of civilian entry, detention and amnestic treatment of any humans who exit, and termination of non-human entities that emerge. The Description section details the spatial anomaly, the day–night cycle, the behavior of SCP-3008-2, and the emergent civilization formed by trapped civilians. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) Attested: The Foundation’s containment efforts prevent further mass entrapment but leave existing civilians inside SCP-3008-1. Incident 3008-1 — Recovered civilian journal and escape attemptAddendum 3008-2 in SCP-3008 article; summarized in fan wikis

In-universe date unspecified; presented as a single incident report

Attested: Incident 3008-1 is a narrative addendum comprising a Foundation report and a recovered civilian diary. The report states that at 12:37 AM on a given date, a man escaped SCP-3008-1 and emerged into the parking lot, only to be killed by an SCP-3008-2 instance shortly thereafter. The Foundation terminates the entity and recovers the man’s journal. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) The diary entries chronicle his experiences inside SCP-3008-1: initial disorientation, encounters with other survivors, settlement in a town called "Exchange", observations of Foundation drones, and repeated nightly attacks by SCP-3008-2. He describes memory gaps among residents, including ignorance of widely known cultural artifacts (e.g., the Statue of Liberty), suggesting that the trapped civilians may originate from alternate universes. Exchange is eventually destroyed by SCP-3008-2, prompting his attempt to reach an exit. He succeeds in leaving SCP-3008-1 but is killed before full debriefing. Fandom survival narratives and game-based storiesRoblox game "SCP-3008" and other fan-made games; WikiHow discussion of game adaptations

Late 2010s–2020s

Attested: SCP-3008 has inspired numerous survival-horror games, most notably a popular Roblox experience where players are trapped in an infinite IKEA-like environment, build bases from furniture, and survive nightly attacks by staff-like entities. These games generate emergent narratives as players cooperate, betray, or experiment with the environment. WikiHow’s article references the Roblox game and explains its mechanics as an accessible entry point into SCP-3008 lore. [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) Attested: Game adaptations significantly broaden SCP-3008’s audience, embedding its themes of disorientation, corporate liminality, and survival into interactive media. They also influence fan expectations about the anomaly’s behavior, sometimes blurring the line between canonical lore and gameplay conventions.
