Who It Is

An IKEA retail store that is dimensionally anomalous: the interior is a theoretically infinite extradimensional space. Once inside, exits become impossible to find, and the interior geography shifts continuously. Survivors have formed tribal societies within the store, battling faceless humanoid entities (designated 'Staff') that appear at night. The Staff are uniformly dressed in IKEA employee uniforms and attack humans on sight.

Form

From the exterior: a normal IKEA building. Interior: endless showrooms, identical furniture displays, and a perpetual twilight sky visible through nonexistent ceiling windows. The 'Staff' are tall, featureless humanoids in yellow IKEA uniforms.

Spatial translocation at entrance Attested: Individuals who enter SCP-3008 through the main entrance and pass out of sight of the doors are translocated into SCP-3008-1, a spatially impossible interior resembling an IKEA store. The transition is imperceptible; victims typically remain unaware until they attempt to leave and cannot find the entrance. This functions as a one-way portal into a pocket dimension or non-Euclidean space. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) Canonical SCP-3008 article; reinforced by fan summaries and WikiHow explanation. Attested: The translocation appears tied specifically to the main entrance and the condition of passing out of sight of the doors. Attempts to breach walls or create alternative entrances reveal only non-anomalous sections of the store, suggesting that the anomaly is threshold-bound rather than building-wide. [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) Disputed: Some fan sources imply that any entrance could lead to SCP-3008-1, while the canonical article emphasizes the main entrance. The exact mechanism—portal vs dimensional overlay—remains speculative.

Seemingly infinite interior (SCP-3008-1) Attested: SCP-3008-1 is described as extending far beyond the physical limits of the retail unit, with measurements indicating at least 10 km² and no visible external terminators. Laser rangefinder tests yield inconclusive results, leading to speculation that the space may be infinite. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[3]](https://scp-db.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008-1) Canonical SCP article; elaborated by SCP3008 Wiki and SCP Database Wiki. Attested: The Foundation has not conclusively proven true infinity; the article explicitly frames infinity as speculative. Exploration is limited by danger from SCP-3008-2 and logistical constraints. Disputed: Fan sources often assert outright that SCP-3008-1 is infinite, whereas the canonical article maintains uncertainty. Scholarly consensus within SCP lore discussions tends to respect the canonical ambiguity.

Autonomous resource replenishment (meatballs and supplies) Attested: Food courts within SCP-3008-1 serve meatballs and other IKEA-style food despite the absence of visible kitchen staff or supply chains. Meatball stations appear to replenish themselves, providing a continuous food source for trapped humans. [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) [[3]](https://scp-db.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008-1) WikiHow explanation and SCP Database Wiki; implied by diary entries describing ongoing access to food. Unknown: The mechanism of replenishment is not described; it may be an emergent property of the pocket dimension or an unobserved staff process. Hypothetical: Some readers interpret the replenishment as evidence of a broader reality-warping ability, while others see it as a narrative convenience. No canonical experiment isolates this effect.

Day–night cycle controlled by lighting Attested: SCP-3008-1 exhibits a day–night cycle determined by the activation and deactivation of overhead lighting, corresponding to the opening and closing times of the original store. During "day" the environment is brightly lit and SCP-3008-2 entities are passive; during "night" the lights switch off and the entities become aggressive hunters. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) Canonical SCP article; fan wikis; WikiHow. Attested: The cycle appears regular but is not fully mapped; trapped civilians rely on it for survival planning. The Foundation has not demonstrated external control over the cycle. None significant; all major sources agree on the basic pattern.

SCP-3008-2 humanoid entities (The Staff) Attested: SCP-3008-2 are humanoid entities inhabiting SCP-3008-1. They resemble humans but possess exaggerated and inconsistent bodily proportions (too tall, too short, elongated limbs) and lack facial features. They wear yellow shirts and blue trousers matching IKEA staff uniforms, which appear integrated with their bodies. During the day they wander passively; at night they become violently hostile, attacking humans and repeating customer-service phrases about store closing. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) Canonical SCP article; fan wikis; WikiHow. Attested: SCP-3008-2 can be killed by sufficient force; the Foundation has terminated at least one instance outside the building. Their senses and exact vulnerabilities are not fully documented, but fortifications and distance can reduce risk. Disputed: Some fan depictions exaggerate their strength, speed, or intelligence beyond canonical descriptions. Canon presents them as dangerous but not invincible.

Randomized exits from SCP-3008-1 Attested: SCP-3008-1 contains exits that allow humans to return to baseline reality, but these exits are rare, difficult to locate, and appear to move or manifest unpredictably. Only a small number of individuals (14 in one fan count) have successfully exited since containment began. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008) Canonical SCP article; SCP Foundation Wikia; WikiHow. Attested: The Foundation has not established a reliable method for locating or stabilizing exits. Disorientation, vast scale, and hostile entities severely limit escape attempts. Fan theories propose that exits are linked to emotional states, specific store sections, or probabilistic anomalies, but these remain speculative and unsupported by canonical experimentation.

Incident Log

Attested: The main SCP-3008 article presents a quasi-technical containment report describing an anomalous IKEA store whose interior (SCP-3008-1) is a vast, possibly infinite pocket dimension populated by faceless humanoid staff (SCP-3008-2) and numerous trapped civilians. The SCP Foundation purchases the retail park, redirects roads, and establishes Site-██ to monitor and restrict access. Special Containment Procedures mandate constant surveillance of the entrance, prohibition of civilian entry, detention and amnestic treatment of any humans who exit, and termination of non-human entities that emerge. The Description section details the spatial anomaly, the day–night cycle, the behavior of SCP-3008-2, and the emergent civilization formed by trapped civilians. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008)

Attested: Incident 3008-1 is a narrative addendum comprising a Foundation report and a recovered civilian diary. The report states that at 12:37 AM on a given date, a man escaped SCP-3008-1 and emerged into the parking lot, only to be killed by an SCP-3008-2 instance shortly thereafter. The Foundation terminates the entity and recovers the man’s journal. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) The diary entries chronicle his experiences inside SCP-3008-1: initial disorientation, encounters with other survivors, settlement in a town called "Exchange", observations of Foundation drones, and repeated nightly attacks by SCP-3008-2. He describes memory gaps among residents, including ignorance of widely known cultural artifacts (e.g., the Statue of Liberty), suggesting that the trapped civilians may originate from alternate universes. Exchange is eventually destroyed by SCP-3008-2, prompting his attempt to reach an exit. He succeeds in leaving SCP-3008-1 but is killed before full debriefing.

Attested: SCP-3008 has inspired numerous survival-horror games, most notably a popular Roblox experience where players are trapped in an infinite IKEA-like environment, build bases from furniture, and survive nightly attacks by staff-like entities. These games generate emergent narratives as players cooperate, betray, or experiment with the environment. WikiHow’s article references the Roblox game and explains its mechanics as an accessible entry point into SCP-3008 lore. [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008)

Content Notes

Attested: During the "night" phase in SCP-3008-1, SCP-3008-2 entities become aggressively hostile, hunting and killing humans trapped within the store. The diary in Incident 3008-1 describes repeated attacks on settlements, destruction of fortifications, and deaths of residents. The Foundation report notes that an escaped civilian was killed by an SCP-3008-2 instance in the parking lot shortly after exiting. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[2]](https://scp-3008.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008)

Within SCP lore, this violence exemplifies survival horror tropes and raises ethical questions about containment vs rescue. The use of corporate uniforms and customer-service phrases during attacks underscores the uncanny distortion of everyday retail environments.

SCP-3008 article; SCP Foundation Wikia; SCP3008 Wiki.

Attested: The town of "Exchange" and other settlements within SCP-3008-1 are attacked and destroyed by SCP-3008-2, resulting in mass casualties and displacement of survivors. The diary recounts the loss of friends and community, highlighting the fragility of improvised civilizations under constant threat. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[3]](https://scp-db.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008-1)

These events resonate with post-apocalyptic and refugee narratives, using the familiar setting of a furniture store to explore themes of community, loss, and resilience.

Incident 3008-1 diary; fan summaries.

Attested: Trapped civilians exhibit memory gaps and inconsistent cultural knowledge, such as ignorance of iconic landmarks. The diary suggests that residents may come from alternate universes, implying forced displacement and identity disruption. Prolonged entrapment, nightly violence, and uncertainty about escape contribute to severe psychological stress. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008) [[1]](https://scp.fandom.com/wiki/SCP-3008) [[4]](https://www.wikihow.com/Scp-3008)

These elements align with horror’s exploration of derealization and multiversal dislocation, and they invite metaphorical readings about consumer culture and alienation.

Incident 3008-1 diary; SCP Foundation Wikia; WikiHow.

Attested: The SCP Foundation detains any humans who exit SCP-3008, subjects them to debriefing, administers amnestics, and releases them with cover stories. Non-human entities are terminated on sight. While framed as necessary for secrecy and public safety, these practices involve coercion, memory alteration, and lethal force. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3008)

In-universe, such measures are standard; meta-critically, they raise questions about institutional violence, surveillance, and the cost of maintaining normalcy in the face of anomalies.

A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine walking into a familiar IKEA—fluorescent lights, flat-pack furniture, the promise of meatballs—and realizing, only hours later, that the exit has vanished and the store stretches on for miles. Night falls when the lights click off, and faceless staff in yellow and blue uniforms begin to hunt, politely insisting that the store is closed as they tear through barricades of sofas and bookshelves. SCP-3008 turns the most mundane weekend errand into an endless, lethal labyrinth, where corporate signage becomes the names of fragile towns and meatball stations are the only proof that the universe still remembers you exist.
