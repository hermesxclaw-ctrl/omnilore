The Approach

In the early 1990s, Japanese horse racing was electrified by a rivalry known as the 'BNW' trio—three colts whose battles defined an era. Among them, Biwa Hayahide stood out not only for his remarkable speed but for his unmistakable presence: a large, handsome face that charmed fans and drew teasing in equal measure. His journey from a farm injury to Triple Crown glory and Horse of the Year honors is a testament to resilience and raw talent.

Content advisory. No dark material beyond the inherent risks of horse racing and a near-fatal injury.
Identity

Biwa Hayahide

Pronunciation BEE-wah ha-YAH-hee-deh

Etymology

The name 'Biwa Hayahide' was given with the wish to 'excel in speed'. 'Hayahide' combines 'haya' (fast) and 'hide' (excellent), while 'Biwa' likely refers to the Biwa region or the biwa lute, but the exact origin is unconfirmed.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese thoroughbred racing; part of the 'BNW' trio era

Disputed Identifications

None noted; his identity is well-documented in racing records.

Description

Appearance

Biwa Hayahide was noted for his large, long face, which was often described as handsome but disproportionately large. His head was large for his body as a foal, with thick legs. He had big black eyes that gave an intelligent look. As a four-year-old, his body lengthened and became more flexible, improving his proportions.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography are documented.

Behaviour & Personality

He was described as having a calm atmosphere, which contributed to his popularity, especially among female fans. His intelligence was noted from a young age.

Domains & Habitats

Trained at Hamada Stables; raced primarily in Japan, including Hanshin, Kyoto, and Tokyo racecourses.

Version Differences

No significant version differences; his physical appearance evolved as he matured, with his body becoming more proportionate.

Powers & Abilities

Racing speed

Source Version All versions

Description

Biwa Hayahide was a top-tier racehorse, winning 10 of 16 races, including three Grade I victories. He set records in the Kikuka-shō and Takarazuka Kinen.

Limitations

He lost his first race in the Asahi Hai Futurity Stakes and had a streak of finishing in the top two in his first 15 races, which was broken in his final race.

Stories & Myths

The BNW Rivalry

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1993

Summary

Biwa Hayahide, Narita Taishin, and Winning Ticket formed the 'BNW' trio, dominating the 1993 Japanese Triple Crown. They finished in the top three in various orders across the Satsuki Shō, Tōkyō Yūshun, and Kikuka-shō, with Biwa Hayahide ultimately winning the Kikuka-shō by five lengths.

Participants

Biwa Hayahide

Narita Taishin

Winning Ticket

Consequences

Biwa Hayahide was named Japanese Horse of the Year and Best Three-Year-Old Colt for 1993.

None noted.

Injury and Recovery

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1991

Summary

As a yearling, Biwa Hayahide collided with a fence, suffering a 10 cm gouge near the metatarsal bone on his right forelimb. The cut was 1 cm from his tendon, which would have been fatal. He recovered and went on to race.

Participants

Biwa Hayahide

Consequences

He was able to train and race, eventually achieving great success.
