Who It Is

In the early 1990s, Japanese horse racing was electrified by a rivalry known as the 'BNW' trio—three colts whose battles defined an era. Among them, Biwa Hayahide stood out not only for his remarkable speed but for his unmistakable presence: a large, handsome face that charmed fans and drew teasing in equal measure. His journey from a farm injury to Triple Crown glory and Horse of the Year honors is a testament to resilience and raw talent.

He was described as having a calm atmosphere, which contributed to his popularity, especially among female fans. His intelligence was noted from a young age.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Biwa Hayahide' was given with the wish to 'excel in speed'. 'Hayahide' combines 'haya' (fast) and 'hide' (excellent), while 'Biwa' likely refers to the Biwa region or the biwa lute, but the exact origin is unconfirmed.

Pronunciation — BEE-wah ha-YAH-hee-deh

Aliases — Biwa Hayahide

Ownership — Japanese thoroughbred racing; part of the 'BNW' trio era

Powers
Racing speed

Biwa Hayahide was a top-tier racehorse, winning 10 of 16 races, including three Grade I victories. He set records in the Kikuka-shō and Takarazuka Kinen.

Limit: He lost his first race in the Asahi Hai Futurity Stakes and had a streak of finishing in the top two in his first 15 races, which was broken in his final race.

Resilience

He recovered from a near-fatal injury as a yearling, which could have severed a tendon.

Limit: None noted; he returned to full racing fitness.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Biwa Hayahide was noted for his large, long face, which was often described as handsome but disproportionately large. His head was large for his body as a foal, with thick legs. He had big black eyes that gave an intelligent look. As a four-year-old, his body lengthened and became more flexible, improving his proportions.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography are documented.

Domains — Trained at Hamada Stables; raced primarily in Japan, including Hanshin, Kyoto, and Tokyo racecourses.

Accounts I
The BNW RivalryProvided source material

1993

Biwa Hayahide, Narita Taishin, and Winning Ticket formed the 'BNW' trio, dominating the 1993 Japanese Triple Crown. They finished in the top three in various orders across the Satsuki Shō, Tōkyō Yūshun, and Kikuka-shō, with Biwa Hayahide ultimately winning the Kikuka-shō by five lengths. Biwa Hayahide was named Japanese Horse of the Year and Best Three-Year-Old Colt for 1993.

Injury and RecoveryProvided source material

1991

As a yearling, Biwa Hayahide collided with a fence, suffering a 10 cm gouge near the metatarsal bone on his right forelimb. The cut was 1 cm from his tendon, which would have been fatal. He recovered and went on to race. He was able to train and race, eventually achieving great success.

Accounts II
Record-Breaking Kikuka-shōProvided source material

1993

In the Kikuka-shō, Biwa Hayahide won by five lengths, breaking the record set by Rice Shower the previous year with a time of 3:04.7. This victory secured his Triple Crown contention and contributed to his Horse of the Year title.

Rivalry with Tokai TeioProvided source material

1993

In the Arima Kinen, Biwa Hayahide was the favorite but was passed by Tokai Teio, who was making his return from injury. This was a notable defeat. Biwa Hayahide finished second, but still earned Horse of the Year honors.

Four-Year-Old Season TriumphsProvided source material

1994

Biwa Hayahide won the Kyoto Kinen, spring Tenno Sho, and Takarazuka Kinen, setting a record time in the latter. He skipped the Japan Cup, leading to some criticism. He was named Best Older Male Horse in 1994.
