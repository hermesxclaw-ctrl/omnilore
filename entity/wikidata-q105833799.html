The Approach

In the dense rainforests of Central Africa, the Fang people tell of a creator god who is not one but three—Nzame, Mebere, and Nkwa—a trinity that shaped the universe and then, dissatisfied with their first human, destroyed everything except him, leaving a malevolent immortal to haunt the world.

Content advisory. Contains themes of divine punishment, destruction, and a malevolent immortal figure.

Nzame

Etymology

The name 'Nzame' is of Fang origin, but its precise etymological roots are not well-documented in available sources. It may be related to other Bantu terms for deity or sky god.

Cultural Ownership

Fang people of Equatorial Guinea, Gabon, and Cameroon

Disputed Identifications

Nzame is sometimes conflated with other African creator deities, but within Fang mythology, Nzame is distinct as part of a trinity.

Stories & Myths

Creation of the First Human

Source Fang oral traditionCulture FangPeriod Pre-colonial

Summary

After creating the universe, the trinity decided to choose a leader for the earth. They rejected the elephant, leopard, and monkey, and instead created a human named Fam. Fam was originally a lizard created by Mebere, which after eight days became the first man. He was given abilities from all three gods and told to rule the earth. However, Fam became a cruel leader, oppressed the animals, and turned his back on Nzame. In response, Nzame destroyed everything except Fam, who was made immortal and became a malevolent figure.
