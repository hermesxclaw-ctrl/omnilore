Archive Dossier — Approach

In the Dutch winter, when the nights grow long and the wind howls through the chimneys, a shadowy figure may appear at the window—a creature of the cold season, known only as Klabotermon. Unlike the mischievous household kobold or the fearsome Krampus, this spirit walks the line between companion and warning, a presence that reminds us that winter is a time of both hardship and hope.

On St. Martin's Eve, children carry paper lanterns through the streets and sing for sweets. But those who do not share or who sing poorly will hear the chains of the Klabotermon.

Who It Is

De Klabotermon (also Klabo termon) is a regional Dutch winter spirit from Limburg folklore, associated with St. Martin's Eve (Sint-Maarten, November 11). The name likely derives from 'klabouter' (kobold/gnome) and 'termoon' (mask/procession). The Klabotermon appears as a dark, hooded figure wearing a carved wooden mask with menacing features — bulging eyes, a hooked nose, and a gaping mouth — and carrying a lantern on a pole. He is part of the St. Martin's procession in which children go door-to-door singing for sweets (a tradition shared with parts of Germany and Belgium). Unlike the benevolent saint, the Klabotermon represents the punitive counterpart: he chases children who are misbehaving or who fail to sing properly, rattling his chains and swinging his lantern. Children who are greedy or who do not share their candy are said to be taken by the Klabotermon. The tradition blends pre-Christian Germanic solstice customs (parading with lights and masks to drive away winter spirits) with the Christian feast of St. Martin of Tours. The Klabotermon figure is related to the broader 'Klabautermann' family of spirits across the Low Countries and Germany, but has a uniquely Limburgish character. The Klabotermon's mask (called 'termoon') is traditionally carved from wood by local craftsmen and passed down through families. Today, the Klabotermon is a beloved part of Limburg's cultural heritage, with folk groups preserving the tradition of masked processions.
