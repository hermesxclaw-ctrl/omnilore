Who It Is

Louhi is the primary antagonist of the Kalevala, the powerful witch and queen of Pohjola — the dark, cold Northland that stands in opposition to the heroes' Southland (Kalevala). She is sometimes called the 'gap-toothed hag of Pohjola' but is no mere crone: she is a shape-shifter of immense magical power, a cunning ruler, and a mother whose desire to secure advantageous marriages for her daughter drives much of the epic's conflict. Her name may be connected to the Finnish word louhikko ('rocky ground'), suggesting her association with the harsh northern landscape.

Louhi wields power through hospitality-turned-entrapment, much like the Greek Circe or the Slavic Baba Yaga. She lures the Kalevala heroes to her realm with promises of her daughter's hand in marriage, then sets them impossible tasks: Väinämöinen must plow a field of vipers, capture the bear of Tuoni, and the pike of Tuonela; Ilmarinen must forge the Sampo; Lemminkäinen must kill the Swan of Tuonela (which proves fatal). She is a gatekeeper: to win the prize, the hero must prove himself against the hostile powers she commands, powers rooted in the dangerous otherworld of the far north.

Her most complex and terrifying transformation occurs during the Sampo's theft. When Väinämöinen, Ilmarinen, and Lemminkäinen steal the Sampo and sail away, Louhi pursues them. First, she raises a fog to blind them and a storm to sink them. When these fail, she transforms into a giant eagle — 'her talons like scythes, her beak like a spear' — and attacks the ship. In the ensuing battle, she catches the Sampo in her claws, but Väinämöinen strikes it from her grasp with his oar, shattering it into the sea. The fragments become the ocean's bounty, but Louhi, enraged, steals the sun and moon and locks them inside a mountain in Pohjola, plunging the world into darkness. The heroes must journey north once more to free the celestial lights.

Louhi is not a simple villain. She is the mother of the Maiden of Pohjola, and much of her motivation — however ruthlessly pursued — is to secure a worthy match for her daughter among the great heroes. Her power is real and formidable: she commands the natural forces of cold, darkness, and disease. In the Kalevala's final runos, she sends nine diseases (the 'Nine Sons of the Plague') against Kalevala in retaliation for the theft of the Sampo and the celestial lights. Louhi embodies the hostile, life-denying powers of the northern winter, but she is also a ruler, a mother, and a sorceress whose magic must be overcome for light and warmth to return.

Some scholars interpret Louhi as a degraded form of an earlier goddess figure — possibly an earth goddess or death goddess from Finno-Ugric tradition — whose demonization in the Kalevala reflects the transition from shamanic worldview to Christian-influenced dualism.
