This page is intentionally blank and ready for source-backed lore, versions, images, and connections. Astaphaios is an archon, a ruler of a specific cosmic sphere. In the seven-archon scheme of the Apocryphon of John and On the Origin of the World, each archon is associated with a planetary sphere (the seven classical planets: Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn). However, the texts do not explicitly assign Astaphaios to a specific planet. The order of his creation (third in Apocryphon of John) does not align with the standard planetary order, making a clear astral identification impossible. His domain is best described as a segment of the lower, material heavens, a realm of counterfeit spirit and ignorance.

Form

The Apocryphon of John describes Astaphaios as having a 'face' that is 'like a [---]' (the text is damaged at this point). Other descriptions of the archons in the same text give them animal-like features (e.g., Yao has a donkey's face, Eloai a hyena's face). It is highly probable, though not explicitly stated due to the lacuna, that Astaphaios also had a theriomorphic (animal-like) appearance. The text does state he has a 'face' (ho), implying a head, but the specific animal is lost.

Powers

Archontic Rule As an archon, Astaphaios possesses authority over a portion of the material cosmos. This power is delegated from his father, Yaldabaoth, and is fundamentally limited. He is a ruler of a counterfeit realm, not a creator. His power is derived from the illegitimate act of his father and is ultimately destined to be dissolved. On the Origin of the World; Apocryphon of John His power is entirely confined to the material world. He has no authority over the spiritual realm (the Pleroma). He is also subject to the higher, hidden God and the aeons, even if he is unaware of this. His power is ultimately undone by the coming of the Savior (Christ) and the imparting of gnosis. None specific to Astaphaios. The texts are consistent in portraying the archons as powerful but fundamentally subordinate and flawed.

Procreation of Archontic Offspring In On the Origin of the World, Astaphaios, as a son of Yaldabaoth, is part of the first generation of archons. The text describes how Yaldabaoth and his offspring, including Astaphaios, each receive a portion of the cosmos to rule. They are also involved in the creation of Adam, though their attempts are flawed and incomplete until the higher powers intervene. On the Origin of the World Their creative power is limited to the material and psychic realms. They cannot create true spirit or life. Their creations are described as 'lame,' 'blind,' or otherwise imperfect. The Apocryphon of John presents a more elaborate scheme where the archons are created by Yaldabaoth, not as his sons, but as emanations from his own flawed nature. The relationship is one of father and offspring, but the process is different. This is a difference in narrative detail, not a direct contradiction of Astaphaios's role.

Psychic Manipulation The archons, including Astaphaios, are responsible for the creation of the 'counterfeit spirit' (antimimon pneuma) that dwells within humanity. This spirit is a psychic or material force that leads humans to be preoccupied with the physical world, passions, and ignorance, thereby trapping them in the cycle of reincarnation and away from the true God. Apocryphon of John; Hypostasis of the Archons This power is not absolute. The true spiritual element (the 'spark' or pneuma) within humans is beyond their reach. Their manipulation can be overcome through gnosis. The mechanism of this manipulation is described in slightly different ways across texts, but the core concept is consistent.

Accounts

The text describes how the chief archon Yaldabaoth, born from the flawed wisdom (Sophia), creates seven powers to help him rule the material world. Each power creates an archon, and Astaphaios is the third of these seven. The text gives a brief description of each archon, including a name and a facial feature (e.g., Yao has a donkey's face, Eloai a hyena's face). Astaphaios's description is partially lost in a lacuna.

The text describes Yaldabaoth as a lion-faced serpent who, in his arrogance, declares himself the only God. He then produces seven sons, each of whom is given dominion over a celestial sphere. Astaphaios is one of these sons. The narrative follows the archons' attempts to create humanity, their failure to give it true spirit, and the eventual intervention of the higher powers to save the spiritual element within Adam.

Scholarly Debates
Genealogy of the Archons
The Apocryphon of John: The archons are emanations created by Yaldabaoth through his 'seven powers.' ⇌ On the Origin of the World: The archons are the direct sons of Yaldabaoth.
Evidence for A: Apocryphon of John, Codex II, 1, 10:19-28
Evidence for B: On the Origin of the World, Codex II, 5, 101:2-3
Planetary Associations
Some scholars have attempted to assign each of the seven archons to a specific planet. ⇌ Other scholars argue that the texts do not support a clear, consistent planetary mapping.
Evidence for A: The order of the archons' creation and their names (e.g., Yao/Yahweh, Eloai/Elohim) suggest a Jewish astral background.
Evidence for B: The order of creation does not match the standard planetary order (e.g., the Moon is usually first, but Yao is not clear
The Number of Archons
The Apocryphon of John and On the Origin of the World list seven archons. ⇌ Other Gnostic texts (e.g., the Hypostasis of the Archons) also have seven, but the names and order can vary.
Evidence for A: The lists in the Apocryphon of John and On the Origin of the World.
Evidence for B: The list in the Hypostasis of the Archons, which does not include Astaphaios.
What We Don't Know

1. The specific appearance of Astaphaios in the Apocryphon of John is lost in a lacuna (a damaged section of the manuscript). 2. The etymology of his name remains uncertain. 3. No specific planetary association is given for him in the primary texts. 4. There is no known archaeological or artistic evidence from antiquity that depicts Astaphaios. 5. His role in the Hypostasis of the Archons is not explicitly detailed, as he is not named in that text. 6. This research is based on English translations of the Coptic texts; a direct study of the Coptic would be necessary for a more nuanced understanding.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Astaphaios not as Satan, but as the ultimate, cosmic-level incompetent bureaucrat. He's the middle manager of the material universe, born from a clerical error in the divine office. He believes he's the CEO, but he's really just the guy who runs the prison's day-to-day operations. He's not evil in a cartoonish way; he's just profoundly ignorant and arrogant, and his 'job' is to keep the real employees (human souls) from ever escaping to the head office. He's a tragic figure, in a way—a being who is himself a prisoner of his own limited nature, doing a job that was created by a mistake and will eventually be shut down.
