Who They Are

Arasy (also spelled Araci) is the Guarani moon goddess and the consort of Tupã, the supreme creator. She is the mother of the stars, the ruler of the night, and the protector of women and lovers. According to Guarani myth, she descended from the heavens with Tupã at the beginning of creation, and standing on a sacred hill, she assisted him in creating the world. Her name means 'Mother of the Day' or 'Mistress of the Day.'

Cultural Significance

Arasy remains an important figure in Guarani spirituality, particularly for women. Her cult has also influenced Brazilian popular religion, where 'Aracy' is a common personal name meaning 'mother of the people' or 'moon mother.' The Guarani practice of holding ceremonies under the full moon continues in many communities, blending traditional beliefs with contemporary Catholic practice. The reverence for the moon as a female deity in Guarani culture reflects the important role of women in Guarani society, where women traditionally controlled the household economy and held significant influence in community decisions.
