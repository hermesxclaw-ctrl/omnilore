Who They Are

Arasy (also spelled Araci) is the Guarani moon goddess and the consort of Tupã, the supreme creator. She is the mother of the stars, the ruler of the night, and the protector of women and lovers. According to Guarani myth, she descended from the heavens with Tupã at the beginning of creation, and standing on a sacred hill, she assisted him in creating the world. Her name means 'Mother of the Day' or 'Mistress of the Day.'

The Approach

Before the world was, the moon goddess Arasy descended with Tupã upon a hill in Areguá, and from that sacred ground, all of creation unfolded. She is the celestial mother whose gaze shaped the stars and whose curse gave birth to the seven legendary monsters of Guarani lore.

Etymology

The name Arasy likely derives from the Guarani words 'ara' (day, sky, or time) and 'y' (water or mother), possibly meaning 'mother of the sky' or 'lady of the waters'. However, this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Guarani people of Paraguay, Argentina, Brazil, and Bolivia

Disputed Identifications

Arasy is sometimes identified with the moon, but some traditions may associate her with the stars or the earth. There is no consensus on her exact domain beyond her role in creation.

Not described in the source material. As a moon goddess, she is likely associated with the moon's appearance, but no specific physical description is given.

Symbols & Iconography

The moon is her primary symbol. She is also associated with the hill in Areguá where creation took place.

Behaviour & Personality

Arasy is portrayed as a benevolent creator goddess who aids Tupã in creation. She is also a moral authority, as she curses the children of Kerana and Tau for their transgression.

Domains & Habitats

Sky, moon, creation, and possibly water (based on etymological speculation). She is said to have descended to a hill in Areguá, Paraguay.

Version Differences

The source notes that regional variations in Guarani mythology may redefine the roles of deities. Arasy's role may vary, but she is consistently associated with the moon and creation.

Translation

The primary figure in most Guarani creation legends is Tupã, the supreme god of all creation. With the help of the moon goddess Arasy, Tupã descended upon the Earth in a location specified as a hill in the region of Areguá, and from that location created all that is found upon the face of the earth, including the ocean, forests, and the animals. It is also said that the stars were placed in the sky at this point.

Cultural Significance

Arasy remains an important figure in Guarani spirituality, particularly for women. Her cult has also influenced Brazilian popular religion, where 'Aracy' is a common personal name meaning 'mother of the people' or 'moon mother.' The Guarani practice of holding ceremonies under the full moon continues in many communities, blending traditional beliefs with contemporary Catholic practice. The reverence for the moon as a female deity in Guarani culture reflects the important role of women in Guarani society, where women traditionally controlled the household economy and held significant influence in community decisions.
