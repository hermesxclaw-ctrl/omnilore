Who It Is

The Death Knight is D&D's answer to the question: 'What happens when the world's greatest paladin falls?' A Death Knight is the undead remnant of a once-noble warrior — typically a paladin or knight — who betrayed their oaths so completely that the gods themselves cursed them to rise again, bound to their armor forever as a punishment. Physically, a Death Knight appears as a skeleton or corpse clad in the blackened, perpetually smoldering armor it wore in life. The armor is fused to its bones, and a deathly chill radiates from every plate. Eyes that once shone with holy light now burn with orange or green flames of pure malevolence.

The most famous Death Knight in D&D history is Lord Soth of the Dragonlance setting. Soth was a Knight of the Rose — the highest order of Solamnic Knighthood — who fell from grace through a series of tragic choices: lust, jealousy, murder, and ultimately the refusal to carry out a divine mission that would have prevented the Cataclysm that devastated Krynn. Cursed by the gods for his failures, Soth became the Knight of the Black Rose, ruling over the cursed fortress of Dargaard Keep, eternally tormented by the whispers of the elven women whose deaths he caused.

In combat, a Death Knight retains all the martial prowess it possessed in life, amplified by undead powers. It wields a sword wreathed in hellfire, can hurl fireballs, command lesser undead, project waves of supernatural fear, and — reflecting its anti-paladin nature — can use a corrupted version of the paladin's smite ability, channeling necrotic energy instead of divine radiance. Death Knights are fully intelligent and maintain their personalities and memories, making them far more dangerous than mindless undead. They often command armies of lesser undead and living cultists, and their lairs are masterpieces of dark fortification.

Death Knights cannot be permanently destroyed through mere combat — if their body is destroyed, it reforms. To finally end a Death Knight, one must achieve redemption (restoring their lost honor in a way that breaks the curse) or permanently destroy their soul in a manner dictated by the nature of their original betrayal. The concept has been enormously influential, inspiring the Death Knight class in World of Warcraft (directly acknowledged by Blizzard as inspired by D&D), the Nazgûl / Ringwraiths of Tolkien (a clear antecedent), and the Dark Knights of Final Fantasy.
