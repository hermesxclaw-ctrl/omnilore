Who It Is

Angra Mainyu (meaning 'Destructive Spirit' or 'Evil Mentality'), also known by his Middle Persian name Ahriman, is the supreme antagonist of Zoroastrian cosmology—the embodiment of druj (falsehood, chaos, destruction) and the eternal adversary of Ahura Mazda and all of creation. In Zoroaster's own Gathas, Angra Mainyu is the twin spirit (Mainyu) who chose evil as Ahura Mazda's twin chose good—not a co-eternal equal but a being who made a fundamental moral choice against asha. Later Zoroastrian theology, particularly in the Bundahishn ('Primal Creation'), developed a more elaborate dualistic cosmology: Ahura Mazda and Angra Mainyu are separated by the Void (Vayu), and when Angra Mainyu became aware of Ahura Mazda's perfect spiritual creation, he arose from the abyss and attacked it, initiating the cosmic conflict that defines all of existence. Angra Mainyu is the creator of all that is harmful, ugly, and destructive: disease, death, noxious creatures (khrafstra) like snakes, scorpions, and ants; drought, famine, winter, and every form of pollution—physical, moral, and spiritual. He is assisted by the Daevas (false gods, demons) and the archdemons (Kamaligan), the most prominent being Aka Manah (Evil Mind), Indra (corrupted from the Vedic deity), and Aeshma (Wrath, the source of the Hebrew Asmodeus). His invasion of the material world introduced death, suffering, and evil into what was originally a perfect creation. However, Zoroastrian eschatology promises that Angra Mainyu will ultimately be defeated: at the end of time, the savior Saoshyant will be born, the dead will be resurrected, and the final battle will result in the destruction of Angra Mainyu and the renovation (Frashokereti) of all existence into a state of eternal perfection. Angra Mainyu thus represents the most philosophically sophisticated concept of evil in ancient religions—a being whose destructive power is real and catastrophic but whose eventual defeat is cosmically guaranteed.
