Who It Is

BEN Drowned is a seminal work of internet horror and one of the earliest and most influential Alternate Reality Games (ARGs) on YouTube. Created by Alex Hall (under the username 'Jadusable') in 2010, the story is presented through a series of first-person accounts, videos, and text logs. The narrator claims to have purchased a used copy of The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask from a garage sale, sold by an elderly man who said his son — named Ben — had drowned. When the narrator plays the game, he discovers the previous save file is named 'BEN,' and strange, unsettling glitches begin occurring: NPCs address the player as 'Ben,' the Happy Mask Salesman's model distorts into a terrifying figure, and the game seems to communicate directly with the player. The events escalate beyond the game — files appear on the narrator's computer, messages pop up saying 'You shouldn't have done that,' and the entity calling itself BEN seems to cross from the digital world into reality. The series culminated in a real-time ARG where viewers were invited to help 'solve' the haunting, blurring the line between fiction and reality. BEN Drowned was groundbreaking in its use of the video medium — the 'glitched' Majora's Mask footage was convincingly edited, and many early viewers believed the haunting was real. The character of BEN — a drowned boy whose spirit was trapped in the game data, twisted into something malevolent — became an iconic figure in digital horror. BEN Drowned established the template for 'haunted media' stories that would dominate creepypasta for years to come: a vintage piece of technology, a dead child, and a ghost that knows more about you than it should. In 2020, the creator revealed that the original videos had been fiction, and released a comprehensive documentary about the ARG's creation, closure, and lasting impact on internet horror culture.
