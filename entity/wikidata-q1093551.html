Who It Is

At thirteen he was already fighting men who could kill with a single joint lock. At eighteen he went looking for the only man on earth who could beat him — his father, Yujiro Hanma, the creature called the Ogre, whose back muscles form the face of a demon. Baki Hanma's story is not about becoming the strongest; it is about surviving the price of trying.

Attested personality: intensely driven, respectful to genuine martial artists, emotionally open and even tender with friends and girlfriend Kozue Matsumoto, but obsessively fixated on surpassing his father. He is not a stoic killer; he is depicted as polite, curious, sometimes goofy, and willing to learn from any opponent. He refuses to kill unnecessarily and often spares defeated foes, contrasting with Yujiro. Name & Tongue

Etymology — Japanese surname Hanma (範馬) combines han/fan (範, 'model, pattern') and ma/ba (馬, 'horse'); given name Baki (刃牙) combines ba/jin (刃, 'blade') and ga/ki (牙, 'fang'). Creator Keisuke Itagaki has not published a formal in-universe etymology; the name evokes 'blade fang' consistent with fighting themes.

Pronunciation — Japanese: /baꜜki hanma/ (BA-ki HAN-ma), often anglicized as BAH-kee HAN-mah; Azerbaijani Bakı is /bɑˈcɯ/, distinct.

Aliases — Hanma Baki, Grappler Baki, Baki, Champion

Ownership — Japanese manga/anime franchise created by Keisuke Itagaki, serialized by Akita Shoten in Weekly Shōnen Champion since 1991. Owned and managed by Akita Shoten (publisher) and Itagak

Powers
Hanma Blood and Demon Face / Demon Brain

Latent genetic trait of the Hanma lineage allowing extreme adrenaline, muscle recruitment, and formation of demon face on the back. Demon Brain is a related state where Baki can unconsciously process combat at extreme speed, reacting in 0.5 seconds without conscious thought. Manifests fully after training and near-death experiences.

Limit: Not constantly active; requires extreme stress, rage, or conscious trigger. Drains stamina; early Baki cannot sustain it and is overwhelmed by Yujiro's superior mastery. Not supernatural magic — framed as extreme physiology.

Total Martial Arts Synthesis and Imaging Training

Mastery of multiple disciplines (karate under Doppo Orochi, boxing, wrestling, jiu-jitsu, Chinese kenpo) and ability to train by vividly imagining opponents (e.g., fighting a mantis, dinosaur, or Yujiro in his mind) with real physical gains. Can copy and counter techniques after seeing them once.

Limit: Imaging cannot fully replicate unknown techniques; still vulnerable to novel or overwhelming force (e.g., Yujiro's sheer power, Pickle's prehistoric strength). Requires prior exposure or understanding.

Endurance, Pain Tolerance, and Recovery

Survives and recovers from injuries that would be fatal: broken bones, concussions, stab wounds, poison. Recovers quickly between fights, attributed to Hanma constitution and willpower. Fought continuously through Maximum Tournament with injuries.

Limit: Not invulnerable; is hospitalized, poisoned successfully by Ryuko Yanagi, and knocked unconscious by Yujiro. Recovery still takes days/weeks in-universe.

0.5 Second Unconscious Combat (Mushin)

Technique where body reacts without brain processing, bypassing conscious reaction time, allowing counters to otherwise unavoidable attacks. Learned through death-row inmate fights and Yujiro encounters.

Limit: Only works when mind is cleared; strong emotions or hesitation break it. Yujiro can still overcome it with superior speed.

Cockroach Tackle and Unorthodox Grappling

Signature low diving tackle mimicking cockroach scuttle, plus joint locks, armbars, and ground fighting adapted from wrestling and vale tudo. Uses environment and leverage over brute force when outmatched.

Limit: Less effective against vastly heavier opponents (e.g., Mount Toba, Pickle) without setup.

Accounts I
The Underground Arena and Childhood VowGrappler Baki Vol.1-5 (1991-1992)

1991-1992 publication; in-universe 1980s-1990s modern Tokyo

Thirteen-year-old Baki Hanma fights in Mitsunari Tokugawa's secret underground arena beneath the Tokyo Dome, defeating seasoned martial artists. Flashbacks reveal his brutal training under his mother Emi Akezawa's funding and his father's cruelty: Yujiro Hanma killed Emi in front of Baki after Baki failed to protect her, forging Baki's vow to surpass his father. Early opponents include Kuraishi, Kureha, and Shinogi Kureha's brother Kosho. Establishes Baki's motivation, Hanma lineage, and underground arena as central stage; sets up father-son conflict that drives entire franchise.

Maximum Tournament SagaGrappler Baki Vol.15-27 (1993-1995)

1993-1995

Tokugawa organizes the Maximum Tournament, gathering 32 fighters from all styles to determine the strongest. Baki, now 17, enters and defeats Zulu, Chiharu Shiba, Roland Istaz, Mount Toba, Kengo Misaki, and finally Jack Hanma (his half-brother) in the semi-final, then defeats Kureha and others to face his rival. He wins the tournament, becoming Champion, but the final is interrupted by Yujiro's presence. The arc showcases every major style and Baki's synthesis. Baki becomes recognized as strongest teenager; introduces Jack Hanma rivalry; cements ensemble cast. Tournament winner title 'Champion' sticks to Baki thereafter.

Death Row Inmates Arc (Most Evil Death Row Convicts)New Grappler Baki / Son of Ogre Vol.1-10 (1999-2002)

1999-2002

Five death-row inmates from around the world (Dorrian, Doyle, Sikorsky, Yanagi, Spec), bored with their invincibility in prison, escape simultaneously to taste defeat in Tokyo. They attack fighters, and Baki must confront each. Baki vs Spec (who destroys the Statue of Liberty's arm in a feat of strength), vs Yanagi (poison hand), vs Sikorsky (high-flying escapes), vs Doyle (who later becomes an ally), and vs Dorrian. Baki is poisoned and nearly dies, saved by Dr. Kureha, and learns to fight without rules. Baki masters fighting dirty opponents and recovers from poison; Doyle's redemption begins; introduces theme that true strength is not institutional. Netflix Season 1 adapts this arc as its opener.

Accounts II
Great Chinese Challenge / Raitai TournamentNew Grappler Baki Vol.12-18 (2002-2004)

2002-2004

Every 100 years, Chinese martial arts hold the Raitai Tournament to decide the strongest Kaiou. American and Japanese fighters are invited. Baki, now 18, enters with Doppo, Katsumi, Retsu Kaioh, and others. He defeats Yoh Kaioh and faces Mohammad Alai Jr. (son of Muhammad Ali) in a parallel storyline about legacy. The tournament exposes Baki to Chinese kenpo's 4000-year history and Kaioh philosophy, culminating in Baki vs Yujiro interrupting the finals. Baki earns recognition from Kaku Kaioh (400-year-old master); Retsu becomes close ally; Alai Jr. arc critiques inherited fame vs earned strength. Leads directly to father-son confrontation.

Son of Ogre: Baki vs Yujiro — The Father-Son WarHanma Baki Vol.1-7 climax (2005-2006, final battle Vol.37)

2005-2006

After years of preparation, Baki finally faces Yujiro in a sanctioned duel in the underground arena, witnessed by Tokugawa and all major fighters. The fight spans multiple chapters, with both unleashing demon faces, demon brain, and ultimate techniques. Yujiro dominates early, breaking Baki's bones, but Baki's endurance and synthesis allow him to land true blows, including a cockroach tackle and imagined techniques. The fight ends not with death but with Baki earning Yujiro's acknowledgment: Yujiro declares Baki has become a worthy opponent and shares a final meal where Baki makes Yujiro admit the food is delicious — a symbolic victory. Resolves 15-year narrative arc; Baki is not stronger than Yujiro but is recognized; redefines their relationship from abuse to rivalry. Subsequent series must find new stakes beyond this climax.

Pickle Arc and the Jurassic FighterHanma Baki Vol.8-20 (2006-2009)

2006-2009

A perfectly preserved Jurassic-era human (Pickle) is found in a salt mine, revived, and brought to Tokyo. Standing 2m, with dinosaur-level strength and predatory instinct, Pickle defeats Katsumi, Retsu, and Jack before Baki volunteers to fight him barehanded. Baki uses imaging training — imagining himself as a dinosaur — to match Pickle's primal movement, ultimately earning Pickle's respect. The arc explores what 'strength' means before martial arts existed. Baki proves he can fight a non-human predator; Pickle becomes recurring character; leads to Pickle vs Musashi later. Popular arc for showcasing Itagaki's paleo-fantasy.

Baki-Dou: Resurrected Miyamoto MusashiBaki-Dou (2014-2018), 22 volumes

2014-2018

Mitsunari Tokugawa's scientists clone Miyamoto Musashi from his preserved soul/spirit and physical remains, resurrecting Japan's greatest swordsman in modern Tokyo without a sword. Musashi, wielding only his bare hands but with sword-master's cutting imagination, defeats nearly every fighter — Doppo, Retsu (who dies from injuries), Hanayama, even Baki initially. Baki must learn to counter a man who can 'cut' with air. The arc ends with Musashi's spirit being put to rest after experiencing modern martial arts and Baki's growth. Death of Retsu Kaioh (major fan shock); Baki learns to fight conceptual cutting; concludes Musashi storyline. Controversial for supernatural cloning premise.

Baki is an anime about a high school delinquent who learns karate to beat bullies. — Baki is not a delinquent-reform story; he is a lifelong prodigy raised to fight his father, fighting in a secret underground arena with professional killers, death-row inmates, and prehistoric revivals. School is minimal.

Baki's demon face is a tattoo or supernatural curse. — It is not a tattoo or curse; it is Itagaki's visual metaphor for extreme back muscle contraction forming an ogre-like face, inherited via Hanma blood. Characters comment on it as muscle, not ink.

Baki (Naruto) and Baki Hanma are the same character or same universe. — They are entirely separate IPs by different authors/publishers (Itagaki/Akita Shoten vs Kishimoto/Shueisha), different designs, ages, and worlds. No crossover exists except fan art.

Baki is based on a true story or real fighter named Baki Hanma. — Baki Hanma is fictional, though Itagaki bases techniques on real martial arts (karate, boxing, wrestling, etc.) and cameos real fighters like Antonio Inoki, Roberto Miura, and references Muhammad Ali via Mohammad Alai. No real 'Hanma' family exists.

Baku (Azerbaijan) and Baki are the same word, so the character is named after the city. — Azerbaijani Bakı (Baku) is a Turkic toponym of disputed etymology (possibly 'wind-pounded' or Persian bagh), while Japanese Baki (刃牙, blade-fang) is Japanese kanji wordplay. No etymological link; homophony is coincidental across languages.
