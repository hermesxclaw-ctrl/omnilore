Who It Is

In the windswept steppes of Mongolia, where the sky meets the earth in an unbroken line, the Luus—serpentine dragons of water and earth—rule the unseen currents beneath the land. They are the guardians of springs, rivers, and mountains, capricious spirits whose favor can bring abundance or ruin. To the herders who share their domain, the Luus are not distant deities but immediate presences, acknowledged in daily rituals and offerings. Their world is one of shifting alliances, where a single misstep can turn a life-giving stream into a source of sickness and misfortune.

Luus are capricious and easily offended. They are protectors of their territory, and any pollution of water or disrespect to sacred places can provoke their wrath. They are believed to control weather, especially rain, and can cause droughts or floods. They are also associated with fertility and wealth, and can grant blessings to those who honor them.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The term 'Luus' derives from the Tibetan 'klu' (gLu), which itself comes from the Sanskrit 'nāga'. In Mongolian, it refers to a class of serpentine spirits associated with water and underground realms. The spelling varies: 'Luus' is common in academic transliteration, while 'Lus' appears in some sou

Pronunciation — Approximately 'loos' (rhymes with 'moose'), with a long 'oo' sound.

Aliases — Lus, Luus (Mongolian: Луус), Lus (Buryat: Лус), Lusyn, Luusyn

Ownership — Mongolian, Buryat, and other Central Asian cultures influenced by Tibetan Buddhism. The Luus are integrated into Mongolian shamanic and Buddhist practices.

Powers
Weather Control

Luus can cause rain, hail, storms, and droughts. They are often invoked in rituals for rain.

Limit: Their power is limited to their domain; they cannot affect areas outside their jurisdiction.

Disease and Healing

Luus can inflict diseases, especially skin and internal ailments, when offended. They can also cure if appeased.

Limit: Their healing often requires ritual appeasement.

Shape-shifting

Luus can take human form, often to interact with humans, sometimes seducing or testing them.

Limit: They may be bound by rules, such as not being able to hide their true nature from certain people.

Bestowing Wealth and Fertility

Luus can grant prosperity, children, and good fortune to those who respect them.

Limit: Requires proper offerings and behavior.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Luus are typically described as serpentine dragons, often with a snake-like body, sometimes with multiple heads or horns. They are associated with water, so they may appear as snakes, fish, or amphibious creatures. In some accounts, they can take human form, often as beautiful or majestic beings. They are said to be white or blue in color, symbolizing water and sky.

Symbols — In Mongolian and Tibetan art, Luus are depicted as coiled serpents or dragons, often beneath bodies of water or in the earth. They are associated with the color white and blue, and with jewels (nāga pearls) that symbolize wealth and wisdom. Ritual objects include offerings of milk, tea, and incense.

Domains — Luus dwell in rivers, lakes, springs, wells, and underground watercourses. They also inhabit mountains and caves, particularly those with water sources. In Mongolian cosmology, they are part of the lower world, but their influence reaches the surface.

Accounts I
The Offended LuusMongolian folklore, collected in various publications

Unknown, likely pre-Buddhist and continued

A herder pollutes a sacred spring by washing dirty items in it. The Luus of the spring becomes angry and curses the herder's family with disease and loss of livestock. A shaman is called to perform a ritual to appease the Luus, offering milk and incense, and the family recovers. The herder learns to respect the spring and makes regular offerings.

The Luus and the HeroMongolian epic tales

Medieval to modern

A hero seeking to marry a princess must obtain a jewel from the underwater palace of the Luus. The hero dives into a lake and negotiates with the Luus king, performing a task to win the jewel. The Luus are portrayed as powerful but honorable. The hero gains the jewel and marries the princess, and the Luus become allies.
