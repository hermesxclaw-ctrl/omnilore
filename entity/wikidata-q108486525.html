Who It Is

You do not find the Great Fairy — she finds you, laughing behind a cracked wall or a flower that suddenly blooms too large, too bright. In Hyrule, when you are at your most battered and empty-pouched, a vine-covered fountain shudders open and a woman the size of a house unfolds herself in a shower of light, ivy, and wind — beautiful, overwhelming, faintly terrifying, and utterly delighted to see you.

Behavior is consistently benevolent, playful, maternal, and flamboyant. She greets Link with booming laughter, surprise, and affection ('Welcome!' / 'Oh, what a cute boy!'), often commenting on his courage or tiredness, then offers healing, blessing, or upgrade. In Ocarina/Majora she is exuberant and slightly teasing; in Wind Waker childlike and giggly; in Breath of the Wild each sister has distinct personality - Cotera warm and welcoming, Kaysa bold and musical, Mija shy and sleepy, Tera brash and desert-tough - but all are theatrical, affectionate, and physically effus

Powers
Healing and Restoration

Instantly restores Link's hearts and, in some games, magic meter. Common fairies heal on contact; Great Fairy heals fully upon visiting fountain, often with heart-refill animation and music.

Limit: Requires visiting fountain; does not grant extra hearts beyond maximum; in Breath of the Wild healing is via fairies held in inventory, not Great Fairy direct heal.

Magic and Ability Bestowal (Spells)

Grants magical spells and techniques: Din's Fire, Farore's Wind, Nayru's Love (Ocarina), Magic Meter upgrades, Enhanced Spin Attack, Double Magic, Double Defense. In Wind Waker grants Fire & Ice Arrows via Fairy Queen.

Limit: Requires specific fountain visit; spells consume magic meter; some require prior items (e.g., Magic Meter).

Equipment Enhancement and Upgrading

Upgrades carrying capacity (wallet, bomb bag, quiver, Rupee purses) and, in Breath of the Wild/Tears of the Kingdom, enhances armor defense and set bonuses up to 4 stars using monster materials. Also upgrades sword/shield in A Link to the Past (throwing into Mysterious Pond).

Limit: Requires materials and rupees; tiered by which Great Fairy is revived (Cotera tier 1, Tera tier 4). Cannot enhance unowned or non-upgradable armor. In Tears of the Kingdom requires stable trotter quest completion to awaken each sister.

Resurrection and Fairy Summoning

Can revive Link from death via a held fairy companion, and can summon swarms of small fairies. Great Fairy herself can be revived from dormancy/bud state via rupee offering or Stray Fairy collection.

Limit: Revival of Great Fairy requires external quest; Link's auto-revival requires having a fairy in bottle/inventory, not direct Great Fairy intervention in combat.

Flight, Levitation, and Teleportation

Hovers, floats, emerges from flower bud in beam of light, disappears in petals. Can teleport Link out of fountain or summon him. Wings imply flight though rarely shown traveling.

Limit: Never shown to leave fountain area for extended travel; bound to spring.

Blessing and Enchantment (Great Fairy Tears / Fountain Water)

Produces Great Fairy's Tears (Twilight Princess) that fully heal and temporarily boost attack, and water that can be bottled. In Skyward Sword, Great Fairy in Skyloft grants pouch upgrades.

Limit: Tears are single-use bottled item; limited quantity.

Accounts I
The Six Fountains of Ocarina of TimeThe Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (Nintendo EAD, 1998)

1998 (in-universe: Era of the Hero of Time)

As child and adult Link, the player discovers hidden Great Fairy Fountains across Hyrule - atop Death Mountain Trail, in Death Mountain Crater, near Hyrule Castle, and in the Desert Colossus. Each Great Fairy, after a laughing emergence, rewards courage with magic: the Great Fairy of Magic on Death Mountain Trail grants Din's Fire; the Great Fairy of Power in the Crater grants Din's Fire upgrade and magic meter expansion; the Great Fairy of Wisdom in the Desert Colossus grants Nayru's Love, etc. The fountains are optional but essential for 100% completion. The Great Fairy also teaches the Spin Attack enhancement. Link gains essential spells and magic upgrades needed to traverse dungeons; establishes Great Fairy as recurring upgrade-giver archetype for 3D Zelda.

The Shattered Great Fairy of Clock Town (Majora's Mask)The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask (Nintendo EAD, 2000)

2000 (in-universe: parallel world Termina, three-day cycle)

The Skull Kid, possessed by Majora's Mask, shatters the Great Fairy of Magic in Clock Town into numerous Stray Fairies scattered across Clock Town and Woodfall. Link must collect the 15 Stray Fairies in Woodfall and return them to the fountain to reassemble her. Restored, she grants Link the ability to shoot bubbles (as Deku) and later, in Snowhead, Great Bay, and Ikana, other Great Fairies grant Double Magic, Double Defense, and the Great Fairy's Sword. The story emphasizes vulnerability - even a Great Fairy can be broken by Majora's power. Introduces collectathon mechanic tied to Great Fairy restoration; rewards include the most powerful sword in the game (Great Fairy's Sword) and defensive upgrades critical for the final cycle.

The Seven Fountains and the Fairy Queen (Wind Waker)The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker (Nintendo EAD, 2002)

2002 (in-universe: Era of the Great Sea, Adult Timeline)

Across the Great Sea, Link finds Great Fairy Fountains on islands (Northern Fairy Island, Eastern Fairy Island, etc.). Each Great Fairy, now in chibi doll form, upgrades Link's wallet, bomb bag, or quiver. At Mother & Child Isles, the Fairy Queen - a small childlike ruler of all fairies - awaits. She tests Link's compassion and grants Fire & Ice Arrows, essential for the Earth and Wind Temples. The Great Fairies are cheerful and giggly, contrasting with the flooded world's melancholy. Upgrades enable progression (larger wallets to afford Triforce charts); Fairy Queen's arrows required for dungeon puzzles.

Accounts II
The Four Sisters and the Stable Trotters (Breath of the Wild)The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild (Nintendo EPD, 2017)

2017 (in-universe: Era of the Wilds)

Four Great Fairy sisters - Cotera near Kakariko, Kaysa in Tabantha, Mija in Akkala, and Tera in Gerudo Desert - have hidden themselves inside giant flower buds after Calamity Ganon's rise and the people's fear. Each requires a rupee offering to bloom: 100, 500, 1000, and 10000 rupees respectively. Once awakened, they offer to enhance Link's armor using monster parts, increasing defense and unlocking set bonuses. Their revival is gated by economy and exploration, and they interact with each other via dialogue (sisters calling each other). Armor enhancement system central to late-game survival; rupee sink and material hunt drive exploration; establishes named, distinct personalities for Great Fairies for first time.

The Music to Wake the Flowers (Tears of the Kingdom)The Legend of Zelda: Tears of the Kingdom (Nintendo EPD, 2023)

2023 (in-universe: shortly after Breath of the Wild)

The Great Fairies have again withdrawn, their flower buds closed because the people have stopped playing music. The Stable Trotters - a traveling band led by Mastro with members Beedle? Actually Violynne (violin), Pyper, Eustus, Mastro - seek to serenade each Great Fairy. Link must reunite the band and transport them via carriage, then perform before each bud (e.g., Cotera hears Violynne's violin near Woodland Stable). The Great Fairy bursts forth in a musical, petal-filled performance and resumes armor enhancements, now up to 4 stars with new materials like Gleeok parts. Recontextualizes Great Fairies as lovers of music and performance; ties upgrade system to side-quest narrative; adds new armor sets (Depths, Zonaite) to enhance.

Venus, Queen of Fairies and the Mysterious PondThe Legend of Zelda: A Link to the Past (Nintendo EAD, 1991) and A Link Between Worlds (2013)

1991-2013 (in-universe: Era of Light and Dark)

At the Waterfall of Wishing and later the Mysterious Pond, Link meets Venus, Queen of Fairies (sometimes localized as Fairy Queen or Super Fairy). By throwing items (Boomerang, Shield, etc.) into the pond, Venus upgrades them (e.g., Magical Boomerang, Red Shield) or, if Link throws in rupees, increases bomb/arrow capacity. In A Link Between Worlds, the Great Fairy at the Fairy Fountain similarly upgrades the Master Sword. This is the earliest codified 'Great Fairy as upgrader' story. Establishes fountain-offering mechanic that persists for 30+ years; template for all later Great Fairy upgrades.

The Great Fairy is Navi or Navi's evolved form. — Navi is a small guardian fairy assigned to Link in Ocarina of Time; Great Fairy is a giant, distinct species/individual who grants magic. They coexist in same game and have different models, voices, and roles. Navi never becomes a Great Fairy.

You must pay the Great Fairy in every Zelda game. — Rupee payment (100-10000) is unique to Breath of the Wild's revival mechanic to gate armor upgrades. In Ocarina, Majora, Wind Waker, Twilight Princess, etc., Great Fairy rewards are free upon discovery (or require Stray Fairy collection, not rupees). Tears of the Kingdom replaces rupees with music quest.

The Great Fairy is always nude and sexualized. — Only Ocarina/Majora and Breath of the Wild/Tears of the Kingdom use the nude-with-hair/vines design. Wind Waker, Minish Cap, Twilight Princess, Skyward Sword, and 2D games depict her fully clothed in dresses or armor-like attire. Design varies dramatically by art style and rating.

Malanya the Horse God is the fifth Great Fairy. — Malanya is classified in-game and in Creating a Champion as Horse God (Uma Shin), not a fairy. He shares fountain mechanics but has distinct lore (revives horses), male presentation, and different flower. Nintendo lists him separately.
