Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Chasse-galerie' is derived from the French words 'chasse', meaning 'hunt', and 'galerie', meaning 'gallery' or 'canoe'.

Pronunciation — The pronunciation of 'Chasse-galerie' is /ʃɑs ɡalɛʁi/

Aliases — The Bewitched Canoe, The Flying Canoe, La Chasse-galerie

Ownership — The Chasse-galerie is a cultural artifact of French-Canadian folklore, originating from the traditions of voyageurs and coureurs des bois.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Chasse-galerie is a canoe that is said to be able to fly through the air, often accompanied by a group of ghostly hunters.

Symbols — The Chasse-galerie is often associated with the Wild Hunt, a mythological procession of ghostly hunters and their dogs.

Domains — The Chasse-galerie is said to inhabit the forests and rivers of Quebec, particularly around the Gatineau River.
