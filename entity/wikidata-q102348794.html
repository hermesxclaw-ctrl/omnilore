The Approach

When the Locust Horde erupted from the ground and humanity's cities turned to slaughterhouses, the last thing the COG needed was a celebrity. But Augustus Cole, the fastest man in the history of Thrashball, traded his jersey for armor and brought something the war effort never expected: a roar. He didn't just fight the war—he narrated it, hyped it, and turned survival into a spectacle. This is the story of the Cole Train, the athlete who became a legend, and the complicated legacy of a character who carried a culture's hopes and a culture's stereotypes on his shoulders.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of war, violence, and gore consistent with the Gears of War franchise. Discusses racial stereotyping and a legal dispute over publicity rights. May include strong language.
Identity

Augustus Cole

Pronunciation AW-gus-tus KOHL (traditionally); 'Cole Train' pronounced as it appears.

Known As

Cole TrainColeThe Cole Train

Etymology

The name 'Augustus' is of Latin origin meaning 'majestic' or 'venerable,' fitting a character of heroic stature. The nickname 'Cole Train' is a play on his surname and his dynamic, unstoppable nature, evoking a train's power and momentum.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Epic Games for the Gears of War franchise; the character is owned by Epic Games and later The Coalition (Microsoft Studios).

Disputed Identifications

Some fans and critics have drawn parallels between Cole and real-life athletes, particularly Lenwood Hamilton, a former NFL player who sued over alleged likeness. However, the character is a distinct fictional creation, and any resemblance is disputed.

Description

Appearance

Augustus Cole is a tall, muscular African-American man with a shaved head and a distinctive goatee. He wears the standard COG armor, often with a customized, more colorful design, and is frequently seen with a helmet. His physique reflects his athletic background, and he carries himself with a confident, energetic swagger.

Symbols & Iconography

Cole is often associated with the number 83, his jersey number from his Thrashball days, which appears on his armor and gear. The nickname 'Cole Train' is also a symbol of his identity, and he is known for his catchphrase 'Woo!', which has become iconic among fans.

Behaviour & Personality

Cole is characterized by his exuberant, optimistic, and boisterous personality. He is a natural motivator, often boosting morale with his enthusiasm and humor. He is fiercely loyal to his friends, especially Damon Baird, and is known for his brash, sometimes reckless, courage in battle. Unlike his more cynical comrades, Cole maintains a positive outlook, using his charisma to lighten the mood even in dire situations.

Domains & Habitats

Cole is primarily found in the war-torn environments of Sera, including ruined cities, underground tunnels, and military bases. He is a soldier of the COG, so his habitat is wherever the war takes him.

Version Differences

Across the games, Cole's personality remains consistent, but his role evolves. In the original trilogy, he is a key supporting character. In Gears of War 4 and Gears 5, he appears as an older, retired soldier and a mentor figure. His appearance also changes to reflect his age and the passage of time.

Racial stereotyping

Source Various critical articles and academic papers

Description

Some critics have accused Cole of being a racial stereotype, pointing to his portrayal as a loud, brash, and physically imposing Black character. This criticism has been discussed in academic and gaming media.

Scholarly Context

The debate centers on whether Cole's characterization reinforces negative stereotypes or subverts them through his competence and heroism. Some argue that his positive traits outweigh the stereotypical elements, while others see the stereotype as problematic.

Publicity rights lawsuit

Source Court documents

Description

Former NFL player Lenwood Hamilton sued Epic Games, claiming that Cole's character used his likeness and persona without permission. The lawsuit was dismissed, with the court finding no substantial similarity.

Scholarly Context

The case is an example of celebrity publicity rights in the context of video game characters. The court's ruling set a precedent that fictional characters inspired by but not directly based on real people do not violate publicity rights.

Contradictions & Disputes

Racial stereotype

Position A

Cole is a positive representation because he is a heroic, competent, and beloved character who breaks the mold of the tragic Black character.

Position A — evidence

Fans and some critics point to his loyalty, bravery, and leadership as evidence of a well-rounded character.

Position B

Cole is a negative stereotype because his loud, athletic, and uneducated persona aligns with harmful tropes about Black men.

Position B — evidence

Critics cite his use of slang, his focus on physical prowess, and his lack of intellectual depth compared to other characters.

Scholarly Consensus

There is no consensus; the debate reflects broader discussions about representation in media.

Legal dispute

Position A

Epic Games created Cole as an original character, and any resemblance to Lenwood Hamilton is coincidental.

Position A — evidence

Epic Games argued that Cole is a fictional character and that Hamilton's claims were unfounded.

Position B

Hamilton claimed that Cole's appearance and persona were modeled after him, infringing on his publicity rights.

Position B — evidence

Hamilton cited similarities in physical appearance and athletic background.

Scholarly Consensus

The court ruled in favor of Epic Games, finding no substantial similarity.
