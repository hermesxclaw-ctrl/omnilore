Who It Is

Tāwhiri-mātea is the Māori god of winds, storms, and weather — one of the children of Rangi-nui (Sky Father) and Papa-tū-ā-nuku (Earth Mother). He is unique among the separating children of Rangi and Papa: when Tāne forced his parents apart, Tāwhiri sided with his father Rangi. Enraged by the separation and by his brothers' betrayal, Tāwhiri unleashed the fury of the winds against them. He created the great storms, hurricanes, squalls, and the driving rain to attack his brothers. He pursued Tāne (god of forests) with winds that felled giant trees, chased Tangaroa (god of the sea) into the depths with towering waves, and pursued Rongo and Haumia until they hid in their mother Papa's body. Only Tūmatauenga (war god) withstood Tāwhiri's assaults. Tāwhiri is the father of winds, each with a specific direction and aspect: the north wind, south wind, east wind, west wind, and the whirlwind. After his defeat of the other brothers (except Tū), Tāwhiri retreated to be with his father in the sky, where he occasionally unleashes storms to remind his brothers of his power. Maori navigators and farmers watched Tāwhiri carefully to predict weather.

Origins

Tāwhiri is the son of Rangi and Papa. He alone among the brothers opposed the separation of his parents. He joined his father in the sky and created all the winds to attack his brothers. He will never be fully pacified.
