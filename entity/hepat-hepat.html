Who It Is

Of Hurrian origin, Hepat rose to become the chief goddess of the Hurro-Hittite pantheon, consort of Teshub (the Hurrian storm-god assimilated to Tarhunt). In the Yazılıkaya sanctuary (13th c. BCE), she is shown standing on a panther — a position of supreme prominence among the goddesses, second only to the Sun-Goddess of Arinna. The Hurrian Kumarbi cycle, which deeply influenced Hittite mythology, culminates in Teshub's kingship with Hepat as his queen. Over time, especially in the Empire period, Hepat was so thoroughly identified with the Sun-Goddess of Arinna that the two became inseparable: the queen of heaven whose authority underwrites the royal dynasty.
