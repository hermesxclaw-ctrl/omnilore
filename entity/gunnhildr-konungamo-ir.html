Who It Is

Did you know that Gunnhildr, the 'Mother of Kings', was not only a queen but also a powerful witch who could shape-shift into a bird and curse her enemies from afar? Her story is a fascinating blend of history, legend, and magic, and it challenges our modern notions of gender and power in the Viking Age.

Gunnhildr is portrayed as ambitious, cunning, and ruthless. She is a master manipulator who uses her intelligence, beauty, and magical powers to influence events and eliminate her enemies. She is fiercely protective of her sons, particularly Eric Bloodaxe, and will go to great lengths to secure their power. She is also vengeful and unforgiving, as seen in her conflicts with Egil Skallagrimsson, whom she curses and plots against. Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Gunnhildr is Old Norse, composed of 'gunnr' (battle) and 'hildr' (battle), effectively meaning 'battle-battle' or 'war-war', a common dithematic name in the Norse tradition. The epithet 'konungamóðir' translates to 'mother of kings', reflecting her role as the mother of several Norse kings.

Pronunciation — GUN-hild-er KOH-noong-ah-MOH-theer (approximate Old Norse: /ˈɡunːˌhildr ˈkonuŋɡaˌmoːðir/)

Powers
Seiðr (Norse magic)

Gunnhildr is depicted as a practitioner of seiðr, a form of Norse magic associated with divination and manipulation of fate. She uses this power to curse her enemies, foresee the future, and influence events. In Egils saga, she casts a curse on Egil, causing him to be restless and uneasy until they meet again. She is also said to be able to shape-shift, often taking the form of a bird, to spy on others or travel great distances.

Limit:

Bewitching beauty

Her physical beauty is described as a form of enchantment, capable of bewitching men and bending them to her will. This is seen in her seduction of Eric Bloodaxe, whom she charms into marrying her, and in her ability to manipulate other men in the sagas.

Limit:

Political manipulation

While not a supernatural power, her political acumen is portrayed as a formidable ability. She orchestrates alliances, plots assassinations, and manages her sons' careers with strategic skill, often outmaneuvering male rivals.

Accounts I
The Wooing of Eric BloodaxeHeimskringla, Fagrskinna

10th century (as depicted in 13th-century sagas)

Gunnhildr, daughter of Ozur Toti, is sent to learn magic from two Finnish wizards. She discovers they plan to sacrifice her, so she outwits them and escapes, taking their magic with her. She then encounters Eric Bloodaxe, who is on a Viking expedition, and they fall in love. Despite her humble origins, she becomes his wife and queen, and she uses her cunning to help him gain the throne of Norway. Gunnhildr becomes queen of Norway, but her rise to power is marked by intrigue and bloodshed. She is blamed for Eric's tyranny and his eventual downfall, leading to their exile.

Accounts II
The Curse on Egil SkallagrimssonEgils saga

10th century (as depicted in 13th-century saga)

Gunnhildr takes an immediate dislike to Egil Skallagrimsson, a famous warrior and poet, when he visits the court of Eric Bloodaxe. She accuses him of causing trouble and persuades Eric to have him executed. Egil escapes, but Gunnhildr uses her magic to curse him, causing him to feel restless and uneasy until they meet again. Later, when Egil is captured, she demands his death, but he composes a poem in Eric's honor and is freed. Gunnhildr continues to harbor a grudge and later sends a witch to kill Egil, but he kills the witch instead. The feud between Gunnhildr and Egil becomes legendary, and Egil's poem 'Höfuðlausn' (Head-Ransom) is composed to save his life. Gunnhildr's curse is said to have caused Egil's unease for years, and her enmity is a recurring theme in the saga.

The Downfall of Eric BloodaxeHeimskringla, Fagrskinna

10th century (as depicted in 13th-century sagas)

After Eric Bloodaxe becomes king of Norway, Gunnhildr is blamed for his harsh rule and his conflicts with other chieftains. She is said to have encouraged his tyranny and to have used her magic to eliminate his rivals. Eventually, Eric is overthrown by his brother Hakon the Good, and he and Gunnhildr are forced into exile. They flee to Orkney and then to England, where Eric is given Northumbria to rule. Gunnhildr continues to wield influence, but Eric is killed in battle, leaving her to raise their sons alone. Eric's death marks the end of his rule, but Gunnhildr remains a powerful figure, working to place her sons on thrones in Norway and Orkney. Her ambition leads to further conflicts, and she is eventually driven out of Norway for good.
