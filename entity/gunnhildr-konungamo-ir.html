Who It Is
Form

The sagas do not provide a detailed physical description of Gunnhildr, but she is consistently portrayed as a formidable and imposing woman. In Egils saga, she is described as 'fair of face' but with a 'sharp' and 'cruel' look, suggesting a beauty that is both alluring and dangerous. Her appearance is often linked to her magical abilities; she is said to have a 'witch's glance' that can bewitch or intimidate. In later sagas, she is depicted as regal and commanding, wearing fine clothing and jewelry befitting a queen, but her eyes are often described as 'snake-like' or 'piercing', reflecting her cunning and malevolent nature. This combination of beauty and menace makes her a memorable figure in the sagas.

Powers

Seiðr (Norse magic) Gunnhildr is depicted as a practitioner of seiðr, a form of Norse magic associated with divination and manipulation of fate. She uses this power to curse her enemies, foresee the future, and influence events. In Egils saga, she casts a curse on Egil, causing him to be restless and uneasy until they meet again. She is also said to be able to shape-shift, often taking the form of a bird, to spy on others or travel great distances.

Bewitching beauty Her physical beauty is described as a form of enchantment, capable of bewitching men and bending them to her will. This is seen in her seduction of Eric Bloodaxe, whom she charms into marrying her, and in her ability to manipulate other men in the sagas.

Political manipulation While not a supernatural power, her political acumen is portrayed as a formidable ability. She orchestrates alliances, plots assassinations, and manages her sons' careers with strategic skill, often outmaneuvering male rivals.

Accounts

Gunnhildr, daughter of Ozur Toti, is sent to learn magic from two Finnish wizards. She discovers they plan to sacrifice her, so she outwits them and escapes, taking their magic with her. She then encounters Eric Bloodaxe, who is on a Viking expedition, and they fall in love. Despite her humble origins, she becomes his wife and queen, and she uses her cunning to help him gain the throne of Norway.

Gunnhildr takes an immediate dislike to Egil Skallagrimsson, a famous warrior and poet, when he visits the court of Eric Bloodaxe. She accuses him of causing trouble and persuades Eric to have him executed. Egil escapes, but Gunnhildr uses her magic to curse him, causing him to feel restless and uneasy until they meet again. Later, when Egil is captured, she demands his death, but he composes a poem in Eric's honor and is freed. Gunnhildr continues to harbor a grudge and later sends a witch to kill Egil, but he kills the witch instead.

After Eric Bloodaxe becomes king of Norway, Gunnhildr is blamed for his harsh rule and his conflicts with other chieftains. She is said to have encouraged his tyranny and to have used her magic to eliminate his rivals. Eventually, Eric is overthrown by his brother Hakon the Good, and he and Gunnhildr are forced into exile. They flee to Orkney and then to England, where Eric is given Northumbria to rule. Gunnhildr continues to wield influence, but Eric is killed in battle, leaving her to raise their sons alone.

Scholarly Debates
Parentage
Gunnhildr is the daughter of Gorm the Old, King of Denmark, as stated in Heimskringla and Fagrskinna. ⇌ Gunnhildr is the daughter of Ozur Toti, a Norwegian chieftain, as stated in Egils saga and other sources.
Historical existence
Gunnhildr is a historical figure who lived in the 10th century and was married to Eric Bloodaxe. ⇌ Gunnhildr is a literary invention, a composite of several women, created by saga authors to serve narrative purposes.
Role in Eric's downfall
Gunnhildr was a loyal wife who supported Eric and was devastated by his death. ⇌ Gunnhildr was a manipulative witch who contributed to Eric's tyranny and downfall through her magic and scheming.
What We Don't Know

There is no archaeological or contemporary historical evidence for Gunnhildr's existence. All knowledge comes from sagas written centuries after her supposed lifetime. The exact nature of her magical abilities and the extent of her political influence are debated. The relationship between Gunnhildr and Eric Bloodaxe is also unclear, with some sources suggesting they were not married but merely lovers. Further research into the historical context of 10th-century Norway and the literary conventions of saga writing may shed light on these gaps.

A way to hold it in your mind

Did you know that Gunnhildr, the 'Mother of Kings', was not only a queen but also a powerful witch who could shape-shift into a bird and curse her enemies from afar? Her story is a fascinating blend of history, legend, and magic, and it challenges our modern notions of gender and power in the Viking Age.
