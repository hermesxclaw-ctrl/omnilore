Who It Is

In the shadow of the great storm god and the radiant sun goddess, there is a whisper of a divine daughter — a figure so elusive she almost vanishes from history. Allanzu, the forgotten child of Ḫepat, stands as a testament to the vastness of the Hurrian pantheon, where even a goddess's presence is a fleeting echo in a ritual text.

Allanzu is a peripheral figure in the texts; she has no known independent myths or actions. She appears almost exclusively in association with her mother, Ḫepat, and her siblings (including Šarruma, the mountain god, and another daughter). She is a 'divine daughter' archetype — a presence in the divine family rather than an active protagonist. Her role is likely that of a tutelary or protective goddess, but this is conjecture. Accounts I
The Song of Ulikummi (Appearance)Hurrian-Hittite myth, part of the Kumarbi cycle

c. 14th-13th century BCE

In this myth, the storm god Tarḫunna (Teššub) is challenged by the stone monster Ulikummi. In a desperate moment, Tarḫunna goes to Ḫepat for advice. She is described as being in a state of distress, and her children, including Allanzu, are present. Allanzu does not speak or act; her role is purely to be part of the divine family scene. None for Allanzu directly; the myth continues with Tarḫunna's eventual victory.

Accounts II
Ritual InvocationsKizzuwatnean ritual texts

c. 15th-13th century BCE

Allanzu is invoked in a variety of rituals, often alongside her mother Ḫepat and other deities. These rituals are primarily for purification, healing, or protection. She is listed among the deities who are called upon to witness or bless the ritual. The rituals are meant to secure divine favor and avert misfortune.

The Divine Family at YazılıkayaArchaeological interpretation of the Yazılıkaya rock sanctuary; Hittite ritual texts

c. 13th century BCE (reign of Tudḫaliya IV)

The rock sanctuary at Yazılıkaya, located two kilometers northeast of the Hittite capital Ḫattuša, contains two rock-cut chambers decorated with reliefs of nearly one hundred deities. Chamber A depicts a grand procession of gods and goddesses converging toward a central meeting scene between the storm-god Teššub and the sun-goddess Ḫepat. Among the goddesses following Ḫepat, a youthful female figure has been tentatively identified as Allanzu. If this identification is correct, it represents one of the only known visual depictions of this elusive deity and situates her cult firmly within the imperial religious program of the late Hittite Empire. The sanctuary was likely used for the celebration of the New Year festival and for royal ancestor veneration, connecting Allanzu to both cosmic renewal and dynastic legitimacy. The Yazılıkaya sanctuary became the most important surviving monument of Hittite-Hurrian religion and provides the most tangible archaeological context for Allanzu's worship, even if her specific identification remains debated.
