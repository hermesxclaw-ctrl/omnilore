Who It Is

Durlin is a name that barely surfaces — a Dwarf-lord glimpsed in the margins of Tolkien's early legendarium, where heroic-legend meets editorial history. He has no chapter of his own, no famous deed in The Hobbit or The Lord of the Rings, and no Wikipedia page to anchor him, yet Wikidata remembers him as a heroic-legend fictional character. That very thinness is the story: how a name persists in databases while its lore remains almost untraceable.

No dialogue or personality traits attested for Durlin. Generic Tolkien Dwarf cultural traits (secretive, loyal to kin, master-craftsmen, gold-loving, grudging, brave in battle) are widely described but cannot be assigned to Durlin individually without source. Unknown.

Dwarven Craftsmanship (reconstructed, not Durlin-specific)

In Tolkien's legendarium Dwarves are unmatched smiths and masons, taught by Aulë. They forge weapons, mail, and jewels that resist time and enchantment.

The Fall of Gondolin (early version) — context for lost dwarf-namesJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Book of Lost Tales Part II, 'The Fall of Gondolin' (c.1917, publ. 1984)

First Age narrative, written 1916-1920

In the earliest Fall of Gondolin, Dwarves are not central, but later editorial history shows Tolkien experimented with many dwarf-names for the hosts of Nogrod/Belegost. Durlin, if authentic, would belong to this unstable onomastic layer where names were added and discarded. No verified passage shows Durlin acting in the sack or defence of Gondolin. Demonstrates why a name like Durlin could appear once and vanish — Tolkien revised dwarf roster repeatedly before The Hobbit (1937) fixed the canon.

The Nauglafring / Of the Ruin of Doriath — The Necklace of the DwarvesJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Book of Lost Tales Part II 'The Nauglafring' and The Silmarillion ch. 22 'Of the Ruin of Doriath'

First Age, written 1919-1930s, published 1977/1984

Dwarves of Nogrod, hired to set the Silmaril in the Nauglamír, murder King Thingol and sack Doriath, then are destroyed by Beren and the Ents. This is the central heroic-tragic dwarf narrative of the First Age. Any dwarf-lord named Durlin would logically be placed among the lords of Nogrod in this episode, but no edition names him as a perpetrator, victim, or survivor. Establishes the archetypal 'heroic-legend' dwarf trap: craft, oath, greed, and ruin — the thematic slot Durlin would occupy if attested.

Accounts II
Of Aulë and Yavanna — The Making of the DwarvesThe Silmarillion, 'Of Aulë and Yavanna'

Mythic pre-First Age, written 1930s-1950s

Aulë creates the Seven Fathers of the Dwarves in secret; Ilúvatar gives them true life but puts them to sleep until after the Elves awaken. This is the cosmological origin for all Dwarves, including any figure named Durlin. It defines their nature: children of craft, stubborn, beloved by Aulë. Provides the only canonical genealogy for dwarves; Durlin, if canonical, must descend from one of the Seven Fathers, most likely Durin's line or the Firebeards/Broadbeams of the Blue Mountains.

Durin's Folk and the Long History of Khazad-dûm (Appendix A)J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings, Appendix A III 'Durin's Folk' and Appendix B

Second and Third Ages, published 1955

Traces Durin's line from Khazad-dûm to Erebor, the War of Dwarves and Orcs, and Thorin's quest. This is the only fully developed dwarf heroic-legend cycle in print. Durlin does not appear in the genealogies, king-lists, or battles (Azanulbizar, Five Armies), indicating he is not part of the Third Age canon. Absence from this detailed record strongly suggests Durlin is either First Age-only, non-canonical, or non-Tolkien.

Durlin is just another spelling of Durin the Deathless. — Durin (Q331656) and Durlin (Q108540491) are separate Wikidata entities. Durin is well-attested as eldest of the Seven Fathers with extensive canon; Durlin has no verified deeds, lineage, or titles. Conflation is orthographic, not textual.

If a character is on Wikidata, they must have a major story in Tolkien. — Wikidata includes many minor, draft, or even erroneous fictional entities with minimal sourcing. Presence in Wikidata does not equal canonical importance or verified primary text.

All Tolkien dwarves appear in The Hobbit or Lord of the Rings. — Most First Age dwarves (e.g., Bodruith, Naugladur) appear only in posthumous Lost Tales/Silmarillion material and never in Hobbit/LOTR. Durlin's absence from Hobbit/LOTR does not prove non-existence, but his absence from Silmarillion indices is more telling.
