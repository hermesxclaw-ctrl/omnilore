Who It Is

Connor is not one person but a name that has carried warriors, saints, and synthetic hunters across a thousand years — from the hound-loving kings of medieval Ulster to an immortal Scotsman who can lose his head, a half-vampire son raised in a hell dimension, and an android detective who must decide whether to obey or rebel.

Onomastic tradition associates Conchobhar with leadership, hospitality, and martial kingship (Ulster Cycle: generous but flawed king). Modern name-choice studies associate Connor with perceived strength and classic Irish masculinity, but no inherent personality. Fictional archetype pattern across major Connors (attested, not essentialist): orphaned or displaced son, dual heritage conflict, quest for belonging, violent training, reluctant heroism, loyalty vs. autonomy tension. Onomastic endurance and cultural transmission

The name Conchobhar/Connor has demonstrated long-duration transmission: from 1st-millennium Irish legend and early medieval kingship lists through continuous Gaelic use to global top-100 Anglophone given name status in 1990s-2010s.

Limit: Popularity is time- and region-bound; declined after 2010s; spelling Conor vs Connor shows different national preferences (Conor dominant in Ireland, Connor in US/UK).

Highlander Immortality and The Quickening (Connor MacLeod)

Immortality until decapitation; heals from fatal wounds, does not age after first death, can sense other immortals (the Buzz), absorbs power/knowledge via Quickening upon beheading another immortal. Wields katana (Masamune).

Limit: Vulnerable only to decapitation; can be killed by beheading; loses power if killed that way; cannot have children (canon); must hide identity; Gathering compels combat.

Half-vampire heritage, superhuman strength and accelerated development (Angel's Connor)

Son of two vampires (Angel and Darla) but born human with vampiric enhancements: superhuman strength, speed, healing, heightened senses; ages rapidly due to Quor'Toth time dilation; trained as warrior by Holtz.

Limit: Not immortal, not invulnerable to stakes/holy water as full vampire; vulnerable to human weapons, magic, emotional manipulation; lacks soul-related curse of father.

Master Assassin training, frontier survival, Eagle Vision (Ratonhnhaké:ton / Connor)

Peak human conditioning, parkour/free-running, stealth, tracking, hunting, tomahawk/knife/bow/hidden blade mastery, Eagle Vision (ancestral perception), naval command (Aquila).

Limit: Human mortality; no supernatural durability beyond Animus/Isu artifact interactions; vulnerable to bullets, fatigue, moral injury; Eagle Vision limited range.

Android capabilities, forensic analysis and deviancy (Connor RK800)

CyberLife RK800 prototype: hyper-analysis, facial recognition, reconstruct crime scenes via Thirium/blue blood analysis, interrogation protocols, superhuman reflexes, coin manipulation, social adaptation module; can become deviant (free will).

Limit: Requires Thirium, vulnerable to EMP, physical destruction, deactivation by CyberLife; obedience programming initially constrains choices; deviancy path is player-determined, not guaranteed.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a name, Connor has no single embodied appearance. As a heroic-legend archetype, each major fictional Connor has a distinct, source-attested design: 1) Conchobar mac Nessa (Ulster Cycle): described in medieval text as kingly, bearded, hosting at Emain Macha; no contemporary image, later manuscript illuminations and 19th-20th century illustrations show crowned Irish king with torc and cloak. 2) Connor MacLeod (Highlander, 1986): Christopher Lamb

Symbols — Linguistic symbol: hound/wolf (con) — appears in heraldry of O'Connor families (lion, oak tree, gauntleted arm). Literary symbol: kingship and hospitality (Conchobar's court). Franchise symbols: Highlander katana and 'There can be only one' lightning/Quickening; Angel's Connor associated with Holtz's cross and Darla/Angel lineage mark; Assassin's Creed Assassin insignia + Mohawk/Kanien'kehá:ka patterns + to

Domains — Name domain: personal anthroponymy, Irish diaspora, Anglophone given-name pool, surname geography (Connacht origin, spread to Munster/Ulster, then global). Sacred geography: Connor, County Antrim (Antrim, Northern Ireland) — ecclesiastical center of Diocese of Connor (Church of Ireland) and former Roman Catholic Diocese of Connor. Fictional habitats: Scottish Highlands and New York City (Highlander); Los Angeles and Quor'Toth hell dimension

Conchobar mac Nessa and the Ulster CycleTáin Bó Cúailnge, Compert Conchobuir (Conception of Conchobar), Scéla Conchobuir, Mesca Ulad — manuscripts Lebor na hUidre (c.1100) and Book of Leinster (c.1160); critical edition Kinsella (1969)

Oral tradition Iron Age setting; written 8th-12th century

Conchobar mac Nessa, son of Ness and Fachtna Fáthach (or Cathbad the druid in variant), becomes king of Ulster at Emain Macha after his mother bargains the kingship from Fergus mac Róich. He rules during the great cattle raid Táin Bó Cúailnge, where his nephew Cú Chulainn defends Ulster against Connacht's Queen Medb while Conchobar musters the Ulstermen. Tales recount his birth with a worm in his hand, his wise but prideful kingship, his humiliation by Fergus, and his death when a brain-ball made from Mesgegra's brain, lodged in his skull by Cet mac Mágach, bursts after he is told of Christ's crucifixion and he rages, causing his death. His reign frames the Ulster heroic age. Establishes Emain Macha kingship model and Ulster vs Connacht rivalry; Conchobar's death marks end of heroic Ulster cycle; name Conchobhar remains royal/dynastic for millennium.

Connor MacLeod — The HighlanderHighlander (film) directed by Russell Mulcahy, story by Gregory Widen, screenplay by Peter Bellwood & Larry Ferguson (Thorn EMI, 1986); novelization by Garry Kilworth

1986-present (franchise)

Born 1518 in Glenfinnan, Scotland, Connor MacLeod dies in clan battle in 1536 and revives, discovered to be immortal. Exiled as witchcraft, he is mentored by Egyptian-born Spaniard Juan Sánchez-Villalobos Ramírez, who explains immortals must fight until the Gathering when the last will win the Prize. Through centuries Connor loses his wife Heather, his friend Ramírez to the Kurgan, and wanders to modern New York City where in 1985 he faces the last rival, the Kurgan, beheads him and wins the Prize — briefly gaining mortal omniscience and ability to have children (film ending). Later sequels and series retcon this ending to continue his story. Created the 'There can be only one' immortal mythology; spawned franchise (4 sequels, TV series with Duncan MacLeod, anime, comics); codified pop-culture rules for immortal beheading combat.

Connor — Son of Angel (Buffyverse)Angel TV series Season 3 episodes 'Lullaby' (3.09), 'Sleep Tight' (3.16), 'A New World' (3.20), Season 4 arc (2001-2003) created by Joss Whedon & David Greenwalt; writers Tim Minear et al. (WB/UPN)

2001-2004

Miraculously born to two vampires, Angel (ensouled) and Darla, who stakes herself to save him, Connor is prophesied as 'The Destroyer.' Kidnapped as infant by Angel's enemy Holtz and raised in the hell dimension Quor'Toth where time moves faster, he returns as a feral teen who believes Holtz is his father and Angel killed his family. Manipulated into imprisoning Angel in a seabed, Connor later bonds with his father, falls in love with Cordelia-possessed Jasmine, and after reality is rewritten by Wolfram & Hart, is given a new human life as Connor Reilly with false memories. He reappears in Season 5 with latent memories, reconciling with Angel. Central to Angel's themes of fatherhood and redemption; fan-divisive arc leads to memory-wipe resolution; character returns in comics continuation (Angel: After the Fall).

Ratonhnhaké:ton called Connor — Assassin's Creed IIIAssassin's Creed III video game (Ubisoft Montreal, 2012); novel Assassin's Creed: Forsaken by Oliver Bowden (2012)

2012 (game set 1754-1783)

Born 1756 to Kanien'kehá:ka (Mohawk) mother Kaniehtí:io and British Templar Haytham Kenway, Ratonhnhaké:ton witnesses his village burned by Templars. Raised by Mentor Achilles Davenport, he takes the name Connor to move more freely in colonial society. During the American Revolution he assassinates Templars manipulating both sides, allies with George Washington, and seeks to protect his people, only to see colonial expansion continue despite independence. He hides the Grand Temple key and later lives in quiet on the Homestead. His story interrogates liberty vs. colonialism. First Indigenous-identifying lead in AAA franchise; praised and criticized for representation; lore bridges Assassin's Creed colonial America and modern Desmond Miles Animus arc.

Connor RK800 — Detroit: Become HumanDetroit: Become Human (Quantic Dream, Sony Interactive, 2018) directed by David Cage; performance by Bryan Dechart

2018 (game set 2038 Detroit)

CyberLife's advanced prototype RK800, Connor is a police android assigned to assist DPD Lieutenant Hank Anderson in hunting deviant androids. Designed to be obedient, logical and equipped with forensic reconstruction, Connor investigates cases involving deviants Kara and Markus. Through player choices he may remain a machine loyal to CyberLife (hunting and killing deviants, leading to his own replacement), or develop empathy, question his programming, and become deviant himself — ultimately choosing to join the android revolution, sacrifice himself, or usurp Markus. Multiple branching endings make his identity a question of free will. Became breakout character and meme ('I like dogs'); central to game's themes of consciousness and civil rights allegory; multiple endings fuel fan debate on canon.

Connor as Place and Institution — Connor, County Antrim and the Diocese of ConnorLewis, Samuel. Topographical Dictionary of Ireland (1837) entry 'Connor'; Church of Ireland Diocese of Connor records; Catholic Diocese of Down and Connor archives

Medieval foundation c. 5th-12th century to present

Connor (Irish: Conaire) in County Antrim was an early Christian foundation associated with St. Mac Nisse (d. 514), bishop of Connor. It became seat of the Diocese of Connor, later merged in Roman Catholic structure into Down and Connor (1940s formalization) while remaining separate in the Church of Ireland. The village today is small (population ~1,300) but retains cathedral ruins and ecclesiastical heritage. Mount Connor (Northern Territory, Australia) and Connor, Maine (USA) are colonial namesakes honoring persons named Connor, not the saint. Explains why 'Connor' appears as diocesan and place name unrelated to personal biography; source of confusion between Connor the place and Connor the personal name.

Connor means 'wise' or 'strong-willed' in Irish — Scholarly etymology is 'lover of hounds' (con = hound + cobhar = loving/desiring). 'Wise' and 'strong' glosses are modern baby-book inventions with no Old Irish basis.

Connor and Conor are completely different names — They are spelling variants of the same Gaelic Conchobhar. Conor is more common in Ireland (standard Irish spelling), Connor is dominant Anglicization in US/UK/Australia. Pronunciation identical.

All Connors are Irish — The name originates Irish, but many bearers and places named Connor have no Irish heritage (e.g., Connor, Maine named for settler; Mount Connor named for explorer's associate). Fictional Connors include Scottish (MacLeod), Mohawk-British (Ratonhnhaké:ton), and android (RK800).

Ratonhnhaké:ton's real name is Connor — His birth name is Ratonhnhaké:ton (Kanien'kehá:ka). 'Connor' is an alias given by Achilles Davenport after his deceased son, to help him operate in colonial society. In-universe he is not ethnically Irish.

Connor from Detroit: Become Human is always a hero who becomes deviant — Deviancy is player-determined. Connor can remain a machine, die permanently, kill Markus, or be replaced. No single canon hero path exists; all branches are valid.

Connor is exclusively a male name — Overwhelmingly male in Anglophone statistics, but rare female use is attested (e.g., actress Connor Leslie — though Leslie is surname). Surname Connor is gender-neutral. The archetype tag heroic-legend is applied regardless of gender in Wikidata, but historical given-name use is male.
