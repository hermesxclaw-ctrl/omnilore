Who It Is

Bunjil (also Bundjil, Bunjel) is the eaglehawk creator deity of the Kulin Nation in what is now Victoria, Australia — the land surrounding Port Phillip Bay and the city of Melbourne. Bunjil is simultaneously a mighty wedge-tailed eagle (Aquila audax) and an ancestral being, one of the most powerful figures in southeastern Australian Aboriginal cosmology. He created the land, the rivers, the mountains, the animals, and the first humans, shaping the landscape of Victoria with his great wings and claws. He taught the Kulin people their laws, their marriage rules, their hunting techniques, and the rituals that maintain the proper order of the world. When his work was complete, Bunjil ascended into the sky, where he became the star Altair in the constellation Aquila (the Eagle) — a stunning convergence of indigenous astronomy and mythology. Before ascending, he instructed Bellin-Bellin (the Crowned Crane) to watch over the people, and he gave the people their totems. Bunjil's sacred site is Baw Baw National Park and the Dandenong Ranges, where rock art depicting him (and his two dingo companions) can be found in Bunjil's Shelter near Stawell — one of the most significant Aboriginal rock art sites in Victoria. In modern Melbourne, Bunjil's presence is honored in public art, the naming of institutions, and in Welcome to Country ceremonies performed by Wurundjeri elders. He represents the Kulin people's enduring connection to their land, even amid one of Australia's largest cities. His eagle form emphasizes the Aboriginal understanding that creator beings are not separate from nature but manifest through the animals and landscapes they made.
