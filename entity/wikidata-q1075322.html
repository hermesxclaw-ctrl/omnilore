Who It Is

In the misty annals of Irish myth, a king loses his arm in battle and with it his throne—only to reclaim both through magic, silver, and the skill of divine healers. Nuada Airgetlám, the silver-handed sovereign of the Tuatha Dé Danann, embodies the ancient Celtic ideal of kingship: physical perfection, martial prowess, and the wisdom to step aside for a greater leader.

Nuada is portrayed as a noble and just king, willing to sacrifice his own position for the good of his people. He shows chivalry in allowing the Fir Bolg to inspect his forces before battle. He is also pragmatic, stepping down in favor of Lugh when he recognizes the youth's superior leadership.

Powers
Kingship and Leadership

As king of the Tuatha Dé Danann, Nuada possesses the authority and responsibility to lead his people in war and peace.

Limit: He loses kingship due to physical imperfection, indicating a ritual requirement of bodily integrity.

Combat Skills

Nuada is a formidable warrior, able to fight the Fir Bolg champion Sreng and later participate in the second Battle of Mag Tuired.

Limit: He is defeated by Sreng, losing his arm.

Magical Healing (via Dian Cécht and Miach)

Nuada receives a functional silver arm from Dian Cécht and Creidhne, and later a flesh-and-blood arm from Miach, restoring his physical perfection.

Limit: The healing is performed by others, not by Nuada himself.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Nuada is described as a king of the Tuatha Dé Danann, but no detailed physical description survives. He is noted for his beauty and physical perfection, which is why his loss of an arm is significant. After his injury, he receives a silver arm made by Dian Cécht and Creidhne, and later a flesh-and-blood arm by Miach.

Symbols — Nuada's primary symbol is his silver hand/arm (Airgetlám). His sword is one of the Four Treasures of the Tuatha Dé Danann, symbolizing his kingship and martial power. In later tradition, he is associated with the plain of Maigh Nuad (Maynooth).

Domains — As king of the Tuatha Dé Danann, Nuada's domain is Ireland, particularly the region of Connacht after the Fir Bolg are given a quarter. His association with Maigh Nuad (Maynooth) in County Kildare suggests a territorial connection.

Accounts I
The First Battle of Mag TuiredCath Maige Tuired (and related texts)

Mythological, pre-Christian

Nuada leads the Tuatha Dé Danann to Ireland and demands half the island from the Fir Bolg. When rejected, war ensues. During the battle, Nuada loses his arm to Sreng. Despite this, the Tuatha Dé win, and Sreng is offered a quarter of Ireland (Connacht). Nuada loses kingship due to his injury; Bres becomes king.

Accounts II
The Second Battle of Mag TuiredCath Maige Tuired

Mythological

After Nuada is restored to kingship, Bres and the Fomorians threaten war. Nuada yields kingship to Lugh, who leads the Tuatha Dé to victory. Nuada is killed and beheaded by Balor. Nuada's death; Lugh becomes king.

The Fate of the Children of TuireannOidheadh Chlainne Tuireann

Late medieval

Nuada is described as having a one-eyed door-keeper, whose eye is replaced by the healers Miach and Oirmiach with a cat's eye. None major.
