Who It Is

Did you know that the terrifying Yao Guai of the Fallout wasteland has its roots in ancient Chinese myths where fox spirits and snake demons could shape-shift into beautiful humans? Explore how a 2,000-year-old folklore concept became a post-apocalyptic icon.

Traditional yaoguai are often depicted as cunning and malevolent, using their shape-shifting abilities to deceive humans and cause harm. They may be vengeful spirits or animals that have cultivated spiritual power through meditation or by absorbing cosmic energy. In the Fallout series, Yao Guai are aggressive and territorial predators, attacking on sight. They are solitary hunters, except during mating season or when raising young. Powers
Shape-shifting (traditional)

In Chinese folklore, yaoguai possess the ability to transform into human form or other animals, often to deceive or seduce victims. This power is central to many tales, where the yaoguai's true nature is revealed only through specific means, such as a Taoist mirror or a holy text.

Limit:

Superhuman strength and resilience (Fallout)

The Fallout Yao Guai possesses immense physical strength, capable of swatting away heavily armored opponents and breaking through barriers. Its mutated physiology grants it high resistance to damage, including ballistic and energy weapons, and it can survive in highly irradiated environments.

Limit:

Radiation absorption

In the Fallout universe, Yao Guai have adapted to consume and metabolize radiation, which enhances their mutations and healing abilities. This makes them more dangerous in irradiated zones, as they become stronger and more aggressive.

Limit:

Elemental manipulation (traditional)

Some yaoguai in Chinese mythology are associated with natural elements, such as controlling wind, fire, or water. For example, the 'Nian' beast is said to be afraid of fire and loud noises, but other yaoguai may wield these forces as weapons.

Accounts I
The Fox Spirit and the ScholarStrange Tales from a Chinese Studio (Liaozhai Zhiyi) by Pu Songling

Qing Dynasty (17th century)

A young scholar encounters a beautiful woman who is actually a fox yaoguai. She seduces him, and he becomes obsessed, neglecting his studies. A Taoist priest reveals her true nature, and the scholar must use a talisman to banish her. The story warns against the dangers of lust and the deceptive nature of yaoguai. The scholar loses his academic honors and nearly his life, but he is saved by the priest. He learns to be wary of appearances and returns to a disciplined life.

Accounts II
The Yao Guai of the WastelandFallout 3 (video game) lore terminals

2277 (in-game timeline)

In the Capital Wasteland, a group of settlers encounters a Yao Guai near the ruins of a national park. The creature attacks their caravan, killing several members. A survivor describes the beast as a 'demon bear' with glowing eyes, and the event becomes a cautionary tale among wastelanders, leading to increased patrols and the use of bear traps. The caravan is destroyed, but the Yao Guai is eventually killed by a group of Brotherhood of Steel paladins. The incident reinforces the fear of mutated creatures and the need for vigilance.

The White Snake and the MonkLegend of the White Snake (Bai She Zhuan)

Ming Dynasty (16th century)

A white snake yaoguai transforms into a beautiful woman and marries a mortal man. A Buddhist monk, Fahai, discovers her true nature and traps her in a pagoda. The story explores themes of love, prejudice, and the potential for yaoguai to be benevolent, as the snake spirit had genuinely loved her husband. The snake is imprisoned, but her son later becomes a scholar and frees her, leading to a reconciliation. The tale is a classic example of yaoguai with complex morality.
