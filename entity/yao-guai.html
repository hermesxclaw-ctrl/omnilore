Who It Is
Form

In traditional Chinese folklore, yaoguai are depicted as shape-shifting entities that can assume animal, human, or hybrid forms. They often retain animalistic features such as claws, fur, or tails, and may be accompanied by supernatural auras or glowing eyes. In the Fallout video game series, the Yao Guai is portrayed as a massive, mutated bear with patchy fur, exposed muscle tissue, and often glowing, feral eyes. Its size is significantly larger than a normal bear, with some specimens reaching up to 10 feet tall when standing. The mutation has also caused its claws to become elongated and razor-sharp, and its jaw to distend, revealing rows of jagged teeth. The overall appearance is grotesque and menacing, reflecting the radiation-induced mutations of the post-apocalyptic world.

Powers

Shape-shifting (traditional) In Chinese folklore, yaoguai possess the ability to transform into human form or other animals, often to deceive or seduce victims. This power is central to many tales, where the yaoguai's true nature is revealed only through specific means, such as a Taoist mirror or a holy text.

Superhuman strength and resilience (Fallout) The Fallout Yao Guai possesses immense physical strength, capable of swatting away heavily armored opponents and breaking through barriers. Its mutated physiology grants it high resistance to damage, including ballistic and energy weapons, and it can survive in highly irradiated environments.

Radiation absorption In the Fallout universe, Yao Guai have adapted to consume and metabolize radiation, which enhances their mutations and healing abilities. This makes them more dangerous in irradiated zones, as they become stronger and more aggressive.

Elemental manipulation (traditional) Some yaoguai in Chinese mythology are associated with natural elements, such as controlling wind, fire, or water. For example, the 'Nian' beast is said to be afraid of fire and loud noises, but other yaoguai may wield these forces as weapons.

Accounts

A young scholar encounters a beautiful woman who is actually a fox yaoguai. She seduces him, and he becomes obsessed, neglecting his studies. A Taoist priest reveals her true nature, and the scholar must use a talisman to banish her. The story warns against the dangers of lust and the deceptive nature of yaoguai.

In the Capital Wasteland, a group of settlers encounters a Yao Guai near the ruins of a national park. The creature attacks their caravan, killing several members. A survivor describes the beast as a 'demon bear' with glowing eyes, and the event becomes a cautionary tale among wastelanders, leading to increased patrols and the use of bear traps.

A white snake yaoguai transforms into a beautiful woman and marries a mortal man. A Buddhist monk, Fahai, discovers her true nature and traps her in a pagoda. The story explores themes of love, prejudice, and the potential for yaoguai to be benevolent, as the snake spirit had genuinely loved her husband.

Modern Interpretations

Video Game Fallout 3 Bethesda Game Studios 2008 Enemy creature The Yao Guai was introduced as a new enemy type, quickly becoming a fan favorite due to its terrifying design and challenging combat. It has since appeared in multiple sequels and spin-offs, cementing its place in gaming culture. Video Game Black Myth: Wukong Game Science 2024 Inspiration for enemy design The game features numerous yaoguai-inspired enemies, drawing directly from Chinese mythology. This has sparked renewed interest in the original folklore, with players seeking out the source material. Literature Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio Pu Songling 1740 Primary source This collection of over 400 stories includes many yaoguai tales, serving as a foundational text for understanding the creature's mythology. It has been translated into multiple languages and remains a classic of Chinese literature.

Scholarly Debates
Classification of yaoguai
Yaoguai are a distinct category of supernatural beings, separate from ghosts (gui) and demons (mo). ⇌ Yaoguai are a broad umbrella term that includes ghosts, demons, and animal spirits, with no clear boundaries.
Moral alignment
Yaoguai are inherently malevolent and dangerous, requiring extermination. ⇌ Yaoguai can be benevolent or neutral, with some seeking enlightenment or forming positive relationships with humans.
Origin of the Fallout Yao Guai
The Fallout Yao Guai is a direct adaptation of the Chinese mythological creature, with intentional cultural references. ⇌ The Fallout Yao Guai is a purely original creation, borrowing only the name for exotic flavor, with no deeper mythological connection.
What We Don't Know

Further research is needed to explore the regional variations of yaoguai in Chinese folklore, particularly in minority cultures such as the Miao or Tibetan traditions. Additionally, the psychological impact of the Fallout Yao Guai on players has not been extensively studied. The etymology of 'yaoguai' in relation to other terms like 'jing' (spirit) requires deeper linguistic analysis. There is also a gap in documenting the use of yaoguai in modern Chinese media beyond gaming, such as in films and web novels.
