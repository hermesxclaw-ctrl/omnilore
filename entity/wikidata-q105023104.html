Who It Is

In the candlelit terraces of Umbanda, a figure emerges from the forest's edge—not as a distant god, but as a presence that walks among the faithful, carrying the bow of the hunter and the wisdom of the wild. This is Caboclo Oxóssi, a spirit of the Brazilian spiritist tradition, where the divine and the ancestral intertwine, and where the forest speaks in the language of arrows and songs.

Caboclo Oxóssi is known for being quiet, wise, and observant. He is a protector of the forest and of those who are lost. In possession rituals, he speaks in a calm, measured tone, often using metaphors of the hunt and nature. He is considered a healer and a guide, offering practical advice and spiritual insight.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Oxóssi' originates from Yoruba, likely from 'Òṣóòsì', associated with hunting and the forest. 'Caboclo' is a Brazilian term for individuals of indigenous and European ancestry, and in Umbanda refers to a class of spirits representing indigenous Brazilian ancestors.

Pronunciation — oh-SHAW-see ka-BOH-kloo

Aliases — Oxóssi, Caboclo da Mata, Caboclo do Arco, Oxóssi Caboclo

Ownership — Afro-Brazilian and Indigenous Brazilian traditions, primarily within Umbanda and related Afro-Brazilian religions; originated in Brazil in the early 20th century.

Powers
Hunting and Provision

As a hunter, he provides for his followers, symbolically ensuring that they have what they need to survive and thrive.

Limit: Not a literal provider of physical food; rather, he offers guidance and opportunities.

Healing

He is known to perform spiritual healings, using herbs and natural remedies, and to cure both physical and spiritual ailments.

Limit: Healing is performed within the context of ritual and faith; no supernatural claims are made outside belief.

Guidance and Protection

He offers guidance to those who are lost or in need, protecting them from spiritual and physical dangers.

Limit: Protection is understood as spiritual, not physical.

Knowledge of the Forest

He possesses deep knowledge of plants, animals, and natural cycles, which he uses to teach and advise.

Limit: Knowledge is transmitted through rituals and personal experience.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Caboclo Oxóssi is typically described as a tall, strong indigenous man, with dark skin, long black hair, and a feathered headdress. He wears simple clothing, often of leather or cotton, and carries a bow and arrow. His face is often painted with tribal designs. He may hold a staff or a blowgun.

Symbols — Bow and arrow are his primary symbols, representing precision and the hunt. He is associated with the colors green and white, and with the forest. His iconography often includes animals like deer, birds, and snakes. In some representations, he is depicted with a single arrow, symbolizing focus.

Domains — He resides in the 'mata' (forest) and is associated with the 'linha de Oxóssi' (line of Oxóssi) in Umbanda, which governs hunting, provision, and knowledge. His domain is the natural world, particularly forests and wild places, and he is invoked for protection, health, and success in endeavors.

Accounts I
The Hunter's GuidanceOral tradition within Umbanda

20th century to present

A person lost in the forest or in life calls out for help. Caboclo Oxóssi appears, offering direction and teaching them to find their way. He may provide a symbolic arrow to point the way or teach them to listen to the sounds of the forest. The individual gains clarity and confidence, often leading to a positive change in their life.

Accounts II
The Protector of the ForestOral tradition within Umbanda

20th century to present

Caboclo Oxóssi defends the forest and its creatures from those who would destroy them. He may appear to hunters who kill without need or to loggers who clear land, warning them to change their ways. Those who heed his warning become protectors of the forest; those who do not suffer misfortune.
