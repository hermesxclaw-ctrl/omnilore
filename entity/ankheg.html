Ankheg

The Plow-Terror, The Acid-Spitter, The Burrower Beneath the Wheat

TypeMonstrosity
CultureDungeons & Dragons (Wizards of the Coast / Hasbro)
RealmFarmlands, Grasslands, Forests
ScaleIndividual / Solitary (territorial)
AlignmentUnaligned (bestial)

The farmhand said something grabbed his plow horse from below. Just yanked it underground. The screaming lasted about three seconds. Then ... nothing.

Who It Is

The Ankheg is D&D's terror of the farmlands — a massive burrowing insectoid that lives just below the surface of cultivated soil, preying on livestock, farmers, and anyone unfortunate enough to plow the wrong field. Imagine a creature the size of a horse, covered in brown-black chitin, with a segmented body like a giant ant or beetle and six powerful, claw-tipped legs adapted for digging. Its most distinctive feature is its head: a flattened, shovel-like plate with oversized mandibles capable of snapping a human body in two, and a throat gland that produces a stream of corrosive acid it can spray up to 30 feet.

Ankhegs are ambush predators. They tunnel just beneath the surface, sensing vibrations through the soil, and erupt directly beneath their prey. The first sign of an Ankheg attack is often the ground collapsing underfoot as the creature bursts upward, mandibles snapping and acid spraying. Farmers in regions with Ankheg infestations learn to walk with heavy steps (to broadcast their location to the blind creature below, giving it less reason to investigate) and to never, ever stand still in an open field.

Interestingly, Ankhegs are not without value. Their chitin can be harvested and treated to create 'Ankheg plate' — armor that is as strong as steel but significantly lighter. Alchemists prize their acid glands. And their tunneling, while dangerous, aerates and fertilizes soil — some frontier communities have learned to coexist with Ankhegs, marking their territories and avoiding them rather than attempting extermination, as the Ankhegs keep the land fertile and deter worse monsters.

Ankhegs are a classic 'mid-level pest' monster in D&D: not a world-ending threat like a Tarrasque or a dragon, but a genuine danger to common people that high-level adventurers often overlook. A single Ankheg can devastate a farmstead, and a breeding pair can render miles of farmland uninhabitable. Their appearance in video games like Baldur's Gate (where Ankhegs in the farmlands outside Baldur's Gate city are a memorable early-game challenge), Neverwinter Nights, and the D&D-based episodes of various media have cemented them as one of D&D's most practical, down-to-earth threats — literally.
