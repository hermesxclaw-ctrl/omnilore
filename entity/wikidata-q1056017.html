Who It Is

From the sun-drenched plains of Takama-ga-hara, a divine prince descended to the misty peaks of Kyushu, carrying with him the mirrored soul of the sun goddess and a mandate that would shape the destiny of an entire nation. This was Ninigi-no-Mikoto, the celestial grandson whose arrival on Earth marked the pivotal moment when the realm of the gods intersected with the world of mortals, setting the stage for the imperial line that claims descent from the sun itself. But his story is not merely a tale of divine triumph; it is a narrative woven with complex negotiations, human-like flaws, and a fateful choice that would cost his descendants their immortality—a poignant reminder that even the gods are not immune to the consequences of their actions.

Ninigi is portrayed as a divine prince who is obedient to the will of the heavenly deities. He is respectful but also makes personal choices, such as selecting a bride based on appearance, which leads to consequences. He is also depicted as suspicious, as seen when he doubts his wife's fidelity.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Ninigi (瓊瓊杵) is often interpreted as 'beloved jeweled mallet' or 'jeweled mallet'. The full name Ame-nigishi-kuni-nigishi-amatsuhiko-hiko-ho-no-ninigi-no-Mikoto includes elements meaning 'heavenly' (ama/ame), 'imperial' (kuni), 'sun' (hi), 'prince' (hiko), and 'ear of rice' (ho). Powers
Divine Authority

As the grandson of Amaterasu and the chosen ruler of the earthly realm, Ninigi possessed the authority to govern and establish the imperial line.

Limit: His authority was granted by the heavenly deities and was subject to their will.

Possession of the Three Sacred Treasures

He was given the sword Kusanagi, the mirror Yata no Kagami, and the jewel Yasakani no Magatama, which became symbols of imperial legitimacy.

Limit: The treasures were not used as weapons by Ninigi in the myths; they were symbols.

Immortality (lost)

Originally, Ninigi and his descendants were immortal, but due to his rejection of Iwanagahime, they became mortal.

Limit: This 'power' was lost, so it is more of a curse.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description is given in the sources. He is typically depicted in art as a noble prince in traditional Japanese attire, often carrying the three sacred treasures.

Symbols — Associated with the Three Sacred Treasures: the sword Kusanagi no Tsurugi, the mirror Yata no Kagami, and the jewel Yasakani no Magatama. These symbols represent valor, wisdom, and benevolence, respectively.

Domains — He descended to Mount Takachiho in Kyushu, Japan, and is associated with the region of Hyūga (now Miyazaki Prefecture). His realm is the earthly world, specifically the land of Japan.

Accounts I
The Descent of NinigiKojiki and Nihon Shoki

Mythological age

Amaterasu, the sun goddess, decided to send her grandson Ninigi to rule the earthly realm. After several failed attempts by other deities, Ninigi was chosen. He was given the Three Sacred Treasures and descended to Mount Takachiho. He was initially blocked by the earthly deity Sarutahiko, but the goddess Uzume persuaded Sarutahiko to let him pass. Ninigi then established his rule in Hyūga. This event, known as Tenson kōrin, established the divine right of the Japanese imperial family. Ninigi's descendants eventually became the emperors of Japan.

Accounts II
Ninigi's Marriage and the Curse of MortalityKojiki and Nihon Shoki

Mythological age

Ninigi met the mountain god Oyamatsumi, who offered his two daughters as brides. Ninigi chose the beautiful Sakuyahime and rejected the ugly Iwanagahime. Iwanagahime, representing longevity, was insulted and cursed Ninigi and his descendants to have short lives. This explains why emperors and humans are mortal.

The Birth of Ninigi's SonsKojiki and Nihon Shoki

Mythological age

Sakuyahime became pregnant after one night, and Ninigi suspected adultery. To prove her fidelity, she entered a burning hut and gave birth to three sons: Hoderi, Hosuseri, and Hoori. The sons survived, and Hoori later became the grandfather of Emperor Jimmu. The birth of these sons continued the divine lineage leading to the first emperor.
