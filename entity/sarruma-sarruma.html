Who It Is

A Hurrian deity adopted into the Hittite pantheon as the son of Teshub/Tarhunt and Hepat, Sarruma ('King of the Mountains') was a youthful warrior-god associated with the Anatolian highlands. He served as the personal protective deity of several Hittite kings, most notably Tudhaliya IV, who appears embraced by Sarruma in the famous relief at Yazılıkaya (Chamber B, relief 81). This representation — the god tenderly cradling the king's arm — is one of the most intimate depictions of divine-human relationship in ancient Near Eastern art and underscores Sarruma's role as mediator between the celestial pantheon and its earthly representative.
