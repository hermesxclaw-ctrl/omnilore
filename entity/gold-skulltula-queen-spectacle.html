Who It Is

Did you know that the Gold Skulltula, a seemingly simple collectible in 'Ocarina of Time,' has inspired a fan-created 'Queen Spectacle' that has taken on a life of its own? Dive into the lore of this golden arachnid and discover how a video game side quest has sparked theories about ancient curses, hidden treasures, and even connections to Mayan deities.

Gold Skulltulas are reclusive and nocturnal, typically found in hidden locations such as under rocks, in trees, or in caves, often requiring the player to perform specific actions to reveal them. They are not aggressive unless provoked, but when attacked, they will defend themselves by jumping and biting. In the games, they are not boss enemies but rather collectible targets, and their behavior is more akin to that of a rare animal than a monster. Powers
Curse of Greed

Upon defeating a Gold Skulltula, the player receives a token that is both a reward and a curse. The token is said to be cursed, and collecting all 100 tokens in 'Ocarina of Time' is required to fully lift the curse from the Skulltula Family, who were transformed into spiders. This ability is not a direct combat power but a narrative mechanic that ties the creature to themes of greed and redemption.

Limit:

Wall Climbing

Gold Skulltulas can climb walls and ceilings with ease, allowing them to escape or ambush prey. This is a standard ability of all Skulltulas, but the Gold variant uses it to hide in hard-to-reach places, making them a test of the player's observation skills.

Limit:

Camouflage

Gold Skulltulas often blend into their surroundings, appearing as a shimmering spot on a wall or floor. They only become visible when the player approaches or uses a specific item like the Stone of Agony (in the GameCube version) or the Shard of Agony (in the 3DS version), which rumbles when a hidden Gold Skulltula is nearby. This ability makes them a puzzle element rather than a combat threat.

Limit:

Venomous Bite

In combat, Gold Skulltulas can bite, inflicting damage and potentially poisoning the player, though in the games, the poison effect is not present; the bite simply causes damage. This is a minor ability, as the creature is not a major threat.

Limit:

Token Drop

When defeated, a Gold Skulltula drops a 'Gold Skulltula Token,' a small golden emblem that is collected and exchanged for rewards. This ability is unique to the Gold Skulltula and is central to the side quest, as the tokens are also used to free the cursed Skulltula Family.

Accounts I
The Curse of the Skulltula FamilyThe Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time

1998

In Kakariko Village, the player encounters a family that has been cursed to become Skulltulas. The curse was placed upon them by a witch, and the only way to break it is to collect 100 Gold Skulltula tokens scattered across Hyrule. The family, led by the father, who is now a Skulltula, pleads with the player to help them. As the player collects tokens, the family gradually transforms back to human form, and the father rewards the player with rupees and a heart piece. This story is a side quest that emphasizes the theme of redemption and the consequences of greed, as the family's curse is a result of their own avarice. The curse is lifted after collecting all 100 tokens, and the family is restored to human form. The player receives a heart piece and a large rupee reward, but the journey is long and requires thorough exploration. The story reinforces the idea that material wealth (the tokens) can be a burden, but perseverance leads to a positive outcome.

Accounts II
The Legend of the Golden SpiderFan fiction and fan theories

2000s-present

A popular fan theory posits that the Gold Skulltula are not merely monsters but guardians of ancient secrets, and the 'Queen Spectacle' is a legendary matriarch who watches over the most valuable treasure in Hyrule. According to this theory, the Queen is a colossal Skulltula that appears only to those who have proven their worth by collecting all 100 tokens, and she offers a choice: take the treasure and be cursed, or leave it and gain wisdom. This story is not canonical but has been widely circulated in fan forums and has inspired fan art and even a fan-made boss fight in a ROM hack. In the fan theory, the Queen's test determines the player's fate, with the 'good' ending granting a unique item or a blessing, and the 'bad' ending resulting in a game over or a permanent curse. This story adds depth to the Gold Skulltula lore, transforming a simple collectible into a narrative device.

The Kukulkan ConnectionComparative mythology analysis

2020s

A fan theory, as discussed in the Boethian Acolyte blog, draws parallels between the Gold Skulltula and the Mayan deity Kukulkan, the 'Plumed Serpent.' The theory suggests that the Gold Skulltula's golden color and its role as a guardian of knowledge (through the token quest) mirror Kukulkan's association with wisdom and the sun. The blog post speculates that the Zelda developers may have been influenced by Mesoamerican mythology when designing the Skulltula, though this is unconfirmed. The theory also notes that Kukulkan is often depicted as a serpent, not a spider, but the shared 'golden' aspect and the idea of a 'spectacle' (the serpent's plumes) are used as points of comparison. This theory has not been widely adopted, but it has sparked discussions about the cultural influences in Zelda games. It remains a fringe interpretation, with most fans viewing the Gold Skulltula as a purely original creation.
