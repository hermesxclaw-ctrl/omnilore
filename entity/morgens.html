Who It Is

The Morgens (or Mari-Morgans, 'Sea-Born' or 'Daughters of the Sea') are the mermaid-fairies of Breton folklore — beautiful, golden-haired sea-women who dwell in underwater palaces off the Breton coast. They are closely related to the Welsh 'Morgen' (from which Morgan le Fay of Arthurian legend likely derives her name — 'Morgan' meaning 'sea-born' in Brythonic Celtic). Breton Morgens comb their long, golden hair with golden combs while sitting on coastal rocks, their singing luring fishermen to the rocks or the deep. They can breathe both water and air and can walk on land if they hide their fish-tails. Like mermaids across cultures, Morgens seduce human men, taking them as lovers in their underwater realms — where time passes differently, and a man may return to the surface to find generations have passed. Unlike the uniformly predatory sirens, Breton Morgens sometimes fall genuinely in love: legends tell of Morgen-marriages that produced remarkable children (often with webbed fingers or supernatural abilities) and of Morgens who rescued drowning sailors. They are associated with specific locations along the Breton coast — the Morbihan Gulf, the islands of the Iroise Sea, the cliffs of the Côte de Granit Rose. The Morgens are also associated with sunken cities: the legendary city of Ker-Ys (Breton Atlantis), whose princess Dahut (or Ahes) was transformed into a Morgen after she caused the city's flooding through her wickedness. The Breton Morgens represent the sea as female, beautiful, and fundamentally other — the coast as a zone of dangerous encounter between the human world and the 'otherworld' beneath the waves, a theme that runs through all Celtic maritime folklore.
