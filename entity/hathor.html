Who They Are

Hathor is one of the most ancient and beloved Egyptian goddesses, embodying love, music, dance, fertility, and the sky. She is depicted as a cow, a woman with cow's horns and a solar disk headdress, or a woman with cow's ears. She is the celestial cow who gave birth to the sun, the goddess who welcomes the dead in the afterlife (offering them food and drink from her sycamore tree), and the fierce Eye of Ra who punishes humanity. Her cult center was at Dendera, where the magnificent Temple of Hathor still stands. She was the patroness of miners and workers in foreign lands (as 'Lady of Byblos'). In the Myth of the Heavenly Cow, Ra sent Hathor as his eye to destroy rebellious humans, but she became Sekhmet in her bloodlust. Hathor is associated with the sistrum, the menat necklace, and the mirror. She is the wife of Horus (the Haroeris aspect) and mother of Ihy, the child god of music.

The Approach

In the vibrant tapestry of ancient Egyptian mythology, Hathor stands as a goddess of duality, embodying both the nurturing aspects of motherhood and the fierce protection of divine justice. Revered as the 'Lady of the Sycamore' and the 'Eye of Ra', she captivates the imagination with her multifaceted identity, crossing the boundaries between love and vengeance, joy and punishment.

Etymology

The name Hathor (ḥwt-ḥr) translates to 'House of Horus', indicating her connection to Horus, the sky god.

Cultural Ownership

Egyptian mythology, particularly during the Old Kingdom and New Kingdom periods.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars debate whether Hathor originally had a singular identity or multiple localized forms reflecting the various aspects of femininity.

Description

Appearance

Hathor is primarily depicted as a woman with cow's horns and a sun disk on her head, but she can also appear as a cow, a lioness, a sycamore tree, or a woman with cow's ears. Her iconography often includes musical instruments, particularly the sistrum, symbolizing joy and celebration.

Symbols & Iconography

Common symbols associated with Hathor include the cow, the sycamore tree, and the sistrum. She is frequently shown in temple reliefs surrounded by musicians and dancers, emphasizing her connection to the arts.

Behaviour & Personality

Hathor embodies dual aspects: as a nurturing mother, she represents love, beauty, and joy; as the Eye of Ra, she can be fierce and vengeful, punishing those who threaten the cosmic order. Her character reflects the complexities of femininity in ancient Egyptian culture.

Domains & Habitats

Hathor is associated with various domains including love, music, fertility, motherhood, and the sky. Her primary cult center was at Dendera in Upper Egypt, but she was venerated across Egypt and even in foreign lands such as Nubia.

Version Differences

Throughout different periods, her role and significance evolved. In the Old Kingdom, she was primarily a maternal figure, while during the New Kingdom, her identity began to blend with that of Isis, leading to some overlap in worship and myth.
