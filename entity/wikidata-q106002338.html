Who It Is

On a spring afternoon in 1993, a chestnut colt with a reputation for finishing second surged from the back of the pack to snatch the Satsuki Shō by a neck, announcing himself as the third point of a rivalry that would define a generation of Japanese racing. Narita Taishin was not the flashiest or the most consistent, but his late charges and his battles with Biwa Hayahide and Winning Ticket—together known as the 'BNW' trio—captured the imagination of a nation. Decades later, his legacy found a new life as a prickly but endearing character in the multimedia phenomenon Umamusume: Pretty Derby, proving that even a horse who never won the Triple Crown could become an enduring icon.

In real life, Narita Taishin was known for his late-closing racing style, often coming from behind. In Umamusume, the character is portrayed as cold and prickly due to being teased for her small stature, but she is deeply insecure about it and puts her all into racing out of spite.

Powers
Late-closing speed

Narita Taishin was known for his ability to make a strong charge in the final stretch, often coming from behind to win or place.

Limit: This style was not always effective, as seen in the Tokyo Yushun and Kikuka Sho where he failed to close successfully.

Stamina

He was able to compete in long-distance races like the Kikuka Sho (3,000 meters), although his stamina was compromised by injury in that race.

Limit: Health issues, including a pulmonary hemorrhage and tendinitis, limited his stamina and career.

Fictional determination

In Umamusume, Taishin's determination is fueled by her insecurity about her small size, making her put her all into racing.

Limit: Fictional trait; not applicable to the real horse.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Narita Taishin was a chestnut Thoroughbred. In Umamusume, the character is depicted as a small-statured girl with chestnut hair, often portrayed in a racing uniform. The real horse's physical build is not described in detail in the source, but he was known for being relatively small compared to his rivals.

Symbols — In Umamusume, Narita Taishin is associated with a cold, prickly demeanor, but also with a determined spirit. Her racing silks are not described in the source, but in the franchise, she wears the colors of the real horse's owner.

Domains — Narita Taishin raced at various Japanese racetracks, including Sapporo, Fukushima, Hanshin, and Tokyo. After retirement, he lived at the JRA's Hidaka Training and Research Center in Hokkaido.

Accounts I
1993 Satsuki Shō VictoryProvided source material

1993

In the first leg of the Japanese Triple Crown, Narita Taishin stayed near the back for most of the race, then charged through the field in the final straight to narrowly defeat Biwa Hayahide by a neck, securing his first Grade I victory. Narita Taishin became a contender for the Triple Crown, and the rivalry with Biwa Hayahide and Winning Ticket intensified, leading to the 'BNW' label.

1993 Tokyo Yūshun (Japanese Derby)Provided source material

1993

In the second leg, Narita Taishin again entered as the third favorite. He was unable to complete his charge through the field and finished third behind Winning Ticket and Biwa Hayahide. Narita Taishin's Triple Crown hopes were dashed, and he was subsequently given a reduced schedule to prepare for the Kikuka Sho.

Accounts II
1993 Kikuka Sho FailureProvided source material

1993

After suffering a pulmonary hemorrhage during training, Narita Taishin was entered in the Kikuka Sho but was not competitive, finishing 17th out of 18, second to last. Narita Taishin's Triple Crown campaign ended in failure, and he was sidelined for a long recovery.

1994 Meguro Kinen Victory and Tenno Sho SecondProvided source material

1994

Narita Taishin began his four-year-old season with a victory at the Meguro Kinen, then finished second in the Tenno Sho, 1.25 lengths behind Biwa Hayahide. This promising start was cut short by a fracture in his right-hind leg, followed by intestinal issues and tendinitis, leading to a long layoff.

Failed Comeback and RetirementProvided source material

1995

After over 13 months off, Narita Taishin returned in the 1995 Takarazuka Kinen but finished 16th of 17. He was being prepared for the Takamatsunomiya Kinen when tendinitis recurred, leading to his retirement. Narita Taishin retired to stud in 1995, but his stud career was underwhelming, and he was retired from stud in 2003.
