The Approach

On a spring afternoon in 1993, a chestnut colt with a reputation for finishing second surged from the back of the pack to snatch the Satsuki Shō by a neck, announcing himself as the third point of a rivalry that would define a generation of Japanese racing. Narita Taishin was not the flashiest or the most consistent, but his late charges and his battles with Biwa Hayahide and Winning Ticket—together known as the 'BNW' trio—captured the imagination of a nation. Decades later, his legacy found a new life as a prickly but endearing character in the multimedia phenomenon Umamusume: Pretty Derby, proving that even a horse who never won the Triple Crown could become an enduring icon.

Identity

Narita Taishin

Pronunciation Nah-ree-tah Tah-ee-sheen (Japanese: ナリタタイシン)

Etymology

Narita Taishin (ナリタタイシン) is a Japanese name. 'Narita' is a common prefix for horses owned by Narita Farm, and 'Taishin' may be a personal name or a combination of characters with auspicious meanings, but the exact etymology is not documented in the provided sources.

Cultural Ownership

Japan (Japanese Thoroughbred racing)

Disputed Identifications

None. There is no known dispute over the horse's identity. In the Umamusume franchise, the character is explicitly based on the real horse, but it is a fictionalized anthropomorphized version.

Description

Appearance

Narita Taishin was a chestnut Thoroughbred. In Umamusume, the character is depicted as a small-statured girl with chestnut hair, often portrayed in a racing uniform. The real horse's physical build is not described in detail in the source, but he was known for being relatively small compared to his rivals.

Symbols & Iconography

In Umamusume, Narita Taishin is associated with a cold, prickly demeanor, but also with a determined spirit. Her racing silks are not described in the source, but in the franchise, she wears the colors of the real horse's owner.

Behaviour & Personality

In real life, Narita Taishin was known for his late-closing racing style, often coming from behind. In Umamusume, the character is portrayed as cold and prickly due to being teased for her small stature, but she is deeply insecure about it and puts her all into racing out of spite.

Domains & Habitats

Narita Taishin raced at various Japanese racetracks, including Sapporo, Fukushima, Hanshin, and Tokyo. After retirement, he lived at the JRA's Hidaka Training and Research Center in Hokkaido.

Version Differences

The real horse is a male Thoroughbred, while the Umamusume character is an anthropomorphized female. The character's personality is a fictional interpretation, though it draws on the horse's racing history and the BNW rivalry.

Stories & Myths

1993 Satsuki Shō Victory

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1993

Summary

In the first leg of the Japanese Triple Crown, Narita Taishin stayed near the back for most of the race, then charged through the field in the final straight to narrowly defeat Biwa Hayahide by a neck, securing his first Grade I victory.

Participants

Narita Taishin

Biwa Hayahide

Winning Ticket (third favorite)

Yutaka Take (jockey)

Consequences

Narita Taishin became a contender for the Triple Crown, and the rivalry with Biwa Hayahide and Winning Ticket intensified, leading to the 'BNW' label.

Variants

None.

1993 Tokyo Yūshun (Japanese Derby)

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1993

Summary

In the second leg, Narita Taishin again entered as the third favorite. He was unable to complete his charge through the field and finished third behind Winning Ticket and Biwa Hayahide.

Participants

Narita Taishin

Winning Ticket (winner)

Biwa Hayahide (second)

Consequences

Narita Taishin's Triple Crown hopes were dashed, and he was subsequently given a reduced schedule to prepare for the Kikuka Sho.

1993 Kikuka Sho Failure

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1993

Summary

After suffering a pulmonary hemorrhage during training, Narita Taishin was entered in the Kikuka Sho but was not competitive, finishing 17th out of 18, second to last.

Participants

Narita Taishin

Consequences

Narita Taishin's Triple Crown campaign ended in failure, and he was sidelined for a long recovery.

1994 Meguro Kinen Victory and Tenno Sho Second

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1994

Summary

Narita Taishin began his four-year-old season with a victory at the Meguro Kinen, then finished second in the Tenno Sho, 1.25 lengths behind Biwa Hayahide.

Participants

Narita Taishin

Biwa Hayahide

Consequences

This promising start was cut short by a fracture in his right-hind leg, followed by intestinal issues and tendinitis, leading to a long layoff.

Variants

Failed Comeback and Retirement

Source Provided source materialCulture Japanese horse racingPeriod 1995

Summary

After over 13 months off, Narita Taishin returned in the 1995 Takarazuka Kinen but finished 16th of 17. He was being prepared for the Takamatsunomiya Kinen when tendinitis recurred, leading to his retirement.

Participants

Narita Taishin

Consequences

Narita Taishin retired to stud in 1995, but his stud career was underwhelming, and he was retired from stud in 2003.

Variants
