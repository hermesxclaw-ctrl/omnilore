The Approach

In the rugged highlands of Arcadia, where myth and geography intertwine, there lived a prince whose name was etched into the very landscape—Thyraeus, son of the infamous Lycaon, and founder of a city that bore his name. Though history remembers him only in fragments, his legacy endures in the names of places that still echo his story.

Stories & Myths

Founding of Thyraeum

Source Pausanias, Description of Greece; possibly Dionysius of HalicarnassusCulture Greek (Arcadian)Period Mythical (pre-Trojan War era)

Summary

Thyraeus, one of the fifty sons of King Lycaon, was the reputed eponymous founder of the Arcadian city of Thyraeum. The Arcadians also believed that Thyrea in Argolis and the Thyrean Gulf were named after him.

Participants

Thyraeus

Consequences

The city Thyraeum and the place-names Thyrea and Thyrean Gulf were established.

Variants

None recorded; the story is consistent across sources.
