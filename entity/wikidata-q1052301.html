Who It Is

Imagine the horror of aging without end: a body that shrinks, wrinkles, and withers, yet never dies. In Greek myth, this was the fate of Tithonus, and the personification of that relentless decay was Geras—a god so old he is depicted as a tiny, shrunken man, often shown being defeated by the youthful hero Heracles. Yet Geras was not merely a figure of dread; in the ancient world, the word 'geras' also meant the honor and spoils won in battle, revealing a complex deity who embodied both the frailty of age and the rewards of glory.

In the lost myth with Heracles, Geras is depicted begging for mercy, suggesting a weak and pitiable character, easily subdued by the hero. He embodies the frailty and decline of old age.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The Greek word γῆρας (gēras) means 'old age' or 'dead skin' or 'slough of a snake'. It is the root of English words like 'geriatric' and 'progeria'.

Personification of Old Age

Geras embodies the process of aging, bringing wrinkles, weakness, and eventual decay to mortals. He is the antithesis of Hebe, goddess of youth.

Limit: He does not cause death directly, but rather the physical decline that precedes it. His power is inevitable but slow.

Granting of 'Geras' (Honor/Prize)

In a metaphorical sense, 'geras' as a concept could be distributed as spoils of war, representing honor and status. Geras as a deity might be seen as the dispenser of such honors, though this is not explicitly stated in myth.

Limit: This is an interpretation of the word's usage, not an attested power of the deity.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Geras is typically depicted as a tiny, shriveled old man, bald and wrinkled. In vase paintings, he is shown as a small, bent figure, often with a walking stick, contrasting with the muscular, youthful Heracles.

Symbols — Geras is associated with old age and decay. In art, he may be shown with a staff or leaning on a stick. His small stature emphasizes his weakness compared to heroes.

Domains — As a personification, Geras has no specific realm or habitat. He exists wherever old age occurs. In art, he appears in scenes with Heracles, but his domain is conceptual.

Geras and HeraclesAncient Greek vase paintings

Archaic/Classical (c. 6th-5th century BCE)

Geras is depicted in combat with Heracles, who appears to be defeating him. Geras is shown as a small, old, wrinkled man, often begging for mercy. The specific myth behind this scene is lost. The story is unknown, but the imagery suggests a triumph over old age by the hero.

Accounts II
Tithonus and EosHomeric Hymn to Aphrodite (lines 218-238)

c. 7th century BCE

Tithonus, a mortal prince, is granted immortality by Zeus but not eternal youth. As he ages, he shrinks and withers, eventually being turned into a cicada by Eos. This story illustrates the horror of aging without death, which is Geras's domain. Tithonus becomes a cicada, symbolizing the endless chatter of old age.
