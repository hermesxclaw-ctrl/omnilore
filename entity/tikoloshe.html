Who It Is

Imagine a creature so small it can hide under a bed, yet so feared that entire communities raise their sleeping platforms to keep it at bay. The Tikoloshe is not just a myth; it is a living presence in the minds of many South Africans, a reminder of the unseen forces that shape our fears and beliefs.

The Tikoloshe is inherently mischievous and malevolent, deriving pleasure from causing fear and chaos. It is often summoned by witches or sorcerers to carry out malicious tasks, such as frightening children, causing illness, or even bringing about death. The spirit is cunning and deceptive, using its ability to become invisible to stalk its victims undetected. It is also known to be sexually aggressive, with stories of it raping women or seducing them in their sleep, leading to nightmares and distress. Powers
Invisibility

The Tikoloshe can become invisible by drinking water or swallowing a stone. This ability allows it to move unseen among humans, making it a formidable and terrifying presence. It is said that the creature uses this power to stalk its victims, appearing suddenly to cause fright or harm.

Limit:

Shape-shifting

In some accounts, the Tikoloshe can change its form, taking on the appearance of a child, an animal, or even a shadow. This ability adds to its deceptive nature, allowing it to approach victims without suspicion.

Limit:

Causing illness and death

The Tikoloshe is believed to have the power to inflict sickness or even death upon those it targets. This is often done at the behest of a witch or sorcerer, but the spirit may also act independently if provoked or offended.

Limit:

Sexual assault

The Tikoloshe is said to be capable of sexually assaulting individuals, particularly women, during their sleep. This is a deeply disturbing aspect of the legend, and it is often cited as a reason for the fear and caution surrounding the spirit.

The Tikoloshe and the Sleeping WomanOral tradition, as recorded in South African folklore collections

Pre-colonial to present

A woman in a rural village begins to suffer from nightmares and a feeling of being watched at night. She consults a sangoma, who reveals that a tikoloshe has been sent by a jealous rival to torment her. The sangoma advises her to raise her bed on bricks and to place a charm under her pillow. The woman follows the advice, and the tikoloshe is unable to reach her, eventually leaving her in peace. The story serves as a cautionary tale about the dangers of envy and the importance of seeking spiritual protection. The woman is freed from the tikoloshe's torment, and the rival's scheme is thwarted. The story reinforces the belief in the tikoloshe's power and the efficacy of traditional remedies.

Accounts II
The Tikoloshe and the Greedy ChiefRecorded in ethnographic studies of Nguni folklore

19th century

A chief becomes greedy and begins to hoard the village's cattle, ignoring the needs of his people. A mysterious dwarf appears, claiming to be a water spirit, and offers to help the chief increase his wealth in exchange for a sacrifice. The chief agrees, but the dwarf, who is actually a tikoloshe, tricks him into giving up his own son. The chief's son is taken to the river, and the tikoloshe disappears, leaving the chief with nothing. The story is a moral lesson about greed and the dangers of making deals with malevolent spirits. The chief loses his son and his wealth, and the village suffers as a result. The story warns against the pursuit of material gain at the expense of family and community.

The Tikoloshe and the Witch's FamiliarContemporary retelling in South African horror fiction

21st century

A young man in a modern township discovers that his neighbor is a witch who keeps a tikoloshe as a familiar. The tikoloshe is used to steal from other homes and to curse those who offend the witch. The young man, with the help of a pastor, manages to banish the tikoloshe by reciting prayers and burning protective herbs. The witch is exposed and driven out of the community. This story reflects the ongoing belief in the tikoloshe in urban settings and the blending of traditional and Christian practices. The witch is ostracized, and the tikoloshe is banished, restoring peace to the community. The story highlights the role of religious leaders in combating supernatural threats.
