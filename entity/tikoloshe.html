Who It Is

The Tikoloshe (Tokoloshe) is one of the most feared supernatural beings in southern African folklore, believed to be a malevolent dwarf-like water spirit created by witches to torment their enemies. Standing about three feet tall with a grotesque appearance — sometimes described as having only one leg, a single buttock, and a disproportionately large head and penis — the Tikoloshe is invisible to most people but can be seen by children and the spiritually gifted. Its primary methods of harm include choking sleeping victims, causing illness, stealing, and engaging in sexual assault. To protect against a Tikoloshe, people in Zulu, Xhosa, and other southern African cultures traditionally raise their beds on bricks, believing the creature is too short to climb up, or place a stone, knife, or Bible beneath the pillow. The Tikoloshe has become a staple of South African popular culture, appearing in novels, films, and television. Journalistic reports from rural areas still document cases of communities attributing mysterious deaths and afflictions to Tikoloshe attacks, demonstrating its enduring presence in the living folk belief system. It occupies a liminal space between demon, witch-familiar, and prankster spirit.
