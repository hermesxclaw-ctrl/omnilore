The Approach

In the vast pantheon of Chinese cosmology, few names carry as many meanings as Taiyi — a single term that has signified the Supreme One, a star, a mountain, a goddess, and even an acupuncture point. This dossier untangles the many threads of Taiyi, tracing its evolution from ancient creation myths to modern astronomical nomenclature.

Content advisory. No dark material found. Some religious concepts may be considered sacred by practitioners; handle with respect.
Identity

Taiyi (太一 / 太乙)

Pronunciation In Mandarin: Tài Yī (太一) or Tài Yǐ (太乙). In English, often rendered as 'Tai-yi' or 'Taiyi'.

Known As

Tai YiTai-yiDonghuang TaiyiTaiyi ZhenrenTaiyi ShengshuiTàiyī YuánjūnTaiyi MountainsTai Yi ShanTaiyi (star)

Etymology

The term 'Taiyi' (太一) literally means 'Great One' or 'Supreme One' in Chinese. The character 太 (tài) means 'great' or 'supreme', and 一 (yī) means 'one'. The variant 太乙 (tàiyǐ) uses 乙 (yǐ), which is the second Heavenly Stem, possibly a phonetic loan or representing a different conceptual nuance. The name has been used in various contexts, from a supreme deity to a geographical feature.

Cultural Ownership

The term 'Taiyi' originates from ancient Chinese culture, specifically within Taoism and Chinese folk religion. It is also used in Chinese geography and modern astronomy, reflecting its deep integration into Chinese intellectual history.

Disputed Identifications

The name 'Taiyi' is highly polysemous. It can refer to a supreme deity, a Taoist concept, a goddess, a star, a mountain, and other entities. There is no single 'true' Taiyi; rather, the name has been applied to various concepts across different contexts. Some scholars debate the relationship between the deity Taiyi and the philosophical concept of the 'Great One' in Taoist cosmology.

Description

Appearance

The appearance of Taiyi varies depending on the context. As a deity, Taiyi is often depicted as a majestic figure, sometimes with a crown and imperial robes, symbolizing his supreme status. In some Taoist iconography, Taiyi Zhenren appears as a scholar or immortal. The goddess Tàiyī Yuánjūn (Doumu) is depicted as a mother goddess with multiple arms and eyes. The star Taiyi is a point of light in the constellation Draco.

Symbols & Iconography

Symbols associated with Taiyi include the number one, representing unity and the origin of all things. In Taoist cosmology, Taiyi is often linked with the concept of the Dao. As a deity, Taiyi may be associated with the Pole Star or the center of the cosmos. In acupuncture, the Taiyi point (ST-23) is on the stomach meridian.

Behaviour & Personality

As a supreme deity, Taiyi is considered the ultimate source of creation and order. In the Taiyi Shengshui myth, Taiyi generates water and then the cosmos, suggesting a generative and creative nature. In later Taoist traditions, Taiyi Zhenren is a compassionate savior who aids the faithful. The goddess Doumu is a protective mother figure.

Domains & Habitats

Taiyi's domains include the celestial realm, where he resides as a supreme deity. The Taiyi Mountains are located in Shaanxi Province, China. The star Taiyi is in the constellation Draco. The acupuncture point Taiyi is located on the human body.

Version Differences

The term 'Taiyi' has multiple distinct referents. In ancient Chinese religion, Taiyi was a supreme deity worshipped by emperors. In Taoist philosophy, Taiyi is the 'Great One' from which all things arise. In later Taoism, Taiyi Zhenren and Taiyi Yuanjun are specific deities. Additionally, Taiyi is a geographical name and an astronomical name. These versions should not be conflated.

Powers & Abilities

Cosmic Creation

Source Version Taiyi Shengshui (c. 300 BC)

Description

In the Taiyi Shengshui text, Taiyi generates water, which then leads to the creation of heaven and earth. This power is fundamental to the cosmogonic myth.

Limitations

This power is described in a specific mythological context and is not attributed to all forms of Taiyi.

Contradictions

Other creation myths in Taoism attribute creation to the Dao or other deities, so Taiyi's role is not universally accepted.

Supreme Divinity

Source Version Han dynasty religion

Description

As a supreme deity, Taiyi was worshipped as the highest god, particularly during the Han dynasty. He was believed to control the cosmos and human affairs.

Limitations

The worship of Taiyi as supreme deity was partly superseded by other deities in later periods.

Contradictions

Some scholars argue that Taiyi was not originally a deity but a philosophical concept that later became personified.

Salvation and Healing

Source Version Taoist traditions, medieval and later

Description

In later Taoism, Taiyi Zhenren and Taiyi Yuanjun are associated with healing, saving souls, and protecting the faithful.

Limitations

These powers are specific to the deities Taiyi Zhenren and Taiyi Yuanjun, not to the philosophical concept.

Contradictions

The roles of different Taiyi deities vary, and some are not clearly distinguished in popular practice.

Stories & Myths

Taiyi Shengshui (The Great One Gives Birth to Water)

Source Guodian Chu bamboo slipsCulture Taoist / ChinesePeriod c. 300 BC

Summary

The text describes how Taiyi (the Great One) first generated water, and from water, heaven and earth came into being. The process continues to explain the generation of all things, emphasizing the cyclical nature of creation.

Participants

Taiyi

Water

Heaven

Earth

Consequences

This myth establishes Taiyi as a primordial creative force in Taoist cosmology.

Variants

Other Taoist creation myths, such as those involving Pangu, offer different cosmogonies.

Imperial Worship of Taiyi

Source Han dynasty historical records (Shiji, Hanshu)Culture Chinese imperial cultPeriod Han dynasty (206 BC – 220 AD)

Summary

Emperors of the Han dynasty, particularly Emperor Wu, performed grand sacrifices to Taiyi as the supreme deity. The worship included elaborate rituals at designated altars.

Participants

Emperor Wu

Court priests

Taiyi

Consequences

The imperial cult elevated Taiyi to a national deity, but also led to political and religious controversies.

Variants

The worship of Taiyi was not continuous; later dynasties favored other deities.

Taiyi Zhenren in Folklore

Source Taoist hagiographies and folk talesCulture Taoist / Chinese folk religionPeriod Medieval and later

Summary

Taiyi Zhenren is a Taoist deity who often appears as a savior, healing the sick, exorcising demons, and guiding souls. He is sometimes identified with the star Taiyi.

Participants

Taiyi Zhenren

Devotees

Demons

Consequences

Taiyi Zhenren became a popular object of worship, particularly in southern China.

Variants

Taiyi Zhenren is sometimes conflated with other deities like Taiyi Yuanjun.

Relationships

Doumu (Tàiyī Yuánjūn)

Type Alternative identitySource Taoist texts and popular religion

Description

Tàiyī Yuánjūn, also known as Doumu, is a goddess in Chinese religion and Taoism. She is sometimes identified as a form of Taiyi, especially in her role as a mother goddess.

Version Note

The identification is not universal; some traditions treat them as separate deities.

Conflicting Accounts

Some sources distinguish between Taiyi (male) and Doumu (female), while others see them as aspects of the same cosmic principle.

Taiyi Shengshui

Type Mythological conceptSource Guodian texts

Description

Taiyi Shengshui is both a deity and a creation myth. As a concept, Taiyi is the primordial source.

Version Note

This is an ancient concept that predates many later Taoist deities.

Conflicting Accounts

The relationship between Taiyi Shengshui and later Taoist deities is not clearly defined.

Mount Taibai

Type Geographical namesakeSource Historical geographical texts

Description

Mount Taibai in Shaanxi was historically called Tai Yi. The mountain is a sacred Taoist site.

Version Note

The name 'Taiyi' for the mountain is ancient.

Conflicting Accounts

None.

Star 8 Draconis

Type Astronomical namesakeSource IAU Working Group on Star Names

Description

The star 8 Draconis is named 'Taiyi' by the IAU, reflecting the historical Chinese association of the name with a celestial body.

Version Note

The star name was officially approved in 2017.

Conflicting Accounts

The historical Chinese star Taiyi may refer to a different star; the IAU designation is a modern naming.
