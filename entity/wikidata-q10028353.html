Who It Is

In the vast pantheon of Chinese cosmology, few names carry as many meanings as Taiyi — a single term that has signified the Supreme One, a star, a mountain, a goddess, and even an acupuncture point. This dossier untangles the many threads of Taiyi, tracing its evolution from ancient creation myths to modern astronomical nomenclature.

As a supreme deity, Taiyi is considered the ultimate source of creation and order. In the Taiyi Shengshui myth, Taiyi generates water and then the cosmos, suggesting a generative and creative nature. In later Taoist traditions, Taiyi Zhenren is a compassionate savior who aids the faithful. The goddess Doumu is a protective mother figure.

Powers
Cosmic Creation

In the Taiyi Shengshui text, Taiyi generates water, which then leads to the creation of heaven and earth. This power is fundamental to the cosmogonic myth.

Limit: This power is described in a specific mythological context and is not attributed to all forms of Taiyi.

Supreme Divinity

As a supreme deity, Taiyi was worshipped as the highest god, particularly during the Han dynasty. He was believed to control the cosmos and human affairs.

Limit: The worship of Taiyi as supreme deity was partly superseded by other deities in later periods.

Salvation and Healing

In later Taoism, Taiyi Zhenren and Taiyi Yuanjun are associated with healing, saving souls, and protecting the faithful.

Limit: These powers are specific to the deities Taiyi Zhenren and Taiyi Yuanjun, not to the philosophical concept.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The appearance of Taiyi varies depending on the context. As a deity, Taiyi is often depicted as a majestic figure, sometimes with a crown and imperial robes, symbolizing his supreme status. In some Taoist iconography, Taiyi Zhenren appears as a scholar or immortal. The goddess Tàiyī Yuánjūn (Doumu) is depicted as a mother goddess with multiple arms and eyes. The star Taiyi is a point of light in the constellation Draco.

Symbols — Symbols associated with Taiyi include the number one, representing unity and the origin of all things. In Taoist cosmology, Taiyi is often linked with the concept of the Dao. As a deity, Taiyi may be associated with the Pole Star or the center of the cosmos. In acupuncture, the Taiyi point (ST-23) is on the stomach meridian.

Domains — Taiyi's domains include the celestial realm, where he resides as a supreme deity. The Taiyi Mountains are located in Shaanxi Province, China. The star Taiyi is in the constellation Draco. The acupuncture point Taiyi is located on the human body.

Accounts I
Taiyi Shengshui (The Great One Gives Birth to Water)Guodian Chu bamboo slips

c. 300 BC

The text describes how Taiyi (the Great One) first generated water, and from water, heaven and earth came into being. The process continues to explain the generation of all things, emphasizing the cyclical nature of creation. This myth establishes Taiyi as a primordial creative force in Taoist cosmology.

Accounts II
Imperial Worship of TaiyiHan dynasty historical records (Shiji, Hanshu)

Han dynasty (206 BC – 220 AD)

Emperors of the Han dynasty, particularly Emperor Wu, performed grand sacrifices to Taiyi as the supreme deity. The worship included elaborate rituals at designated altars. The imperial cult elevated Taiyi to a national deity, but also led to political and religious controversies.

Taiyi Zhenren in FolkloreTaoist hagiographies and folk tales

Medieval and later

Taiyi Zhenren is a Taoist deity who often appears as a savior, healing the sick, exorcising demons, and guiding souls. He is sometimes identified with the star Taiyi. Taiyi Zhenren became a popular object of worship, particularly in southern China.
