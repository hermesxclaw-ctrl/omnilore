Who It Is

In the world of Uma Musume, Silence Suzuka is the embodiment of free-spirited speed—a quiet girl who finds her voice in the thunder of hooves. But behind the anime's graceful stride lies the true story of a racehorse whose final race ended in tragedy, leaving a legacy that still gallops through the hearts of fans.

In Uma Musume, Silence Suzuka is portrayed as quiet, introspective, and deeply passionate about running. She is shown as a reserved but kind character who expresses herself through racing. She is known for her love of 'running freely' and her philosophical approach to speed. She is also depicted as having a strong rivalry and friendship with other characters like Tokai Teio.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In Uma Musume, Silence Suzuka is depicted as a tall, slender girl with long, flowing silver-white hair and red eyes. She wears a racing uniform inspired by the real horse's racing colors (white and red). Her official character design emphasizes a calm, composed demeanor, often shown with a gentle smile.

Symbols — Her symbol is often a stylized representation of a horse's silhouette, and her racing number is typically 1 or 2. The color scheme of white and red is associated with the real horse's owner, Symboli Stud. In the franchise, she is often depicted with a motif of wind or speed, reflecting her running style.

Domains — The character primarily exists in the fictional world of Uma Musume, which is set in a modern Japan where horse girls train and race. Her 'habitat' is the training center, racetrack, and dormitory. The real horse's domain was the racetracks of Japan, primarily in the mid-1990s.

Accounts I
Real Racing CareerJRA records, contemporary news reports

1994–1998

Silence Suzuka was a Thoroughbred racehorse who won several Grade races in Japan, including the Takarazuka Kinen in 1998. He was known for his strong front-running style. In 1998, during a race at Kyoto Racecourse, he suffered a fatal leg fracture and was euthanized. His death shocked the racing community and led to increased attention on horse safety. He was posthumously awarded the JRA Best Sprinter or Miler? Actually, he won the JRA Award for Best Older Male Horse in 1998.
