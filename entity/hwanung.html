Who It Is

Hwanung is the celestial prince of Korean mythology, the son of Hwanin (the Lord of Heaven) and the father of Dangun, the founder of Gojoseon. His myth embodies the archetype of the divine civilizer—a heavenly being who voluntarily descends to the mortal realm to establish order, law, and culture among humanity. According to the Samguk Yusa, Hwanung looked down from heaven and was moved by the suffering of the human world. He repeatedly petitioned his father, Hwanin, for permission to descend and govern the earth. Recognizing his son's compassion and capability, Hwanin examined the lands below and selected Mount Taebaek as the site for Hwanung's earthly domain. He sent Hwanung down with the Cheonbuin (three heavenly seals representing divine authority), along with 3,000 followers and the ministers of Wind, Rain, and Cloud. Hwanung descended beneath a sacred sandalwood tree on Taebaek and established the divine city of Sinsi (City of the Spirits). There, he governed with 360 specific responsibilities, teaching humanity agriculture (the growing of grains), medicine (the healing of diseases), law and morality (the distinction between good and evil), and the arts of civilization. He also commanded the spirits of wind, rain, and cloud to regulate the seasons. It was during his earthly reign that a bear and a tiger came to him, praying to become human, setting the stage for the birth of Dangun. After Dangun's birth and the establishment of Gojoseon, Hwanung eventually returned to heaven. His role as a divine civilizer closely parallels other East Asian culture heroes and reflects shamanistic concepts of heavenly descent and sacred kingship that were fundamental to early Korean polity formation.
