Who It Is

In the neon-lit underworld of South Town, one man commands respect not through titles but through sheer terror: Geese Howard, the crime lord who can stop a bullet with his bare hands and hurl a wave of cutting wind that tears through steel. His name is whispered in fear, his power is legend, and his story is one of betrayal, ambition, and a rivalry that burns across generations.

Geese is calculating, ruthless, and arrogant. He is a master manipulator who values power and control above all. He is also a martial arts prodigy, having mastered the ancient martial art of Hakkyokuseiken, which he uses to dominate his enemies. He shows no mercy to those who oppose him, but he has a twisted sense of honor, respecting only those who can challenge him. He is also a devoted father to his son Rock Howard, though his methods are harsh.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Geese' is likely chosen for its menacing, avian connotation, possibly referencing a predatory bird. 'Howard' is a common English surname, giving the character a Western, American feel. The alias 'Reppuken Master' refers to his signature technique, the Reppuken, a wave of cutting wind.

Pronunciation — GEESE HOW-erd

Aliases — Reppuken Master, The Crime Lord of South Town, The King of South Town, The Nightmare of South Town

Ownership — Geese Howard is a fictional character owned by SNK (formerly SNK Corporation). He is a central antagonist in the Fatal Fury series and a recurring character in The King of Fighters

Powers
Reppuken

A projectile technique that sends a crescent wave of energy across the ground. It is Geese's signature move and can be used in the air.

Limit: Can be jumped over or blocked. In some games, it has limited range.

Shippuken

A diving kick that Geese uses to strike opponents from the air.

Limit: Requires precise timing; can be punished if blocked.

Jakuho

A counter move that allows Geese to parry an opponent's attack and then strike them with a powerful palm thrust.

Limit: Must be timed correctly; does not work on throws.

Raging Storm

Geese's super move, where he unleashes a massive wave of energy that envelops the opponent, dealing heavy damage.

Limit: Requires a full power gauge; can be avoided by jumping or moving away.

Hakkyokuseiken

An ancient martial art that Geese mastered. It focuses on powerful strikes and throws, and is also used by his brother Krauser and his son Rock.

Limit: Requires intense training and discipline.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Geese Howard is a tall, imposing man in his mid-40s with slicked-back blond hair and a sharp, angular face. He typically wears a white suit with a black undershirt, a long black coat, and a golden pendant. His eyes are cold and piercing, and he often carries a cane, which he uses as a weapon. His look exudes wealth and power, befitting a crime lord.

Symbols — Geese is often associated with his tower, the Geese Tower, which symbolizes his dominance over South Town. His golden pendant is a recurring emblem. His signature pose, with arms crossed, is iconic. The Reppuken wave is visually depicted as a blue energy wave.

Domains — Geese rules South Town, a fictional American city, from his skyscraper, Geese Tower. He controls the city's criminal underworld, including gambling, smuggling, and extortion. He is also associated with the King of Fighters tournament, which he uses to lure strong fighters.

Accounts I
The Rise of Geese HowardFatal Fury: Wild Ambition (1999)

Before the first King of Fighters tournament

Geese Howard, a young martial artist, rises to power in South Town by defeating the local crime lord. He then kills Jeff Bogard, a martial artist who protected the city, to eliminate any opposition. This act sets the stage for Terry Bogard's revenge. Jeff Bogard's death leads Terry and Andy to seek revenge, becoming Geese's lifelong rivals.

The King of Fighters TournamentFatal Fury: King of Fighters (1991)

1991

Geese hosts a martial arts tournament to lure strong fighters and eliminate them. Terry Bogard enters and defeats Geese, causing him to fall from his tower. Geese is presumed dead. Geese's apparent death leaves South Town in chaos, but he later returns.

Accounts II
The Return of GeeseFatal Fury 2 (1992)

1992

Geese survives the fall and returns as a hidden character, revealing that he is the true mastermind behind the second King of Fighters tournament. He continues to antagonize Terry. Geese's survival solidifies his status as Terry's archenemy.

The Death of Geese HowardFatal Fury: Real Bout (1995)

1995

In the final battle, Terry defeats Geese again, and Geese falls from his tower, this time seemingly to his death. His body is never found, leaving room for his return. Geese's death allows his son Rock to be taken in by Terry.

Geese's Revival in King of FightersThe King of Fighters '96 (1996)

1996

Geese is resurrected by the mysterious power of the Orochi, and participates in the tournament as a sub-boss. He continues to appear in subsequent KOF games, often as a rival to Terry. Geese's presence in KOF expands his role beyond the Fatal Fury series.
