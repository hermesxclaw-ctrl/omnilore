The Approach

In the neon-lit underworld of South Town, one man commands respect not through titles but through sheer terror: Geese Howard, the crime lord who can stop a bullet with his bare hands and hurl a wave of cutting wind that tears through steel. His name is whispered in fear, his power is legend, and his story is one of betrayal, ambition, and a rivalry that burns across generations.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of violence, criminal activity, murder, and themes of vengeance and abuse. Some content may be disturbing to younger readers.

Etymology

The name 'Geese' is likely chosen for its menacing, avian connotation, possibly referencing a predatory bird. 'Howard' is a common English surname, giving the character a Western, American feel. The alias 'Reppuken Master' refers to his signature technique, the Reppuken, a wave of cutting wind.

Cultural Ownership

Geese Howard is a fictional character owned by SNK (formerly SNK Corporation). He is a central antagonist in the Fatal Fury series and a recurring character in The King of Fighters series.

Disputed Identifications

There are no known disputed identifications. However, some fans have speculated on his possible real-world inspirations, such as the crime lord from the film 'The Yakuza' or the character of Frank Castle, but these are unconfirmed.

Description

Appearance

Geese Howard is a tall, imposing man in his mid-40s with slicked-back blond hair and a sharp, angular face. He typically wears a white suit with a black undershirt, a long black coat, and a golden pendant. His eyes are cold and piercing, and he often carries a cane, which he uses as a weapon. His look exudes wealth and power, befitting a crime lord.

Symbols & Iconography

Geese is often associated with his tower, the Geese Tower, which symbolizes his dominance over South Town. His golden pendant is a recurring emblem. His signature pose, with arms crossed, is iconic. The Reppuken wave is visually depicted as a blue energy wave.

Behaviour & Personality

Geese is calculating, ruthless, and arrogant. He is a master manipulator who values power and control above all. He is also a martial arts prodigy, having mastered the ancient martial art of Hakkyokuseiken, which he uses to dominate his enemies. He shows no mercy to those who oppose him, but he has a twisted sense of honor, respecting only those who can challenge him. He is also a devoted father to his son Rock Howard, though his methods are harsh.

Domains & Habitats

Geese rules South Town, a fictional American city, from his skyscraper, Geese Tower. He controls the city's criminal underworld, including gambling, smuggling, and extortion. He is also associated with the King of Fighters tournament, which he uses to lure strong fighters.

Version Differences

In the original Fatal Fury, Geese is the final boss and is killed. In later games, he is revived without clear explanation, and his personality is consistent. In the KOF series, he is often a sub-boss or hidden character, and his role varies. In the anime film 'Fatal Fury: The Motion Picture' (1994), his backstory is expanded, and he is killed again, but the film's canonicity is debated.

Powers & Abilities

Reppuken

Source Version Fatal Fury series, King of Fighters series

Description

A projectile technique that sends a crescent wave of energy across the ground. It is Geese's signature move and can be used in the air.

Limitations

Can be jumped over or blocked. In some games, it has limited range.

Contradictions

In some games, the Reppuken is a ground wave; in others, it can be thrown in the air. The exact properties vary.

Shippuken

Source Version Fatal Fury series, King of Fighters series

Description

A diving kick that Geese uses to strike opponents from the air.

Limitations

Requires precise timing; can be punished if blocked.

Contradictions

None known.

Jakuho

Source Version Fatal Fury series, King of Fighters series

Description

A counter move that allows Geese to parry an opponent's attack and then strike them with a powerful palm thrust.

Limitations

Must be timed correctly; does not work on throws.

Contradictions

In some games, it is a command counter; in others, it is a special move.

Raging Storm

Source Version Fatal Fury series, King of Fighters series

Description

Geese's super move, where he unleashes a massive wave of energy that envelops the opponent, dealing heavy damage.

Limitations

Requires a full power gauge; can be avoided by jumping or moving away.

Contradictions

The exact appearance and damage vary across games.

Hakkyokuseiken

Source Version Fatal Fury series

Description

An ancient martial art that Geese mastered. It focuses on powerful strikes and throws, and is also used by his brother Krauser and his son Rock.

Limitations

Requires intense training and discipline.

Contradictions

The origin and extent of this art are not fully detailed.

Stories & Myths

The Rise of Geese Howard

Source Fatal Fury: Wild Ambition (1999)Culture Japanese video game lorePeriod Before the first King of Fighters tournament

Summary

Geese Howard, a young martial artist, rises to power in South Town by defeating the local crime lord. He then kills Jeff Bogard, a martial artist who protected the city, to eliminate any opposition. This act sets the stage for Terry Bogard's revenge.

Participants

Geese Howard

Jeff Bogard

Consequences

Jeff Bogard's death leads Terry and Andy to seek revenge, becoming Geese's lifelong rivals.

Variants

Some sources suggest Geese and Jeff were rivals, and the killing was a result of a duel.

The King of Fighters Tournament

Source Fatal Fury: King of Fighters (1991)Culture Japanese video game lorePeriod 1991

Summary

Geese hosts a martial arts tournament to lure strong fighters and eliminate them. Terry Bogard enters and defeats Geese, causing him to fall from his tower. Geese is presumed dead.

Participants

Geese Howard

Terry Bogard

Consequences

Geese's apparent death leaves South Town in chaos, but he later returns.

Variants

In the anime film, Geese is killed by Terry in a similar manner.

Contradictions & Disputes

Geese's death and revival

Position A

Geese dies in Fatal Fury: Real Bout and stays dead in the Fatal Fury timeline.

Position A — evidence

Fatal Fury: Real Bout ending shows him falling to his death.

Position B

Geese is revived in The King of Fighters series without clear explanation.

Position B — evidence

KOF '96 features Geese as a character, and later games include him.

Scholarly Consensus

The KOF series is a separate continuity from the Fatal Fury series, so Geese's death in one does not necessarily affect the other.
