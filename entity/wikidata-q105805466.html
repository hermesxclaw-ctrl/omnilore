The Approach

On a small Mediterranean island, a bay horse with a name meaning 'clover' became an unlikely global phenomenon, stringing together 56 consecutive victories—a world record that still stands—and proving that greatness can emerge from the most unexpected corners of the harness racing world.

Stories & Myths

Early career in Spain

Source WikipediaCulture SpanishPeriod 2009-2011

Summary

Trebol began racing at age two on Menorca, then moved to Mallorca. He won 17 of his first 23 starts, including a 13-race winning streak.

Participants

Trebol

Various Spanish competitors

Consequences

Established his talent and led to a move to France.

Variants

French campaign and first Group 1

Source WikipediaCulture FrenchPeriod 2011-2015

Summary

Shipped to France, he had modest success but earned significant purse money. In 2015, he finished second in the Prix Jean Rene Gougeon, then won the Kymi Grand Prix in Finland, becoming the first Spanish-bred trotter to win an international Group 1.

Second Kymi Grand Prix and later career

Source WikipediaCulture FinnishPeriod 2016-2017

Summary

Trebol won the Kymi Grand Prix again in 2016, and also won the Prix Luxembourg in France. After a winless year in 2017, he returned to Spain and won a race at San Pardo, intended as his final start.

Participants

Trebol

Consequences

Brief retirement, but he returned to racing.

Record-breaking winning streak

Source WikipediaCulture Spanish (Menorcan)Period 2018-2019

Summary

Returning to Menorca, Trebol began a winning streak that reached 56 consecutive victories, tying the world record set by Camarero. He eventually lost and retired with 59 wins in his last 61 starts.

Participants

Consequences
