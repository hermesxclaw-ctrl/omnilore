Ahool

Giant Bat of Java, Indonesian Flying Cryptid

A-hool! A-hool! The cry rings through the mountain forest at night. Then you see it -- a shadow against the moon, bigger than any bird, with wings that span the width of a road.

Who It Is

The Ahool is a cryptid reported from the dense rainforests of the Salak Mountains on the Indonesian island of Java. Its name derives from its distinctive, penetrating cry -- 'A-hool!' -- which echoes through the jungle canopy. Eyewitnesses describe a giant bat-like creature with a wingspan of 10 to 12 feet, a body the size of a large dog, dark gray fur, a flat, monkey-like face with large dark eyes, and clawed forearms. Unlike bats, the Ahool is said to roost on the forest floor in dense undergrowth rather than hanging upside-down. The first Western account came from naturalist Dr. Ernest Bartels in 1925, who heard and may have glimpsed the creature while exploring a waterfall near the Salak Mountains. His account sparked interest among cryptozoologists who noted its similarity to reports of the Kongamato in Africa and the Ropen in Papua New Guinea -- all described as large, flying creatures with reptilian or mammalian features. The Ahool is often proposed as a surviving population of pterosaur (the 'living fossil' theory popular in cryptozoology) or, more plausibly, an oversized species of bat -- possibly a giant relative of the flying fox (Pteropus vampyrus) which already has the largest wingspan of any bat at up to 5 feet. The 12-foot wingspan claimed for the Ahool would be extraordinary but not impossible for an undiscovered megabat species. Skeptics attribute sightings to misidentified large owls, such as the Javan wood owl, or exaggerated observations of known flying foxes seen in poor light. The creature remains one of Asia's most intriguing but least-investigated cryptids.
