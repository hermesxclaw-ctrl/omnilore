Who It Is

Ngenechen (also Ngenechén or Ngenemapun) is the supreme creator deity of the Mapuche people, the indigenous nation of southern Chile and Argentina that successfully resisted both Inca expansion and Spanish colonization for over 300 years — one of the longest sustained indigenous resistances to European colonialism in the Americas. Ngenechen is understood as a fourfold being: Old Man, Old Woman, Young Man, Young Woman — a divine quadrinity that represents the completeness of creation, youth and age, male and female, all simultaneously present in the divine nature. This sophisticated theological conception has drawn comparisons to Christian Trinitarian thought while being entirely indigenous in origin. Ngenechen created the cosmos, the Mapuche people, and established the admapu (the Mapuche moral and customary law that governs all social relations). In the Mapuche worldview, the cosmos is divided into the Wenu Mapu (the sky/upper world, Ngenechen's realm), the Nag Mapu (the earth where humans live), and the Minche Mapu (the underworld). Ngenechen is invoked in the nguillatún — the most important Mapuche ceremony, a multi-day communal ritual of prayer, dance, and animal sacrifice that petitions Ngenechen for rain, fertility, health, and protection. This ceremony is still actively practiced today. Unlike the remote supreme beings of many traditions, Ngenechen is approached directly by the Mapuche through the machi (shaman), who serves as a mediator between the human and divine realms. The endurance of Ngenechen worship, despite centuries of warfare, forced Christianization, and state repression, testifies to the resilience of Mapuche spirituality and identity.

Attested in Oral tradition / recorded by Jesuit missionaries and later ethnographersEra Pre-colonial to present; one of the few indigenous peoples never fully conquered by Inca or Spaniard
