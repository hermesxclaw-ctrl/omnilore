The Approach

In the world of The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker, a small boat with a crimson sail and a lion's prow carries a boy named Link across a vast, sunken ocean. But this is no ordinary vessel: it is the King of Red Lions, the spirit of the last king of Hyrule, guiding Link on a quest to save his sister and challenge the power of the gods themselves.

Content advisory. None. The character appears in a family-friendly video game with mild fantasy violence.

Etymology

The name 'King of Red Lions' derives from the boat's appearance: a red lion figurehead. In Japanese, it is 赤いライオンの王 (Akai Raion no Ō), meaning 'King of Red Lions'.

Cultural Ownership

Nintendo, specifically the Zelda series created by Shigeru Miyamoto and Takashi Tezuka. The character is a fictional creation of the Zelda team, directed by Eiji Aonuma for The Wind Waker.

Disputed Identifications

None known. The character is explicitly revealed to be King Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule in the game's climax.

Description

Appearance

The King of Red Lions is a small, wooden sailboat with a red lion figurehead at the prow. The lion's head is carved with a regal mane, and the boat has a single mast with a red sail. The boat is large enough for Link to stand on and pilot, and it can traverse the Great Sea. In its true form, King Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule is an elderly man with white hair and a beard, wearing royal robes and a crown.

Symbols & Iconography

The red lion is a symbol of royalty and courage, fitting for the king of Hyrule. The boat's red sail and lion figurehead evoke the Triforce of Courage and the royal family's emblem. The boat itself becomes a symbol of hope and guidance for Link.

Behaviour & Personality

The King of Red Lions is wise, solemn, and paternal. He speaks formally and often provides guidance to Link, though he is not overly talkative. He is deeply regretful of Hyrule's past and his own failures as a king, and he is determined to help Link save his sister and defeat Ganondorf. He is also critical of the gods' decision to flood Hyrule, viewing it as an overreaction.

Domains & Habitats

The King of Red Lions inhabits the Great Sea, a vast ocean that covers the flooded kingdom of Hyrule. He can sail anywhere on the sea, often appearing at key locations to assist Link. In his true form, he resides in the sunken Hyrule Castle.

Version Differences

In the original GameCube version, the King of Red Lions is fully voiced in English and Japanese. In the HD remake, his model and textures are updated, but his dialogue and role are unchanged. No other versions exist.

Powers & Abilities

Boat Sailing

Source Version All versions of The Wind Waker

Description

The King of Red Lions serves as Link's primary mode of transportation across the Great Sea. He can sail at high speeds, navigate through reefs, and withstand rough weather. He can also submerge to allow Link to explore underwater ruins.

Limitations

He cannot sail on land or in shallow water; he requires a certain depth of water.

Contradictions

None.

Communication

Source Version All versions

Description

The King of Red Lions can speak to Link telepathically or through direct speech, even when Link is away from the boat. He also appears to have knowledge of the ancient history of Hyrule and the Triforce.

Limitations

He cannot intervene directly in combat or physically protect Link beyond his role as a boat.

Contradictions

None.

Immortality/Spirit Form

Source Version Revealed at the end of the game

Description

As the spirit of the deceased King Daphnes, the King of Red Lions is bound to the boat. He cannot be killed, but he also cannot fully interact with the physical world except through the boat.

Limitations

His spirit is tied to the boat; if the boat were destroyed, his spirit might be dispersed, though this is not shown.

Contradictions

None.

Royal Authority

Source Version All versions

Description

As the king of Hyrule, he possesses the Triforce of Wisdom (in the form of a shard) and can command the sages. He also has knowledge of the Triforce's power and the ancient pact with the gods.

Limitations

His authority is largely symbolic; he cannot use the Triforce directly except in the final wish.

Contradictions

None.

Stories & Myths

The Flood of Hyrule and the King's Legacy

Source In-game lore, dialogue from the King of Red LionsCulture Fictional, Zelda seriesPeriod Before the events of The Wind Waker

Summary

Long ago, when Ganondorf threatened Hyrule, the gods chose to flood the land to seal him away. King Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule, the last king, was offered the chance to be sealed with the Triforce and the kingdom, but he chose to remain in the world as a spirit to guide the hero. He was bound to a boat, the King of Red Lions, to await the chosen hero.

Participants

King Daphnes Nohansen Hyrule

The gods

Ganondorf

Consequences

Hyrule is flooded, creating the Great Sea. The King of Red Lions becomes the guide for Link.

Variants

This backstory is only revealed in The Wind Waker; no other versions exist.

The Final Wish

Source The Legend of Zelda: The Wind WakerCulture Fictional, Zelda seriesPeriod Climax of the game

Summary

After defeating Ganondorf, Link and Zelda (Tetra) are about to wish on the Triforce. The King of Red Lions appears in his true form and wishes for Hyrule to be washed away, hoping to give Link and Zelda a future free from the past. He then fades away, leaving the Triforce to Link and Zelda.

The King of Red Lions is a separate entity from King Daphnes.
