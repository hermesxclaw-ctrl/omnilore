Who It Is

Before he turned thirty, Michelangelo had already carved two of the most famous sculptures in Western art—the Pietà and David—and yet he would spend the next six decades locked in a struggle with popes, patrons, and his own relentless perfectionism, leaving behind a body of work that redefined what art could be.

Michelangelo was known for his intense, solitary, and often irascible temperament. He was fiercely independent, proud, and driven by a relentless pursuit of artistic perfection. He was also deeply religious, and his faith influenced much of his work. He had few close friends, but formed deep, sometimes turbulent relationships with patrons and family.

Powers
Sculpture

Michelangelo was a master sculptor, capable of carving intricate, lifelike figures from marble. His works include the Pietà, David, and the Moses.

Limit: None documented; his skill was considered near-superhuman by his contemporaries.

Painting

Although he considered himself primarily a sculptor, Michelangelo was also a brilliant painter, as demonstrated by the Sistine Chapel ceiling and The Last Judgment.

Limit: He often worked reluctantly on painting commissions, and used assistants for some areas.

Architecture

Michelangelo designed the Laurentian Library, the Medici Chapel, and contributed to St. Peter's Basilica, innovating in architectural design.

Limit: Some projects were completed after his death with modifications.

Poetry

Michelangelo wrote over 300 poems, sonnets, and madrigals, which are now considered important literary works of the Renaissance.

Limit: His poetry was not widely known during his lifetime.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Michelangelo was of medium height, with a broad shoulders and a lean build. He had a distinctive broken nose, flattened by a blow from fellow sculptor Pietro Torrigiano in his youth. His face was deeply lined in later life, with a bushy beard and thick eyebrows. He often dressed plainly, sometimes in rough clothing, and was known for his intense, piercing gaze.

Symbols — Michelangelo's works often feature powerful, muscular figures, contorted poses, and dramatic use of light and shadow. Common symbols include the Christian cross, classical mythological figures, and biblical scenes. His signature is the 'non-finito' (unfinished) style, where blocks of marble are left partially carved.

Domains — Michelangelo was active primarily in Florence and Rome, but also worked in Bologna and Venice. He lived in Rome for most of his adult life, where he worked for a succession of popes.

The PietàSt. Peter's Basilica, Vatican

1498–1499

Michelangelo sculpted a moving depiction of Mary holding the dead Christ, a masterpiece of Renaissance sculpture. It established his fame as a sculptor.

DavidFlorence, Italy

1501–1504

Michelangelo sculpted a colossal statue of the biblical David, symbolizing the defense of Florence. The statue became a symbol of Florentine freedom and a masterpiece of Renaissance art.
