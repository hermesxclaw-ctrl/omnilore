Who It Is

The Bulette (pronounced boo-LAY or bull-ET) — universally known as the 'Land Shark' — is D&D's most famous burrowing predator and a creature of pure, aggressive hunger. Its body is a biological tank: a squat, heavily armored quadruped about 12 feet long and 7 feet tall at the shoulder, covered in thick, overlapping plates of blue-gray to blue-brown chitinous armor. Its head is almost entirely mouth — a snapping, beak-like jaw structure reminiscent of a snapping turtle crossed with a shark, capable of biting a fully armored knight in half. Its four clawed feet are massive, shovel-like appendages designed for digging through soil and loose rock at shocking speed. A Bulette cannot tunnel through solid stone, but through earth, sand, or gravel it 'swims' with terrifying velocity, emerging from the ground directly beneath its prey without warning.

The Bulette was one of D&D's earliest original monster creations, first appearing in Issue #1 of The Dragon magazine in 1976, where co-creator Tim Kask described it as a creature that 'lives only to eat.' Its behavior is straightforward: it senses vibrations through the ground (footsteps, hoofbeats, wagon wheels), burrows toward the source, erupts in an explosion of dirt and gravel, and attacks. If it kills prey, it drags the body underground to feed. If faced with organized resistance, it burrows away and circles around for another ambush — a tactic that makes hunting a Bulette a nerve-wracking game of cat and mouse across open terrain.

The Bulette is infamous for its 'deadly leap' — it can burst from the ground and land directly on targets with its full weight, crushing them under its armored bulk while simultaneously biting and clawing. Its armor is thick enough to repel most non-magical weapons, and its metabolism is so aggressive that a Bulette must consume its own body weight in fresh meat roughly every two weeks. When a Bulette enters a settled area, livestock vanish, farmers disappear, and the very ground becomes a threat.

The name 'Bulette' has a charmingly mundane origin: Tim Kask named it by adapting the French word for 'dumpling,' because the original plastic toy miniature used for the creature (like the Rust Monster and Owlbear before it) was a squat, lumpy figure that reminded him of a dumpling. Despite — or because of — this whimsical origin, the Bulette has become one of D&D's most recognizable and feared monsters, appearing across video games (Baldur's Gate series, Neverwinter), board games, and inspiring similar creatures in other fantasy properties. The term 'Land Shark' has become so synonymous with the Bulette that players often forget it's a nickname.
