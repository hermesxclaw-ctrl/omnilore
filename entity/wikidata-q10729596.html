Who It Is

In the heart of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, a city named Kamina stands as a crossroads of iron and air—where the continent's oldest railway ambitions meet the ghosts of Cold War airfields. But this is not the Kamina of anime legend; it is a real place, a capital of Haut-Lomami Province, whose story is written in steel rails, military bases, and a stadium that waited a decade for its moment. Here, the name 'Kamina' carries the weight of history, not fiction.

As a city, it functions as a transport and military hub. Its identity is shaped by its railway connections and military base.

Transportation Hub

Kamina is a major railway junction with three lines heading north, west, and south-east. It also has two airports, one civil and one military, and is connected by major roads (N1 and R630).

Limit: The railway lines may not be fully operational due to DRC's infrastructure challenges.

Military Base

The city hosts a large military base complex, historically used by Belgian forces and later the Congolese military. It serves as a 'brassage' centre for integrating former warring factions into the national army.

Accounts I
Belgian Military Base EstablishmentWikipedia (2026)

After World War II to 1960

The Belgian Armed Forces established a large military base in Kamina after World War II. It included Base 1 (air base for flying training) and Base 2 (paratroop training). From September 1953 to 1960, the Advanced Pilots' School of the Belgian Air Force operated about 60 North American Harvards from the base. The base became a significant military installation in the Belgian Congo, later handed over to the UN and then to the Congolese Armed Forces.

Accounts II
Transition to Independence and UN ControlWikipedia (2026)

1960-1964

When the Congo gained independence in June 1960, Belgium initially retained control of Kamina under agreement with the Congolese government. In October 1960, control was taken over by the United Nations. The base was never under Katangese control, although Katangese troops occupied the nearby town of Kaminaville. In early 1964, the UN handed over Kamina to the Congolese Armed Forces. The base became part of the Congolese military, later used for 'brassage' (integration of former warring factions).

Stade Joseph Kabila KabangeWikipedia (2026)

2008-2019

In 2019, the Governor of Haut-Lomami inaugurated the 'Stade Joseph Kabila Kabange', a modern sports arena. The stadium was started in 2008 with 2 million US dollars of funding from Katanga Province. After work halted for nearly a decade, the Governor announced the stadium was completed as a gift from then-President Joseph Kabila to the population of Haut-Lomami. The stadium provides a modern sports facility for the region.
