The Approach

In the heart of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, a city named Kamina stands as a crossroads of iron and air—where the continent's oldest railway ambitions meet the ghosts of Cold War airfields. But this is not the Kamina of anime legend; it is a real place, a capital of Haut-Lomami Province, whose story is written in steel rails, military bases, and a stadium that waited a decade for its moment. Here, the name 'Kamina' carries the weight of history, not fiction.

Content advisory. Contains references to colonial military presence, post-independence conflict, and the process of 'brassage' (military integration) in the DRC. No graphic violence described.

Description

Appearance

Kamina is a city in the Haut-Lomami Province of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It is an important railway node with two airports (civil and military). The city lies along National Road 1 (N1) and Regional Road 630 (R630). Specific urban appearance (buildings, layout) is not described in available sources.

Stories & Myths

Belgian Military Base Establishment

Source Wikipedia (2026)Culture Belgian colonial and post-colonialPeriod After World War II to 1960

Summary

The Belgian Armed Forces established a large military base in Kamina after World War II. It included Base 1 (air base for flying training) and Base 2 (paratroop training). From September 1953 to 1960, the Advanced Pilots' School of the Belgian Air Force operated about 60 North American Harvards from the base.

Participants

Consequences

The base became a significant military installation in the Belgian Congo, later handed over to the UN and then to the Congolese Armed Forces.

Transition to Independence and UN Control

Source Wikipedia (2026)Culture Congolese independence eraPeriod 1960-1964

Summary

When the Congo gained independence in June 1960, Belgium initially retained control of Kamina under agreement with the Congolese government. In October 1960, control was taken over by the United Nations. The base was never under Katangese control, although Katangese troops occupied the nearby town of Kaminaville. In early 1964, the UN handed over Kamina to the Congolese Armed Forces.

Stade Joseph Kabila Kabange

Source Wikipedia (2026)Culture Contemporary CongolesePeriod 2008-2019

Summary

In 2019, the Governor of Haut-Lomami inaugurated the 'Stade Joseph Kabila Kabange', a modern sports arena. The stadium was started in 2008 with 2 million US dollars of funding from Katanga Province. After work halted for nearly a decade, the Governor announced the stadium was completed as a gift from then-President Joseph Kabila to the population of Haut-Lomami.
