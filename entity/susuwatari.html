Who It Is

Also called Makkuro Kurosuke or soot sprites, these are small, pitch-black, fuzzy-haired spirits with two large eyes and long, thin limbs. They move by hovering and can lift objects many times their own weight. They appear in abandoned houses and dissolve into soot if crushed. They later work in Kamaji's boiler room in Spirited Away.

Powers

Hovering, object lifting, soot transformation

The Approach

In the dim corners of an old house, or the sooty depths of a bathhouse boiler room, tiny black spheres with enormous eyes scurry and squeak. These are the susuwatari—the 'wandering soot'—creatures born from the imagination of Hayao Miyazaki, who have charmed audiences worldwide while embodying a subtle folklore of domesticity, work, and the unseen life of dust.

Content advisory. None. The susuwatari are gentle, whimsical creatures. No dark material is associated with them.

Etymology

The name 'Susuwatari' (煤渡り) combines 'su' (煤, soot) and 'watari' (渡り, crossing/wandering), literally 'soot crossing' or 'wandering soot'. The alias 'Makkuro kurosuke' (まっくろくろすけ) uses 'makkuro' (pitch black), 'kuro' (black), and '-suke' (a common suffix for male names), roughly meaning 'pitch-black black guy'.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Hayao Miyazaki and Studio Ghibli. The susuwatari are original fictional beings, not derived from traditional Japanese folklore, though they evoke a Japanese aesthetic of household spirits and dust.

Disputed Identifications

None. The susuwatari are clearly identified as fictional creations of Miyazaki. There is no dispute about their origin or nature.

Description

Appearance

Susuwatari are depicted as small, golf ball-sized spheres of black soot with fuzzy, hair-like textures. They have two large, white eyes that are expressive, and long, thin, stick-like limbs that they can extend from their bodies. They lack mouths and other facial features. When moving, they hover or float, but can also use their limbs to carry objects. When crushed or harmed, they dissolve into a puff of soot.

Symbols & Iconography

The susuwatari symbolize the hidden life of dust and soot in domestic and industrial spaces. Their black, fuzzy appearance evokes dust bunnies, but they are given life and personality. In 'My Neighbor Totoro', they represent the spirits of an old house, and their departure signifies a home's peace. In 'Spirited Away', they are industrious workers, symbolizing the value of labor and the transformation of soot into purpose.

Behaviour & Personality

Susuwatari are generally shy but curious. In 'My Neighbor Totoro', they are initially startled by the human children but quickly become friendly. They are communal, moving in groups. They make squeaky murmuring sounds when excited. They are helpful, as seen in 'Spirited Away' where they carry coal and assist Chihiro. They are also playful, as shown in the 'Zen – Grogu and Dust Bunnies' short where they interact with Grogu. They are known to flee from bright light, as they are creatures of darkness and shadow.

Domains & Habitats

Susuwatari dwell in dark, dusty places: abandoned houses, attics, and boiler rooms. In 'My Neighbor Totoro', they inhabit the Kusakabe family's old farmhouse. In 'Spirited Away', they live and work in the boiler room of the bathhouse, under the care of Kamaji. They are associated with the boundary between light and darkness, and can be seen when one's eyes adjust from bright light to darkness.

Version Differences

In 'My Neighbor Totoro', the susuwatari are more mysterious and are rationalized as an optical illusion ('Makkuro Kurosuke') by the father. They leave the house after the family settles in. In 'Spirited Away', they are more prominent, with defined roles as workers in Kamaji's boiler room. They are shown to turn back into soot if they don't have a job. The 2022 short is a crossover, where they are called 'dust bunnies' and interact with Grogu, but retain their essential characteristics.

Stories & Myths

The Soot Sprites of the Old House

Source My Neighbor Totoro (1988)Culture Japanese (anime)Period 1950s Japan (setting)

Summary

When the Kusakabe family moves into an old rural house, the youngest daughter Mei discovers small black creatures in the attic. They are the susuwatari, who have been living in the house's dusty corners. The family initially mistakes them for dust or an optical illusion. As the family settles in and the house becomes clean and bright, the susuwatari decide to leave, moving to another abandoned house, as they prefer dark, dusty places.

Participants

Mei Kusakabe

Satsuki Kusakabe

Susuwatari

Consequences

The susuwatari leave, and the house becomes a happy home for the Kusakabe family. The encounter establishes the magical atmosphere of the film.

Variants

In the English dub, they are called 'soot sprites' or 'soot gremlins' depending on the version.

The Boiler Room Workers

Source Spirited Away (2001)Culture Japanese (anime)Period Contemporary (setting)

Summary

In the spirit world, Chihiro (Sen) is taken to Kamaji's boiler room, where she sees susuwatari working, carrying coal to the furnace. They are overworked and underfed. Chihiro helps one struggling sprite by carrying a lump of coal, earning their friendship. Lin explains that if the susuwatari don't work, they turn back into soot. After Chihiro gains employment at the bathhouse, the susuwatari become admiring and assist her in small ways, such as hiding her shoes.

Consequences

Chihiro's kindness wins the loyalty of the susuwatari, who later help her navigate the bathhouse. They remain workers in the boiler room.

Grogu and the Dust Bunnies

Source Zen – Grogu and Dust Bunnies (2022)Culture Japanese (anime) / American (Star Wars)Period Star Wars timeline (setting)

Summary

In this short, Grogu (from The Mandalorian) encounters a group of susuwatari (called dust bunnies) in a dark place. They interact playfully, and the dust bunnies give Grogu a flower as a gift. The short is a crossover collaboration between Studio Ghibli and Lucasfilm.

Contradictions & Disputes

Nature as optical illusion vs. real creatures

Position A

The susuwatari are real, supernatural beings.

Position A — evidence

They are shown moving and interacting, and in Spirited Away they are clearly real.

Position B

The susuwatari are an optical illusion, as stated by the father in My Neighbor Totoro.

Position B — evidence

The father explains them as 'Makkuro Kurosuke', a phenomenon of light and darkness.

Scholarly Consensus

The father's explanation is a rationalization within the story; the film presents them as real. There is no scholarly debate.

Research Scope

This dossier focuses on the fictional susuwatari as created by Hayao Miyazaki and Studio Ghibli, as depicted in 'My Neighbor Totoro' (1988) and 'Spirited Away' (2001), and the 2022 Star Wars crossover short. It covers their identity, descriptions, roles, relationships, and reception, drawing from official art books, film analyses, and academic commentary. It does not cover the unrelated concept of dust bunnies in general, except where relevant to the susuwatari's reception. Research cutoff: 2026-08-01.
