Who It Is

Mamaragan is the Aboriginal thunder god and lightning spirit of northern Australia, the powerful ancestral being who controls the storms that bring the life-giving monsoon rains. Known as 'Lightning Man' or 'Thunder Man,' Mamaragan creates the lightning by throwing stone axes from the clouds — when lightning strikes, it is one of his axes hitting the earth. The thunder is the sound of his voice. Mamaragan is a central figure in the Wardaman people's Dreaming tradition of the Northern Territory. He is depicted in rock art as a tall, slender figure with axes on his head, shoulders, elbows, and knees — the source of his lightning bolts. Mamaragan is also a creator being who shaped the landscape during the Dreaming, carving out rivers and waterholes with his lightning strikes. He is the enforcer of the law, striking down those who break sacred laws. The lightning's ability to start bushfires also connects Mamaragan to the cycle of renewal — the burning of old growth to make way for new. Mamaragan represents the creative-destructive power of nature: the same storms that can destroy also bring the rains that make life possible in Australia's harsh climate. In some stories, he travels with the Rainbow Serpent.

Origins

Mamaragan shaped the landscape during the Dreaming with his lightning strikes. He controls the monsoon storms, throwing stone axes from the clouds to create lightning. In some traditions, he traveled with the Rainbow Serpent, lighting the way for her creation journey.
