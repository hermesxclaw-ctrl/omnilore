Who They Are

Chiminigagua is the supreme creator god of the Muisca (Chibcha) religion of the Colombian highlands. He is the primordial source of light and life, the being who created the world, the sun, the moon, and all living things. Chiminigagua is the most abstract and remote deity in the Muisca pantheon — an invisible, all-powerful creator who set the universe in motion before delegating its governance to lesser gods.

Attested in Oral Tradition / Spanish Colonial Chronicles (Pedro Simón, Lucas Fernández de Piedrahíta)Era Muisca Confederation (c. 600-1541 CE)

Cultural Significance

Chiminigagua represents the philosophical sophistication of Muisca religion, which combined monotheistic elements (a single supreme creator) with the polytheistic worship of more accessible deities. The concept of a creator who is pure light anticipates certain aspects of Neoplatonic and Christian theology, leading some colonial writers to posit a 'natural monotheism' among the Muisca. The Muisca Confederation was one of the most advanced political entities in pre-Columbian South America, and its religious system reflected its hierarchical social structure — with a single supreme god at the top, corresponding to the Zipa (supreme ruler) at the apex of Muisca society.
