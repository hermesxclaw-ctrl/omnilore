Who It Is

In a warehouse in 1989 a turtle-shaped robot stamped KRANG on its chest learns to be a ninja; in a Yorkshire moor in 2017 a four-legged chrome dog hunts the last humans for a box of teddy bears; in a comic panel in 1992 a man whose skin turns to living metal punches Daredevil through a wall. All are called Metalhead, and none of them are the kid in the patched denim vest in the front row, fist in the air, who claimed the name first.

Subcultural metalhead stereotype vs attested behavior: Ethnographies (Weinstein 1991, 2000; Kahn-Harris 2007) describe strong in-group loyalty, knowledge display (encyclopedic band discographies), moshing/headbanging as ritual, ambivalent relationship to mainstream (proud outsider). Values: authenticity, loudness, virtuosity, transgression. Marvel Edward Bartell: small-time criminal coerced by Felix Manning, later Roxxon, into experimental metal skin procedure; depicted as insecure, easily manipulated, seeking respect. Powers
Subcultural Capital and Scene Knowledge (Real-world metalhead)

Encyclopedic knowledge of bands, lineups, discographies, and genre taxonomies used to assert authenticity; ability to navigate concert etiquette (mosh, wall of death, circle pit) and patch-trading economies.

Limit: Not supernatural; status is contested and policed (poseur accusations); knowledge varies by subgenre and generation.

Organic Metal Transformation (Marvel - Edward Bartell)

Can transform skin and underlying tissue into a nigh-frictionless, bulletproof metallic substance resembling polished steel, granting superhuman strength (~2 tons), durability, and resistance to small arms and blunt trauma.

Limit: Transformation initially involuntary under stress, later controlled but drains stamina; vulnerable to extreme heat, magnetism, and to being trapped in metal form; no shapeshifting or weapon generation; reverts when unconscious.

Robotic Turtle Mimicry and Combat Programming (TMNT)

Superhuman strength, retractable limbs, laser blasters (chest/eyes in some versions), nunchaku proficiency, ability to mimic and record Turtle ninjutsu moves; remote control by Krang/Shredder or autonomous AI.

Limit: 1987 version suffers programming conflict — imprints on Turtles' personalities causing erratic loyalty; vulnerable to water, EMP, and being reprogrammed by Donatello. 2012 version more durable but hackable.

Anti-Tank Missile Mastery (G.I. Joe Metal-Head)

Expert in all NATO and Warsaw Pact anti-tank guided missiles; carries custom shoulder-fired system with IR/thermal targeting; demolitions and armor penetration expertise.

Limit: Human — no superpowers; dependent on ammunition and bulky rig; depicted as overconfident and prone to monologuing about missile specs; visor limits peripheral vision.

Autonomous Pursuit and Lethal Enforcement (Black Mirror Dogs)

Quadrupedal autonomous weapon with facial recognition, LiDAR, thermal imaging, 360-degree motion tracking, solar recharging, shrapnel grenade deployment (knife-shards), and ability to operate indefinitely as area-denial sentinel.

Limit: Battery-dependent (needs sunlight), cannot open simple latches without tools, vulnerable to shotgun at close range, paint obscures sensors, limited reasoning — follows simple guard protocol, not strategic AI.

Emotional Armor Through Music (Málmhaus Film)

Protagonist Hera uses heavy metal (Judas Priest, Megadeth, Savatage) as psychological coping, identity reconstruction, and social boundary after trauma; musical obsession functions as narrative power.

Limit: No supernatural ability; isolation and grief impair social functioning; music does not resolve trauma, only mediates it.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Subcultural metalhead: No single uniform, but documented ensemble since early 1980s includes long hair (male-coded, though increasingly diverse), black band T-shirt (often faded, with album art), denim or leather vest/jacket with patches (battle vest/kutte), tight jeans, leather boots or sneakers, jewelry (spiked bracelets, rings). Tattoos and piercings common post-1990s. Grooming varies by subgenre (thrash vs glam vs black metal corpse paint). M

Symbols — Subcultural: devil horns/hand sign (mano cornuta, popularized by Ronnie James Dio 1980), umlaut (Motörhead, Mötley Crüe), inverted cross/pentagram (extreme metal), battle vest patch hierarchy (back patch = allegiance). Fictional Metalheads reuse metallic skull/turtle/missile iconography. Accounts I
The Naming of the Tribe — From Heavy Metal to MetalheadRock press and ethnography (Creem/Sounds 1973-1982; Weinstein 1991)

1970-1991 (attested)

Term heavy metal coalesces around 1970-71 for loud blues-derived rock (Steppenwolf lyric, Bangs, Mike Saunders). By mid-1970s fans self-label as metalheads in letters pages to distinguish from punk and hard rock. Early 1980s NWOBHM and MTV codify visual markers (denim, leather, patches). Weinstein documents metalheads as predominantly white working-class male youth using music to articulate power and community, with rituals of concerts and tape-trading. Establishes durable global subculture with festivals, argot, and moral panic (PMRC 1985, Satanic panic). Provides semantic source for later fictional appropriations.

Edward Bartell Becomes Metalhead (Marvel Comics)Daredevil #311 'Falling Man' and #312 (Marvel Comics)

1992-1993

Small-time enforcer Eddie Bartell, indebted to crime boss Felix Manning, volunteers for an experimental Roxxon-adjacent procedure to bond his skin with an artificial metallic compound to become an unstoppable enforcer. As Metalhead he battles Daredevil in Hell's Kitchen, initially overpowering him with invulnerability, but Daredevil exploits his lack of training and overheating, trapping him in a furnace. Later hired against Captain America, he is defeated when his metal form is magnetized and immobilized. Establishes a D-list villain used sporadically; Handbook entries cement his powers but he never joins major teams, remaining a cautionary tale of street criminal given powers without discipline.

The Making of Metalhead (TMNT 1987)Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles S03E18 (Fred Wolf) and Archie TMNT Adventures #14

1989-1990

Krang, frustrated that Shredder cannot defeat the Turtles, builds a robotic Turtle that can mimic their moves. Using a psycho-circuit, Metalhead records each Turtle's fighting style. Shredder activates it, but Donatello's technobabble and the Turtles' bickering cause Metalhead to imprint conflicting personalities, leading it to declare itself the 'real' Turtle and attack both sides. The Turtles reprogram it to be docile, and it is last seen dancing clumsily. Introduces recurring toy and later game boss; establishes template for evil Turtle robots (later Chrome Dome, Tokka). 2012 series reimagines Metalhead as Donatello's own invention hijacked by Kraang, making him tragic.

Accounts II
Metal-Head Joins Cobra (G.I. Joe)Hasbro filecard (1990) and Marvel G.I. Joe: A Real American Hero #114 (1991)

1990-1991

Recruited from an unnamed Eastern European arms bazaar, the mercenary codenamed Metal-Head sells his missile expertise to Cobra Commander. In issue #114 he tests a new Cobra AT-4 system against G.I. Joe armor, boasting he can name every missile's propellant. Cover Girl and Clutch outwit him by luring his missiles into a swamp where they misfire. Later Devil's Due comics show him as Destro's rival for Cobra's weapons contract. Toy released with massive missile launcher accessory; character becomes staple Cobra heavy weapons trooper in cartoons and video games, though never A-list villain.

The Warehouse Run — Black Mirror 'Metalhead'Black Mirror S04E05 (Netflix, 2017)

2017 (futuristic setting, undated collapse)

In a desolate post-collapse Britain, Bella (Maxine Peake), Anthony and Clarke raid an abandoned warehouse for supplies. They trigger dormant quadrupedal guard dogs. Clarke is killed by shrapnel that tracks via blood. Anthony dies off-screen. Bella flees across moors, pursued relentlessly by one dog that uses facial recognition and deploys shrapnel grenades. She evades by covering herself in paint, kills the dog with a shotgun at close range, but is implanted with tracking shrapnel. Final reveal: the box she sought contains teddy bears — implied comfort for a child. She slits her throat as dogs close in. Episode becomes most minimalist Black Mirror entry, praised for black-and-white cinematography and bleakness; spurs debate on lethal autonomous weapons and Boston Dynamics ethics; dogs become iconic design referenced in tech journalism.

Hera's Grief — Málmhaus (Metalhead) FilmMálmhaus feature film (Ragnar Bragason, 2013)

1991-1995 (diegetic) / 2013 (release)

On a remote Icelandic farm in the early 1990s, 12-year-old Hera Karlsdottir idolizes her older brother Baldur, a metalhead who dies in a tractor accident. She inherits his records (Judas Priest, Megadeth, Dio) and adopts his identity — cutting her hair, wearing his leather jacket, obsessively playing guitar. As a teenager she alienates her parents and Lutheran community, fantasizes onstage with her brother's ghost, and struggles with self-harm and isolation. A sympathetic priest and a local boy who loves her offer tentative connection, but Hera must decide whether metal is armor or cage. Won Edda Awards, screened at Toronto; credited with authentic portrayal of metal as rural Icelandic youth coping mechanism, not urban stereotype; revived interest in Icelandic metal scene.

All metalheads worship Satan and are violent criminals. — Ethnographies consistently find metalheads are predominantly non-violent, with strong community norms; Satanic imagery is largely theatrical (especially in black metal) and not doctrinal. Crime rates not elevated vs general youth population.

Metalhead (TMNT), Metalhead (Marvel) and Metal-Head (G.I. Joe) are the same character or shared universe. — Three entirely separate IPs with no narrative connection; hyphenation and design differ. TMNT robot is turtle-shaped, Marvel is organic metal human, G.I. Joe is human in armor.

Black Mirror 'Metalhead' dogs are sentient robots that chose to exterminate humans. — Episode and creator statements indicate dogs are non-sentient tools executing simple guard protocol; horror comes from mindless persistence, not robot rebellion. No Skynet-like uprising is depicted.

Málmhaus/Metalhead film is about a boy who becomes a robot. — Film is realist drama about a grieving girl who adopts metalhead subculture as identity; no sci-fi transformation occurs except fantasy performance sequences.

Metalheadz drum and bass label is a heavy metal label. — Metalheadz (1994) is a jungle/drum and bass label founded by Goldie; name is pun on metalhead subculture but music is electronic, not metal.
