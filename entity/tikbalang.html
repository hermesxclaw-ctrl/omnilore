Who It Is

The tikbalang is a horse-headed, human-bodied creature with disproportionately long legs that can fold up to prodigious heights. It dwells in deep forests and mountains, where it delights in leading travelers astray — causing them to walk in circles, lose their sense of direction, and never find the path home. The tikbalang is also known to mate with horses, producing rare offspring. To break a tikbalang's spell, a traveler should turn their shirt inside out, ask permission aloud to pass through the forest, or sprinkle salt on the trail. Some stories say tikbalangs can be tamed by pulling a single golden hair from their mane, after which they become loyal servants. The creature is sometimes depicted as a guardian of the forest who protects the Aswang or works as its mount. In modern times, the tikbalang appears in Filipino fantasy novels, films, comics, and video games. The legend shares striking parallels with European centaurs and Japanese baku.
