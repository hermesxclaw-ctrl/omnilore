Who It Is

The Fouke Monster, also known as the Boggy Creek Monster, is Arkansas's most famous cryptid -- a Southern variant of Bigfoot reported from the dense swamps and bottomlands of the Sulphur River near Fouke, Arkansas. The creature is described as a large, bipedal ape-like creature 7 to 8 feet tall, covered in dark, shaggy hair, with a wide chest, long arms reaching to its knees, and glowing red or yellow eyes. The Fouke Monster legend exploded into national consciousness in 1971-1972, when multiple families in the Fouke area reported terrifying encounters. The most famous incident involved the Ford family, who claimed a large, hairy creature reached through a window screen of their isolated home and attempted to grab a sleeping woman. The family's husband fired shots at the creature, which fled into the woods. Over the next year, multiple other families reported sightings, tracks measuring 14 inches long, and three-toed footprints (unusual for Bigfoot, which typically has five toes). The events inspired the 1972 independent horror documentary film 'The Legend of Boggy Creek,' which became a cult classic and cemented the Fouke Monster in American pop culture. The creature's three-toed tracks have led some researchers to propose it may be a different species from the Pacific Northwest Sasquatch, while skeptics attribute the tracks to overlapping footprints of known animals or to hoaxing. The Fouke Monster remains a beloved part of Arkansas folklore, celebrated in an annual festival and local tourism.
