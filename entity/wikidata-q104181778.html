Who It Is

In the dying years of the Roman Republic, a soldier named Lucilius stood on a battlefield at Philippi, stripped of hope, and chose to die for a friend. When his ruse was revealed, the enemy commander—Marcus Antonius, the man who would one day rule the East—was so moved that he spared him and made him a friend. This is the story of a name that flickers through Roman history: a poet who forged a genre, a jurist who shaped law, and a man whose loyalty outshone the chaos of civil war.

Members display a range of traits: literary genius (Gaius Lucilius), legal expertise (Lucius Lucilius Balbus), Stoic philosophy (Quintus Lucilius Balbus), political ambition (Sextus Lucilius, tribune), loyalty (Lucilius at Philippi), and brutality (centurion 'Cedo Alteram').

Literary creation (Gaius Lucilius)

Gaius Lucilius originated Roman satire, writing in a distinctive style that influenced later satirists like Horace and Juvenal.

Limit: No complete works survive; only fragments.

Legal expertise (Lucius Lucilius Balbus)

A jurist who studied under Quintus Mucius Scaevola and taught Servius Sulpicius Rufus, known for his learned and deliberate opinions.

Limit: No writings survive.

Military command (various)

Several Lucilii served as military tribunes, cavalry officers, and fleet commanders, including Marcus Lucilius Rufus (moneyer), Sextus Lucilius (tribune killed at Mount Amanus), and Lucilius Bassus (fleet commander under Vitellius and Vespasian).

Limit: Varies by individual; some died in combat, others survived.

Political office

Members held the praetorship, tribunate, consulship (suffect), and procuratorships, indicating political influence.

Limit: Only Lucilius Longus reached the consulship (AD 7).

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical descriptions of Lucilii are recorded in the source material. Cognomina such as Balbus (stammerer), Bassus (stout), Capito (large-headed), and Rufus (red-haired) suggest physical or speech characteristics, but these are not detailed.

Symbols — Coins minted by Marcus Lucilius Rufus feature Pallas (Minerva) on the obverse and Victoria in a biga on the reverse, indicating the family's engagement with Roman state iconography.

Domains — The gens originated from Suessa Aurunca, an ancient town of the Aurunci, where a Latin colony was established in 313 BC. Members were active in Rome, across the empire (Asia, Sicily, Judaea), and in military campaigns.

Accounts I
The Loyalty of Lucilius at PhilippiThe provided source material; also known from ancient historians (e.g., Appian, Plutarch)

42 BC

After the Battle of Philippi, a partisan of Brutus named Lucilius pretended to be Brutus to allow his friend to escape. He was captured, but when his identity was revealed, Marcus Antonius pardoned him and they became friends. Lucilius was spared and befriended by Antonius, demonstrating the value of loyalty even among enemies.

Accounts II
The Death of Sextus LuciliusThe provided source material

86 BC

Sextus Lucilius, tribune of the plebs and a partisan of Sulla, was thrown from the Tarpeian Rock by his successor Publius Popillius Laenas when Sulla's enemies controlled the city. Sextus Lucilius was executed, illustrating the violent political reprisals of the period.

The Tomb of Marcus Lucilius PaetusThe provided source material

Augustan era

A large round tomb, about 34 metres across, was built for Marcus Lucilius Paetus and his sister Lucilia Polla, discovered in 1885 near the Villa Albani. The tomb provides archaeological evidence of the family's wealth and status.
