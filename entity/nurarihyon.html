Nurarihyon is the gentleman burglar of the yokai world: an old man with an enormously elongated, gourd-shaped bald head, dressed in fine silk, who slips into your house at dusk when everyone is busy, settles into the best seat, drinks your tea, smokes your tobacco, and behaves so completely like the master of the house that no one can bring themselves to challenge him — indeed, while he sits there, everyone somehow believes he is the master. Then he leaves as unhurriedly as he came.

Who It Is

Nurarihyon is the gentleman burglar of the yokai world: an old man with an enormously elongated, gourd-shaped bald head, dressed in fine silk, who slips into your house at dusk when everyone is busy, settles into the best seat, drinks your tea, smokes your tobacco, and behaves so completely like the master of the house that no one can bring themselves to challenge him — indeed, while he sits there, everyone somehow believes he is the master. Then he leaves as unhurriedly as he came. Originally a minor slippery-to-catch spirit (his name suggests something that slides from your grip, like a gourd bobbing in water), Edo scroll painters and modern manga elevated him into the Supreme Commander of All Yokai, leader of the hyakki yagyo, the Night Parade of One Hundred Demons. That promotion — from tea-stealing houseguest to king of monsters — is itself a very nurarihyon achievement: he simply acted like he was in charge until everyone agreed.

Origins

Appears unnamed in Edo yokai scrolls as a well-dressed old man with a gourd-shaped skull. The commander-of-yokai title was largely a twentieth-century invention, popularized by Shigeru Mizuki's GeGeGe no Kitaro and later canonized by the manga Nura: Rise of the Yokai Clan.

Attested in Edo-period yokai scrollsEra Edo period to present

Form

An elderly man of refined dress with a massively swollen, smooth, elongated skull, carried with aristocratic poise; often shown arriving by palanquin as if expected.

Powers

An uninvited-guest glamour — an aura of legitimacy so total that residents perceive him as the household's rightful master; slipperiness that defeats any grip; command, in modern canon, over the entire yokai night parade.

Weaknesses & Wards

None recorded. He is not violent, and so has never needed to be defeated — the most nurarihyon fact of all.

Field Notes

⍟ In Okayama coastal lore, nurarihyon also names a jellyfish-like sea blob that sinks when grasped — slipping the fisherman's hand exactly as the house-guest slips social challenge.

⍟ His dusk visits target the hour when households are busiest with dinner preparations, the moment attention is weakest.

⍟ Mizuki drew him as the recurring arch-villain of GeGeGe no Kitaro, cementing his supreme-commander rank in pop culture.

Cultural Significance

He embodies a very Japanese comic horror: the social intrusion no one is quite rude enough to stop. Modern yokai media rank him at or near the top of the monster hierarchy almost entirely on the strength of his composure.
