Who It Is

Metatron is the most exalted angel in Jewish mysticism — a figure so elevated that he is granted the unimaginable title 'Lesser YHWH' and seated upon a throne beside God's own. His origins and nature are among the most complex and debated in angelology. The dominant tradition, crystallized in the mystical text Sefer Hekhalot (3 Enoch, c. 5th-6th century CE), identifies Metatron as the transfigured Enoch, the antediluvian patriarch who 'walked with God' in Genesis 5:24 and was taken bodily into heaven without dying. In this account, God transforms Enoch into Metatron: his flesh becomes flame, his sinews fire, his bones glowing coals, his eyelashes lightning, and his stature fills the cosmos. He is given seventy-two wings (thirty-six on each side) and 365,000 eyes, and is enthroned at the entrance to the seventh heavenly palace. God reveals to him all the secrets of creation, the heavenly Torah, and the divine names, and appoints him as the celestial scribe who records Israel's deeds. The Talmud (Chagigah 15a) records the controversial story of Elisha ben Abuyah (Acher) who, upon seeing Metatron seated in heaven, thought there were 'two powers in heaven' — a heretical conclusion that led to his apostasy. To prevent such misinterpretation, Metatron was subsequently given sixty fiery lashes. In Kabbalistic tradition (Zohar), Metatron is the angel of the divine countenance, the intermediary through whom God's presence is mediated to the lower worlds. He presides over the angelic hosts, maintains the records of human deeds, and teaches the souls of departed children in the heavenly academy. Some traditions distinguish between two Metatrons — Metatron the Prince of the Countenance (Enoch) and a primordial Metatron who emanated directly from God. Metatron's name may derive from the Greek meta-tronos (beside the throne) or the Latin metator (guide, measurer). His role as divine scribe, cosmic record-keeper, and the bridge between human mortality and angelic immortality makes him one of the most distinctive and philosophically rich figures in the entire angelic pantheon.
