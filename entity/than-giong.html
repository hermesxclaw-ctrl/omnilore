Who It Is

Than Giong (Thánh Gióng, 'Saint Giong'), also known as Phu Dong Thien Vuong (Heavenly King of Phu Dong), is one of the most beloved folk heroes of Vietnam—a legendary figure who embodies Vietnamese resistance against foreign invasion and the supernatural protection of the nation. According to legend, during the reign of the 6th Hung King, the kingdom of Van Lang was invaded by An (Yin Shang) forces from the north. The king sent messengers throughout the land seeking anyone who could defend the nation. In the village of Phu Dong, an elderly couple who had long been childless were astonished when their toddler son—who, despite being three years old, had never spoken, smiled, or walked—suddenly sat up and spoke, demanding to see the king's messenger. He declared he needed an iron horse, an iron staff, and an iron armor to drive back the invaders. Miraculously, the boy began to grow at an astonishing rate, consuming vast quantities of rice and eggplant brought by the villagers—the entire village had to feed him. When the iron horse, staff, and armor were delivered, the now-giant youth mounted the horse, which breathed fire from its nostrils, and charged into battle. He laid waste to the enemy army with his staff, and when the staff broke, he uprooted bamboo groves to use as weapons. After routing the invaders, he rode to the summit of Soc Son Mountain, removed his armor, and ascended into heaven on his horse, becoming a celestial deity. The Giong Festival ('Hoi Giong') at Phu Dong Temple and Soc Temple is celebrated annually and was recognized as an Intangible Cultural Heritage by UNESCO in 2010. Than Giong represents the Vietnamese paradigm of the reluctant hero—a seemingly ordinary person who rises in times of national crisis, transforms through collective support, and achieves extraordinary feats.
