Who It Is

Ryomen Sukuna is the King of Curses — the most powerful curse in existence and the central antagonist of Jujutsu Kaisen. A thousand years ago during the Heian Era, Sukuna was a human sorcerer of such terrifying power that even after his death, his cursed energy refused to dissipate. His 20 fingers were preserved as indestructible cursed objects, each containing a fragment of his soul and immense power. If all 20 fingers are consumed by a single vessel, Sukuna will be fully resurrected. Sukuna is depicted with four arms, two faces, and an inhuman physique that reflects his monstrous nature — a physical manifestation of his rejection of human limitations. His mastery of cursed energy is absolute: he can perform barrier techniques (Domain Expansion), reverse cursed technique (healing), and barrierless Domain Expansions with a skill that even the strongest modern sorcerers cannot match. His ultimate technique is Malevolent Shrine, a Domain Expansion that does not create a barrier but instead inscribes his shrine into the surface of reality itself, allowing his guaranteed-hit effect to reach everything within a 200-meter radius without the usual vulnerability of a barrier domain. Sukuna possesses a four-armed combat style that allows him to perform multiple techniques simultaneously, and his Furnace technique can incinerate anything within his domain's range. Sukuna's personality is defined by his utter amorality and hedonism. He does not seek to destroy the world or conquer it — he simply wants to do whatever he pleases, whenever he pleases. He kills for amusement, tortures for entertainment, and views all other beings as insects beneath his notice. Yet he is not a mindless monster: he is a cunning, patient, and terrifyingly intelligent strategist who bides his time within Yuji Itadori's body, waiting for the moment he can fully incarnate. Sukuna represents the ultimate expression of selfish, unrestrained power. He is the darkness that exists when a being has absolute strength and absolutely no moral framework — the disaster that results when power serves only itself. His awakening is the doomsday clock of the Jujutsu Kaisen world, and everyone — sorcerer and curse alike — fears the day the King of Curses fully returns.

The Approach

In the golden age of jujutsu, when sorcerers were at their peak, there lived a being so terrifying that even the most powerful shamans trembled at his name. Ryomen Sukuna, the King of Curses, was a walking disaster—a calamity in human form whose very existence defied the natural order. With two faces, four arms, and an insatiable appetite for destruction, he was not merely a sorcerer but a force of nature, feared by gods and demons alike. His legend, born in the Heian Era, has echoed through the ages, and now, a thousand years later, his fingers still hold the power to plunge the world into chaos.

Etymology

Ryomen (両面) means 'two-faced' or 'two-sided' in Japanese, referring to his literal two faces and his dual nature. Sukuna (宿儺) is an archaic term of uncertain meaning, but it is used in the Nihon Shoki to denote a powerful and malevolent deity or demon. The name is derived from the historical myth of Ryomen-sukuna, a figure from the Hida Province who was described as having two faces and four arms.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese pop culture, specifically the manga and anime series Jujutsu Kaisen, created by Gege Akutami and published by Shueisha. The character is based on a mythological figure from Japanese folklore, which is part of Japan's cultural heritage. The fictional character is owned by Shueisha and its licensees.

Disputed Identifications

There is some debate among fans about the exact nature of Sukuna's original form—whether he was a human sorcerer who became a curse, or a curse that took human form. The manga has not fully clarified this, leading to speculation. Additionally, some fans confuse the fictional character with the historical myth, which is inaccurate as the two are distinct.

Description

Appearance

Ryomen Sukuna appears as a tall, muscular man with pink hair and red eyes. In his true form, he has two faces—one on the front and one on the back of his head—and four arms. His body is covered in black tattoos that resemble a demonic pattern, and he often has a menacing grin. In his incarnated form within Megumi Fushiguro, he retains his appearance but with Megumi's facial features, including his spiky black hair and distinctive dark eyes.

Symbols & Iconography

Sukuna's iconic symbol is his four arms and two faces, representing his overwhelming power and dual nature. He is often associated with the imagery of a demon or oni in Japanese folklore. His tattoos are also a distinctive feature, often depicted as a form of cursed energy manifestation.

Behaviour & Personality

Sukuna is characterized as cruel, narcissistic, depraved, and supremely arrogant. He has no regard for human life and takes pleasure in causing fear and pain. He is highly intelligent and strategic, often manipulating others to achieve his goals. He is also shown to be indifferent to most things, but becomes interested in those who show strength or potential, such as Megumi Fushiguro and Satoru Gojo. He is a hedonist who enjoys fighting and chaos.

Domains & Habitats

Sukuna's domain is the Malevolent Shrine, a terrifying space where his cursed technique automatically attacks all within it. In terms of habitat, he was originally from the Heian Era, where he lived as a powerful sorcerer. In the modern era, he resides within the body of Yuji Itadori, and later Megumi Fushiguro.

Version Differences

There are two main versions of Sukuna: the original Heian Era sorcerer and the incarnated curse within Yuji. In the Heian Era, he was a human sorcerer (or possibly a curse) who was feared for his immense power. In the modern era, he is a cursed object that can manifest through vessels. His powers and abilities remain consistent, but his behavior may vary depending on the vessel and situation.

Stories & Myths

The Heian Era Legend

Source Nihon Shoki, Jujutsu Kaisen mangaCulture JapanesePeriod Heian Era (794-1185)

Summary

According to the Nihon Shoki, Ryomen-sukuna was a powerful being from Hida Province who had two faces and four arms. He was feared as a demon and was eventually defeated by the Yamato general. In Jujutsu Kaisen, Sukuna is depicted as the strongest sorcerer of the Heian Era, a cruel and malevolent figure who was feared by all. He had an underling named Uraume and was known for his immense cursed energy.

Participants

Ryomen Sukuna

Uraume

Yamato clan

Consequences

Sukuna's power was so great that after his death, his body was divided into 20 fingers, which were sealed and scattered across Japan to prevent his resurrection.

Variants

The historical myth and the fictional version differ significantly; the historical figure is a demon, while the fictional version is a sorcerer who may have become a curse.

Yuji Itadori's Vessel

Source Jujutsu Kaisen manga, chapters 1-10Culture JapanesePeriod Modern Era

Summary

Yuji Itadori, a high school student, accidentally swallows one of Sukuna's fingers and becomes his vessel. Sukuna gains consciousness within Yuji and can take control of his body. Initially, Yuji has full control, but Sukuna can take over when Yuji allows it or when he is in danger. Sukuna's presence makes Yuji a target for execution by the Jujutsu world, but Satoru Gojo protects him.

Contradictions & Disputes

Human or curse

Position A

Sukuna was originally a human sorcerer who became a curse after death.

Position A — evidence

The manga describes him as a 'curse user' during the Heian Era, and his fingers are classified as cursed objects.

Position B

Sukuna was always a curse, never a human.

Position B — evidence

His appearance and behavior are demonic, and he is referred to as a 'curse' in the modern era.

Scholarly Consensus

The manga has not explicitly clarified, but the term 'curse user' suggests he was human. However, his transformation into a curse is not fully explained.

Death of Gojo

Position A

Sukuna killed Gojo fairly in a one-on-one duel.

Position A — evidence

The duel was official, and Sukuna won by using his technique.

Position B

Sukuna used a binding vow and a new technique, which some consider a cheap tactic.

Position B — evidence

The World Cutting Slash was introduced as a new ability, and the binding vow gave him an advantage.

Scholarly Consensus

Most fans accept that Sukuna won, but debate the fairness of the technique.
