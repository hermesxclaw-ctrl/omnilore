Who It Is

Before the universe existed, before time and space had meaning, there were six singularities. When the Big Bang erupted, their remnants were forged into six stones, each embodying a fundamental aspect of existence. The Space Stone, the blue cube known as the Tesseract, controls the very fabric of space itself, allowing its wielder to travel anywhere in the cosmos instantly. It has powered armies, unlocked light-speed, and transported heroes and villains across the galaxy. From the depths of the ocean to the vaults of Asgard, the Space Stone has shaped the fate of the Marvel Cinematic Universe.

As an inanimate object, the Space Stone does not have a personality. However, it is portrayed as a highly active and reactive artifact, responding to the intentions and commands of its wielder. It can open portals, teleport objects and people, and emit powerful energy blasts. It seems to have a will of its own at times, as seen when it transports Red Skull to Vormir, possibly as a form of punishment or cosmic justice.

Powers
Space Manipulation

The Space Stone allows its wielder to manipulate space, enabling instantaneous travel across vast distances, opening portals, and teleporting objects or people. It can also create wormholes and potentially fold space.

Limit: Requires a wielder with sufficient strength or technology to harness its power. The Stone itself is extremely powerful and can be dangerous if mishandled.

Energy Projection

The Space Stone can project powerful beams of blue energy, capable of causing massive destruction. It has been used to power weapons and vehicles.

Limit: Energy output may be limited by the wielder's control or the container.

Power Augmentation

The Space Stone can enhance the abilities of other beings or technology. For example, it gave Carol Danvers her powers when she absorbed its energy.

Limit: The effects vary depending on the recipient; it may be lethal to some.

Wormhole Creation

The Space Stone can create stable wormholes, as seen when Loki uses it to bring the Chitauri army to Earth.

Limit: May require additional technology or a specific location to open a large wormhole.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Space Stone is a small, blue, glowing gem. When contained in the Tesseract, it appears as a luminous blue cube with a bright internal light. The cube is about the size of a hand, with a smooth, crystalline surface. When the Stone is removed from the Tesseract, it is a small, irregularly shaped blue crystal that emits a powerful blue glow.

Symbols — The Tesseract is often depicted as a symbol of immense power and cosmic significance. Its blue color represents the vastness of space and the energy of the cosmos. In the MCU, it is frequently shown as a source of light and energy, often powering advanced technology or weapons.

Domains — The Space Stone has been housed in various locations throughout the MCU: the Norwegian village of Tønsberg, Hydra's facilities, S.H.I.E.L.D. headquarters, the Arctic Ocean, a Flerken's stomach, Odin's vault on Asgard, and the Statesman. Its power allows it to exist anywhere in space, and it can be transported through portals.

Accounts I
The Tesseract in World War IICaptain America: The First Avenger (2011)

1942-1945

Johann Schmidt (Red Skull), leader of Hydra, steals the Tesseract from a church in Tønsberg, Norway. He uses its power to develop advanced weapons and energy-based technology for Hydra. During a confrontation with Steve Rogers, Schmidt touches the Tesseract and is transported to Vormir, where he becomes the guardian of the Soul Stone. The Tesseract falls into the Arctic Ocean, later recovered by Howard Stark. The Tesseract becomes a key asset for S.H.I.E.L.D., and its power inspires future technological advancements.

The Space Stone and Captain MarvelCaptain Marvel (2019)

1989-1995

Dr. Wendy Lawson, a Kree scientist, attempts to use the Tesseract to develop a light-speed engine to help Skrull refugees. During a test flight, Carol Danvers destroys the engine to prevent the Kree from obtaining it, absorbing the energy of the Space Stone. This grants her superhuman powers. The Tesseract is later recovered by Goose the Flerken, who swallows it and later regurgitates it onto Nick Fury's desk. Carol Danvers becomes one of the most powerful heroes in the MCU. The Tesseract is placed in S.H.I.E.L.D. custody.

Accounts II
The Battle of New YorkThe Avengers (2012)

2012

Loki, sent by Thanos, steals the Tesseract from S.H.I.E.L.D. and uses it to open a wormhole above New York City, allowing the Chitauri to invade. The Avengers assemble to stop the invasion, and after their victory, Thor takes the Tesseract back to Asgard. The Tesseract is stored in Odin's vault on Asgard. Loki is taken back to Asgard as a prisoner.

Thanos Acquires the Space StoneAvengers: Infinity War (2018)

2018

As Ragnarok destroys Asgard, Loki takes the Tesseract from the vault. Thanos intercepts the refugee ship, kills Loki, and takes the Tesseract. He crushes it to reveal the Space Stone and adds it to the Infinity Gauntlet. Thanos now possesses the Space Stone, bringing him closer to his goal.

The Time HeistAvengers: Endgame (2019)

2023 (time travel to 2012, 1970)

To undo the Blip, the Avengers travel back in time to collect the Infinity Stones from different periods. They retrieve the Space Stone from the 2012 timeline during the Battle of New York, and also from the 1970s S.H.I.E.L.D. facility. Steve Rogers later returns the Stones to their original timelines. The Avengers successfully create a new Infinity Gauntlet and undo Thanos's snap.
