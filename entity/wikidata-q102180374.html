Who It Is

Before the first wrestler ever stepped into a dohyō, before the first sword was forged for an emperor's conquest, there was Takemikazuchi—the thunder god who sat atop an inverted blade on a wave-battered shore and crushed his rival's hand like a reed. He is the god who pins the great catfish of earthquakes beneath a stone, the deity whose sword fell from heaven through a storehouse roof to guide the first emperor of Japan. His story is a saga of divine conquest, martial might, and the birth of sumo itself.

Takemikazuchi is portrayed as a decisive, authoritative, and powerful deity. He is direct in his dealings, as seen when he demands the surrender of Izumo. He is also shown to be physically overwhelming, as in his wrestling match with Takeminakata. He is a martial deity, embodying the might of the heavenly gods.

Powers
Thunder and Lightning Control

As a god of thunder, Takemikazuchi is associated with lightning and storms. However, the primary texts do not explicitly describe him using thunder as a weapon.

Limit: Not explicitly detailed in the myths.

Supernatural Strength

In the wrestling match with Takeminakata, Takemikazuchi crushes his opponent's hand 'like a young reed,' demonstrating immense physical power.

Limit: None stated.

Sword Mastery and Divine Weapon

Takemikazuchi is closely associated with swords. He uses the Totsuka-no-Tsurugi as a seat and later sends his sword, Futsu-no-mitama, to aid Emperor Jimmu. The sword is said to have the power to fell evil deities spontaneously.

Limit: The sword's power is only demonstrated in the Jimmu episode.

Subduing Earthly Deities

Takemikazuchi is sent by the heavenly gods to subjugate the terrestrial deities of the Middle Country. He successfully persuades Kotoshironushi to yield and defeats Takeminakata in combat.

Limit: He does not subdue all deities himself; some yield without direct conflict.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Takemikazuchi is not described in detail in the primary texts. In art, he is often depicted as a fierce warrior in armor, sometimes holding a sword or a lightning bolt. In the namazu-e prints, he is shown as a powerful figure pressing down on a giant catfish with a stone or a sword.

Symbols — His primary symbol is the sword, particularly the Totsuka-no-Tsurugi (ten-fist sword) and the Futsu-no-mitama. He is also associated with thunder, lightning, and the 'pinning rock' (kaname-ishi) that restrains the earthquake catfish.

Domains — His primary domain is Kashima (modern Ibaraki Prefecture), where the Kashima Shrine is located. He is also associated with the sea, as he and his companions land on a shore and sit on a sword atop a wave. He is a god of thunder, war, and the imperial house.

Accounts I
Birth from Izanagi's SwordKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (mythological)

After Izanagi returns from the underworld, he beheads the fire god Kagu-tsuchi. The blood from his ten-fist sword gives birth to several deities, including Takemikazuchi, who emerges from the blood near the base of the blade. Takemikazuchi is born as a powerful deity associated with the sword and thunder.

Accounts II
Quelling of the Middle CountryKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Age of the Gods (mythological)

Amaterasu and Takamusubi decide to send Takemikazuchi (or his father) to conquer the terrestrial world. He and Ame-no-torifune land at Izumo, sit on an inverted sword, and demand that Ōkuninushi surrender. After Ōkuninushi consults his sons, Kotoshironushi yields, but Takeminakata challenges Takemikazuchi to a strength contest and is defeated. Takeminakata is exiled to Suwa. The Middle Country is subjugated by the heavenly gods, and Takeminakata becomes the deity of Suwa Grand Shrine. This episode is considered the mythical origin of sumo wrestling.

Assisting Emperor JimmuKojiki, Nihon Shoki

Mythological imperial era

During Jimmu's eastern expedition, he and his troops are incapacitated by a bear or poison fumes. Takakuraji presents a sword that Jimmu uses to subdue the evil deities. Takakuraji explains that in a dream, Amaterasu and Takamusubi commanded Takemikazuchi to descend, but he said it was enough to send his sword, which he deposited through the roof of Takakuraji's storehouse. Jimmu is able to continue his conquest, and the sword, known as Futsu-no-mitama, becomes the main object of worship at Isonokami Shrine.

Takemikazuchi is the same as Futsunushi. — They are distinct deities in the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki, though they have similar roles and are often paired.

Takemikazuchi is the god of sumo wrestling. — He is not the patron deity of sumo, but his wrestling match with Takeminakata is considered the mythical origin of the sport.

Takemikazuchi is a purely benevolent deity. — He is a martial deity who uses force and intimidation to achieve his goals, reflecting the violent nature of some myths.
