Who It Is

Aryong-Jong sits at the strange boundary between queen and goddess — born from a dragon, married to another dragon-born king, and later worshipped when the sky went dry. She is proof that in Korea's oldest legends, sovereignty and weather were governed by the same bloodline.

In Korean tradition, she is associated with rain rituals and fertility. According to later legend, she was called upon during drought through prayer while water was poured through a sieve, simulating rain.

Powers
Rain-making and weather influence

As a rain goddess, Aryong-Jong is called upon in times of drought. Ritual invocations paired physical action with prayer, pouring water through a sieve to simulate rain and appeal to her power.

Limit: No surviving detailed records describe the exact conditions under which the ritual succeeded or failed.

Dragon-born origin

Legend states she was born from a dragon, making her a liminal being between celestial, royal, and chthonic domains. This origin supports her later status as first queen of Silla.

Limit: The historicity of dragon birth is mythological rather than literal.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No surviving visual description from primary Korean mythological texts survives. Later historiography and legend describe her as a woman born from a dragon, not as an anthropomorphic goddess with fixed iconographic attributes.

Symbols — Dragon, well, sieve or perforated vessel used in rain-making ritual, egg or cockatrice egg in variant traditions.

Domains — Rain, weather, agricultural fertility, royal legitimacy.

Accounts I
Birth from the dragon and the founding of SillaSamguk Yusa; Samguk Sagi; Swedish Wikipedia 'Aryong-Jong'; English Wikipedia 'Lady Aryeong'

Legendary/early historic (traditionally 1st century BCE)

Lady Aryeong was born from the side of a dragon that appeared near a well. She married Hyeokgeose of Silla, founder of the Silla kingdom. Together, the dragon-born couple became the first royal pair of Silla. The two major chronicles differ on whether she emerged from the left side (Samguk Yusa) or the right side (Samguk Sagi) of the dragon. Her marriage established the royal lineage of Silla and linked the dynasty to dragon symbolism, reinforcing kingship through divine or semi-divine birth.

Accounts II
Rain-making ritual and posthumous worshipSwedish Wikipedia 'Aryong-Jong'; Korean ritual history

Post-legendary / Joseon-era practice

After her death, Aryong-Jong was worshipped as a goddess of rain. During drought, she was invoked through prayer while water was poured through a sieve, an action meant to simulate rain and petition her aid. The pilgrimage site associated with her origin became a place where women prayed for fertility. The ritual reinforced her role as a fertility and rain deity. The pilgrimage site became embedded in local religious practice.

Arirang etymological legendEnglish Wikipedia article 'Arirang'

Later tradition

Some traditions trace the term 'Arirang' to the name of Lady Aryeong, suggesting the famous Korean folk song may have evolved from a poem praising her. This link connects royal foundation mythology with Korean musical folklore. If true, the etymology would tie Korea's most famous folk song directly to Silla's founding myth.
