The Hands on the Wheel, The Phantom Hands of Dartmoor

I was riding home along the Postbridge road when I felt it -- a pull on the handlebars. I looked down, and there they were: two great, hairy hands, gripping the bars, trying to steer me off the road into the moor. I fought them, but I don't know how I survived.

Who It Is

The Hairy Hands is a uniquely specific and terrifying British urban legend from Dartmoor in Devon. According to reports dating from the early 20th century, a stretch of road (the B3212 near Postbridge) is haunted by a pair of disembodied, hairy, ape-like hands that appear on the steering wheel of vehicles -- particularly motorcycles and open-topped cars -- attempting to wrench control from the driver and force them off the road. The hands are described as large, muscular, covered in dark hair, and possessing terrifying strength. The first documented incident occurred in 1910, when a motorcyclist lost control on the road and crashed; after recovering, he claimed a pair of large, hairy hands had gripped his handlebars and forced him off the road. Multiple similar accounts followed through the 1910s and 1920s, including a 1921 report in which a young couple in a sidecar motorcycle both claimed to see the hands grab the bars before they crashed. The most tragic associated incident was the death of Dr. E.H. Helby, the medical officer at Dartmoor Prison, who was killed in a motorcycle crash on the same road in 1921 -- though he did not report seeing the hands; his death was later attributed posthumously to the legend. Some accounts suggest the phenomenon was caused by a malevolent spirit of a man killed on the road, a vengeful pixie (Dartmoor is rich in pixie folklore), or an ape escaped from a traveling circus. Skeptics attribute the accidents to the dangerous nature of the road itself (sharp turns, poor visibility) combined with the power of suggestion and mass hysteria. The Hairy Hands remain a classic example of British roadside paranormal folklore.
