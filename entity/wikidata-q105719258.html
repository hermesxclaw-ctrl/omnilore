The Approach

In a world where magic is weaponized and gods walk among mortals, Iron Tager stands as a monument to scientific ambition—a man transformed into a living engine of destruction, yet bound by a loyalty that defies his monstrous form.

Content advisory. Contains themes of body horror, scientific experimentation, and violence inherent to a fighting game narrative.

Etymology

The name 'Tager' is likely derived from the German word 'Tager' meaning 'day' or from 'Tiger', but official sources have not confirmed an etymology. 'Iron' refers to his metallic body.

Cultural Ownership

BlazBlue is a Japanese fighting game series created by Arc System Works. Iron Tager is a fictional character owned by Arc System Works.

Disputed Identifications

There is no significant dispute over Tager's identity, but some fan theories speculate about his original human name and appearance, which have never been officially revealed.

Description

Appearance

Iron Tager is a massive, imposing figure with a body almost entirely composed of metal. He has a large, barrel-shaped torso, thick arms, and a head that is mostly covered by a helmet-like structure with glowing red eyes. His body is covered in rivets and pipes, and he has a large exhaust pipe on his back. His color scheme is primarily red and black, with metallic gray accents.

Symbols & Iconography

Tager's iconography is that of a 'living weapon' – he is often depicted with electrical arcs, heavy machinery, and a red scarf that contrasts with his metallic body. The scarf is a notable symbol of his humanity and his connection to his creator, Kokonoe.

Behaviour & Personality

Tager is calm, stoic, and fiercely loyal to his creator, Kokonoe. He speaks in a low, measured tone and rarely shows emotion. He is highly disciplined and follows orders without question, but he also displays a sense of honor and a protective nature towards those he considers allies. Despite his intimidating appearance, he is not malevolent and often acts as a mentor figure to younger characters.

Domains & Habitats

Tager is typically found in the NOL's laboratories or in Kokonoe's secret base, where he serves as her chief enforcer and assistant. He is also seen in various battlefields across the BlazBlue world, often deployed to retrieve or protect important objects or individuals.

Version Differences

Across the games, Tager's design has remained largely consistent, with minor updates to his armor and effects. In Central Fiction, he gains a new move, 'Gigantic Tager', which is a super move that reflects his increased power. His role also shifts from a simple enforcer to a more central figure in the fight against the main antagonist, Terumi.

Tager's Origin and Creation

Source BlazBlue: Calamity Trigger - Character ProfileCulture Japanese video gamePeriod Before the events of Calamity Trigger

Summary

Tager was originally a human scientist who was gravely injured in an accident. He was saved by Kokonoe, a brilliant scientist and a member of the NOL, who rebuilt him as a cyborg. Tager became Kokonoe's loyal assistant and enforcer, carrying out her orders in her quest to defeat the NOL and uncover the truth about the Azure.

Forced Cyborgization

Source BlazBlue games - character profile

Description

Tager was originally a human who was severely injured and then rebuilt as a cyborg by Kokonoe. While this saved his life, it also stripped him of much of his humanity, and it is implied that the process was painful and non-consensual in some interpretations.

Scholarly Context

The theme of cyborgization and loss of humanity is a common trope in science fiction and cyberpunk, often exploring the ethics of human enhancement and the definition of identity.

Use as a Weapon

Source BlazBlue games - story mode

Description

Tager is often used as a weapon by Kokonoe, who deploys him on dangerous missions without regard for his safety. He is treated as a tool rather than a person, which raises questions about autonomy and exploitation.

Scholarly Context

This reflects the broader theme of individuals being used as pawns in larger conflicts, a recurring motif in the BlazBlue series.

Violence

Source BlazBlue games - gameplay

Description

As a combatant, Tager engages in brutal physical confrontations, often using his strength to crush and throw opponents. The game's violence is typical of fighting games and is not graphic, but it is present.

Scholarly Context

Fighting games inherently involve violence, but Tager's fighting style emphasizes raw power and intimidation.

Contradictions & Disputes

Tager's Original Name

Position A

Some fans speculate that Tager's original name was 'Tager' even before his cyborgization.

Position A — evidence

The name 'Tager' is used consistently across all media, and no alternate name has been given.

Position B

Others believe he had a different name that has never been revealed.

Position B — evidence

The fact that he was a different person before his transformation suggests he might have had a different name.

Scholarly Consensus

No official confirmation. The consensus is that his original name is unknown and likely irrelevant to his character.

Tager's Humanity

Position A

Some interpret Tager as having lost all his humanity, becoming a cold machine.

Position A — evidence

His stoic demeanor and lack of emotional expression.

Position B

Others see him as retaining his humanity, as shown by his loyalty and protective nature.

Position B — evidence

His actions often show care for others, such as protecting Makoto and respecting Ragna.

Scholarly Consensus

Most fans agree that Tager retains his humanity, albeit suppressed, and this is supported by his character development.
