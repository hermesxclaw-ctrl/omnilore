Who It Is

Imagine a name whispered on two opposite ends of the known world — a sun-drenched island where men become pigs, and a golden city at the edge of the dawn, where a dragon guards a fleece of pure gold. Both are called Aea, and both are haunted by the same family of sorceresses. But was there ever a goddess behind the name? Or is Aea just a ghost — a word that outlived the deity it once named?

If Aea is understood as Circe (as some late traditions suggest), then her behavior is well-known: she is a powerful enchantress who is initially hostile to strangers, but becomes a helpful guide and lover to Odysseus after he resists her magic with the herb moly. She is meticulous, brewing potions and singing while she weaves on a loom. If Aea is the earth goddess, no behavior is recorded. If Aea is the city, its 'behavior' is that of a prosperous, magical, and dangerous frontier capital.

Powers
Enchantment and Transformation (if identified with Circe)

The power to transform humans into animals (pigs, lions, wolves) through magical potions and a wand. This is the defining power of Circe, and by extension, of her island Aeaea.

Limit: The transformation is not permanent; it can be reversed with another herb (moly) and a counter-spell. The magic is ineffective against a prepared or protected hero.

Geographic Sanctity and Liminality

The island of Aeaea is a liminal zone, a place where the normal rules of mortality, hospitality, and nature are suspended. It is a threshold to the underworld (Odysseus visits Hades from Aeaea).

Limit: The power is tied to the place itself, not to a person. It is not a 'power' in the sense of an active ability, but a property of the location.

Royal and Solar Authority (if identified with the city of Aea)

The city of Aea is the seat of King Aeëtes, son of Helios. The city is thus bathed in solar authority and is the center of a powerful, wealthy, and magical kingdom.

Limit: This is a property of the city, not a personal power. It is vulnerable to invasion (by the Argonauts).

Accounts I
Odysseus and Circe on AeaeaHomer, Odyssey, Books X-XII

c. 8th century BCE

Odysseus and his men arrive at the island of Aeaea. Circe invites the men to a feast and turns them into swine. Odysseus, protected by the herb moly given to him by Hermes, resists her magic and forces her to restore his men. He then becomes her lover and stays on the island for a year. Before he leaves, she instructs him on how to travel to the Underworld to consult the prophet Tiresias, and later warns him of the dangers of the Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis. Odysseus gains crucial knowledge for his journey home. Circe becomes an ally and a mother to his son Telegonus (in later traditions). The island is forever marked as a place of dangerous enchantment.

Accounts II
The Argonauts and the Golden Fleece in AeaApollonius of Rhodes, Argonautica; Pindar, Pythian Ode 4

c. 3rd century BCE (Apollonius); 5th century BCE (Pindar)

Jason and the Argonauts sail to the city of Aea in Colchis to retrieve the Golden Fleece, which is nailed to an oak tree in a grove sacred to Ares, guarded by an unsleeping dragon. King Aeëtes, the ruler of Aea, is hostile and sets Jason impossible tasks (yoking fire-breathing bulls, sowing dragon's teeth). Medea, Aeëtes' daughter and a powerful sorceress, falls in love with Jason and uses her magic to help him succeed and steal the Fleece. The Argonauts escape with the Fleece and Medea, who kills her own brother Absyrtus to delay pursuit. The city of Aea is left in turmoil. Medea's story continues with tragedy in Corinth, where she murders her children by Jason.

The location of AeaAeaea (Circe's island) is in the western Mediterranean, near Italy.Aea (the city) is in the eastern Black Sea, in Colchis (modern Georgia).

Aea is a major goddess in the Greek pantheon. — Aea is not attested as a goddess in any ancient text. It is a place name. The goddess interpretation is a modern scholarly hypothesis with no direct evidence.

The island of Aeaea and the city of Aea are the same place. — They are distinct places in the myths. Homer's Aeaea is a mythical island in the west. Apollonius' Aea is a city in the east (Colchis). They are linked only by the family of Circe and Aeëtes.

Aea is another name for Medea. — Medea is from Aea (the city), but she is never called Aea. The name Aea is the place, not the person.
