The Approach

In the shadowed highlands of ancient Arcadia, a name flickers like a distant fire: Peraethus, son of the infamous Lycaon, whose very name means 'to kindle' — yet whose story survives only as a spark among the ashes of Greek myth.

Content advisory. Contains references to mythological violence (Lycaon's impiety and Zeus's punishment), but no explicit graphic content.

Peraethus

Pronunciation /pɛˈriːθəs/ (English approximation)

Known As

PeraithosPeraethenses (eponym)

Etymology

Ancient Greek Περαιθεῖς, from περαιθειν (peraithein) meaning 'to light up, kindle'.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically Arcadian local tradition.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars conflate Peraethus with other Lycaonids due to fragmentary sources; no major disputes.

Stories & Myths

Eponymous Foundation of Peraethenses

Source PausaniasCulture Greek (Arcadia)Period Mythical era

Summary

Peraethus, as one of the fifty sons of Lycaon, was reputed to have founded the Arcadian town of Peraethenses.

Participants

Peraethus

Consequences

The town bore his name.

Variants

None recorded.

Relationships

Lycaon

Type fatherSource Apollodorus, Pausanias

Description

Peraethus was one of the fifty sons of Lycaon, the impious king of Arcadia.

Version Note

Lycaon's impiety led to Zeus's punishment; sons were often killed or transformed.

Conflicting Accounts

Mother varies: Cyllene, Nonacris, or unknown woman.

Cyllene, Nonacris

Type mother (disputed)Source Various scholia

Description

Various naiads are given as mothers of Lycaon's sons.

Version Note

Not all sons have the same mother.

Conflicting Accounts

Only some sons are attributed to these naiads; Peraethus's mother is uncertain.

Lycaon's impiety

Source Apollodorus, Pausanias

Description

Lycaon's actions (possibly including human sacrifice) led to Zeus's punishment, often involving the destruction of his sons.

Scholarly Context

This myth reflects ancient Greek concerns about hospitality and piety.
