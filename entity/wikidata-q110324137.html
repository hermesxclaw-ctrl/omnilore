Who It Is

At the very bottom of existence, beneath the earth you stand on and beneath even the black waters that hold it up, Mandaean scripture places a final, lightless ground: Siniawis, the lower earth of darkness. It is not a hell where souls are punished, but the cold floor of the World of Darkness itself — the exact opposite of a luminous, life-giving ocean called Piriawis in the World of Light.

Siniawis is a place, not a being, and has no agency, personality, speech, or behavior. It does not act, create, or rule. It is inert ground that contains or supports other dark beings (Ruha, Ur, Krun and their offspring) in some interpretations, though GR 5.1 does not explicitly say Siniawis houses them. Distinction: attested as passive location; disputed whether it is inhabited or merely foundational.

Cosmological Antithesis to Piriawis

Siniawis functions as the structural opposite of Piriawis, the great Jordan/water of the Lightworld. Where Piriawis is luminous, living, flowing water that nourishes lightbeings, Siniawis is dark, fixed earth that anchors the World of Darkness. This is not a 'power' but a cosmological role: to complete the dualist symmetry of Light vs Darkness.

Limit: No active ability; purely positional/symbolic. Cannot be invoked, wielded, or personified.

Foundation of the Dark Realm

As arqa titaita (lower/second earth), Siniawis serves as the ultimate floor or foundation of the World of Darkness, below which nothing is described. In vertical cosmologies, it prevents infinite regress of darkness and provides a base for the Black Waters and intermediate dark earths.

Limit: Does not support creation or life; unlike Lightworld earths, it is sterile and will be annihilated at the end of days according to Mandaean eschatology (though GR 5.1 does not state this explicitly for Siniawis).

Eschatological Annihilation (Implied)

Mandaean eschatology holds that at the end of days the World of Darkness, including its earths and mountains, will be consumed or sealed away and will not persist. While Siniawis is not named in eschatological passages, by being part of alma ḏ-hšuka it shares this fate. The Lightworld alone endures.

Limit: Attribution is reconstructive/inferred, not directly attested for Siniawis by name. No spell or ritual targets Siniawis specifically.

Accounts I
The Catalogue of Light and Darkness (Right Ginza 5.1 — The Book of the Underworlds)Ginza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 5, Chapter 1 (Lidzbarski 1925 pp. 121-124; Gelbert 2011 pp. 153-158; Petermann 1867 pp. 121-127)

Late antiquity, Sasanian period (3rd-7th c. CE) in final redaction; manuscripts 16th-19th c.

The chapter opens with a systematic revelation of cosmology, attributed to a Lightworld messenger. It enumerates the regions of the World of Light — including Piriawis, the great Jordan of living waters — and then the corresponding regions of the World of Darkness. Siniawis is named in this second list as 'the lower earth of darkness' (arqa titaita ḏ-hšuka), placed among the Black Waters, the stinking sea, and the abodes of Ruha and her brood. The text presents no narrative action, only a descriptive inventory that establishes the moral and physical geography of the universe: light above, darkness below, with Tibil suspended between. Provides the only direct scriptural definition of Siniawis and establishes the paired dualism that structures all later Mandaean cosmology. All subsequent scholarly references derive from this passage.

The Emanation of Darkness and the Black WatersRight Ginza Book 5, Chapters 1-2; Right Ginza Book 3; summarized by Rudolph 1960 and Buckley 2002

Late antiquity

Before Tibil is made, Darkness (hšuka) emanates or is revealed as a self-existing principle opposite the Great Life. From it come the Black Waters (mia siawia) — fetid, cold, lightless waters — and the dark earths. Siniawis belongs to this primordial dark stratum. The story is not a creation by a deity but an exposition of co-eternal principles: Light did not create Darkness; both exist, and Darkness generates its own earths and seas without light. Siniawis is thus uncreated by Lightbeings and inherently impure. Explains why Siniawis cannot be redeemed or illuminated; it is ontologically dark. Underpins Mandaean ritual need for repeated baptisms (masbuta) in living water to wash off contamination from the dark world.

Ptahil, Ruha and the Creation of Tibil Above the Dark WatersRight Ginza Book 3 (The Creation of the World); Haran Gawaita; Drower 1937 pp. 23-27

Late antiquity

The demiurgic figure Ptahil (son of Abatur) is commissioned by the Lightworld to create the material world, but he fails to do so purely and mixes with Ruha (Spirit, queen of the World of Darkness) and her son Ur. Together they fashion Tibil (the earth) and fashion Adam Pagria (the physical Adam). Tibil is constructed above and from the Black Waters, suspended between Light and Darkness. Siniawis lies far below this construction, as the lowest earth of the dark realm that Ptahil looked down upon. The story explains Tibil's flawed, mortal nature: because its foundations touch the dark waters and dark earths like Siniawis, it is not wholly light. Situates Siniawis in the vertical cosmos as the depth from which Tibil is separated. Explains Mandaean ambivalence toward the material world and the need for the soul (nišimta) to ascend past the dark earths after death.

Accounts II
Hibil Ziwa's Descent into the World of DarknessRight Ginza Book 5, Chapter 3 (Hibil Ziwa cycle); Left Ginza 3.9; Diwan Abatur

Late antiquity

The Lightbeing Hibil Ziwa (also Abel Ziwa), with his brothers Shitil and Anush, descends from the Lightworld into the World of Darkness to subdue Ruha, Ur, and Krun and to fetch the mysteries. He traverses the Black Waters, the abodes of the dark rulers, and the mountains of darkness. While Siniawis is not named in the descent narrative itself, the itinerary passes through the dark earths catalogued in GR 5.1, of which Siniawis is the lowest. In some priestly commentary, Siniawis is identified as the ultimate depth Hibil reaches before ascending. He binds Ur and Krun, deceives Ruha, and returns with stolen power (the mysteries/gnosis) needed to create Adam's soul. Demonstrates that even Lightbeings can enter the dark earths but do not remain; establishes precedent for the soul's post-mortem ascent past the same regions. Siniawis as lowest point marks the limit of the descent.

The Ascent of the Soul (Nishimta) Past the Watch-Houses and Dark EarthsQolasta (Canonical Prayerbook) — prayers for the dying and ascension (e.g., Drower 1959 nos. 1-31, 59-72); Left Ginza Book 3 (Book of Souls); Right Ginza Book 15

Late antiquity to present (liturgical use continuous)

After death, the soul (nišimta) accompanied by its helper (a Lightbeing, often Hibil or Manda ḏ-Haiyi) must ascend northward through the mataratta (watch-houses/toll-houses) guarded by Ruha and her sons, across the Black Waters, and away from the dark earths. Liturgical texts describe the soul being shown the dark earths and the stinking sea and being told 'this is not your place.' Siniawis, as the lower earth of darkness, represents the realm the soul must definitively leave behind; to fall back toward Siniawis is to be lost to the Lightworld. Successful souls are baptized in Piriawis/Jordan in the Lightworld, the antithesis of Siniawis. Gives Siniawis its soteriological meaning: it is what salvation is salvation from. Explains why Mandaean death rituals (masiqta, abahata) emphasize guiding the soul upward away from darkness.

The End of Days: Sealing and Annihilation of the World of DarknessRight Ginza Book 3, Book 15, Book 18; Left Ginza 3.60-69; summarized by Buckley 2002 pp. 89-92 and Rudolph 1961

Late antiquity

At the end of time, after all light-souls have been gathered from Tibil, the Lightworld will draw up and seal off the World of Darkness. The Black Waters will dry, the dark mountains (including Krun) will collapse, and the dark earths will be consumed by fire or sealed in eternal darkness, unable to harm the Lightworld again. While Siniawis is not individually named in eschatological chapters, it is included by category as part of alma ḏ-hšuka that is destroyed or quarantined. The Lightworld, with Piriawis, alone remains perfect and eternal. Provides the ultimate fate of Siniawis: impermanence. Unlike Piriawis which is eternal, Siniawis is contingent and doomed, reinforcing Mandaean ethical dualism.

Siniawis is a demon or god of the underworld, similar to Nergal or Ereshkigal. — Siniawis is a place — the lower earth of darkness — not a being. It has no agency, iconography, or cult. The rulers of the World of Darkness are Ruha, Ur, and Krun, not Siniawis.

Siniawis is the Mandaean name for hell where sinners are punished. — Mandaean cosmology has no hell of punishment in the Abrahamic sense. Siniawis is not a place of judgment or torture; the soul's failure is to remain trapped in the World of Darkness generally, not to be sent to Siniawis specifically. Judgment occurs at the watch-houses (mataratta) and Abatur's scales.

Siniawis and Piriawis are two names for the same place, or variant spellings. — They are deliberate opposites: Piriawis is luminous living water in the World of Light; Siniawis is dark earth in the World of Darkness. GR 5.1 presents them as antitheses in parallel clauses.

Siniawis appears throughout Mandaean literature — in the Qolasta, Haran Gawaita, and Diwan Abatur. — In published translations, Siniawis is attested only in Right Ginza 5.1. It does not appear by name in the Qolasta, Haran Gawaita, Diwan Abatur, or Left Ginza in Lidzbarski, Drower, or Gelbert translations. Broader darkworld lore is present, but the specific name is rare.

Siniawis has been adapted in games, films, or fantasy novels (e.g., as a dungeon or planet). — No documented adaptation in film, television, games, comics, or commercial fiction was found. Siniawis is known only in scholarly and community religious literature and in Wikidata. Any use in games would be homonym or unrelated namesake, not a direct adaptation.
