Who It Is

1st Prize is a robot who wants to hug you so desperately that he'll ram you through a wall to make it happen — and somehow, in Baldi's unhinged schoolhouse, this makes him both the most terrifying and the most useful character you'll meet. His marriage proposals are delivered in the monotone drone of a 1992 text-to-speech engine, his heart is held in place by what the game describes as 'cheap wires,' and the Safety Scissors you find on the floor are apparently the only thing standing between you and an eternity of being pushed headlong into your teacher. Does unconditional love become a threat if the lover literally cannot stop?

1st Prize is characterized throughout official and community documentation as an irrepressibly affectionate robot who lacks any apparent capacity for understanding personal boundaries or recognizing danger. His core behavioral loop is simple: wander the hallways of the Schoolhouse or Super Schoolhouse aimlessly until he spots the player, at which point he executes a slow, laborious 360°-capable turn (taking a full 24 seconds for a complete rotation) and then accelerates directly toward the player to deliver a 'hug' — which in mechanical terms means he rams into the player and push

Powers
Forced Locomotion / Hugging Push

1st Prize's primary and defining mechanic: upon sighting the player, he accelerates toward them and, on contact, physically carries the player along his trajectory until they collide with a wall or obstacle. The player cannot escape this push through normal movement; the push overrides player locomotion. This is framed narratively as an attempt to hug, but mechanically functions as forced displacement. The push speed can exceed the player's normal running speed, making it both a potential threat (pushing into Baldi) and an asset (rapid hallway traversal). Stamina does not regenerate during the push in Classic/Birthday Bash versions.

Limit:

Target Acquisition and Slow Turn

1st Prize continuously roams hallways searching for the player. Once he detects the player within his line of sight, he begins a slow reorientation — taking a full 24 seconds for a 360° turn — before accelerating. This extremely slow turn radius is both a weakness (players can often escape before he faces them) and a strategic element (if already facing the player, he charges immediately). He will track and pursue the player relentlessly once spotted; losing the player causes a state of sadness/idle searching rather than immediate reset.

Limit:

Window Destruction

In Baldi's Basics Plus and Classic Remastered, when 1st Prize travels at high speed and collides with a window, he shatters it. Broken windows can then be traversed by any character (including the player), opening new escape or entry routes into locked rooms. The window-break noise value (120) is extremely high, attracting Baldi and Dr. Reflex from a distance. In Classic and Birthday Bash, this ability does not exist — he cannot break windows in those versions.

Limit:

Noise Generation (Bang! Alarm)

When 1st Prize collides with a wall at high speed (in Plus/Classic Remastered), a loud 'Bang!' sound plays with a noise value of 64. This attracts Baldi and Dr. Reflex to the collision location, effectively functioning as an alarm beacon. Players can counteract this using the WD-NoSquee item, which silences his movement noises. In Birthday Bash, the thud sound occurs even at slow speeds but does not attract Baldi — a version-specific behavioral difference. The noise generation ability makes 1st Prize an inadvertent alarm system for Baldi.

Limit:

Wire Vulnerability (Safety Scissors Counter)

1st Prize's wires, described in-game as 'cheap wires,' can be cut by the Safety Scissors item. When his wires are cut, he immediately stops all movement and spins in place for 30 seconds (15 seconds in Classic/Birthday Bash), unable to push the player. Multiple Safety Scissors uses accumulate the spin-out duration. This is his only known weakness and the primary counter available to the player. The store description of Safety Scissors explicitly mentions cutting 'cheap wires,' canonically linking the item to 1st Prize.

Limit:

Techno-Boots Bypass (Passive Vulnerability)

When the player equips the Techno-Boots item, 1st Prize loses the ability to push them — he passes through the player without effect. This does not stop him from moving or making noise; it only negates the forced-displacement mechanic. The Faculty Nametag (added in V0.7.1) similarly makes him ignore the player entirely, preventing pursuit.

Limit:

Campfire Frenzy Player Control

In the Camping Field Trip minigame within Baldi's Basics Plus, 1st Prize is temporarily under the player's direct control. The player uses him as a vehicle/tool to throw fuel items toward the campfire, with the objective of keeping the fire burning. In this mode, he makes no noise, does not pursue independently, and presents no threat to the player — a complete inversion of his normal role. This represents the only context in which 1st Prize becomes an instrument of player agency rather than opposition.

Accounts I
The First Sighting: Debut in Version 1.3Baldi's Basics in Education and Learning V1.3 changelog and wiki documentation

2018

1st Prize was absent from the original release of Baldi's Basics (V1.0, March 31, 2018) and was added in the V1.3 update as a new character. His introduction narrative was delivered via the in-game description card: 'Won 1st Prize at the Science Fair! Loves hugging people, rushing towards anyone it sees. Sadly, it turns super slowly.' This sparse framing established his entire canonical identity — a prize-winning robot science project with an uncontrollable drive to embrace. Players encountering him for the first time in the dark hallway spawning zone found his sudden appearance disorienting, as his googly-eyed, heart-wired form combined affectionate framing with threatening mechanical behavior. Established the dual nature of 1st Prize as simultaneously benign-intentioned and mechanically dangerous; became one of the most discussed characters in the game's early community reception.

The Birthday Surprise FinaleBaldi's Basics Birthday Bash (April 1, 2019)

2019

Baldi's Basics Birthday Bash, released as an anniversary event on April Fools' Day 2019, features a narrative ending in which the player navigates to the cafeteria's last exit. Upon reaching this destination, the entire cast of Schoolhouse characters — including 1st Prize — appears together to shout a birthday surprise for the player. This is the only canonical story moment in which 1st Prize acts cooperatively and celebratorily alongside all other characters rather than as an obstacle. Notably, 1st Prize does not wear any birthday-themed accessories in this version, unlike nearly all other characters, which the community has noted as a deliberate or accidental design choice. Established 1st Prize as part of the collective cast in a rare celebratory context; unique ending sequence reinforces the game's satirical framing of educational game conventions.

Accounts II
The Campfire Frenzy: Player Controls 1st PrizeBaldi's Basics Plus — Camping Field Trip minigame

2020 onward (Early Access)

In the Camping Field Trip of Baldi's Basics Plus, the player finds themselves at an outdoor campsite where Baldi chases them if the campfire goes out due to insufficient fuel. The Campfire Frenzy minigame inverts the usual player-1st Prize dynamic entirely: the player takes direct control of 1st Prize and uses him to throw fuel items toward the campfire. 1st Prize is the instrument of play, not a threat. He sits silently at the campsite otherwise, facing the fire and making no sounds — his affective voice lines and pursuit behavior are completely suspended. This minigame is one of the most tonally unusual sequences in the game, transforming the aggressive hugger into a cooperative tool. Provides a unique perspective on 1st Prize as a mechanical agent capable of directed, controlled action; the minigame was originally part of the Baldi's Basics Field Trip demo (July 24, 2018) released to promote the Kickstarter campaign.

The Deactivated Machine: Full Game Early Demo AppearanceBaldi's Basics Full Game Early Demo

Pre-2020 (demo period)

In the Baldi's Basics Full Game Early Demo, 1st Prize is encountered in a static, deactivated state in the faculty room — the same room where the Grappling Hook item is located. He can be viewed from multiple angles, demonstrating that his 3D model is fully rendered and present in the game world, but he does not move, speak, react, or respond to the player or even to BSODA sprays. This appearance functions as a preview or placeholder — the character exists in the space without enacting his usual behavior. The deactivated 1st Prize reads as an eerie inversion of his normal exuberant pursuit, the warm heart-robot reduced to an inert sculpture. Demonstrated 1st Prize's presence in the planned full game world; his stillness contrasts with his characteristic kinetic energy in other versions.

The Accidental Rescuer: Navigational Asset DynamicCommunity strategy guides and Steam discussions (notably Steam Guide ID 3382977784)

2020–2026 (ongoing Baldi's Basics Plus Early Access community documentation)

Through community play and documentation, players discovered that 1st Prize's forced push mechanic, while typically framed as a threat, can serve as a powerful survival tool. When 1st Prize pushes the player down a long hallway, their effective movement speed is dramatically increased without stamina expenditure — making 1st Prize an inadvertent escape vehicle from Baldi. Players have documented winning runs in which 1st Prize's push carried them through dangerous zones at speeds impossible to achieve independently. The Steam community guide cited one user's account: 'I just won my first round... something made it super easy... First prize. He can move you around, you won't even lose stamina even tho you're going much faster than normally.' 1st Prize can also break windows allowing entry into locked rooms, and his collision with the Principal's office windows can enable unlimited escapes from detention. Reframed 1st Prize in community understanding from pure obstacle to high-variance strategic element; his dual-use nature is now a central feature of advanced gameplay discussion.
