The Approach

In the grim, candlelit world of Re:Zero, few figures embody the terror of fanatic devotion as vividly as Petelgeuse Romanée-Conti—a man whose name evokes a legendary wine, but whose deeds are steeped in madness. He is the Witch Cult's most zealous Archbishop of Sin, a man who has torn his own body apart to prove his love, and whose very existence challenges the boundaries of sanity and loyalty.

Content advisory. Contains depictions of self-mutilation, psychological manipulation, religious fanaticism, violence, and body horror. Some scenes may be disturbing to sensitive readers.

Etymology

The name 'Petelgeuse' is a likely corruption or play on 'Betelgeuse', the star, while 'Romanée-Conti' is a famous and expensive Burgundy wine. This juxtaposition of celestial and luxurious elements reflects his grandiose self-image and twisted nobility. The author, Tappei Nagatsuki, often uses such names for symbolic effect.

Cultural Ownership

Petelgeuse is a fictional character created by Tappei Nagatsuki for the Japanese light novel series 'Re:Zero - Starting Life in Another World'. He is owned by the author and publisher (Kadokawa). The character has been adapted into anime, manga, and spin-off media.

Disputed Identifications

There is no real-world dispute about his identity, but within the story, his true identity is revealed to be that of a man named 'Betelgeuse' who was once a follower of the Witch of Envy. Some fans mistakenly conflate him with the star Betelgeuse or the wine, but these are only name inspirations.

Description

Appearance

Petelgeuse is a tall, gaunt man with pale skin and long, unkempt white hair. He has a thin, almost skeletal frame. His most distinctive feature is his face, which he habitually disfigures by tearing at his own skin and pulling it apart, often exposing muscle and bone. He wears a black tattered robe reminiscent of a clergyman's vestments, and his eyes are wild and bloodshot. His hands are often stained with blood from his self-inflicted wounds.

Symbols & Iconography

He is associated with the Witch Cult's symbol, a black flame or a stylized eye. He often carries a small, leather-bound book, which he reads obsessively. His signature pose involves stretching his arms wide and exclaiming 'Love love love love love!' in a maniacal manner.

Behaviour & Personality

Petelgeuse is a fanatical devotee of the Witch of Envy (Satella). He believes that 'love' is the ultimate virtue and that his own acts of self-mutilation are demonstrations of his love. He is highly erratic, prone to fits of laughter and rage. He is also extremely possessive and jealous, viewing any perceived insult to the Witch as a grave sin. Despite his madness, he is cunning and methodical, using his authority as an Archbishop to command the Witch Cult's followers. He is also a skilled manipulator, able to sway others with his rhetoric, though his instability often undermines his plans.

Domains & Habitats

He operates within the Kingdom of Lugunica, primarily in the area around the Roswaal mansion and the nearby village of Arlam. He uses a hidden chapel in the forest as his base of operations. He is also seen in the capital city of Lugunica during his attempts to possess Subaru's body.

Version Differences

In the light novel, his backstory is more detailed, including his past as a human named Betelgeuse who was a follower of the Witch of Envy before her sealing. The anime condenses some scenes but remains faithful. In the manga, his design is slightly more exaggerated. There are no major contradictions between versions.

Powers & Abilities

Unseen Hands

Source Version Light novel, anime, manga

Description

Petelgeuse can manifest invisible, intangible hands that can grab, crush, and manipulate objects from a distance. These hands are invisible to normal sight but can be sensed by those with magical abilities. He can also use them to possess others by inserting his 'fingers' into their bodies, controlling them like puppets.

Limitations

The hands are not truly invisible to those with strong spiritual senses; they can be countered by magical barriers or by severing the connection. Possession requires physical contact and can be resisted by strong willpower.

Contradictions

In some scenes, the hands are described as having a limited number, while in others he seems to summon many. The exact limit is not consistently stated.

Authority of Sloth

Source Version Light novel, anime

Description

As the Archbishop of Sloth, Petelgeuse wields the Witch Factor of Sloth, which grants him the Unseen Hands and also enhances his physical resilience. The Authority allows him to ignore pain and continue fighting despite severe injuries.

Limitations

The Authority is tied to his mental state; when he is emotionally unstable, his control over it wavers. Using it excessively also accelerates his physical decay.

Contradictions

The exact nature of the Authority's power is not fully explained in the anime, leading to some fan speculation about its limits.

Possession

Source Version Light novel, anime

Description

By using his Unseen Hands, Petelgeuse can insert his fingers into a person's body and take control of their movements. He can also transfer his consciousness into a new body if his original is destroyed.

Limitations

Possession requires the victim to be alive and physically intact. The process is painful and can be resisted by those with strong willpower or magical protection. If the possessed body is killed, he can attempt to jump to another, but this is risky.

Contradictions

In the anime, he is shown to possess Subaru temporarily, but the mechanics are not fully detailed. The light novel provides more clarity.

Stories & Myths

The Witch Cult's Attack on the Roswaal Mansion

Source Re:Zero light novel, Arc 3; anime episodes 15-21Culture Japanese light novel/animePeriod Fictional timeline, year 1 of Subaru's arrival

Summary

Petelgeuse leads a group of Witch Cult followers to attack the Roswaal mansion and the nearby village, seeking to capture or kill those associated with the royal selection. He confronts Subaru Natsuki, who is trying to protect Emilia and the villagers. Petelgeuse's obsession with the Witch of Envy drives him to target Subaru, whom he sees as a heretic. Through multiple loops of Subaru's 'Return by Death' ability, Subaru eventually learns Petelgeuse's weaknesses and, with the help of allies like Rem and Beatrice, defeats him.

Consequences

Petelgeuse is killed, but his actions cause significant suffering and death among the villagers. Subaru's repeated deaths and resurrections take a toll on his psyche. The event also brings Subaru closer to Emilia and the others, strengthening his resolve.

Variants

In the light novel, the attack is more prolonged and includes additional scenes of Petelgeuse's backstory. The anime condenses some of these but retains the core narrative.

The Possession of Subaru

Source Re:Zero light novel, Arc 3; anime episode 21Culture Japanese light novel/animePeriod Fictional timeline, year 1

Summary

After being defeated, Petelgeuse attempts to possess Subaru's body as a last resort. He partially succeeds, taking control of Subaru's hand and causing him to attack his friends. Subaru, with the help of Beatrice and others, manages to expel Petelgeuse's spirit, ultimately destroying him for good.

Petelgeuse is permanently destroyed. Subaru is left with psychological scars from the experience. This event also reveals more about the nature of the Witch Factors.

Variants

The anime shows the possession in a more dramatic light, with Petelgeuse's voice echoing in Subaru's mind. The light novel provides more internal monologue from Subaru.

Description

Petelgeuse is utterly devoted to the Witch of Envy, viewing her as the embodiment of love. He believes that his self-mutilation is a form of worship and that he is her most loyal servant. His love is possessive and fanatical, and he is willing to kill anyone who speaks ill of her.

Version Note

In his past, he was a follower of the Witch before her sealing, and he has spent centuries trying to free her.

Self-mutilation

Source Anime episode 15, light novel volume 4

Description

Petelgeuse repeatedly tears at his own face and body, pulling skin and muscle, as a demonstration of his love for the Witch. This is depicted graphically in both the anime and light novel.

Scholarly Context

This can be interpreted as a representation of extreme religious zealotry and self-destructive devotion, often seen in cult-like behavior.

Mass murder

Source Anime episode 16-20, light novel volumes 4-6

Description

He orders the slaughter of villagers and mansion staff without hesitation, viewing them as obstacles to his mission. He also kills his own followers if they fail him.

Scholarly Context

This reflects the dehumanization of others in fanatical movements.

Possession and violation of body

Source Anime episode 21, light novel volume 6

Description

He uses his Unseen Hands to forcibly control others, which can be seen as a metaphor for violation and loss of autonomy. His attempt to possess Subaru is particularly invasive.

Scholarly Context

This can be analyzed as a horror trope of body snatching and loss of self.

Contradictions & Disputes

Extent of his power

Position A

Some fans believe Petelgeuse is one of the strongest Witch Cult Archbishops, based on his performance in the anime.

Position A — evidence

He is able to defeat several powerful characters in combat and use his Unseen Hands effectively.

Position B

Others argue that his mental instability makes him weaker than other Archbishops, as he is easily tricked and defeated.

Position B — evidence

He is repeatedly outwitted by Subaru, and his obsession leads to his downfall.

Scholarly Consensus

No official ranking exists, but the author has stated that all Archbishops are dangerous in their own ways.

His past as Betelgeuse

Position A

Some believe he was always insane, even before becoming an Archbishop.

Position A — evidence

His behavior as an Archbishop is consistently erratic.

Position B

Others think he was once a rational follower who was corrupted by the Witch Factor.

Position B — evidence

The light novel hints that he was once a normal human, and his devotion to the Witch may have driven him mad over centuries.

Scholarly Consensus

The author has not fully clarified his past, leaving it open to interpretation.
