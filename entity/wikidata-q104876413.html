The Approach

In the sprawling, bloodstained mythology of Five Nights at Freddy's, few figures loom as large—or as ambiguously—as William Afton. He is the smiling founder of Freddy Fazbear's Pizza, the grieving father, the cunning killer, and the revenant trapped in a decaying animatronic suit. His story is a horror tapestry woven from scattered minigames, cryptic voice lines, novels, and fan speculation, making him one of gaming's most debated villains.

Content advisory. Child murder, implied violence, psychological horror, disturbing imagery, discussion of death and trauma.
Identity

William Afton

Pronunciation AF-ton (rhymes with 'raft on')

Known As

SpringtrapScraptrapAftonThe Purple GuyDave MillerThe Yellow Rabbit

Etymology

The name 'Afton' is likely a reference to Afton, a brand of canned meat, fitting the character's connection to the Freddy Fazbear's Pizza chain. The alias 'Springtrap' refers to the spring-lock animatronic suit he is trapped in.

Cultural Ownership

Created by Scott Cawthon, owner of the Five Nights at Freddy's franchise. The character is a central figure in the franchise's mythology.

Disputed Identifications

Fans have debated whether the character seen in minigames as the 'Purple Guy' is always William Afton, or if some appearances might be another character. In the games, the identity of the killer is confirmed as William Afton through multiple sources, but the exact timeline and number of victims remain disputed.

Description

Appearance

In the minigames, Afton is depicted as a purple-skinned figure with a wide smile, often called the 'Purple Guy'. As Springtrap, he is a yellow-green rabbit animatronic with a decaying suit, exposed endoskeleton, and human remains visible. In the novels, he is described as a tall, thin man with gray hair and a gaunt face.

Symbols & Iconography

The spring-lock suits, particularly the yellow rabbit (Spring Bonnie), are his iconic symbol. His signature color is purple, and he is associated with the Fazbear brand.

Behaviour & Personality

Afton is intelligent, manipulative, and charismatic, presenting a friendly facade while being a cold-blooded killer. He is obsessed with creating animatronics and achieving a form of immortality. He shows no remorse for his victims.

Domains & Habitats

He is primarily associated with Freddy Fazbear's Pizza locations, including the original restaurant, the FNAF 2 location, and the Fazbear's Fright horror attraction. In the novels, he also has a house and a lab.

Version Differences

In the games, Afton is the main antagonist, but his backstory is fragmented. In the novels, he is more explicitly a serial killer with a different alias and personality. In the film, he is a technician who kills children to possess animatronics. The exact details of his family and motivations vary between continuities.

Powers & Abilities

Spring-lock suit manipulation

Source Version Game canon, novel canon, film canon

Description

Afton is skilled at using and surviving in spring-lock suits, which are designed to be worn by humans. He uses them to lure children.

Limitations

The suits are dangerous; if the spring-locks malfunction, they will impale the wearer, as seen with his fate.

Contradictions

None, consistent across media.

Animatronic engineering

Source Version Game canon (Sister Location, Pizzeria Simulator), novel canon

Description

Afton is a brilliant engineer who designs and builds animatronics, including the Funtime animatronics and possibly the original ones.

Limitations

No apparent limitations, but his creations often have homicidal tendencies.

Contradictions

In the games, it is implied he built the Funtime animatronics to capture children, while in the novels, he is more of a technician than a creator.

Immortality/Revenant state

Source Version Game canon (FNAF 3, Pizzeria Simulator, Security Breach)

Description

After being spring-locked, Afton's spirit survives within the Springtrap suit, unable to die. He is later burned but returns in later games.

Limitations

His body is decaying and he is in constant pain. He can be temporarily destroyed by fire, but his spirit persists.

Contradictions

The exact nature of his immortality is unclear; it may be due to remnant (a substance from souls) or his own will.

Stories & Myths

The Missing Children Incident

Source Game minigames (FNAF 2, FNAF 3), novelsCulture Video game horrorPeriod 1980s (in-universe)

Summary

William Afton lures five children into a back room and murders them, hiding their bodies in the animatronic suits. Their spirits then haunt the animatronics, causing them to attack adults at night.

Participants

William Afton

Five unnamed children (later identified as Gabriel, Jeremy, Susie, Fritz, and Cassidy in fan canon)

Consequences

The animatronics become haunted, leading to the shutdown of the original Freddy Fazbear's Pizza. The children's spirits are eventually freed in FNAF 3 after Springtrap is destroyed.

Variants

In the novels, the incident is slightly different: Afton kills Charlie (the protagonist's friend) and then later other children. In the film, he kills children and stuffs them in suits, but the details differ.

The Bite of '83

Source Five Nights at Freddy's 4 (2015)Culture Video game horrorPeriod 1983 (in-universe)

Summary

Afton's son (the Crying Child) is accidentally bitten by Fredbear animatronic during his birthday party, leading to his death. This event is depicted in FNAF 4 minigames.

Participants

William Afton

His son (unnamed, often called 'Crying Child' or 'Evan' by fans)

Fredbear

Consequences

The child dies, and this event likely motivates Afton's descent into madness and his obsession with animatronics.

Variants

The identity of the child and the exact circumstances are not fully clear. Some theories suggest it was a deliberate act by Afton or that the child survived.

The Spring-lock Failure

Source Five Nights at Freddy's 3 (2015), minigamesCulture Video game horrorPeriod 1990s (in-universe)

Summary

Afton, hiding in the Spring Bonnie suit, is spring-locked when the suit's mechanisms fail, impaling him. He becomes trapped in the suit, becoming Springtrap.

Participants

William Afton

Consequences

He survives but is horribly mutilated. He is later found and taken to Fazbear's Fright, where he escapes after the fire.

Variants

In the novels, he is spring-locked in a similar manner but is later discovered and killed by the protagonists. In the film, he is spring-locked in the same way.

The Fire at Fazbear's Fright

Source Five Nights at Freddy's 3 (2015)Culture Video game horrorPeriod 2023 (in-universe)

Summary

Afton, as Springtrap, is trapped in the Fazbear's Fright horror attraction, which is set on fire in an attempt to destroy him. He survives, however.

Participants

William Afton

The night guard (unnamed)

Consequences

The attraction burns down, but Springtrap escapes, leading to his appearance in later games.

Variants

None known.

The Afton Family Tragedy

Source Sister Location (2016), Pizzeria Simulator (2017), novelsCulture Video game horrorPeriod 1980s-1990s (in-universe)

Summary

Afton's daughter Elizabeth is killed by Circus Baby, an animatronic Afton built, leading to her spirit possessing the robot. Afton's son Michael (likely the protagonist of Sister Location) is sent to free her, but instead is scooped, his body hollowed out, and he becomes a walking corpse.

Participants

William Afton

Elizabeth Afton

Michael Afton

Circus Baby

Consequences

Afton loses his family, and Michael becomes a zombie-like figure who later seeks to destroy his father. This storyline is central to the later games.

Variants

In the novels, Elizabeth is killed by Baby, but Michael is not present. The exact fate of the family is different.

Relationships

Henry Emily

Type partner/co-founderSource Pizzeria Simulator (2017), novels

Description

Co-founder of Freddy Fazbear's Pizza. Henry is the creator of the original animatronics, while Afton is often seen as the business mind. Henry later seeks to destroy Afton.

Version Note

In the games, Henry's role is revealed in the ending of Pizzeria Simulator. In the novels, Henry is the father of Charlie and is killed by Afton.

Conflicting Accounts

The exact nature of their partnership and the division of labor is unclear. Some fans believe Afton built the animatronics, while others give that role to Henry.

Michael Afton

Type sonSource Sister Location, Pizzeria Simulator, FNAF 4 (as the older brother)

Description

Michael is Afton's son, who becomes the protagonist of several games. He is likely the night guard in FNAF 1, FNAF 2, and Sister Location, and he is the one who sets the fire in FNAF 3. He is determined to stop his father.

Version Note

In the games, Michael's identity is inferred from dialogue and clues. In the novels, Michael is not a major character.

Conflicting Accounts

Some fans believe the protagonist of FNAF 1 is Mike Schmidt, who is distinct from Michael Afton, but many theories equate them.

Elizabeth Afton

Type daughterSource Sister Location, Pizzeria Simulator

Description

Elizabeth is Afton's daughter, killed by Circus Baby. Her spirit possesses Baby, and she later becomes part of the amalgamation 'Ennard' and then 'Scrap Baby'.

Version Note

In the novels, Elizabeth is a central character, possessing Baby and later being freed.

Conflicting Accounts

None significant.

The Missing Children

Type victimsSource FNAF 1, FNAF 2, FNAF 3, novels

Description

Afton murders five children, whose spirits haunt the animatronics. They are the primary antagonists in the early games.

Version Note

The identities and number of victims vary slightly between games and novels.

Conflicting Accounts

In the games, there are five victims, but in the novels, there are more, and the details differ.

Spring Bonnie

Type wielder/creatorSource FNAF 3, FNAF 4, Sister Location (as Springlock suit)

Description

Spring Bonnie is the yellow rabbit animatronic that Afton uses to lure children. He becomes trapped in the suit as Springtrap.

Version Note

In the games, Spring Bonnie is a separate character, but Afton's use of it is central. In the novels, he uses a similar suit.

Conflicting Accounts

None.

Serial child murder

Source FNAF 2 minigames, FNAF 3 minigames, novels, film

Description

Afton murders multiple children, luring them with animatronic suits. He shows no remorse and continues his crimes over years.

Scholarly Context

The depiction of child murder in a mainstream video game has been criticized for its graphic nature, but also praised for handling it with a degree of restraint.

Body horror and mutilation

Source FNAF 3, film

Description

The spring-lock failure is a gruesome event where metal rods pierce Afton's body, leaving him a living corpse. This is depicted in FNAF 3 and the film.

Scholarly Context

The horror is visceral and has been analyzed as a metaphor for the consequences of technological hubris.

Domestic abuse and neglect

Source Sister Location, FNAF 4

Description

Afton's treatment of his children is neglectful and abusive, leading to their deaths. He shows no grief and continues his work.

Scholarly Context

The family dynamics are a point of analysis, with some critics seeing Afton as a critique of toxic masculinity.

Contradictions & Disputes

Number of victims

Position A

In the games, there are five missing children in the original incident, but other children are killed in later incidents.

Position A — evidence

FNAF 1-3 minigames show five children in the 'SAVE THEM' minigames.

Position B

In the novels, there are more victims, and the details are different.

Position B — evidence

The Silver Eyes mentions multiple victims, including Charlie.

Scholarly Consensus

The game canon is inconsistent, and the novels are a separate continuity, so there is no single answer.

Afton's role in creating animatronics

Position A

Afton is the creator of the Funtime animatronics and possibly the original ones.

Position A — evidence

Sister Location's Blueprints show Afton Robotics as the manufacturer.

Position B

Henry Emily is the creator of the original animatronics, while Afton is only a co-founder.

Position B — evidence

Pizzeria Simulator's Henry speech implies Henry created the original characters.

Scholarly Consensus

Most fans believe Henry created the originals, while Afton created the Funtimes, but it's not explicitly stated.

Afton's fate after the fire in FNAF 3

Position A

Afton survives the fire and returns as Scraptrap in Pizzeria Simulator.

Position A — evidence

Scraptrap is visually similar to Springtrap and is called 'Afton' in the credits.

Position B

Afton is destroyed in the fire, and Scraptrap is a separate entity.

Position B — evidence

Some fans argue that the fire should have killed him, and the design is inconsistent.

Scholarly Consensus

The official canon is that Afton survives, as confirmed by the games' story.

Known Gaps

The exact timeline of Afton's actions is unclear, including the order of murders and the creation of the animatronics. The fate of his wife is unknown. The extent of his knowledge of remnant and soul possession is not fully explained. The relationship between the game canon and the novel canon is ambiguous. Fan theories are abundant but not officially confirmed.
