Who It Is

They look like ordinary villagers at first—tired faces, worn clothes, the clatter of farm tools in a remote Spanish valley. Then the shouting starts, the eyes catch the light in an unnatural red, and the crowd moves with a terrifying, coordinated purpose. These are Ganados, humans turned into living livestock for a parasite called Las Plagas: intelligent, armed, and utterly devoted to a cult that has rewritten their bodies and their wills. Understanding them means tracing a fictional history of crusades, bioweapons, and the horror of watching a familiar human shape become a weapon that thinks.

Attested: Ganados retain basic human intelligence, speech, and coordination but exhibit extreme aggression toward outsiders and unwavering loyalty to Los Iluminados leadership. They speak Spanish (or pseudo-Spanish) phrases, shout threats, coordinate attacks, and use tools and weapons tactically. Their behavior is pack-oriented: they swarm intruders, flank, throw projectiles, and set ambushes. In daily life, they neglect hygiene and sanitation, leaving rotting food and corpses in living spaces, suggesting either Plaga-driven disregard for normal social norms or cult-induced nihilism. Powers
Enhanced physical resilience

Attested: Ganados can withstand multiple gunshot wounds, blunt trauma, and environmental hazards that would incapacitate normal humans. They continue fighting after severe injury, sometimes only collapsing when critical damage is inflicted or the Plaga is destroyed.

Limit: They remain vulnerable to sufficient ballistic damage, explosives, and decapitation. Once the host body is destroyed or the parasite is targeted (e.g., via specialized radiation therapy in lore), the Ganado ceases to function.

Retained intelligence and tool use

Attested: Unlike t‑virus zombies, Ganados retain human-level intelligence, speech, and fine motor skills. They wield farming tools, medieval weapons, firearms, explosives, and shields; they can operate machinery, set traps, and coordinate attacks.

Limit: Their strategic thinking is limited to cult objectives and parasite-driven aggression; they do not exhibit complex independent planning beyond ambushes and pursuit.

Pack mentality and coordinated aggression

Attested: Plagas are social organisms, and their hosts form collective groups. Ganados respond to calls, converge on intruders, and exhibit pack tactics such as surrounding the player, attacking from multiple angles, and retreating briefly to regroup.

Limit: Coordination is primarily short-range and reactive; Ganados can be lured into chokepoints or separated. Dominant Plaga hosts (e.g., Saddler) exert higher-level control, but if these are destroyed, collective organization collapses.

Parasite emergence and secondary attack forms

Attested: When a Ganado sustains severe damage, the adult Plaga may tear through the neck or destroyed head, emerging as a grotesque organism with whipping tendrils, bladed appendages, or bulbous sacs. These emergent forms can lash, impale, or grab targets, significantly increasing threat level.

Limit: Emergent Plagas are highly vulnerable to bright light (UV, flash grenades) and concentrated fire. Their photosensitivity means they rarely appear in full daylight unless forced by damage.

Central nervous system control

Attested: Plagas attach to the host’s central nervous system between the lungs and heart, exerting control over behavior, emotions, and loyalty. Dominant Plaga hosts can issue commands via ultra-high-frequency sound waves, which subordinate Plagas receive through specialized organs, causing Ganados to obey cult leaders like Osmund Saddler.

Limit: Control is not absolute; hosts may retain fragments of prior personality or routine, especially before full maturation. If the brain is destroyed or compromised, control fails.

Photosensitivity and vulnerability to light

Attested: Plagas are extremely sensitive to ultraviolet radiation and bright light. They avoid daylight, and emergent parasites can be destroyed by intense light sources such as flash grenades, which in gameplay instantly kill exposed Plagas while leaving the host body inert.

Limit: This weakness applies primarily to exposed parasites; internal Plagas in early infection stages are less directly affected by ambient light.

Biochemical enhancement and mutation potential

Attested: Plagas alter host biochemistry, increasing strength, durability, and sometimes causing gross mutations (e.g., El Gigante, other B.O.W. variants). Standard Ganados show enhanced stamina and pain tolerance; specialized variants may exhibit unique mutations such as elongated necks or hardened skin.

Limit: Not all hosts undergo extreme mutation; many remain outwardly human. Excessive mutation can reduce fine motor skills or cause hostility toward other hosts (e.g., Gigantes attacking Ganados).

Accounts I
The rural Spanish village outbreakResident Evil 4 (2005 and 2023), in‑game narrative and files

In-universe: early 21st century; real-world release 2005/2023

Attested: After the rediscovery of Las Plagas beneath Salazar Castle, bioweapons researcher and cult leader Osmund Saddler forms Los Iluminados and begins infecting villagers with Plaga eggs under the guise of religious baptism. The villagers become Ganados—loyal, violent followers who worship Saddler as a prophet and attack outsiders on sight. When U.S. agent Leon S. Kennedy arrives to rescue the kidnapped president’s daughter, Ashley Graham, he encounters the village Ganados, who relentlessly assault him with farming tools, explosives, and emergent parasites. Through exploration and recovered files, Leon learns that the villagers were once ordinary people coerced and transformed into living weapons. Attested: Leon’s mission results in the destruction of key Dominant Plaga hosts (Saddler, Méndez, Salazar) and the decimation of the Ganado population. The Spanish government later seals off the region, but some displaced locals who return are re-parasitized, suggesting ongoing risk. The event becomes a central case in Resident Evil’s bioterrorism chronology.

Accounts II
Historical pagan Ganados and Salazar’s crusadeResident Evil Wiki "Las Plagas" and "Ganado" (synthesizing in‑game cave art and files), TheGamer article

In-universe: Bronze Age origins; late 1400s–Spanish Inquisition era crusade

Attested/interpretive: Lore materials describe earlier waves of Plaga infection in a remote Spanish region, where locals became Ganados and engaged in pagan sacrificial rituals. Cave art at Wharf B depicts human sacrifices and Plaga worship. During the Reconquista and Spanish Inquisition, Count Gregorio Salazar leads a brutal crusade against these Ganados, slaughtering infected villagers and sealing surviving Plagas in underground caves beneath a mound. A fortress (later Salazar Castle) is built above the caves, and the Salazar line serves as castellans tasked with preventing another outbreak. Attested: The crusade temporarily ends the pagan Ganado cult but does so through mass violence and forced containment rather than cure. The sealed Plagas remain dormant until the modern era, when Ramon Salazar is manipulated into releasing them, leading to the events of Resident Evil 4.

Bioweaponization and global contextResident Evil Wiki "Las Plagas" and "Plaga", Capcom Database "Las Plagas"

In-universe: late 20th–early 21st century; real-world releases 2005–2009

Attested: Los Iluminados and later corporate actors (e.g., TRICELL) refine Las Plagas into multiple types (Subordinate, Dominant, Type 2, Type 3, Type 4). Ganados represent the Subordinate Plaga human host class used as servants, bodyguards, and private army units. While Resident Evil 5 focuses on Majini in Africa, Ganados remain a reference point for Plaga-based B.O.W.s—demonstrating the effectiveness of parasites that preserve host intelligence and coordination. Files and secondary sources frame Ganados as a prototype for later, more globally deployed Plaga hosts. Attested: The success of Ganados as controllable, intelligent B.O.W.s encourages further Plaga research, contributing to broader bioterrorism threats in the series. However, the destruction of Dominant Plaga hosts and key cult infrastructure in Spain limits immediate spread.
