Who It Is

The fallen archangel Lucifer, commander of the rebel angels, who famously declares 'Better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven.' Milton's Satan is charismatic, eloquent, psychologically complex, and arguably the poem's most compelling character — a tragic anti-hero who knowingly chose damnation.

Cultural Significance

The great aesthetic paradox: Milton, a devout Puritan, created a Satan so compelling that generations have mistaken him for the hero. The poem's theological project — 'to justify the ways of God to men' — is often overshadowed by Satan's magnificent speeches.
