Who It Is

In the shadow of Mount Helicon, where the Muses sang, a king's scheme unfolded—a plan to fill his halls with the seed of a hero. Among the fifty daughters sent to Heracles's bed, Heliconis's name survives only as a footnote to a legend, yet her son would carry the blood of the greatest Greek hero to the shores of Sardinia.

No direct characterization is provided in ancient sources. She is one of many daughters who bore children to Heracles. In one version, she is noted among those who slept with Heracles, but her personality is not described.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Heliconis (Ἑλικωνίς) means 'dweller on Helicon' in Ancient Greek, likely referencing Mount Helicon in Boeotia, associated with the Muses. It is a patronymic or toponymic epithet indicating her connection to the region.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No physical description of Heliconis survives in ancient sources. She is typically assumed to be a beautiful young princess, as are the other daughters of Thespius, but this is not attested.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography are associated with Heliconis. In ancient art, the daughters of Thespius are rarely depicted individually; they may appear as a group in some scenes of Heracles's labors.

Domains — Heliconis was a princess of Thespiae, a city in Boeotia, Greece. She likely resided in the royal palace of Thespius.

Accounts I
The Thespian DaughtersApollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.10; Diodorus Siculus, Library of History 4.29; Pausanias, Description of Greece 9.27; Athenaeus, Deipnosophistae 13.556

Classical to Roman era (5th century BCE - 2nd century CE)

King Thespius of Thespiae, wishing to have his fifty daughters bear children by the great hero Heracles, entertained Heracles as a guest while he hunted the Cithaeronian lion. Each night, Thespius sent a different daughter to Heracles's bed, often making him drunk so he did not realize he was with a different woman. Heliconis was one of these daughters, and she conceived a son named Phalias. In some versions, the intercourse took place over seven nights, with seven daughters each night. In another variant, all daughters slept with Heracles in a single night, except one who refused and was punished. Heliconis gave birth to Phalias, who became one of the many sons of Heracles. These sons were sent by Heracles to colonize Sardinia, according to some accounts.
