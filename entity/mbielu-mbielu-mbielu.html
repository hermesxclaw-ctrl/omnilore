Who It Is

Imagine standing on the bank of a Congo swamp in the late afternoon, watching the water lie flat and opaque—and then, without warning, a row of green, algae-coated ‘planks’ rises from the surface like the spine of some hidden machine. For the villagers of Bounila and Ebolo, that brief glimpse is all they claim to know of Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu, the ‘plated’ animal whose body remains stubbornly unseen. Whether it is misremembered reptile, folkloric river spirit, or the last echo of a dinosaur dream, the creature sits at the uneasy intersection of indigenous testimony and global hunger for living fossils.

Attested: Reports describe Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu as an amphibious or semi-aquatic animal that spends most of its time submerged in rivers and marshes of the Likouala region. It is said to be herbivorous, feeding on aquatic or riparian vegetation, and is most active in the late afternoon. There are no detailed accounts of aggression, vocalization, or interaction with humans; villagers’ testimony focuses on brief visual encounters. Powers
Herbivorous feeding

Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu is repeatedly described by local informants (as reported by Mackal and later writers) as herbivorous, feeding on plants rather than animals. This distinguishes it from crocodiles and many large reptiles in the region and is one reason cryptozoologists consider it anomalous.

Limit: No direct observation of feeding has been documented; herbivory is inferred from villagers’ statements and the absence of predatory behavior in stories.

Semi-aquatic lifestyle

The creature is said to be amphibious or semi-aquatic, spending most of its time submerged in rivers and marshes, with only its plated back visible above the surface. It is reportedly most active in the late afternoon.

Limit: No systematic observation exists; activity patterns and degree of aquatic adaptation are based on brief sightings and second-hand reports.

Dorsal plates (‘planks’)

The defining feature of Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu is a series of large, plank-like or plate-like structures protruding from its back, often covered in green algae. These plates are the basis for its name and for comparisons with stegosaurid dinosaurs.

Limit: Only the dorsal region is consistently described; the exact shape, number, and arrangement of plates are not standardized across accounts.

Territorial or elusive behavior (inferred)

The rarity of reported sightings and the lack of close encounters have led some writers to infer that Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu is either highly elusive or occupies remote, difficult-to-access territories within the swamp system.

Limit: This is an interpretive inference; no explicit indigenous statement about territoriality or shyness is recorded.

Accounts I
Reports from Bounila and Ebolo villagesRoy P. Mackal, A Living Dinosaur?; summarized in cryptid reference works and fan wikis.

Late 20th century (expeditions in 1979 and 1981; reports published 1987)

During expeditions to the Congo Basin in 1979 and 1981, biologist Roy P. Mackal visited the villages of Bounila and Ebolo while searching for the better-known cryptid mokele-mbembe. There he heard of another animal, Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu, described by villagers as an amphibious, herbivorous creature with plates on its back. Witnesses reported seeing the animal in nearby rivers and marshes, usually with most of its body submerged and only the plated back visible, sometimes covered in green algae. Mackal noted that the pygmy informants were unable to describe the entire body, and he regarded the animal as an ‘enigma.’ [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu) [[2]](https://cryptidz.fandom.com/wiki/Mbielu-Mbielu-Mbielu) [[0]](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu) [[1]](https://en-academic.com/dic.nsf/enwiki/2547378) These reports introduced Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu into cryptozoological literature as a distinct African water cryptid and prompted speculative identifications with stegosaurid dinosaurs and other reptiles.

Ikekesse near Epena sightingEncyclopaedia of Cryptozoology (Fandom), drawing on Mackal and secondary sources.

Undated (reported in late 20th-century cryptozoological literature)

One reported sighting of Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu allegedly occurred near Epena, at a place called Ikekesse, on an unspecified date. Witnesses claimed to see the animal emerging from the water, revealing its plated back. Details of the head, limbs, and tail were not recorded, and the report survives only as a brief note in cryptozoological compilations. The Ikekesse report is often cited to show that Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu is known beyond Bounila and Ebolo, but its vagueness underscores the fragmentary nature of the evidence.

Accounts II
Odette Gesonget’s stegosaur selectionRoy P. Mackal, A Living Dinosaur?; summarized in Encyclopaedia of Cryptozoology and Wikipedia.

1980 (selection event); reported 1987

In 1980, a woman from Bouanila named Odette Gesonget was shown books containing images of various animals by Roy P. Mackal. Asked which image resembled the animal her parents had told her about, she selected a picture of a Stegosaurus as matching Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu. However, she clarified that she had never personally seen the creature; her knowledge was second-hand. Mackal and later writers used this selection to support the hypothesis that Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu might be a living stegosaurid, though Mackal himself acknowledged the tenuous nature of the evidence. Gesonget’s choice became a key piece of anecdotal support for the ‘living stegosaur’ interpretation and influenced subsequent artistic reconstructions and popular imagination.

Cave paintings of elephant-like animals with plated backsRoy P. Mackal, A Living Dinosaur?; summarized in Encyclopaedia of Cryptozoology.

Prehistoric or historic (exact dating not provided)

Mackal cites anecdotal evidence of African cave paintings depicting elephant-like animals with stegosaur-like backs—large bodies with dorsal plates or ridges. He suggests that such imagery might reflect cultural memory of animals resembling Mbielu-mbielu-mbielu or support the idea of a living stegosaurid. However, the specific location, dating, and scholarly interpretation of these paintings are not detailed in cryptozoological sources. The cave painting claim is often mentioned in cryptozoological discussions as suggestive but remains weakly documented and has not been integrated into mainstream archaeological or paleontological scholarship.
