Who It Is

No flash, no wound, no scream — just a soft, floating lightness behind the eyes. Under the Imperius Curse, you feel perfectly happy to obey. That is what makes it the most insidious of the three Unforgivable Curses: the victim believes the master's will is their own, until someone strong enough learns to fight back from inside their own mind.

The curse does not have personality, but its effect on behavior is extensively attested: victim enters a highly suggestible, euphoric trance where the caster's voice/commands feel like the victim's own brilliant idea. Victim will perform any action ordered - from harmless acrobatics to murder, theft, or self-harm - without resistance unless they possess exceptional willpower. Victim typically has hazy or absent memory afterward, though strong-willed victims may recall being ordered. Powers
Total Mental Domination

Caster gains complete control over victim's thoughts and actions. Victim will obey any verbal or mental command, including actions against their morals, loyalties, or self-preservation. Described as 'I could make it jump out of the window, drown itself, throw itself down one of your throats' (Moody/Crouch Jr., Goblet of Fire).

Limit: Requires wand, incantation 'Imperio', and line of sight/proximity. Control is not permanent; must be maintained and can weaken if caster is distracted or victim is strong-willed. Victim must be a being with a mind susceptible to magic - effective on humans, goblins (Bogrod), possibly other sentient beings; unclear on house-elves, giants, or non-sentient creatures beyond spider demonstration.

Euphoric Compliance / Memory Suppression

Victim experiences blissful emptiness and perceives the caster's commands as their own good ideas. After release, victim often has incomplete or dreamlike memory of actions, enabling legal defense of 'I was Imperiused'.

Limit: Not absolute: strong-willed victims (Harry, Barty Crouch Jr. after years) retain some awareness. Memory suppression is variable; Ministry requires Veritaserum or Pensieve evidence to verify claim.

Resistance Through Willpower

Exceptionally strong-willed individuals can throw off the curse entirely. Harry Potter resists Moody/Crouch Jr.'s Imperius in class after four attempts, and later resists Voldemort's implied Imperius-adjacent influence. Barty Crouch Jr. eventually threw off his father's Imperius after prolonged exposure.

Limit: Extremely rare. Dumbledore notes 'many witches and wizards' cannot resist. Strength of caster matters: Voldemort and powerful Death Eaters maintain stronger holds. Resistance causes physical discomfort - Harry describes fighting as 'like trying to push a heavy weight'.

Prolonged Enthrallment and Personality Degradation

Long-term subjugation can cause mental damage, confusion, and breakdown. Barty Crouch Sr.'s long-term Imperius on his son and on house-elf Winky left both disoriented; Bertha Jorkins was left permanently damaged after combined memory charms and Imperius-adjacent coercion.

Limit: Damage is not guaranteed; Pius Thicknesse functioned as effective Minister while Imperiused for nearly a year. Duration and caster skill affect severity.

Political and Legal Weaponization

Imperius enables covert control of institutions without direct violence. Voldemort's regime used it to control the Ministry (Pius Thicknesse), Gringotts goblins, and to create deniable agents. Post-First War, many Death Eaters claimed Imperius as legal defense and were acquitted.

Limit: Ministry developed methods to detect false claims via Veritaserum, Priori Incantatem, and trial testimony, but imperfectly. After Second War, Wizengamot was skeptical of Imperius defense.

Accounts I
The Defence Against the Dark Arts DemonstrationHarry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, Chapter 14 'The Unforgivable Curses'

1994 (narrative present); published 2000

Disguised as Alastor Moody, Barty Crouch Jr. demonstrates the three Unforgivable Curses to fourth-year students. He casts Imperio on a spider, making it perform acrobatics, then casts it on students. Only Harry Potter successfully throws it off after several attempts, astonishing Moody/Crouch. The lesson establishes the curse's euphoric effect and the possibility of resistance, and foreshadows Harry's later use of willpower as a thematic defense. Students learn the nature and illegality of Unforgivables; Harry is identified as unusually strong-willed; Dumbledore's later training emphasizes mental resistance. Ministry would consider this demonstration itself illegal outside authorized Auror training.

The Enslavement of Barty Crouch Jr. and WinkyHarry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, Chapters 30, 33, 35 (Veritaserum confession)

1981-1995 (flashback within 1994 narrative)

After his son's conviction as a Death Eater, Barty Crouch Sr. smuggles Barty Jr. from Azkaban and keeps him under a powerful, prolonged Imperius Curse at home, hidden under an Invisibility Cloak and guarded by house-elf Winky. When Winky is dismissed after the Quidditch World Cup, Crouch Sr. weakens; Voldemort and Wormtail rescue Crouch Jr., who then reverses the curse and places his own father under Imperius, forcing Crouch Sr. to act normally at the Ministry while secretly imprisoned. Crouch Jr. eventually murders his father. Reveals long-term Imperius can be maintained for years but erodes victim's will and eventually fails against strong hatred; Crouch Sr.'s death and disappearance destabilize Ministry; explains Bertha Jorkins' discovery and murder.

Viktor Krum in the Triwizard MazeHarry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, Chapters 31-32 'The Third Task'

1995 (narrative present)

Barty Crouch Jr., still disguised as Moody, casts Imperio on Durmstrang champion Viktor Krum inside the hedge maze to clear Harry's path and to attack Cedric Diggory. Under Imperius, Krum stuns Fleur Delacour and attempts to torture Cedric with the Cruciatus Curse until Harry stuns him. Harry later finds Krum dazed and bewildered, with no memory of his actions. Allows Harry to reach the Triwizard Cup (a Portkey) with Cedric; leads directly to Cedric's murder and Voldemort's resurrection; demonstrates Imperius as tool for indirect assassination without caster present.

Accounts II
Madam Rosmerta and the Cursed NecklaceHarry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince, Chapters 12, 15, 27

1996-1997

Draco Malfoy, under pressure to assassinate Dumbledore, cannot enter Hogwarts himself and instead Imperiuses Madam Rosmerta, proprietor of the Three Broomsticks in Hogsmeade. While under Imperius for months, Rosmerta Imperiuses student Katie Bell (via cursed opal necklace) and later poisons mead intended for Dumbledore via Horace Slughorn. Rosmerta has no memory of acting and is only freed after Draco's failure. Shows Imperius can be layered (caster controls intermediary who controls another victim) and maintained long-term over a functional adult; highlights moral horror of using innocents as weapons; Rosmerta is exonerated.

The Gringotts Break-InHarry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, Chapter 26 'Gringotts'

1998

To retrieve a Horcrux from the Lestrange vault, Harry, Ron, and Hermione infiltrate Gringotts using Polyjuice Potion. When goblin Bogrod grows suspicious and Death Eater Travers becomes threatening, Harry casts Imperio for the first time. He successfully controls Travers (making him act as if all is normal) and Bogrod (forcing him to open the vault). Harry experiences guilt and notes the ease and euphoria of casting it, paralleling his earlier victimhood. Enables theft of Hufflepuff's Cup Horcrux; demonstrates that even heroes will use Unforgivables when desperate; raises question of whether goblins have different resistance (Bogrod obeys but later struggles); Harry's successful casting confirms Imperius requires genuine intent to dominate.

The Ministry Coup and the Imperiused MinisterHarry Potter and the Deathly Hallows, Chapters 1, 12-13; Goblet of Fire Chapter 27 (historical precedent)

First Wizarding War (1970-1981) and Second Wizarding War (1997-1998)

During both wars, Voldemort's forces systematically used Imperius to infiltrate government. In the First War, Death Eaters like Augustus Rookwood (and allegedly Lucius Malfoy, Avery, Crabbe, Goyle) claimed Imperius to escape Azkaban. In the Second War, Corban Yaxley places Imperius on Pius Thicknesse, Head of Magical Law Enforcement, who is then installed as puppet Minister for Magic after the fall of Rufus Scrimgeour. Thicknesse issues anti-Muggle-born decrees while under control. Stan Shunpike is also implied to be Imperiused when he is seen among Death Eaters. Allows Voldemort to control Ministry without open battle; creates legal chaos and moral ambiguity about guilt; post-war trials must determine who was truly Imperiused vs. willing collaborator, echoing real-world 'following orders' defenses.

The Imperius Curse is just a harmless mind trick like the Confundus Charm. — Imperius is one of three Unforgivable Curses, carrying automatic life sentence in Azkaban. Unlike Confundus (which causes confusion), Imperius grants total, euphoric control and can compel murder, with lasting psychological damage. Moody explicitly distinguishes them in Goblet of Fire Ch.14.

Harry Potter is immune to Imperius because of his mother's love protection. — Harry's resistance in Goblet of Fire is attributed to strong willpower and character, not Lily's sacrificial protection (which protects against Avada Kedavra and Voldemort's touch). Dumbledore and Moody frame resistance as rare willpower, not blood magic. Harry struggles and only succeeds on fourth try.

Anyone who claimed they were Imperiused after the First Wizarding War was lying to escape Azkaban. — Canon shows both true and false claims. Some (Pius Thicknesse, possibly Stan Shunpike) were genuinely Imperiused; others (Lucius Malfoy, Avery) likely lied but were acquitted due to influence and lack of verification. Post-Second War, Ministry became more skeptical.

The incantation is 'Imperius' and it shoots a green light like Avada Kedavra. — Incantation is 'Imperio' (imperative form). Books describe no light color or beam for Imperio; green light is specific to Avada Kedavra. Film adds subtle visual but not green. 'Imperius' is the English name of the curse, not the spell word.

Imperius is a modern invention of Dark wizards like Voldemort. — Pottermore dates Ministry classification of Unforgivables to 1717, and historical use predates Voldemort by centuries. The curse is old Dark Arts, not Voldemort's creation; he merely weaponized it at scale during both wars.
