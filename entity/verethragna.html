Who It Is

Verethragna (Vərəθraγna), whose name means 'the smasher of resistance' or 'the breaker of obstacles,' is the Zoroastrian yazata of victory in battle and offensive power. He is the divine personification of the triumphant force that overcomes all opposition—the unstoppable warrior spirit that ensures the triumph of asha (truth and order) over druj (falsehood and chaos). His name is etymologically identical to the Vedic epithet Vritrahan ('slayer of Vritra'), originally applied to Indra, revealing his deep Indo-Iranian heritage, but in Zoroastrianism, he was separated from Indra (who was demonized as a daeva) and elevated as an independent yazata serving Ahura Mazda. The Bahram Yasht (Yasht 14) describes Verethragna appearing to Zoroaster in ten successive incarnations, each a different form: a strong wind, a golden-horned bull, a white horse with golden trappings, a burden-bearing camel, a wild boar (his most famous and fearsome form), a fifteen-year-old youth (radiant and fair), a swift bird (the Varengana falcon), a wild ram, a sharp-horned buck, and a warrior with a golden blade. Each form embodies different aspects of victorious power—speed, ferocity, beauty, wisdom, and martial prowess. The wild boar form is particularly significant, as boar imagery appears on Sassanian seals and military equipment as a symbol of Verethragna's protection. Verethragna bestows victory in battle, strength of arms, health of body, and the power to overcome all opponents, both physical and spiritual. He is the patron of warriors and the granter of the khvarenah (divine glory) that legitimizes Persian kingship. He is closely associated with the planet Mars (Bahram in Persian astronomy) and the twentieth day of each month in the Zoroastrian calendar. The most sacred fire temple in Zoroastrianism, the Atash Behram (Fire of Victory), bears his Middle Persian name.
