Tawa

Sun Spirit; The Life-Giver; Father of the Kachinas

TypeSolar Deity / Creator Spirit

RealmThe Sun / The Sky

I cross the sky every day without fail. You do not need to understand why the sun rises — only to greet it with a grateful heart.

Who It Is

Tawa is the Hopi sun spirit, the source of all light, warmth, and life. In some Hopi traditions, Tawa is identified with Taiowa (the supreme creator), while in others he is a distinct solar deity who governs the daily journey of the sun across the sky. Tawa is considered the father of the kachinas — the spiritual beings who mediate between the divine realm and the Hopi people, bringing rain, fertility, and blessings. Every morning, the sun's rising is a manifestation of Tawa's ongoing creative presence, and Hopi prayers, particularly those said at dawn, address him directly. He is associated with the east (the direction of new beginnings), the color yellow, and the eagle — who flies closest to his realm. Tawa's role in the Hopi emergence narrative is crucial: when the people emerged from the sipapu into the Fourth World, Tawa guided them, setting the rhythm of days and seasons that structure agricultural and ceremonial life. Unlike more tumultuous deities, Tawa represents constancy, order, and the reliable gift of light. His spirit is honored in the Soyal ceremony (the winter solstice), when the Hopi work to turn the sun back from its southern journey and ensure the return of warmth. Tawa embodies the Hopi recognition that every day is a gift — the sun does not have to rise, yet it does, and this daily miracle is the foundation of gratitude.

Attested in Oral tradition / Hopi kachina traditionEra Pre-colonial, active in Hopi ceremonial life
