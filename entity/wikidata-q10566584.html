In the misty pages of the Shanhaijing, an ancient Chinese bestiary, there gallops a beast of golden hue, a living emblem of good fortune. Its name, Tenghuang, echoes through millennia as a symbol of benevolent portents, a creature whose very appearance was said to herald the blessings of heaven.

Etymology

Tenghuang (騰黃) literally means 'soaring yellow' or 'rising yellow' in Chinese, combining 騰 (téng, to soar or gallop) and 黃 (huáng, yellow). The alternate names similarly incorporate color terms: Chenghuang (乘黃) may mean 'riding yellow' or 'mount yellow', Feihuang (飛黃) 'flying yellow', Guhuang (古黃) 'ancient yellow', and Cuihuang (翠黃) 'jade-green yellow' or 'emerald yellow'.

Cultural Ownership

Chinese mythology; appears in the Shanhaijing (Classic of Mountains and Seas), an ancient Chinese text compiled from the 4th to 2nd centuries BCE.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars may conflate Tenghuang with other yellow-colored mythological beasts, such as the Huanglong (Yellow Dragon) or the Qilin, but Tenghuang is a distinct creature in the Shanhaijing. The exact nature and appearance of Tenghuang are not fully described in available sources, leading to ambiguity.
