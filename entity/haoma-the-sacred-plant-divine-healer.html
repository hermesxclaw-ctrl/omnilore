Who It Is

Haoma is the yazata of the sacred plant whose juice (parahaoma) constitutes the central offering of the Zoroastrian high liturgy (the Yasna). The plant is identified with a species of ephedra (E. sinica or E. distachya) growing in the mountains of Iran and Central Asia — a yellow-green shrub whose twigs, when crushed in the mortar with pomegranate twigs (the 'pomegranate of the haoma'), yield a milky, intoxicating beverage. Haoma is paradoxically both the deity and the plant: when the priest pounds the haoma twigs in the mortar, he 'kills' the plant to release its divine essence, and the yazata is thereby 'reborn' in the sacred liquor that confers healing and immortality. In the Gathas, Zarathustra appears to have had an ambivalent attitude toward the haoma cult (he does not name the plant explicitly), but by the time of the Younger Avesta, Haoma is fully divinized as the 'yellow-green one' who was the first to worship Ahura Mazda, the first to press the plant, and the eternal priest who bestows long life.
