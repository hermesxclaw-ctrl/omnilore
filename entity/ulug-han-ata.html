Who It Is

Uluğ Han Ata ('Great Father Lord') is the supreme creator deity in pre-Islamic Uyghur cosmology, part of the broader Tengriist tradition shared by Turkic and Mongolic peoples. He is the personification of the creative principle, the father of all gods and humans, who dwells in the highest layer of the sky. Uluğ Han Ata created the universe by separating the heavens from the earth and then created the first humans from clay and light. Unlike the more abstract Tengri (the Eternal Blue Sky), Uluğ Han Ata is anthropomorphic — an old, wise man with a long white beard, dressed in white robes, seated on a golden throne in a palace of light. He is associated with justice, wisdom, and the establishment of social order. The ancient Uyghurs, who were predominantly Manichaean and Tengriist before their conversion to Islam (beginning around the 10th-12th centuries), invoked Uluğ Han Ata in prayers for protection, prosperity, and cosmic harmony. Elements of Uluğ Han Ata's mythology survive in Uyghur folk tales, epic poetry (particularly the Epic of the Uyghurs and the Oghuz Khan epic), and in the traditional blessing rituals of the Uyghur people. In modern times, the figure has been reclaimed as part of Uyghur cultural heritage distinct from the dominant Han Chinese and Islamic narratives. Uluğ Han Ata is often equated with the Turkish deity Kayra Han or Kara Han.

The Approach

In the high steppes where the sky bends close to the earth, the Uyghurs once told of a primordial father, Ulug Han Ata, whose name echoes with the weight of creation itself. He is not merely a god of the heavens, but the ancestral root from which the Turkic peoples claim their spiritual lineage, a figure who bridges the celestial realm of Tengri and the mortal world of shamans and heroes.

Identity

Ulug Han Ata

Pronunciation /uˈluɣ ˈhɑn ɑˈtɑ/ (approximate; Uyghur: ئۇلۇغ خان ئاتا)

Known As

Ulugh Khan AtaUlug Khan AtaUlu Han AtaUlug Ata

Etymology

From Old Turkic 'ulug' (great, supreme) + 'han' (khan, ruler) + 'ata' (father, ancestor). The name can be translated as 'Great Khan Father' or 'Supreme Ancestor Ruler'.

Cultural Ownership

Uyghur tradition, with parallels in other Turkic peoples (e.g., Altai, Siberian Turks). Often associated with Tengriist cosmology.

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars conflate Ulug Han Ata with Tengri (the sky god) or with other creator deities like Ülgen or Kayra. The relationship is unclear; he may be an aspect or a separate entity.

Description

Appearance

No detailed physical description survives. As a sky father, he is likely envisioned as an old, wise man with a white beard, robed in celestial light, residing in the upper sky. This is reconstructed from Turkic depictions of similar deities.

Symbols & Iconography

Sky, eagle, mountain, and the cosmic tree (as in many Turkic cosmologies). The number 'nine' may be associated with him (nine skies in Turkic cosmology).

Behaviour & Personality

As a creator ancestor, he is benevolent, wise, and powerful. He may serve as a mediator between the supreme god Tengri and humans, or be an aspect of Tengri himself. He is likely invoked in prayers for protection and fertility.

Domains & Habitats

Heavenly realm, the sky, possibly the upper layer of the cosmic tree. He is not tied to a specific earthly location.

Version Differences

In some Uyghur traditions, he is the father of the Uyghur people specifically; in other Turkic traditions, he is a more universal ancestor. Some versions identify him with Tengri, others keep them distinct.

Stories & Myths

The Father of the Uyghurs

Source Uyghur oral tradition (recorded in modern collections)Culture UyghurPeriod Unknown; likely ancient

Summary

Ulug Han Ata is said to be the primordial ancestor from whom the Uyghur people descend. He may have emerged from the cosmic tree or been born from the sky, and he gave rise to the first Uyghur clans.

Participants

Ulug Han Ata

Uyghur ancestors

Consequences

The Uyghur people trace their lineage to him, giving them a sacred identity.

Variants

In some versions, he is the father of the entire Turkic race; in others, he is a culture hero who taught the Uyghurs to use iron.
