Who It Is

Uluğ Han Ata ('Great Father Lord') is the supreme creator deity in pre-Islamic Uyghur cosmology, part of the broader Tengriist tradition shared by Turkic and Mongolic peoples. He is the personification of the creative principle, the father of all gods and humans, who dwells in the highest layer of the sky. Uluğ Han Ata created the universe by separating the heavens from the earth and then created the first humans from clay and light. Unlike the more abstract Tengri (the Eternal Blue Sky), Uluğ Han Ata is anthropomorphic — an old, wise man with a long white beard, dressed in white robes, seated on a golden throne in a palace of light. He is associated with justice, wisdom, and the establishment of social order. The ancient Uyghurs, who were predominantly Manichaean and Tengriist before their conversion to Islam (beginning around the 10th-12th centuries), invoked Uluğ Han Ata in prayers for protection, prosperity, and cosmic harmony. Elements of Uluğ Han Ata's mythology survive in Uyghur folk tales, epic poetry (particularly the Epic of the Uyghurs and the Oghuz Khan epic), and in the traditional blessing rituals of the Uyghur people. In modern times, the figure has been reclaimed as part of Uyghur cultural heritage distinct from the dominant Han Chinese and Islamic narratives. Uluğ Han Ata is often equated with the Turkish deity Kayra Han or Kara Han.
