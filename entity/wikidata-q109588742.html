Who It Is

On the storm-lashed platforms of Kamino, the cloners tried to do what the Jedi said could not be done: bottle the Force itself. The result was not another Jango Fett copy in white armor, but a lanky, haunted young man in gunmetal grey who could lift a starfighter with a thought and had no idea whether he was a weapon, a mistake, or a person. His name was stamped on his cell — Jek-14 — the fourteenth attempt, and the first that lived long enough to ask why.

Attested as introverted, laconic, melancholic, and morally conflicted. Raised in isolation as a lab experiment, Jek-14 exhibits social awkwardness, distrust of authority, and a deep yearning for belonging. He is not quippy in the style of other LEGO Star Wars protagonists; his humor is dry and reluctant. He shows strong empathy once befriended, particularly toward Yoda, who treats him as a person rather than a weapon. Powers
Force Sensitivity (Engineered)

Artificially engineered Force-sensitivity via splicing Jango Fett DNA with DNA from an unidentified Force-sensitive donor. Allows intuitive use of the Force without formal Jedi training. Depicted as raw, powerful, and initially unstable.

Limit: Power is unstable and emotionally triggered; Jek-14 fears losing control. No formal training initially, so technique is crude. Requires emotional regulation learned from Yoda.

Telekinesis (Push, Pull, Levitation)

Can lift and throw large objects including starfighters, debris, and clone troopers. Levitation and Force push are his most used abilities. In Attack of the Jedi, lifts wreckage to save allies.

Limit: Exertion visibly tires him; overuse leads to collapse. Range limited to line-of-sight in animation.

Mind Trick / Force Persuasion

Attempts weak mind influence; shown to be poor at it initially, improving with Yoda's guidance. Used comically and not always successfully.

Limit: Only works on weak-minded individuals; fails on strong-willed adversaries like Vader or Sidious. Portrayed as unreliable for humor.

Lightsaber Combat (Self-Taught, Form V-ish)

Wields a blue-bladed lightsaber he constructs himself after leaving Kamino. Style is aggressive, using telekinetic augmentation and clone-trained physicality. Holds his own against Vader and Inquisitor-like opponents in LEGO combat.

Limit: Not a master duelist; relies on raw Force augmentation over technique. Vulnerable to more experienced duelists if not using Force creatively.

Enhanced Physiology (Clone Durability)

As Jango Fett clone, possesses accelerated growth, peak human conditioning, and rapid healing relative to baseline human. Survives falls and blaster near-misses that would incapacitate normal humans.

Limit: Ages normally after creation? No accelerated aging stated unlike standard clones, but growth was accelerated to young adulthood. Psychological trauma and isolation are major vulnerabilities.

Stealth Starfighter Piloting / Technical Intuition

Exceptional pilot of his custom Stealth Starfighter (75018), equipped with cloaking device. Demonstrates intuitive mechanical understanding, likely enhanced by Force.

Limit: Ship-dependent; cloaking device has limited duration and is comedicly unreliable.

Accounts I
Project Phantom Clone: Creation on KaminoLEGO Star Wars: The Yoda Chronicles — The Phantom Clone (2013)

2013 production; in-universe set during Clone Wars parody timeline (c. 22-19 BBY equivalent)

Count Dooku, under orders from Darth Sidious, commissions Kaminoans to create a Force-sensitive clone army. Using Jango Fett's template spliced with DNA from a mysterious Force-sensitive, thirteen failures precede Jek-14, the first viable subject. Grown in isolation and monitored as a weapon, Jek-14 displays terrifying raw power as a child, levitating entire cloning chambers. Dooku intends to present him to Sidious as the ultimate Sith enforcer. Yoda, sensing a disturbance, infiltrates Kamino and discovers the boy, not a weapon, but a frightened youth. Jek-14 escapes with Yoda's help, rejecting his intended purpose. Establishes Jek-14's origin as engineered being and his core conflict: weapon vs. person. Sets up Sith desire to recapture him and Jedi desire to protect/teach him. His escape deprives the Sith of their prototype army.

The Fugitive Apprentice: Flight and Yoda's GuidanceLEGO Star Wars: The Yoda Chronicles — The Phantom Clone (second half) and Menace of the Sith (bridging scenes)

2013

After escaping Kamino, Jek-14 hides in his Stealth Starfighter, refusing to join either side. Yoda pursues not to recruit but to reassure, offering the choice to walk away from war. Jek-14, haunted by visions of hurting others with his power, chooses exile on a remote asteroid. Yoda leaves him a holocron and the promise that the Force belongs to no army. This quiet, character-driven interlude is unusual for LEGO Star Wars comedy and reframes Jek-14 as a trauma survivor rather than a superhero. Defines Jek-14's moral stance: neutrality born of fear, not apathy. Establishes Yoda as surrogate father figure and explains his later return — he comes back because he chooses to, not because he is ordered.

Menace of the Sith: The Holocron HeistLEGO Star Wars: The Yoda Chronicles — Menace of the Sith (2013)

2013

Darth Sidious dispatches Darth Vader and Asajj Ventress analogs to steal a Jedi holocron that could locate future Force-sensitive children — and reveal Jek-14's donor. Jek-14 is drawn from hiding when he senses younglings in danger. On Tatooine and later Coruscant, he teams reluctantly with Anakin Skywalker and Obi-Wan Kenobi, using his telekinesis to collapse a Separatist droid factory and save a village. He refuses to kill, even when Vader taunts him that he was made to be a better Vader. He destroys the holocron rather than let either side use it, declaring that no child should be made into what he was. Jek-14 transitions from fugitive to active protector. Destruction of the holocron thwarts Sith cloning program and protects future Force-sensitives. Cements his heroic alignment and enmity with Vader, who sees him as a rival creation.

Accounts II
Attack of the Jedi: The Choice on HothLEGO Star Wars: The Yoda Chronicles — Attack of the Jedi (2013)

2013

Sidious lures the Jedi into a trap on an ice planet (Hoth analog) using a fake distress signal from Jek-14. Believing Jek-14 has fallen to the dark side, Luke Skywalker and the Jedi prepare to confront him. In truth, Jek-14 has been captured and is being tortured with Force-suppression to force a dark-side outburst. He resists, and when Luke arrives, Jek-14 breaks free not with rage but by centering himself — a direct echo of Yoda's teaching. Together, Jek-14 and Luke repel Vader and save the Jedi younglings. Jek-14 then chooses permanent exile, believing the galaxy is safer without a living template for Force clones, and flies into the Unknown Regions. Provides narrative closure: Jek-14 proves he is more than his programming, saves the next generation, and removes himself to prevent further exploitation. Leaves door open for return but explains his absence from later LEGO specials. Final shot mirrors his first escape — but this time he leaves by choice.

The Empire Strikes Out — Prototype EchoLEGO Star Wars: The Empire Strikes Out (2012)

2012 (pre-dates Yoda Chronicles but shares continuity)

While Jek-14 does not appear by name in this earlier special, the special establishes the comedic LEGO Star Wars universe, Sith cloning jokes, and the visual language later used for Jek-14. Retrospectively, Lucasfilm's press kit lists The Empire Strikes Out as tonal predecessor, and some viewers conflate background clone cameos with Jek-14. This entry is included to document the paratextual pre-history and to clarify that his canonical first appearance is 2013, not 2012. No direct consequence for Jek-14, but establishes that LEGO Star Wars specials are standalone parodies, not continuity-strict. Helps explain why Jek-14 never appears in Clone Wars or Rebels timelines.

The Stealth Starfighter Legend: Product NarrativeLEGO Set 75018 JEK-14's Stealth Starfighter — Box narrative and LEGO Star Wars Magazine comic (2013-2014)

2013-2014

Paratextual micro-stories on packaging and in LEGO Star Wars Magazine expand Jek-14's lore in 2-4 page comics: Jek-14 uses his cloaked E-wing to rescue stranded clones, outwit bounty hunters, and evade Sidious's probe droids. One comic shows him returning to Kamino to free failed clones (Jek-13 stasis tubes), choosing to leave them in peace rather than awaken them. These vignettes reinforce his stealth, compassion, and loner archetype without altering main animation canon. Expands worldbuilding but remains secondary canon (LEGO parody). Demonstrates merchandising-driven storytelling typical of LEGO Star Wars. No impact on main animated plot.

Jek-14 is a canon Star Wars character who appears in The Clone Wars or Rebels — He has never appeared in any canon or Legends television series, film, novel, or comic. He exists solely in the non-canon LEGO Star Wars: The Yoda Chronicles (2013-2014) and related LEGO paratexts. Lucasfilm explicitly separates LEGO continuity from main continuities.

Jek-14 is the same as Starkiller (Galen Marek) or his clone — Separate characters, creators, and continuities. Starkiller is from Star Wars: The Force Unleashed (2008, Legends) — Vader's secret apprentice, later cloned. Jek-14 is a LEGO-original clone of Jango Fett spliced with Force-sensitive DNA, created for comedy-parody continuity in 2013. Powers and backstories rhyme (both are Force-sensitive clones questioning identity) but they never meet and have different designs, ships, and arcs.

Jek-14's donor was definitively revealed to be a specific Jedi and his lightsaber is that Jedi's — No official source names the donor or explains lightsaber provenance beyond 'he built it himself.' LEGO.com and episodes keep donor anonymous. His blue lightsaber is self-constructed after escape, not inherited. Any named donor is fan speculation.

Jek-14 is a villain or Sith apprentice who becomes Darth Jek — He is consistently heroic, albeit reluctant. He is created to be a Sith weapon but rejects both Sith and Jedi institutional control, choosing to protect others and then exile himself to prevent further exploitation. He never takes a Darth title, never falls to the dark side in primary sources (he resists torture-induced fall).
