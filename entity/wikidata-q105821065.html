The Approach

In the beginning, the creator god Obassi was one being, but he split into two: one who rules the sky where souls are born, and one who rules the earth where they live and die. This divine division frames the world of the Ekoi people, where even water and fire are gifts from the heavens, and where a single careless act can almost drown the world.

Behaviour & Personality

Obassi Osaw is portrayed as a creator who grants requests but also punishes disobedience. He is distant but intervenes in human affairs. Obassi Nsi is less featured but is the ruler of the earth and associated with human life and death.

Stories & Myths

The Origin of Water

Source Source materialCulture EkoiPeriod Mythological

Summary

The first man, Etim 'Ne, asked Obassi Osaw for water. Obassi gave him seven stones, each of which could create a body of water when placed on earth. Etim 'Ne and his descendants used them one at a time to create water sources. One grandchild placed all seven stones together on his farm, causing a great flood. Etim 'Ne prayed to Obassi Osaw, who stopped the flood but left a lake at the farm.

Participants

Obassi Osaw

Etim 'Ne

The disobedient grandchild

Consequences

The flood was stopped, but a lake remained. The misuse of the stones led to near destruction.

None provided.

The Origin of Fire

Source Source materialCulture EkoiPeriod Mythological

Summary

Etim 'Ne sent a boy to the sky to ask Obassi Osaw for fire. Obassi refused, but the boy stayed and worked in his household. After proving himself, Obassi sent him to fetch a lamp from one of his wives. The boy lit a log from the lamp and brought it to earth. Obassi discovered the theft and sent his son Akpan Obassi to punish the boy, who cursed him to never walk again.

Fire was brought to earth, but the boy was crippled as punishment.
