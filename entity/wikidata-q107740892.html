Who It Is

For a hundred years on an island where dinosaurs still roam, two giants have traded blows without pause — not for treasure or conquest, but because neither could agree which sea king was bigger. Dorry, the Blue Giant with the horned helm and laughing battle-cry of 'Gegyagyagyagyagya!', is half of that endless duel, a warrior whose honor is measured in decades and whose sword can split a sea king and a volcano in the same arc.

Attested as jovial, boisterous, deeply honorable, and stubborn. Dorry greets strangers with laughter and immediately offers drink and combat respect. He adheres to Elbaf's warrior code: a duel once begun must be fought honorably to the death, with no interference, and he refuses to strike a dishonored opponent. He is highly respectful of worthy opponents (bows to Luffy and Usopp after they defend his honor) and shows paternal warmth to younger giants (Oimo and Kashii). Powers
Giant Physiology - Superhuman Strength and Durability

As an Elbaf giant, Dorry possesses immense physical strength capable of slaying dinosaurs and Sea Kings with single sword strokes, splitting the island's central volcano's eruption flow, and clashing with Brogy with force that shakes Little Garden. Durability allows survival of a century of daily lethal duels, volcanic explosions, and being impaled/exploded by Baroque Works weaponry.

Limit: Not invulnerable; critically wounded by Mr. 3's explosive wax and Brogy's axe when dishonored (requires medical aid from Straw Hats). Stamina limited by age and accumulated wounds; needs recovery time between duels. Vulnerable to human-scale trickery (alcohol bomb).

Master Swordsman - Terry Sword

Wields a giant longsword (Terry Sword) with two-handed technique. Demonstrated technique: overhead cleave that beheads a dinosaur and simultaneously cuts a Sea King in flashback; combined attack with Brogy that pierces the giant goldfish that swallowed them (later retcon in Elbaf lore). Sword is proportioned to giant scale, blade length ~10m.

Limit: Sword can be dulled/broken after 100 years of clashing against Brogy's axe and shield; by Chapter 129 both weapons are heavily notched and later break. Requires shield for defense; without it leaves opening.

Shield Mastery and Defensive Technique

Uses massive round shield to parry Brogy's axe 'Kabutowari' equivalent blows and to block volcanic debris. Shield face design intimidates foes.

Limit: Shield similarly degraded over century; eventually shattered after repeated clashes and Mr. 3's wax binding.

Haki (Implied, not explicitly shown pre-timeskip)

As a veteran New World giant warrior, Dorry is presumed capable of Armament and Observation Haki per One Piece power system, but no explicit Haki use is depicted or named for Dorry in manga up to Chapter 1130. Giant Warrior Pirates as a group are stated to be Haki users in later arcs, but Dorry's individual feats are not labeled as Haki.

Limit: Attested vs hypothetical: Haki for Dorry specifically is reconstructed/hypothetical, not attested by name. Do not treat as confirmed technique.

Longevity and Warrior's Honor Code

Giants live ~300 years; Dorry at 158 years is middle-aged, allowing century-long duel without aging out. Honor code grants psychological resilience and social authority among giants (commands respect from Oimo, Kashii, Hajrudin).

Limit: Honor code is also vulnerability — refuses to dodge dishonorable attacks, leading to near-fatal injury when Mr. 3 exploits duel rules.

Accounts I
The Giant Warrior Pirates and the Sea King DisputeOne Piece manga Chapters 116-117, Vivre Card, Elbaf flashbacks Chapter 1130+

In-story: ~100-120 years before present (c. 1400s in One Piece timeline); published 2000, expanded 2024-2025

Dorry and Brogy co-captained the Giant Warrior Pirates, famous across the Grand Line as the strongest pirate crew of their era. While hunting at sea, they each caught an enormous Sea King and argued over which was larger. Unable to settle the dispute, they agreed to duel on Little Garden until one conceded. Their crew left them to their honor, and the World Government later listed them as captains with 100,000,000 berry bounties each (frozen due to inactivity). Establishes Elbaf honor culture and explains 100-year absence of the captains; bounties become plot point when reactivated after Straw Hat encounter; foreshadows Elbaf arc's thematic focus on inherited will and pride.

One Hundred Years of Duels on Little GardenOne Piece manga Chapters 116-129

In-story: 100-year period ending 12 years after Gol D. Roger's execution (current story year 1522); published 2000

Stranded on Little Garden, Dorry and Brogy dueled daily, each bout ending in a draw and signaled by the island's central volcano eruption. They survived by hunting dinosaurs and drinking fermented jungle alcohol. Explorer Louis Arnot's log (found by Sanji) warns that Little Garden's jungle and long log-pose setting trap travelers for a year, explaining why few leave. The giants' duel becomes a natural clock for the island. Creates time-dilation trap for Straw Hats; demonstrates giant longevity and the futility/pride of endless honor duels; provides world-building for Elbaf and prehistoric islands.

Baroque Works Intrusion and the Dishonored DuelOne Piece manga Chapters 118-129

Published 2000; in-story: Little Garden arc

Baroque Works agents Mr. 3 (Galdino), Miss Goldenweek, Mr. 5 and Miss Valentine arrive to capture the giants' bounties on Crocodile's orders. Mr. 3 tricks Dorry by offering explosive alcohol that detonates inside him, critically wounding him before his duel with Brogy. Dorry, unaware of the trick, fights Brogy while severely injured and is struck down. Brogy weeps, believing he killed his friend. Usopp and Luffy, enraged that sacred dueling honor was mocked, defeat Baroque Works, free Dorry from Mr. 3's wax binding (Candle Champion), and reveal the trick. Dorry and Brogy reconcile and resume honorable combat, now with weapons broken but spirit restored. Dorry and Brogy indebted to Straw Hats; Usopp vows to visit Elbaf, becoming his long-term dream; Baroque Works defeated; Straw Hats obtain Eternal Pose to Alabasta; Dorry and Brogy's weapons destroyed, symbolizing end of century-long stalemate.

Accounts II
The Parting Gift - Piercing the Giant GoldfishOne Piece manga Chapter 129 'Straight Ahead!!!'

Published 2000

As the Straw Hats leave Little Garden, they are swallowed by a massive island-eating goldfish (later identified as similar to Elbaf's sea fauna). Dorry and Brogy, recovered and standing together, perform a combined attack — a massive piercing thrust with their remaining weapons — to kill the goldfish from outside and free the Going Merry. They declare the Straw Hats as honored friends of Elbaf and tell them to head straight ahead, no matter what. Straw Hats escape Little Garden; Dorry and Brogy's combined strength mythologized; sets up Elbaf as promised destination for Usopp; Going Merry's figurehead is damaged, foreshadowing later ship arc.

Return of the Giant Warrior Pirates - Egghead IncidentOne Piece manga Chapters 1106-1111, 1120-1125

Published 2024; in-story: Egghead Island, 2 years after timeskip (1522)

After two years, Dorry and Brogy reappear as leaders of the newly reformed Giant Warrior Pirates, having reunited with their crew and with Hajrudin, Oimo, and Kashii. Responding to Vegapunk's global broadcast and Luffy's distress, they shatter the Marine blockade around Egghead, destroying several Marine warships with sheer size and Haki-imbued attacks, and declare they have come to repay their debt to the Straw Hats and to take them to Elbaf. Their arrival turns the Egghead Incident into a three-way clash with the Five Elders. Reactivates Giant Warrior Pirates as major New World force; bounties reactivated at 1.8 billion berries each (combined?); provides Straw Hats escape route to Elbaf; confirms giants as key allies in final saga; sets up Elbaf arc (2024-2025).

Elbaf Homecoming and the Sun God LoreOne Piece manga Chapters 1130-1138+ (Elbaf arc, ongoing)

Published 2024-2025, ongoing

Arriving at Elbaf, Dorry and Brogy lead the Straw Hats into the giant homeland, a towering tree-island with layered realms. They introduce Luffy to Elbaf's politics, the reverence for the Sun God Nika, and the lingering schism between old Giant Warrior Pirates and newer generation (Hajrudin's crew). Dorry recounts the true history of the Sea King dispute as a parable of pride that nearly destroyed their crew, now used to teach young giants. He participates in the defense of Elbaf against World Government infiltration tied to the Road Poneglyphs. Pays off Usopp's 25-year dream to reach Elbaf; expands giant mythology and Joy Boy/Nika lore; positions Dorry as elder statesman rather than just duelist. Arc ongoing, details subject to update.

Dorry is the same as Dory from Finding Nemo, or a reference to it — Entirely separate characters, different franchises, different species and spelling. Dorry (One Piece, 2000) predates Pixar's Dory (2003) by three years and is a male Viking giant, not a blue tang fish. The single-letter difference causes search engine conflation.

Dorry and Brogy's 100-year duel was over who was stronger — Manga explicitly states the duel began over which Sea King was larger — a trivial size dispute, not strength. Their strength was already considered equal as co-captains. The pettiness is the point, illustrating pride.

Dorry died on Little Garden — Dorry was critically wounded by Mr. 3's bomb and wax but survived after Luffy/Usopp freed him and Brogy reconciled. Both giants survive and reappear 24 years later in-story at Egghead/Elbaf.

Dorry is a filler character not important to main plot — Oda has stated Little Garden was deliberate foreshadowing for Elbaf, the final saga's key island. Dorry's return at Egghead with 1.8B bounty and role in Elbaf arc proves long-term plot relevance, with Usopp's dream tied directly to him.
