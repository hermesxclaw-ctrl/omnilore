Who It Is

In the dense bamboo groves of Tosa Province, a creature called the basan is said to dwell, its fiery breath illuminating the night with an eerie, cold flame. Unlike the fearsome phoenix or the thunderbird, this Japanese bird is more of a spectral oddity, a cryptid of folklore that has puzzled and intrigued for centuries.

The basan is a nocturnal creature, appearing in bamboo groves or near villages. It is generally shy and non-aggressive, but its sudden appearance and the strange sound it makes ('basan') can startle people. It is said to feed on charcoal or ashes, which explains its fiery breath.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'basan' (バサン) is thought to be onomatopoeic, possibly derived from the sound of flapping wings or the crackling of fire. Some sources suggest a connection to the verb 'basu' (to flap) or the sound 'basan' as in a sudden flame.

Pronunciation — BAH-sahn

Aliases — Basan, Basan (yōkai), Basan (legendary bird)

Ownership — Japanese folklore, specifically from the Tosa Province (present-day Kōchi Prefecture) on Shikoku island.

Powers
Fiery breath

The basan is said to breathe a cold fire or emit a phosphorescent glow, which can be used to scare away predators or humans.

Limit: The fire is not hot and does not burn, making it more of a visual deterrent.

Sound mimicry

The basan makes a distinct flapping or 'basan' sound, which is said to be the origin of its name. This sound can be mistaken for a fire crackling.

Limit: Not a supernatural power, but a characteristic behavior.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The basan is typically described as a large bird, similar in size to a rooster or a pheasant, but with a distinctive feature: it is said to have a fire-like glow emanating from its body or mouth. Some accounts describe it as having a dark plumage with glowing red or orange markings, while others say it emits sparks or flames from its tail.

Symbols — In Sekien's illustration, the basan is depicted as a bird with a long tail and flames around its body, suggesting a connection to fire. However, the fire is often described as 'cold fire' or 'will-o'-the-wisp' like, which is a common motif in Japanese yōkai lore.

Domains — Bamboo groves in the mountains of Tosa Province (Kōchi Prefecture), Shikoku, Japan.

Accounts I
The Basan of TosaRegional folklore collections

Edo period to present

In the mountains of Tosa, a basan is said to appear on moonless nights. It perches on bamboo stalks and makes a sound like 'basan, basan', while its body glows with a pale, cold flame. Villagers who encounter it are often frightened but unharmed. Some stories say that if you see a basan, it is a sign of a fire in the village, but the basan itself is not a cause of fire. The basan is generally harmless, but its appearance is considered an omen or a curiosity.
