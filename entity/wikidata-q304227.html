Who It Is

Imagine a god so vast and so complete that after the final act of creation, he simply... left. No throne, no commandments, no cosmic drama. Just a world, set in motion, and a silence where a divine presence should be. This is the essence of 'Aba' — a name whispered across two continents, attached to two very different creators, who both chose the ultimate power move: retirement. In the humid forests of Nigeria, he is the distant, omnipotent source of all being, approached through a bustling pantheon of spirits. In the frozen expanse of the Arctic, he is the primordial architect who finished his work and vanished into the white noise of the wind. They share a name, a role, and a profound sense of absence. But are they the same being? The evidence says no. The story, however, is far more interesting.

Igbo: Aba/Chukwu is a distant, benevolent creator who is not actively involved in the day-to-day affairs of humanity. He is approached through lesser deities and ancestors. Inuit: The Inuit Aba is a creator who, after making the world, withdrew from it, leaving it to function on its own. This is a classic 'deus otiosus' (retired god) archetype.

Powers
Creation (Igbo)

Aba is the ultimate creator of the universe, all things seen and unseen. He is the source of all 'Chi' (personal spiritual essence).

Limit: He is a transcendental creator; he does not intervene directly in human affairs, operating through a pantheon of minor deities.

Creation (Inuit)

Aba is credited with the initial act of creation, forming the world and its inhabitants.

Limit: His power is limited to the initial creation; he is absent from the ongoing narrative of the world, a deus otiosus.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Igbo: Aba, as the supreme god, is generally not depicted in physical form. He is an abstract, omnipresent force, more akin to a concept of ultimate reality than a being with a body. Inuit: Aba is described as a featureless, powerful being, often associated with the sky or the void. He is not described in anthropomorphic terms, but as a force of creation.

Symbols — Igbo: The sky, the sun, and the concept of 'Chi' (personal spirit). There are no physical idols of Aba, as he is considered too great to be represented. Inuit: The sky, the wind, the vast empty spaces of the Arctic.

Domains — Igbo: Supreme deity, sky, creation, ultimate justice, source of all being. Inuit: Primordial creation, the establishment of the world order.

Accounts I
The Igbo High GodIgbo oral tradition

Pre-colonial to present

Aba, also known as Chukwu, is the supreme deity who created the world and everything in it. He is a distant, benevolent father figure who gave humans the world to live in and a 'Chi' (personal spirit) to guide them. He is not directly worshipped with temples or idols, but is acknowledged in prayers and through the veneration of the minor deities (Alusi) who serve as his intermediaries. He is the ultimate arbiter of justice and morality. Establishes the Igbo cosmological order, where a supreme, transcendent god is served by a pantheon of more accessible spirits.

Accounts II
The Inuit Creator Who LeftInuit oral tradition

Pre-contact to present

In one version of Inuit cosmology, Aba is the being who created the earth, the sea, and the sky. After completing his work, he simply left, departing to an unknown place. He does not rule, judge, or intervene. The world operates according to the laws he set in motion, and the Inuit live in it without expecting direct help or interference from him. This makes him a classic example of a 'deus otiosus'. Leaves humanity to navigate the world with the help of other, more active spirits and shamans, rather than a supreme god.

Aba is a single, universal god known to all ancient cultures. — This is a classic example of 'comparative mythology' gone wrong. The name appears in unrelated cultures, but the deities are distinct and share no common origin.

The Igbo do not have a concept of a supreme god. — They do. Aba/Chukwu is a supreme creator god, but he is a 'deus otiosus' who is approached through intermediaries, which led early missionaries to mistakenly believe the Igbo were animists without a high god.

Aba is the same as the Sumerian god 'Abu'. — Abu is a minor Sumerian god of vegetation, while 'aba' is a Sumerian word for 'elder'. They are different entities, and the names are only superficially similar.
