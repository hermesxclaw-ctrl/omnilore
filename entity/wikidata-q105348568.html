The Approach

In the silent halls of the Pyramid Texts, an unexpected guardian watches over the dead: a pelican goddess named Henet, whose open beak promises safe passage through the underworld and whose image protected the pharaoh from serpents. She is a reminder that even the most unassuming creatures could hold divine power in ancient Egypt.

Content advisory. None. No graphic or sensitive content associated with Henet.
Identity

Henet

Pronunciation Pronunciation uncertain; commonly rendered as 'Heh-net'.

Known As

The Pelican

Etymology

The name 'Henet' is the Egyptian word for pelican.

Cultural Ownership

Ancient Egyptian

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars debate whether Henet is a fully independent goddess or a manifestation of a broader protective deity. The title 'mother of the king' strongly suggests goddess status, but the scarcity of evidence leaves room for interpretation.

Description

Appearance

Henet is depicted as a pelican, a large water bird with a long beak and a throat pouch. In tomb scenes, she appears in natural settings, often near water or among livestock. No anthropomorphic depictions are known.

Symbols & Iconography

The pelican itself is the primary symbol. Its open beak is symbolic of the deceased's ability to leave the tomb and access the sun's rays, possibly paralleling the tomb shaft.

Behaviour & Personality

Henet is portrayed as a protective and nurturing figure. She is called 'mother of the king' and acts as a guardian against snakes in funerary contexts. She is also associated with purification, scooping dangerous elements (symbolized as fish) from the waters.

Domains & Habitats

Henet's domain is primarily the afterlife, particularly the Underworld and the tomb. She is also connected to the Nile and its waters, as pelicans are water birds.

Version Differences

In the Pyramid Texts, Henet is a protective symbol against snakes. In later funerary papyri, she is associated with safe travel and the ability to leave the tomb. These may reflect evolving beliefs, but the core protective role remains consistent.

Contradictions & Disputes

Goddess status

Position A

Henet is a goddess because she is called 'mother of the king', a title exclusive to goddesses.

Position A — evidence

Pyramid Texts usage of 'mother of the king' for goddesses.

Position B

Some may argue the title could be honorary and not necessarily divine, but the exclusivity suggests otherwise.

Position B — evidence

Lack of explicit cult or temple dedicated to Henet.

Scholarly Consensus

Scholars generally accept Henet as a goddess based on the title.
