Who It Is

In the green hills of the Basque Country, where the Atlantic winds meet ancient oak forests, the sun is not a distant ball of fire but a living grandmother—Eguzki Amandre, the Sun Mother, whose daily journey across the sky is watched by shepherds, witches, and the goddess Mari alike. Her light is a blessing, her wrath a warning, and her story is woven into the very fabric of Basque identity.

Eguzki Amandre is benevolent and protective, especially toward children and the vulnerable. She is seen as a life-giver, nurturing the crops and the earth. However, she can also be stern and punishing, especially against those who show disrespect or who commit evil acts. She is a guardian of moral order, rewarding good and punishing wickedness.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Basque 'eguzki' (sun) and 'amandre' (mother, grandmother; a respectful term for an elderly woman). The name literally means 'Sun Mother' or 'Grandmother Sun'.

Pronunciation — eh-GOOS-kee ah-MAHN-dreh (approximate)

Powers
Solar radiance

She controls the sun's light and heat, bringing life to the world. Her light can also expose hidden truths and drive away evil.

Limit: She must follow the daily cycle; she cannot stop the sun's course.

Protection and blessing

She blesses those who honor her, ensuring good harvests, fertility, and safety. She protects against witches (sorginak) and evil spirits.

Limit: Her protection is conditional on moral behavior and respect.

Punishment

She can strike with sunstroke, drought, or fire those who offend her or commit grave sins.

Limit: Not clearly defined; she is not a vengeful deity but a just one.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Eguzki Amandre is typically envisioned as a maternal, elderly woman, radiating warmth and light. She may be depicted as a beautiful matron or a kindly grandmother, often associated with the golden color of the sun. In some folk tales, she is said to have a face so bright that mortals cannot look at her directly. No physical description is consistently recorded, so this is largely reconstructed.

Symbols — The sun itself is her primary symbol. She is associated with solar motifs, gold, and the colors yellow and orange. In Basque rural life, the sun was a protective force against evil spirits and witches. Her iconography is minimal in surviving art; she is more a presence in oral tradition than a depicted figure.

Domains — She dwells in the sky, but her influence is felt on earth, particularly in fields, mountains, and homes. She is closely tied to the daily cycle of sunrise and sunset. She is also associated with the hearth, as the sun's fire is mirrored in the domestic fire.

Accounts I
The Sun Mother and the WitchBasque oral tradition (reconstructed)

Pre-Christian to modern folk

A witch (sorgin) tries to curse a village by stealing the sun's light. Eguzki Amandre confronts her, and the sun's rays burn the witch's power, forcing her to flee. The village is saved, and the people learn to honor the Sun Mother with offerings. The witch is defeated; the village establishes a ritual of gratitude to the sun.

Accounts II
The Sun's DaughterBasque folklore (reconstructed)

Unknown

A mortal man falls in love with Eguzki Amandre's daughter, who lives in a castle in the sky. He must pass three trials to win her hand. With the Sun Mother's hidden help, he succeeds, but he must never look at her directly. He breaks the taboo, and she vanishes, leaving him with a lesson about humility. The man loses his love; the story warns against overreaching.
