Who It Is

Huitzilopochtli ('Left-Handed Hummingbird' or 'The Hummingbird of the South') is the brutal and splendid patron god of the Mexica-Aztecs — a tribal war-deity who became the supreme celestial power of the imperial pantheon. His birth myth at Coatepec is the foundational cosmogonic drama of Aztec religion: his mother Coatlicue (the Serpent Skirt) becomes pregnant by a ball of hummingbird feathers; her 400 sons (the stars) and daughter Coyolxauhqui (the moon) plan to kill her; Huitzilopochtli emerges from her womb fully armed, slays and dismembers his siblings, and hurls Coyolxauhqui's head into the sky to become the moon. This myth, ritually reenacted annually at the Templo Mayor of Tenochtitlan, establishes Huitzilopochtli as a solar warrior who daily slays the moon and stars to allow the sun to rise — a cosmic struggle that required the 'nourishment' of human sacrifice. As the guide of the Mexica migration, Huitzilopochtli led his people to the prophesied site of an eagle devouring a snake on a cactus, where they founded Tenochtitlan in 1325 CE. His cult was the most blood-drenched in the Aztec pantheon: the dedication of the Templo Mayor in 1487 CE reportedly saw thousands of war-captives sacrificed over four days.
