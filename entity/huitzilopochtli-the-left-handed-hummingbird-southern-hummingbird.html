Huitzilopochtli (the Left-Handed Hummingbird / Southern Hummingbird)

also known asthe Left-Handed Hummingbird / Southern Hummingbird
Typegod (tribal war-god and solar deity)
Who It Is
Form

Huitzilopochtli is often depicted as a blue or green hummingbird, which symbolizes his connection to the sun and the sky. In some representations, he is shown with a helmet or a headdress adorned with feathers, which signifies his role as a warrior and a leader. His appearance is also associated with the south, which was considered the left side of the world by the Aztecs. This directional association is reflected in his name and his role as a guide for the Aztecs during their migration. Huitzilopochtli's appearance is not limited to his physical form, as he is also associated with various symbols and attributes, such as the sun, the eagle, and the cactus. These symbols emphasize his connection to the natural world and his role as a powerful deity.

Powers

Solar Power Huitzilopochtli is said to have the power to control the sun and bring light and warmth to the world. This power is associated with his role as a solar deity and his connection to the sun and the sky.

Accounts

According to Aztec mythology, Huitzilopochtli guided the Aztecs during their migration from Aztlan to Tenochtitlán, which would become the capital of the Aztec empire. During this journey, Huitzilopochtli appeared to the Aztecs in various forms, including as a hummingbird and a warrior, and provided them with guidance and protection.

According to Aztec mythology, Huitzilopochtli was born from the heart of his mother, Coatlicue, who was impregnated by a ball of feathers. This birth is associated with the cactus and the desert, which are symbols of Huitzilopochtli's connection to the natural world.
