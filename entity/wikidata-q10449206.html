The Approach

In the high plains of the Colombian Andes, the Muisca people once told of a god who carried the world on his back, and when he shifted his burden, the earth trembled. Chibchacum, the bringer of rain and thunder, was both a life-giving force and a bring of destruction, a deity whose anger flooded the land and whose punishment became the very ground beneath their feet.

Content advisory. Contains mythological themes of rebellion, divine punishment, and natural disasters.

Etymology

The name Chibchacum is believed to derive from the Muisca language, with possible meanings related to 'support' or 'carrier', reflecting his role as the bearer of the earth. However, the exact etymology is uncertain.

Cultural Ownership

Muisca culture, pre-Columbian Altiplano Cundiboyacense (present-day central Colombia)

Disputed Identifications

Some scholars have drawn parallels between Chibchacum and other earth-bearing deities like Atlas in Greek mythology, but these are comparative interpretations, not identifications. There is no evidence of a direct syncretism.

Appearance

No specific physical description of Chibchacum is provided in the available source. As a deity, he is likely anthropomorphic, but details are unknown.

No specific symbols or iconography are documented. He is associated with rain, thunder, and earthquakes.

Behaviour & Personality

Chibchacum is portrayed as a powerful and temperamental deity. He is easily angered, causing destructive floods, but he also responds to offerings of gratitude. He is rebellious, vindictive, and ambitious, as seen in his rebellion against Bochica and his subsequent revenge.

Domains & Habitats

Chibchacum is associated with the Earth, particularly the Altiplano Cundiboyacense. He is said to carry the Earth on his back, and his movements cause earthquakes. He also controls rain and thunder.

Stories & Myths

The Rebellion of Chibchacum and the Flood

Source Ocampo López (2013)Culture MuiscaPeriod Pre-Columbian era (before the Spanish conquest)

Summary

Chibchacum, angered by the Muisca's worship of Bochica, rebelled. He descended to Earth and recruited a woman named Chié, promising her to be his queen. Chié incited discord and civil war among the people. Bochica cursed Chié, turning her into a barn owl. Chibchacum then used an evil incantation to cause a great flood. The people begged Bochica for mercy and promised to worship him. Bochica created a waterfall to drain the floodwaters and punished Chibchacum by forcing him to carry the Earth on his back.

Consequences

Chibchacum became the earth-bearer, causing earthquakes when he shifts his burden. Chié was transformed into a barn owl. The Muisca vowed to worship Bochica.

The provided source does not mention variants, but other versions of the flood myth may exist in Muisca oral tradition.

Divine punishment
