Who It Is

Lemminkäinen (full epithet: Lemminkäinen kaukomieli, 'Lemminkäinen the Far-Minded') is the rash young hero of the Kalevala — handsome, reckless, gifted in magic, and constantly in trouble. He is the Don Juan, the warrior, and the fool; the hero who dies and is resurrected; the figure whose adventures bring comedy, tragedy, and chaos to the epic. Where Väinämöinen is ancient wisdom and Ilmarinen is patient craft, Lemminkäinen is uncontrollable youth and unbridled desire.

Lemminkäinen's defining characteristic is his inability to stay out of trouble. His mother — one of the most poignant figures in the Kalevala — repeatedly warns him against foolish quests, and he repeatedly ignores her. His first major adventure is a disastrous attempt to win a bride from the wealthy household of Saari. He seduces every woman on the island except one — the one he wanted — and is driven out by the island's enraged men. Undaunted, he sets his sights on the Maiden of Pohjola, like every other male hero of the epic.

His quest for the Pohjola bride leads to his most famous exploit: the attempt to kill the Swan of Tuonela. Louhi demands this as one of the bride-tasks, and Lemminkäinen, confident in his magical powers, sets out for the dark river of the dead. But the blind herdsman of Pohjola (whom Lemminkäinen had insulted) lies in wait and kills him with a serpent-tipped arrow, dismembering his body and scattering the pieces in the black river of Tuonela. His mother, sensing his death through a comb that begins to bleed, travels to Tuonela, rakes the river with a copper rake, recovers every piece of his body, and sings him back to life with magical ointments and incantations — one of the most beautiful and moving episodes in all epic literature.

Resurrected, Lemminkäinen does not reform. He joins Väinämöinen's expedition to steal the Sampo, where his musical talents are needed to lull the people of Pohjola to sleep. But his pride causes disaster: when the Sampo has been loaded onto the ship and the heroes are sailing away, Lemminkäinen insists on singing a victory song. His singing wakes a crane, whose cry wakes the entire population of Pohjola, leading to Louhi's pursuit and the destruction of the Sampo. Despite his flaws, Lemminkäinen's vitality is irresistible. He embodies the Finnish concept of sisu — stubborn, reckless courage — and his mother's love, which refuses to accept death, represents the deepest theme of the Kalevala: the triumph of persistence and love over even the dark powers of the underworld.
