Who It Is

In the vast, wine-soaked epic of Nonnus' Dionysiaca, where the god Dionysus marches east to conquer India, a host of wild creatures follows in his train. Among the leaders of the satyrs—those half-human, half-beast companions of the god—stands Lamis, a name that flickers briefly in the ancient text, leaving us with more questions than answers. Who was this obscure figure, and what role did he play in the god's triumphant campaign?

No details survive. He is only named as a leader of satyrs in the army of Dionysus, implying martial or organizational role.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Lamis (Λάμις) is of uncertain etymology. It may be related to the Greek word 'lamos' (λάμος) meaning 'throat' or 'gullet', but this is speculative.

Pronunciation — LAM-is (Ancient Greek: Λάμις, with a short 'a' and stress on the first syllable).

Aliases — Lamis

Ownership — Greek mythology, specifically the epic tradition of Nonnus of Panopolis (5th century CE).

Form & Signs

Appearance — No description survives. As a satyr leader, he would typically be depicted as a male companion of Dionysus with horse-like or goat-like features (tail, ears, sometimes legs), but this is inferred from the general iconography of satyrs.

Symbols — No specific symbols or iconography associated with Lamis. As a satyr, he might be associated with the thyrsus (staff), wine, and musical instruments, but this is conjecture.

Domains — No specific domain or habitat. Satyrs typically inhabit wild, wooded areas, but Lamis is mentioned only in the context of the military campaign.

5th century CE (composition); mythological setting in the heroic age

Dionysus assembles a vast army to invade India. Among the troops are satyrs, led by several leaders including Lamis. The campaign is a major theme of the epic, but Lamis himself is not given any individual deeds. Lamis's role is purely part of the larger army; no individual consequences are recorded.
