The Approach

In the vast, wine-soaked epic of Nonnus' Dionysiaca, where the god Dionysus marches east to conquer India, a host of wild creatures follows in his train. Among the leaders of the satyrs—those half-human, half-beast companions of the god—stands Lamis, a name that flickers briefly in the ancient text, leaving us with more questions than answers. Who was this obscure figure, and what role did he play in the god's triumphant campaign?

Identity

Lamis

Pronunciation LAM-is (Ancient Greek: Λάμις, with a short 'a' and stress on the first syllable).

Etymology

The name Lamis (Λάμις) is of uncertain etymology. It may be related to the Greek word 'lamos' (λάμος) meaning 'throat' or 'gullet', but this is speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically the epic tradition of Nonnus of Panopolis (5th century CE).

Disputed Identifications

No known disputes, but the name is extremely rare and may be a hapax legomenon (appearing only once in surviving literature).

Description

Appearance

No description survives. As a satyr leader, he would typically be depicted as a male companion of Dionysus with horse-like or goat-like features (tail, ears, sometimes legs), but this is inferred from the general iconography of satyrs.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography associated with Lamis. As a satyr, he might be associated with the thyrsus (staff), wine, and musical instruments, but this is conjecture.

Behaviour & Personality

No details survive. He is only named as a leader of satyrs in the army of Dionysus, implying martial or organizational role.

Domains & Habitats

No specific domain or habitat. Satyrs typically inhabit wild, wooded areas, but Lamis is mentioned only in the context of the military campaign.
