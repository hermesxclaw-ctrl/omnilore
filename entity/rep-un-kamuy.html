Who It Is

In the stormy waters off Hokkaido, the Ainu people saw not mere whales but the great sea god Rep-un-Kamuy, who could appear as a playful youth with a harpoon or as the mighty orca itself. His gifts sustained villages, but his true nature was one of generosity and trust, as revealed in a tale of a lying bird and a village's quiet reverence.

He is often portrayed as carefree and generous, but also mischievous. He shows a sense of humor and is moved by genuine piety rather than rigid ritual.

Etymology — The name is derived from Ainu 'rep' (sea) and 'kamuy' (deity), meaning 'god of the sea' or 'sea deity'.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Rep-un-Kamuy is sometimes depicted as an orca (killer whale). In other instances, he appears as a carefree, somewhat mischievous young man armed with a harpoon.

Symbols — The orca is his primary symbol, representing his dominion over the sea. The harpoon signifies his role as a provider and hunter.

Domains — He resides in the sea, particularly in the waters around Hokkaido and the Kuril Islands. He is associated with marine life, fishing, and maritime activities.

Accounts I
The Whale Gift and the Lying Sea WrenAshkenazy; Etter; Munro

Traditional (pre-modern)

Rep-un-Kamuy harpoons a whale and her young, throwing them ashore near a human village. A sea wren later tells him that the humans are disrespectfully cutting the whales with sickles and axes. Instead of anger, he laughs, saying the meat is theirs to do with as they like. Later, he passes the village and finds the wren lied: the humans are using sacred swords and wearing ritual robes, showing proper respect. Moved, he assures them of future bounty. The village is blessed with continued prosperity and protection from famine.

Accounts II
Defeat of YaushikepAshkenazy

Traditional

Rep-un-Kamuy saves a village on Hokkaido from a giant spider named Yaushikep by pulling it into the sea from Uchiura Bay and transforming it into the octopus-like creature Akkorokamui. The village is saved, and Yaushikep becomes Akkorokamui, a creature that may still appear in the sea.
