Who It Is

In the shadow of Mount Cithaeron, where a lion terrorized the cattle of Thespiae, a king's desperate plan would entwine the fates of fifty princesses with the greatest hero of Greece. Among them was Eurytele, a name whispered in genealogies, a mother of a hero, yet a woman whose own story remains a single line in the vast tapestry of myth.

Eurytele's personality is not detailed in any surviving source. She is one of the fifty sisters who bore children to Heracles, and in one version, one sister (not Eurytele) refused and was punished. Eurytele's role is passive; she is a recipient of Heracles' advances.

Pronunciation — yoo-RIT-uh-lee (English approximation); in Classical Greek: eu-ry-te-lē

Aliases — Eurytele

Form & Signs

Appearance — No ancient source describes Eurytele's physical appearance. As a daughter of Thespius, she was likely a princess of Thespiae, but no specific attributes are recorded. In artistic depictions of the daughters of Thespius, they are typically shown as young Greek women, but no distinct iconography for Eurytele exists.

Symbols — No symbols or iconography are specifically associated with Eurytele. In general, the daughters of Thespius may be represented in group scenes with Heracles, but individual identification is rare.

Domains — Eurytele lived in Thespiae, a city in Boeotia, central Greece. She was a princess, daughter of King Thespius. No other domains or habitats are recorded.

Accounts I
The Daughters of Thespius and HeraclesPseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.10; Diodorus Siculus, Bibliotheca Historica 4.29.3; Athenaeus, Deipnosophistae 13.556f

Archaic/Classical mythology, recorded in later sources

King Thespius of Thespiae, wanting his daughters to bear children by the great hero Heracles, hosted him while he hunted the Cithaeronian lion. Over the course of the hunt, Heracles slept with all fifty daughters, believing each time he was with the same woman. Eurytele was one of these daughters, and she bore a son named Leucippus. Eurytele gave birth to Leucippus, who became one of the many sons of Heracles. These sons, the Heracleidae, later played a significant role in Greek mythology and history.
