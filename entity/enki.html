Enki, the lord of wisdom, who sees into the depths, whose cunning saves humanity and whose word shapes civilization.

Who They Are

Enki (Ea in Akkadian/Babylonian) is the god of wisdom, fresh water (the underground Abzu), magic, crafts, and creation. He is the cleverest of the gods, known for his cunning, eloquence, and problem-solving abilities. Enki is depicted as a bearded figure wearing a horned cap and a long robe, with streams of water and fish flowing from his shoulders. His sacred animal is the goat-fish (Capricorn). Enki resides in the Abzu (the primeval freshwater aquifer beneath the earth) at Eridu, the oldest Sumerian city. He is the creator of humans (formed from clay mixed with the blood of a slain god) in multiple creation myths. In the Atrahasis Epic, he saves humanity from the Flood by advising Atrahasis to build an ark. In the Descent of Inanna, he resurrects Inanna by creating kurgarru and galaturu (gender-ambiguous beings) who charm Ereshkigal. Enki holds the ME (the divine decrees of civilization), which he sometimes gives to or recovers from other gods. He is the protector of humanity against the other gods' harsh decrees.

Attested in Sumerian hymns / Atrahasis Epic / Enki and NinmahEra Sumerian to Neo-Babylonian (c. 3000-500 BCE)

Form

Enki is depicted in Mesopotamian art as a bearded male deity wearing a horned crown (the symbol of divinity) and a long, flounced robe. He is often shown with streams of water and fish flowing from his shoulders or from a vase he holds, representing the Apsu. He is frequently accompanied by a two-faced attendant god, Usmu (Isimud), who represents his wisdom and ability to see all sides of a matter. Some depictions show him standing on a bird (the Anzu bird) or with the Oannes-like fish-man figure nearby, though this is likely the apkallu, not Enki himself.

Powers

Mastery of the Me Enki holds the Me, the divine decrees or powers that are the foundation of civilization: kingship, priesthood, crafts, music, writing, justice, and even negative aspects like falsehood. He possesses, guards, and dispenses these powers. Inanna and the Me He can lose them when drunk (as Inanna famously exploits). Some texts show the Me as being held by Enlil or Anu, but the primary tradition attributes them to Enki.

Wisdom and Cunning Enki is the god of wisdom (gestu), intelligence, and understanding. He is the ultimate problem-solver, often using trickery and clever plans rather than direct confrontation. All Mesopotamian traditions His cleverness can backfire, as when his plan to create flawed humans in the Enki and Ninmah story is challenged. None significant.

Creation Enki, with Ninmah (or Ninhursag), creates humanity from clay mixed with the blood of a slain god (in the Atra-Hasis version). He also creates various plants, animals, and the first human-created crafts. Enki and Ninmah; Atra-Hasis He requires a partner (Ninmah) or a sacrificial god (We-ila) for creation. The blood used is from a minor god (We-ila) in one version, but in other versions, the creation is less violent.

Control over Water As lord of the Apsu, Enki controls the freshwater ocean beneath the earth. He can fill the Tigris and Euphrates with life-giving water, but also cause floods. He is responsible for the fertility of the land. Enki and the World Order His power is over fresh water; the salt sea (Tiamat) is a separate, often hostile, entity. In the Enuma Elish, he kills Apsu (the personified fresh water), showing his power over even this primordial force.

Magic and Exorcism Enki is the ultimate source of all magical knowledge (incantations, rituals, purification rites). Exorcists and priests invoke him as their patron. He is the god who can cleanse evil and demons. Surpu, Maqlu, and countless incantation texts He typically acts through intermediaries (the exorcist), not directly. None.

Divine Kingship and Authority Enki is one of the great gods of the Mesopotamian pantheon, a member of the ruling triad (with Anu and Enlil). He holds authority over his own realm (the Apsu) and is consulted in major divine decisions. All texts He is subordinate to Anu (sky) and often to Enlil (air/storm), and he is eventually superseded by his son Marduk. In the Enuma Elish, his authority is effectively transferred to Marduk.

Behavior & Habitat

Enki is the archetypal clever god: cunning, resourceful, and often subversive. He is a trickster figure who uses wit rather than force to achieve his ends, frequently acting as a benefactor to humanity against the wishes of other gods. He is associated with wisdom, magic, and creation, and is often depicted as a mediator and problem-solver. Unlike the stormy Enlil, Enki is generally calm and rational, though he can be petty and boastful, as seen in the drinking contest with Ninmah. He is also a fertility god, associated with the life-giving waters of the Apsu.

Accounts

Enki travels to Sumer, blesses the cities (especially Eridu), and assigns each god their role. He establishes the Me, the divine decrees that organize civilization. He fills the Tigris and Euphrates with water, brings fertility to the land, and establishes the temple E-Abzu.

The gods complain about their hard labor. Enki and Ninmah create humans from clay to bear the burden. After a feast, Enki and Ninmah engage in a drinking contest. Ninmah creates flawed humans (a blind man, a eunuch, a sterile woman), and Enki finds a place for each in society, proving his wisdom. Enki then creates a flawed human (Umul) that Ninmah cannot fix.

Inanna, the goddess of love and war, travels to Eridu to visit Enki. She gets him drunk on beer and convinces him to give her the Me (the divine decrees). Once he sobers up, Enki realizes what happened and sends his servant Usmu to retrieve them, but Inanna has already left. The Me are transferred to Uruk.

The gods decide to destroy humanity with a flood. Ea (Enki) secretly warns Atra-Hasis (or Utnapishtim in Gilgamesh), advising him to build a boat and save himself, his family, and animals. Ea uses a clever trick to avoid breaking his oath to the gods: he speaks to the man through a reed wall, so he doesn't literally 'tell' him directly.

The primordial gods Apsu (fresh water) and Tiamat (salt water) are disturbed by the younger gods. Apsu plots to destroy them, but Ea (Enki) learns of the plan and puts Apsu to sleep with a spell, killing him. Ea then builds his home on Apsu's corpse (the Apsu). He and his wife Damkina have a son, Marduk, who becomes the champion of the gods against Tiamat. Marduk defeats Tiamat and creates the world from her body.

Enki is credited with creating the seven apkallu (wise sages), half-fish, half-human beings, who brought civilization, arts, and crafts to humanity. They are the mythical ancestors of the exorcists.

Adapa, a priest of Ea, breaks the wing of the South Wind. Ea, angry, advises Adapa to go to heaven and refuse the food and drink offered by Anu, claiming it is the food of death. Adapa follows the advice but the food and drink are actually the food of life. He loses the chance at immortality for humanity.

Scholarly Debates
Genealogy
Enki is the son of Anu (the sky god). ⇌ In the Enuma Elish, Ea is the son of Apsu and Tiamat (primordial waters), making Anu his brother.
Evidence for A: Standard Sumerian god lists.
Evidence for B: The Enuma Elish (Tablet I).
Domain: Earth vs. Water
Enki means 'Lord of the Earth' (EN.KI). ⇌ Enki's domain is the Apsu (fresh water), and 'KI' refers to the underworld or the earth's depths.
Evidence for A: Etymological reading of the name.
Evidence for B: His role in myths and his temple at Eridu, which was centered on a freshwater pool.
Role in the Flood
Enki is a benevolent god who saves humanity. ⇌ Enki is a disobedient god who undermines the divine council's decision.
Evidence for A: His actions in Atra-Hasis and Gilgamesh.
Evidence for B: His deception and manipulation of the other gods.
Theft of the Me
Inanna steals the Me from Enki, proving her superiority. ⇌ Enki allows the theft, as part of his plan to spread civilization.
Evidence for A: The text of Inanna and the Me.
Evidence for B: Interpretive readings that see Enki's drunkenness as a ruse.
What We Don't Know

The Sumerian origin myths (Enki and Ninmah, Enki and the World Order) are fragmentary, and our understanding relies on Old Babylonian copies. The exact nature of the 'Me' is debated. The relationship between the Sumerian Enki and the Akkadian Ea is not fully understood; they may have been merged from different origins. The visual depictions of Enki are relatively rare compared to other gods, and his iconography is not fully defined. Access to primary archaeological sites (Eridu) is limited by current political instability in Iraq.

A way to hold it in your mind

Enki is the ancient world's archetypal 'clever god.' If Zeus is the CEO who throws thunderbolts, and Odin is the brooding strategist, Enki is the brilliant, slightly chaotic engineer in the basement who knows how the universe actually works. He is the one who fixes the plumbing of the cosmos, invents civilization, and hides a spare key to the apocalypse in case the other gods get too power-hungry. He's the god you want on your side in a crisis, as long as you don't invite him to a drinking contest.
