The Approach

A prince of Mycenae, son of a king, brother to the mother of Heracles—yet his name survives only as a footnote in a single ancient library. Chirimachus stands among the forgotten sons of Electryon, whose lives were cut short in a dynastic feud over a throne and a stolen herd.

Content advisory. Contains themes of familial violence, murder, and dynastic conflict.
Identity

Chirimachus

Pronunciation kī-RIM-ə-kəs (English); Khei-ri-makh-os (Classical Greek)

Known As

Cheirimachus

Etymology

From Greek χείρ (cheir, 'hand') and μάχη (machē, 'battle'), possibly meaning 'hand-to-hand fighter' or 'one who fights with his hands'.

Cultural Ownership

Greek mythology, specifically the Mycenaean heroic cycle, as recorded in Hellenistic sources.

Disputed Identifications

No significant disputes; he is clearly the son of Electryon in the Bibliotheca, though some ancient sources list different numbers of sons, and Chirimachus may be omitted or conflated with other brothers.

Description

Appearance

No description survives; as a Mycenaean prince, he would be expected to be of noble bearing, but specific details are unattested.

Symbols & Iconography

None known; no ancient art or artifacts depict Chirimachus.

Behaviour & Personality

Unknown; he is only mentioned as a victim in the war against the Taphians.

Domains & Habitats

Mycenae, the ancient city in the Argolid, Greece.

Version Differences

Apollodorus is the only source to name Chirimachus among Electryon's sons. Other sources (e.g., Pausanias) give different lists of Electryon's children, sometimes without naming all sons, so Chirimachus may be a later addition or a variant.

Stories & Myths

The Death of the Sons of Electryon

Source Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.5-6Culture GreekPeriod Heroic Age (mythical)

Summary

Chirimachus and his brothers were killed by the sons of Pterelaus, king of Taphos, who came to Mycenae to claim the kingdom of Mestor, their maternal grandfather and Electryon's brother. The Taphians, led by Pterelaus' sons, demanded the throne, and when Electryon refused, they attacked and killed his sons, including Chirimachus, and also carried off his cattle.

Consequences

Electryon's kingdom was left without male heirs except his nephew Amphitryon, who later avenged the deaths by leading a campaign against the Taphians. Electryon's daughter Alcmene became the mother of Heracles.

Variants

Some versions of the myth may have different numbers of sons or different details of the conflict, but Apollodorus provides the most complete account.

Dark History

Warning: Violence and murder. —

Massacre of Electryon's sons

Source Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.4.5-6

Description

Chirimachus and his brothers were killed in a violent attack by the sons of Pterelaus, who sought to claim the Mycenaean throne. The attack also involved the theft of cattle, indicating a raid with lethal intent.

Scholarly Context

This reflects the heroic-age pattern of dynastic disputes and raids common in Greek mythology.
