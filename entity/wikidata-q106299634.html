The Approach

In the Republic of San Magnolia, the war against the Legion is a lie: the machines are piloted by the persecuted Colorata, the '86', who are denied names and humanity. At the heart of this brutal deception stands Shinei Nouzen, a sixteen-year-old squad leader known as 'Undertaker' and 'The Reaper', who carries the metal scraps of his fallen comrades and listens to the voices of the dead. His story is one of survival, guilt, and a fragile hope found in a handler who dares to see him as human.

Content advisory. The subject matter includes systemic racism, genocide, war crimes, suicide, psychological trauma, and violence. The character's backstory involves the murder of his family and the death of his brother. The narrative consistently portrays the dehumanization of a minority group and the psychological toll of war. Reader discretion is advised.
Identity

Shinei Nouzen

Pronunciation Shin-ei No-zen (IPA: ɕiɴ.eː noː.zeɴ)

Known As

ShinUndertakerThe ReaperShinei Nouzen (シンエイ・ノウゼン)

Etymology

The surname 'Nouzen' is likely a transliteration of a Japanese name, but its meaning is not explicitly stated in the source material. The given name 'Shinei' may be derived from the Japanese word 'shin' (心) meaning 'heart' or 'mind', but this is speculative. His call sign 'Undertaker' refers to his role in burying his comrades' remains, and 'The Reaper' is a nickname given by others due to his grim reputation and his ability to hear the voices of the dead.

Cultural Ownership

86 is a Japanese light novel series created by Asato Asato. The character is part of the fictional world of San Magnolia and is not based on any real cultural figure. The series is licensed in North America by Yen Press.

Disputed Identifications

There is no known dispute about the character's identity. However, his nationality and ethnicity within the fictional world are a central theme: he is a Colorata, a minority group in the Republic of San Magnolia, but his family name 'Nouzen' suggests a possible connection to the Empire of Giad, which is later revealed to be significant.

Description

Appearance

Shinei Nouzen is a young man of sixteen with dark hair and dark eyes, typical of the Colorata. He is often depicted wearing the military uniform of the 86, which is dark in color. In the anime, he has a lean build and a stoic expression. He is usually seen with his Juggernaut, a mecha, and carries a box of dogtags. After defecting to the Giad Federacy, he wears the Federacy's uniform, which is similar but with different insignia.

Symbols & Iconography

His call sign 'Undertaker' is represented by a coffin or grave motif. He carries a box of makeshift dogtags, each made from a piece of a fallen comrade's Juggernaut, symbolizing his role as the one who remembers and buries the dead. His personal mark on his Juggernaut is a skull, fitting his nickname 'The Reaper'.

Behaviour & Personality

Shin is calm, stoic, and seemingly emotionless, a result of his trauma and his constant exposure to death. He is fiercely protective of his squad members and is willing to do whatever it takes to ensure their survival, even if it means becoming a ruthless killer. He is haunted by the voices of the dead, which he hears as a result of his ability, and he keeps them at bay by focusing on his mission. He is introverted and has difficulty expressing his emotions, but he develops deep bonds with his comrades, especially with Lena, whom he comes to love. He is also highly intelligent and strategic, often outthinking his enemies on the battlefield.

Domains & Habitats

Shin's primary domain is the battlefield, specifically the Eastern Front of the Republic of San Magnolia, where the Spearhead Squadron operates. After defecting, he resides in the Giad Federacy, where he lives with his adopted siblings and continues to fight as part of the Nordlicht Squadron. He is most comfortable in the cockpit of his Juggernaut, where he can control the chaos around him.

Version Differences

In the light novel, Shin's internal monologue provides more depth to his thoughts and feelings. The anime adaptation condenses some scenes but remains faithful to the source material. In the manga adaptation, his appearance is similar, but the art style differs. The spin-off manga '86: Operation High School' presents an alternate universe where the characters are in a high school setting, and Shin is depicted as a more typical student, though still with his stoic personality. No significant contradictions exist between versions.

Powers & Abilities

Combat Skills

Source Version Light novel, anime, manga

Description

Shin is an exceptionally skilled pilot and fighter. He has survived countless battles and is known for his ruthlessness and precision. He is able to pilot his Juggernaut with great agility and accuracy, often taking on multiple enemies at once.

Limitations

His skills are limited to his physical endurance and the capabilities of his Juggernaut.

Contradictions

None.

Ability to Hear the Voices of the Dead

Source Version Light novel, anime

Description

Shin has the ability to hear the voices of the dead, particularly those of the Legion's Shepherds, which are the remnants of human minds. This ability allows him to sense the presence of Legion units and understand their intentions, giving him a tactical advantage. It also enables him to communicate with the dead, as seen with his brother.

Limitations

The constant voices cause him psychological distress and can be overwhelming. The ability is not fully controllable and can be a burden.

Contradictions

The exact nature of this ability is not fully explained, and it is unclear if it is a supernatural power or a result of trauma and enhanced senses. The series does not provide a definitive explanation.

Leadership and Strategy

Source Version All versions

Description

As the leader of the Spearhead Squadron, Shin demonstrates strong leadership and tactical acumen. He is able to command his squad effectively, making split-second decisions that often lead to victory.

Limitations

His leadership is often marred by his personal trauma and his tendency to take on too much responsibility, leading to burnout.

Murder of Shin's Family

Source 86: Eighty-Six, Volume 2

Description

When Shin was a child, his family was killed by the Legion. He and his brother were the only survivors, but his brother later died in battle. This event is the root of Shin's trauma and his ability to hear the dead.

Scholarly Context

The series uses this tragedy to explore themes of loss and survival in war.

Systemic Oppression of the 86

Source 86: Eighty-Six, all volumes

Description

The 86 are a persecuted minority who are forced to fight in the war and are denied basic human rights. Shin and his comrades are subjected to inhumane treatment, including being interned in camps and being used as expendable soldiers.

Scholarly Context

The series is a commentary on racism, discrimination, and the dehumanization of marginalized groups in wartime.

Suicide of Previous Handlers

Source 86: Eighty-Six, Volume 1

Description

Many of Shin's previous Handlers have gone insane or committed suicide. This is implied to be due to the psychological burden of communicating with Shin, who is constantly surrounded by death.

Scholarly Context

This element underscores the psychological toll of war on both the 86 and their commanders.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

The research is limited to the English-translated light novels up to Volume 12, the anime series, and the manga adaptation. Later volumes of the light novel (13+) have not been translated into English as of the research date, so the character's development in those volumes is unknown. Additionally, the spin-off manga '86: Operation High School' and '86: Fragmental Neoteny' have not been fully translated, so their content is not covered. The exact nature of Shin's ability to hear the dead is never fully explained in the source material, leaving a gap in understanding. The character's full backstory, including details about his family and his time before the war, is not fully explored. There is also a lack of scholarly analysis of the character, as most sources are fan-based or promotional.
