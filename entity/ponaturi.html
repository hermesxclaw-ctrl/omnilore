Who It Is

The ponaturi are the hostile sea people of Maori tradition — pale, long-fingered goblin-folk with reddish hair and claws, who live beneath the ocean by day and come ashore at night to raid, kill, and occupy houses that are not theirs. They are defined by one absolute vulnerability, and the great hero-tales are all engineered around it: sunlight kills them. Not weakens — kills, instantly, like a struck match. In the Tawhaki cycle, the ponaturi murder the hero's father Hema and keep his bones hanging in their house as trophies, his captive mother Urutonga forced to serve as their door-keeper, tasting the night air and calling the hours. Tawhaki's revenge is the archetype of the daylight trap: he and his brother seal every chink, crack, and window of the ponaturi's house while they sleep inside, and Urutonga, asked for the hour, answers again and again — it is still deep night — until the sun is high and blazing. Then the doors are thrown open. The entire raiding nation dies in one flood of morning. The ponaturi are the Pacific's purest expression of the night-raider fear of every coastal people — the shapes that come out of the surf after dark — and their defeat is the oldest military doctrine on Earth: know the enemy's one weakness, and control the information they depend on. The door-keeper decides the war.

Origins

Core Maori hero-cycle antagonists (Tawhaki, Rata), belonging to the wider Polynesian family of hostile sea-spirits; their trophy-keeping of Hema's bones makes them the epics' designated atrocity, the wrong that launches the plot.

Attested in Maori oral tradition (Tawhaki and Rata cycles)Era Precolonial

Form

Pale sea-goblins with red hair, claw-fingered hands, and eyes suited to black water — numerous as a school of fish, ashore only between dusk and the dawn they must outrun.

Powers

Undersea dwelling, night-raiding in force, house-seizure, trophy-craft — and the drowning strength of hands evolved for pulling things under.

Weaknesses & Wards

Sunlight, absolutely and fatally; dependence on a door-keeper's honest count of the hours — the single point of failure through which Tawhaki destroyed them.

Field Notes

☘ Urutonga's false hour-calls make her one of mythology's decisive double agents.

☘ In the Rata cycle they animate and reassemble the bones of the dead — necromancers as well as raiders.

☘ Their sunlight-death predates European vampire convention by centuries of oral transmission — the Pacific got there first.

Cultural Significance

The sealed-house massacre at dawn is one of the most retold episodes in Maori narrative art, carved into meeting-house panels — the victory of patience and information over numbers.
