Etymology

'Caboclo' is a Brazilian Portuguese term historically used to refer to people of mixed Indigenous and European ancestry, and in Afro-Brazilian religions it denotes a class of spirits representing Indigenous Brazilians. 'Serra Grande' means 'Big Mountain' or 'Great Mountain range' in Portuguese, suggesting a connection to a specific highland or mountain, possibly in the interior of Brazil.

Cultural Ownership

The figure appears to belong to the broader Afro-Brazilian religious complex, particularly Umbanda, which blends African, Indigenous, and Catholic elements. The 'caboclo' spirits are central to Umbanda, but similar figures may appear in Candomblé de Caboclo and other regional traditions. The specific 'Serra Grande' is likely a local or regional manifestation.
