Who It Is

He is the thundering protector of the common man, the red-bearded giant who rides a chariot pulled by goats and hurls a hammer that returns to his hand like a boomerang. Thor is not the wise king of the gods — that's his father Odin — but the brawn, the bodyguard, the one who drinks oceans and wrestles old age itself. He is the god who eats his own goats for dinner and resurrects them the next morning, who dresses as a bride to get his hammer back, and who will one day slay the World Serpent only to die from its venom. In the age of Marvel, he's a superhero; in the Viking Age, he was the most beloved of gods, the one whose hammer amulets were worn by farmers and warriors alike. This is the real Thor — not the Avenger, but the ancient thunder that still echoes in the day Thursday.

Thor is impulsive, hot-tempered, and direct. He is the protector of humanity and the gods against the giants (jötnar). He is not particularly clever, often relying on strength and his hammer. He is also depicted as having a sense of humor (e.g., in Þrymskviða, he cross-dresses as Freyja). Despite his temper, he is a loyal ally and a defender of the cosmic order.

Powers
Mjölnir (Hammer)

A war-hammer that is Thor's primary weapon. It is described as being able to level mountains, and it always returns to Thor's hand after being thrown. It also has a sanctifying function, used in blessings (e.g., at weddings and funerals).

Limit: The handle is famously short, due to Loki's mischief during its forging. It is also heavy and requires Thor's strength (and belt) to wield effectively.

Megingjörð (Belt of Strength)

A belt that when worn doubles Thor's already-godly strength.

Limit: It is a physical item, so it can be removed or lost (as in Þrymskviða, where Thor needs it to lift the hammer).

Járngreipr (Iron Gloves)

Iron gloves required to grip Mjölnir's hot handle.

Limit: Only useful with Mjölnir.

Gríðarvölr (Staff)

A staff borrowed from the giantess Gríðr, used in the encounter with Geirröðr.

Limit: Not a permanent possession; used only in that specific adventure.

Chariot and Goats

Thor rides a chariot pulled by two goats, Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr. He can eat the goats and resurrect them the next day with Mjölnir, as long as their bones are unbroken.

Limit: The goats cannot be resurrected if their bones are broken (as in the story of Þjálfi and Röskva).

Form & Signs

Appearance — Thor is described as a massive, red-bearded god of immense strength. In the Prose Edda, he is said to have 'red hair and a red beard' (though this may be a later Christian-era interpretation). He is often depicted with his hammer Mjölnir, and in artistic representations (e.g., the Altuna runestone) he appears as a giant figure fishing for the World Serpent. His eyes are described as fierce, and he is known for his prodigious appetite and loud voi

Symbols — Mjölnir (hammer), Megingjörð (belt of strength), Járngreipr (iron gloves), Gríðarvölr (staff), the chariot pulled by goats Tanngrisnir and Tanngnjóstr, the hall Bilskirnir, the realm Þrúðheimr/Þrúðvangr, and the day of the week Thursday (Thor's day).

Domains — Thunder, lightning, storms, oak trees, strength, protection, fertility (of land and people), sanctification (of marriages, births, and funerals), and the common farmer/peasant class.

Accounts I
The Theft of Mjölnir (Þrymskviða)Poetic Edda

Viking Age (or earlier, oral tradition)

The giant Þrymr steals Mjölnir and demands Freyja as his bride in exchange. Thor, with Loki's help, dresses as Freyja (with Loki as his handmaiden) and goes to the wedding. After eating and drinking prodigiously (and glaring at the giants), he retrieves Mjölnir and slaughters the giants. Mjölnir is recovered; the giants are killed; the story is a comedic highlight of the Eddas.

Thor's Fishing Trip (Hymiskviða)Poetic Edda

Viking Age

Thor and the giant Hymir go fishing. Thor uses a giant ox head as bait and catches Jörmungandr, the World Serpent. The serpent thrashes, and Hymir cuts the line, but not before Thor's strength is demonstrated. The story ends with Thor and Hymir's contentious feast, after which Thor steals Hymir's cauldron. Thor and Jörmungandr's enmity is cemented; the story is a prelude to Ragnarök.

Accounts II
The Journey to Útgarða-Loki (Gylfaginning)Prose Edda

13th century (recorded)

Thor, Loki, Þjálfi, and Röskva travel to Jötunheimr. They face a series of challenges (eating, running, wrestling) that they fail, only to learn that Útgarða-Loki had tricked them with illusions (e.g., wrestling with old age itself). Thor's strength is shown to be immense but not absolute. The story illustrates the limits of even the gods and the deceptive nature of the giants.

Thor's Duel with HrungnirSkáldskaparmál

13th century (recorded)

The giant Hrungnir challenges Thor to a duel. Hrungnir has a heart of stone and a shield of stone, but Thor throws Mjölnir, which shatters the giant's club and kills him. A whetstone from Hrungnir's club lodges in Thor's head, and the seeress Gróa fails to remove it (because she is distracted by news of her husband). Thor is left with a piece of whetstone in his head; the duel is a famous display of his power.

Ragnarök: The Final BattleVafþrúðnismál, Gylfaginning

Viking Age (oral), 13th century (recorded)

At Ragnarök, Thor fights Jörmungandr for the final time. He slays the serpent, but after walking nine steps, he succumbs to its venom and dies. Thor's death is a key event in the destruction and rebirth of the world.

Thor is the god of thunder and lightning, but he is also a god of war. — Thor is associated with strength and protection, but he is not primarily a war god (that role is more often given to Odin and Tyr). Thor's domain is more about defending and sanctifying, not conquest.

Thor's hammer Mjölnir is a weapon that can only be wielded by Thor. — In the myths, the hammer is so heavy that only Thor can lift it, but this is due to his strength, not an enchantment. In the MCU, Mjölnir has an enchantment that makes it only worthy of the worthy, which is a Marvel invention.

Thor is a blond, muscular god. — The original descriptions (Prose Edda) say he has red hair. His physical build is not described in detail, but he is strong. The blond hair is a Hollywood/Marvel invention.

Thor and Loki are brothers. — In Norse mythology, Loki is Odin's blood-brother, making him Thor's uncle, not his brother. They are not siblings.

Thor is a god of fertility in the sense of a 'fertility god' like Freyr. — Thor is associated with fertility of the land (rain, thunder) and sanctification of marriages, but he is not a god of sexual fertility in the same way as Freyr or Freyja.
