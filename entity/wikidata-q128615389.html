Who It Is

Forget the gods of myth and legend, with their jealousies and thunderbolts. Aeternitas is not a character in a story; she is the story itself — the promise that an empire, a dynasty, and an idea would never end. She is the face on a billion coins, the silent guarantor of a power that claimed to outlast time itself. She is the Roman Empire's greatest, and most tragic, self-portrait.

As a personification, Aeternitas does not have a 'personality' in the narrative sense. She is a symbolic representation of an abstract concept: the unending, unchangeable, and perpetual nature of the Roman state and the cosmos. Her 'behavior' is the state of being eternal itself.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Derived from the Latin 'aeternus' meaning 'eternal, everlasting, immortal'. The root is related to 'aevum' (age, lifetime, eternity). In Roman cult, Aeternitas was the personification of eternity, often associated with the imperial cult and the perpetual nature of Roman power.

Pronunciation — /iːˈtɜːrnəs/ or /aɪˈtɜːrnəs/ (Classical Latin: [ae̯ˈtɛr.nʊs])

Aliases — Aeternitas, Aeternum, The Eternal One, The Everlasting

Ownership — Roman (Imperial period), later syncretized in various esoteric and occult traditions. The concept has been adopted in Neoplatonic philosophy, Renaissance magic, and modern pop-cult

Powers
Personification of Eternity

As the embodiment of the concept of eternity, Aeternitas represents the state of being without beginning or end. This is not a 'power' in the sense of a magical ability, but a fundamental aspect of her being.

Limit: She is a concept, not an active agent. She does not intervene in mortal affairs; she simply represents the eternal nature of the cosmos and the empire.

Imperial Continuity

In the context of the imperial cult, Aeternitas was invoked to guarantee the unbroken succession of emperors and the perpetual survival of the Roman state. She was a divine guarantee of political stability.

Limit: This was a political and religious claim, not a literal magical effect. The actual continuity of the empire was often threatened by civil wars and succession crises.

Cosmic Order (in Neoplatonism)

In Neoplatonic philosophy, 'Aeternitas' (often translated as 'Aion') was seen as the highest, most perfect mode of being, transcending the flow of time. It is the eternal 'now' in which all things exist simultaneously.

Limit: This is a philosophical concept, not a personal power. It describes a mode of reality, not an ability to act.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In Roman art, Aeternitas is typically depicted as a female figure, often veiled, holding symbols of eternity: the Phoenix (a symbol of cyclical rebirth), the Sun and Moon (representing the everlasting celestial cycles), or a globe (representing the eternal empire). She may also hold a scepter or a cornucopia. In some coin types, she is shown as a radiant, solar figure.

Symbols — Phoenix, globe, scepter, cornucopia, Sun, Moon, stars, veil, the zodiac.

Domains — Eternity, perpetuity, imperial continuity, cosmic order, the unending cycle of time, the afterlife (in funerary contexts).

Accounts I
The Apotheosis of Antoninus Pius and FaustinaRelief on the Column of Antoninus Pius, Rome (c. 161 CE)

2nd century CE

The emperor Antoninus Pius and his wife Faustina die and are carried to the heavens by a winged figure, often identified as Aeternitas. She is shown as the divine agent that ensures the emperor's eternal rule in the afterlife and his apotheosis (deification). The scene is a powerful piece of imperial propaganda, linking the emperor's personal eternity with the eternity of the state. The relief reinforces the imperial cult's promise of eternal life for the emperor and the eternal nature of Rome. It is a visual representation of the state's ideological foundation.

Accounts II
The Phoenix as the Symbol of the Eternal EmpireRoman coinage, particularly under Hadrian and later emperors

2nd-4th centuries CE

The legend 'AETERNITAS' is often paired with the image of the Phoenix, a mythical bird that regenerates from its own ashes. This imagery was used to symbolize the cyclical renewal of the Roman Empire and the promise of its eternal existence. The Phoenix was a powerful emblem of the empire's ability to overcome crises and be reborn. The Phoenix imagery became a standard part of imperial iconography, reinforcing the idea of Rome's indestructibility.

Aeternus is a major, active god with a personality and mythology. — Aeternitas is a personification of an abstract concept, not a narrative deity. She has no myths, no personality, and does not intervene in mortal affairs.

Aeternus is the same as the Greek god Chronos. — Chronos is the god of chronological time. Aeternitas is the personification of timelessness and eternity. They are related but distinct.

The concept of Aeternitas was primarily a religious belief about the afterlife. — While it had funerary associations, its primary function was political and ideological, supporting the Roman imperial state.
