Who It Is

Deep in the damp, shadowed cellars of old Irish estates, a small figure perches astride a wine cask, tankard in hand, singing a slurred tune. This is the clurichaun—a solitary fairy of intoxicating mischief, who might guard your household with fierce loyalty one moment and terrorize your cook the next. More than a mere leprechaun's drunk cousin, the clurichaun embodies the Irish fascination with drink, hospitality, and the unpredictable spirits that dwell at the edges of domestic life.

Mischievous, drunken, and fond of practical jokes. He can be helpful to the household he attaches himself to, protecting property and lives, but is quick to anger if slighted. He loves drinking, singing, and pipe smoking. He is a solitary fairy, not found in groups.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Irish 'clobhair-ceann', possibly meaning 'glutton' or 'drinker' (uncertain). The term 'clurichaun' is an anglicization.

Pronunciation — KLOO-ri-khawn (approximate)

Aliases — Cluricaune, Cluracan, Clúrachán, Clobhair-ceann, The Cellar Fairy

Ownership — Irish folklore; specifically Munster and other southern regions, but found throughout Ireland.

Powers
Intoxication Immunity

Can drink enormous quantities of alcohol without becoming incapacitated, as seen in Spider Robinson's stories where Naggeneen drinks vast amounts.

Limit: None specified.

Pass Through Keyholes

Can pass through keyholes to invade homes and wine cellars.

Limit: None specified.

Transformation of Rushes

Can transform bog rushes into horses for riding, and can fly through the air on rushes like witches.

Limit: Requires rushes.

Magical Purse

Carries a purse (or pewter cup) that either contains a 'lucky shilling' that always returns, or is always full of money. He may carry two purses, one with the magic coin, one with a normal coin to trick captors.

Limit: If captured, he can vanish if the captor looks away.

Knowledge of Hidden Treasure

Like leprechauns, he may know the location of hidden treasure and can be forced to reveal it, but he may create illusions of multiple markers.

Limit: Must be captured and forced.

Servitude Enchantment

Can compel a person to serve him for a set period if they accept a drink and refuse to pay.

Limit: Can be broken by invoking the blessing of God.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Typically a small man, about 6 inches tall, with a face like a withered apple, twinkling eyes, and a red and purple nose from heavy drinking. Wears a red nightcap, a short leather apron, light blue stockings, and shoes with large silver buckles. Some accounts say he wears red like other solitary fairies. In Gaiman's Sandman, he appears as a tall, elegant blond fairy due to glamour.

Symbols — Associated with wine casks, tankards, clay pipes ('fairy pipes'), and leather aprons. Often depicted with a jug of ale or a hammer.

Domains — Haunts breweries, pubs, wine cellars, and domestic cellars. He is a household spirit of sorts, attached to a particular family or place.

Accounts I
The Haunted CellarThomas Crofton Croker, Fairy Legends and Traditions of the South of Ireland

19th century (collected)

A clurichaun named Naggeneen haunts the wine cellar of an Irish lord, drinking and playing pranks. He is discovered by the master, who tries to move, but Naggeneen implies he will move with him. The lord decides to stay, and the clurichaun continues to haunt the cellar.

Accounts II
Master and ManIrish folktale (recorded in various collections)

19th century (collected)

Billy Mac Daniel accepts a drink from a clurichaun but refuses to pay, so he is compelled to serve the fairy for seven years and a day. He eventually breaks the servitude by invoking God's blessing. Billy gains his freedom and returns home.

Little WildbeanThomas Keightley, The Fairy Mythology

19th century (collected)

A clurichaun named Little Wildbean haunts the cellar of a Quaker gentleman, Harris. He is helpful, stopping beer leaks, but when the cook leaves him a poor dinner, he drags her down the cellar stairs, injuring her. The cook is bedridden for three weeks; Harris tries to move but Wildbean moves with him.
