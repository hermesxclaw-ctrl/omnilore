Who It Is

In the vast, wind-scoured steppes of Central Asia, before the first shaman beat his drum or the first epic was sung, there was Kayra Han—the silent architect of the universe, the supreme god who breathed order into chaos and then withdrew, leaving his creation to find its own way.

Kayra Han is depicted as a benevolent but distant creator. He is wise, just, and merciful, but he seldom intervenes directly in the affairs of mortals. He delegates authority to his sons, especially Ülgen, who is more active in the world. Kayra Han is also seen as a source of moral law, rewarding the good and punishing the evil, but his punishments are often carried out by subordinate deities. He is patient, but he can be angered by human wrongdoing, as seen in the flood myth.

Powers
Cosmic Creation

Kayra Han is the creator of the universe. He is said to have created the sky, the earth, the sun, the moon, the stars, and all living beings. In some myths, he creates the world by speaking or thinking it into existence; in others, he uses a divine substance or the body of a primordial being.

Limit: His creative power is not unlimited in the sense that he does not create evil; evil arises from Erlik's rebellion. He also does not create the primordial chaos, which is eternal.

Supreme Authority

Kayra Han is the supreme ruler of the universe, with absolute authority over all other deities, spirits, and mortals. He is the ultimate judge and arbiter of destiny.

Limit: He delegates most of his authority and rarely exercises direct power in the world.

Omniscience and Omnipresence

Kayra Han is all-knowing and present everywhere, though he is especially associated with the sky. He sees all human actions and thoughts, and he knows the fate of every being.

Limit: He does not intervene to prevent evil, allowing free will and the consequences of actions.

Blessing and Cursing

Kayra Han has the power to bestow blessings of fertility, health, and prosperity, and to curse those who defy him with misfortune, illness, or death.

Limit: He rarely curses directly; he often delegates punishment to Erlik or other spirits.

Control over Cosmic Elements

He is said to control the elements, including wind, rain, thunder, and lightning, which he uses to reward or punish humanity. This power is sometimes shared with his son Ülgen.

Limit: He is not a storm god per se; his control is more cosmic and less frequent.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kayra Han is rarely described in physical terms. In the few accounts, he is depicted as an immense, formless being of pure light, or as an old man with a long white beard, symbolizing wisdom and age. He is often said to reside in the highest layer of the sky, beyond the visible heavens, in a golden palace. No specific iconography is attested; he is generally represented symbolically as the sky or as a shining light.

Symbols — Kayra Han is associated with the sky, light, and creation. The sky itself is his symbol, and he is often invoked in prayers for creation and blessing. In some traditions, a white bird (often a goose or swan) is his messenger. He is also associated with the sacred tree at the center of the world, which he is said to have planted. No anthropomorphic images are known from historical sources; modern depictions are artistic interpretations.

Domains — Kayra Han dwells in the highest celestial realm, often called 'Kairakan' or 'Kairakan-Tengri,' a place of pure light and order. He rules over the entire universe and is the ultimate source of all life. His domain is the sky, and he is the lord of the heavens. In some myths, he is said to have created the earth, the sky, and all beings, and he continues to sustain them.

Accounts I
The Creation of the WorldAltai creation epic, recorded by Radlov and Verbitsky

Pre-Islamic, recorded in 19th century

In the beginning, there was only water. Kayra Han flew over the primordial ocean in the form of a white goose. He met Erlik, who was also flying, and asked him to dive to the bottom of the sea and bring up mud. Erlik brought up mud, and Kayra Han used it to create the earth. He then created the sky, the sun, the moon, and the stars. He created humans and animals, and gave them souls. Erlik, however, grew proud and sought to create his own world, leading to his rebellion and fall. The creation of the world and the establishment of order. Erlik's rebellion leads to his exile to the underworld and the origin of evil.

The Creation of Erlik and the Origin of EvilAltai mythology, as recorded by Anokhin

Recorded early 20th century

Kayra Han created Erlik as a powerful spirit to assist him. Erlik, however, became jealous of Kayra Han's power and sought to create his own world. He tried to create a being like a human, but his creation was incomplete and evil. Kayra Han punished Erlik by casting him down to the underworld, where he became the lord of evil and death. The establishment of the underworld and the existence of evil in the world. Erlik becomes the tempter of humanity, leading them away from Kayra Han's laws.

Accounts II
The Great FloodAltai and other Siberian Turkic traditions

Pre-Islamic, recorded in folklore

After humanity became wicked and forgot the laws of Kayra Han, he decided to punish them by sending a great flood. He warned a righteous man (sometimes identified with Nama or a similar figure) to build a raft or an ark. The flood covered the earth, destroying all but the righteous man and his family. After the flood, Kayra Han repopulated the earth. The cleansing of the earth and the renewal of humanity. This myth parallels the biblical flood and may reflect later cultural influences.

Kayra Han and the Sacred TreeAltai shamanic cosmology

Traditional, recorded in ethnographic accounts

Kayra Han planted a sacred tree at the center of the world, with its roots in the underworld and its branches reaching into the heavens. This tree, often identified as a birch or larch, serves as the axis mundi, connecting the three worlds. The shaman climbs this tree during rituals to communicate with the gods. The establishment of the cosmic order and the possibility of communication between the human and divine worlds.

Kayra Han is the same as the Islamic God (Allah). — Kayra Han is a pre-Islamic Turkic deity, distinct from Allah. While some Turkic peoples later used the name Kudai for Allah, Kayra Han's mythology is indigenous.

Kayra Han is a malevolent or evil god. — Kayra Han is a benevolent creator and judge. Evil is attributed to Erlik, not Kayra Han.

Kayra Han is a minor deity. — He is the supreme god in Altai mythology, the creator and ultimate authority.

Kayra Han is only found in Altai mythology. — He is recognized across Siberian Turkic peoples and in Pan-Turkic mythology, though his prominence varies.
