Kayra Han sits on the golden throne above the ninth sky, and all creation is the work of his mercy.

Who It Is

Kayra Han (also Kaira Khan) is the supreme creator god of the Turkic peoples, especially the Altai and Siberian Turks. He is the eternal, benevolent sky father who created the world, the other gods, and humanity. Kayra Han is not an active, intervening god — he is the distant source of all existence, the compassionate origin who set the cosmic order in motion and then entrusted it to the other spirits. According to Altai creation epic, Kayra Han created the first being (Erlik) and then, after Erlik's rebellion, created the world from the materials provided by Erlik. Kayra Han created the first humans from clay or reeds and placed them on the earth, instructing them to live according to the natural order. He dwells in the highest heaven, seated on a golden throne, surrounded by light and the spirits of righteous ancestors. His symbol is the white swan, and his color is white. Shamanic prayers to Kayra Han are performed with a white horse sacrifice (symbolically) and involve climbing a cosmic birch tree (the shaman's 'world tree'). Kayra Han represents the monotheistic impulse within the shamanic Turkic tradition — a single, supreme, merciful creator who stands above the lesser spirits and divinities. His name is preserved in the Mongolian 'Küri' (circle, assembly) and is reflected in the concept of 'Han' (king, ruler).

The Approach

In the vast, wind-scoured steppes of Central Asia, before the first shaman beat his drum or the first epic was sung, there was Kayra Han—the silent architect of the universe, the supreme god who breathed order into chaos and then withdrew, leaving his creation to find its own way.

Kayra Han

Etymology

The etymology of 'Kayra' is uncertain. Some scholars link it to Old Turkic 'qayra' meaning 'to create' or 'to bring forth,' while others suggest a connection to the Mongolic 'qair' meaning 'mercy' or 'grace.' In some interpretations, it is derived from 'qara' (black) or 'qayra' (rock), but these are speculative. The title 'Han' (Khan) means 'ruler' or 'lord,' indicating his supreme authority.

Cultural Ownership

Kayra Han is a supreme deity in the traditional religion of the Altai Turks and other Siberian Turkic peoples, including the Khakas, Shors, and some Tuvan groups. He is also recognized in broader Pan-Turkic mythology as a creator god, though his prominence varies among different Turkic cultures. His worship is primarily historical and mythological, with modern revival movements among Turkic neopagans.

Disputed Identifications

Kayra Han is sometimes identified with the more widely known Turkic creator god Tengri (or Ülgen). Some scholars see him as a distinct deity, while others consider him an aspect or epithet of Tengri. In some traditions, he is conflated with Ülgen, his son, or with Kudai, a general name for God. The relationship between Kayra Han and the cosmic spirit 'Kaira' is also debated, with some viewing them as identical and others as separate entities.

Description

Appearance

Kayra Han is rarely described in physical terms. In the few accounts, he is depicted as an immense, formless being of pure light, or as an old man with a long white beard, symbolizing wisdom and age. He is often said to reside in the highest layer of the sky, beyond the visible heavens, in a golden palace. No specific iconography is attested; he is generally represented symbolically as the sky or as a shining light.

Symbols & Iconography

Kayra Han is associated with the sky, light, and creation. The sky itself is his symbol, and he is often invoked in prayers for creation and blessing. In some traditions, a white bird (often a goose or swan) is his messenger. He is also associated with the sacred tree at the center of the world, which he is said to have planted. No anthropomorphic images are known from historical sources; modern depictions are artistic interpretations.

Behaviour & Personality

Kayra Han is depicted as a benevolent but distant creator. He is wise, just, and merciful, but he seldom intervenes directly in the affairs of mortals. He delegates authority to his sons, especially Ülgen, who is more active in the world. Kayra Han is also seen as a source of moral law, rewarding the good and punishing the evil, but his punishments are often carried out by subordinate deities. He is patient, but he can be angered by human wrongdoing, as seen in the flood myth.

Domains & Habitats

Kayra Han dwells in the highest celestial realm, often called 'Kairakan' or 'Kairakan-Tengri,' a place of pure light and order. He rules over the entire universe and is the ultimate source of all life. His domain is the sky, and he is the lord of the heavens. In some myths, he is said to have created the earth, the sky, and all beings, and he continues to sustain them.

Version Differences

The most significant variation is in his relationship with other gods. In some Altai myths, Kayra Han is the supreme god who creates the world and then retires, leaving Ülgen as the active ruler. In other versions, Kayra Han is identified with Ülgen himself, or with Tengri, the sky god. In some traditions, he is not the creator but the first being, who then creates the world. The story of the creation of Erlik also varies: in some accounts, Kayra Han creates Erlik as a helper, while in others, Erlik emerges from the primordial chaos or is created by accident.

Powers & Abilities

Cosmic Creation

Source Version Common to most Turkic creation myths featuring Kayra Han.

Description

Kayra Han is the creator of the universe. He is said to have created the sky, the earth, the sun, the moon, the stars, and all living beings. In some myths, he creates the world by speaking or thinking it into existence; in others, he uses a divine substance or the body of a primordial being.

Limitations

His creative power is not unlimited in the sense that he does not create evil; evil arises from Erlik's rebellion. He also does not create the primordial chaos, which is eternal.

Contradictions

Some versions attribute creation to Ülgen or Tengri instead, leading to debates about whether Kayra Han is the original creator or a later syncretic figure.

Supreme Authority

Source Version Attested in Altai shamanic invocations and epic poetry.

Description

Kayra Han is the supreme ruler of the universe, with absolute authority over all other deities, spirits, and mortals. He is the ultimate judge and arbiter of destiny.

Limitations

He delegates most of his authority and rarely exercises direct power in the world.

Contradictions

His supreme authority is sometimes overshadowed by the more active role of Ülgen, leading to questions about whether Kayra Han is merely a passive figurehead.

Omniscience and Omnipresence

Source Version Implied in prayers and hymns that invoke him as the all-seeing judge.

Description

Kayra Han is all-knowing and present everywhere, though he is especially associated with the sky. He sees all human actions and thoughts, and he knows the fate of every being.

Limitations

He does not intervene to prevent evil, allowing free will and the consequences of actions.

Contradictions

Some traditions ascribe these attributes to Tengri, making Kayra Han a redundant or syncretic figure.

Blessing and Cursing

Source Version Attested in shamanic rituals and folk prayers.

Description

Kayra Han has the power to bestow blessings of fertility, health, and prosperity, and to curse those who defy him with misfortune, illness, or death.

Limitations

He rarely curses directly; he often delegates punishment to Erlik or other spirits.

Contradictions

None noted.

Control over Cosmic Elements

Source Version In the flood myth, he sends a flood to punish humanity.

Description

He is said to control the elements, including wind, rain, thunder, and lightning, which he uses to reward or punish humanity. This power is sometimes shared with his son Ülgen.

Limitations

He is not a storm god per se; his control is more cosmic and less frequent.

Contradictions

Some myths attribute storm control solely to Ülgen.

Stories & Myths

The Creation of the World

Source Altai creation epic, recorded by Radlov and VerbitskyCulture Altai TurkicPeriod Pre-Islamic, recorded in 19th century

Summary

In the beginning, there was only water. Kayra Han flew over the primordial ocean in the form of a white goose. He met Erlik, who was also flying, and asked him to dive to the bottom of the sea and bring up mud. Erlik brought up mud, and Kayra Han used it to create the earth. He then created the sky, the sun, the moon, and the stars. He created humans and animals, and gave them souls. Erlik, however, grew proud and sought to create his own world, leading to his rebellion and fall.

Participants

Kayra Han

Erlik

Consequences

The creation of the world and the establishment of order. Erlik's rebellion leads to his exile to the underworld and the origin of evil.

Variants

In some versions, Kayra Han creates the world alone, without Erlik's help. In others, Erlik is created by Kayra Han and then rebels. The number of worlds created varies, with some myths describing three or nine layers.

The Creation of Erlik and the Origin of Evil

Source Altai mythology, as recorded by AnokhinCulture Altai TurkicPeriod Recorded early 20th century

Summary

Kayra Han created Erlik as a powerful spirit to assist him. Erlik, however, became jealous of Kayra Han's power and sought to create his own world. He tried to create a being like a human, but his creation was incomplete and evil. Kayra Han punished Erlik by casting him down to the underworld, where he became the lord of evil and death.

Participants

Kayra Han

Erlik

Consequences

The establishment of the underworld and the existence of evil in the world. Erlik becomes the tempter of humanity, leading them away from Kayra Han's laws.

Variants

In some accounts, Erlik is not created by Kayra Han but emerges from the primordial chaos. In others, he is a fallen angel-like figure who was once good.

The Great Flood

Source Altai and other Siberian Turkic traditionsCulture Altai Turkic, Khakas, ShorPeriod Pre-Islamic, recorded in folklore

Summary

After humanity became wicked and forgot the laws of Kayra Han, he decided to punish them by sending a great flood. He warned a righteous man (sometimes identified with Nama or a similar figure) to build a raft or an ark. The flood covered the earth, destroying all but the righteous man and his family. After the flood, Kayra Han repopulated the earth.

Participants

Kayra Han

Nama (or similar righteous man)

Humanity

Consequences

The cleansing of the earth and the renewal of humanity. This myth parallels the biblical flood and may reflect later cultural influences.

Variants

The name of the righteous man varies; in some traditions, it is Nama, in others, it is a different figure. The method of survival also varies, from a raft to a boat made of iron.

Kayra Han and the Sacred Tree

Source Altai shamanic cosmologyCulture Altai TurkicPeriod Traditional, recorded in ethnographic accounts

Summary

Kayra Han planted a sacred tree at the center of the world, with its roots in the underworld and its branches reaching into the heavens. This tree, often identified as a birch or larch, serves as the axis mundi, connecting the three worlds. The shaman climbs this tree during rituals to communicate with the gods.

Participants

Kayra Han

Shamans

Consequences

The establishment of the cosmic order and the possibility of communication between the human and divine worlds.

Variants

The tree is sometimes said to be planted by Ülgen or Tengri instead.

Relationships

Ülgen

Type sonSource Altai mythology, ethnographic records

Description

Ülgen is the son of Kayra Han and the active ruler of the upper world. He is the protector of humanity and the chief deity of the sky in many Altai traditions. Kayra Han delegates much of his authority to Ülgen, who is more directly involved in human affairs.

Version Note

In some traditions, Ülgen is not the son but an aspect of Kayra Han, or even identical to him.

Conflicting Accounts

Some scholars argue that Ülgen was originally the supreme god and that Kayra Han is a later addition or an epithet.

Erlik

Type creation/adversarySource Altai creation myths

Description

Erlik is the creation of Kayra Han, but he rebelled and became the lord of the underworld. He is the chief adversary of Kayra Han and the source of evil, temptation, and death. Kayra Han confines Erlik to the underworld but allows him to test humanity.

Version Note

In some versions, Erlik is not created by Kayra Han but is a primordial being or a fallen angel.

Conflicting Accounts

The nature of Erlik's fall varies: some say he was cast out for pride, others for trying to create a world, and others for disobedience.

Umay

Type associate/consortSource Turkic mythology, particularly among Siberian and Central Asian Turkic peoples

Description

Umay is the goddess of fertility, childbirth, and children. She is often associated with Kayra Han as a benevolent female deity who protects women and children. She is sometimes considered his consort or daughter, though the relationship is not consistently defined.

Version Note

Umay is more prominent in some traditions than others; in Altai mythology, she is a major figure.

Conflicting Accounts

Her exact relationship to Kayra Han is unclear; some sources do not connect them directly.

Tengri

Type identification/overlapSource Comparative mythology

Description

Tengri is the sky god in many Turkic and Mongolic traditions. Kayra Han is often identified with Tengri, or considered a specific manifestation of the supreme sky god. The two names are used interchangeably in some contexts.

Version Note

The identification is strongest in Pan-Turkic contexts, where Kayra Han is seen as the creator aspect of Tengri.

Conflicting Accounts

Some scholars maintain that Tengri and Kayra Han are distinct deities with different origins and functions.

Kudai

Type identification/overlapSource Turkic linguistics and religious studies

Description

Kudai is a general term for God in many Turkic languages, often used in Islamic contexts to refer to Allah. In some pre-Islamic traditions, it was used for the supreme deity, and it is sometimes applied to Kayra Han.

Version Note

The use of Kudai for Kayra Han is more common in southern Siberian traditions.

Conflicting Accounts

The term Kudai is ambiguous and may refer to different deities depending on the region.

Erlik's Rebellion and Punishment

Source Altai creation myths, recorded by Anokhin and others

Description

Erlik, created by Kayra Han, rebels against his creator out of pride and ambition. Kayra Han punishes him by casting him into the underworld, where he becomes the lord of evil. This myth establishes the theological basis for the existence of evil and suffering in the world.

Scholarly Context

This narrative parallels other creation myths where a divine being falls from grace, such as the fall of Lucifer in Christianity. Scholars note that the myth may have been influenced by later religious contact, but it also has indigenous roots.

The Great Flood as Divine Punishment

Source Altai and Siberian Turkic flood myths

Description

Kayra Han sends a catastrophic flood to destroy humanity because of their wickedness. This act of mass destruction is a central event in the myth, leading to the death of all but a few righteous survivors.

Scholarly Context

The flood myth is widespread across cultures and may have been influenced by Mesopotamian and biblical narratives. However, the Turkic version has distinct elements, such as the use of a raft or boat and the involvement of Kayra Han.

The Origin of Evil and Death

Source Altai mythology

Description

The myth explains that evil, death, and suffering are not created by Kayra Han but arise from Erlik's rebellion. This theological position absolves Kayra Han of responsibility for evil, but it also introduces a dualistic worldview where the supreme god is opposed by a powerful evil spirit.

Scholarly Context

This dualism is common in many religious traditions, but it is notably present in Turkic mythology. Some scholars see it as an indigenous development, while others argue for external influences from Zoroastrianism or Christianity.

Contradictions & Disputes

Identity with Tengri

Position A

Kayra Han is a distinct deity, separate from Tengri.

Position A — evidence

Some Altai myths clearly distinguish Kayra Han as the creator and Tengri as the sky god.

Position B

Kayra Han is an aspect or epithet of Tengri.

Position B — evidence

In many Turkic traditions, Tengri is the supreme god and creator, and Kayra Han is used as a synonym.

Scholarly Consensus

No consensus. The relationship is debated, with some scholars seeing a syncretic blending over time.

Relationship with Ülgen

Position A

Ülgen is the son of Kayra Han.

Position A — evidence

Ethnographic records from the Altai explicitly state that Ülgen is the son.

Position B

Ülgen is identical to Kayra Han.

Position B — evidence

In some rituals, prayers are addressed to Ülgen as the creator, suggesting he holds the same role as Kayra Han.

Scholarly Consensus

Most scholars accept the father-son relationship, but the identification is noted in some sources.

Role in Creation

Position A

Kayra Han is the sole creator of the universe.

Position A — evidence

Some versions of the creation myth describe Kayra Han creating everything alone.

Position B

Kayra Han created the world with the help of Erlik or others.

Position B — evidence

The most common version has Erlik diving for mud to create the earth.

Scholarly Consensus

The version with Erlik is more widespread, but the solitary creation is also attested.

Nature of Erlik

Position A

Erlik was created by Kayra Han.

Position A — evidence

In many myths, Kayra Han creates Erlik as a helper.

Position B

Erlik is an uncreated, primordial being.

Position B — evidence

In others, Erlik exists before creation, and Kayra Han encounters him in the primordial ocean.

Scholarly Consensus

The creation of Erlik is more common, but the primordial nature is also attested.

Archivist Notes

Known Gaps

Direct primary sources in the original Altai language were not accessible in this research. The dossier relies on translations and secondary scholarship. The exact etymology of 'Kayra' remains uncertain. The relationship between Kayra Han and other deities (Tengri, Ülgen) is debated, and this entry reflects the uncertainty. Modern neopagan practices involving Kayra Han are not well documented in academic sources. The reception of Kayra Han in popular culture is limited; most references are scholarly or in niche online communities. The depth of research is moderate; further access to Russian-language ethnographic literature could enhance this entry.

Research Scope

This dossier synthesizes available scholarly and folkloric sources on Kayra Han, focusing on his role in Turkic and Siberian creation myths. It covers his identity, attributes, stories, relationships, and reception, with explicit attention to regional variations and scholarly debates. The research relies on published ethnographic studies, mythological dictionaries, and primary oral traditions as documented by researchers. Where direct primary sources are unavailable, this is noted. The scope is limited to the most widely recognized traditions, primarily among the Altai and other Siberian Turkic peoples.
