Who It Is

In the fog-shrouded streets of old Antwerp, where the Scheldt's waters lapped against the quays, a giant of shifting form was said to lurk—Lange Wapper, the tall one who could grow to the height of a house or shrink to a child, a trickster whose very name whispered fear and fascination into the ears of Flemish folk.

Lange Wapper is a trickster and a shape-shifter. He delights in frightening people, especially those who are out late at night. He may appear as a child to lure victims, then grow to a terrifying height. He is not generally malevolent in a deadly sense but is a source of fear and mischief. He is also said to be vulnerable to being caught or outwitted, and some tales involve him being tricked in return.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name is Dutch/Flemish: 'lange' meaning 'tall' or 'long', and 'wapper' possibly derived from 'wapperen' (to flutter, wave, or dangle), suggesting a tall, flapping, or swaying figure. It may also relate to 'wapper' as a term for a ghost or specter.

Pronunciation — LAHNG-uh VAH-puhr (approximate Dutch pronunciation: [ˈlɑŋə ˈʋɑpər])

Aliases — Langewapper, Lange Wapper van Antwerpen

Ownership — Belgian (Flemish) folklore, specifically associated with the city of Antwerp and the region of Flanders. The figure is a local legend, not widely known outside the Low Countries.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Lange Wapper is described as an unnaturally tall man, often with a thin, gaunt frame. He can change his size at will, from a giant towering over houses to a small child. Some accounts describe him as wearing a hat and carrying a stick, but details vary. He is often depicted as a shadowy, menacing figure.

Symbols — No specific symbols are consistently associated with him, but he is often shown as a tall, dark silhouette against the Antwerp skyline. In modern statues and illustrations, he is depicted as a lanky, mischievous figure.

Domains — Primarily urban: the streets, quays, and alleys of Antwerp, often near the Scheldt river. He is a water-adjacent spirit, but his haunt is the city itself.

The Trickster of AntwerpOral tradition, recorded in 19th-century collections

Traditional, pre-19th century

Lange Wapper wanders the streets of Antwerp at night, playing tricks on late-night revelers. He may appear as a small child asking for directions, then grow to a towering height to scare the passerby. Other tales involve him following people home or lurking near the river. His victims are frightened but usually unharmed. Some tales say he can be repelled by certain actions, such as not showing fear.
