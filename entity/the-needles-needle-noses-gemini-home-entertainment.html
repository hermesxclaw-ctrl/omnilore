Who It Is

Entities described in the 'Games for Kids' tape — humanoid figures whose heads are masses of long, thin, needle-like protrusions pointing in all directions. The instructional tape presents 'identifying' Needle Noses as a children's game: 'Can you spot the Needle Nose? Is it in your room? Is it behind you?' Needle Noses are always shown in the background of family photographs — standing in doorways, at the edge of yards, visible only when the photo is enlarged. They have always been there. The tape implies they are attracted to children who 'see things they shouldn't' and that the 'game' of spotting them is how they find new targets.

Form

Humanoid figures, approximately 2m tall, with unnaturally thin bodies covered in gray, bark-like skin. Their heads are spherical masses of long (30-50cm), thin, needle-like protrusions radiating in all directions. No eyes, no mouth — the needles are sensory organs. They stand perfectly still for hours, visible only in photographs or peripheral vision.

Cultural Significance

The Needle Nose's design — a head of radiating needles — is one of Gemini Home's most striking visuals, and the 'can you spot it?' game format makes the viewer complicit in searching for something terrible.
