The Approach

Ulti, or Ultimó, is Hungary's national card game, a three-player trick-taking game of dizzying complexity and strategic depth, virtually unknown beyond its borders. Its name alone hints at its most thrilling feature: winning the last trick with the lowest trump, a feat that can turn a game on its head.

Etymology

The name 'Ulti' derives from the Italian 'ultimo' meaning 'last', referring to the winning of the last trick with the lowest trump, a feature derived from games like Trappola and Tapp Tarock.

Description

Appearance

Ulti is played with a 32-card pack of German-suited cards (Acorns, Leaves, Hearts, Bells), with ranks Ace, Ten, King, Over, Under, Nine, Eight, Seven.

Symbols & Iconography

No specific symbols or iconography associated with the game itself.

Behaviour & Personality

Ulti is a game of intricate bidding and strategic play. The auction is unique, involving a talon of two cards that players can pick up and replace. The game is known for its complex scoring system and the many possible contracts, which can be combined.

Domains & Habitats

Primarily played in Hungary, in homes, clubs, and possibly online.

Version Differences

Rules vary significantly between groups of players. The provided source describes one common set of rules, but notes that other rule sets exist and can alter strategy.

History of Ulti

Summary

Ulti evolved from Mariaš, a game first mentioned in Hungary in 1787 and described in 1883. It incorporates features from Trappola and Tapp Tarock, and ultimately derives from the French game Mariage.
