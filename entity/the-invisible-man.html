Who It Is
Form

The Invisible Man is a scientist who has devoted himself to research into optics and invents a way to change a body's refractive index to that of air so that it neither absorbs nor reflects light. As a result, he becomes invisible, but his appearance is not entirely absent, as his voice and footsteps can still be perceived. The Invisible Man's appearance is often described as a kind of 'negative space', where his presence is felt but not seen. In the novel, Wells describes The Invisible Man as a 'tall, thin man with a gaunt face and sunken eyes', but this description is not necessarily a physical appearance, but rather a kind of 'aura' or 'presence' that he exudes. The Invisible Man's appearance has been interpreted in various ways, with some seeing him as a symbol of the 'invisible' or 'hidden' aspects of human nature, while others see him as a representation of the 'other' or the 'outsider'. The character's appearance has also been influenced by various cultural and literary traditions, including the works of Jules Verne and the scientific discoveries of the late 19th century.

Powers

Invisibility The Invisible Man has the ability to become invisible, which allows him to move freely and unnoticed. He achieves this through a scientific process that changes his body's refractive index to that of air, making him neither absorb nor reflect light.

Accounts

The story follows the protagonist, Griffin, a scientist who becomes invisible after discovering a way to change his body's refractive index. He uses his newfound power to commit crimes and wreak havoc, but ultimately meets his downfall.

The film is an adaptation of the novel, starring Claude Rains as the Invisible Man. It follows the same basic plot as the novel, but with some significant changes and additions.
