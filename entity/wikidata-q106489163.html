Who It Is

In 1971, two jazz enthusiasts in Munich founded a record label that would become a sanctuary for avant-garde improvisers, world music pioneers, and straight-ahead masters alike. Enja Records didn't just release albums—it curated a sonic universe where Cecil Taylor's free jazz collided with Turkish saz virtuosity and Indonesian vocal traditions, all under the banner of uncompromising artistry.

The label is characterized by its eclectic taste, releasing both avant-garde and straight-ahead jazz, as well as world music. It appears to prioritize artistic merit over commercial trends.

Accounts I
Founding and Early ReleasesWikipedia

1971 onwards

Founded by Matthias Winckelmann and Horst Weber, the label's first release was by Mal Waldron. Early releases included European and Japanese avant-garde artists, as well as American jazz musicians. Established Enja as a significant label in the jazz world, known for supporting both avant-garde and mainstream artists.

Accounts II
Expansion into World MusicWikipedia

Later years

The label branched out to release world music productions from artists like Abdullah Ibrahim, Rabih Abou-Khalil, Mahmoud Turkmani, Gypsy bands, Monica Akihary, and Taner Akyol. Broadened the label's scope and introduced diverse global sounds to its audience.
