The Approach

In the mid-1950s, a Brazilian medium named Hercílio Maes published a book claiming that Mars was inhabited by spiritually advanced beings—and that the text had been dictated to him by a spirit called Ramatis. This encounter sparked a syncretic spiritual movement that blends Kardecist spiritism, Eastern mysticism, Umbanda, and UFO lore, yet remains firmly outside the orthodox Spiritist mainstream.

Etymology

The name 'Ramatis' is said to recall the Iraqi city of Ramadi, or the fossil 'Ramadis Kadaba' found in Ethiopia, meaning 'root' in the Afar language. However, these are speculative connections proposed by followers or researchers; no definitive etymology is given.

Cultural Ownership

Brazilian Spiritist and esoteric movement; specifically associated with the medium Hercílio Maes and his followers, who call themselves 'Ramatisists'.

Disputed Identifications

Some orthodox Kardecist spiritists, such as Herculano Pires, have dismissed Ramatis as a 'mystifying spirit' or 'false prophet of erraticism', questioning the authenticity of the mediumistic communication.

Description

Appearance

No physical description is provided; Ramatis is a disembodied spirit. In past incarnations, he is said to have been a Hindu guru in the 10th century, a previous incarnation of Pythagoras, a resident of Atlantis, an advisor to King Solomon, a son of Moses, and a bodyguard of Jesus.

Symbols & Iconography

The 'Fraternity of the Cross and the Triangle' is a symbol associated with Ramatis, combining Christian and Eastern elements.

Behaviour & Personality

Ramatis is described as a wise and evolved spirit, guiding humanity through his teachings. He is portrayed as a coordinator of a spiritual team, promoting unity between Western and Eastern spiritual traditions.

Domains & Habitats

Ramatis is said to reside in the spiritual realm, possibly on Mars or in higher astral planes. He is associated with the spiritual governance of Earth's evolution.

Version Differences

Different authors attribute their inspiration to Ramatis, but the core identity remains consistent. However, the emphasis on certain teachings varies: Maes focuses on UFOs and cosmic Christianity, while others like Norberto Peixoto emphasize Umbanda and mediumship.
