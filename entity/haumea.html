Who It Is

In the volcanic heart of Hawaiʻi, where the earth splits and gives birth to islands, there dwells a goddess of formidable power and paradoxical grace—Haumea, the mother of Pele and the keeper of the land's fecundity. Her story is one of transformation, of life wrested from stone, and of a divine family whose fiery passions shaped the archipelago itself.

Haumea is depicted as a powerful, sometimes capricious deity, deeply connected to the land and its life-giving forces. She is a mother figure, though her actions can be morally ambiguous, especially in stories involving her relationships with her descendants.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Haumea' is often interpreted as 'red' or 'sacred' (from 'haʻu' meaning 'red' or 'sacred', and 'mea' meaning 'thing' or 'being'), but the exact etymology is uncertain and debated among linguists.

Pronunciation — How-MAY-ah (approximate Hawaiian pronunciation: [hauˈmɛjə])

Aliases — Papa, Haumea-nui-Kalana-wai-ʻulaʻula, Haumea-nui-Hanau-moku, Haumea-ʻia, Haumea-ʻia-ka-lani

Form & Signs

Appearance — Haumea is not consistently described in physical form; she is a shape-shifter, often taking the form of an old woman or a young maiden. In some stories, she appears as a beautiful woman, in others as a hag. No canonical physical description exists.

Symbols — Associated with the earth, fertility, childbirth, and the island of Hawaiʻi itself. She is linked to the ʻōhiʻa tree and the lehua blossom, and to the volcanic forces that create land.

Domains — She resides in the earth and is associated with the island of Hawaiʻi, particularly the volcanic regions. She is a goddess of childbirth, fertility, and the earth itself.

Accounts I
The Birth of PeleVarious Hawaiian oral traditions, documented by Beckwith and others

Pre-contact to 19th century

Haumea gives birth to Pele, the volcano goddess, often from her thigh or in a miraculous manner. In some versions, Pele is born from Haumea's side, and the birth is accompanied by volcanic eruptions and earthquakes. Pele becomes the dominant volcano deity, and her rivalry with her sister Nāmaka leads to the creation of the Hawaiian Islands.

Accounts II
Haumea and the Incestuous UnionsDocumented in Fornander and other collections

Pre-contact

Haumea engages in relationships with her descendants to renew the earth and maintain her divine power. These stories are often used to explain the sacred nature of certain genealogies and the concept of divine incest in Hawaiian nobility. These unions produce powerful offspring and reinforce the mana (spiritual power) of certain lines, but also present moral complexities.

Haumea as Earth MotherSome genealogical chants

Pre-contact

Haumea is identified with the earth itself, and her body is the land. She gives birth to islands and plants. Establishes her as a primordial creator figure.
