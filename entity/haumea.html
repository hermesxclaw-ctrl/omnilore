Who It Is

Haumea is the Hawaiian goddess of fertility, childbirth, and the earth — a powerful, ancient deity who is both the mother of Pele and the other fire goddesses and a shapeshifting trickster in her own right. She is able to take on different forms, particularly appearing as an old woman to test or help humans. Haumea is associated with the hala (pandanus) tree and with the childbirth practices of ancient Hawaiians. She is the mother of many important deities, including Pele, Kāne-milohaʻi, Kā-moho-aliʻi, Hiʻiaka, Laka, and others. In some traditions, she is married to Wākea (Sky Father). A key myth involves Haumea's magical ability to give birth from different parts of her body, enabling her to have many children without aging. She also saved her daughter Pele from the sea goddess Namaka by throwing her across the islands. Haumea represents the generative, nurturing power of the earth, but also its cycles of death and rebirth. The dwarf planet Haumea (discovered 2004) is named after her, as are its moons Hiʻiaka and Namaka.

Origins

Haumea is one of the most ancient Hawaiian deities, sometimes described as arriving from Kahiki (Tahiti). She is the wife of Wākea (Sky Father) and mother of the goddesses who shaped the Hawaiian landscape. She taught Hawaiians the sacred birthing practices and blessed the hala tree.
