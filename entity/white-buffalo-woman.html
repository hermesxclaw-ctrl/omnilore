Who It Is

White Buffalo Woman (Ptesan-Wi in Lakota) is the most sacred figure in Lakota religion and one of the most revered prophetic figures in all of Native American spirituality. She is the divine messenger who brought the Seven Sacred Rites and the Sacred Pipe (Chanunpa) to the Lakota people, establishing the ceremonial foundation of Lakota religious life. Her story is not merely a myth but a foundational spiritual narrative that continues to shape Lakota identity, ritual, and ethics.

Physically, White Buffalo Woman is described as an extraordinarily beautiful young woman dressed in white buckskin, carrying a bundle wrapped in buffalo calf skin. Her eyes are described as deeply knowing, her movements fluid and graceful, and her presence radiant. When she first appeared, she seemed to float over the ground rather than walk. At the climax of her visit, she transformed before the people's eyes: she rolled on the ground and became a white buffalo calf, then a black buffalo, then a yellow buffalo, then a red buffalo — representing the four directions and the four races of humanity — before disappearing over the horizon.

The story of White Buffalo Woman's visitation is one of the most detailed and sacred narratives in Lakota tradition. Long ago, during a time of great famine and confusion, two Lakota scouts hunting on the prairie saw a beautiful woman approaching from the distance, carrying a bundle. One scout had lustful thoughts; as he reached for her, a cloud enveloped them, and when it cleared, only a pile of bones remained. The other scout, who had kept a pure heart, was instructed to return to the camp and prepare for her arrival.

When White Buffalo Woman entered the Lakota camp, she presented the people with the Sacred Pipe, explaining each part's significance: the bowl (red stone representing the Earth and the blood of the people), the stem (wood representing all growing things), the eagle feather attached (representing the sky and the Great Spirit). She taught the Lakota the Seven Sacred Rites: the Sweat Lodge (Inipi), the Vision Quest (Hanbleceyapi), the Keeping of the Soul, the Sun Dance (Wiwanyag Wachipi), the Making of Relatives (Hunkapi), the Girl's Coming of Age Ceremony (Ishna Ta Awi Cha Lowan), and the Throwing of the Ball. She instructed the people in prayer, in the proper relationship with Wakan Tanka (the Great Spirit), and in the ethics of generosity, courage, respect, and wisdom.

Before leaving, White Buffalo Woman prophesied that she would return at the end of time, when the Lakota way of life was in danger of being lost, signaled by the birth of a white buffalo calf. The birth of white buffalo calves in 1994 (named 'Miracle') and in subsequent years has been interpreted by many Lakota as the fulfillment of this prophecy and a call for spiritual renewal. The White Buffalo Woman narrative is not a dead document of ancient folklore — it is a living religious reality that guides Lakota ceremony, ethics, and hope.
