Who It Is

Deep in the Colombian Andes, where the mist clings to the coffee slopes and the rivers run cold, there is a sound that makes even the boldest hunter freeze: the uneven thump of a single foot on the forest floor. She is La Patasola, the One-Foot, a spirit of the wild that punishes those who break the forest's laws — and lures the unfaithful to their doom.

La Patasola is a vengeful spirit who punishes those who harm the forest (hunters, loggers, miners) and those who betray romantic partners. She is a seductress who lures unfaithful men into the wild, then reveals her true form and kills them. She also protects wild animals and may attack those who mistreat them. She is capricious and can be appeased with offerings.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Spanish 'pata' (leg or foot of an animal) and 'sola' (alone), literally 'the one-footed one'.

Pronunciation — lah pah-tah-SOH-lah

Aliases — La Pata Sola, The One-Foot

Ownership — Colombian folklore, particularly Andean and Amazonian regions; also present in Venezuelan and Ecuadorian variants.

Form & Signs

Appearance — La Patasola is typically described as a beautiful woman from the front, but with a single leg ending in a hoof or a twisted foot. Her eyes are often said to be glowing or hollow. She may appear as a seductive woman in white or rags, sometimes with wild hair. From behind, she is hideous, with a gaping mouth or no face.

Symbols — The single foot is her defining symbol, representing her liminal nature between human and animal. She is associated with the forest, rivers, and caves. Some depictions show her with a machete or a child in her arms, but these are less consistent.

Domains — Deep forests, mountains, rivers, and caves in Colombia, especially in the Andean coffee region and the Amazon. She is said to appear near water sources and in isolated trails.

Accounts I
The Unfaithful HusbandCollected in Colombian folklore anthologies

20th century

A man who is unfaithful to his wife meets a beautiful woman in the forest. She leads him deep into the woods, then reveals her single hoof and attacks him. He dies, and his body is found torn apart. The story warns against infidelity and entering the forest with bad intentions. Death of the man; the community recognizes her work.

Accounts II
The Hunter's PunishmentOral tradition

20th century

A hunter who kills more than he needs is confronted by La Patasola. She appears as a beautiful woman and asks him to dance. As they dance, she leads him to a cliff, and he falls to his death. The story teaches respect for nature. Death of the hunter; the forest remains protected.
