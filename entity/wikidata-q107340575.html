Who It Is

In the shadow of Troy, a man named Euchenor once stood at a crossroads: to die slowly of disease at home, or to fall swiftly in battle abroad. He chose the spear. But Euchenor is not one man—he is a name scattered across Greek myth, worn by princes, seers' sons, and wind-kings' offspring, each meeting a fate written by the gods.

The Corinthian Euchenor is characterized by his decision to accept death at Troy rather than suffer a lingering illness at home, showing courage and fatalism. The other Euchenors are only listed in genealogies with no personality traits.

Appearance — No specific physical description is given in ancient sources for any of the Euchenors. They are depicted as mortal men of heroic or royal status.

Accounts I
The Slaying of the Sons of AegyptusPseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 2.1.5

Mythic (Bronze Age)

Euchenor, son of Aegyptus by an Arabian woman, is one of the fifty sons of Aegyptus who marry the fifty daughters of Danaus. On their wedding night, all but Lynceus are killed by their brides, who obey their father's command. Euchenor is killed by Iphimedousa. The Danaids are later punished in the Underworld; Lynceus and Hypermnestra become ancestors of the Argive royal line.

The Choice of EuchenorHomer, Iliad 13.660-672

Mythic (Trojan War)

Euchenor, son of the seer Polyeidos, knows from his father's prophecy that he will either die of a painful illness at home or fall in battle at Troy. He chooses to go to Troy and is killed by Paris, fulfilling the prophecy of death in battle. His death is mourned by his men; the story illustrates the theme of fate and choice.

Accounts II
The Sons of AeolusTzetzes, Allegories of the Odyssey

Mythic

Euchenor is one of the sons of Aeolus, keeper of the winds, who marries his sons and daughters to each other to preserve family harmony. This marriage arrangement ensures the unity of the family.

Father of EchetusUnknown (mentioned in summary)

Mythic

A third Euchenor is the father, by Phlogea, of Echetus, a king in mainland Greece. No further details are known. None recorded.
