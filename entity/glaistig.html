Who It Is

In the mist-locked glens of the Scottish Highlands, where the boundary between the human world and the realm of the fae blurs with the twilight, there wanders a figure both beautiful and terrible: the glaistig. Sometimes a green-clad woman of haunting grace, sometimes a goat-legged guardian of herds, she embodies the wild duality of the land itself—nurturing and vengeful, protector and predator, a spirit who has haunted the Celtic imagination for centuries.

The glaistig is dual-natured. She is a protector of cattle and herds, and may assist farmers who treat her with respect. She is also known to lure travelers, especially men, to their deaths, either by drowning them in streams or by leading them astray. She is capricious and can be vengeful if insulted or if her boundaries are crossed.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'glaistig' is derived from Scottish Gaelic, likely from 'glas' meaning 'green' or 'grey', possibly referencing her green attire or her association with water and cattle. The term may also be related to 'glaise' meaning 'stream' or 'burn'.

Pronunciation — GLAS-tik (with a hard 'g' and 't' as in 'stick')

Aliases — The Green Lady, Maighdean Uaine, The Green Glaistig

Ownership — Scottish Highland folklore, particularly Gaelic-speaking regions; also present in Irish tradition as a similar water spirit.

Powers
Shape-shifting

The glaistig can appear as a beautiful woman or an old hag, and may transform her lower body to hide her goat legs.

Limit: She may be forced to reveal her true form if her dress is lifted or if she is addressed directly.

Enchantment and allure

She can charm men with her beauty, leading them to follow her into dangerous places.

Limit: Men who are cautious or who carry iron may be resistant.

Protection of cattle

She ensures the health and fertility of herds, and may punish those who harm them.

Limit: Her protection is conditional on respect and offerings.

Cursing

She can curse individuals or families, causing illness, misfortune, or death.

Limit: Curses may be lifted if she is appeased.

Water manipulation

She can cause streams to flood or to be calm, and may drown victims.

Limit: She is bound to her particular stream or glen.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The glaistig is typically described as a beautiful woman dressed in a green gown, often with long flowing hair. However, her lower body may be that of a goat, or she may have goat's hooves or horns. In some accounts, she appears as an old hag, but can transform into a young woman. Her eyes are sometimes described as wild or glowing.

Symbols — Green clothing, goat attributes, association with streams and cattle, sometimes depicted with a comb or mirror (as with other fairy women).

Domains — Highland glens, streams, waterfalls, and areas near cattle pastures. She is particularly associated with the region of Argyll and the Hebrides.

Accounts I
The Glaistig of the CattleOral tradition, recorded in various folklore collections

Unknown (pre-19th century)

A farmer leaves his cattle in a glen, and a green-clad woman appears to watch over them. She protects them from wolves and thieves, and the farmer's herd thrives. In gratitude, the farmer leaves offerings of milk and bread. However, one day he spies on her and sees her goat legs; she curses him and his cattle die. The farmer loses his livelihood and learns to respect the fae's privacy.

Accounts II
The Glaistig's LureOral tradition, recorded in Katharine Briggs' encyclopedia

Unknown

A young man walking home at night meets a beautiful woman in green who invites him to her home. He follows her into a ravine, but at the last moment he recites a prayer or makes the sign of the cross, and she vanishes, revealing a dangerous drop. The man escapes, and the glaistig is seen no more in that area.

The Glaistig and the MidwifeOral tradition, recorded in various collections

Unknown

A glaistig asks a human midwife to assist her in childbirth. The midwife agrees and is rewarded with the gift of second sight or a magical ointment. However, when the midwife tells others of her experience, the glaistig appears and blinds her in one eye. The midwife gains a supernatural gift but loses an eye.
