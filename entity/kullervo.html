Who It Is

Kullervo is the most tragic figure in the Kalevala — a hero born under a curse, whose life is one unbroken chain of suffering, violence, and revenge. His story (Runes 31–36 of the Kalevala) is one of the darkest and most psychologically complex episodes in any epic tradition. J.R.R. Tolkien acknowledged Kullervo as a primary inspiration for Túrin Turambar in The Silmarillion — another doomed hero whose life is an instrument of fate's cruelty.

Kullervo's tragedy begins before his birth. His father, Kalervo, was murdered by his brother Untamo, and Kalervo's entire clan was slaughtered — except for Kullervo's pregnant mother, who was taken captive. When Kullervo is born, Untamo tries three times to kill him: drowning him (he floats), burning him (he sits in the flames and rakes the ashes), hanging him (the rope breaks). Failing to kill the child, Untamo puts him to work as a slave, but Kullervo destroys everything he touches. He is sold to Ilmarinen — a disastrous transaction for all involved.

Given to Ilmarinen's wife (the Maiden of Pohjola), Kullervo is sent to herd cattle. His mistress, mocking him, bakes a stone into his bread. When Kullervo's knife — the only inheritance from his father — shatters on the stone, he flies into a rage of supernatural power. He summons bears and wolves from the forest and transforms them into cows. When his mistress goes to milk them, the beasts tear her apart. Kullervo flees into the wilderness, an outcast and murderer.

Believing his entire family dead, Kullervo wanders until he discovers that his parents and siblings survived the massacre and live in hiding on the borderlands of Lapland. Reunited, he attempts to live a normal life, but his curse follows him. On a journey home, he meets a girl gathering berries. He forces her into his sleigh and seduces or rapes her. Only afterward do they discover they are brother and sister — his long-lost sister, who believed he had died in infancy. Overcome with horror, she throws herself into a river. Kullervo returns home and confesses to his mother, who — in the deepest gesture of maternal love in the epic — tells him it does not matter, she still loves him, he can go far away and find peace.

But Kullervo cannot accept peace. He wages a one-man war against Untamo's clan, slaughtering every man, woman, and child — exacting total vengeance. When he returns home to find his own family dead (they died of grief in his absence), he places his sword in the ground, asks it if it will drink guilty blood, and the sword answers yes. Kullervo throws himself upon the blade. The Kalevala moralizes: 'Never bring a child up crookedly, never rock him with a foolish rocking; a child reared without wisdom, though he might have understanding, cannot find his right way of living.'
