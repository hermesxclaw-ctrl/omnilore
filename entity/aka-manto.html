Who It Is

In the hushed silence of a school bathroom after dark, a figure in a flowing red cloak may step from the shadows. He is masked, handsome beneath his disguise, and he asks a simple question: red or blue? But this is no game—every answer leads to a gruesome end. The only escape is to refuse, to run, or to ignore him entirely. This is Aka Manto, the Red Cloak, a spirit of the toilet who has haunted Japanese schoolyards for nearly a century.

Aka Manto is a malevolent spirit who appears to individuals alone in public or school bathrooms, often at night. He engages his victims with a deceptive choice, offering red or blue toilet paper (or cloak/cape). He is patient and cunning, but can be thwarted by refusal or escape. He may also play tricks, such as making toilet paper vanish.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Aka (赤) means 'red' in Japanese; Manto (マント) is the Japanese loanword for 'cloak' or 'cape.' Historically, 'manto' referred to a sleeveless kimono-style jacket, leading to variations in appearance across generations.

Pronunciation — AH-kah MAHN-toh

Aliases — Red Cloak, Red Cape, Red Vest, Akai-Kami-Aoi-Kami, Red Paper, Blue Paper, Aoi Manto

Ownership — Japanese urban legend, originating in schoolyard rumors and transmitted through oral tradition and modern media.

Supernatural Appearance

Aka Manto can appear or manifest in bathrooms, seemingly from nowhere.

Limit: None specified

Choice Manipulation

He presents victims with a fatal choice (red or blue). Choosing either leads to death, unless the victim refuses or flees.

Limit: Can be avoided by not choosing or by ignoring him.

Laceration

If red is chosen, the victim is stabbed or flayed, resulting in a blood-soaked corpse.

Limit: Only occurs if red is chosen.

Strangulation or Blood Drain

If blue is chosen, the victim is strangled or has all blood drained from their body.

Limit: Only occurs if blue is chosen.

Dragging to Hell

If the victim asks for a different color (e.g., yellow), they are dragged to the underworld.

Limit: Only occurs if an alternative color is requested.

Toilet Paper Vanishing

If the victim brings their own toilet paper, it disappears, forcing them to rely on Aka Manto's offer.

Limit: None

Hairy Arm Fondling

In the kainade version, a hairy arm of the chosen color rises from the toilet to fondle the victim.

Limit: Only in that variant

Form & Signs

Appearance — Aka Manto is typically described as a male figure wearing a flowing red cloak or cape and a mask that conceals his face. Beneath the mask, he is sometimes said to be handsome and charming. In some versions, he appears as a tall man with a sickly, bluish-white face. The historical ambiguity of 'manto' has led to descriptions of him wearing a sleeveless jacket in older accounts.

Symbols — The red cloak is the primary symbol, representing blood and danger. The mask adds an element of mystery and hidden identity. The toilet setting is itself symbolic of filth and vulnerability.

Domains — Public and school bathrooms, particularly female restrooms. He is said to haunt the furthest stall or the wall at the end of the bathroom.

The Red and Blue ChoiceSchoolyard rumor, transmitted orally and in modern compilations

Since at least the 1930s

A person is using a public or school bathroom when Aka Manto appears and asks whether they want red or blue toilet paper. Choosing red results in being stabbed or flayed; choosing blue results in strangulation or blood drainage. The only escape is to refuse or flee. Death for the victim if a choice is made; survival if they refuse or run.
