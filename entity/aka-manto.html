The Approach

In the hushed silence of a school bathroom after dark, a figure in a flowing red cloak may step from the shadows. He is masked, handsome beneath his disguise, and he asks a simple question: red or blue? But this is no game—every answer leads to a gruesome end. The only escape is to refuse, to run, or to ignore him entirely. This is Aka Manto, the Red Cloak, a spirit of the toilet who has haunted Japanese schoolyards for nearly a century.

Content advisory. Contains themes of violence, murder, and supernatural horror, including descriptions of lacerations, strangulation, blood drainage, and drowning. Some versions involve sexualized fondling. Not suitable for young children.

Etymology

Aka (赤) means 'red' in Japanese; Manto (マント) is the Japanese loanword for 'cloak' or 'cape.' Historically, 'manto' referred to a sleeveless kimono-style jacket, leading to variations in appearance across generations.

Cultural Ownership

Japanese urban legend, originating in schoolyard rumors and transmitted through oral tradition and modern media.

Disputed Identifications

Some versions identify Aka Manto as a yōkai called kainade, a hairy creature that lives in toilets. Others describe him as a ghost or serial killer. The exact nature is debated among folklorists.

Description

Appearance

Aka Manto is typically described as a male figure wearing a flowing red cloak or cape and a mask that conceals his face. Beneath the mask, he is sometimes said to be handsome and charming. In some versions, he appears as a tall man with a sickly, bluish-white face. The historical ambiguity of 'manto' has led to descriptions of him wearing a sleeveless jacket in older accounts.

Symbols & Iconography

The red cloak is the primary symbol, representing blood and danger. The mask adds an element of mystery and hidden identity. The toilet setting is itself symbolic of filth and vulnerability.

Behaviour & Personality

Aka Manto is a malevolent spirit who appears to individuals alone in public or school bathrooms, often at night. He engages his victims with a deceptive choice, offering red or blue toilet paper (or cloak/cape). He is patient and cunning, but can be thwarted by refusal or escape. He may also play tricks, such as making toilet paper vanish.

Domains & Habitats

Public and school bathrooms, particularly female restrooms. He is said to haunt the furthest stall or the wall at the end of the bathroom.

Version Differences

Variations include the color choices (red/blue, red/white), the nature of the spirit (ghost, yōkai, serial killer), the method of killing (stabbing, flaying, strangulation, blood drainage, drowning), and the entity's appearance (masked, handsome, hairy). Some versions involve a hairy arm rising from the toilet.

Stories & Myths

The Red and Blue Choice

Source Schoolyard rumor, transmitted orally and in modern compilationsCulture JapanesePeriod Since at least the 1930s

Summary

A person is using a public or school bathroom when Aka Manto appears and asks whether they want red or blue toilet paper. Choosing red results in being stabbed or flayed; choosing blue results in strangulation or blood drainage. The only escape is to refuse or flee.

Participants

Aka Manto

Unnamed victim

Consequences

Death for the victim if a choice is made; survival if they refuse or run.

Variants

Choices may be red/blue cloaks or capes, or red/white paper. Some versions involve a hairy arm fondling the victim.
