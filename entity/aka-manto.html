You're in the stall when you hear it -- a voice from nowhere. 'Red paper... or blue paper?' There is no right answer. There is only the color of your death.

Who It Is

Aka Manto ('Red Cape') is a sinister Japanese urban legend associated with public and school bathrooms. The spirit is described as a male apparition wearing a flowing red cape and a mask that conceals his face. He appears to an occupant of the last toilet stall (or sometimes any stall), who has unwittingly chosen his domain. When the victim is trapped, Aka Manto asks a question -- typically: 'Do you want red paper or blue paper?' Choosing red paper results in the victim being flayed alive or drenched in their own blood; choosing blue paper results in strangulation or having all blood drained from the body. If the victim says any other color, hands reach up from beneath the toilet and drag them to hell. In some variants, Aoi Manto ('Blue Cape') also exists, and choosing blue involves a different horrible death. There is no correct answer that leads to survival -- the legend is designed to be inescapable. The story's origins are uncertain, but it gained widespread popularity in Japanese schools by the 1930s and persists to the present day. Some researchers connect Aka Manto to the Japanese concept of the 'aka' (red) as a color associated with the supernatural and danger (red is the color of the underworld in Japanese folklore), while other versions suggest Aka Manto was a handsome but sexually predatory man who was killed in a bathroom and now returns as a vengeful spirit. The legend serves as a warning about stranger danger, the vulnerability of being alone in a bathroom, and the anxiety of being trapped with no escape. Aka Manto has been featured in films, manga, and horror games.
