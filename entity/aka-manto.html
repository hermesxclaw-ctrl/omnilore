You're in the stall when you hear it -- a voice from nowhere. 'Red paper... or blue paper?' There is no right answer. There is only the color of your death.

Who It Is

Aka Manto ('Red Cape') is a sinister Japanese urban legend associated with public and school bathrooms. The spirit is described as a male apparition wearing a flowing red cape and a mask that conceals his face. He appears to an occupant of the last toilet stall (or sometimes any stall), who has unwittingly chosen his domain. When the victim is trapped, Aka Manto asks a question -- typically: 'Do you want red paper or blue paper?' Choosing red paper results in the victim being flayed alive or drenched in their own blood; choosing blue paper results in strangulation or having all blood drained from the body. If the victim says any other color, hands reach up from beneath the toilet and drag them to hell. In some variants, Aoi Manto ('Blue Cape') also exists, and choosing blue involves a different horrible death. There is no correct answer that leads to survival -- the legend is designed to be inescapable. The story's origins are uncertain, but it gained widespread popularity in Japanese schools by the 1930s and persists to the present day. Some researchers connect Aka Manto to the Japanese concept of the 'aka' (red) as a color associated with the supernatural and danger (red is the color of the underworld in Japanese folklore), while other versions suggest Aka Manto was a handsome but sexually predatory man who was killed in a bathroom and now returns as a vengeful spirit. The legend serves as a warning about stranger danger, the vulnerability of being alone in a bathroom, and the anxiety of being trapped with no escape. Aka Manto has been featured in films, manga, and horror games.

Form

Aka Manto is described as a figure wearing a flowing red cape or cloak that completely conceals its body and often its face. When visible, the face is described as pale or obscured in shadow. Some accounts describe it as appearing like a person in a red coat or a figure with a red hood, while others describe a more ghostly or demonic appearance. The cape is always described as being a vivid, blood-red color. The entity typically appears in the last stall of a school or public bathroom, often in the girls' bathroom, though some accounts place it in the boys' bathroom or gender-neutral restrooms.

Powers

Paper Choice Trap Aka Manto presents a binary choice (red paper or blue paper) to its victim. The choice is a trap — both options lead to death. Red paper results in being slashed or cut to death, blue paper results in being strangled or hanged. Core legend, all versions The entity must ask the question; it cannot attack without first presenting the choice. The victim's choice determines the method of death. Some variants include additional paper colors with different death methods, but the core red/blue dichotomy is consistent.

Pursuit and Drag If the victim refuses to choose, attempts to leave, or runs away, Aka Manto will pursue them and drag them into the afterlife or kill them in a more gruesome manner. The entity is described as being capable of pulling victims into the toilet or into a dark void. Core legend, most versions The entity's pursuit appears limited to the bathroom or its immediate vicinity in most accounts. Some variants describe the entity as capable of appearing anywhere in the school if the victim flees, while others maintain it stays in the bathroom.

Concealment and Disguise Aka Manto's face is typically obscured or shadowed, and its body is fully concealed by the red cape. It can appear and disappear without sound or warning. Core legend The entity is always described as wearing the red cape; it cannot disguise itself as a normal person. None documented.

Behavior & Habitat

Aka Manto's behavior follows a strict, ritualistic pattern: it waits in a stall and asks the occupant a single, binary question — 'Do you want red paper or blue paper?' The outcome depends on the choice: Red paper means being slashed or cut to death. Blue paper means being strangled or hanged. Choosing neither, attempting to leave, or running away results in being dragged into the afterlife or killed in a more gruesome manner. Some variants include additional choices (e.g., yellow paper for being drowned, white paper for being impaled), but the core red/blue dichotomy remains constant. The entity is never described as leaving the bathroom or pursuing victims outside of it.

Accounts

A student enters the last stall of the school bathroom. After a moment, a voice from the adjacent stall (or from above) asks, 'Do you want red paper or blue paper?' The student, confused, answers 'Red.' The voice responds, 'Then you'll die by blood,' and the student is found slashed to death. If the student answers 'Blue,' they are found strangled. If the student ignores the voice or flees, they are found dead with a look of terror, having been dragged into the toilet or the afterlife.

A student stays after school for club activities and needs to use the bathroom. As they enter the last stall, they see a figure in a red cape standing in the corner. The figure asks the paper question. The student, terrified, tries to run, but the figure grabs them and drags them into a dark void that opens in the floor. The student is found the next morning, dead, with a look of extreme terror.

Field Notes

⍟ Aka Manto is often listed among the 'top 10 scariest Japanese urban legends' on English-language folklore sites.

⍟ The legend's binary choice (red/blue) is a classic example of a 'no-win scenario' in folklore, similar to Kuchisake-onna's 'Am I beautiful?' question.

⍟ Some Japanese schools reportedly posted signs in bathrooms warning students about Aka Manto, though this is likely an urban legend itself.

⍟ The red/blue paper choice has been parodied in Japanese comedy sketches and internet memes.

⍟ Aka Manto's design — a figure in a red cape — has been compared to classic Western horror figures like the Grim Reaper, but with a uniquely Japanese twist.

A way to hold it in your mind

Aka Manto is like a deadly automated teller machine: you walk in, you get a choice, and no matter what you pick, you lose. It's a horror story built on the unbearable tension of a forced decision, a trap with no escape. It's the nightmare version of 'Would you rather?' played with your life.
