Who They Are

Khors is the second sun of the Kiev pantheon and the great etymological mystery of Slavic religion: Vladimir's idols of 980 included both Dazhbog and Khors — two solar gods on one hill — and a millennium of scholarship has argued about why. The leading answer is written in his name: Khors appears to derive from Iranian (Persian khorshid, the sun), imported through the Sarmatian and Alan steppe peoples the Rus traded and fought and married among — meaning Kiev's pantheon practiced solar diversity, honoring the native sun (Dazhbog, the giver) alongside the steppe's sun (Khors, the radiant disc itself). His unforgettable moment comes, again, from the Tale of Igor's Campaign: Prince Vseslav the sorcerer-prince, who ran as a wolf by night, raced from Kiev to reach Tmutorokan before cockcrow — and crossed the path of great Khors as a wolf: he outran the sun's own road, werewolf-fast, the one recorded footrace between a prince and a god. That single image — the wolf-prince crossing the solar track in the dark hours — is most of what survives of Khors's actual mythology, and it suggests his domain was specifically the sun's course, the road the disc travels above and below the earth. The rest is inference and absence: no folk tales, no offerings recorded, just a name in the idol-list, a line in the epic, and the tantalizing certainty that Kiev thought one sun-god was not enough.

Origins

Attested in the Primary Chronicle idol-list and the Igor epic; the Iranian etymology, if correct, makes him the clearest documented case of steppe religious import into Slavic paganism — a god carried up the trade roads.

Attested in Primary Chronicle; Tale of Igor's CampaignEra Pre-Christian Slavic Europe

Form

Unrecorded — reconstructed as the radiant disc itself or a white-horsed rider on the sun's road; his best portrait remains negative space: the bright track a wolf crossed at night.

Powers

Embodiment of the solar disc and mastery of its daily road, including the hidden night-segment under the world — the route by which Vseslav's crossing was even possible.

Weaknesses & Wards

Obscurity itself: outrun once by a werewolf prince in the only story he appears in, and outlasted by nothing — his cult vanished so completely that his job description is a scholarly reconstruction.

Cultural Significance

Khors is Slavic paganism's exhibit A for steppe-Iranian influence, and the Vseslav passage is a foundational text for Slavic werewolf lore — the sun-god remembered chiefly for the night something crossed his path.
