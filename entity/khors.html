Khors is the second sun of the Kiev pantheon and the great etymological mystery of Slavic religion: Vladimir's idols of 980 included both Dazhbog and Khors — two solar gods on one hill — and a millennium of scholarship has argued about why. The leading answer is written in his name: Khors appears to derive from Iranian (Persian khorshid, the sun), imported through the Sarmatian and Alan steppe peoples the Rus traded and fought and married among — meaning Kiev's pantheon practiced solar diversity, honoring the native sun (Dazhbog, the giver) alongside the steppe's sun (Khors, the radiant disc itself).

Who They Are

Khors is the second sun of the Kiev pantheon and the great etymological mystery of Slavic religion: Vladimir's idols of 980 included both Dazhbog and Khors — two solar gods on one hill — and a millennium of scholarship has argued about why. The leading answer is written in his name: Khors appears to derive from Iranian (Persian khorshid, the sun), imported through the Sarmatian and Alan steppe peoples the Rus traded and fought and married among — meaning Kiev's pantheon practiced solar diversity, honoring the native sun (Dazhbog, the giver) alongside the steppe's sun (Khors, the radiant disc itself). His unforgettable moment comes, again, from the Tale of Igor's Campaign: Prince Vseslav the sorcerer-prince, who ran as a wolf by night, raced from Kiev to reach Tmutorokan before cockcrow — and crossed the path of great Khors as a wolf: he outran the sun's own road, werewolf-fast, the one recorded footrace between a prince and a god. That single image — the wolf-prince crossing the solar track in the dark hours — is most of what survives of Khors's actual mythology, and it suggests his domain was specifically the sun's course, the road the disc travels above and below the earth. The rest is inference and absence: no folk tales, no offerings recorded, just a name in the idol-list, a line in the epic, and the tantalizing certainty that Kiev thought one sun-god was not enough.

Origins

Attested in the Primary Chronicle idol-list and the Igor epic; the Iranian etymology, if correct, makes him the clearest documented case of steppe religious import into Slavic paganism — a god carried up the trade roads.

The Approach

In the shadowy recesses of Slavic mythology, Khors emerges as a figure of luminous paradox—a deity entwined with the sun's radiant embrace yet whispering secrets of the night. As the enigmatic radiance of Kiev's pantheon, he beckons the curious to unravel the mysteries of his celestial journey.

Etymology

The theonym Khors is often linked to the Iranian word for sun, though this connection is contentious among modern linguists. Alternative native Slavic etymologies have been proposed but remain largely speculative.

Cultural Ownership

Khors is primarily associated with the medieval Slavic pantheon, particularly in the context of Kievan Rus.

Disputed Identifications

There are debates about whether Khors is a sun god, a moon god, or represents both celestial bodies. His role in the pantheon alongside Dazhbog, another solar deity, raises questions about solar plurality in Slavic mythology.

Description

Appearance

Khors is often depicted as a radiant figure or a sun disk, sometimes with wolf-like features, reflecting his association with both the sun and the night. The imagery varies across different sources.

Symbols & Iconography

Common symbols include the sun disk, rays of light emanating from his figure, and occasionally wolf motifs, which suggest a duality in his nature.

Behaviour & Personality

Khors embodies characteristics of brightness, warmth, and life-giving energy. He is often portrayed as a neutral force, neither benevolent nor malevolent, representing the cyclical nature of day and night.

Domains & Habitats

Primarily associated with the sky and celestial realms, Khors is believed to traverse both day and night, symbolizing the solar cycle.

Version Differences

While Khors is consistently portrayed as a sun god in various texts, interpretations of his role vary. Some sources suggest a dual aspect, linking him to lunar qualities or nighttime domains.
