Who It Is

He has heard. From a desperate mother's cry in Genesis to a smartphone in your pocket, the name Simon has been listening for nearly three thousand years — prophet, apostle, magician, chipmunk, and cat.

Attested personalities diverge sharply by bearer. Genesis Simeon: fierce, vengeful loyalty to sister Dinah (Gen 34), later bound as hostage in Egypt (Gen 42:24), cursed by Jacob for anger. Simon Peter in Gospels: impulsive, outspoken, loyal yet fearful — first to confess Jesus as Messiah and first to deny him, rebuked as 'Satan' then restored as shepherd (John 21). Acts portrays him as bold preacher and miracle worker. Simon Magus: archetypal false prophet/magician seeking to purchase spiritual power (origin of 'simony'), depicted as prideful charlatan in orthodox source

Powers
Apostolic Authority and Keys of the Kingdom (Simon Peter)

In Matthew 16:18-19 Jesus renames Simon as Peter (Petros/Cephas, 'rock') and gives him 'keys of the kingdom of heaven' with power to bind and loose. Interpreted as ecclesiastical primacy, power to forgive/retain, and foundation of the Church. Acts attributes to Peter healing (shadow heals sick, Acts 5:15), raising Tabitha (Acts 9:40), and Pentecost preaching.

Limit: Denied Jesus three times; rebuked and called Satan (Mark 8:33); imprisoned and required angelic rescue (Acts 12); martyred (tradition: crucified inverted under Nero, c. 64 CE). Power is delegated, not innate, and contingent on faith.

Magical Arts and Flight (Simon Magus)

Acts 8:9-11 says Simon 'amazed the people of Samaria' with magic, called 'Great Power of God.' Later apocrypha expands to levitation, transformations, creating a boy from air, and attempting to fly over Rome before Peter's prayer brings him down.

Limit: Fails against Peter; offer to buy Holy Spirit rejected ('simony' condemned); flight ends in fall, broken legs, death in some versions. Justin Martyr claims he was honored as god in Rome, but no archaeological confirmation.

Tribal Martial Prowess (Simeon son of Jacob)

Genesis 34:25-26: Simeon and Levi take swords and kill all males of Shechem to avenge Dinah. Genesis 49:5-7 describes their anger as fierce and weapons as instruments of cruelty. Tribe of Simeon later noted as warriors (1 Chronicles 4:39-43).

Limit: Jacob curses their anger and scatters them in Israel; Simeon's tribal allotment is absorbed within Judah and later disappears as distinct entity. Simeon is taken hostage by Joseph (Gen 42:24).

Mnemonic and Pattern Mastery (Simon game and Simon cipher as cultural extensions)

The Milton Bradley Simon game (1978) tests and trains sequential memory: device plays increasingly long light/sound sequences player must repeat. The NSA-designed Simon lightweight block cipher (2013) provides efficient encryption on constrained hardware. Both borrow the name for memorability, not continuity with biblical figure.

Limit: Game limited to 4 tones and human working memory; cipher limited to specific block/key sizes (32-128 bit) and subject to cryptanalytic advances. No supernatural ability.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a name, Simon has no single visual form; as borne by figures, iconography is highly version-specific. Simeon son of Jacob is rarely depicted except in medieval Jacob's sons cycles as a bearded Israelite tribesman. Simon Peter is one of the most depicted saints in Western art: older, bearded, often balding or with short curly grey hair, holding one or two keys (Matt 16:19), sometimes a fish, boat, or inverted cross (martyrdom). Accounts I
The Birth and Naming of Šimʿōn (Simeon son of Jacob and Leah)Hebrew Bible, Genesis 29:33

Iron Age traditions, codified 6th-5th c. BCE

Leah, unloved wife of Jacob, bears a second son after Reuben. She declares 'Because YHWH has heard that I am hated, he has given me this son also' and names him Šimʿōn ('He has heard'). He becomes second of twelve sons who become the Twelve Tribes of Israel. His birth establishes the etymology that will travel for millennia. Founding of Tribe of Simeon; etiology for name; sets narrative arc for Simeon's later violence and dispersal.

Simeon and Levi Avenge Dinah at ShechemGenesis 34; Jubilees 30; Judith 9:2-4; Testament of Simeon

Iron Age traditions; Second Temple elaborations

When Shechem son of Hamor rapes Dinah, daughter of Jacob/Leah, he offers bride-price. Jacob's sons deceive Shechemites into circumcising themselves, then Simeon and Levi, Dinah's full brothers, enter the city while men are in pain and slaughter every male, plunder the city, and rescue Dinah. Jacob rebukes them for making him odious; on his deathbed he curses their anger: 'I will divide them in Jacob and scatter them in Israel' (Gen 49:5-7). Explains Simeon's scattered inheritance within Judah and Levites' landless priestly status; becomes moral test case for zeal vs. cruelty in Jewish and Christian ethics.

Simon Called to Be Peter, Fisher of MenMark 1:16-18, 3:16; Matthew 4:18-20, 16:13-20; Luke 5:1-11; John 1:40-42

c. 30 CE events, narrated c. 65-90 CE

Simon son of Jonah (or John), fisherman from Bethsaida/Capernaum, brother of Andrew, is working nets on Sea of Galilee when Jesus calls 'Follow me and I will make you fishers of men.' He immediately leaves nets. Jesus renames him Cephas/Petros (Rock). Simon becomes leader of the Twelve, first to confess Jesus as Messiah at Caesarea Philippi, witnesses Transfiguration, walks on water briefly (Matt 14:28-31), and is told he will be rock on which the Church is built. Foundation of Petrine primacy, papal succession claims, and Christian use of Simon/Peter as baptismal name; Peter's name appears ~150 times in NT, most of any apostle.

Accounts II
Denial, Restoration, and Martyrdom of Simon PeterMark 14:66-72, John 18:15-27, John 21:15-19; Acts 12; 1 Clement 5; Eusebius Ecclesiastical History 2.25, 3.1

c. 30 CE (denial) and c. 64 CE (martyrdom tradition)

At Jesus' trial, Simon Peter follows at distance, then three times denies knowing Jesus before cock crows, weeping bitterly. After resurrection, Jesus restores him with threefold 'Do you love me? Feed my sheep' (John 21). Acts shows Peter leading Jerusalem church, imprisoned by Herod Agrippa I, freed by angel. Early tradition (1 Clement, Tertullian, Eusebius) holds he went to Rome and was crucified upside-down under Nero, requesting inverted position as unworthy to die like Jesus. Model of repentance and pastoral office; inverted cross becomes Petrine symbol; burial under St. Peter's Basilica becomes pilgrimage center; feast June 29 (Peter and Paul).

Simon Magus Tries to Buy the Holy SpiritActs 8:9-24; Justin Martyr Apology 1.26,26; Irenaeus Adversus Haereses 1.23; Acts of Peter 4-32

c. 35 CE event, narrated c. 80-90 CE; expanded 2nd-3rd c.

In Samaria, Simon, a magician who amazed people claiming to be 'Great Power,' believes Philip's preaching and is baptized. When Peter and John lay hands to impart Holy Spirit, Simon offers money: 'Give me also this power.' Peter rebukes: 'Your money perish with you... you have no part in this matter... repent.' Simon asks Peter to pray for him. Later heresiologists make him father of all heresies, founder of Gnosticism, who goes to Rome, performs wonders, and contests Peter before Emperor Claudius/Nero, attempting to fly and falling to his death when Peter prays. Coinage of term 'simony' (buying/selling ecclesiastical office); archetype of false prophet and magician vs apostle; extensive anti-gnostic polemic.

Simon of Cyrene Carries the CrossMark 15:21, Matthew 27:32, Luke 23:26; Gospel of Nicodemus

c. 30 CE event

As Jesus is led to Golgotha, Roman soldiers seize Simon from Cyrene (in modern Libya), father of Alexander and Rufus, coming in from the country/field, and compel him to carry the cross behind Jesus. Mark's naming of sons suggests audience knew them. Later tradition makes Simon a convert and his sons missionaries. Powerful image of compelled participation in Passion; adopted in African and African-diaspora Christianity as Black presence at crucifixion; Stations of the Cross (5th station); literary reworkings (e.g., Faulkner, Colm Tóibín).

Simon the Zealot, the Apostle Sent to the Ends of the EarthLuke 6:15, Acts 1:13 (lists); Eusebius, Jerome, Golden Legend; Ethiopian Synaxarium

1st c. apostolic lists; 4th-13th c. hagiographic expansions

Listed among Twelve as Simon the Zealot (Zelotes) or Simon the Canaanite (Kananaios, Aramaic qan'ana 'zealous'). No deeds in NT. Later legends send him with Jude Thaddeus to Persia where both are martyred (sawn in half), or to Egypt, or to Britain where he is crucified. Western icon shows him with saw, Eastern with boat. Patron of zealots, curriers, tanners; feast Oct 28 (West) with Jude; illustrates how minimal canonical data generates maximal legendary expansion.

Simon is an English name meaning 'to hear' that originated in England. — Simon is Hebrew in origin (Šimʿōn), transmitted via Greek and Latin through Christianity; English adopted it via Norman French after 1066. Its Hebrew meaning 'he has heard' refers to God hearing, not the bearer hearing.

Simon Peter and Simon Magus were the same person, or Simon Magus was Peter's disciple. — They are distinct antagonists in Acts 8 and later apocrypha; Peter rebukes Magus. No canonical text identifies them as same.

Simon Says and the Simon game are named after Simon Peter or a person named Simon. — Simon Says is generic placeholder name for leader (like 'Jack says'); the electronic Simon game was named after the folk game, not a person. IBM Simon was code-named Angler, renamed for marketing simplicity.

The Simon Necronomicon is an ancient Sumerian text translated by someone named Simon. — It is a modern 1977 pastiche by anonymous authors (pseudonym Simon) blending Lovecraft's fiction with Mesopotamian mythology; no ancient manuscript exists. Scholars and Lovecraft estate confirm fabrication.

All Simons in the New Testament are the same individual. — New Testament names nine distinct Simons: Simon Peter, Simon the Zealot, Simon Magus, Simon of Cyrene, Simon the Leper (Mark 14:3), Simon the Pharisee (Luke 7:40), Simon brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3), Simon the tanner (Acts 9:43), Simon father of Judas Iscariot (John 6:71). Careful reading distinguishes them.

Simon (cipher) stands for an acronym. — NSA designers stated Simon and Speck were simply friendly names chosen for lightweight ciphers, not acronyms; no official expansion.
