Who It Is

In the stormy nights of the Basque Country, a young red bull emerges from his cave, a spirit of justice and warning. This is Aatxe, the 'young bull' who punishes the wicked and protects the innocent.

Aatxe is a nocturnal spirit, emerging at night, especially during storms. He attacks criminals and malevolent people, and protects people by warning them to stay home when danger is near.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Aatxe typically appears as a young red bull, but is a shapeshifter and can take the form of a cow, calf, or a man. He is often described as dark or shadowy.

Symbols — As a bull, he symbolizes strength and virility. Red color may be associated with danger or the underworld. Cave-dwelling connects him to the earth and the underworld.

Domains — He inhabits caves and hollows in the Basque Country. Specific caves associated with him include Sagastigorri and Covairada, as well as Isturits, Sare, and Errenteria.

Accounts I
The Bull of the CaveBasque oral tradition

Unknown; pre-Christian origins

Aatxe emerges from his cave at night, especially during storms, to punish wrongdoers and warn people of danger. Villagers stay indoors at night to avoid danger; criminals are attacked.

Aatxe and MariScholarly interpretation

20th century scholarship

Aatxe is theorized to be a representative or enforcer of the goddess Mari, punishing those who cheat her. Reinforces the moral authority of Mari.

The Red Bull of SagastigorriRegional Basque folklore

Unknown

Aatxe is associated with the cave of Sagastigorri, where he is said to reside and from which he emerges to roam the countryside. People avoid the cave at night.

The ShapeshifterBasque folklore

Unknown

Aatxe changes his form from bull to cow, calf, or man, often to approach humans unnoticed. Humans are wary of strange animals or strangers at night.

The Storm WarningBasque oral tradition

Unknown

Aatxe appears during storms to warn people of impending danger, causing them to stay home. Villagers take shelter and avoid accidents.
