Accounts I
Pontoppidan's Fishermen and the Floating Island (Kraken) - CONTEXTErik Pontoppidan, Det første Forsøg paa Norges Naturlige Historie (1752-53), English translation The Natural History of Norway (1755)

1752-1753 (reporting older oral tradition)

Fishermen report a massive creature rising like an island, with many horns/masts (tentacles). They row over it thinking it is land, then flee as it submerges creating a dangerous whirlpool. Pontoppidan collects multiple testimonies, attempts to classify it as a giant polyp or starfish, and warns sailors to avoid its grounds. This is the foundational Kraken narrative; no character named Isaak appears. Established Kraken in European natural history and cartography; influenced Linnaeus, Tennyson, Verne, and all later fiction. No known consequence for Kraken Isaak.

Tennyson's 'The Kraken' - The Deep Sleeper - CONTEXTAlfred Tennyson, Poems, Chiefly Lyrical (1830)

1830

A 15-line sonnet describes the Kraken sleeping for ages in the abyssal sea, battened by giant sponges, lit by phosphorescence, until the latter fire heats the deep and it will rise and die on the surface. No plot or named human character; purely atmospheric. Cemented Kraken as symbol of primordial, apocalyptic deep time; heavily quoted in Lovecraftian and modern Kraken fiction.

Verne's Giant Poulpe Attack on the Nautilus - CONTEXTJules Verne, Vingt mille lieues sous les mers (1870)

1870

The submarine Nautilus is attacked by a school of giant poulpes (translated as squid/cuttlefish/kraken) in the Atlantic. Crew members are seized by tentacles; Conseil, Ned Land and Nemo fight with axes and harpoons. One sailor is killed, pulled into the beak. The creatures are described as 25 feet long with parrot beaks and blue blood. Popularized the tentacled Kraken as a tangible, killable animal rather than supernatural island; established visual template for film krakens.

Accounts II
Pirates of the Caribbean: The Kraken Serves Davy Jones - CONTEXTPirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man's Chest (2006), dir. Gore Verbinski, screenplay Ted Elliott & Terry Rossio

2006

The Kraken is summoned by Davy Jones via the Black Spot to drag marked sailors to the depths. It destroys the Edinburgh Trader and later the Black Pearl, dragging Jack Sparrow down. It is depicted as a colossal, scarred squid-like leviathan with prehensile tentacles that can board ships. Killed off-screen between films by Lord Cutler Beckett's order. Definitive modern visual Kraken for global audiences; revived Kraken as franchise boss monster in games and theme parks.

One Piece: Surume the Kraken and Wadatsumi - CONTEXTEiichiro Oda, One Piece manga Chapter 604-605, anime Episode 523 (2011)

2011

The Straw Hat Pirates encounter Surume (named Kraken in translation), a giant octopus-like sea monster tamed at Fish-Man Island, later allied with them. Wadatsumi is a giant pufferfish-man sometimes conflated. No character named Isaak appears in this arc; included to show modern heroic-legend reinterpretation where Kraken becomes ally rather than pure monster. Example of Kraken shifted from horror to heroic-legend ally, relevant to archetype tag but not verified for Isaak.
