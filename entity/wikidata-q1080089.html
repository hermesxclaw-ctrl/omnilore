Stories & Myths

Pontoppidan's Fishermen and the Floating Island (Kraken) - CONTEXT

Source Erik Pontoppidan, Det første Forsøg paa Norges Naturlige Historie (1752-53), English translation The Natural History of Norway (1755)Culture Norwegian / Danish-Norwegian natural historyPeriod 1752-1753 (reporting older oral tradition)

Summary

Fishermen report a massive creature rising like an island, with many horns/masts (tentacles). They row over it thinking it is land, then flee as it submerges creating a dangerous whirlpool. Pontoppidan collects multiple testimonies, attempts to classify it as a giant polyp or starfish, and warns sailors to avoid its grounds. This is the foundational Kraken narrative; no character named Isaak appears.

Participants

Kraken (unnamed creature)

Norwegian fishermen

Erik Pontoppidan (compiler)

Consequences

Established Kraken in European natural history and cartography; influenced Linnaeus, Tennyson, Verne, and all later fiction. No known consequence for Kraken Isaak.

Variants

Olaus Magnus Carta marina (1539) shows sea monsters off Norway that may be precursors; later retellings reduce size and identify as giant squid after Steenstrup (1857).

Tennyson's 'The Kraken' - The Deep Sleeper - CONTEXT

Source Alfred Tennyson, Poems, Chiefly Lyrical (1830)Culture British Romantic literaturePeriod 1830

Summary

A 15-line sonnet describes the Kraken sleeping for ages in the abyssal sea, battened by giant sponges, lit by phosphorescence, until the latter fire heats the deep and it will rise and die on the surface. No plot or named human character; purely atmospheric.

Participants

The Kraken

Consequences

Cemented Kraken as symbol of primordial, apocalyptic deep time; heavily quoted in Lovecraftian and modern Kraken fiction.

Variants

No variants with Isaak; poem often misattributed to later authors.

Verne's Giant Poulpe Attack on the Nautilus - CONTEXT

Source Jules Verne, Vingt mille lieues sous les mers (1870)Culture French scientific romancePeriod 1870

Summary

The submarine Nautilus is attacked by a school of giant poulpes (translated as squid/cuttlefish/kraken) in the Atlantic. Crew members are seized by tentacles; Conseil, Ned Land and Nemo fight with axes and harpoons. One sailor is killed, pulled into the beak. The creatures are described as 25 feet long with parrot beaks and blue blood.

Variants

Pirates of the Caribbean: The Kraken Serves Davy Jones - CONTEXT

Source Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man's Chest (2006), dir. Gore Verbinski, screenplay Ted Elliott & Terry RossioCulture American blockbuster film franchisePeriod 2006

It is depicted as a colossal, scarred squid-like leviathan with prehensile tentacles that can board ships. Killed off-screen between films by Lord Cutler Beckett's order.

Kraken archetype violence (context only)

Source Pontoppidan 1755 translation pp. 348-350; Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man's Chest (2006)

Description

Historical Kraken accounts describe sailors being dragged to the depths, ships crushed, and crews drowned. Pontoppidan notes fishermen's fear of being pulled under by the whirlpool. Modern films depict graphic tentacle impalement and drowning. This is archetype context, NOT attributed to Isaak.
