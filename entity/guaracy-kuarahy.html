Who It Is

In the Guarani cosmos, Guaracy — the Sun — is not merely a celestial body but a living divine being whose journey across the sky shapes the rhythm of life, death, and renewal. Known also as Kuarahy, this solar deity embodies the warmth that nurtures the earth and the fierce gaze that oversees the moral order of the world.

Guaracy is often described as benevolent and generous, providing warmth and light for crops and life. However, he can also be stern and punitive, punishing those who violate moral laws or disrespect the natural order. He is a guardian of justice and a witness to all deeds.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Guaracy derives from the Tupi-Guarani word 'kuarasy' or 'kuarachy', meaning 'sun'. The root 'kua' relates to 'to see' or 'to know', reflecting the sun's role as the all-seeing eye of the sky.

Pronunciation — gwa-RAH-see (approximate; Guarani pronunciation may vary regionally)

Aliases — Kuarahy, Guaraci, Kuaracy

Ownership — Guarani people of South America, primarily in Paraguay, Brazil, Argentina, and Bolivia. The deity is also recognized in Tupi-Guarani traditions more broadly.

Powers
Solar Radiance

Guaracy emits intense light and heat that sustains life on earth, promoting growth and fertility.

Limit: His power diminishes at night when he travels through the underworld.

All-Seeing Eye

From his position in the sky, Guaracy observes all human actions, serving as a moral witness and enforcer.

Limit: He cannot see into the underworld or darkened places.

Fire Control

Guaracy has command over fire, both celestial and terrestrial, and can send lightning or cause droughts as punishment.

Limit: Unclear; may require the cooperation of other deities like Tupã (thunder god).

Form & Signs

Appearance — Guaracy is typically envisioned as a radiant human-like figure, often male, with a brilliant face and golden rays emanating from his head. In some accounts, he is depicted as a youth or a powerful warrior. His presence brings light and warmth, and his gaze is piercing and all-seeing.

Symbols — The sun itself is the primary symbol. In Guarani art, he is represented by solar motifs, circular patterns, and golden or red colors. His association with fire and light is central. In some modern depictions, he holds a staff or torch.

Domains — He resides in the sky, traveling from east to west each day. His celestial journey is essential for the cycle of day and night. He is also associated with the east, where he rises, and with the zenith, where his power is strongest.

Accounts I
The Creation of Day and NightGuarani oral tradition, recorded by Nimuendajú (1914)

Pre-colonial; recorded in early 20th century

In the beginning, the world was in darkness. Guaracy, the sun, emerged from the east, bringing light and separating day from night. His daily journey across the sky established the rhythm of life, and his setting in the west marked the passage to the underworld. The alternation of day and night became fundamental to Guarani cosmology, influencing rituals and daily activities.

Guaracy and the Moral OrderEthnographic accounts of Guarani beliefs

Unknown; traditional

Guaracy is said to watch over humans, rewarding the virtuous with good harvests and punishing wrongdoers with drought or excessive heat. His gaze is a constant reminder of the community's ethical obligations. Reinforces social norms and respect for nature.
