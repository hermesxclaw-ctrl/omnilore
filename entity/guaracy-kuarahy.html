Who They Are

Guaracy is the Guarani sun god, the ruler of the day and the source of warmth, light, and life. He is the brother of Tupã (the thunder god) and Jakaira (the god of mist). Guaracy's daily journey across the sky is the fundamental rhythm of Guarani life, and his return after the winter solstice is celebrated as the Guarani New Year. He is also the protector of warriors and hunters.

The Approach

In the Guarani cosmos, Guaracy — the Sun — is not merely a celestial body but a living divine being whose journey across the sky shapes the rhythm of life, death, and renewal. Known also as Kuarahy, this solar deity embodies the warmth that nurtures the earth and the fierce gaze that oversees the moral order of the world.

Content advisory. No explicit dark material is documented in available sources. However, as with many indigenous traditions, colonial-era accounts may have distorted or suppressed aspects of Guarani cosmology. Readers should approach historical sources with awareness of potential bias.

Etymology

Guaracy derives from the Tupi-Guarani word 'kuarasy' or 'kuarachy', meaning 'sun'. The root 'kua' relates to 'to see' or 'to know', reflecting the sun's role as the all-seeing eye of the sky.

Cultural Ownership

Guarani people of South America, primarily in Paraguay, Brazil, Argentina, and Bolivia. The deity is also recognized in Tupi-Guarani traditions more broadly.

Disputed Identifications

Some sources conflate Guaracy with other solar deities of neighboring indigenous groups, such as the Inca Inti or the Tupi Perudá. These are distinct traditions and should not be merged without clear evidence.

Description

Appearance

Guaracy is typically envisioned as a radiant human-like figure, often male, with a brilliant face and golden rays emanating from his head. In some accounts, he is depicted as a youth or a powerful warrior. His presence brings light and warmth, and his gaze is piercing and all-seeing.

Symbols & Iconography

The sun itself is the primary symbol. In Guarani art, he is represented by solar motifs, circular patterns, and golden or red colors. His association with fire and light is central. In some modern depictions, he holds a staff or torch.

Behaviour & Personality

Guaracy is often described as benevolent and generous, providing warmth and light for crops and life. However, he can also be stern and punitive, punishing those who violate moral laws or disrespect the natural order. He is a guardian of justice and a witness to all deeds.

Domains & Habitats

He resides in the sky, traveling from east to west each day. His celestial journey is essential for the cycle of day and night. He is also associated with the east, where he rises, and with the zenith, where his power is strongest.

Version Differences

In some Tupi-Guarani traditions, the sun is female (e.g., in certain Tupi groups), while in Guarani proper, Guaracy is predominantly male. Regional variations may also attribute different consorts or roles, such as being the brother or son of other deities like Tupã or Jacy (the Moon).

Stories & Myths

The Creation of Day and Night

Source Guarani oral tradition, recorded by Nimuendajú (1914)Culture Guarani (Apapocúva subgroup)Period Pre-colonial; recorded in early 20th century

Summary

In the beginning, the world was in darkness. Guaracy, the sun, emerged from the east, bringing light and separating day from night. His daily journey across the sky established the rhythm of life, and his setting in the west marked the passage to the underworld.

Participants

Guaracy

Jacy (Moon)

Tupã (creator god)

Consequences

The alternation of day and night became fundamental to Guarani cosmology, influencing rituals and daily activities.

Variants

Some versions say Guaracy and Jacy are siblings, others say they are spouses, and some say they are rivals.

Relationships

Jacy (Moon)

Type sibling/consort/rival (varies)Source Guarani mythology, general

Description

Jacy is the moon goddess, often considered Guaracy's sister or wife. In some myths, they alternate in the sky, and their union or conflict explains eclipses.

Version Note

The relationship varies by region and subgroup; no single canonical version.

Conflicting Accounts

Some sources say they are siblings, others say they are lovers, and others say they are enemies.

Tupã

Type creator/fatherSource Guarani mythology, general

Description

Tupã is the supreme god and creator, who may be seen as Guaracy's father or superior. Guaracy serves as Tupã's agent in the sky.

Version Note

In some accounts, Tupã created the sun and moon as his eyes or children.

Conflicting Accounts

Some traditions elevate Guaracy to an independent creator role, while others subordinate him to Tupã.

Cultural Significance

The Guarani solar cycle is fundamental to agricultural and ritual life. The New Year celebration at the winter solstice (June 21 in the Southern Hemisphere) involves community gatherings, prayer, and offerings to encourage Guaracy's return. The name Guaracy is a common personal name in Paraguay and Brazil, particularly among people of indigenous heritage. The daily rhythm of Guarani life — waking with the sun, working through the morning, resting at midday, and evening gatherings — reflects the understood relationship with Guaracy.
