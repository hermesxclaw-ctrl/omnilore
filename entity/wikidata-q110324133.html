Who It Is

In the lightless depths where even Ruha fears to tread, Hibil Ziwa — the radiant emissary of the Lightworld — finds the road blocked not by a monster with fangs and claws, but by a named presence: Zartai-Zartanai and his wife Amamit. He is not the great devourer Ur or the mountain of flesh Krun, but one of the countless lords of the World of Darkness who must be named, bound, and sealed if light is ever to return.

In GR 5.1, Zartai-Zartanai is presented as a stationary lord or guardian within the World of Darkness (alma ḏ-hšuka) whom Hibil Ziwa must confront during his descent. The narrative pattern for each encounter is: Hibil arrives bearing light, great splendor, and secret names/weapons from the Lightworld; the Darkworld being attempts to trap or resist; Hibil reveals his superior light and lineage (son of Yushamin, grandson of the Great Life), binds or seals the being, and proceeds deeper. Powers
Lordship over a region of the World of Darkness

As one of the enumerated lords (maran) of the alma ḏ-hšuka, Zartai-Zartanai exercises authority over a portion of the Darkworld and its inhabitants, comparable to other beings like Gaf, Giu, Qin, and Hag. This lordship is cosmological rather than elemental.

Limit: Power is strictly confined to the World of Darkness and is nullified by Lightworld beings bearing the Great Life's name and radiance. Hibil Ziwa overcomes him without recorded difficulty, indicating subordinate rank to Ruha, Ur, and Krun.

Conjugal alliance with Amamit/Libat

Possession of a wife, Amamit, who is identified as an epithet of Libat (the planet Venus, daughter of Ruha). This suggests alliance with the Ruha-Libat lineage, the female sovereigns of darkness.

Limit: Amamit/Libat herself is subordinate to Ruha and ultimately to Hibil Ziwa's light; the alliance does not prevent subjugation.

No attested independent supernatural technique

Unlike major Darkworld rulers (Ur who devours, Krun who is mountain of flesh, Qin who is mother of darkness), no specific power, weapon, transformation, or incantation is attributed to Zartai-Zartanai in the surviving text. The absence is itself attested.

Limit: Cannot be credited with powers assigned to neighboring beings. Modern summaries that assign generic demon powers (shapeshifting, possession) to Zartai-Zartanai are unattested inventions.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No physical description is given for Zartai-Zartanai in Right Ginza 5.1. The text names him and his wife Amamit as part of an itinerary of beings Hibil Ziwa encounters and overcomes, without detailing anatomy, clothing, size, or iconography. This is typical of the Ginza's treatment of minor World of Darkness lords, who are listed rather than described. Attested: name and paired spousal association only. Reconstructed: none. Accounts I
The Descent of Hibil Ziwa to the World of Darkness and the Encounter with Zartai-Zartanai and AmamitGinza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 5, Chapter 1 (GR 5.1)

Late Antiquity (composition 3rd-6th century CE, manuscripts 16th-19th century)

At the command of the Great Life (Mana Rba) and his father Yushamin, the Lightworld uthra Hibil Ziwa (also called Hibil, Abel the Great) descends through the mataratas to the World of Darkness to subdue Ruha and her offspring and to bring the stolen light and the soul of Ptahil's creation into order. Bearing the Great Radiance, secret names, and the weapon of light, he passes a sequence of dark lords. He comes to Zartai-Zartanai and Amamit his wife. As with the other lords, Hibil reveals his light, declares his lineage from the Lightworld, and binds/seals them so they cannot hinder his passage to the central darkness where Ruha, Ur, and Krun dwell. The episode is brief, formulaic, and serves to demonstrate the completeness of Hibil's conquest of every region of darkness. Zartai-Zartanai is sealed/bound, clearing Hibil's path deeper. Theologically, the story establishes that no part of darkness, even minor paired lords, escapes Lightworld sovereignty. Hibil Ziwa's Encounter with Gaf and Gafan (Parallel Watch-House Lords)Ginza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 5, Chapter 1 (same cycle, preceding/following Zartai-Zartanai)

Late Antiquity

Immediately before or after Zartai-Zartanai in the itinerary, Hibil encounters the paired demons Gaf and Gafan (and similarly Giu and Giu). The narrative formula is identical: arrival, challenge, revelation of light, binding. This parallel demonstrates the literary structure into which Zartai-Zartanai fits and confirms that his role is part of a catalog of conquests rather than a unique myth. Establishes the pattern of total subjugation of the Darkworld's watch-houses; used by scholars to argue that Zartai-Zartanai's brevity is not an omission but the intended form.

Hibil Ziwa's Confrontation with Qin (Qina), Mother of DarknessGinza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 5, Chapter 1

Late Antiquity

Deeper in the descent, Hibil confronts Qin (also Qinath), the great mother of the World of Darkness and consort of Anatan. Unlike the brief Zartai-Zartanai notice, Qin receives extended dialogue and is explicitly bound. The contrast highlights the hierarchy: Zartai-Zartanai is a minor lord, Qin is a major sovereign. Qin's binding is a major cosmological event, after which Hibil can confront Ruha herself. Zartai-Zartanai's prior binding is presented as a prerequisite step.

Accounts II
Hibil Ziwa's Binding of Ruha and Her Sons Ur, Krun, and ShdumGinza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 5, Chapter 1-2; also Left Ginza 3.43

Late Antiquity

At the nadir of the descent, Hibil confronts Ruha (Spirit, queen of darkness) and her monstrous offspring: Ur (Leviathan-like dragon), Krun (the great mountain of flesh), Shdum, and others. He binds them with chains of light and seals the Black Waters. This climax gives meaning to the earlier minor encounters including Zartai-Zartanai: all darkness must be sealed before creation can be ordered. Cosmological ordering; Ruha's power is curtailed; Ur and Krun are fettered; Hibil ascends with the mysteries. The sealing is ritually re-enacted in Mandaean baptism (masbuta) and death rites (masiqta) where the soul's ascent mirrors Hibil's.

The Ascent and Purification of Hibil Ziwa and the Creation of PtahilGinza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 3, Chapter 1 and Book 5, Chapter 3; Mandaean Book of John Chapter 14

Late Antiquity

After sealing the Darkworld (including Zartai-Zartanai's region), Hibil Ziwa is himself polluted by contact with darkness and must be purified in the Lightworld's yardna (heavenly river) by his brothers Shitil and Anush. Only then does he participate in the creation of Ptahil, the demiurge who will create the material world. This frames Zartai-Zartanai's defeat as part of a larger theogonic sequence. Explains why even victorious Lightworld beings need purification; establishes ritual precedent for Mandaean baptism after impurity.

Libat (Amamit/Venus) as Daughter of Ruha and Queen of the PlanetsGinza Rabba, Right Ginza Book 5, Chapter 1 and Book 3; Qulasta prayers 25, 71; E.S. Drower 'The Canonical Prayerbook' 1959

Late Antiquity to medieval liturgical codification

Libat, called Amamit in the Zartai-Zartanai passage, is the planet Venus, daughter of Ruha and sister of the other planets (Shamish/Sun, etc.). In Mandaean astrology, the planets are hostile archons who steal light and hinder souls. Libat/Amamit's marriage to Zartai-Zartanai links a planetary archon to a Darkworld lord, illustrating the intermarriage of Ruha's lineage with the depths. In other passages, Libat acts independently as a seductress and bringer of impurity. Explains why Amamit is named alongside Zartai-Zartanai: the pair represents the union of planetary and abyssal darkness. In ritual, Libat is invoked in exorcistic and apotropaic prayers.

Zartai-Zartanai is a major demon king like Ur or Krun, or a Zoroastrian figure. — He is a minor lord in a catalog of many, attested only in a single line of Right Ginza 5.1, subordinate to Ruha, Ur, and Krun. He is not Zoroastrian; the name is Mandaean Aramaic and his context is wholly Mandaean cosmology.

Zartai-Zartanai has a described monstrous appearance or specific powers like shapeshifting or possession. — No appearance or specific power is described in the primary source. The text only names him and his wife. Attributing generic demon powers is modern invention.

Amamit is a separate goddess independent of Venus. — In the Zartai-Zartanai passage, Amamit is explicitly glossed as Libat, the planet Venus (daughter of Ruha). While Amamit may appear as an epithet elsewhere, in this context she is Venus.

Mandaean demons like Zartai-Zartanai are fallen angels or punishers of human sinners. — Mandaean World of Darkness beings are primordial inhabitants of darkness, not fallen Lightworld uthras, and not hellish torturers of the dead. They are cosmological jailers of light and toll-collectors for ascending souls, but the primary punishment narrative is not central.
