Who It Is

In the shadowed groves of ancient Greece, where the boar's bristle and the myrtle's bloom intertwine, there dwelt a goddess known by a name that whispers of triple power—Triclaria. Though her worship has faded into the dust of millennia, her epithets and sanctuaries still echo in the fragments of poets and the stones of forgotten temples, inviting us to piece together the enigma of a deity who may have been Artemis herself, or a power wholly her own.

As an aspect of Artemis, she would be a virgin goddess of the hunt, wilderness, and childbirth, protective of young animals and women. She might also have had a local role as a protector of the city of Patras.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Triclaria' is likely derived from Greek elements: 'tri-' meaning 'three' and 'klar-' possibly related to 'klaros' (lot, inheritance) or 'kleos' (glory), but the exact derivation is uncertain. It may signify 'thrice-called' or 'of the three lots,' though these are speculative.

Pronunciation — trih-KLAR-ee-uh (English approximation); in Greek: Τρικλαρία (Triklaria).

Aliases — Triclaria

Ownership — Ancient Greek, specifically associated with the region of Achaea in the Peloponnese, particularly the city of Patras.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No direct description of Triclaria's appearance survives. If she is an epithet of Artemis, she would share the typical iconography of Artemis: a young huntress, often depicted with a bow and arrows, wearing a short chiton, and accompanied by a deer or hunting dog. However, local variations might exist, and no specific image of Triclaria is known.

Symbols — Possible symbols include the bow, arrow, deer, and hunting implements, consistent with Artemis. The name 'Triclaria' itself might have been associated with a triple form or a triple shrine, but this is speculative.

Domains — Triclaria's cult was centered in Patras, Achaea, Greece. Likely sanctuaries included a temple or sacred grove, possibly near a spring or a natural feature. She would have been a local deity with a regional following.

Accounts I
Foundation of the Cult of Triclaria in PatrasPausanias, Description of Greece 7.19

Mythical/historical, possibly Archaic or earlier

Pausanias records that the people of Patras worshipped Artemis under the name Triclaria. He does not provide a founding myth, but it is likely that the cult was established in the city's early history, possibly brought by settlers or founded by a legendary figure. The festival in her honor may have included processions and sacrifices. The cult became a central civic institution in Patras, with a sanctuary and annual festival.
