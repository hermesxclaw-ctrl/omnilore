Who They Are

Chaac is the Maya rain god, one of the most essential and most frequently invoked deities in the Maya pantheon. As a civilization that depended entirely on rain for its agricultural prosperity, the Maya devoted enormous resources to securing Chaac's favor — through prayer, sacrifice, and the construction of sacred wells (cenotes). He is depicted with a reptilian snout, fangs, and a lightning axe in his hand.

Cultural Significance

Chaac's worship was the most widespread and practical of all Maya cults — every community, from the smallest village to the greatest city, had rituals dedicated to him. Cenotes — natural sinkholes that were the only source of fresh water in much of Yucatan — were considered Chaac's sacred portals to the underworld. The Sacred Cenote at Chichen Itza was a major pilgrimage site where offerings (including, during droughts, human sacrifices) were thrown into the water to appease Chaac. The Chaac dance (Danza del Chaac) is still performed in Maya communities today, particularly in Yucatan, as part of rain-invocation ceremonies (Cha-Chaac ritual).
