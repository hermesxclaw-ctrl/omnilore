Who It Is

Above the forests and peat bogs of the Udmurts, Inmar does not thunder or strike — he watches. The sky itself is his body, distant, bright, and impossibly high, and every prayer shouted toward the clouds, every white offering laid in the sacred grove, was once an attempt to be heard across that distance.

Inmar is benevolent but remote. He is creator and sustainer, giver of fate (shudbur), life, breath (lul), and cosmic order, but not an everyday intervener. He hears prayers (kuriskon) mediated by lower deities and priests (vösyas', tuno). He is moral: punishes oath-breaking, incest, desecration of groves, and human wickedness (flood myth). He does not engage in trickery or sexual exploits common to other pantheons. Attested personality: just, distant, luminous, paternal. Aliases — Inmar (Инмар), Inmär, In-mu, Inmu, Inmar-Kyldysin-Kuaz as triad member

Ownership — Udmurt people (Udmurt: Удмурт, historically Votyak), Permian branch of Finno-Ugric peoples, indigenous to Udmurtia (Volga-Ural region, Russian Federation), with related traditions

Powers
Cosmogony and Creation

Creator of sky, earth, sun, and humanity. In earth-diver myth, Inmar commands creation from primordial ocean; in other variants he creates humans from clay, wood, or his own breath.

Limit: Cannot create alone without opposition of Keremet/Shaitan who spoils or steals earth; requires helper/adversary. Does not create ex nihilo in folk versions - works with pre-existing waters.

Fate and Life-Giving (Shudbur/Lul)

Dispenses fate, soul/breath (lul), lifespan, and fortune. Invoked in prayers for children, health, harvest, and good fortune.

Limit: Fate can be petitioned but not compelled; Inmar's will is mediated by lower spirits. Does not resurrect the dead.

Moral Judgment and Punishment

Sends flood, famine, or disease to punish human wickedness, oath-breaking, or desecration. Flood myth: drowns humanity except for righteous survivors in boat/raft.

Limit: Punishment is collective and delayed; Inmar warns via priests before acting. Does not punish arbitrarily in most tales.

Distance and Inaccessibility (Deus Otiosus)

Power expressed as withdrawal - too high and pure to be approached directly. Humans must pray through intermediaries (Kyldysin, vorshud, priest).

Limit: This very distance limits his cultic accessibility; everyday needs are taken to lower spirits.

Blessing of Sacred Space

Consecrates lud groves and kuala sanctuaries; presence invoked by white offerings. White horse sacrifice (yir-pid soton) historically offered to Inmar for community welfare.

Limit: Requires ritual purity: participants must be clean, women historically excluded from kuala interior in some groups, no blood may be spilled impurely.

Accounts I
The Earth-Diver Creation - Inmar and KeremetBernát Munkácsi, Wotjakische Volksdichtung (1887) No. 3-5; Uno Harva, Finno-Ugric, Siberian Mythology (1927) pp. 152-158; V.E. Vladykin (1994) pp. 62-70

Oral tradition recorded 1880s-1990s; motifs pre-Christian, likely medieval or earlier

In the beginning only primordial ocean existed and Inmar dwelt above. Inmar sends Keremet (also called Shaitan, Wumurt, Kuris') to dive to the bottom and bring up earth in his mouth/beak. Keremet secretly keeps some earth in his mouth for himself. Inmar spreads the earth on the water and it grows. The stolen earth swells in Keremet's mouth, causing mountains and swamps when spat out. Inmar curses Keremet and casts him down, establishing dualism: Inmar's flat fertile earth vs Keremet's mountains, bogs, weeds, and evil. In some variants Inmar creates earth on the back of a great bull or duck. Explains origin of earth's topography, origin of evil and imperfect world, and eternal opposition between Inmar (good, sky) and Keremet (evil, lower world). Basis for Udmurt dualistic worldview.

Creation of Humanity from ClayMax Buch, Die Wotjaken (1882) p.142; Vladykin (1994) p.71

Recorded 1880s, oral tradition older

After creating the world, Inmar forms the first humans from clay, wood, or earth, breathes life (lul) into them, and gives them laws. Keremet/Shaitan tries to spoil creation by making humans weak to disease, inserting evil thoughts, or creating ugly spirits. Inmar drives dogs to guard the unfinished humans, but Keremet bribes dogs with fur coats, allowing him to spit on or defile humans, explaining why humans are susceptible to illness and sin. Inmar then gives humans clothing and fire to protect them. Explains human mortality, vulnerability to disease, need for ritual purity, and origin of dogs' fur. Establishes Inmar as life-giver and lawgiver.

The Great Flood - Inmar's PunishmentUno Harva (1927) p.162 citing Munkácsi and Wichmann; Vladykin (1994) p.78-79

Recorded late 19th century

Humanity becomes wicked, quarrelsome, forgets sacrifices and defiles sacred groves. Inmar warns through priests and elders but is ignored. He sends a great flood (inundation) covering the earth for many days, drowning all but a few righteous people who survive in a boat, raft, or on a high mountain (variant: on a floating kuala). After waters recede, survivors repopulate earth and Inmar re-establishes proper rites, commanding regular prayers and white sacrifices. In some tellings, Kyldysin or Kuaz executes the flood at Inmar's command. Etiology for flood legends, renewal of covenant between Inmar and humans, justification for strict ritual observance and moral code.

Accounts II
The Division of the Triad - Inmar, Kyldysin, and KuazBuch (1882) pp. 136-140; Harva (1927) pp. 145-150; Vladykin (1994) pp. 48-60

Systematized in 19th-century ethnography; oral roots older

Inmar, Kyldysin (earth/fertility), and Kuaz (weather, wind, atmosphere) originally dwell together in sky. They divide cosmic labor: Inmar retains supreme sky and fate, Kyldysin takes earth, childbirth, fertility and the harvest, Kuaz takes weather, rain, thunder and wind. In some myths they are brothers, in others father (Inmar) and sons. Humans must honor all three, but Inmar is eldest and highest. One myth tells that Kyldysin descends to live among humans to teach agriculture, then withdraws when humans offend him, leaving only Inmar distant. Another says Kuaz quarrels with Inmar and causes storms when angry. Explains Udmurt pantheon structure, division of prayers (each deity has specific kuriskon), and why Inmar is invoked first but Kyldysin/Kuaz more frequently for daily needs.

Inmar and the Sacred Grove (Lud) and KualaPallas (1776) Vol.3 p.50; Buch (1882) pp. 143-150; Vladykin (1994) pp. 90-105

Ritual tradition observed 18th-20th century

Inmar teaches the first priest (vösyas', tuno) how to establish sacred space: a clan sanctuary hut (kuala) with eternal hearth fire, and a forest grove (lud, keremet) where no tree may be felled, no blood spilled impurely. Inmar instructs that prayers must be spoken facing east at dawn, with white offerings, and that the grove must be kept pure. When humans violate the grove by cutting trees or bringing impure persons, Inmar withdraws his blessing, crops fail, and Keremet's power grows. Restoration requires expiatory white horse sacrifice and reconsecration by elders. Foundation myth for Udmurt sacred architecture and ritual law; explains taboos surrounding lud and kuala, and priestly authority.

Inmar's Bees and the Origin of AgricultureMunkácsi (1887) folklore No. 12; Vladykin (1994) p.84

Recorded 1880s

Humans initially have no grain or honey. Inmar pities them and sends bees (or grain seeds) from the sky via Kyldysin. Keremet tries to steal or spoil the gift by creating wasps, weeds, or by making bees sting. Inmar teaches humans to keep bees in hollow logs (bort) and to sow barley and rye, instructing them to offer first fruits back to him. Those who share first bread with Inmar prosper; those who do not suffer blight. Explains apiculture (important Udmurt economy), agriculture, and first-fruits offering rite (akin to harvest festival).

Inmar is the Udmurt god of blacksmithing or is the same as Finnish Ilmarinen. — Inmar has no smithcraft domain; Ilmarinen is a Finnish/Karelian smith-hero. Similarity is phonetic coincidence. Inmar is sky/creator god; confusion stems from 19th-century amateur comparisons and Wikipedia homonym lists.

Inmar, Kyldysin, and Kuaz are just three names for the Christian Trinity translated into Udmurt. — Missionaries promoted this equation to facilitate conversion, but folk prayers and ethnography show distinct functions and separate invocations. Syncretism existed, but indigenous triad predates and differs from Trinity.

Udmurts worshipped idols/statues of Inmar in temples. — Inmar was aniconic; worship centered on sacred groves (lud) and clan huts (kuala) with no anthropomorphic idol. Vorshud boxes are clan guardians, not images of Inmar. No temple buildings existed.

Inmar mythology is extinct and only known from books. — Inmar remains actively worshipped in Udmurt Vos revival since 1990s, with regular prayers in sacred groves in Udmurtia. Tradition was suppressed but not broken; elders preserved prayers orally through Soviet period.
