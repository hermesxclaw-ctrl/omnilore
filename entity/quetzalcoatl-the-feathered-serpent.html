Who It Is

Quetzalcoatl ('Feathered Serpent') is the synthesis of a bird (the quetzal — celestial, airy) and a serpent (coatl — terrestrial, chthonic), embodying the union of heaven and earth, spirit and matter, wind and water. As Ehecatl, he is the wind-god who sweeps the path before Tlaloc's rain; as the morning-star aspect Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli, he is the dying-and-rising planet Venus. Quetzalcoatl is the patron of the priesthood, the inventor of the calendar, the discoverer of maize, and the founder of the arts (writing, featherwork, jade-carving). The Toltec-Aztec tradition conflates the deity with a historical priest-king, Ce Acatl Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl of Tula, whose exile across the Eastern Sea (perhaps to the Yucatan, where he appears as Kukulkan) structured the political theology of both Tollan and Chichen Itza. His 'return' as the bearded white man in the year 1-Reed (1519 CE, when Cortés arrived) was a catastrophic misrecognition — the gap between divine promise and human greed that sealed the empire's fate.
