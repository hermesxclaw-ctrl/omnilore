Who It Is

The Trasgu (also Trasgo, Trasno) is a mischievous goblin-like creature from the folklore of northern Spain, particularly Asturias, Cantabria, Galicia, and León. He is described as a small, dark-skinned man with a hunched back, long arms, a crooked nose, and wearing a red cap (the same cap worn by Asturian farm laborers) and a leather apron. He often has a hole in his left hand through which he constantly loses things — a telltale sign of his identity. The Trasgu lives in houses, barns, and stables, typically in the attic, the stable loft, or behind the hearth. Like the German Heinzelmännchen or the Scottish brownie, the Trasgu can be helpful: he performs household chores at night, repairs broken tools, and tends to livestock. However, his help comes with a constant stream of irritating pranks — he moves objects around, hides keys, switches salt with sugar, trips sleeping family members, and makes loud noises at night. Getting rid of a Trasgu is notoriously difficult. The most common method is to trick him into performing a seemingly impossible task, such as carrying water in a sieve or counting the grains of rice in a sack. If he fails, he leaves in humiliation. Alternatively, offering him a tiny suit of clothing (as in the English brownie tradition) offends him and causes him to depart. The Trasgu is considered inoffensive but relentless, and Asturian families traditionally accept his presence as part of rural life. The name derives from the Latin 'transitus,' meaning 'passage' or 'crossing.'
