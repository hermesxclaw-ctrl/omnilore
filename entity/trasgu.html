Who It Is

In the quiet hours of an Asturian farmhouse, a small figure with a red cap and a hole in his left hand might be tidying the kitchen—or, if crossed, hurling pots and spooking cattle. This is the trasgu, a domestic goblin of northern Spain, a creature of mischief and hidden help, whose pranks have echoed through centuries of Iberian folklore.

Mischievous, nervous, helpful if treated well, destructive if offended. Moves objects, breaks pots, spooks cattle, spills water. Does house chores at night when pleased.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Derived from the Latin 'trans' (beyond) or 'transitus' (passage), possibly referring to a being that crosses thresholds or moves between worlds. Also linked to the Spanish 'trasgo' meaning goblin.

Pronunciation — TRASS-goo (Asturian), TRASS-go (Spanish)

Aliases — Trasgo, Trasno, Cornín, Xuan Dos Camíos, Gorretín Coloráu

Ownership — Northern Spain (Asturias, Cantabria, Galicia) and northern Portugal (Trás-os-Montes). Celtic origin claimed.

Powers
Invisibility/Stealth

Enters homes at night unseen, moves objects, performs chores.

Limit: Must hide from humans; in Cantabria, wears leaf clothing to blend in.

Teleportation/Following

Follows families when they move, even to new homes.

Limit: Cannot be easily escaped.

Supernatural Strength?

Can move heavy objects, break pots, stir chests, but not cause permanent damage.

Limit: Limited to mischief; no lasting harm.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Typically a tiny man with a limp in his right leg, dark skin, red clothing, and a pointed red hat. He has a hole in his left hand. Sometimes described with horns, tail, sheep ears, long legs, and a black/gray cloak. Alternatively, small with long thin legs and a tight dark brown dress.

Symbols — Red cap, hole in left hand, limp, domestic settings, nocturnal activity.

Domains — Homes, especially kitchens and stables; in Cantabria, forests.

Impossible TasksAsturian oral tradition

Unknown

To expel a trasgu, ask him to bring water in a basket, pick up millet from the floor (falls through his hand hole), or whiten a black sheep. He tries until exhausted and leaves in shame. The trasgu leaves and does not return.
