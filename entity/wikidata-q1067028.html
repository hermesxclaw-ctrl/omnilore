Who It Is

In the shadowed margins of Japanese mythology, where the divine and the discarded meet, there exists a figure whose very name means 'leech-child' — Hiruko, the firstborn of the primordial deities Izanagi and Izanami, cast adrift upon the sea in a boat of reeds. His story is a haunting tale of imperfection, exile, and the fragile boundaries of the sacred.

No detailed personality is provided in the myths; his story is primarily about his birth and abandonment. In later folk tradition, when conflated with Ebisu, he is a benevolent deity of fishermen and prosperity.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Hiruko (蛭子) literally means 'leech child' or 'leech offspring'. The name combines 'hiru' (蛭, leech) and 'ko' (子, child), reflecting the child's boneless, jelly-like form.

Pronunciation — HEE-roo-koh

Aliases — Hiruko no Mikoto, Hiruko-gami, Leach Child, Leech Child, Ebisu (disputed)

Ownership — Japan (Japanese mythology, Shinto)

Powers
None attested

Hiruko has no recorded powers or abilities in the core myths. He is a passive figure, cast adrift. Any powers attributed to him are due to later conflation with Ebisu, who is a deity of luck and fishing.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Hiruko is described as a child born without bones or with a soft, jelly-like body, resembling a leech. The Kojiki states that he was placed in a boat of reeds and set adrift because he was not able to stand even at age three.

Symbols — Often depicted as a small child, sometimes with a fishing rod or a sea motif, especially when identified with Ebisu. In art, he may be shown in a reed boat.

Domains — Primarily the sea, as he was cast adrift. In the Ebisu association, he is a sea deity and patron of fishing.

Accounts I
The Birth and Abandonment of HirukoKojiki and Nihon Shoki

Mythological (creation era)

After Izanagi and Izanami created the islands of Japan, they gave birth to various deities. Their first child was Hiruko, but he was born without bones (or as a leech-child) and could not stand even at age three. Because of his imperfection, his parents placed him in a boat of reeds and set him adrift on the sea. He is not mentioned again in the main creation narrative. Hiruko disappears from the main mythological narrative. His abandonment is often interpreted as a myth explaining the exclusion of imperfect beings from the divine order. In later tradition, he is sometimes said to have drifted to the shore and become the deity Ebisu.
