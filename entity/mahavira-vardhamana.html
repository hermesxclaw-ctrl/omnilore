Who It Is

Mahāvīra ('Great Hero') was the 24th and last Tīrthaṅkara ('Ford-Maker') of Jainism, the historical founder-figure of the Jain tradition as it exists today. He was born Prince Vardhamāna to King Siddhartha and Queen Trishala in Kundagrama (near modern Vaishali, Bihar). Renouncing the world at age 30, he undertook 12 years of extreme asceticism, meditation, and silence, enduring abuse from villagers and wild animals alike, until he attained kevala jñāna (omniscience) under a Sala tree on the banks of the Rijupaluka River. For the next 30 years, he preached the path of asceticism, non-violence (ahiṃsā), non-attachment, and the principle of multiple viewpoints (anekāntavāda). He organized his followers into a fourfold order of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen. Mahāvīra's teachings form the core of Jain philosophy: the eternal nature of the soul (jīva), the doctrine of karma as a physical substance that clings to the soul, the necessity of severe asceticism to burn off karma, and the goal of liberation (moksha) through the three jewels of right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct. He attained nirvana (final liberation) at Pavapuri at age 72. Unlike the Buddha, Mahāvīra was not the founder of a new tradition but the last in a long lineage of Tīrthaṅkaras stretching back to Ādinātha (Rishabhanatha).

Origins

Mahāvīra was born as the son of King Siddhartha and Queen Trishala. His mother had 14 auspicious dreams before his birth, foretelling the birth of a Tīrthaṅkara. He was married to Princess Yashoda and had a daughter (whose name varies in texts). At 30, he renounced the world, tore out his hair in five handfuls (a ritual renunciation), and began his ascetic journey.
