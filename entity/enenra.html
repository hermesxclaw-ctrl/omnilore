Who It Is

The enenra is the quietest entry in the yokai canon: a being composed entirely of smoke, born from bonfires — especially the smoldering heaps of autumn leaves and rice-straw burned at the field's edge — that rises, gathers, and for a moment takes form: a human face, a reaching figure, a woman's sleeve, before folding back into formlessness and drifting apart. It harms no one. It steals nothing, hunts nothing, warns of nothing. Its single recorded property is the most haunting credential in the whole archive: the enenra can be seen clearly only by the pure of heart. To everyone else it is just smoke behaving as smoke briefly seems to — a trick of the eddy, a face that was never there. The Edo compilers who recorded it (it appears in the great yokai compendia of the eighteenth century) essentially canonized a universal human experience: everyone has watched smoke make a shape that watched them back. The enenra names the shape, and then adds its terrible, gentle test — if you saw it, really saw it, that says something about you; and if you never have, that may too. It is pareidolia given a moral dimension, the campfire's oldest optical illusion promoted to spirit, and proof that the yokai tradition, so rich in predators and tricksters, also had room for a being whose entire existence is a moment of smoke resembling a soul.

Origins

Edo-period yokai compendia (recorded among Sawaki Suushi's and later Sekien's smoke-and-flame spirits); a literary-era yokai born of art and observation rather than village fear.

Attested in Edo-period yokai compendiaEra Edo period

Form

Smoke — and then, for a breath, more than smoke: a face, a figure, a gesture of reaching or bowing, dissolving the instant attention hardens into scrutiny.

Smoke/Darkness form Enenra is literally composed of smoke and darkness rather than flesh. It has no fixed solid body and can manifest as drifting gauze-like smoke. Sekien/Konjaku Hyakki Shūi (c. 1781); Wikidata/Wikipedia description as 'yōkai composed of smoke and darkness' The smoke-form is intangible and conditional; the being is not described as physically strong, corporeal, or able to grasp/affect the material world through brute force in the source mythology. Later adaptations grant it violent physicality (see modern reception — e.g. Power Rangers monsters with tentacle/dark-mist attacks), but these are franchise inventions, not attested in the Edo-period source.

Human shape-taking When Enenra emerges from a bonfire it takes a human shape or form, presenting as (or near) a human figure made of smoke. Konjaku Hyakki Shūi The human shape is still smoke-based — a silhouette, not a solid body. It emerges specifically from fire, implying it cannot sustain that manifest form away from its fire source indefinitely. Description is terse; no detailed account of whether it can speak, interact, or be touched in human form.

Bonfire residency / emergence Enenra mostly resides in bonfires and emerges when the fire burns — a habitat-and-emergence power tying its existence to ritual and festival fire. Konjaku Hyakki Shūi Dependent on an external fire source; the creature's manifestation is conditionally tied to the presence of burning wood/ashes. None beyond the general dependence on fire.

Selective perceptibility (pure-of-heart condition) Enenra can only be seen by the pure of heart. Ordinary or impure observers may see smoke but not the being, making perception itself a moral/spiritual faculty. Konjaku Hyakki Shūi / Wikipedia summary This is a limiting property as much as a power — it restricts whom the creature is visible to, and implies Enenra cannot or does not reveal itself to the impure. The exact threshold of 'purity' is undefined; it functions as folklore shorthand for spiritual discernment rather than a testable criterion.

Death-transformation (becoming an enenra) A second, rarer class of enenra is stated to be humans who died and were transformed into enenra — a metamorphic afterlife power by which a deceased person becomes a smoke-spirit. Konjaku Hyakki Shūi (as reported in Wikipedia) Rare and unspecified in mechanism; the cause of such a death-transformation is not detailed (e.g. whether it requires burn-death, unfinished business, or a failed grave-rite). Tension with the 'born purely as enenra' class — the taxonomy of two origin-types is asserted but not reconciled.

Smoke manipulation / body-to-smoke (franchise expansion) In modern adaptations Enenra's power is expanded to active, structured smoke-control: the Dandadan Red Baron can 'manipulate smoke and turn his own body into smoke as well'; Power Rangers enemies use dark-mist / teapot-fireball forms. Dandadan (manga/anime, 2021/2024); Power Rangers Ninja Steel & Samurai (toyetic villain designs) These are franchise-invented combat applications, not Edo folklore; they reify the elemental smoke concept into a superpower with offensive/defensive uses. Franchise powers diverge sharply from the source: the original Enenra is a conditional apparition, whereas Dandadan's Red Baron actively weaponizes smoke and Power Rangers' Gigertox/Toxitea fight as cannon-fodder monsters.

Weaknesses & Wards

Wind, and the end of the fire. The enenra cannot persist, cannot stay, cannot be held — impermanence is not its weakness but its entire biography.

Accounts

Enenra dwells within bonfires. When the fire burns and smoke rises, the enenra emerges, taking a human shape composed of smoke and darkness. It is a being of fire-wisps, gauze-like and intangible, and can only be seen by the pure of heart. The account frames the creature as at once demonic and divine — an elemental presence of darkness and smoke rather than a flesh-and-blood menace.

Legend holds that there are two types of enenra. The first and most common are enenra born purely as enenra — intrinsically elemental smoke-beings. The second, rarer type are humans who have died and been transformed into enenra — a ghostly subclass by which death converts a person into a smoke-spirit. This dual origin gives Enenra both a divine/elemental and a mournful/ghostly register.

Enenra has been repeatedly re-materialized as a combatant. In Power Rangers Ninja Steel, Toxitea is an Enenra/kettle-themed monster loyal to Madame Odius who fires teapot-shaped fireballs and brews lethal poison. In Power Rangers Samurai, Gigertox is an Enenra-themed Nighlok with stretchable tentacles who, powered up, becomes a giant dark-mist snake monster. In Dandadan, the Red Baron (an assassin of the Black Paladins / Delacroix family) acquires the yōkai power of Enenra (煙々羅), enabling smoke manipulation and turning his body into smoke; Enenra's 'Little Red Riding Hood' power is also consumed in building the 'ultimate yōkai' Sir Vlad Delacroix.

Field Notes

⍟ Its name is written with characters suggesting smoke, mist, and gauze-thin silk — a textile of vapor.

⍟ Mortal Kombat's smoke-ninja Enenra borrowed the name for something considerably less contemplative.

⍟ It is one of the few yokai with no recorded victim, no defense, and no advice — there is nothing to survive.

Cultural Significance

The enenra is the yokai tradition's meditation on impermanence — smoke, form, dissolution — and its purity-test visibility gave Japanese art and literature a delicate device: what a character sees in the smoke reports on their soul.

Content Notes

A documented subclass of enenra are humans who died and became enenra. The premise implies certain deaths convert the soul into a smoke-ghost, an afterlife of unresolved/transmuted identity — a mournful, arguably tragic register that the concise source does not develop. The mechanism (burning? smoke-inhalation? dying by/at fire? unfinished-beginning) is unstated, a gap that leaves room for darker speculation absent from the attested account.

Fits the broad yōkai pattern of restless or transformed dead; the specificity of fire/smoke as the medium is Enenra's distinctive trait. Whether real folk belief underlies this or it is Sekien's systematization is unverified.

Konjaku Hyakki Shūi as summarized in Wikipedia

The condition that Enenra is 'only seen by the pure of heart' doubles as a moral gate that writes most humans out of the encounter. Those considered 'impure' would silently walk past a possibly-present supernatural being — an exclusionary framing that, read critically, presumes a knowable, ranked purity and positions the folk-encounter as a privilege of the virtuous.

A recurring folklore motif of moral/ritual discernment; here it is notable that the perceiver's character, not the creature's, determines visible reality — an inversion of most monster-encounter logic.

Konjaku Hyakki Shūi / Wikipedia summary

Modern toyetic franchises convert Enenra's intangible, conditional, pure-of-heart-gated presence into physicalized combatants. Toxitea brews lethal poison and fires fireballs; Gigertox has stretchable tentacles and a giant dark-mist snake 'third life'; Dandadan's Red Baron turns his body into smoke to assassinate and steal powers. This re-materialization flattens the source's subtle conditional-ness into cannon-fodder villainy and re-frames a liminal fire-omen as raw threat material.

A documented example of the broader phenomenon where corporate toyetic media (Saban/Bandai Power Rangers, Shueisha manga) commodity yōkai — stripping folk ritual nuance and repurposing names/imagery for villain rosters. It also reflects the modern yōkai export boom begun by Shigeru Mizuki's GeGeGe no Kitarō.

List of Power Rangers Ninja Steel / Samurai characters; List of Dandadan characters (Wikipedia)

As a Sekien wooden-page yōkai mass-produced in books and later exported through franchises and online taxonomies, Enenra participates in the Orientalist/exotic 'Japanese monster' commodity — a creature known mainly through Western-circulated images and toy lines (Power Rangers' yōkai-themed villain rosters) rather than through its social or ritual context. The ambiguity of demon-vs-divine in the sources is often collapsed into 'evil monster' in export.

Consistent with scholarship on yōkai as both a living folk category and a globalized exotic commodity; the 'pure of heart' nuance is lost in most adaptations.

What We Don't Know

Primary gaps in this tick's research: (1) The mechanism and cause of the death-transformation (human → enenra) is unstated in the source; Japanese-language primary sources might clarify. (2) Whether Enenra corresponds to any pre-1781 rural oral belief, or is purely Sekien's invention, is not resolved by English sources — a Japanese folkloristics literature review (minzokugaku) is needed. (3) The exact ritual/seasonal context of the 'bonfire' (bonfires for obon vs festival fires vs hearth fires) is unspecified in the English extract; a reading of Sekien's original volume, including which of the three volumes (Cloud/Mist/Rain) holds the entry and any sekien annotation, would resolve habitat context. A way to hold it in your mind

Stand beside a midsummer bonfire at dusk, when the flames drop low and the smoke turns to thin grey veils that hang in the still air — and know that, in the Edo-period imagination of Toriyama Sekien, something in that drift might be watching you right back, visible only to the pure of heart. Enenra (煙々羅, 'lightweight-fabric smoke') is the yōkai of the bonfire's afterglow: a being composed of smoke and darkness that rises from the fire and takes a human shape, a liminal presence that is at once demon and god, elemental spirit and transformed dead. It never attacks in its native folklore — it simply waits in the smoke, reserving its true form for those whose hearts are clean. Only in the modern era, pulled through the machinery of Power Rangers villain rosters and Dandadan's grudge-warriors, has this conditional ghost been forced into flesh, given fists, and turned loose to fight.
