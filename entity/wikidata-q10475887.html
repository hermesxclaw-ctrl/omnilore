Who It Is

In the thunderous skies above the lands of the Songhai, a name echoes with the force of a storm: Dongo, the god of thunder and war. But this is not a simple tale of a mighty deity; it is a story of divine conflict, of a father's wrath and a son's defiance, and of the eternal struggle between the old ways and the new.

Dongo is a fierce and wrathful deity, quick to anger and swift to punish. He is a warrior god, embodying the destructive power of storms. He is also a protector of his people, but his methods are harsh.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Dongo is associated with thunder and lightning in Songhai and related West African languages. It may be derived from the root for 'thunder' or 'to thunder'.

Pronunciation — DON-goh

Aliases — Dongo-Séno, Dongo, Thunder God

Ownership — Songhai people of West Africa (Niger, Mali, Burkina Faso, Nigeria, Benin).

Form & Signs

Appearance — Dongo is often described as a powerful, fearsome deity associated with storms. He may be depicted as a warrior or a force of nature, wielding thunderbolts. Specific physical descriptions are not widely documented.

Symbols — Thunder, lightning, storm clouds, possibly a double-headed axe or other weapons.

Domains — He resides in the sky, associated with storms and thunder. He is a central figure in Songhai cosmology, with influence over rain, warfare, and justice.

Accounts I
The Conflict with His FatherSonghai oral tradition

Pre-Islamic, with continued oral transmission

Dongo is often said to be the son of the sky god (or a creator deity). In a major myth, Dongo rebels against his father, leading to a cosmic battle. Dongo is victorious and becomes the ruler of the world or a dominant deity. Dongo establishes his supremacy, but the conflict explains the origins of thunder and lightning as remnants of the battle.

Accounts II
Dongo and the Great FloodEthnographic accounts

Ancient, as recorded in 20th century

In some traditions, Dongo is associated with a great flood that destroyed the world, after which he recreated or repopulated it. The flood serves as a punishment for human wrongdoing, and Dongo emerges as a civilizing hero.
