Warhammer Manticore

Who It Is
Form

The Warhammer Manticore is a colossal beast, standing as tall as a warhorse but far more massive, with a body resembling a lion's but covered in thick, dark fur that often appears matted with blood or filth. Its head is that of a lion, but with a grotesque, almost human-like visage in some depictions, complete with a mane of spiky, black hair. Its most distinctive features are its enormous, leathery wings, similar to those of a bat, which allow it to fly with surprising agility despite its bulk. The tail is long and serpentine, ending in a scorpion's stinger that drips with venom, and some illustrations show additional spikes along the tail. The creature's claws are razor-sharp, capable of rending steel plate, and its eyes glow with a malevolent intelligence. In the Warhammer setting, its appearance is often further corrupted by Chaos, with mutations such as extra eyes, twisted horns, or patches of scales, making each individual unique but uniformly terrifying.

Powers

Scorpion Tail Venom The Manticore's tail ends in a scorpion-like stinger that delivers a potent venom. In tabletop rules, this is often represented as a poisoned attack that can cause additional wounds or even instant death on a failed toughness test. The venom is described as causing excruciating pain and paralysis, and in the lore, it is harvested by alchemists and witches for use in poisons.

Flight The Manticore's large bat-like wings allow it to fly, making it a highly mobile predator. In combat, it can swoop down on enemies, using its momentum to increase the force of its attacks. This ability also enables it to escape danger quickly and to attack from unexpected angles, making it a fearsome opponent in both open battle and ambush.

Terror The mere presence of a Manticore is enough to instill fear in the hearts of even the bravest warriors. In tabletop rules, this is represented by the 'Terror' special rule, which forces enemy units to take a leadership test or be routed. In the lore, its roar is described as a sound that combines a lion's roar with a human scream, and it is said to cause madness in those who hear it.

Rage Manticores are known for their uncontrollable fury, which can be both a boon and a bane to their handlers. In some editions, they have a rule that forces them to charge the nearest enemy if they are not controlled by a rider, and they may even attack their own side if enraged. This rage is also reflected in their high strength and attack characteristics, making them devastating in close combat.

Accounts

Kharaz was a renowned Beastmaster of Naggarond who captured a particularly ferocious Manticore from the mountains of Naggaroth. He spent months training it, using cruel whips and dark magic to subdue it. However, during a public display in the arena of Naggarond, the Manticore broke free of its chains and turned on Kharaz, mauling him to death before being slain by the city's guards. The event was seen as a sign of the Manticore's untamable nature and a warning against hubris.

In the depths of the Drakwald Forest, a Manticore terrorized the local villages, killing livestock and travelers. A band of knights from the Order of the Reiksguard was dispatched to hunt it down. After a long and bloody pursuit, they cornered the beast in a ruined watchtower. The knights suffered heavy casualties, but eventually managed to slay the Manticore with a combination of crossbow fire and a well-aimed lance thrust through its heart. The head was taken back to Altdorf as a trophy, where it was displayed in the Imperial Zoo.

A Dark Elf sorceress named Morathi, the Hag Sorceress, is said to have made a pact with a Manticore in the mountains of Naggaroth. She offered it a portion of her magical power in exchange for its service as a mount. The Manticore, being intelligent, accepted, and it became her steed in several battles. However, the pact was broken when Morathi attempted to use the Manticore as a sacrifice to gain favor with the Chaos Gods. The Manticore, sensing her betrayal, turned on her and fled, but not before cursing her with a mark that would attract other Manticores to attack her.

Modern Interpretations

Tabletop Wargame Warhammer Fantasy Battle Games Workshop 1983-2015 Monster unit for Dark Elves and Chaos armies The Manticore has been a staple of the Dark Elf army since early editions, and its rules have evolved over time. It is often used as a powerful flying monster, but its unpredictable nature (due to the Rage rule) adds a tactical risk. It has been well-received by players for its visual impact and destructive potential. Role-Playing Game Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay Games Workshop / Cubicle 7 1986-present Monster entry in bestiaries In WFRP, the Manticore is presented as a terrifying encounter for player characters, with detailed stats for its venom and attacks. It is often used as a climactic boss monster in adventures, and its rarity makes it a memorable foe. Video Game Total War: Warhammer series Creative Assembly 2016-2024 Flying monster unit for Dark Elves and Chaos The Manticore appears as a recruitable unit in the Total War: Warhammer games, where it is used as a flying shock cavalry unit. It has been praised for its animations and effectiveness in battle, and it is a popular choice for players who want a fast, hard-hitting monster. Novel The Sundering: Malekith Gav Thorpe 2008 Mount for Dark Elf characters In the novel 'Malekith', the Manticore is mentioned as a mount for Dark Elf nobles, and its ferocity is described in detail. The novel expands on the lore of the Dark Elves and their use of monsters, giving the Manticore a more prominent role in the narrative.

Scholarly Debates
Origin of the Manticore
Some sources claim that Manticores are native to the Warhammer world, having evolved naturally in the mountains and forests. ⇌ Other sources suggest that Manticores are the result of Chaos mutation, created by the corrupting influence of the Chaos Wastes or the use of warpstone.
Intelligence of the Manticore
In some lore, Manticores are depicted as cunning and intelligent, capable of understanding commands and even making bargains. ⇌ In other sources, they are portrayed as mindless beasts driven purely by instinct and rage, with no capacity for reasoning.
Relationship with Dark Elves
The Dark Elves are able to tame Manticores through brutal training and dark magic, and the beasts are loyal to their masters. ⇌ Manticores are untamable, and any control is temporary, as they will inevitably turn on their handlers, as seen in the story of Kharaz.
What We Don't Know

There is limited official lore on the Manticore's origins, with most sources focusing on its use as a mount for Dark Elves. The creature's role in other factions, such as the Skaven or Beastmen, is underdeveloped, and there is no canonical explanation for its ability to fly, which is unusual for a creature with a scorpion tail. Additionally, the Manticore's relationship with the Chimera is not fully explored, and it is unclear whether they are related species or separate entities. Further research could delve into the Manticore's appearances in other media, such as the Warhammer Online game or the Black Library novels, to provide a more comprehensive picture.
