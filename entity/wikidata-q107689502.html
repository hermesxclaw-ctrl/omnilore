Who It Is

On the quiet slope above Syrup Village, two men in cat-paw gloves wait to kill a teenage liar — and the larger one, Buchi, is already breathing hard. He is not a devil-fruit monster or a warlord, just a pirate brawler in a striped shirt whose claws are steel, whose courage is borrowed from hypnosis, and whose fifteen minutes against Roronoa Zoro tell you everything about how Eiichiro Oda builds a world: even the henchmen have a gimmick, a backstory, and a hard fall.

Attested: Buchi is presented as lazy, overconfident, and dependent on his partner Sham; the pair fight in tandem and boast about being undefeated. He is loyal to Captain Kuro out of fear and profit, not ideology. Under hypnosis he becomes temporarily fearless, aggressive, and pain-resistant, attacking without self-preservation. Outside hypnosis he shows cowardice when overmatched — after Zoro defeats them, both brothers are left unconscious and abandoned. Powers
Neko-te Cat Claws (Cat Paw Gloves)

Pair of glove-weapons with 3-4 steel claws per hand, worn over hands, used for slashing, raking, and pouncing attacks. The Nyaban Brothers fight with cat-like movements — crawling, pouncing, synchronized slashes.

Limit: Purely physical weapon; no ranged attack, no devil fruit, no haki. Claws can be blocked by swords (Zoro blocks and breaks them). Requires close range and coordination with Sham. Ineffective against superior swordsmen.

Synchronized Duo Combat (Nyaban Brothers Combination)

Buchi and Sham attack as a pair, alternating high/low slashes to overwhelm a single opponent. Stated in-universe as undefeated as a team before Zoro.

Limit: Teamwork collapses if one is incapacitated; both are individually weaker than Zoro. Strategy fails against area attacks or three-sword style.

Hypnotic Enhancement: 1-2 Jango Berserk State

Jango's hypnosis (1-2 Jango, ichi-ni Jango) puts Buchi and Sham into a trance where they ignore pain, fatigue, and fear, attacking with greater speed and ferocity. Visually eyes become blank, speech becomes guttural.

Limit: Not a true power-up; no measurable stat increase beyond pain suppression and morale. Temporary (minutes), requires Jango's command and concentration. Victim is suggestible and can be put to sleep with '1-2 Jango, sleep' (Jango later puts them to sleep). No resistance to stronger hypnotic command. Does not grant durability — Zoro still defeats them in one decisive slash.

Enhanced Agility (Hypnosis-Boosted)

Despite overweight build, hypnotized Buchi can leap, dash, and pounce with surprising speed, keeping up with Zoro briefly.

Limit: Agility is burst-only and uncoordinated; Zoro outmaneuvers with Santoryu. No sustained speed feat beyond this fight. Base (non-hypnotized) Buchi is shown as slow and panting.

Accounts I
The Black Cat Pirates' Three-Year PlotOne Piece manga Vol. 3-4, Chapters 24-37

1998 (in-universe: three years prior to Syrup Village Arc)

Captain Kuro, tired of being hunted, fakes his death, has hypnotist Jango alter a subordinate's memory to take his place for execution, and infiltrates Syrup Village as the wealthy heiress Kaya's butler 'Kurahadol.' For three years he grooms Kaya to write a will leaving everything to him, planning to murder her and have the Black Cat Pirates sack the village. Buchi and Sham remain with the crew aboard the Bezan Black offshore, awaiting the signal to attack. Establishes Buchi's role as loyal enforcer in Kuro's grand deception; explains why a minor brawler is present at Syrup Village. Plan is foiled by Usopp and the Straw Hats.

First Appearance: The Nyaban Brothers UnleashedManga Chapter 27 '1-2 Jango' / Chapter 28 'Crescent Moon'; Anime Episode 14

1998 manga / 1999 anime

As Luffy, Zoro, Nami and Usopp defend Syrup Village's northern slope against the incoming Black Cat Pirates, Kuro orders Sham and Buchi forward as his vanguard. The brothers, in cat-paw gloves, mock Zoro and claim no one has beaten them as a pair. Jango, to ensure victory, hypnotizes them with his pendulum: '1-2 Jango, you become super strong!' Their eyes blank and they lunge with feral speed. Introduces Buchi's only canonical fight and his dependence on hypnosis; sets up Zoro's first serious East Blue duel after Cabaji.

Zoro vs. The Nyaban BrothersManga Chapters 29-30 'The Slope' / 'The Great Captain Usopp'; Anime Episode 15

1998-1999

Zoro faces both brothers alone while Luffy is delayed. Initially pressured by their synchronized claw attacks, Zoro is cut on the shoulder. He then adapts, using two swords to block and one in mouth, and declares the difference in strength. With a single Santoryu technique (later named Tora Gari / Tiger Hunt in databooks), he defeats both, sending them crashing. The hypnosis does not save them; Zoro notes they were only 'a little stronger than before.' Buchi and Sham are incapacitated for the remainder of the arc, proving East Blue pirate grunts are no match for a future Straw Hat swordsman. Fight establishes Zoro's combat superiority and the limits of Jango's hypnosis.

Accounts II
Abandoned by Kuro — The Crew Left BehindManga Chapters 37-39; Anime Episodes 16-17

1998-1999

After Luffy defeats Kuro (with his Shakushi / Cat Claws technique), Kuro attempts to kill his own crew for failing him, declaring pirates who cannot follow orders are useless. Jango puts the still-groggy Buchi and Sham (and others) to sleep with '1-2 Jango, sleep' to protect them from Kuro's indiscriminate attack. The Straw Hats ultimately drive Kuro off. The Black Cat Pirates, including Buchi and Sham, are left unconscious on the hillside as the villagers celebrate. Ends Buchi's active role; crew disbands as a fighting force. Jango's second hypnosis shows coercive control can be used defensively. Buchi does not reappear in main story.

Aftermath and Disappearance — Where Are They Now?Manga cover story 'Jango's Dance Paradise' Chapters 126-128 (cover serial); Databook Vivre Card

1999-2000 (cover serial); 2018 Vivre Card

The cover serial follows Jango after Syrup Village as he becomes a Marine choreographer, but Buchi and Sham do not appear in it or any subsequent cover story (Buggy, Coby, etc.). Vivre Card databook entries confirm their status as former Black Cat Pirates with no new bounty or affiliation. No canonical return, death, or redemption is depicted; they are effectively retired East Blue pirates. Confirms Buchi as a one-arc minor antagonist with no later plot relevance, unlike Jango who recurs. Explains gap in lore — Oda chose not to revisit them.

Live-Action Reimagining: Tell No TalesOne Piece (Netflix) Season 1 Episode 3 'Tell No Tales' (2023), Tomorrow Studios

2023

In the Netflix adaptation, Sham and Buchi are reimagined as Kuro's twin enforcers who infiltrate Kaya's mansion alongside Kuro/Klahadore. They fight Zoro, Nami, and Usopp inside the mansion rather than on the hillside, using realistic claw gloves and coordinated knife work. The hypnosis subplot is removed; instead they are simply elite mercenaries. They are defeated when Zoro and Luffy intervene, and Kuro leaves them behind. Introduces Buchi to a new global audience; demonstrates how Oda's comedic henchmen are adapted for grounded television. Creates a distinct continuity where Buchi's abilities are non-supernatural.

Buchi is a cat Mink or Zoan devil fruit user — Buchi is a normal human wearing cat-themed costume and weapons; no devil fruit, no Mink Tribe heritage (Minks introduced much later in Zou Arc). His cat ears are a headband, claws are gloves.

Buchi and Sham are major recurring villains like Buggy or Alvida — They appear only in Syrup Village Arc (4 chapters/2 episodes) and never return in manga canon. Unlike Jango (who recurs) or Buggy (who becomes Warlord), Buchi has no post-East Blue role.

The name Buchi comes from German Büchi or Igbo Buchi meaning 'is God' — One Piece Buchi's name is Japanese ブチ (spotted cat) pun, unrelated to German surname Büchi (as in Büchi automaton) or Igbo suffix -buchi. Homonym coincidence.

Buchi was put to sleep permanently or killed by Zoro/Kuro — Zoro defeats but does not kill them — they are left unconscious but alive; Jango's second hypnosis puts them to sleep to protect them from Kuro, and they survive. No death is depicted or stated.
