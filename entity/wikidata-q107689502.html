The Approach

On the quiet slope above Syrup Village, two men in cat-paw gloves wait to kill a teenage liar — and the larger one, Buchi, is already breathing hard. He is not a devil-fruit monster or a warlord, just a pirate brawler in a striped shirt whose claws are steel, whose courage is borrowed from hypnosis, and whose fifteen minutes against Roronoa Zoro tell you everything about how Eiichiro Oda builds a world: even the henchmen have a gimmick, a backstory, and a hard fall.

Content advisory. Fantasy violence (claw weapons, sword combat, beating), hypnotic coercion, piracy and attempted murder. No sexual violence. Suitable for teen+ with context.
Identity

Buchi

Pronunciation Japanese: /bu.tɕi/ (boo-chee), two morae bu-chi, pitch accent flat. English dub: /ˈbuːtʃi/ (BOO-chee). Early VIZ and fan translations varied as Butchie/Buchi.

Known As

Nyaban Brothers (with Sham)Buchi of the Black Cat PiratesButchie (early English transliteration)ブチ (Japanese katakana)

Etymology

Japanese ブチ (buchi) commonly means 'spotted, mottled, brindled' — used for calico/spotted cats (buchi-neko). Oda's Nyaban Brothers are cat-themed; the name fits the pattern of cat-pun names in the Black Cat Pirates (Kuro = black, Sham and Buchi = cat coat patterns). No official etymology statement from Oda located; this is linguistic reconstruction, not author-confirmed. Not related to German surname Büchi or Igbo suffix -buchi ('...is God').

Cultural Ownership

Fictional character created by Eiichiro Oda, first published by Shueisha in Weekly Shōnen Jump (Japan). Intellectual property owned by Eiichiro Oda / Shueisha / Toei Animation / Netflix (for live-action). Cultural context is Japanese shōnen manga, not folklore or public domain.

Disputed Identifications

Must be distinguished from: (1) Büchi automaton/arithmetic (Julius Richard Büchi, logic/mathematics), (2) Swiss surname Büchi (Alfred, George, Hernán), (3) Nigerian given name/suffix Buchi (Onyebuchi, etc., Igbo 'is God'), including Buchi Emecheta (writer), Buchi Atuonwu (artist), Buchi (comedian Onyebuchi Ojieh), (4) Japanese bachi (drumsticks) and Filipino butsi/jin deui pastry, (5) Yutaka Izubuchi nickname. Within One Piece, not to be confused with Sham (his partner), with Kuro, Jango, or with Mink Tribe cat-characters. Wikidata Q107689502 resolves to the One Piece character, not the other homonyms.

Description

Appearance

Buchi is the larger, overweight member of the Nyaban Brothers. Manga/anime design: tall (~188 cm per Vivre Card), heavy build, pale skin, small cat-ear headband, painted whiskers and nose, long sharp fingernails extended by metal cat-paw gloves (neko-te) with retractable claws. Wears a blue-and-white horizontally striped long-sleeve shirt, dark pants, simple shoes, and a tail-like belt. Sham is thin and tall; Buchi is broad and round, visual contrast for comedy. After Jango's 1-2 Jango hypnosis, Buchi's eyes become blank/white, muscles appear tensed, and he moves with sudden feral agility despite size. Anime colors match manga cover: shirt light blue/white, gloves beige with pink pads, claws metallic. Live-action 2023 redesigns both brothers as more realistic human assassins in muted cat-themed tactical gear, without exaggerated anime proportions or overt cat makeup, and with practical claw gloves.

Symbols & Iconography

Cat motif throughout Black Cat Pirates: Kuro's ship Bezan Black (Bekuzan Black) has cat figurehead, crew wears cat ears/paws, Jolly Roger is cat skull. Buchi's weapon is the neko-te — traditional Japanese claw weapon reimagined as cat paws, signaling stealth and slashing. Striped shirt evokes both prison stripes and domestic tabby. Hypnosis pendulum and Jango's '1-2 Jango' command are visual symbols of coercive enhancement rather than innate power.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: Buchi is presented as lazy, overconfident, and dependent on his partner Sham; the pair fight in tandem and boast about being undefeated. He is loyal to Captain Kuro out of fear and profit, not ideology. Under hypnosis he becomes temporarily fearless, aggressive, and pain-resistant, attacking without self-preservation. Outside hypnosis he shows cowardice when overmatched — after Zoro defeats them, both brothers are left unconscious and abandoned. No canonical backstory, hometown, or personal motive beyond piracy is given. All personality is inferred from 2-3 chapters of dialogue and combat; no SBS or novel expands him. Behavior is adaptation-specific: manga/anime Buchi is more cartoonish; live-action Buchi is quieter and more menacing.

Domains & Habitats

Affiliation: Black Cat Pirates (Kuro Pirates), East Blue. Base: Bezan Black (ship) anchored off Syrup Village for three years during Kuro's undercover plot as butler Kurahadol to Kaya. Battlefield: the slope/gate leading to Kaya's mansion in Syrup Village, Gecko Islands. No other habitats attested. Post-defeat, cover story 'Jango's Dance Paradise' (Ch. 126 cover) shows Jango, but Buchi/Sham do not reappear in canon cover stories; their whereabouts after Syrup Village are not depicted in manga beyond being left behind. Non-canon filler does not add new habitat.

Version Differences

Manga (primary): Buchi and Sham fight Zoro together, are hypnotized mid-battle, then defeated by Zoro's three-sword style. Anime: extends fight with filler choreography, adds reaction shots, but outcome identical. English manga early VIZ and fan scans used 'Butchie' vs 'Buchi'; current VIZ standard is Buchi. Anime dub pronounces BOO-chee. Live-action (2023): both brothers are leaner, less comedic, fight as a duo against Zoro and Luffy's crew with altered choreography; hypnosis element is downplayed/removed, replaced with simple intimidation. Vivre Card adds databook stats (height, birthday May 3 shared? actually Sham and Buchi share entry) not present in manga dialogue. No devil fruit, haki, or later power-up in any version.

Powers & Abilities

Neko-te Cat Claws (Cat Paw Gloves)

Source VersionManga Ch. 28-29; Anime Ep. 14-15; Databook Blue

Pair of glove-weapons with 3-4 steel claws per hand, worn over hands, used for slashing, raking, and pouncing attacks. The Nyaban Brothers fight with cat-like movements — crawling, pouncing, synchronized slashes.

Limitations

Purely physical weapon; no ranged attack, no devil fruit, no haki. Claws can be blocked by swords (Zoro blocks and breaks them). Requires close range and coordination with Sham. Ineffective against superior swordsmen.

Contradictions

Anime adds spark effects and longer claws for visual flair; manga claws are shorter. Live-action uses practical metal claws that are shorter and less stylized.

Synchronized Duo Combat (Nyaban Brothers Combination)

Source VersionManga Ch. 28 dialogue (Sham: 'We never lose together')

Buchi and Sham attack as a pair, alternating high/low slashes to overwhelm a single opponent. Stated in-universe as undefeated as a team before Zoro.

Limitations

Teamwork collapses if one is incapacitated; both are individually weaker than Zoro. Strategy fails against area attacks or three-sword style.

Contradictions

Databook claims 'perfect combination' but manga shows immediate defeat once hypnosis wears off or Zoro gets serious — hype vs feat discrepancy noted by fans.

Hypnotic Enhancement: 1-2 Jango Berserk State

Source VersionManga Ch. 28; Anime Ep. 14 — Jango swings pendulum and commands '1-2 Jango, you are super strong!'

Jango's hypnosis (1-2 Jango, ichi-ni Jango) puts Buchi and Sham into a trance where they ignore pain, fatigue, and fear, attacking with greater speed and ferocity. Visually eyes become blank, speech becomes guttural.

Limitations

Not a true power-up; no measurable stat increase beyond pain suppression and morale. Temporary (minutes), requires Jango's command and concentration. Victim is suggestible and can be put to sleep with '1-2 Jango, sleep' (Jango later puts them to sleep). No resistance to stronger hypnotic command. Does not grant durability — Zoro still defeats them in one decisive slash.

Contradictions

Fans sometimes misread as superhuman strength; Oda presents it as psychological. Anime exaggerates speed lines; manga is more restrained. Live-action omits this ability entirely, creating continuity divergence.

Enhanced Agility (Hypnosis-Boosted)

Source VersionManga Ch. 29-30

Despite overweight build, hypnotized Buchi can leap, dash, and pounce with surprising speed, keeping up with Zoro briefly.

Limitations

Agility is burst-only and uncoordinated; Zoro outmaneuvers with Santoryu. No sustained speed feat beyond this fight. Base (non-hypnotized) Buchi is shown as slow and panting.

Contradictions

Vivre Card lists no formal agility stat; fan wikis sometimes assign inflated speed tiers without source.

Stories & Myths

The Black Cat Pirates' Three-Year Plot

Source One Piece manga Vol. 3-4, Chapters 24-37Culture Japanese shōnen manga (East Blue Saga)Period 1998 (in-universe: three years prior to Syrup Village Arc)

Summary

Captain Kuro, tired of being hunted, fakes his death, has hypnotist Jango alter a subordinate's memory to take his place for execution, and infiltrates Syrup Village as the wealthy heiress Kaya's butler 'Kurahadol.' For three years he grooms Kaya to write a will leaving everything to him, planning to murder her and have the Black Cat Pirates sack the village. Buchi and Sham remain with the crew aboard the Bezan Black offshore, awaiting the signal to attack.

Zoro vs. The Nyaban Brothers

Source Manga Chapters 29-30 'The Slope' / 'The Great Captain Usopp'; Anime Episode 15Culture Japanese manga/animePeriod 1998-1999

Summary

Zoro faces both brothers alone while Luffy is delayed. Initially pressured by their synchronized claw attacks, Zoro is cut on the shoulder. He then adapts, using two swords to block and one in mouth, and declares the difference in strength. With a single Santoryu technique (later named Tora Gari / Tiger Hunt in databooks), he defeats both, sending them crashing. The hypnosis does not save them; Zoro notes they were only 'a little stronger than before.'

The hypnosis subplot is removed; instead they are simply elite mercenaries. They are defeated when Zoro and Luffy intervene, and Kuro leaves them behind.

Relationships
Buchi→Shamally

Partner and other half of the Nyaban Brothers (Nyaban Burazāzu). The pair are presented as inseparable duo who fight in perfect synchronization, claiming to be undefeated together. Buchi is the heavy, Sham the lean; they share cat motif and weapon type. No familial relation stated — 'brothers' is a team name, not blood.

Buchi→Captain Kuro (Kurahadol / Klahadore)creator

Captain of the Black Cat Pirates and Buchi's captain. Kuro recruited Buchi and Sham as elite vanguard. He views crew as expendable tools; after Buchi/Sham lose to Zoro, Kuro attempts to kill his own men for incompetence and abandons them on Syrup Village.

Buchi→Jangoally

Hypnotist and first mate/strategist of the Black Cat Pirates. Jango enhances Buchi and Sham with 1-2 Jango hypnosis before the Zoro fight, and later puts them to sleep to save them from Kuro's wrath. Relationship is instrumental — Jango controls Buchi's mental state via pendulum.

Buchi→Roronoa Zorohostile

Primary opponent. Zoro defeats Buchi and Sham single-handedly on the Syrup Village slope, cutting through their claw attack with Santoryu. Buchi is Zoro's first named human duo opponent in East Blue, establishing Zoro's superiority over East Blue pirates.

Buchi→Monkey D. Luffy and Usopp (with Nami)hostile

Enemy crew. Luffy is the captain who ultimately defeats Kuro, indirectly causing Buchi's abandonment. Usopp is the Syrup Village defender whose lies ironically prove true; Buchi and Sham were sent to kill Usopp and the villagers. Nami observes the fight and supports Zoro. Buchi never fights Luffy directly in manga.

Buchi→Kaya and Merryvictim

Intended victims of Kuro's plot. Kaya, the sickly heiress, and Merry, her loyal servant, were to be murdered after signing over estate; Buchi and Sham as part of the crew would have participated in sacking the village and killing them. They never directly interact with Buchi, but are the beneficiaries of his defeat.

Buchi→Black Cat Pirates (crew collective: Nuggy, etc.)ally

Buchi belongs to the ~30-member Black Cat Pirates, known for cat theme and stealth tactics. Other members include the brothers' subordinates who carry torches and clubs. After Syrup Village, crew effectively disbands; some members appear in Jango's cover story as civilians.

Conspiracy to murder a teenage heiress and massacre a village

Source Manga Ch. 24-25 (Kuro explains plan to Jango); Ch. 37-38

Description

Kuro's three-year plan, executed with Buchi and Sham as enforcers, involved grooming 17-year-old Kaya (age per Vivre Card) to sign over her inheritance, then murdering her and allowing the crew to pillage Syrup Village, implied to include killing villagers and servants. Buchi is complicit as crew member awaiting the order to attack.

Scholarly Context

One Piece East Blue arcs frequently deal with predatory piracy vs vulnerable communities; Oda frames this as unequivocal villainy, with Usopp's community defense as heroic response. No sexual violence in this plot.

Hypnotic coercion and bodily autonomy

Source Manga Ch. 27-28, Ch. 37

Description

Jango's 1-2 Jango hypnosis overrides Buchi and Sham's will, forcing them into a berserk state without informed consent and later forcing them into sleep to avoid Kuro's attack. While played comically, it depicts non-consensual mental manipulation of subordinates.

Scholarly Context

Oda treats hypnosis as comedic stage trick, but real-world hypnotic coercion raises consent issues; the narrative does not interrogate it, presenting Jango as goofy rather than abusive.

Intra-crew violence and abandonment

Source Manga Ch. 37-38

Description

After defeat, Kuro declares his crew worthless and attempts to kill them himself with his Shakushi claws, forcing Jango to hypnotize Buchi/Sham to sleep to save them. Kuro then flees alone, abandoning wounded subordinates on the hillside.

Scholarly Context

Depicts pirate hierarchy as exploitative; Oda uses Kuro's betrayal to contrast with Luffy's loyalty to crew, a core One Piece theme.

Contradictions & Disputes

Name transliteration: Buchi vs Butchie vs Buchi

Position A

Correct romanization is Buchi (ブチ)

Position A — evidence

Japanese katakana ブチ is Hepburn 'Buchi'; VIZ current edition, Vivre Card, and One Piece official English site use Buchi. Oda's romanization in Vivre Card is BUCHI.

Position B

Early English sources use Butchie / Butchi

Position B — evidence

1990s fan scans and early VIZ single chapters used Butchie (adding -ie to anglicize), and some dubs subtitled Butchi. Anime closed captions varied.

Scholarly Consensus

Buchi is now standard; Butchie is deprecated fan/early-translation variant. No dispute in Japanese.

Are Sham and Buchi actual brothers?

Position A

They are literal brothers

Position A — evidence

English phrase 'brothers' suggests family; some fan wikis list as siblings.

Position B

'Nyaban Brothers' is a team name, not blood relation

Position B — evidence

Manga never states blood relation, no shared surname, no family backstory; Oda often uses 'Brothers' for duos (e.g., Funk Brothers) without blood tie. Databook lists them separately without family note.

Scholarly Consensus

Attested status is 'duo/partners'; blood relation is unknown/unconfirmed — should be labeled as team, not family.

Effect of Jango's hypnosis — real power boost or placebo?

Position A

Hypnosis grants superhuman strength/speed

Position A — evidence

Anime visual effects show aura/speed lines; characters shout 'so strong!'

Position B

Hypnosis only removes fear/pain, no real stat boost

Position B — evidence

Manga dialogue: Zoro says 'you're a little stronger but still weak' (Ch. 30), and Jango's hypnosis is consistently portrayed as suggestion (e.g., making a man think he's a dog). Oda's SBS has described Jango's power as psychological.

Scholarly Consensus

Consensus among databooks and scholarly reading: psychological boost, not supernatural. Anime exaggeration is adaptation-specific.

Live-action vs manga canon — does Buchi have hypnosis?

Position A

Buchi's power includes hypnotic enhancement (canon)

Position A — evidence

Manga Ch. 28 and anime Ep. 14 explicitly show hypnosis.

Position B

Live-action Buchi has no hypnosis, is just a skilled fighter

Position B — evidence

Netflix Episode 3 (2023) omits hypnosis entirely; Oda approved change for realism.

Scholarly Consensus

Both are valid within their continuities; manga is primary canon, live-action is secondary adaptation continuity. Must not merge.
