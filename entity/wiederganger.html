Who It Is

The Wiedergänger (literally 'again-walker') is the medieval Germanic revenant — a corpse that rises from the grave and physically returns to haunt the living. Unlike the transparent specter, the Wiedergänger is a material, rotting body that batters down doors, attacks family members, spreads plague, consumes livestock, and — in some accounts — drinks blood. Church chronicles record waves of Wiedergänger panics, particularly in times of plague or famine, when unusual deaths were attributed to the malevolent dead. The causes of becoming a Wiedergänger included dying by violence, suicide, dying unbaptized, dying excommunicated, or having been a particularly wicked person in life. Methods of disposal were brutal: decapitation, staking through the heart, placing a stone or coin in the mouth, or dismembering the corpse and burning the pieces. The medieval Wiedergänger is a crucial link in the chain of European undead belief — bridging the Old Norse draugr with the early modern vampire panics of Eastern Europe. The term 'Nachzehrer' (after-devourer) overlaps, describing a corpse that 'eats' its burial shroud and sympathetically consumes the life-force of its living relatives. These medieval revenants demonstrate that the 'living dead' was not an 18th-century vampire import but a deeply rooted Germanic folk belief, documented in texts like William of Newburgh's 'Historia Rerum Anglicarum' (which records similar revenants in England) and the 'Chronicon' of Thietmar of Merseburg.
