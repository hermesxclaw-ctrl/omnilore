Who It Is

When the Argonauts set sail to steal the Golden Fleece, the poet Apollonius paused mid-story to call on a Muse who could make hearts race: Erato, the goddess of desire made verse. Her name is a promise—'the lovely one'—and in her hands, a lyre could turn longing into song. Yet for all her power over passion, her own story is a puzzle of fragments: a bride given to a mortal king, a mother of a princess whose daughter became a star-crossed lover, and a silent presence in the halls of Olympus. She is the Muse who inspires love stories, but the greatest mystery is her own.

As a Muse, Erato inspires poets and musicians, particularly those who write about love. In the Orphic hymn, she 'charms the sight'. She is not described with a distinct personality in myths; her role is functional.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Ancient Greek Ἐρατώ (Eratō), derived from ἐρατός (eratos) meaning 'desired' or 'lovely'. Apollonius of Rhodes playfully connects it to Eros, the god of love, in Argonautica 3.

Pronunciation — /ˈɛrətoʊ/ (English), /e.ra.tɔ̌ː/ (Classical Greek)

Aliases — Erato

Ownership — Ancient Greek religion and mythology; later adopted by Roman culture (as one of the Muses).

Powers
Inspiration

As a Muse, Erato inspires poets and musicians, granting them the ability to compose beautiful and moving works, particularly in the realm of love poetry.

Limit: Not explicitly defined; Muses are generally sources of inspiration, not direct causal agents.

Charm of Sight

In the Orphic Hymn to the Muses, Erato is said to 'charm the sight', suggesting a power to enchant viewers through visual beauty or artistic representation.

Limit: Not elaborated; likely metaphorical.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Since the Renaissance, Erato is typically depicted with a wreath of myrtle and roses, holding a lyre or a small kithara. In some depictions, she holds a golden arrow, symbolizing the 'eros' she inspires. Occasionally, she is accompanied by Eros holding a torch. In Simon Vouet's paintings, two turtle-doves eat seeds at her feet.

Symbols — Myrtle and roses (associated with love), lyre or kithara (musical instrument of Apollo), golden arrow (symbol of desire), turtle-doves (birds of love), torch (carried by Eros).

Domains — Resides on Mount Olympus with the other Muses. Her domain is erotic lyric poetry and mimic imitation (as per some sources).

Accounts I
Theogony: Birth of the MusesHesiod, Theogony 53-79

Archaic (c. 700 BCE)

Hesiod recounts that the Muses were born to Zeus and Mnemosyne in Pieria. They are nine sisters who delight in song and dance, and they inspire kings and poets. Erato is listed among them. Establishes Erato as one of the nine Muses, daughter of Zeus and Mnemosyne.

Invocation in the ArgonauticaApollonius of Rhodes, Argonautica 3.1-5

3rd century BCE

Apollonius invokes Erato at the start of Book III, asking her to tell of the love of Jason and Medea. He playfully suggests her name is derived from Eros. Associates Erato specifically with erotic poetry and the telling of love stories.

Accounts II
Invocation in the AeneidVirgil, Aeneid 7.37

1st century BCE

Virgil invokes Erato at the beginning of the second half of the Aeneid, asking her to help him sing of the war in Italy. This marks a shift to the 'Iliadic' section. Shows Erato's broader association with epic poetry, not just love, in Roman tradition.

The Story of RhadineStrabo, Geography 8.3.20 (quoting lost poem)

Archaic or Classical (uncertain)

A lost poem 'Rhadine' began with an invocation to Erato. The story, as referenced by Strabo and Pausanias, involves Rhadine and her lover, and their tomb on Samos became a pilgrimage site for star-crossed lovers. Cemented Erato's association with tragic love stories.

Erato as Bride of MalusPseudo-Apollodorus, Bibliotheca 1.3.3

1st-2nd century CE (text)

According to Pseudo-Apollodorus, Zeus gave Erato to Malus, eponym of Malea, as a bride. She bore him a daughter, Cleophema, who later bore Aegle (Coronis) by Phlegyas. Provides a rare genealogical story for Erato, linking her to mortal kings and a tragic lineage.
