Who It Is

Party Pat just wants to keep the party going forever — even if that means dancing non-stop inside the belly of a god who might eat him. In Adventure Time's Land of Ooo, he is the most anxious hedonist you'll ever meet: a tiny bear in a party hat who believes that if the music stops, everyone dies.

Attested: Party Pat is anxious, earnest, and charismatically frantic. He is the de facto spokesperson for the Party Bears, explaining their predicament to Finn and Jake. He genuinely believes that ceasing to party will cause the Party God to awaken and kill them, and he enforces non-stop dancing out of fear rather than joy. He is hospitable (invites Finn and Jake to party) but also fatalistic and peer-pressuring. He shows relief when Finn and Jake propose a solution, and participates in the escape. Rave Endurance and Ritual Dancing

Ability to dance and party continuously for extended periods without rest, as part of a ritual to keep the Party God docile. Party Pat leads chants and maintains rhythm.

Limit: Clearly limited by exhaustion, dehydration, and despair; bears are shown collapsing, sweating, and begging for relief. Ritual fails if music stops — Party God stirs. No supernatural stamina beyond comedic cartoon endurance.

Social Persuasion / Hosting

Charismatic ability to recruit newcomers (Finn and Jake) into the party and explain the group's mythology convincingly.

Limit: Persuasion is not mind control; Finn and Jake question and ultimately reject the endless-party solution. Depends on shared fear of Party God.

Survival Inside Gastric Environment

Survives inside the belly of a giant monster with limited protection from digestive acid, implying cartoon-logic resistance to digestion.

Limit: Not immunity: characters actively avoid acid pools and express fear of being digested; survival is situational, not a power. No attested resistance if submerged.

Belly of the Beast — The Party That Never EndsAdventure Time Season 2, Episode 20 'Belly of the Beast' — Written & storyboarded by Tom Herpich and Bert Youn; Directed by Larry Leichliter; Aired 2011-04-21 on Cartoon Network

2011 (production 2010-2011)

Finn and Jake follow a trail of music to a forest clearing where a tribe of Party Bears, led by spokesperson Party Pat (voiced by Andy Samberg), are raving non-stop. Party Pat explains they must keep partying or the Party God will awaken and destroy them. Finn and Jake join the party, only to discover the clearing is actually inside the belly of a gigantic, sleeping bear-like creature — the Party God itself — whose stomach they have been partying inside. When the music stops, the creature stirs and growls, confirming the danger. Finn and Jake realize the bears' strategy is unsustainable. They devise an escape: they irritate the creature's stomach lining to make it vomit, expelling everyone. The Party Bears are freed to the outside world, and the Party God wanders off. The episode ends with Finn and Jake reflecting on the bears' misguided faith. In-universe: Party Bears are freed from the belly; Party God is revealed to be a biological creature rather than a metaphysical deity; Finn and Jake reinforce their heroic problem-solving role. The Party God Mythos — False Deity in OooAdventure Time S2E20 'Belly of the Beast' dialogue and series bible; supplementary material in 'Adventure Time Encyclopedia' (2013)

2011-2013

Within Ooo, the Party Bears have constructed a theology around the Party God: a slumbering giant whose wrath is held at bay only by eternal celebration. Party Pat acts as priest-explainer of this theology. The episode deconstructs the myth: the 'god' is a large, non-sapient animal that swallowed the bears while they were partying, and its 'divine anger' is simply its digestive reflex. The story functions as a parable about cargo-cult religion and anxiety-driven ritual, a recurring Adventure Time motif alongside other cults (e.g., the followers of the Lich, the worship of Grob Gob Glob Grod). Provides world-building that Ooo contains localized, naive religions arising after the Mushroom War; informs later scholarly readings of Adventure Time as post-apocalyptic folklore. No further canonical elaboration of Party God worship appears in later seasons.

Accounts II
Casting Andy Samberg — SNL Star Joins OooCartoon Network press release and interviews: The A.V. Club 'Adventure Time guest stars' (2011), Andy Samberg interview excerpts; episode credits

2010-2011 (casting and recording)

For Season 2, the crew sought a guest voice who could convey manic, lovable panic. Andy Samberg, then a cast member on Saturday Night Live and member of The Lonely Island, was cast as Party Pat. Recording was done in the show's characteristic group session style where actors record together to capture natural dialogue. Samberg's improvisational energy shaped Party Pat's rapid, slightly desperate line readings. The casting was announced alongside other Season 2 guests including Martin Olson, Peter Stormare, and Biz Markie, signaling the show's growing ability to attract comedy talent. Helped cement Adventure Time's reputation as a comedian-friendly show; Samberg later returned to voice other roles? No — Party Pat remains his sole Adventure Time credit, but the collaboration is cited in retrospectives of the show's guest-star strategy.

Production of Season 2 Under Constraint — 'Belly of the Beast' in the PipelineFrederator blog post by Eric Homan (2010) and Cartoon Network Studios production notes; Season 2 DVD liner notes

2010-2011

Season 2 was ordered quickly after Season 1's success, with 26 eleven-minute segments produced simultaneously at Cartoon Network Studios (Burbank) and animated overseas by Rough Draft Korea and Saerom Animation. 'Belly of the Beast' was produced mid-season (production code 1008-049) while the premiere 'It Came from the Nightosphere' was still being finished. Story outlines of 2-3 pages were expanded into full storyboards by Herpich and Youn. The episode's belly interior required distinctive fleshy background design and acid-pool effects, handled by the background paint team. Demonstrates the pipeline that allowed Adventure Time to maintain weekly broadcast despite overseas animation delays; the episode is an example of the show's ability to produce body-horror comedy within TV-PG constraints.

Afterlife in Print — Party Bears in the Encyclopedia and Comics CultureAdventure Time Encyclopedia (written by Martin Olson, 2013, Abrams Books) and KaBOOM! Adventure Time comics line (2012-2018)

2013-2018

Although Party Pat does not headline a comic issue, the Party Bears and Party God are catalogued in the official Encyclopedia as an example of Ooo's 'micro-cults,' with a dedicated entry summarizing 'Belly of the Beast.' The KaBOOM! comics, which expand Ooo with new adventures, have not given Party Pat a starring issue as of the cutoff date, but the Party Bears as a species appear in background cameos and are listed in fan-compiled indices. This print afterlife keeps the one-episode character discoverable for new viewers and scholars. Provides the only official print record of Party Pat beyond the episode itself; ensures the character is preserved in reference works despite single appearance.
