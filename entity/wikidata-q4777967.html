Who It Is

Imagine a god so obscure that even the Romans, who dutifully recorded every local deity they encountered, left us only a single whisper of his name. Anvallus is that whisper — a name carved into stone two thousand years ago, a name that scholars can translate but cannot explain. He is the ultimate blank slate of the Celtic pantheon: a deity who exists, but about whom we know almost nothing. No myths, no temples, no statues, no worshippers' prayers — just a name that hints at strength or power, and a mystery that has survived the collapse of empires. Anvallus is the god of what we don't know, a reminder that the ancient world is full of voices we can hear but never fully understand.

Unknown. No mythological narratives, cult practices, or behavioral descriptions of Anvallus survive. The deity's personality, temperament, and sphere of action can only be inferred from the name's possible etymology (if the 'strength' or 'power' root is correct) and from general patterns of Celtic deity worship during the Roman period, which often syncretized local gods with Roman counterparts.
