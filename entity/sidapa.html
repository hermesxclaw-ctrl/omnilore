Archive Dossier — Identity

Sidapa

Pronunciation see-DAH-pah

Etymology

The name 'Sidapa' is of Visayan origin, but its precise linguistic roots are uncertain. Some scholars suggest a connection to the concept of 'measuring' or 'determining', fitting his role as a god of fate and death.

Cultural Ownership

The Visayan people of the Philippines, particularly those of the Panay and Negros islands, claim Sidapa as part of their indigenous belief system. His worship is pre-colonial and has been transmitted through oral tradition and early Spanish colonial accounts.

Disputed Identifications

Some modern sources conflate Sidapa with other deities of death or fate, such as the Tagalog god Sitan or the Visayan god Magwayen. However, these identifications are speculative and not supported by primary evidence.

Archive Dossier — Approach

In the highlands of the Visayas, where the spirits of the dead journey across the seas, there stands a god who measures the length of every human life. Sidapa, the arbiter of fate, is said to reside atop Mount Madja-as, watching over the threads of mortality. His story, though sparse in surviving records, reveals a profound connection between death, destiny, and the divine order of the ancient Filipino world.

The tree knows every life by its branch. When the branch reaches its full length, I come. Not as a punishment — as a completion.

Who It Is

Sidapa is the Visayan god of death who determines the moment of every person's death by measuring their life with a magical tree on Mount Madia-as. According to myth, a sacred tree grows on the summit of Mount Madia-as, and for every person born, a branch sprouts on this tree. When the branch reaches a certain length, the person dies — and Sidapa is the one who measures it. He works closely with Magwayen, who ferries souls to Sulad, the underworld. Sidapa is not an evil god — he is an impartial arbiter of fate, neither punishing nor rewarding but simply measuring. Some accounts describe him as a handsome, well-built man who wears a loincloth (bahag) and carries a knife. His role is purely administrative: every soul has its time, and Sidapa knows it before anyone else. In pre-colonial Visayan society, Sidapa was deeply respected — people offered him prayers and small sacrifices, not to avoid death but to request a peaceful passing at the appointed time. The concept of a fixed, measured lifespan predates Spanish influence and shows how ancient Filipinos understood mortality as a natural, orderly process.

Archive Dossier — Description

Appearance

No specific physical description of Sidapa survives in historical sources. He is typically represented in modern artistic interpretations as a stern, elderly man, often holding a staff or a measuring device, symbolizing his role as the arbiter of life spans.

Symbols & Iconography

Sidapa is symbolically associated with the act of measuring, often depicted with a scale or a thread. His dwelling on Mount Madja-as is a significant symbol, as mountains are often considered sacred in Filipino indigenous religions.

Behaviour & Personality

Sidapa is believed to be a just but impartial deity, who determines the exact length of each person's life at birth. He is not malevolent but rather a neutral executor of cosmic order.

Domains & Habitats

Sidapa resides on Mount Madja-as in Panay, which is considered his sacred abode. He is also associated with the realm of the dead, where souls go after death, though his specific role there is not clearly defined.

Version Differences

There is minimal variation in the descriptions of Sidapa across sources, as the surviving accounts are few. Some modern retellings may add details not found in primary sources, which should be treated with caution.

Archive Dossier — Powers

Determination of Lifespan

Source Version Attested in the accounts of Loarca and Alcina.

Description

Sidapa is said to measure the length of a person's life at the moment of their birth, using a symbolic thread or a scale. This act determines the exact moment of their death.

Limitations

No limitations are recorded, but it is implied that his decisions are absolute and cannot be altered by other deities or mortals.

Contradictions

None recorded.

Authority over Death

Source Version Inferred from his classification as a death deity in modern scholarship.

Description

As a death deity, Sidapa presides over the transition from life to death, though the specifics of his role in the afterlife are not well-documented.

Limitations

The extent of his authority is unclear; he may share this domain with other deities like Magwayen.

Contradictions

Some sources attribute the role of guiding souls to Magwayen, while Sidapa is more focused on the moment of death itself.

Magwayen

Type associated deitySource Modern scholarly interpretations of Visayan mythology.

Description

Magwayen is another Visayan deity, often associated with the sea and the afterlife. The relationship between Sidapa and Magwayen is not clearly defined, but they may share responsibilities in the realm of death.

Version Note

No primary source explicitly links Sidapa and Magwayen.

Conflicting Accounts

Some sources suggest Magwayen is the primary guide of souls, while Sidapa determines the time of death. Their exact interaction is unknown.
