Who It Is

In the highlands of the Visayas, where the spirits of the dead journey across the seas, there stands a god who measures the length of every human life. Sidapa, the arbiter of fate, is said to reside atop Mount Madja-as, watching over the threads of mortality. His story, though sparse in surviving records, reveals a profound connection between death, destiny, and the divine order of the ancient Filipino world.

Sidapa is believed to be a just but impartial deity, who determines the exact length of each person's life at birth. He is not malevolent but rather a neutral executor of cosmic order.

Powers
Determination of Lifespan

Sidapa is said to measure the length of a person's life at the moment of their birth, using a symbolic thread or a scale. This act determines the exact moment of their death.

Limit: No limitations are recorded, but it is implied that his decisions are absolute and cannot be altered by other deities or mortals.

Authority over Death

As a death deity, Sidapa presides over the transition from life to death, though the specifics of his role in the afterlife are not well-documented.

Limit: The extent of his authority is unclear; he may share this domain with other deities like Magwayen.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No specific physical description of Sidapa survives in historical sources. He is typically represented in modern artistic interpretations as a stern, elderly man, often holding a staff or a measuring device, symbolizing his role as the arbiter of life spans.

Symbols — Sidapa is symbolically associated with the act of measuring, often depicted with a scale or a thread. His dwelling on Mount Madja-as is a significant symbol, as mountains are often considered sacred in Filipino indigenous religions.

Domains — Sidapa resides on Mount Madja-as in Panay, which is considered his sacred abode. He is also associated with the realm of the dead, where souls go after death, though his specific role there is not clearly defined.

Accounts I
The Measurement of LivesMiguel de Loarca, Relacion de las Islas Filipinas

Pre-colonial, recorded in 1582

According to Loarca, the Visayans believed that when a child was born, Sidapa would measure the child's life with a thread or a scale, determining the exact number of years they would live. This belief underscored the inevitability of fate and the power of the gods. This belief instilled a sense of fatalism among the Visayans, as they accepted that their lifespan was predetermined and could not be changed.

Accounts II
Dwelling on Mount Madja-asFrancisco Ignacio Alcina, Historia de las islas e indios de Bisayas

Pre-colonial, recorded in 1668

Alcina mentions that Sidapa was believed to live on Mount Madja-as, a sacred mountain in Panay. This location was considered a dwelling place of gods and a gateway to the spirit world. Mount Madja-as became a site of reverence and pilgrimage for the Visayans, who believed that the gods watched over them from its peak.
