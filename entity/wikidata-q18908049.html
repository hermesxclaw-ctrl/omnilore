Who It Is

He is the only man in Division 01 who remembers policing without the Sibyl System — a scarred, one-armed old detective who chose to become a hunting dog so his son would never have to know his name.

Attested: Calm, laconic, paternal, and wryly humorous. Acts as the emotional anchor and moral mediator of Division 01. Deeply respectful of Akane Tsunemori's idealism, treating her as a surrogate daughter. Harbors a dry, self-deprecating wit about his age and status as a 'hound'. Shows immense patience and deductive caution, preferring observation, interrogation, and analogue reasoning over reliance on Sibyl's cymatic scans. Powers
Veteran Detective Intuition and Deductive Reasoning

Decades of pre-Sibyl investigative experience. Able to read crime scenes, notice analogue clues (footprints, smell of tobacco, paper books) that Sibyl-reliant colleagues miss. In Episode 2 he correctly deduces the victim's occupation from calluses and ink stains without scanner assistance.

Limit: Not superhuman; relies on experience and is sometimes slower than Sibyl's instant analysis. His Crime Coefficient remains high, so he cannot wield a Dominator as Inspector.

Hand-to-Hand Combat and Firearms Proficiency

Trained police combatant. Effective with standard-issue Dominator (in Enforcer mode, Lethal Eliminator) and with conventional firearms when available. Physically strong enough to restrain younger suspects despite age. His prosthetic arm grants enhanced grip strength and can be used as a blunt weapon or shield.

Limit: Age and prosthetic maintenance are vulnerabilities; prosthetic requires charging and can be damaged by EMP or severe trauma. Not on par with Kogami or Makishima in peak physical duels.

Interpersonal Mediation and Mentorship

Social ability rather than power: de-escalates conflicts between Kogami and Ginoza, guides Akane's growth as Inspector. His paternal presence stabilizes Division 01's latent-criminal/inspector hierarchy.

Limit: Cannot override Sibyl's systemic authority; his advice is often ignored by the system itself.

Latent Criminal Status - Dominator Restriction

As a latent criminal (潜在犯), his Crime Coefficient is permanently above 100 (measured 127.9+), meaning he is legally a hound rather than a holder of the Dominator's Paralyzer mode. He can only fire when authorized by an Inspector's Dominator.

Limit: Cannot act independently with Dominator; legally confined to Bureau facility when off-duty; subject to therapy and monitoring.

Accounts I
The Last Old Detective - From Detective to EnforcerPsycho-Pass Season 1 Episode 6, Episode 12 dialogue; Official Profiling backstory

In-universe c. 2090s-2112; Broadcast 2012

Before the series, Tomomi Masaoka served as a top Detective in the Metropolitan Police, working alongside a young Joji Saiga (later profiler). As Sibyl's judgments became absolute, cumulative stress and disillusionment clouded his hue. Unlike those who commit a crime, his Crime Coefficient rose gradually from the pressure of the job itself, reclassifying him as a latent criminal. Rather than undergo therapy/exile, he accepted demotion to Enforcer, choosing to remain a policeman even as a hound. He deliberately concealed his identity from his young son, Nobuchika, allowing his wife to raise the boy under her family name Ginoza to protect him from stigma. Establishes Masaoka's core tragedy and thematic role: the man who remembers justice before algorithms, and who sacrifices fatherhood to remain on the force. Explains his estrangement from Ginoza.

Division 01's Anchor - The Akane Tsunemori InductionPsycho-Pass Season 1 Episodes 1-4

2112-2113; Broadcast 2012

When 20-year-old idealist Akane Tsunemori joins Division 01 as its new Inspector, Masaoka is the first Enforcer to treat her with gentle respect rather than testing or condescension. He explains the realities of Enforcers vs Inspectors, warns her not to rely solely on Sibyl, and demonstrates analogue investigation at the crime scene of the drone-manufacturing victim. He quietly covers for her hesitation to fire the Dominator, buying her time to make her own judgment. Builds Akane's trust in Enforcers and positions Masaoka as her surrogate father figure, contrasting with Ginoza's rigid adherence to Sibyl.

The Helmet Riots and the Spooky Boogie CasePsycho-Pass Season 1 Episodes 5-8 (Talisman / Spooky Boogie arc)

2112; Broadcast 2012

During Shogo Makishima's distribution of Sibyl-jamming helmets that turn wearers criminally asymptomatic, Division 01 investigates artist Rikako Oryo. Masaoka provides crucial analogue insight — recognizing that Oryo's plastic-body art references classic guro aesthetics and that her victims were drugged before being dismembered, something scanners missed because helmets returned clear hues. He physically protects Akane when a helmeted mob attacks the Bureau's transport. Demonstrates limits of Sibyl and value of Masaoka's pre-Sibyl knowledge; foreshadows Makishima's larger plan to expose Sibyl's flaw.

Accounts II
Reconciliation with Ginoza - The Father RevealedPsycho-Pass Season 1 Episodes 10-13

2113; Broadcast 2013

Mid-season, Akane and Kogami discover Masaoka is Ginoza's estranged father. Ginoza has spent his career trying to avoid becoming like his father — obsessively keeping his hue clear, despising Enforcers. Masaoka never reveals himself directly, but in Episode 11, after Ginoza is wounded and his hue begins to cloud, Masaoka tends to him in the infirmary and finally speaks plainly: he became an Enforcer so Ginoza would not be burdened, and he is proud of him regardless of hue. The two begin a tentative, unspoken reconciliation, working side-by-side without formal acknowledgment. Central emotional arc of Season 1; Ginoza's later character development hinges on this relationship. Masaoka's paternal love humanizes the Enforcer system.

The Hunt for Makishima and Death in the Wheat FieldPsycho-Pass Season 1 Episodes 14-16

2113; Broadcast 2013

After Makishima kidnaps Akane's friend Yuki Funahara and escapes, Division 01 pursues him to an abandoned agricultural zone. Masaoka, Kogami, and Ginoza corner Makishima. In Episode 16 'The Gates of Judgment,' Masaoka engages Makishima in close combat to protect his son and colleagues. Makishima, who is criminally asymptomatic (Sibyl cannot judge him), uses a conventional shotgun and a knife. Masaoka's prosthetic arm is damaged, and Makishima fatally slashes his throat/abdomen with a straight razor. He dies in the field, his last words urging Ginoza to live — 'You... become a good...'. His death is the first major loss for Division 01 and triggers Ginoza's hue to finally cloud permanently, demoting Ginoza to Enforcer in turn. Masaoka's death is the series' pivotal sacrifice, completing the father-son cycle and exposing that even Sibyl's hounds cannot stop an asymptomatic criminal with old weapons. Ginoza inherits his father's position literally.

Posthumous Legacy - The Coat and the Enforcer's PathPsycho-Pass Season 1 Episodes 17-22; Psycho-Pass Season 2 Episode 1; Psycho-Pass: The Movie (2015)

2113-2114; Broadcast 2013-2015

After his death, Masaoka's influence persists. Ginoza, now an Enforcer with clouded hue and wearing his father's old coat and glasses, explicitly takes on Masaoka's role as Division 01's veteran anchor. Akane keeps Masaoka's lessons about not trusting Sibyl blindly, which informs her eventual confrontation with the Sibyl System itself. Photographs of Masaoka appear in the Bureau memorial and in Ginoza's quarters. In the film and Sinners of the System, Ginoza's character design (haircut, stubble, coat) is a direct visual inheritance. Establishes generational theme: the old detective lives on through his son, and analogue justice persists within the system.

Masaoka is Ginoza's uncle or mentor, not his father. — He is Ginoza's biological father. The series deliberately hides this until Episode 11, leading early viewers to assume mentor relationship. Official Profiling and Episode 11 explicitly confirm father-son.

Masaoka was a criminal who earned his latent criminal status by committing a crime. — He never committed a crime; his Crime Coefficient rose gradually from cumulative stress and disillusionment as a detective under Sibyl, a common path for Enforcers. The series emphasizes this systemic injustice.

Masaoka survives and appears in Psycho-Pass Season 2 or the movie. — He dies definitively in Season 1 Episode 16 and does not appear alive thereafter. Later appearances are flashbacks, photographs, or Ginoza wearing his coat as inheritance.

His prosthetic arm gives him superhuman strength or is a Sibyl-controlled weapon. — It is a standard high-output prosthetic with enhanced grip but not superhuman combat power; it is not linked to Sibyl and requires manual maintenance. He still loses a close-quarters fight to Makishima.
