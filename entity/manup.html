Who It Is

Manup is the brother rival of Kilibob in the dualistic mythology of the Sepik River people of Papua New Guinea. In the foundational myth of the Sepik coastal and inland peoples, Manup and Kilibob were brothers (or two original beings) who traveled the land, creating the world's features and establishing human culture. Manup represents the inland, the sky, and the people of the upper Sepik, while Kilibob represents the coast, the sea, and the people of the river's mouth. According to the myth, the two brothers originally shared one language and one culture, but a quarrel — triggered by jealousy over a woman, a fish, or a taro garden (depending on the version) — led them to separate. Manup went inland, where he became the ancestor of the bush-dwelling people, while Kilibob went to the coast. Their conflict shattered the original unity, causing the diversification of languages and customs. In some versions, Manup was tricked by Kilibob into causing the first death, which created the cycle of mortality. Manup is associated with the sky, rain, and mountain spirits. His relationship with Kilibob embodies the fundamental dualities of Sepik cosmology: coast vs. inland, saltwater vs. freshwater, sea vs. sky, chaos vs. order. The Manup-Kilibob cycle explains the extraordinary linguistic and cultural diversity of the Sepik region (over 100 languages in a single river basin) and provides a moral framework for understanding human conflict and cultural difference.
