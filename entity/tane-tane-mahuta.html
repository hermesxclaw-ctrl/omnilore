Who It Is

Tāne (Tāne-mahuta) is the Māori god of forests, birds, and all living creatures — one of the most important deities in the Māori pantheon. He is the son of Rangi-nui (Sky Father) and Papa-tū-ā-nuku (Earth Mother). When Rangi and Papa were locked in an eternal embrace, Tāne alone had the strength to separate them, pushing his parents apart with his shoulders while lying on his mother and pushing up with his legs. This act created the world of light (Te Ao Mārama). Tāne is also credited with creating the first woman, Hine-wahine (or Hine-hau-one), from the clay of the earth, breathing life into her nostrils. She bore his children, and their daughter Hine-tītama fled to the underworld when she learned her father was also her husband. Tāne brought knowledge to humanity, specifically the three baskets of knowledge (te kete tuauri, te kete tuatea, te kete aronui) from the heavens. He is associated with the sun, the stars (especially the sun as a star that Tāne hung in the sky), and the insect world. In Hawaii, he is Kāne, one of the four great gods.

Origins

Tāne is the son of Rangi-nui (Sky Father) and Papa-tū-ā-nuku (Earth Mother). He and his brothers lived in darkness between their parents. Tāne tried to separate them, failing initially, then succeeded by lying on his back and pushing with his legs. He then created the first woman and obtained the three baskets of knowledge.
