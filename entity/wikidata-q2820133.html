Who It Is

Imagine the night sky before the moon existed — a vast, unbroken darkness. Then, a primordial hunter, Abaangui, makes a choice that will light the world forever: he cuts off his own nose and hurls it into the heavens. That severed nose becomes the silver disc that has guided travelers, inspired lovers, and marked time for countless generations. This is not a story of a god reaching into the void; it's a story of a being who gives a piece of himself to create something beautiful.

Abaangui is depicted as a powerful, primordial ancestor or deity who is capable of creating celestial bodies. He is not a trickster, but a creator figure whose actions are direct and world-shaping. He is also depicted as a hunter, which is a common motif in Guaraní mythology.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name's etymology is not definitively established in available sources. It is a proper name from the Guaraní language family, but its root meaning is unclear. Some interpretations suggest a connection to the moon or to a primordial being, but this is speculative.

Pronunciation — Ah-bahn-GWEE (approximate). The 'g' is hard, as in 'go'. The 'ui' is a single diphthong, like 'wee'.

Aliases — Abaanguy, Abaangüí, Abaangui (Moon God), The Moon

Ownership — Guaraní people (indigenous peoples of Paraguay, Brazil, Argentina, and Bolivia). The myth is part of the broader Guaraní creation mythology.

Names are cult layers, not one fixed label.

Powers
Self-Mutilation / Creation via Body Part

Abaangui creates the moon by cutting off his own nose and throwing it into the sky. This is a powerful act of creation through sacrifice and bodily transformation. It is a common motif in creation myths, where a god's body parts become parts of the cosmos.

Limit: This is a one-time act. He does not use this power repeatedly.

Primordial Creation

As a being who acts in the primordial time of creation, he has the power to shape the world and the heavens. This is a general power of his class of being, not a specific, repeatable ability.

Limit: The myth only describes his creation of the moon.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The myth does not describe Abaangui's physical appearance in detail. He is a primordial being, likely anthropomorphic in form, existing in a time before the world was fully formed. He is not described as a celestial body himself, but rather as the agent who creates the moon.

Symbols — The Moon itself is his primary symbol. He is also associated with the act of creation and the concept of primordial time (the time before the world as we know it).

Domains — Creation, Sky, Moon, Primordial Time, Hunting.

Primordial Time (before the current world order)

In the time before the moon existed, the world was dark at night. Abaangui, a powerful primordial being, was walking through the forest. He was a great hunter. He took out his knife and cut off his own nose. Then, he threw the severed nose high into the sky. The nose stuck there and transformed, becoming the moon, which shone with a soft, silvery light, illuminating the night world for the first time. The creation of the moon, which brought light to the night and established the rhythm of the lunar cycle. This act is part of the larger creation cycle that shaped the world.

Role and Status of AbaanguiAbaangui is a minor deity or a specific culture hero.Abaangui is an aspect of a more general primordial ancestor figure.

Abaangui is a 'god' in the same sense as a god in a polytheistic pantheon like the Greek or Norse gods. — He is more accurately a primordial ancestor or culture hero from a specific creation myth. The Guaraní concept of divinity is complex and does not map neatly onto Western categories.

The myth is a 'weird' or 'silly' story. — It is a profound and meaningful creation myth that explains the origin of the moon through an act of sacrifice and transformation. It should be treated with respect.
