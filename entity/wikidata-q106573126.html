Who It Is

At Six Flags Magic Mountain, Tatsu isn't just a ride—it's a myth made of steel and adrenaline. Since 2006, this flying coaster has launched riders into a horizontal flight 170 feet above the park, twisting through the world's tallest pretzel loop and a zero-gravity roll that defies the senses. But beneath the thrills lies a story of engineering ambition, record-breaking rivalry, and a name that echoes both Japanese legend and the park's Samurai Summit theme.

As a roller coaster, Tatsu is a static attraction. Its 'behavior' is the ride experience: it dispatches from the station, climbs a 170-foot lift hill, and navigates a series of inversions and turns, reaching a top speed of 62 mph (100 km/h). The ride lasts approximately two minutes.

Flying coaster layout

Riders are positioned in a horizontal, flying position after the train rotates seats backward 90 degrees. The layout includes a 170-foot lift hill, a 111-foot drop, a 103-foot corkscrew, a 96-foot zero-gravity roll, an 80-foot horseshoe element, a 124-foot pretzel loop (world's tallest), an inline twist, and a 135-degree downward right turn.

Limit: Riders must be at least 54 inches tall. The ride is not accessible to those with certain medical conditions.

Record-breaking height and pretzel loop

On opening, Tatsu was the tallest, fastest, and longest flying coaster in the world. It remains the tallest flying coaster, and its pretzel loop is the tallest in the world at 124 feet.

Limit: Records are subject to change as new coasters are built.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Tatsu is a steel flying roller coaster with a track length of 3,602 feet (1,098 m) and a height of 170 feet (52 m). The track was originally red and yellow with orange supports, later repainted to orange track with green supports. The trains are painted green, yellow, orange, and red, with each train consisting of eight cars seating four riders in a single row.

Symbols — The ride's name and location (Samurai Summit) evoke Japanese dragon and samurai imagery. The coaster's layout includes a pretzel loop, a signature element of flying coasters, and a zero-gravity roll, the only one on a flying coaster model.

Domains — Tatsu is located in the Samurai Summit area of Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California. The area has a Japanese theme, complementing the coaster's name.

Accounts I
Construction and OpeningNews reports, construction permits, official announcements

2005-2006

Land clearing began in mid-2005 around Samurai Summit. Construction permits and a trademark for the name 'Tatsu' were discovered by the public. The coaster was officially announced on November 17, 2005. Revolution and Roaring Rapids were temporarily closed during construction. After testing, Tatsu opened to the public on May 13, 2006. Tatsu became the park's seventeenth coaster and broke world records for flying coasters.

Accounts II
Record-Breaking DebutAmusement Today Golden Ticket Awards, news coverage

2006-2016

Upon opening, Tatsu was the tallest, fastest, and longest flying coaster in the world. It held these records until The Flying Dinosaur at Universal Studios Japan opened in 2016, which matched Tatsu's speed and broke its length record. Tatsu remains the tallest flying coaster. Tatsu's records were partially surpassed, but it remains notable for its pretzel loop and zero-gravity roll.

Mega Builders EpisodeDiscovery Channel

2006

Discovery Channel's Mega Builders aired an episode following the construction of Tatsu, showing how the coaster was assembled and the problems construction crews had to overcome. Increased public awareness of the engineering behind the ride.
