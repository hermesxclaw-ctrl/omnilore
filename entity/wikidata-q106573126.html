The Approach

At Six Flags Magic Mountain, Tatsu isn't just a ride—it's a myth made of steel and adrenaline. Since 2006, this flying coaster has launched riders into a horizontal flight 170 feet above the park, twisting through the world's tallest pretzel loop and a zero-gravity roll that defies the senses. But beneath the thrills lies a story of engineering ambition, record-breaking rivalry, and a name that echoes both Japanese legend and the park's Samurai Summit theme.

Identity

Tatsu

Pronunciation TAT-soo (approximate)

Etymology

The name 'Tatsu' is likely derived from the Japanese word for dragon (竜 or 龍, pronounced 'tatsu'), fitting the coaster's location in the Samurai Summit area of the park. This is inferred, not officially confirmed.

Cultural Ownership

American theme park culture, specifically Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California. The ride's name and theme draw on Japanese culture, but it is an American attraction.

Disputed Identifications

The metadata incorrectly identifies Tatsu as a fictional character. It is a real roller coaster. No other disputed identifications.

Description

Appearance

Tatsu is a steel flying roller coaster with a track length of 3,602 feet (1,098 m) and a height of 170 feet (52 m). The track was originally red and yellow with orange supports, later repainted to orange track with green supports. The trains are painted green, yellow, orange, and red, with each train consisting of eight cars seating four riders in a single row.

Symbols & Iconography

The ride's name and location (Samurai Summit) evoke Japanese dragon and samurai imagery. The coaster's layout includes a pretzel loop, a signature element of flying coasters, and a zero-gravity roll, the only one on a flying coaster model.

Behaviour & Personality

As a roller coaster, Tatsu is a static attraction. Its 'behavior' is the ride experience: it dispatches from the station, climbs a 170-foot lift hill, and navigates a series of inversions and turns, reaching a top speed of 62 mph (100 km/h). The ride lasts approximately two minutes.

Domains & Habitats

Tatsu is located in the Samurai Summit area of Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California. The area has a Japanese theme, complementing the coaster's name.

Version Differences

The coaster's track was repainted from red/yellow/orange to orange/green. No other version differences exist, as it is a single physical ride.

Powers & Abilities

Flying coaster layout

Source Version Current ride configuration

Description

Riders are positioned in a horizontal, flying position after the train rotates seats backward 90 degrees. The layout includes a 170-foot lift hill, a 111-foot drop, a 103-foot corkscrew, a 96-foot zero-gravity roll, an 80-foot horseshoe element, a 124-foot pretzel loop (world's tallest), an inline twist, and a 135-degree downward right turn.

Limitations

Riders must be at least 54 inches tall. The ride is not accessible to those with certain medical conditions.

Record-breaking height and pretzel loop

Source Version Since 2006; length and speed records broken by The Flying Dinosaur in 2016

Description

On opening, Tatsu was the tallest, fastest, and longest flying coaster in the world. It remains the tallest flying coaster, and its pretzel loop is the tallest in the world at 124 feet.

Limitations

Records are subject to change as new coasters are built.

Contradictions

Some may argue that 'tallest' refers to the lift hill height, but the pretzel loop height is a separate record.

Stories & Myths

Construction and Opening

Source News reports, construction permits, official announcementsCulture American theme park industryPeriod 2005-2006

Summary

Land clearing began in mid-2005 around Samurai Summit. Construction permits and a trademark for the name 'Tatsu' were discovered by the public. The coaster was officially announced on November 17, 2005. Revolution and Roaring Rapids were temporarily closed during construction. After testing, Tatsu opened to the public on May 13, 2006.

Record-Breaking Debut

Source Amusement Today Golden Ticket Awards, news coverageCulture Amusement park industryPeriod 2006-2016

Summary

Upon opening, Tatsu was the tallest, fastest, and longest flying coaster in the world. It held these records until The Flying Dinosaur at Universal Studios Japan opened in 2016, which matched Tatsu's speed and broke its length record. Tatsu remains the tallest flying coaster.
