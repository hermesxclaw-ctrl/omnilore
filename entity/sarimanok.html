Who It Is

The Sarimanok is the legendary bird of the Maranao people of Mindanao, a fowl with a magnificent, colorful tail and wings, often depicted carrying a fish (the pakakaraya) in its beak or perched on a base. It is not a deity per se but a powerful mythological symbol representing good fortune, prosperity, and divine favor. The Sarimanok originates from Maranao oral epics (the Darangen), where it was said to be a magical bird that guided a lost princess to safety or a totem creature that brought abundant harvests to its keeper. The bird is always depicted in brilliant hues of red, gold, yellow, green, and blue — colors that dominate Maranao arts and crafts. In the Darangen (an epic poem recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity), the Sarimanok appears as the companion of heroes and a messenger of the gods. Today, it is the most recognizable symbol of Maranao culture and Muslim Filipino identity, appearing on everything from traditional okir wood carvings to the national airline's livery. The fish in its beak symbolizes the cycle of life.

The Approach

From the lakes and mountains of Mindanao rises a bird of brilliant feathers, its beak clasping a fish, its wings alive with swirling okir patterns. The Sarimanok is not merely a creature of myth; it is the living emblem of the Maranao people—a symbol of good fortune, artistic identity, and cultural pride that has flown from royal umbrellas to university seals and national television screens.

Content advisory. No dark material found in the researched sources. The Sarimanok is a positive cultural symbol; however, respect for Maranao cultural protocols regarding its display is advised.
Identity

Sarimanok

Pronunciation sá·ri·ma·nók

Known As

papanok (feminine form)Itotoro (totem bird)Inikadowa (twin spirit bird)

Etymology

From Maranao 'sari' (assorted/various) and 'manok' (originally 'bird', now 'chicken'). Thus, 'bird of assorted colors'.

Cultural Ownership

Maranao people of Mindanao, Philippines

Disputed Identifications

Some sources may confuse the Sarimanok with other Philippine mythical birds like the Minokawa or Ibong Adarna, but these are distinct. The Sarimanok is specifically Maranao.

Description

Appearance

A fowl with colorful wings and feathered tail, holding a fish in its beak or talons. The head is profusely decorated with scroll, leaf, and spiral motifs (okir).

Symbols & Iconography

The Sarimanok is a ubiquitous symbol of Maranao art, representing good fortune. It is often depicted with intricate okir patterns and is associated with royalty and prestige. It is traditionally displayed with flags, standards, and vexilloids, though modern usage may vary.

Behaviour & Personality

As a mythical bird, it is not described with a personality but is a symbol of prosperity and artistic identity. In the Islamic legend, a celestial rooster (possibly related) crows to rouse creatures.

Domains & Habitats

The Sarimanok is a legendary bird, not tied to a specific habitat; it represents the cultural realm of the Maranao people in Mindanao.

Version Differences

The Sarimanok is derived from the Itotoro, a totem bird that serves as a medium to the spirit world via its twin spirit bird Inikadowa. The later Islamic legend of a giant rooster in the heavens is an additional layer. The feminine form is called papanok.

Muhammad found a rooster in the first of seven heavens, so large its crest touched the second heaven. Its crow roused every living creature except man. The Day of Resurrection would come once this rooster ceased to crow.
