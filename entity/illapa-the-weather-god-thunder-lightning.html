Who It Is

Illapa is the Inca god of weather — of thunder, lightning, rain, and the rainbow that follows the storm. He is the third-ranking deity in the imperial pantheon, approached only by Viracocha and Inti. Illapa's meteorological actions are described as the activity of a divine slinger: he carries a sling (warak'a) and a bronze mace; the Milky Way (Mayu, 'the River') is the celestial watercourse from which he draws water; when he releases his sling-stone, the crack is thunder, the flash is lightning, and the whir is the wind — and water pours from his jug to fall as rain. Illapa was both a god of agricultural blessing and a war-god: his image was carried into battle, and victories were attributed to his lightning. The Inca elite watched the position of the Pleiades (which they called the 'storehouse' of Illapa) to predict the coming rains.
