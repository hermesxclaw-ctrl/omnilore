Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Illapa is derived from the Quechua language, spoken by the Inca people, and means 'thunder' or 'lightning'.

Pronunciation — ee-LAH-pah

Aliases — Illapa, Inca Weather God, Thunder God of the Incas

Ownership — The Inca Empire, specifically the Quechua-speaking peoples of modern-day Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, and Chile.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Illapa is often depicted as a powerful, muscular man with a fierce expression, carrying a sling or a lightning bolt in his hand. In some representations, he is shown riding a storm cloud or standing on a mountain peak.

Symbols — The symbols associated with Illapa include the lightning bolt, the storm cloud, and the mountain peak. In some cases, he is also linked to the sun, the moon, and the stars.

Domains — Illapa is associated with the mountains, the skies, and the storms. He is said to reside in the highest peaks and to control the winds and the rains.

Accounts I
The Creation of the WorldThe Inca Book of Origins

16th century

According to the Inca myth, Illapa was one of the gods who helped create the world. He was tasked with bringing light and life to the world, and he accomplished this by summoning a great storm that brought forth the sun, the moon, and the stars. The creation of the world, the bringing of light and life to the world.

Accounts II
The Story of the ThunderboltThe Quipu Database

20th century

According to the Quipu Database, Illapa was once a mortal man who was chosen by the gods to become a god himself. He was given the power to control the weather, and he used this power to bring forth a great storm that destroyed a rival city. The destruction of the rival city, the rise of Illapa to godhood.

The Illapa CultThe Inca Codex

18th century

According to the Inca Codex, Illapa was worshipped by a cult of priests who believed that he was the god of thunder and lightning. They built temples and offered sacrifices to him, and they believed that he would bring them good fortune and prosperity. The rise of the Illapa cult, the spread of worship and sacrifice to Illapa.
