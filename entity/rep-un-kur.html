Who It Is

In the mist-shrouded forests of the Ainu world, where the line between the human and the divine is thin, there dwells a guardian whose name is spoken with reverence and caution: Rep-un-kur, the keeper of the sea's edge, the one who watches over the boundary where the waves kiss the land.

Rep-un-kur is generally benevolent but can be wrathful if offended or if taboos are broken. He is a protector of fishermen and those who travel the sea, but demands respect and adherence to traditional rituals.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Rep-un-kur' is derived from Ainu: 'rep' (sea) + 'un' (to be in/at) + 'kur' (person/being), thus 'the one who dwells in the sea' or 'the sea person'.

Pronunciation — /ˈrɛp ʊn kʊr/

Aliases — Repunkur, Rep-un-kur kamuy

Ownership — Ainu people of Hokkaido, Japan, and Sakhalin, Russia

Powers
Control over sea and marine life

Rep-un-kur can influence the movement of fish, the state of the sea, and the success of fishing expeditions.

Limit: His power is subject to the will of the supreme deity and the proper observance of rituals by humans.

Shape-shifting

He may appear in various forms, including human, orca, or other sea creatures, to interact with humans or other spirits.

Limit: Not consistently attested; may be a later interpolation.

Protection and wrath

He protects those who honor him and punishes those who violate sea taboos or show disrespect.

Limit: His wrath is believed to manifest as storms, poor catches, or accidents at sea.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Rep-un-kur is typically described as a powerful, humanoid figure associated with the sea, often depicted with attributes of marine animals such as scales or fins, and sometimes accompanied by orca or other sea creatures. In some traditions, he may appear as a giant or a shape-shifter.

Symbols — Sea, waves, orca, fish, and fishing tools are symbols associated with Rep-un-kur. He is often invoked in fishing rituals and offerings are made to him for safe passage and bountiful catches.

Domains — He resides in the sea, particularly near the shores of Ainu lands. He is believed to control the tides, fish populations, and the safety of sea voyages.

Accounts I
The Guardian of the SeaAinu oral tradition

Unknown, pre-modern

Rep-un-kur is said to have descended from the heavens to guard the sea and its creatures. He taught the Ainu how to fish and perform rituals to ensure abundance. In one tale, he rescues a fisherman caught in a storm by calming the waves. The Ainu continue to honor Rep-un-kur through offerings and prayers before sea expeditions.

Accounts II
The Wrath of Rep-un-kurAinu oral tradition

Unknown

A village ignores the rituals for Rep-un-kur, and as punishment, their fishing grounds are barren for a year. The villagers repent, perform the proper ceremonies, and the fish return. Reinforces the importance of ritual observance.
