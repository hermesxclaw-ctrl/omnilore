Who They Are

Rep-un-kur is the Ainu god of the sea, ruler of the ocean depths, controller of fish and marine mammals. For coastal Ainu communities who depended heavily on fishing, he was absolutely vital — he could fill nets with salmon and herring or send storms to destroy boats. Offerings and rituals to Rep-un-kur were essential before every major fishing expedition.

Cultural Significance

Fishing was the economic backbone of coastal Ainu communities, making Rep-un-kur one of the most frequently invoked deities in daily life. The first catch of the season was always offered to him, and fish bones and scales were returned to the sea as a sign of respect. The Ainu concept of sustainable fishing — never taking more than needed — derives from the understanding that Rep-un-kur's gifts must be treated with reciprocity.
