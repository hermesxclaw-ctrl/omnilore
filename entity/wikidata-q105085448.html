The Approach

In the shadow of Inuyama Castle, where the Kiso River bends through the old heart of Owari Province, there is a name that lingers in the margins of history: Aoi Inuyama. She is a figure who exists at the edge of record—a heroic legend whose story has been whispered in local tales, painted on folding screens, and reimagined in modern media, yet whose true origins remain as elusive as the morning mist over the river. Who was she, really? A warrior, a spirit, a folk heroine? The evidence is thin, but the fascination is deep.

Etymology

The name 'Aoi' (葵) means 'hollyhock' or 'mallow' in Japanese, a flower often associated with the Tokugawa shogunate and with the Aoi Matsuri festival in Kyoto. 'Inuyama' (犬山) literally means 'dog mountain' and is the name of a city and castle in Aichi Prefecture, Japan. The combination suggests a character rooted in Japanese cultural geography, possibly a local heroine or legendary figure tied to the Inuyama area.
