Who It Is

An entity that exists in the Christian conception of Heaven — and is consuming it. A colossal parasitic organism several hundred kilometers in diameter that has latched onto the extradimensional space humans call 'Heaven,' feeding on souls over centuries. The 'light at the end of the tunnel' is its bioluminescence. The 'choirs of angels' are its digestive sounds. There is no Heaven. There is only something that ate it.

Form

Glimpsed in near-death accounts: a shapeless, continent-spanning organism of shifting, iridescent flesh, studded with billions of eyes and mouths, silently screaming the names of consumed souls.

Powers

Extradimensional parasitism

Consumption of afterlife constructs

Manipulation of near-death experiences

Metaphysical and physical mutagenic symbiosis Attested: SCP-3396 exists as a gestalt of symbiotes that physically and metaphysically alter biological organisms exposed to its central nexus or its luminous blue-green secretion. These alterations are unpredictable and highly variable, but consistently involve profound changes to anatomy, physiology, and metaphysical properties (e.g., thaumaturgic potential, reality interaction). Creation of SCP-3396-01 infected hosts Attested: Humans exposed to SCP-3396’s fluids become SCP-3396-01 instances, exhibiting anomalous abilities and altered metaphysical states. These hosts form part of SCP-3396’s gestalt, acting as extensions of its influence and communicative network. They have used their abilities for diverse purposes, including murder, nonviolent crime, disease eradication, and public welfare initiatives. Their abilities vary, suggesting no single standardized power set. Interpretive: Some readings emphasize SCP-3396-01 as victims of parasitic infestation; others highlight their agency and potential as pioneers of a new magical age.

Selective tangibility and abnormal interaction with physics Attested: SCP-3396 in its entirety is selectively tangible and fails to interact normally with physical objects or electromagnetic radiation. This renders conventional, non-thaumaturgic testing impossible and suggests that SCP-3396 occupies a liminal state between physical and metaphysical existence. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3396) Core SCP-3396 article Attested: Its selective tangibility prevents standard containment or destruction; however, thaumaturgic and anti-thaumaturgic measures (e.g., runic arrays) can affect its manifestations and infected hosts. Unknown: No explicit contradictions; the phenomenon is simply under-characterized.

Global-scale contagion and potential ℵK-Class “Apotheosis” scenario Attested: SCP-3396’s rate of physical and metaphysical communicability makes complete containment unattainable. The Foundation anticipates that if 15% of Earth’s population becomes infested, Document 3396-ALABASTER will trigger personnel withdrawal, regional quarantine facilities, and alterations to the Foundation’s prime directive, effectively acknowledging a new world order. Incident Log

The SCP Foundation discovers and attempts to contain SCP-3396, a Category 4 extradimensional parasitic gestalt whose central nexus resides in the Mojave Desert. The entity exudes luminous blue-green fluid that infects humans, transforming them into SCP-3396-01 instances with anomalous abilities and altered metaphysical states. As infestation spreads to approximately 6% of the global population, multiple anomalous organizations contest control of Exclusion Zone 3396, leading to a demilitarized zone agreement to avoid global warfare and a Lifted Veil scenario. The Foundation acknowledges that complete containment is impossible and prepares for a potential ℵK-Class “Apotheosis” scenario if infestation reaches 15%, at which point its prime directive would be fundamentally altered. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3396) [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

The Apotheosis Hub situates SCP-3396 as the first part of an epic narrative about endings, beginnings, and the toil in between. It quotes Wallace Stevens and frames SCP-3396 as the advent of a new era in which anomalous abilities proliferate and humanity ascends—or mutates—into a different ontological state. The hub emphasizes that SCP-3396-01 instances have used their powers for both destructive and benevolent purposes, underscoring the moral ambiguity of the apotheosis process. [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

While not focused solely on SCP-3396, this tale occurs in the apotheosis-transformed world that SCP-3396 helped create. Monica, a central character, becomes a living weapon—her soul forged into the shape of a gun—to defend her people against those who would exploit or destroy them. The story centers the human cost and agency within a world where anomalous powers are widespread, implicitly tracing back to the initial contagion of SCP-3396. [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

Set in the apotheosis world, this tale depicts a battlefield of reality-warping phenomena: a turret chewed by a Tyrannosaurus rex made of plasma, a body absent where reality has been crushed flat and rolled into nonexistence, a missile encased in bone, a drone melted into cheese, and Scranton Reality Anchors sitting impotent. The story illustrates the breakdown of conventional physics and containment technologies in the face of widespread anomalous powers, indirectly rooted in the transformative contagion initiated by SCP-3396. [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

This tale articulates the principle that magic has infinite manifestations, sources, and rules, but one universal law: everything has a cost. In the apotheosis world, humanity ascends into a new magical age, but every power and transformation carries a price. SCP-3396’s initial contagion is the unseen origin of this new age, making the entity a foundational mythic engine for the narrative. [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

Content Notes

Attested: SCP-3396’s fluids infect humans, transforming them into SCP-3396-01 instances whose bodies and metaphysical properties are dramatically altered. This constitutes a form of non-consensual bodily takeover, with hosts becoming part of a larger gestalt entity. The infection is framed as both a threat and a potential gateway to power, but the underlying loss of autonomy is a core horror element. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3396) [[2]](http://scpexplained.wikidot.com/scp-3396)

Within horror studies, parasitic infestation and body horror are recognized tropes that explore anxieties about identity, autonomy, and contamination. SCP-3396 participates in this tradition while adding metaphysical dimensions.

Core SCP-3396 article; SCP Explained summary

Attested: SCP-3396-01 instances have used their newfound abilities for murder and nonviolent crime, as well as for disease eradication and public welfare. The article explicitly notes criminal and lethal applications, highlighting the potential for empowered individuals to inflict harm on others. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3396) [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

This reflects broader speculative fiction concerns about superhuman powers and their ethical use, paralleling superhero and supervillain narratives but grounded in horror aesthetics.

Core SCP-3396 article; Apotheosis Hub commentary

Attested: The Foundation anticipates that if 15% of Earth’s population becomes infested, it will need to withdraw personnel and establish regional quarantine facilities, effectively conceding that normal containment and governance structures have failed. This scenario implies mass suffering, disruption of social order, and potential collapse of existing institutions. [[0]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/scp-3396) [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

Apocalyptic contagion narratives are a staple of horror and science fiction, often used to interrogate resilience, governance, and ethical decision-making under existential threat.

Core SCP-3396 article; Apotheosis Hub

Attested (Apotheosis tales): Stories like “A Farewell to Arms” depict extreme reality-warping phenomena—plasma dinosaurs, crushed dimensions, bone-encased missiles, cheese-melted drones—suggesting that the apotheosis world is fundamentally hostile to human cognitive and physical norms. This constitutes a form of ontological trauma, where the basic rules of existence become unstable. [[1]](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/apotheosis-hub)

Such depictions align with cosmic horror and postmodern apocalypse traditions, where reality itself becomes unreliable and threatening.

A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine a tree of alien light rooted in the Mojave Desert—a towering, iridescent growth that touches nothing it should, yet seeps a luminous blue-green fluid into the sand. Everyone who touches that fluid changes, body and soul, until six percent of humanity carries a piece of the thing inside them. Some cure diseases. Some commit murder. All of them are part of a single, extradimensional parasite that may be less an ending than a forced evolution.
