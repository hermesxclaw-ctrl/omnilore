Ereshkigal, the queen of the dead, who sits in the darkness and the dust, whose judgment no living soul can escape.

Who They Are

Ereshkigal is the queen of the underworld (Irkalla), the realm of the dead. She is the older sister of Inanna (Ishtar). Unlike the Greek Hades, Ereshkigal does not rule from a throne of judgment but from a dark, dusty palace where the dead eat clay and drink muddy water. Her name means 'Lady of the Great Below.' In the Descent of Inanna, Ereshkigal strips her sister of all power and hangs her corpse on a hook — only through Enki's intervention is Inanna resurrected. In the myth of Nergal and Ereshkigal, Nergal visits the underworld, offends her, but later returns and marries her, becoming co-ruler. Ereshkigal is depicted as a terrifying, naked figure with claws and snakes for hair, though sometimes shown as a regal figure wearing a horned crown. She represents the inescapability of death — once a soul enters her realm, it cannot return. Her decrees are absolute, binding even the other gods. Yet she is also tragic: a goddess forced to dwell in darkness while her sister enjoys the light.
