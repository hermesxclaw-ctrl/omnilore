Who It Is

In the summer of 1995, a two-year-old filly at Shadai Farm Hayakita was nearly blocked in the final straight of a minor stakes race—yet she powered through to win by a large margin. Her trainer, Tsutomu Ito, watching from the rail, later recalled thinking: 'Normally you can't win from that position. That was the moment I became convinced that this was no ordinary filly, but a filly who could beat male horses.' That filly was Air Groove, who would go on to become the 'Empress' of Japanese racing—a mare who not only matched the colts but beat them, and whose bloodline would carry her legacy into the next generation.

On the track, Air Groove was known for her tenacity and ability to overcome adversity, as shown in her Ichō Stakes win. She was a late bloomer, improving with age. In Umamusume, she is strict, dignified, and heavy-handed with unruly students, but softer with her juniors, showing a caring side.

Racing ability

Air Groove was a Grade 1-winning racehorse, victorious in the Yushun Himba (Oaks) and the Autumn Tenno Sho. She was capable of beating male horses, as evidenced by her Tenno Sho win. Her racing style included strong finishes and the ability to overcome traffic trouble.

Limit: She had a leg fracture in 1996 that sidelined her; she also had a tendency to be unsettled by camera flashes, as seen in the Shuka Sho.

Leadership and discipline (Umamusume)

As student council vice-president, she is known for heavy-handed discipline, aiming to improve unruly students. She is strict and dignified.

Limit: Her strictness sometimes backfires, but her intentions are good.

Form & Signs

Appearance — As a horse, Air Groove was a bay filly, typical of thoroughbreds. Specific physical conformation details are not provided in the source. In Umamusume, she is depicted as a tall, dignified young woman with long dark hair, often wearing the Tracen Academy uniform; her design reflects her 'Empress' nickname.

Symbols — The 'Empress' nickname is a key symbol, evoking royalty and dominance. In Umamusume, she is associated with the student council and discipline.

Domains — Raced on turf tracks in Japan, primarily at distances from 1200m to 2500m. As a broodmare, she lived at Shadai Farm.

Accounts I
1996 Yushun Himba (Japanese Oaks)Provided source material

1996

Air Groove won the Oaks, defeating the Oka Sho winner Fight Gulliver. This made her and her mother Dyna Carle the second mother-daughter pair to both win the race. Established her as a top filly; led to a break and subsequent Shuka Sho attempt.

Accounts II
1997 Autumn Tenno ShoProvided source material

1997

Air Groove won the Grade 1 race over 2000m, beating the previous year's winner Bubble Gum Fellow by a neck. She became the first mare to win the race in 17 years and the first since it became a 2000m race. She was named Japanese Horse of the Year, the first mare in 26 years to receive the honor.
