Who It Is

In the tightly-packed terraces of a fictional village called Aberflyarff, on the River Efflew in Scrumcap Valley, two sheep carried messages, a tattooed barmaid poured pints at the Golden Dap, and an entire nation saw itself reflected back in ink. That was Gren's Wales — not the Wales of castles and princes, but of rugby mud, valley chapels, and local councillors who recognised themselves in the morning paper and laughed anyway.

Accounts I
Childhood in Hengoed and the Hymn-Book CaricaturesBiographical summary citing Jones's own recollections of Ronald Niebour ('Neb')

1934-1950s

Born 13 June 1934 in Hengoed, Rhymney Valley, son of coal miner Harry Jones. At age eight began drawing caricatures of neighbours and copying pocket cartoons in the style of Ronald Niebour ('Neb') of the Daily Mail into his chapel hymn book, inspired by seeing the Daily Mail at his grandmother's house. Quoted: 'Neb was responsible for my first interest in cartoons. I used to go to my grandmother's, who would have the Daily Mail, and I was amazed how the cartoonist could do a different drawing every day.' Began sending drawings to agents while still young, driven to earn a living from drawing. Established lifelong obsession with daily cartooning and the 'draw what you know' principle later reinforced by mentor Jon; set trajectory toward freelance cartooning despite lack of formal art training.

Travelling Rep, Engineering Designer and First SalesBiographical summary; Spick & Span and Birmingham Mail publication records

1950s-1963

As a young man Jones worked as a travelling representative selling tiles and machine tools, scribbling cartoons in lay-bys when he should have been selling. From 1958 to 1963 he was employed as an engineering designer. During this period he made first sales: a joke cartoon to Spick & Span (first published drawing) and, with help and advice from John Philpin Jones ('Jon') of the News Chronicle (later Daily Mail), a first news-related cartoon to the Birmingham Mail. Jon's advice - 'draw what you know. And I knew about rugby and the Valleys' - became his creative maxim. Transition from engineering to freelance cartooning; validation of valley/rugby subject matter as marketable; foundation for later Western Mail topical work.

The Knights of the Round Table and The Barron KnightsBiographical summary; Barron Knights history

1960-1963

In 1960 Jones became one of the founder members of 'The Knights of the Round Table,' a pop group that later transformed into the successful satirical/parody group The Barron Knights (known for musical parodies and comedy). Jones's involvement was as a founder member before the group achieved national fame; he left to pursue cartooning as the group shifted to full-time musical satire. Demonstrates early engagement with popular entertainment and satire; connection not continued as career focused on visual satire rather than musical performance.

Accounts II
Staff Cartoonist at the Western Mail and the Daily Topical CartoonWestern Mail staff records; biography

1968-1999 (staff), continuing freelance 1999-2007

After several years as a freelance cartoonist, Jones obtained a staff position on the Western Mail in 1968. He produced a daily topical cartoon, often conceived and executed rapidly after morning discussions with the newspaper's editor. He became one of Wales's best-known and longest-serving newspaper cartoonists, remaining on staff until retirement on his 65th birthday in 1999, and continuing to produce cartoons for the Echo evening paper from home thereafter. He described his remit: 'When I started out I enjoyed the same things as I'm drawing now. Wales, rugby, local politicians, anything we in this part of the world are able to relate to – I aim to reflect our life and it goes down well with the readers.' Established Gren as a Welsh press institution; daily presence shaped popular perception of Welsh politics and rugby; model for provincial cartoonist career.

Ponty and Pop and the Invention of AberflyarffFootball Echo (Saturday sports edition of South Wales Echo) archive

1970s-2007 (weekly strip)

Jones is best known for creating the weekly strip 'Ponty and Pop' in the Football Echo, the Saturday sports edition of the Western Mail's sister paper South Wales Echo. The strip depicted South Wales valley life centred on the fictional village of Aberflyarff in Scrumcap Valley on the River Efflew - names that parody Welsh toponymy and rugby culture. Recurring characters included Nigel and Neville the message-bearing sheep, the titular Ponty and Pop, and Bromide Lil, the tattooed barmaid of the Golden Dap. Rugby, terraced streets and local politicians provided constant material, with cameos of real figures such as Max Boyce, Harry Secombe and Wyn Calvin. Created a durable fictional Valleys universe that became shorthand for South Wales identity; most remembered part of his oeuvre; cited in every obituary as signature work.

Big Deal / Threadneedle, Sporting Calendars and the Welsh Rugby Union 'War Artist'Biographical summary; Bemrose Publishers records; Welsh Rugby Union

c. 1970s-2007

Parallel to newspaper work, Jones produced the syndicated strip 'Big Deal,' also known as 'Threadneedle' (referencing Threadneedle Street, London's financial centre), syndicated for thirty-five years. He also produced golf, cricket and rugby sporting calendars, as well as business-themed calendars for Bemrose Publishers for 20 years. He served as official 'war artist' for the Welsh Rugby Union, documenting tours and matches. For charity he produced an annual rugby calendar and sold numerous original sketches. Extended reach beyond Wales to UK-wide business press and sporting audiences; provided steady commercial income and charitable fundraising platform.

Gold Disc, Honours and Final Years in LlandaffEMI Records; honours lists; University of Glamorgan; obituaries

1975-2007

Jones's local popularity led to drawing the cover for Max Boyce's album 'We All Had Doctors' Papers' (1975), resulting in him becoming the first cartoonist to receive a gold disc from EMI. Through his career he produced over 24 books of cartoons. He was voted best provincial cartoonist in Britain by the Cartoonists' Club of Great Britain (CCGB) four times (1983, 1985, 1986, 1987), honoured by the Variety Club for charity work, awarded an honorary degree by the University of Glamorgan in 2004, and appointed MBE for services to newspapers in 1989. He lived in Llandaff, Cardiff, married to Anne with two sons (both became policemen) and four grandchildren at death, with constant companion Charlie Friday, a Cocker Spaniel. He retired on his 65th birthday but continued drawing for the Echo from home until death at University Hospital of Wales, Cardiff, on 4 January 2007. Gren was a national political cartoonist in the mould of Westminster satirists — Gren explicitly avoided national party-political satire, focusing on Welsh valley life, rugby and local politicians. Quote: 'I'm not trying to prove any points. I try not to get into the political area as that isn't my audience.' His topical cartoons were Welsh-regional, not Westminster-focused.

Ponty and Pop was a daily strip in the Western Mail — Ponty and Pop was a weekly strip in the Football Echo, the Saturday sports edition of the Western Mail's sister paper South Wales Echo - not the daily Western Mail topical cartoon. The daily and weekly were distinct formats with different characters and rhythms.

Gren was primarily a rugby player or official who drew on the side — Gren was a professional cartoonist and former engineering designer/travelling rep; his rugby role was as official 'war artist' for the Welsh Rugby Union and as a chronicler of rugby culture, not as a player or administrator. His authority came from observation and fandom ('crazy on rugby' per Bill Tidy).

Gren is a fictional or legendary Welsh folk hero — Grenfell Jones was a real, documented 20th-century person (1934-2007) with verifiable press employment, honours (MBE 1989, honorary degree 2004), and death record at University Hospital of Wales.
