Who It Is

Mama Sara ('Mother Maize') is the Inca goddess of maize — the staple grain upon which the empire's agricultural economy was built. Hers is a household-level cult rather than a state-temple religion: every family selected the most perfect ear of corn from the harvest — typically one with double rows of kernels (a sign of exceptional fertility) — and dressed it in fine textiles and jewelry as the 'Saramama' (the 'mother maize'). This ear was placed in the pirwa (granary) and annually replaced; it was believed to ensure abundance and the multiplication of the next harvest. The first corn beer (chicha) of the season was poured onto the fields as an offering to Mama Sara before the family drank. The maize-mother was also responsible for the storage and preservation of food: families who neglected her would have their granaries infested by mice.
