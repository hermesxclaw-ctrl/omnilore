Who It Is

The name 'Doma' is a hall of mirrors. Say it in Tokyo and you mean the cool earthen floor where you take off your shoes. Say it in Eorzea and you mean a castle that burned for its honor. Say it in Marineford and you mean a monkey-tailed pirate who charged an admiral for Whitebeard. Say it in Washington and you mean a law that defined marriage for a generation. One sound, at least a dozen lives — and Wikidata Q108688554 sits at the center of that tangle, a thinly-documented 'fictional character, heroic-legend' entry that forces you to ask which Doma you mean before you can tell any story at all.

For non-character referents, behavior is functional: doma as workspace for cooking, farming tools, geta removal. For One Piece Doma: attested as loyal, hot-headed, willing to charge Marine Admiral Akainu to avenge comrades; limited dialogue, characterized as boisterous ally (Japanese: 'Doma the Monkey' tone). For Final Fantasy Doma as polity: characterized as honorable, isolationist, samurai-code-driven kingdom; its people (Doman) as resilient, stoic, culturally proud under Garlean occupation; leaders like Lord Kaien (FFVI) and Lord Hien (FFXIV) embody bushido-like self-sacrifice.

Powers
One Piece Doma - Swordsmanship and Monkey Agility

Wields a long katana (nodachi-like) with one-handed and two-handed grips; exhibits enhanced agility, tail prehension, and jumping ability consistent with mink/monkey motif (though not a confirmed Mink tribe member). Participates in Marineford melee, cutting through Marine infantry.

Limit: No Devil Fruit shown; no Haki explicitly depicted for Doma; defeated/off-screened when Akainu counters allied captains; not ranked among top-tier commanders. Power level is mid-tier allied captain, not Yonko commander level.

Final Fantasy VI Doma - Castle Fortifications and Samurai Retainers

As a kingdom, Doma's 'abilities' are military: fortified castle walls, samurai retainers (Cyan, guards), and siege resilience. Cyan's Bushido techniques (e.g., Dispatch, Retort) are the personal combat expression of Doman martial tradition.

Limit: Vulnerable to chemical warfare: Kefka poisons the castle's water supply, bypassing walls; entire garrison except Cyan is killed. No magical barrier.

Final Fantasy XIV Doma - Doman Martial Arts and Shinobi Techniques

Doman culture produces samurai (Hien, Gosetsu), ninja/shinobi (Yugiri), and conscripted foot soldiers; techniques include Iaijutsu, Ninjutsu, and katana arts. The Doman Enclave later demonstrates reconstruction/crafting as a 'power' (restoration via donation mechanics).

Limit: Under Garlean occupation, Doma is militarily suppressed for 25 years; requires foreign (Eorzean) alliance to liberate. No magitek parity with Empire until liberation.

Hyphaene / Doma Palm - Ecological Resilience

Drought tolerance, branching habit rare in palms, edible fruit mesocarp, fiber for weaving, and use as famine food. Not a supernatural power but an attested biological capacity.

Limit: Slow growth, limited cold tolerance, fruit astringent when unripe.

DOMA (Law) - Federal Definitional Power

Section 3 defined marriage as 'between one man and one woman' for all federal purposes, affecting 1,000+ statutes (tax, immigration, benefits). Section 2 allowed states to refuse recognition of same-sex marriages from other states.

Limit: Ruled unconstitutional in Windsor (2013) (Section 3) and Obergefell v. Hodges (2015) (state bans); fully repealed by Respect for Marriage Act (2022). Never regulated religious rites, only civil/legal recognition.

Appearance — No single appearance; description varies by referent. (A) Japanese doma: not a character but a space — a beaten-earth or mortar floor, often 10-30 cm lower than the raised wooden ita-no-ma, dark gray-brown, open to the outside via large sliding doors, containing a kamado (cooking hearth), water jar, and work tools; barefoot or in straw sandals. (B) Hyphaene/doma palm: stout, dichotomously branching palm 6-10m tall, fan-shaped leaves, brown fibrou

Accounts I
The Poisoning of Doma CastleFinal Fantasy VI - Scenario by Yoshinori Kitase and Hiroyuki Ito, Square

1994 (Heisei era game, fictional world)

The Gestahlian Empire besieges Doma Castle, which holds out under Lord Kaien and his retainer Cyan Garamonde. Kefka Palazzo, frustrated by the stalemate, poisons the castle's water supply. Cyan, away with his family outside the walls, returns to find his wife Elayne and son Owain dead along with the entire garrison. He joins the Returners to avenge Doma. The castle remains a poisoned ruin, visited later as the Phantom Train sequence. Establishes FFVI's moral stakes and Cyan's grief arc; Doma becomes shorthand for empire's cruelty; later referenced in Dissidia and FFXIV as legacy name. No rebuilding within FFVI narrative.

The Marineford War - Doma Charges the MarinesOne Piece manga Chapters 551-578, anime Episodes 461-483, Eiichiro Oda/Shueisha/Toei

2009-2010 (publication/airing), in-story 1522 Age of the Sea

Doma, captain of his own pirate crew and ally of the Whitebeard Pirates, answers Whitebeard's call to rescue Portgas D. Ace from execution at Marineford. Introduced among 43 allied captains, he is seen clashing with Marine officers and later joining the enraged charge after Ace's death, attempting to attack Admiral Akainu alongside other allies before being repelled. He survives the war and is later seen in the post-war world. Demonstrates Whitebeard's vast alliance network; Doma's survival confirms allied crews persist after Whitebeard's death; no further major arc for Doma as of 2024, but he remains a background presence in alliance charts.

The Liberation of Doma (Stormblood)Final Fantasy XIV: Stormblood Main Scenario Quests, Square Enix

2017 (Patch 4.0), in-game Year 1577 of Sixth Astral Era

After 25 years under Garlean rule, Doma (capital Doma Castle, region Yanxia) rises in rebellion led by Lord Hien Rijin, Yugiri Mistwalker, and Gosetsu. The Warrior of Light, having liberated Ala Mhigo, crosses to Othard, helps rekindle Doman hope after a failed earlier uprising, infiltrates Doma Castle, defeats Magitek and viceroy Yotsuyu goe Brutus, and restores Hien to the throne. Post-liberation, the Doman Enclave is rebuilt from ruins through player-driven donations (patches 4.1-4.3). Core expansion story; establishes Doma as major cultural hub (samurai/ninja job origins); Doman Enclave reconstruction becomes long-term player activity; referenced in Endwalker (2021) as allied nation.

Accounts II
The Doma Floor - Daily Life in the MinkaNishi & Hozumi, What is Japanese Architecture? (1983); Brown, Azby. The Very Small Home (2005); plus Edo-period pictorial sources (e.g., Minka field surveys)

Edo period (1603-1868) through Shōwa era (1926-1989) ethnographic record; architectural origin medieval

In a traditional farmhouse (minka), the doma is the earthen-floored area where outdoor work enters the home: rice is threshed, tools are repaired, meals are cooked on the kamado, and guests remove footwear before stepping up to the tatami. Folkloric accounts describe the doma as the domain of the kamado-gami (hearth deity) and as a liminal space where itinerant monks, peddlers, and even fox spirits (kitsune) might be entertained without fully entering the pure interior. Informs modern Japanese house design (genkan/doma revival) and museum reconstructions (e.g., Nihon Minka-en open-air museum, Kawasaki). Linguistic survival: modern apartments advertise 'doma space' for bicycles and outdoor gear.

The Doum Palm and the Market of DomaEthnobotanical fieldwork on Hyphaene thebaica (doum/doma palm) and geographic record for Doma, Nigeria

Precolonial use through present; Doma LGA created 1976 (Nasarawa State formed 1996)

The doma/doum palm provides fruit, leaves for mats and ropes, and shade in arid zones; Hausa proverbs reference its branching ('like doma, you go two ways'). The town of Doma in Nasarawa State, Nigeria, historically a market and farming center for yams, rice, and palm products, gives its name to Doma Local Government Area. Oral histories link the town's name to the palm's abundance, though colonial gazetteers list alternative founder-name etymologies. Continued economic importance of palm products; Doma LGA remains agricultural hub; botanical vernacular 'doma' persists in West African markets, causing confusion with Japanese doma in global search results.

DOMA - From Law to Landmark CaseU.S. Congressional Record and Supreme Court opinions: Windsor (2013), Obergefell (2015)

1996-2022 (enactment to repeal)

Congress passes DOMA in 1996 (85-14 Senate, 342-67 House) defining marriage federally as man-woman. For 17 years, same-sex couples are denied federal benefits. Edith Windsor sues after being taxed $363,053 on her wife Thea Spyer's estate because DOMA blocked 'surviving spouse' status; Supreme Court strikes Section 3 (5-4) in 2013. In 2015, Obergefell extends marriage equality nationwide; in 2022, Respect for Marriage Act repeals DOMA and requires interstate recognition. Landmark LGBTQ+ rights trajectory; over 1,000 federal provisions affected; cultural shorthand 'DOMA' becomes symbol in activism, journalism, and art.

Doma is a single character/place that appears across One Piece and Final Fantasy. — One Piece's Doma (person) and Final Fantasy's Doma (kingdom) are unrelated creations by different companies (Shueisha/Toei vs. Square Enix) with no shared canon. Same katakana spelling (ドーマ/ドマ) causes search-engine conflation.

DOMA (Defense of Marriage Act) banned same-sex marriage nationwide. — DOMA did not ban states from issuing same-sex marriage licenses; it defined marriage for federal purposes (Section 3) and allowed states to refuse recognition of other states' same-sex marriages (Section 2). State-level bans were separate statutes/amendments.

The Japanese doma is a dirty or primitive dirt floor, sign of poverty. — Doma is a functional, intentionally designed earthen work floor for cooking, craft, and transition; in well-maintained minka it is clean, hard-packed, and culturally valued. Modern architects revive it as desirable genkan/doma space.

Doma (One Piece) is a Mink or has a Devil Fruit power. — No canonical source confirms Doma as a Mink tribe member or Devil Fruit user; his tail and monkey features are character design, not confirmed species/ability. No Vivre Card, SBS, or databook assigns him a fruit or tribe as of 2024.

TV Doma and Doma TV are the same channel. — TV Doma (Slovakia, 2009, Markíza Group) and Doma TV (Croatia, 2011, Nova TV) are separate national channels with similar names meaning 'at home,' operated by different companies in different languages.
