Who It Is

Before the Dreaming settled into its long, slow current, before the rivers carved their paths and the mountains learned to hold the sky, there was Baiame. He was not a distant, abstract force, but a maker with hands, a father with a voice, a lawgiver who walked the earth before he leaped into the heavens, leaving his footprints on a mountain as a promise to the people he had created. He is the Sky Father of the southeastern nations, a figure of immense power and authority, yet his story is not one of conquest, but of creation, law, and a profound, enduring connection to the land he shaped.

Baiame is a creator and lawgiver. He is generally benevolent but expects adherence to his laws, which are encoded in the Dreaming. He is a father figure, both to humanity and to other spirit beings. He is a remote, sky-dwelling figure who delegated much of the day-to-day oversight of the world to his son Daramulum. He is associated with the Bora ceremony, a male initiation rite, and is believed to preside over it, ensuring its correct performance. He is a figure of ultimate authority, not a trickster or a capricious being.

Powers
Creation

Baiame created the world, including the landscape, animals, plants, and humans. He is the ultimate source of all life.

Limit: His creation is seen as complete; he is not actively creating new things now, but rather maintaining the order he established.

Omniscience (in some traditions)

Baiame is sometimes described as all-seeing and all-knowing, watching over humanity from his sky dwelling.

Limit: This attribute is not consistently emphasized across all traditions.

Lawgiving

He established the sacred laws, customs, and ceremonies, particularly the Bora initiation rite, which he gave to humanity.

Limit: His laws are absolute and must be followed.

Power over the Bora

He presides over the Bora ceremony, and his power is invoked during the initiation process. The bullroarer represents his voice.

Limit: The ceremony must be performed correctly for his power to be effective.

Power over Life and Death

He is the giver of life and can also take it away. He is the ultimate arbiter of human destiny.

Limit: This power is not often described in detail in the surviving records.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In rock art, Baiame is typically depicted as a large, often imposing human-like figure, frequently with outstretched arms. He may have a headdress or radiating lines around his head, suggesting a divine or radiant nature. In some depictions, he is shown with a large, elongated body. The Baiame Cave painting shows him with a large face, small body, and arms spread wide, surrounded by smaller figures, possibly representing people, spirits, or clan

Symbols — The Bora ring, a ceremonial ground, is a key symbol associated with Baiame. The sky itself, particularly the Milky Way, is seen as his domain. The eagle, in some regions, is associated with him. His outstretched arms in rock art are a powerful symbol of his encompassing power and authority. The bullroarer, used in the Bora ceremony, is considered a symbol of his voice.

Domains — Baiame's primary domain is the sky, where he resides after creating the world. He is a creator deity, responsible for the creation of the earth, its features, and its inhabitants. He is also the lord of the Bora ceremony and the source of sacred law and custom. He is associated with the sun in some traditions, and with the constellation of Orion in others.

Accounts I
The Creation of the WorldVarious oral traditions, recorded by Howitt, Mathews, and others

The Dreaming (creation period)

In the beginning, Baiame moved across a featureless land. He created the mountains, rivers, and plains, filled them with animals and plants, and finally created humans. In some versions, he created the first people from trees or clay. He then gave them the laws of the Dreaming and the Bora ceremony to ensure their proper conduct. After his work was complete, he ascended to the sky, where he remains. The world as it is known was established, and the laws for human life were set. Baiame's departure to the sky marked the beginning of the current era.

The Giving of the BoraRecorded by R.H. Mathews

The Dreaming

Baiame, seeing that people needed guidance, gave them the Bora ceremony. He taught the elders the songs, dances, and rituals that would initiate young men into adulthood and the sacred knowledge of the tribe. This ceremony is the central institution of religious life in the regions where Baiame is known. The Bora ceremony was established as the central rite of passage for men, ensuring the transmission of knowledge and law from one generation to the next.

Accounts II
Baiame's Ascent at Mount YengoLocal Darkinjung and Wonnarua tradition

The Dreaming

After creating the world and establishing the law, Baiame stood on the summit of Mount Yengo. He placed his feet firmly, and then, with a great leap, he ascended into the sky, leaving his footprints imprinted in the rock. These footprints are still visible today at the summit. Mount Yengo became a deeply sacred site, and the footprints are a physical reminder of Baiame's presence and his connection to the land.

Baiame and the First InitiateRecorded by A.W. Howitt

The Dreaming

In some accounts, Baiame is said to have personally initiated the first man, or to have initiated Daramulum, who then passed the knowledge on to humanity. This establishes a direct lineage from the creator to the initiated men of the tribe. This story reinforces the sacredness and authority of the Bora ceremony and the knowledge it imparts.

Baiame is the same as the Christian God. — While early anthropologists drew parallels, Baiame is a distinct, pre-contact deity with a different origin, role, and mythology. He is not a 'native version' of the Christian God.

Aboriginal Australians did not have a concept of a 'high god' before European contact. — Baiame is one of several examples of a 'Sky Father' or supreme being in Aboriginal religion, demonstrating the complexity and depth of pre-contact belief systems. This misconception stems from a colonial bias that assumed Indigenous religions were 'primitive'.

Baiame is a universal deity for all Aboriginal Australians. — Baiame is specifically a deity of the southeastern nations. Other regions have their own creator figures, such as Bunjil in Victoria, Wandjina in the Kimberley, and various other beings elsewhere. The Dreaming is a diverse and complex set of beliefs, not a single unified religion.

The Bora ceremony is a 'secret cult' that is sinister. — The Bora is a sacred male initiation ceremony that is central to the social and religious life of the communities that practice it. It is a rite of passage that teaches young men the laws, traditions, and skills they need to become adults. The 'secrecy' is a matter of respect for sacred knowledge, not a sign of sinister activity.
