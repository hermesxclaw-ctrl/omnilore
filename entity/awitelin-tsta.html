You are standing on me now — every step, every seed, every breath. I am not in the sky; I am under your feet, and I have been holding you since your first ancestor was born.

Who It Is

Awitelin Tsta is the Zuni Earth Mother — the divine feminine principle who, together with Sky Father Uchtsiti, was born from Awonawilona (the supreme dual creator) to populate and sustain the world. Her name translates to 'Fourfold Womb Mother,' reflecting her association with the four cardinal directions and her role as the source of all earthly fertility. Every plant, animal, and human being is nurtured within her body. In Zuni theology, Awitelin Tsta is not a distant abstraction but the literal substance of the earth: the soil underfoot, the clay from which pottery is made, the ground that yields corn. When Zuni farmers plant their crops, they are placing seeds into the body of Awitelin Tsta, and harvest is her gift returned. She is especially honored by Zuni women, whose pottery-making, childbearing, and agricultural work are all understood as participation in her creative power. The Zuni, unlike many cultures, did not gender the earth as exclusively passive: Awitelin Tsta actively receives the rain from the Sky Father, transforming it into life. She is associated with the kiva (the subterranean ceremonial chamber), which represents the womb of the earth into which initiates descend for ritual rebirth. Her presence pervades Zuni daily life with a quiet, omnipresent sacredness — every step on the earth is a step on the body of the Mother.
