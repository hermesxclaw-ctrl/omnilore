Poverty is not a virtue — it is a cage. I crack open the cage with a stream of jewels, so that you may turn your mind to the only wealth that matters: wisdom and compassion.

Who It Is

Dzambhala (also Jambhala) is the Tibetan Buddhist deity of wealth, abundance, and prosperity, derived from the Hindu god Kubera and the Buddhist Four Great Kings (specifically Vaishravana, the Guardian of the North). He is one of the most popular deities among lay Tibetans, who invoke him for material prosperity and the removal of poverty. However, in Buddhist context, Dzambhala's wealth is not an end in itself — he represents the material abundance that allows practitioners to focus on spiritual practice without the distress of poverty. Dzambhala appears in five principal forms, distinguished by color: Yellow (the most popular), White, Black, Red, and Green. The Yellow Dzambhala is depicted holding a mongoose that vomits jewels (the mongoose is the traditional enemy of snakes, and the jewels represent generosity conquering miserliness). He also holds a jewel-spitting citron fruit and a victory banner. Dzambhala is often depicted with a crown, adorned with jewels, with a slightly corpulent body (representing abundance). His practice involves specific sadhanas (liturgical texts) recited for prosperity, accompanied by offerings of torma (ritual cakes), gold, and jewels. The Dzambhala practice is often given as an initiation or jenang (permission) by lamas to lay communities. In Mongolia, Dzambhala is similarly venerated and appears in the iconography of Mongolian Buddhist temples alongside indigenous wealth spirits.
