The Approach

He was the man who crowned himself emperor, redrew the map of Europe, and changed the course of history — but was he a visionary reformer or a ruthless tyrant?

Identity

Napoleon Bonaparte

Pronunciation /nəˈpoʊliən ˈboʊnəpɑːrt/

Known As

Napoleon INapoleone di BuonaparteNabulioneNabulioNapolionneNapulioneThe Little CorporalCorsican Ogre

Etymology

The name 'Bonaparte' derives from Italian 'buona parte' meaning 'good part' or 'good share'. 'Napoleone' is an Italian name, possibly from Greek 'Napoleon' meaning 'lion of Naples'.

Cultural Ownership

French (naturalized Corsican of Italian descent)

Disputed Identifications

Some claim Napoleon was not French but Italian, as he was born in Corsica before it was fully integrated; also, his exact birth date is disputed due to calendar reforms.

Description

Appearance

Napoleon was of short stature, approximately 5'6" (168 cm), which was average for his time but often exaggerated as short. He had dark hair, grey-blue eyes, and a thin build. He often wore a distinctive bicorne hat and a simple green uniform with white lapels.

Symbols & Iconography

The Imperial Eagle, the letter 'N' with a crown, the bee (symbol of the Merovingians), and the laurel wreath. His portrait with a hand tucked into his waistcoat is iconic.

Behaviour & Personality

Charismatic, ambitious, brilliant tactician, tireless worker, often ruthless and controlling. He had a photographic memory and could work long hours with little sleep. He was known to be both charming and intimidating.

Domains & Habitats

Born in Corsica, educated in mainland France, his power base was in Paris. He campaigned across Europe, from Egypt to Russia.

Version Differences

Historical accounts vary widely: some portray him as a heroic reformer, others as a tyrant. Modern scholarship emphasizes his complex legacy.

Powers & Abilities

Military Genius

Source Version Historical

Description

Napoleon was a master of artillery and maneuver warfare, known for his rapid campaigns and ability to concentrate forces at decisive points.

Limitations

He overextended in Russia and Spain, leading to his downfall.

Contradictions

Some argue his success was due to superior numbers and subordinates.

Political Reformer

Source Version Historical

Description

Implemented the Napoleonic Code, centralized administration, established the Bank of France, and reformed education.

Limitations

Reforms often curtailed individual freedoms and women's rights.

Contradictions

His reforms were inspired by revolutionary ideals but also served to consolidate his power.

Description

In 1802, Napoleon reinstated slavery in French colonies, reversing the abolition of 1794.

Contradictions & Disputes

Napoleon's Height

Position A

He was extremely short.

Position A — evidence

British propaganda and cartoons depicted him as small.

Position B

He was of average height for his time.

Position B — evidence

His height was measured at 5'6" (168 cm) after death, which was average.

Scholarly Consensus

The short myth was a result of propaganda and confusion between French and British inches.

Legacy as Reformer vs. Tyrant

Position A

He was a great reformer who modernized Europe.

Position A — evidence

His legal and administrative reforms, including the Napoleonic Code.

Position B

He was a tyrant who suppressed liberties and caused immense suffering.

Position B — evidence

His censorship, police state, and reinstatement of slavery.

Scholarly Consensus

Both views are valid; his legacy is complex and contradictory.

Cause of Death

Position A

He died of stomach cancer.

Position A — evidence

Autopsy and modern analysis confirm stomach cancer.

Position B

He was poisoned with arsenic.

Position B — evidence

High levels of arsenic found in hair samples.

Scholarly Consensus

Stomach cancer is the accepted cause; arsenic was likely from environmental exposure.
