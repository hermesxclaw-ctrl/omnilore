Who It Is

In Loguetown, the town where Gold Roger was born and executed, a nervous shopkeeper keeps two blades he is afraid to sell — one that has killed every owner who drew it, and one that has never tasted blood. When a lost swordsman with three earrings walks in looking for a cheap replacement, Ipponmatsu has to decide whether to cheat him or trust him with a family heirloom.

Attested: Initially mercenary and deceptive — he attempts to overcharge Zoro and to pass off the cursed Sandai Kitetsu as a bargain while concealing its history, motivated by debt and fear of the blade. After Zoro tests the curse by risking his arm and demonstrates luck/nerve, Ipponmatsu undergoes a rapid moral shift: awe, remorse, and generosity. He gifts Zoro both Sandai Kitetsu (at a steep discount / effectively free in anime) and his family's heirloom Yubashiri (a Ryō Wazamono, 50-million-berry class blade), stating Zoro is worthy. Powers
Meito Appraisal and Sword Lore

Expert knowledge of graded swords (Meito) and cursed blades, specifically the Kitetsu lineage (Shodai, Nidai, Sandai Kitetsu) and the Ryō Wazamono grade of Yubashiri. He can identify grade, value, and curse reputation on sight.

Limit: Knowledge is commercial and folkloric, not supernatural; he fears the Sandai Kitetsu and cannot wield or purify it. He misjudges Zoro's worthiness initially.

Merchant Negotiation (Civilian Skill)

Ability to set prices, haggle, and assess customers. Initially attempts to exploit Zoro's ignorance, later negotiates a symbolic price for the cursed blade.

Limit: Poor judgment under financial pressure; easily intimidated by Tashigi and by Zoro's curse test. No combat or leadership ability.

Custodianship of Heirloom Blade Yubashiri

Legal/social ownership of Yubashiri, a Ryō Wazamono (21 Great Grade swords) passed down in his family, which he gifts to Zoro. This is a possession-based ability, not a personal power.

Limit: He does not wield Yubashiri effectively; its later destruction (by Marine Captain Shu's Sabi Sabi no Mi rust power in Chapter 212) is unrelated to him. He cannot reclaim it after gifting.

Accounts I
Zoro Enters the Ippon-Matsu Arms ShopOne Piece manga Chapter 97; anime Episode 49

1999 (in-universe: shortly after Straw Hats arrive in Loguetown, concurrent with Buggy/Alvida alliance)

Roronoa Zoro, having broken two of his three swords against Mihawk, seeks replacements in Loguetown. He enters Ipponmatsu's shop looking for cheap katanas. Ipponmatsu, noting Zoro's shabby appearance, tries to sell him inferior blades at inflated prices while hiding his best stock. Zoro, unimpressed, scans the bargain bins and pulls out the Sandai Kitetsu, drawn to its sharpness despite its ominous reputation. Ipponmatsu panics and warns him the blade is cursed. Establishes Ipponmatsu's mercenary introduction and the cursed-sword lore that will define his arc; sets up the test of worthiness.

The Curse Test of Sandai KitetsuOne Piece manga Chapter 97; anime Episode 49

1999

Ipponmatsu explains that every previous owner of Sandai Kitetsu died miserably and that the sword is a cursed Wazamono (Grade Sword) forged by Kitetsu. Zoro, to test luck versus curse, throws the blade spinning into the air and extends his arm, letting it fall — declaring he will let fate decide. The blade spirals around his arm without cutting him. Ipponmatsu and onlookers are stunned; Ipponmatsu interprets this as proof Zoro can master the curse, where lesser men could not. Zoro earns the right to wield Sandai Kitetsu; Ipponmatsu's fear turns to awe and triggers his change of heart. The scene becomes a canonical illustration of One Piece's theme of luck, will, and cursed weapons.

Accounts II
The Gift of Yubashiri — Heirloom EntrustedOne Piece manga Chapter 98; anime Episode 49

1999

Moved by Zoro's nerve, Ipponmatsu confesses his earlier deception and reveals his family's heirloom: Yubashiri (Snow Walker), a light, finely crafted Ryō Wazamono (21 Great Grade) worth far more than anything in the shop. He explains it has been in his family for generations and he had refused to sell it even in debt. Recognizing Zoro as a true swordsman who will honor the blade, he gifts Yubashiri to Zoro alongside Sandai Kitetsu, asking only a token payment for the cursed blade to seal the transaction. Zoro leaves with two new swords to complement his Wado Ichimonji. Zoro's three-sword style is restored with two new Meito that will accompany him through Alabasta to Enies Lobby; Ipponmatsu is established as a generous benefactor figure. Yubashiri later becomes narratively significant when it is destroyed on Jaya/Sky Piea arc, marking the end of its lineage.

Tashigi's Patronage and the Shop's ReputationOne Piece manga Chapter 96-98 (background); anime Episodes 48-49; Vivre Card

1999 (with retroactive databook elaboration 2018)

Marine Warrant Officer Tashigi, stationed in Loguetown under Captain Smoker, is a regular at Ipponmatsu's shop, known for her encyclopedic knowledge of Meito and her inability to afford them. Ipponmatsu knows her by sight and is both annoyed and intimidated by her inspections — she can identify swords by name and grade. His willingness to gift Yubashiri to Zoro is contrasted with his refusal to sell it to Tashigi, underscoring his judgment that Zoro, not the Marine collector, is the blade's true wielder. Tashigi later encounters Zoro outside the shop, foreshadowing their Kuina-related connection. World-building for Loguetown's Marine-civilian economy and for Tashigi's character as a sword otaku; explains why Yubashiri was still available. Reinforces Ipponmatsu's role as gatekeeper of Meito in East Blue.

Aftermath: The Empty Pedestal and Loguetown's MemoryOne Piece manga Chapter 100-101 (departure from Loguetown); anime Episode 52; fan-observed absence in later arcs

1999-2000 (in-universe: immediately after Loguetown arc; meta: post-arc absence)

After Zoro departs, Ipponmatsu's shop is left with an empty display where Yubashiri once rested. He is not shown again in the manga's present timeline; his shop is not revisited when Loguetown is later referenced (e.g., Smoker's transfer, cover stories). Databooks list him as a Loguetown resident with no further bounty, battle, or death record. His narrative function is complete: a one-arc benefactor whose generosity enables Zoro's next power level. In-universe, townspeople later recall the day a pirate swordsman tamed the cursed blade. Ipponmatsu becomes a static, remembered figure — archetypal 'wise merchant who recognizes the hero.' His swords continue to carry his legacy; Yubashiri's later rust-destruction (Chapter 212, by Captain Shu) closes his heirloom's story without his direct involvement.

Ipponmatsu is a powerful swordsman or former pirate who can wield Meito. — He is a civilian merchant with no combat ability; he fears the cursed blade and cannot wield Yubashiri effectively. His expertise is appraisal, not swordsmanship.

Ipponmatsu forged Yubashiri and Sandai Kitetsu himself. — Sandai Kitetsu was forged by the Kitetsu lineage (in lore, by the Kitetsu smiths); Yubashiri is a family heirloom he inherited, not forged. He is a retailer, not a craftsman.

Ipponmatsu appears in the Netflix One Piece live-action series. — As of Season 1 (2023, covering East Blue), Loguetown was condensed and Ipponmatsu did not appear on screen. No live-action casting is attested.

Ipponmatsu is a yōkai or folklore figure (Ippon-Datara). — Ipponmatsu the One Piece character is a modern fictional human. The similarly named yōkai is Ippon-Datara / Ippondatara, a one-eyed, one-legged mountain spirit — a distinct folklore subject with no narrative connection.
