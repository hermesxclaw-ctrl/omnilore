The Approach

In Loguetown, the town where Gold Roger was born and executed, a nervous shopkeeper keeps two blades he is afraid to sell — one that has killed every owner who drew it, and one that has never tasted blood. When a lost swordsman with three earrings walks in looking for a cheap replacement, Ipponmatsu has to decide whether to cheat him or trust him with a family heirloom.

Content advisory. Mild content advisory: discussion of cursed weapons and fatal sword accidents (fictional), brief depiction of commercial deception and debt. No sexual violence, graphic gore, or hate content attested for this character.
Identity

Ipponmatsu

Pronunciation Japanese: /ip.poɴ.ma.tsɯ/ — ip-pon-ma-tsu (four morae, pitch accent varies by dialect; English fandom often anglicizes as ih-PON-maht-su). Hyphenated form Ippon-Matsu reflects shop-name parsing.

Known As

Ippon-Matsu (hyphenated English rendering)いっぽん松 (Japanese kana rendering commonly cited)Ipponmatsu-san (honorific form used in dialogue)

Etymology

Japanese 一本松 (ippon-matsu), literally 'single pine tree' or 'one pine tree'. Ippon is counter for slender objects (trees, swords) + matsu 'pine'. The name is a common Japanese toponym and personal/shop name; as a fictional shop name it evokes a solitary pine as a landmark. The 'Ippon-Matsu Arms Shop' (Ippon-Matsu Buki-ten) uses the same reading.

Cultural Ownership

If the Q107736360 identification is correct, the character is owned by Eiichiro Oda / Shueisha / Toei Animation as part of the One Piece franchise (1997-present), a Japanese shōnen manga/anime property with global distribution. The name itself (Ipponmatsu) is not culturally restricted and is also a widespread Japanese place name. No independent folklore ownership claim is attested for this specific fictional character.

Description

Appearance

Attested (manga Chapters 97-98, anime Episode 49): Ipponmatsu is depicted as a middle-aged human man of average-to-stout build, light skin, short dark hair with receding hairline, thin mustache, and round eyeglasses. He wears a patterned kimono-style shop coat over a dark under-robe, typical of a Loguetown merchant. His demeanor is nervous and sweaty when handling the Kitetsu blades, contrasting with pride when presenting Yubashiri. No supernatural or non-human traits are depicted. Databook art (Vivre Card) reproduces this design without variation. No alternative costume or age variant is attested; post-timeskip reappearance has not been shown in manga as of 2024.

Symbols & Iconography

His iconography is mercantile rather than martial: the Ippon-Matsu Arms Shop sign (with pine-tree motif implied by name), rows of displayed katana, and two specific swords as narrative symbols — the cursed Sandai Kitetsu (red scabbard, wavy hamon, reputed to bring death to owners) and the heirloom Yubashiri (light, well-balanced Ryō Wazamono with plain black scabbard). He is associated with the Meito (Named Swords) grading system and the Kitetsu curse lore, not with a personal crest or weapon. The pine-tree name itself is the only heraldic symbol.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: Initially mercenary and deceptive — he attempts to overcharge Zoro and to pass off the cursed Sandai Kitetsu as a bargain while concealing its history, motivated by debt and fear of the blade. After Zoro tests the curse by risking his arm and demonstrates luck/nerve, Ipponmatsu undergoes a rapid moral shift: awe, remorse, and generosity. He gifts Zoro both Sandai Kitetsu (at a steep discount / effectively free in anime) and his family's heirloom Yubashiri (a Ryō Wazamono, 50-million-berry class blade), stating Zoro is worthy. He is also shown as deferential and fearful toward Marine officer Tashigi, who frequents his shop. No cruelty or combativeness is shown; he is a civilian shopkeeper whose courage is moral rather than physical.

Domains & Habitats

Domain: civilian commerce in Loguetown (Roguetown), East Blue — the 'Town of the Beginning and the End' where Pirate King Gol D. Roger was born and executed. His habitat is the Ippon-Matsu Arms Shop, a ground-floor storefront with sword racks, located in the town's commercial district near the execution platform. He does not travel, fight, or hold territory beyond the shop. His social domain is the world of Meito connoisseurship and East Blue arms dealing, with connections to Marine personnel (Tashigi) and passing pirates.

Version Differences

No major redesign between manga and anime; anime adds filler dialogue and extends the curse-test tension but does not alter outcome. English translations vary: VIZ renders Sandai Kitetsu as 'Third Generation Demon Splitter' and Yubashiri as 'Snow Walker' (later standardized as Yubashiri). The hyphenation Ippon-Matsu vs Ipponmatsu varies by translation; Vivre Card uses 'Ippon-Matsu'. No alternate universe, live-action, or remake version of the character has appeared — he is absent from the Netflix live-action Season 1 (2023) which adapts East Blue but condenses Loguetown, and has no video-game-exclusive variant design. Power-scaling or age changes are not attested.

Powers & Abilities

Meito Appraisal and Sword Lore

Source VersionManga Chapters 97-98; Vivre Card; One Piece Yellow databook

Expert knowledge of graded swords (Meito) and cursed blades, specifically the Kitetsu lineage (Shodai, Nidai, Sandai Kitetsu) and the Ryō Wazamono grade of Yubashiri. He can identify grade, value, and curse reputation on sight.

Limitations

Knowledge is commercial and folkloric, not supernatural; he fears the Sandai Kitetsu and cannot wield or purify it. He misjudges Zoro's worthiness initially.

Contradictions

None; consistent across sources. Fan wikis sometimes inflate him to 'master swordsmith' — he is explicitly a dealer, not a smith.

Merchant Negotiation (Civilian Skill)

Source VersionManga Chapter 97-98

Ability to set prices, haggle, and assess customers. Initially attempts to exploit Zoro's ignorance, later negotiates a symbolic price for the cursed blade.

Limitations

Poor judgment under financial pressure; easily intimidated by Tashigi and by Zoro's curse test. No combat or leadership ability.

Contradictions

None attested.

Custodianship of Heirloom Blade Yubashiri

Source VersionManga Chapter 98

Legal/social ownership of Yubashiri, a Ryō Wazamono (21 Great Grade swords) passed down in his family, which he gifts to Zoro. This is a possession-based ability, not a personal power.

Limitations

He does not wield Yubashiri effectively; its later destruction (by Marine Captain Shu's Sabi Sabi no Mi rust power in Chapter 212) is unrelated to him. He cannot reclaim it after gifting.

Contradictions

Some fan summaries claim he forged Yubashiri — contradicted by manga which states it is a family heirloom he inherited, not forged.

Stories & Myths

Zoro Enters the Ippon-Matsu Arms Shop

Source One Piece manga Chapter 97; anime Episode 49Culture Japanese shōnen manga (One Piece, East Blue Saga, Loguetown Arc)Period 1999 (in-universe: shortly after Straw Hats arrive in Loguetown, concurrent with Buggy/Alvida alliance)

Summary

Roronoa Zoro, having broken two of his three swords against Mihawk, seeks replacements in Loguetown. He enters Ipponmatsu's shop looking for cheap katanas. Ipponmatsu, noting Zoro's shabby appearance, tries to sell him inferior blades at inflated prices while hiding his best stock. Zoro, unimpressed, scans the bargain bins and pulls out the Sandai Kitetsu, drawn to its sharpness despite its ominous reputation. Ipponmatsu panics and warns him the blade is cursed.

Participants

Ipponmatsu

Roronoa Zoro

Consequences

Establishes Ipponmatsu's mercenary introduction and the cursed-sword lore that will define his arc; sets up the test of worthiness.

Variants

Anime extends the haggling and adds reaction shots; manga is more concise. English VIZ translation adds explanatory notes on Kitetsu curse.

The Curse Test of Sandai Kitetsu

Source One Piece manga Chapter 97; anime Episode 49Culture Japanese shōnen mangaPeriod 1999

Summary

Ipponmatsu explains that every previous owner of Sandai Kitetsu died miserably and that the sword is a cursed Wazamono (Grade Sword) forged by Kitetsu. Zoro, to test luck versus curse, throws the blade spinning into the air and extends his arm, letting it fall — declaring he will let fate decide. The blade spirals around his arm without cutting him. Ipponmatsu and onlookers are stunned; Ipponmatsu interprets this as proof Zoro can master the curse, where lesser men could not.

Participants

Ipponmatsu

Roronoa Zoro

Sandai Kitetsu (cursed sword as agent)

Shop onlookers

Consequences

Zoro earns the right to wield Sandai Kitetsu; Ipponmatsu's fear turns to awe and triggers his change of heart. The scene becomes a canonical illustration of One Piece's theme of luck, will, and cursed weapons.

Variants

Anime dramatizes the spin with slow motion and sound effects; manga uses three panels. No contradictory version where Zoro is cut exists.

The Gift of Yubashiri — Heirloom Entrusted

Source One Piece manga Chapter 98; anime Episode 49Culture Japanese shōnen mangaPeriod 1999

Summary

Moved by Zoro's nerve, Ipponmatsu confesses his earlier deception and reveals his family's heirloom: Yubashiri (Snow Walker), a light, finely crafted Ryō Wazamono (21 Great Grade) worth far more than anything in the shop. He explains it has been in his family for generations and he had refused to sell it even in debt. Recognizing Zoro as a true swordsman who will honor the blade, he gifts Yubashiri to Zoro alongside Sandai Kitetsu, asking only a token payment for the cursed blade to seal the transaction. Zoro leaves with two new swords to complement his Wado Ichimonji.

Participants

Ipponmatsu

Roronoa Zoro

Yubashiri (heirloom sword)

Consequences

Zoro's three-sword style is restored with two new Meito that will accompany him through Alabasta to Enies Lobby; Ipponmatsu is established as a generous benefactor figure. Yubashiri later becomes narratively significant when it is destroyed on Jaya/Sky Piea arc, marking the end of its lineage.

Variants

Anime has Ipponmatsu explicitly say 'take them' without payment for Yubashiri; manga implies a nominal fee for Sandai Kitetsu. No variant where he retains Yubashiri.

Tashigi's Patronage and the Shop's Reputation

Source One Piece manga Chapter 96-98 (background); anime Episodes 48-49; Vivre CardCulture Japanese shōnen mangaPeriod 1999 (with retroactive databook elaboration 2018)

Summary

Marine Warrant Officer Tashigi, stationed in Loguetown under Captain Smoker, is a regular at Ipponmatsu's shop, known for her encyclopedic knowledge of Meito and her inability to afford them. Ipponmatsu knows her by sight and is both annoyed and intimidated by her inspections — she can identify swords by name and grade. His willingness to gift Yubashiri to Zoro is contrasted with his refusal to sell it to Tashigi, underscoring his judgment that Zoro, not the Marine collector, is the blade's true wielder. Tashigi later encounters Zoro outside the shop, foreshadowing their Kuina-related connection.

Participants

Ipponmatsu

Tashigi

Smoker (mentioned as Tashigi's superior)

Roronoa Zoro (indirect)

Consequences

World-building for Loguetown's Marine-civilian economy and for Tashigi's character as a sword otaku; explains why Yubashiri was still available. Reinforces Ipponmatsu's role as gatekeeper of Meito in East Blue.

Variants

Anime adds a brief Tashigi browsing scene not in manga; Vivre Card confirms Tashigi's frequent visits. No contradictory account where Tashigi purchases Yubashiri.

Aftermath: The Empty Pedestal and Loguetown's Memory

Source One Piece manga Chapter 100-101 (departure from Loguetown); anime Episode 52; fan-observed absence in later arcsCulture Japanese shōnen mangaPeriod 1999-2000 (in-universe: immediately after Loguetown arc; meta: post-arc absence)

Summary

After Zoro departs, Ipponmatsu's shop is left with an empty display where Yubashiri once rested. He is not shown again in the manga's present timeline; his shop is not revisited when Loguetown is later referenced (e.g., Smoker's transfer, cover stories). Databooks list him as a Loguetown resident with no further bounty, battle, or death record. His narrative function is complete: a one-arc benefactor whose generosity enables Zoro's next power level. In-universe, townspeople later recall the day a pirate swordsman tamed the cursed blade.

Participants

Ipponmatsu

Loguetown townspeople (implied)

Roronoa Zoro (absent, remembered)

Consequences

Ipponmatsu becomes a static, remembered figure — archetypal 'wise merchant who recognizes the hero.' His swords continue to carry his legacy; Yubashiri's later rust-destruction (Chapter 212, by Captain Shu) closes his heirloom's story without his direct involvement.

Variants

No manga chapter shows his reaction to Yubashiri's destruction; that event occurs far from Loguetown. Some fan fiction imagines a reunion — non-canonical. Anime does not add a post-timeskip cameo.

Relationships
Ipponmatsu→Roronoa Zorobenefactor / customer

Ipponmatsu is Zoro's Loguetown arms dealer and benefactor. He first attempts to cheat Zoro, then after the curse test gifts him Sandai Kitetsu and Yubashiri, enabling Zoro's three-sword style. Zoro respects the gift and carries both blades until Yubashiri's destruction. Relationship is one-arc, transactional turned reverent.

Ipponmatsu→Tashigiregular customer / intimidator

Marine Warrant Officer Tashigi frequents Ipponmatsu's shop to admire Meito she cannot afford. He knows her expertise and is wary of her inspections. He withholds Yubashiri from her, judging Zoro more worthy. Their dynamic is comic-deferential.

Ipponmatsu→Yubashiri (Meito)ownership / heirloom

Family heirloom Ryō Wazamono (one of the 21 Great Grade swords) kept by Ipponmatsu for generations, never for sale. He entrusts it to Zoro. Later destroyed by Captain Shu's Rust-Rust Fruit (Sabi Sabi no Mi) on Jaya, ending its lineage.

Ipponmatsu→Sandai Kitetsu (Cursed Sword)ownership / custodianship

Cursed Wazamono-grade sword of the Kitetsu lineage (forged by Kitetsu, third generation). Ipponmatsu kept it hidden due to its lethal reputation, then sold/gifted it to Zoro after Zoro tamed its curse. Later wielded by Zoro alongside Enma.

Ipponmatsu→Smoker (Captain, Loguetown)authority / indirect

Marine Captain Smoker commands Loguetown and is Tashigi's superior. Ipponmatsu operates under Marine jurisdiction; Smoker's presence frames Loguetown as a Marine stronghold. No direct dialogue between Ipponmatsu and Smoker is attested, but Ipponmatsu's deference to Tashigi reflects Smoker's authority.

Ipponmatsu→Kuina (via Tashigi resemblance)thematic / indirect

No direct relationship, but Ipponmatsu's scene parallels Zoro's promise to Kuina (his childhood rival who wielded Wado Ichimonji). Tashigi's resemblance to Kuina, noted by Zoro outside the shop, adds thematic weight to Ipponmatsu's judgment that Zoro will honor swords — echoing Kuina's dream of being the world's greatest swordsman.

Cursed Sword Lore and Fatal Accidents

Source Manga Chapter 97; One Piece Yellow (Kitetsu lore)

Description

Sandai Kitetsu is presented as a cursed blade that brings violent death to unworthy owners. Ipponmatsu states every previous owner died miserably, and he feared selling it. Zoro's curse test (letting the spinning blade fall toward his arm) dramatizes lethal risk. This is fictional sword folklore within One Piece, not real-world historical practice.

Scholarly Context

One Piece uses Japanese katana folklore (cursed Muramasa-type legends) as world-building. The curse is narrative, not supernatural gore; no graphic death is shown on-page for Ipponmatsu's customers.

Commercial Deception and Debt Pressure

Source Manga Chapter 97-98

Description

Ipponmatsu initially attempts to overcharge and deceive Zoro, concealing the best blades and misrepresenting value, motivated by debt and fear of being stuck with the cursed sword. He confesses and repents within the same scene. The behavior is framed as cowardly but redeemable, not as systemic exploitation.

Scholarly Context

Common shōnen trope of the flawed merchant redeemed by the hero's virtue; no real-world fraud case or historical allegation is involved.

Contradictions & Disputes

Name spelling and hyphenation

Position A

Canonical spelling is 'Ipponmatsu' (single word, as in Wikidata Q107736360 and Japanese 一本松).

Position A — evidence

Wikidata label Q107736360 and Japanese toponym standard use Ipponmatsu as one word.

Position B

Official English materials and fan wikis use 'Ippon-Matsu' (hyphenated) to reflect shop name parsing: Ippon-Matsu Arms Shop.

Position B — evidence

VIZ translation, Vivre Card English summaries, and One Piece Wiki hyphenate as Ippon-Matsu to clarify 'one pine' shop name.

Scholarly Consensus

Both refer to same character; hyphenation is translation style, not a separate identity. No scholarly dispute.

Was Yubashiri sold or gifted?

Position A

Yubashiri was gifted as a family heirloom, not sold.

Position A — evidence

Manga Chapter 98 dialogue: Ipponmatsu says he will not sell it at any price but will entrust it to Zoro; he asks only a nominal price for Sandai Kitetsu to make the transaction auspicious.

Position B

Some fan summaries state Zoro purchased Yubashiri.

Position B — evidence

Anime English dub and some wiki paraphrases compress dialogue to 'take them,' leading to purchase misreading.

Scholarly Consensus

Primary manga supports gift/heirloom entrustment; purchase reading is a paraphrase error.

Is Ipponmatsu a swordsmith?

Position A

He is a sword dealer/shop owner, not a smith.

Position A — evidence

Manga explicitly calls him owner of Ippon-Matsu Arms Shop (Buki-ten), a retailer; he displays but does not forge blades.

Position B

Occasional fan descriptions call him a swordsmith.

Position B — evidence

Conflation with other One Piece smiths (e.g., Kitetsu the smith, Tenguyama Hitetsu) and generic 'arms shop = smith' assumption.

Scholarly Consensus

Dealer, not smith — consistent across databooks.
