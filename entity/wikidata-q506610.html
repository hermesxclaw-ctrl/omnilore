Who It Is

The great thunder-god of Kamo wept so loudly as a baby that his mother had to sail him around the whole of Japan just to calm him down — and the boom you hear in a summer storm is, the oldest Japanese chronicle insists, nothing more than that divine infant's tantrum echoing across the islands. Then, grown, he once destroyed a funeral for a friend who looked too much like him — because being mistaken for a corpse, in a world ruled by the fear of kotodama and defilement, was an insult no god could swallow silently. This is Ajisukitakahikone: the storm-wielding, pride-violent, ever-radiant son of the great land-master Ōkuninushi, whose living shrine still guards the gates of ancient Kyoto.

Ajisukitakahikone is a Shinto kami of thunder and agriculture, a son of the great terrestrial deity Ōkuninushi (head of the earthly kunitsukami) by Tagori-hime, one of the three Munakata goddesses who guard the sea routes between Japan and the Korean peninsula. His sister is Shitateruhime (also Takahime), and he is the father of the waterfall god Takitsuhiko ('Prince Cataract') and nephew-by-marriage of the thunder/combat god Takemikazuchi. In the Kojiki he is portrayed as a close friend of the heavenly messenger Ame-no-Wakahiko, whom the high gods sent down to demand Ōkuninushi&#

Powers
Thunder generation / control

Ajisukitakahikone is classed among the thunder gods. The Kojiki explains the growling sound of thunder as the echo of his crying while his mother carried him up and down a ladder as an infant — an etiological myth, but one that firmly attaches the thundering register to him. He is listed in Category:Thunder gods and in lists of thunder/agricultural deities.

Limit:

Radiant / luminous aspect

After destroying the funeral hut, Ajisukitakahikone is described as flying off with a radiance that 'illuminated the space of two hills and two valleys' — a solar/luminous divine display that is a recurring motif for high kami in the Kojiki and that, in his legend, finally distinguishes him from the (now-corpse) Ame-no-Wakahiko he resembled.

Limit:

Ten-span sword (totsuka no tsurugi) wielding

In his anger he draws his ten-span sword and physically hacks the funeral hut to pieces, then kicks it away — a display of the destructive martial power that also characterises his relatives Takemikazuchi and the thunder deity Kamo Wake-ikazuchi at the Kamo shrines.

Limit:

Agricultural / ploughing patronage

His name's 'suki' element ('spade'/'plough') and his classification as an agricultural deity link him to farming prosperity; the Kamo shrines he patronises were historically the objects of prayers for a successful rice harvest (Shimogamo's association with the annual rice-harvest prayers).
