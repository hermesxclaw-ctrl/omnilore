Who It Is

Scratch is a name that appears across cultures, media, and disciplines—from a ghost in a children's cartoon to a programming language used by millions. Yet its most enduring identity may be that of a folkloric devil, a shadowy tempter known in English tradition as 'Old Scratch.' This dossier explores the many faces of Scratch, separating fact from fiction and tracing its evolution from folktale to digital age.

In folklore, Old Scratch is cunning and deceptive, offering wealth and power in exchange for souls. In children's media, characters named Scratch are often mischievous but ultimately friendly. The programming language encourages creativity and learning.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The origin of 'Scratch' as a name for the devil is uncertain. It may derive from the Old Norse 'skratte' (goblin) or from the Middle English 'scrat' (devil). Some scholars suggest a connection to the act of scratching, as in marking a contract or the devil's claws.

Pronunciation — /skrætʃ/

Aliases — Old Scratch, Mr. Scratch, Scratchy, The Scratch

Ownership — Primarily English and American folklore, but the name has been adopted in various media globally.

Names are cult layers, not one fixed label.

Temptation and Contract Making

In folklore, Old Scratch offers worldly gains in exchange for the victim's soul, often with a written contract.

Limit: The devil's power is limited by the terms of the contract and the cleverness of the victim.

Shapeshifting

The devil is often said to be able to change his form to deceive humans.

Limit: Not always explicitly stated but implied in many tales.

Programming Language Features

Scratch allows users to create interactive stories, games, and animations using block-based coding.

Limit: Designed for educational purposes, not for professional software development.

Turntablism

Scratching is a DJ technique of moving a vinyl record back and forth to create rhythmic sounds.

Limit: Requires turntables and vinyl records.

Form & Signs

Appearance — In folklore, Old Scratch is often depicted as a dark, shadowy figure with cloven hooves, horns, and a tail, sometimes carrying a pitchfork. In modern media, the appearance varies widely: a ghost, a robot, an alpaca, etc. The programming language Scratch uses a friendly orange cat mascot.

Symbols — The devil is associated with crossroads, contracts, and fire. The programming language uses a cat as its logo, and the turntable technique uses vinyl records. In music, 'scratch' refers to a percussive sound.

Domains — The devil is often associated with wilderness, crossroads, and hell. The programming language exists in the digital realm. The turntable technique is found in music studios and clubs.

Accounts I
The Devil and Tom WalkerWashington Irving

19th century

Tom Walker makes a deal with the devil, who calls himself 'Old Scratch,' for buried treasure. Tom becomes a usurer but eventually tries to cheat the devil and is carried away on a black horse. Tom loses his soul and his wealth; the devil gets his due.

Accounts II
The Devil and Daniel WebsterStephen Vincent Benét

20th century

Jabez Stone sells his soul to 'Mr. Scratch' and is defended by Daniel Webster in a trial before a jury of damned souls. Webster wins by appealing to their American patriotism. Stone is freed from the contract.

Scratch (programming language)MIT Media Lab

21st century

Scratch was developed to teach children programming through a visual, block-based interface. It has become one of the most popular educational tools worldwide. Has influenced computer science education and inspired similar platforms.
