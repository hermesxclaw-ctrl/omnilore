The Approach

Scratch is a name that appears across cultures, media, and disciplines—from a ghost in a children's cartoon to a programming language used by millions. Yet its most enduring identity may be that of a folkloric devil, a shadowy tempter known in English tradition as 'Old Scratch.' This dossier explores the many faces of Scratch, separating fact from fiction and tracing its evolution from folktale to digital age.

Content advisory. Some versions of Old Scratch involve themes of temptation, damnation, and violence, which may be disturbing to some readers. The programming language Scratch is designed for children and contains no such content.

Etymology

The origin of 'Scratch' as a name for the devil is uncertain. It may derive from the Old Norse 'skratte' (goblin) or from the Middle English 'scrat' (devil). Some scholars suggest a connection to the act of scratching, as in marking a contract or the devil's claws.

Cultural Ownership

Primarily English and American folklore, but the name has been adopted in various media globally.

Disputed Identifications

The name 'Scratch' is often conflated with other devil figures such as Satan, Beelzebub, and Mephistopheles, but it is a distinct folkloric persona, particularly in tales of Faustian bargains.

Description

Appearance

In folklore, Old Scratch is often depicted as a dark, shadowy figure with cloven hooves, horns, and a tail, sometimes carrying a pitchfork. In modern media, the appearance varies widely: a ghost, a robot, an alpaca, etc. The programming language Scratch uses a friendly orange cat mascot.

Symbols & Iconography

The devil is associated with crossroads, contracts, and fire. The programming language uses a cat as its logo, and the turntable technique uses vinyl records. In music, 'scratch' refers to a percussive sound.

Behaviour & Personality

In folklore, Old Scratch is cunning and deceptive, offering wealth and power in exchange for souls. In children's media, characters named Scratch are often mischievous but ultimately friendly. The programming language encourages creativity and learning.

Domains & Habitats

The devil is often associated with wilderness, crossroads, and hell. The programming language exists in the digital realm. The turntable technique is found in music studios and clubs.

Version Differences

The name is used for radically different entities: a folkloric devil, a programming language, a ghost in a cartoon, a robot, an alpaca, a musician, etc. These versions have little in common besides the name.
