Who It Is

Atabey (or Atabeyra, Atabex) is the great mother-goddess of the Taíno people — the feminine source of all life, who gave birth to Yúcahu without a father (a 'virgin birth' motif recorded by Ramón Pané). She is the goddess of fresh and salt waters: rivers, lakes, rain, and the sea are all her manifestations and her 'body.' Atabey is also the patroness of women in childbirth — the moon, whose phases regulate the female cycle, is her celestial aspect, and the coquí frog (whose call announces the rainy season) is her animal messenger. The Taíno carved small female figures representing Atabey in wood, stone, or cotton (the 'cotton goddess' figures mentioned by Columbus). These female cemis were kept in households and petitioned for fertility, healthy children, and abundance. Atabey occupied a distinct but complementary sphere to Yúcahu: she governed the 'soft' forces of the world (water, blood, moonlight, birth) while he governed the 'hard' forces (cassava, stone, the embedded trigonolite, salt sea).
