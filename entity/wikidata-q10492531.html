Who It Is

In the misty mountains of Fujian, a mortal woman named Chen Jinggu defied the heavens, died in childbirth, and returned as the goddess who commands the thunder, protects women in labor, and has been worshipped for over a thousand years. Her legend is a tapestry of sacrifice, vengeance, and maternal power—a story that has shaped the spiritual lives of millions across southern China and beyond.

Lady of Linshui is portrayed as a fierce protector of women and children, especially during childbirth. She is compassionate but also vengeful against evil spirits and demons. She is known for her martial prowess and her willingness to battle demons and even the heavens themselves. She is also a devoted daughter and wife, though her marriage is overshadowed by her tragic death.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Linshui' means 'near water' or 'waterside,' referring to her temple's location near a river. 'Lady' (Furen) is an honorific title. Her personal name, Chen Jinggu, is composed of 'Chen' (a common surname), 'Jing' (meaning calm or pure), and 'Gu' (meaning maiden or girl).

Pronunciation — Chen Jinggu: [tʂʰən tɕiŋ ku] (Mandarin); Linshui Furen: [lin ʂwei fu ʐən] (Mandarin). In Fujianese dialects, the names are pronounced differently.

Aliases — Chen Jinggu (陳靖姑), Linshui Furen, Chen Shisi Niang (陳十四娘), Saint Mother Chen (陳聖母), The Goddess of Childbirth, The Thunder Goddess

Powers
Control over childbirth

She can protect women during labor, ease pain, and ensure safe delivery. She is also believed to protect newborns from evil spirits.

Limit: Her power is believed to be conditional on the woman's moral conduct and devotion.

Command over thunder and rain

She can summon thunder, lightning, and rain to fight demons or to aid her worshippers.

Limit: She is said to have been stripped of some powers after her death, but she regained them through divine investiture.

Sword combat and exorcism

She wields a magical sword that can slay demons and break spells.

Limit: Her sword is specifically effective against evil spirits, but she must be ritually pure to use it.

Transformation and flight

She can transform into different forms, such as a bird or a dragon, and can fly through the air.

Limit: Not consistently mentioned.

Healing

She can cure illnesses, especially those affecting women and children.

Limit: She is said to have lost her healing powers after her death, but she still grants them to her devotees.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Lady of Linshui is typically depicted as a beautiful woman in imperial robes, often holding a sword or a ritual implement. She may be shown riding a dragon or a tiger, or standing on clouds. In some depictions, she is accompanied by two attendants, the 'Jie Zi' and 'Jie Nu' (the Boy and Girl who guard children). She is sometimes shown with a red mark on her forehead, symbolizing her divine power.

Symbols — Her primary symbols are the sword (often a bronze or wooden ritual sword), the dragon, the tiger, and the motherly protection of children. She is also associated with thunder and rain, and thus with dragons and weather phenomena. In temples, her statue is often placed in a side hall dedicated to childbirth and child protection.

Domains — Her primary domain is the Linshui region, associated with water and rivers. She is believed to reside in her temple in Gutian, Fujian, and to have authority over the realm of childbirth, children, and women's health. She also has a role as a thunder goddess, controlling storms and lightning.

Accounts I
The Birth of Chen JingguLinshui Furen Zhuan and oral tradition

Tang dynasty (618-907) according to legend

Chen Jinggu is born to a local official in Fuzhou or Gutian. Her birth is miraculous—she is born from a drop of blood that fell from the moon or from a celestial pearl. She shows extraordinary intelligence and spiritual aptitude from a young age. She is destined for a spiritual life.

Learning the DaoLinshui Furen Zhuan

Tang dynasty

Chen Jinggu leaves home to study Daoist arts under a master, often identified as Xu Xun or a female immortal. She learns magical spells, sword techniques, and the ability to command spirits. She becomes a powerful exorcist and healer.

Accounts II
The Marriage and Tragic DeathLinshui Furen Zhuan

Tang dynasty

Chen Jinggu is forced to marry a man named Liu Qi. She resists, but eventually agrees. She becomes pregnant, but during a drought, she must perform a rain-making ritual, which is forbidden for pregnant women. She miscarries or dies in childbirth as a result of the ritual. Her body is left in the wilderness, but she is revived by a celestial being and becomes a goddess. She becomes a goddess and is granted the title 'Lady of Linshui.'

Battling the White Snake DemonOral tradition and opera

Ming dynasty or later

Chen Jinggu battles a powerful white snake demon that terrorizes the region. She defeats it using her sword and magical powers, often with the help of her sworn sisters. She is celebrated as a demon-slayer.

Protecting Women in ChildbirthFolk belief

Ongoing

After her death, Chen Jinggu is believed to protect women during childbirth. She is often invoked by midwives and pregnant women. She is also believed to protect children from disease and evil spirits. She becomes the patron goddess of childbirth and children.
