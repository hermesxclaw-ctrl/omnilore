Who It Is

Not a person; behavior is institutional/commercial/linguistic. LaCroix water: market behavior — flavor innovation (pamplemousse, passionfruit), health-positioned '0-calorie, 0-sweetener' sparkling water, cult social-media following. Toponyms: administrative behavior — small rural communes (populations typically 200-4,000) with mayoral governance under French communal law. Powers
Toponymic persistence and administrative identity

Ability of the name-form to persist for centuries as official commune identifier, conferring postal, electoral, and cadastral identity. Example: Lacroix-Barrez attested since medieval charters, maintained through Revolutionary renaming and modern INSEE coding.

Limit: Limited to French administrative law; name can be changed by decree (rare). No supernatural power; purely legal-geographic function.

Editorial and moral authority (press)

La Croix newspaper exercises agenda-setting within French Catholic and generalist press: Vatican coverage, social doctrine, and since 2010s digital paywall reach (~100k print + digital subscribers). Recognized as 'presse d'opinion' with influence on Catholic intellectual life.

Limit: Circulation modest vs Le Monde/Le Figaro; editorial line constrained by Catholic identity and Bayard ownership; not a doctrinal authority of the Church.

Brand recognition and flavor proliferation (beverage)

LaCroix sparkling water achieved national US distribution and cult status via 2010s flavor expansion (20+ flavors) and social-media aesthetic, without traditional advertising. Market power: National Beverage reported LaCroix as driver of revenue growth 2015-2019.

Limit: No caffeine, sweetener, or functional ingredient; market share vulnerable to competitors (Spindrift, Waterloo); trademark disputes over 'LaCroix' pronunciation and spelling.

Eponymic memorialization (lunar crater)

Name confers permanent memorialization on lunar surface via IAU nomenclature; used in selenography, atlases, and amateur astronomy.

Limit: No physical influence; crater is degraded and not prominent for observation vs Tycho/Copernicus.

Accounts I
Foundation of La Croix newspaper by the AssumptionistsBnF Gallica first issue 16 June 1880; Bayard Presse corporate history; Brugerette, J. 'Le Père Bailly'

1880-1883 (monthly to daily)

Priest Vincent de Paul Bailly, of the Augustinians of the Assumption founded by Emmanuel d'Alzon, launches La Croix as a monthly in 1880, becoming a daily in 1883. Conceived as a Catholic response to secular republican press (notably anti-clerical laws of Jules Ferry), it adopts an explicitly Catholic, initially monarchist and intransigent editorial line, with the cross as masthead emblem. It survives the 1900 Assumptionist expulsion and evolves under Bayard Presse. Becomes France's leading Catholic daily; shapes Catholic opinion through Dreyfus Affair, World Wars, Vatican II, and into digital era at la-croix.com. Historical archive is primary source for French Catholic history.

La Crosse to LaCroix: the Wisconsin sparkling waterNational Beverage Corp. history page; USPTO TESS; Beverage Digest interviews 1992, 2015

1981-present

G. Heileman Brewing Company's La Crosse, Wisconsin plant creates a sparkling water line in 1981. National Beverage Corp. acquires the brand in 1992. Name is explained by the company as a portmanteau: La (from LaCrosse) + Croix (from the St. Croix River on the Wisconsin-Minnesota border). Originally regional, it expands nationally in the 2000s-2010s with pamplemousse (grapefruit) as flagship flavor, riding the unsweetened sparkling water trend. Becomes leading US sparkling water brand by volume 2015-2018, spawns internet fandom, meme culture ('LaCroix tastes like...'), and competitor wave. Name pronunciation becomes cultural shibboleth.

Naming of Lacroix crater for the mathematicianIAU Gazetteer of Planetary Nomenclature; Whitaker, E.A. 'Mapping and Naming the Moon' (1999)

1935 (IAU formalization; crater formation ~3.9 Ga)

Small impact crater at 37.9°S 59.0°W on the lunar near side is assigned the name Lacroix in the first standardized IAU lunar nomenclature (Blagg and Müller, 1935). Eponym is Sylvestre François Lacroix (1765-1843), French mathematician and textbook author whose 'Traité du calcul différentiel et intégral' dominated 19th-century teaching. The crater is 38 km diameter, with a degraded rim and central peak, lying northwest of Schickard. Permanent lunar memorial; appears in all modern lunar atlases (Rükl, USGS) and LRO data. Distinguishes from other Croix-named features.

Accounts II
Lacroix-Barrez and the Aveyron crossroadsINSEE COG; Dictionnaire topographique de l'Aveyron (Abbé Bousquet); Cassini maps 18th c.

Medieval attestation to present (commune created 1790)

Commune in northern Aveyron (pop. ~500) whose name derives from a monumental cross marking a barrez (Occitan barra = barrier/pass). Like many 'La Croix' communes, it originates as La Croix de Barrez, a hamlet around a wayside cross at a mountain pass on the Aubrac plateau. Formalized as a commune during the Revolutionary reorganization, it retains the solidified form Lacroix-Barrez. Illustrates the generic mechanism for all 20 listed communes: cross + qualifier (river, lord, region). Name preserves medieval Christian landscape marking.

Maurice Lacroix: from Desco trading to Swiss watch manufactureMaurice Lacroix SA official history; Europa Star watch press 1975-2020

1975-present

Zurich-based Desco von Schulthess, a silk and watch trading house since 1889, decides to create its own watch brand. In 1975 it establishes Maurice Lacroix in Saignelégier (Jura), named after a Desco board member, Maurice Lacroix (not a watchmaker). Initially assembling private-label watches, it moves to manufacture with the 2006 ML106 caliber and Masterpiece collection, positioning as 'accessible luxury' between Swatch Group and haute horlogerie. Brand becomes known for Aikon and Masterpiece lines; demonstrates how Lacroix surname migrates into commercial eponymy unrelated to cross symbolism.

The surname Lacroix: from cross-bearer to diasporaAlbert Dauzat, 'Dictionnaire étymologique des noms de famille' (1951); INSEE Fichier des noms patronymiques; Quebec PRDH

12th century to present

Lacroix ranks among the most common French surnames (top 20, ~70,000 bearers in France). Origin is topographic/habitational for someone living near a cross, or occasionally a nickname for a crusader or cross-bearer in processions. Concentrated in Nord, Lorraine, and Bourgogne. Emigration carries it to Quebec (where Lacroix is top 50), Louisiana, and the US. Notable bearers include mathematician Sylvestre François Lacroix, chemist Alfred Lacroix, and fictional Lucien LaCroix (Forever Knight). Explains frequency of Lacroix toponyms and crater eponym; surname becomes global via diaspora and commercial use.

LaCroix sparkling water is French and contains French spring water — LaCroix is American, produced in the US (Wisconsin, etc.) with US-sourced water; name is a Wisconsin-Minnesota portmanteau, not an import. It contains only carbonated water and natural essences.

La Croix newspaper is the official Vatican newspaper in French — La Croix is owned by Bayard Presse (Assumptionists) and is editorially independent; the Vatican's daily is L'Osservatore Romano (and Vatican News). La Croix is respected for Vatican coverage but not an organ of the Holy See.

Lacroix is pronounced 'la-crocks' and spelled as one word in all contexts — In French, La Croix is two words, /la.kʁwa/; as a surname it is solidified Lacroix; the beverage stylizes as LaCroix but communes and newspaper use La Croix. Pronunciation varies by referent.

Maurice Lacroix and Christian Lacroix are the same house / family — Maurice Lacroix is a Swiss watch brand named for a Desco executive; Christian Lacroix is a French couturier from Arles. No familial or corporate link.

All La Croix communes are named for the same cross or for the newspaper — Each commune's 'Croix' refers to a distinct local wayside cross (croix de chemin) at a crossroads, pass, or parish boundary, predating the newspaper by centuries. The newspaper took its name from the same generic symbol, not vice versa.
