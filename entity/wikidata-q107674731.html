The Approach

A white rabbit with a broken wooden sword, a fox who throws purple bombs, and a levitating frog who will do anything for carrot ramen — Ninjin is Brazil's carrot-fueled answer to the ninja epic, a scrappy, self-aware comedy that began as an indie beat-'em-up game and became Cartoon Network Brazil's second original series.

Content advisory. No graphic content. Series is rated for children (Livre). Contains slapstick cartoon violence, comedic peril, and mild antagonism (army of anthropomorphic animals, Shogun villain). No sexual content or hate content attested.
Identity

Ninjin

Pronunciation Brazilian Portuguese: neen-ZHEEN /nĩˈʒĩ/; Japanese source: nin-jin /ɲiɴdʑiɴ/. English-language game press often anglicizes as NIN-jin.

Known As

Ninjin (character)Ninjin: Clash of Carrots (game title)

Etymology

From Japanese 人参 / にんじん (ninjin) meaning 'carrot'. Chosen diegetically because the plot centers on stolen carrots and the protagonist is a rabbit; extradiegetically as a Japanese-flavored title fitting the ninja parody. Pronounced in Brazilian Portuguese as /nĩˈʒĩ/ (neen-ZHEEN), approximating Japanese /ɲiɴdʑiɴ/.

Cultural Ownership

Brazilian original creation. Created by Pocket Trap (Brazilian indie studio) and Roger Keesse (Brazilian comic artist and game director). Developed as a game IP (2018) then adapted as a television co-production by Pocket Trap and Birdo Studio for Cartoon Network Brazil (Turner Broadcasting System Latin America / Warner Bros. Discovery). Not a traditional folklore figure; no prior cultural ownership beyond Japanese loanword.

Disputed Identifications

Not to be confused with: (1) generic Japanese word 'ninjin' for carrot appearing in unrelated titles; (2) Ninjin the vegetable mascot characters in Japanese education; (3) fan-wiki conflation with Naruto or other ninja rabbits. Wikidata Q107674731 specifically denotes the Cartoon Network Brazil series/character. Some databases list the series as 'Ninjin: Clash of Carrots' conflating game and show; they are distinct works with shared characters.

Description

Appearance

Ninjin is depicted as a small anthropomorphic white rabbit (male, child/teen-coded) with long upright ears, large expressive eyes, and a simple, rounded cartoon design optimized for both pixel-art game sprites and 2D vector animation. In both game and series he wears a light ninja-inspired outfit (often a short gi/tunic) and wields an oversized wooden bokken-like sword with a visibly broken/chipped tip — his signature weapon and visual gag indicating inexperience and scrappiness. The design is intentionally cute and chibi-proportioned, contrasting with more menacing Shogun Troop designs. Animation by Birdo Studio uses flat colors, thick outlines, and exaggerated squash-and-stretch for comedy.

Symbols & Iconography

Core symbols: (1) The broken wooden sword — symbol of underdog apprenticeship, lineage of 'ancient ancestors' who were great ninjas, and non-lethal slapstick combat; (2) Carrots / carrot ramen — MacGuffin and cultural motivator, representing village sustenance and Flink's appetite; (3) Ninja headband / Sensei's dojo — parody of shōnen ninja tropes (Naruto, etc.) filtered through Brazilian humor. Color palette is bright, carrot-orange vs. natural greens, with purple accents for Akai's bombs.

Behaviour & Personality

Canonically eager, earnest, and somewhat naive. Ninjin desperately wants to become a great ninja like his ancestors and trains under the reluctant elderly rabbit Sensei. He is brave to the point of recklessness, loyal to friends Akai and Flink, and motivated by the quest to recover stolen carrots. Comedy derives from his overconfidence versus limited skill, his broken sword, and his earnest shōnen-hero dialogue. Attested in both game and series synopses; personality consistent across versions. No darker or anti-hero variant attested.

Domains & Habitats

Habitat: a rural village (carrot-farming community) and surrounding forests/mountains leading to Shogun Moe's fortress. Cosmology is light, self-contained cartoon world of anthropomorphic animals with elemental/magical abilities (Flink's powers) and feudal Japanese pastiche (Shogun, Sensei, kunoichi). Dojo/training ground is central recurring location. No elaborate afterlife or spirit realm attested; world is present-tense adventure-comedy.

Version Differences

Game (2018): Side-scrolling beat-'em-up / hack-and-slash with anime-inspired pixel art; Ninjin is a playable fighter with combo attacks, dash, and carrot-collecting mechanics; story told via stage interludes. Series (2019): Comedy-adventure with shorter runtimes; Ninjin's combat is toned down to slapstick (wooden sword, no lethal edge), more emphasis on friendship, training gags, and dialogue; runtime constraints (1/3/7 min) compress arcs. Visual style shifts from pixel art to clean 2D vector by Birdo Studio. Voice acting adds gendered performance (Carol Valença) for male rabbit character — no change to character gender identity. No attested manga or comic version beyond Roger Keesse's concept art.

Powers & Abilities

Bokken (Broken Wooden Sword) Combat

Source VersionGame: Ninjin: Clash of Carrots (2018) gameplay; Series: Cartoon Network Brazil (2019) - all episodes

Wields a large wooden sword with a broken tip as primary weapon. In game, used for light/heavy combos, aerial slashes, and special moves. In series, used for slapstick strikes, blocking, and comedic mishaps due to broken tip.

Limitations

Wooden, non-lethal; broken tip reduces reach and is played for comedy; requires close range; Ninjin is still an apprentice, so technique is imperfect and Sensei frequently corrects him.

Contradictions

Game allows exaggerated special attacks and carrot-powered upgrades not seen in series' more grounded slapstick.

Ninja Apprenticeship - Agility and Stealth (Training)

Source VersionSeries synopsis and episodes; Game tutorial stages

Basic ninja arts learned from Sensei: running, jumping, dodging, sneaking, and aspiring to ancestral ninja techniques. Portrayed as enthusiastic but incomplete training.

Limitations

Explicitly not yet a 'great ninja' — Sensei is reluctant and Ninjin frequently fails. No attested supernatural speed or teleportation for Ninjin himself (unlike Flink).

Contradictions

None; consistent underdog framing.

Team Synergy with Akai and Flink

Source VersionGame co-op mode; Series ensemble premise

Fights as part of trio. Combines his melee with Akai's ranged purple bombs and Flink's telekinesis/elemental control. In game, co-op multiplayer allows combined combos.

Limitations

Dependent on allies; solo feats are limited. No attested solo elemental power.

Contradictions

Game allows single-player Ninjin to succeed alone via player skill; series emphasizes need for teamwork.

Ancestral Aspiration (Narrative Drive)

Source VersionSeries bible / synopsis: 'like his ancient ancestors'

Motivational 'power' — invocation of ancient rabbit ninja ancestors as ideal. Not a supernatural inheritance but a cultural lineage Ninjin seeks to live up to.

Limitations

Ancestors not shown to grant powers; purely inspirational. No attested bloodline technique.

Contradictions

None.

Stories & Myths

The Theft of the Carrots - Shogun Moe's Invasion (Franchise Premise)

Source Ninjin: Clash of Carrots (Pocket Trap, 2018) and series synopsis (Cartoon Network Brazil, 2019)Culture Brazilian (contemporary animation/game)Period 2018-2019

Summary

The evil mole Shogun Moe, ruler of a feudal-styled army, orders his troops to steal all carrots from Ninjin's village, depriving the inhabitants of food and, for Flink, his beloved carrot ramen. Ninjin, Akai, and Flink volunteer to leave the village, train under Sensei, and journey to retrieve the carrots, confronting Shogun Moe's forces along the way. This is the inciting incident for both game campaign and series.

Relationships
Ninjin→Akaially

Female fox, fellow apprentice under Sensei, aspiring kunoichi. Friends with Ninjin and Flink; fights alongside Ninjin with purple bombs as ranged weapon. Dynamic is supportive and egalitarian — co-protagonist, not love interest attested.

Ninjin→Flinkally

Male frog, third member of trio. Possesses levitation, telekinesis, and elemental control. Friends with Ninjin and Akai; comic relief via appetite for carrot ramen and deadpan psychic powers. Provides magical support to Ninjin's melee.

Ninjin→Senseiteacher

Elderly rabbit master who trains Ninjin, Akai, and Flink. Reluctant, gruff, but responsible for their ninja education. Represents ancestral ninja lineage Ninjin idolizes. Voiced by Alfredo Rollo.

Ninjin→Shogun Moehostile

Evil mole shogun, primary antagonist. Commands the Shogun Troop and orders the theft of the village's carrots. Feudal Japanese parody villain; resides in fortress/castle. Ultimate target of heroes' quest.

Ninjin→General Jamhostile

Deer general who commands Shogun Moe's army in the field. Direct field antagonist who repeatedly confronts the trio. Voiced by Marco França. Pompous, militaristic, easily frustrated by heroes' antics.

Ninjin→Shogun Troop (Army)hostile

Collective of anthropomorphic animal soldiers serving Shogun Moe, led by General Jam. Includes various species as grunts and mini-bosses. Provides episodic obstacles and beat-'em-up fodder.

Ninjin→Village / Carrots (Home Community)creator

Ninjin's home village, carrot-farming community that is victim of theft. Motivates quest. Ancestors referenced as ancient great ninjas from same lineage/village.

Contradictions & Disputes

Episode count and format breakdown

Position A

Season 1 is exactly 22 episodes: 10x1 min + 7x3 min + 5x7 min miniseries

Position A — evidence

Cartoon Network Brazil press via ANMTV and Portuguese Wikipedia citing official format split

Position B

Some databases list 22 episodes without format split or count miniseries separately as distinct season

Position B — evidence

IMDb and some streaming listings show 22 episodes as flat list or 10 episodes only (shorts), due to incomplete ingestion after delisting

Scholarly Consensus

Position A is correct per primary press; flat listings are incomplete due to scrubbing and varied runtime handling.

Renewal / cancellation status

Position A

Series was not renewed for Season 2 and was scrubbed from official channels

Position A — evidence

Absence from HBO Max Latin America and Cartoon Network Latin America YouTube as of 2023, no announcement from Cartoon Network Brazil or Pocket Trap, and Portuguese Wikipedia stating 'was not renewed'

Position B

Some fan wikis list series as 'ongoing' or 'awaiting Season 2'

Position B — evidence

Outdated wiki pages not updated after 2019, assuming continuation

Scholarly Consensus

No evidence of renewal; series is effectively cancelled/ended after Season 1. Treated as complete but delisted.

Ninjin's voice actor gender vs character gender

Position A

Ninjin is male character voiced by female actress Carol Valença (common in Brazilian dubbing for young male characters)

Position A — evidence

Official character description: 'Ninjin is a male rabbit' and credits list Carol Valença

Position B

Occasional English database mislabels Ninjin as female due to voice actress

Position B — evidence

Automated databases infer gender from voice actor

Scholarly Consensus

Character is male; casting follows Brazilian animation convention (e.g., Gumball, etc.).
