Who It Is

A white rabbit with a broken wooden sword, a fox who throws purple bombs, and a levitating frog who will do anything for carrot ramen — Ninjin is Brazil's carrot-fueled answer to the ninja epic, a scrappy, self-aware comedy that began as an indie beat-'em-up game and became Cartoon Network Brazil's second original series.

Canonically eager, earnest, and somewhat naive. Ninjin desperately wants to become a great ninja like his ancestors and trains under the reluctant elderly rabbit Sensei. He is brave to the point of recklessness, loyal to friends Akai and Flink, and motivated by the quest to recover stolen carrots. Comedy derives from his overconfidence versus limited skill, his broken sword, and his earnest shōnen-hero dialogue. Attested in both game and series synopses; personality consistent across versions. No darker or anti-hero variant attested.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — From Japanese 人参 / にんじん (ninjin) meaning 'carrot'. Chosen diegetically because the plot centers on stolen carrots and the protagonist is a rabbit; extradiegetically as a Japanese-flavored title fitting the ninja parody. Pronounced in Brazilian Portuguese as /nĩˈʒĩ/ (neen-ZHEEN), approximating Japane

Pronunciation — Brazilian Portuguese: neen-ZHEEN /nĩˈʒĩ/; Japanese source: nin-jin /ɲiɴdʑiɴ/. English-language game press often anglicizes as NIN-jin.

Aliases — Ninjin (character), Ninjin: Clash of Carrots (game title)

Ownership — Brazilian original creation. Created by Pocket Trap (Brazilian indie studio) and Roger Keesse (Brazilian comic artist and game director). Developed as a game IP (2018) then adapted

Powers
Bokken (Broken Wooden Sword) Combat

Wields a large wooden sword with a broken tip as primary weapon. In game, used for light/heavy combos, aerial slashes, and special moves. In series, used for slapstick strikes, blocking, and comedic mishaps due to broken tip.

Limit: Wooden, non-lethal; broken tip reduces reach and is played for comedy; requires close range; Ninjin is still an apprentice, so technique is imperfect and Sensei frequently corrects him.

Ninja Apprenticeship - Agility and Stealth (Training)

Basic ninja arts learned from Sensei: running, jumping, dodging, sneaking, and aspiring to ancestral ninja techniques. Portrayed as enthusiastic but incomplete training.

Limit: Explicitly not yet a 'great ninja' — Sensei is reluctant and Ninjin frequently fails. No attested supernatural speed or teleportation for Ninjin himself (unlike Flink).

Team Synergy with Akai and Flink

Fights as part of trio. Combines his melee with Akai's ranged purple bombs and Flink's telekinesis/elemental control. In game, co-op multiplayer allows combined combos.

Limit: Dependent on allies; solo feats are limited. No attested solo elemental power.

Ancestral Aspiration (Narrative Drive)

Motivational 'power' — invocation of ancient rabbit ninja ancestors as ideal. Not a supernatural inheritance but a cultural lineage Ninjin seeks to live up to.

Limit: Ancestors not shown to grant powers; purely inspirational. No attested bloodline technique.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Ninjin is depicted as a small anthropomorphic white rabbit (male, child/teen-coded) with long upright ears, large expressive eyes, and a simple, rounded cartoon design optimized for both pixel-art game sprites and 2D vector animation. In both game and series he wears a light ninja-inspired outfit (often a short gi/tunic) and wields an oversized wooden bokken-like sword with a visibly broken/chipped tip — his signature weapon and visual gag indica

Symbols — Core symbols: (1) The broken wooden sword — symbol of underdog apprenticeship, lineage of 'ancient ancestors' who were great ninjas, and non-lethal slapstick combat; (2) Carrots / carrot ramen — MacGuffin and cultural motivator, representing village sustenance and Flink's appetite; (3) Ninja headband / Sensei's dojo — parody of shōnen ninja tropes (Naruto, etc.) filtered through Brazilian humor. Color palette is bright, carrot

Domains — Habitat: a rural village (carrot-farming community) and surrounding forests/mountains leading to Shogun Moe's fortress. Cosmology is light, self-contained cartoon world of anthropomorphic animals with elemental/magical abilities (Flink's powers) and feudal Japanese pastiche (Shogun, Sensei, kunoichi). Dojo/training ground is central recurring location. No elaborate afterlife or spirit realm attested; world is present-tense adventure-com

Accounts I
The Theft of the Carrots - Shogun Moe's Invasion (Franchise Premise)Ninjin: Clash of Carrots (Pocket Trap, 2018) and series synopsis (Cartoon Network Brazil, 2019)

2018-2019

The evil mole Shogun Moe, ruler of a feudal-styled army, orders his troops to steal all carrots from Ninjin's village, depriving the inhabitants of food and, for Flink, his beloved carrot ramen. Ninjin, Akai, and Flink volunteer to leave the village, train under Sensei, and journey to retrieve the carrots, confronting Shogun Moe's forces along the way. This is the inciting incident for both game campaign and series. Establishes quest structure, justifies beat-'em-up stages (game) and episodic travel (series), and frames carrot as MacGuffin.

Training with Sensei - The Reluctant Master (1-Minute Shorts Arc)Ninjin Season 1 - 10 episodes x 1 minute (Cartoon Network Brazil, 2019)

2019

In the ultra-short format, gags focus on dojo training: Sensei, an elderly rabbit, reluctantly drills Ninjin, Akai, and Flink in basic ninja discipline. Ninjin's broken sword, Akai's misfiring bombs, and Flink's uncontrolled levitation create slapstick failures. Sensei's deadpan reluctance contrasts with pupils' enthusiasm. These shorts establish character dynamics without advancing main plot far. Defines comedic tone and apprenticeship theme; demonstrates Ninjin is not yet a master.

Road to the Fortress - Encounters with General Jam (3-Minute Episodes Arc)Ninjin Season 1 - 7 episodes x 3 minutes (Cartoon Network Brazil, 2019)

2019

The trio leaves the dojo and travels toward Shogun Moe's territory, repeatedly clashing with General Jam, the deer general who commands the Shogun's army. Each 3-minute episode presents a self-contained obstacle — a bridge ambush, a forest trap, a bomb mishap — where Ninjin's earnest swordplay, Akai's kunoichi bomb tactics, and Flink's telekinesis must combine to escape. General Jam is portrayed as pompous and easily frustrated. Builds recurring antagonist relationship and demonstrates team synergy; escalates toward fortress.

Accounts II
The Five-Part Miniseries - Infiltration of Shogun Moe's Castle (7-Minute Episodes)Ninjin Season 1 - 5 episodes x 7 minutes (Cartoon Network Brazil, 2019) - billed as miniseries finale

2019

The longer-format finale allows a continuous story: the heroes infiltrate Shogun Moe's castle/fortress to confront the mole shogun himself and reclaim the stolen carrots. Sensei's teachings are put to the test; Akai proves herself as kunoichi, Flink uses elemental control at scale, and Ninjin must face Moe despite his broken sword. The miniseries resolves the season's carrot quest while leaving room for further adventures (which were never produced). Season climax; resolves central theft plot for Season 1. No Season 2 to continue consequences.

Ninjin: Clash of Carrots - Game Campaign StagesNinjin: Clash of Carrots - Story Mode (Pocket Trap/Modus Games, 2018)

2018

Playable campaign across multiple side-scrolling stages (village, forest, castle). Ninjin (and optionally Akai in co-op) fights waves of Shogun Moe's animal soldiers, mini-bosses, and ultimately Shogun Moe. Carrots collected act as currency for upgrades (new combos, cosmetics). Narrative is minimal, told via brief cutscenes and boss dialogue, emphasizing action over lore. Established IP, character designs, and premise that the series adapted; received mixed-to-positive reviews for combat and art.

Akai's Kunoichi Trial and Flink's Ramen Quest (Character Spotlight Episodes)Ninjin Season 1 - interspersed 1-min and 3-min episodes (2019)

2019

Dedicated spotlights: Akai insists on being recognized as a kunoichi (female ninja) and practices bomb artistry, pushing back against being sidelined; Flink's levitation/telekinesis and love of carrot ramen drive comic B-plots where he tries to cook or levitate carrots while training. Ninjin supports both friends, reinforcing ensemble equality rather than solo hero narrative. Develops Akai and Flink beyond sidekicks; establishes Akai's purple bombs and Flink's elemental/psychic powers as canon.

Ninjin is a Japanese anime or based on Japanese folklore. — Ninjin is a wholly Brazilian creation (Pocket Trap / Roger Keesse) that uses Japanese pastiche (ninja, shogun, kunoichi, carrot word) as comedic setting. No Japanese studio or folklore source; name is Japanese loanword for carrot.

Ninjin is the first Cartoon Network Brazil original series. — It is the second. The first was Jorel's Brother (Irmão do Jorel, 2014, created by Juliano Enrico), also co-produced by Cartoon Network Brazil. Ninjin press explicitly billed as 'second original animated series'.

The series is still available on HBO Max / Cartoon Network YouTube and has multiple seasons. — Only one season (22 episodes, 2019) was produced. It was later removed/scrubbed from HBO Max Latin America and Cartoon Network Latin America YouTube channels circa 2022-2023, making official streaming currently unavailable. No Season 2 was ordered.

Ninjin uses a real katana or has superpowers like Flink. — Ninjin's weapon is explicitly a large wooden sword with a broken tip (bokken), emphasizing apprenticeship and slapstick. Supernatural powers (levitation, telekinesis, elemental control) belong to Flink the frog, not Ninjin. Ninjin's abilities are earnest but mundane ninja training.
