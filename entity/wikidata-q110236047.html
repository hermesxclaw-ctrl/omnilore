Who It Is

In the lightless beds where Mandaean mothers labour, a lilith watches — not to steal the child, but to keep it alive. Her name is Zahreil, daughter of the queen of darkness, sister to the restless Ruha, and unwilling mother of the angel who botched the material world.

Attested behavior is dual: 1) Cosmological: As participant in Hibil Ziwa's descent, she becomes consort/wife to Hibil Ziwa during his sojourn in the underworld and mother to Ptahil. The text presents this as a cosmogonic marriage, not a romance; Hibil Ziwa's motive is to neutralize or contain the dark world powers. 2) Folkloric: As genius of childbirth, she 'serves to ensure the wellbeing of the child before and after birth' (Drower). Powers
Lilith Presence in Childbirth Beds

Ability to dwell invisibly in the beds of pregnant women to ensure wellbeing of the child before and after birth. Described by Drower as 'genius of childbirth' — a protective presence during the vulnerable perinatal period.

Limit: Not omnipotent protection; Mandaean magic literature still prescribes amulets, prayers (Qolasta) and lead rolls to protect infants from other liliths and malevolent ruha spirits. Her protection is specific to her own function, not a general ward against all harm.

Darkworld Lineage and Progeny of the Demiurge

As daughter of Qin (Queen of Darkness) and sister of Ruha, she carries darkworld substance. Through union with Hibil Ziwa (lightworld uthra), she produces Ptahil, the creator of the material universe. This makes her the maternal source of demiurgic creative power.

Limit: She does not herself create; creation is delegated to Ptahil. Her power is generative via lineage, not direct world-making. Ptahil's creation is flawed and requires correction by Abatur and later saviors.

Threshold Mediation Between Light and Darkness

By marrying Hibil Ziwa, a lightworld emissary (son of Abatur), Zahreil becomes the point of contact between light and darkness. Her womb becomes the vessel where light and dark mix to produce Ptahil, who is neither fully light nor fully dark.

Limit: She remains of the World of Darkness; she does not ascend to the World of Light. Hibil Ziwa leaves the underworld after the union; Zahreil stays.

No Attested Magical or Destructive Powers

Unlike Ruha (who commands the seven planets and twelve constellations, stirs storms, and opposes John the Baptist) or Qin (who rules darkness), Zahreil has no attested powers of destruction, illusion, or domination in primary texts. She is not invoked in magic bowls for harm.

Limit: Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence; unpublished manuscripts may contain more.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No attested iconography, statue, or manuscript illustration of Zahreil is published or catalogued. Attested description is textual and functional, not visual. She is classified as a lilita (Classical Mandaic ࡋࡉࡋࡉࡕࡀ, lilita) — a female spirit/demon of the World of Darkness (alma d-hšuka). In Mandaean demonology, liliths are typically anthropomorphic female spirits associated with liminal spaces (water, beds, thresholds). Drower's ethnographic note

Symbols — No verified symbols, sigils, or cult objects associated with Zahreil. Unlike Ruha (associated with breath/wind, sometimes dove/serpent imagery in scholarly commentary) and Ptahil (associated with demiurgic tools), Zahreil has no attested icon. Her domain symbol is the bed / birthing bed itself. In Mandaean magic bowls and lead rolls (which do depict liliths), no bowl has been securely identified as depicting Zahreil by name; generic lilith figure

Domains — Primary habitat: World of Darkness (alma d-hšuka), specifically the realm of Qin and Ruha, often described as the 'black waters' or underworld beneath the earth. Secondary habitat (folkloric): the beds of pregnant women in the material world (tibil), where she dwells as guardian. Domain: childbirth, perinatal protection, liminal transition between worlds (darkness to material creation via Ptahil). She bridges dark-world lineage and demi

Accounts I
Hibil Ziwa's Descent to the World of Darkness and Marriage to ZahreilRight Ginza (Ginza Yamina) Book 5, Tractate 1 — Lidzbarski pp. 137-145; Gelbert 2011 pp. 154-165; Drower The Secret Adam pp. 44-48

Text redacted Late Antiquity (3rd-7th c. CE); manuscripts 16th-19th c.

At the behest of the Great Life (Mana Rba) and Abatur, the lightbeing Hibil Ziwa (also called Gabriel) descends to the World of Darkness (alma d-hšuka) to contain its powers. There he encounters Qin, queen of darkness, and her daughters Ruha and Zahreil. In the narrative, Hibil Ziwa takes Zahreil as wife/consort while in the underworld. Drower summarizes: 'during Hibil Ziwa's descent... Hibil Ziwa marries Zahreil.' The marriage occurs in darkness, before his ascent. It is presented as part of his mission, not a romantic episode, and is tied to the subsequent birth of Ptahil. The union produces Ptahil, who will create the material world (tibil). This establishes the mixed origin of the physical cosmos — born from light (Hibil) and darkness (Zahreil). It also explains why Ptahil is flawed and needs redemption by later light emissaries (Manda d-Haiyi, Hibil, Shitil, Anush).

The Birth of Ptahil the CreatorRight Ginza 5.1; Left Ginza 2.12 (variant); Drower The Secret Adam p. 45

Late Antiquity

After the marriage, Zahreil gives birth to Ptahil (Classical Mandaic: ࡐࡕࡀࡄࡉࡋ). Ptahil is described as an uthra (angel) but with darkworld maternal substance. In GR 5, Ptahil is explicitly 'son of Zahreil and Hibil Ziwa.' He is later given the task (or seizes the task) of creating the earth (tibil) and forming Adam's physical body, which he does imperfectly. In other Ginza books, Ptahil is called son of Ruha, creating the contradiction. Ptahil's flawed creation (earth is dark, Adam's body soulless until light soul is infused) drives the rest of Mandaean salvation history. Zahreil's maternity thus makes her ancestress of the material world, though she takes no further role.

Zahreil the Lilith in the Beds of Pregnant WomenE.S. Drower, The Mandaeans of Iraq and Iran (1937) p. 89; The Secret Adam (1960) p. 44; repeated in Buckley 2002 p. 47 and Rudolph 1978

Ethnographically recorded 1920s-1930s; belief likely older

Beyond cosmogony, Zahreil survives in Mandaean folk belief as a lilita who dwells in the beds of pregnant women, 'serving to ensure the wellbeing of the child before and after birth' (source material). Drower describes her as a 'genius of childbirth' — not an attacker but a guardian. Mandaean women would be aware of her presence in the birthing bed; her function is protective during the liminal period when mother and infant are vulnerable to other malevolent liliths and ruha spirits. This is distinct from her cosmogonic role and suggests a domestic cult or apotropaic belief. Explains Mandaean birth customs and the need for protective prayers and amulets (qmaiha) even though a benevolent lilith is present. Shows how a darkworld being can have a positive folk function. Parallels Jewish folklore of Senoy, Sansenoy and Semangelof who protect mothers/newborns.

Accounts II
Qin, Ruha and Zahreil: The Dark Triad GenealogyRight Ginza 5.1; Left Ginza 3.1; Drower 1937 genealogy charts

Late Antiquity

Qin (also called Qin, daughter of darkness) is mother of Ruha and Zahreil. Ruha (Spirit, often equated with Ruha d-Qudsha) becomes mother of Ur (Leviathan) and the seven planets (Shamesh/Sun, Libat/Venus, etc.) and twelve constellations. Zahreil, her sister, becomes mother of Ptahil. Thus the two sisters generate the two branches of flawed creation: Ruha generates the archontic rulers, Zahreil generates the demiurge himself. Some texts make Qin herself daughter of Zahreil? No — consistent is Qin as mother. This genealogy structures Mandaean demonology. Establishes Zahreil's place in the darkworld royal family and explains why Ptahil and the planets are kin (cousins). Later lightworld saviors must defeat both branches.

Ptahil's Creation and Failure — Zahreil's LegacyRight Ginza 3 (The Creation of the World by Ptahil), Left Ginza 2; Buckley, The Great Stem of Souls (2005) pp. 50-70

Late Antiquity

Ptahil, son of Zahreil, descends to the black waters and creates tibil (earth) with the help of Ruha and her son Ur, but fails to make it perfect. He forms Adam's body from earth but cannot animate it; the Great Life sends Manda d-Haiyi and Hibil to infuse the light soul (mana) into Adam. Ptahil is then rebuked and bound. Zahreil does not appear in this creation narrative directly, but her maternity is invoked to explain Ptahil's inadequacy — he is 'son of a lilith' and thus not pure light. Later, Ptahil repents and is given a place north of the earth. Zahreil's son becomes the flawed demiurge whose world must be redeemed. This cements her indirect responsibility for material existence in Mandaean Gnosticism, where matter is not evil per se but flawed and in need of light.

Hibil Ziwa's Ascent and Abandonment of the Dark WorldRight Ginza 5.1 continuation; Drower The Secret Adam pp. 46-48

Late Antiquity

After marrying Zahreil and fathering Ptahil, Hibil Ziwa does not remain in darkness. He receives the mysteries, baptizes himself, and ascends back to the World of Light, sealing the dark world. Zahreil remains below with her kin. The text emphasizes Hibil's purification after contact with darkness. The marriage is thus temporary and cosmologically functional, not an ongoing union. No further interaction between Hibil and Zahreil is narrated. Establishes the separation of light and darkness after a single point of mixing. Zahreil is left as single mother in darkness; Hibil returns to purity. This abandonment motif parallels other Gnostic descents where the savior touches darkness but is not trapped.

Zahreil is the same as the Jewish Lilith who kills babies. — Zahreil is a Mandaean lilita (lilith-type) but her attested folk function is opposite: she protects pregnant women and ensures wellbeing of the child before and after birth (Drower 1937). Jewish Lilith (lilit) in Alphabet of Ben Sira and folklore is a child-killing demon warded off by Senoy, Sansenoy and Semangelof. The typology is related (both are liliths) but function is inverted; conflating them erases Zahreil's protective role.

Zahreil is Ruha, or just another name for Ruha. — Primary sources distinguish them as sisters, daughters of Qin. Zahreil mothers Ptahil in GR 5; Ruha mothers Ur and the planets (and Ptahil in other passages). They share darkworld lineage but are separate persons in GR 5 genealogy. Collapse is due to variant traditions where Ruha replaces Zahreil as Ptahil's mother, not identity.

Zahreil is a goddess worshipped by Mandaeans with prayers and rituals. — No liturgical prayer to Zahreil is attested in Qolasta or Ginza liturgy. Mandaeans pray to the Great Life (Mana Rba), Manda d-Haiyi, and lightworld uthras; darkworld beings like Qin, Ruha, Zahreil are not worshipped. Zahreil's role is mythological and folkloric (guardian of beds), not cultic. Mandaean priests do not invoke her.

Zahreil appears widely in games, films, and fantasy novels. — No verified appearance in major film, television, game, or fantasy franchise was found. Unlike better-known Gnostic figures (Sophia, Demiurge, Lilith), Zahreil remains obscure and largely confined to scholarly literature on Mandaeism. Any modern esoteric list including 'Zahriel' as an angel is likely a homonym unrelated to Mandaean Zahreil.

Hibil Ziwa and Zahreil had a romantic love story. — Right Ginza 5 presents the marriage as a cosmogonic event during Hibil's descent to darkness to contain its powers, not a romance. Hibil leaves after fathering Ptahil and returns to light; Zahreil remains in darkness. No dialogue, affection, or ongoing relationship is narrated. Romanticization is modern invention.
