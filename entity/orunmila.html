Who It Is

Orunmila (also called Ifa or Orula) is the Yoruba orisha of wisdom, knowledge, divination, and destiny. He is the divine witness present at the creation of every human soul, who knows every person's destiny (ori) from the moment of their birth. As the keeper of the Ifa divination system — one of the most elaborate and sophisticated systems of geomancy in the world, recognized by UNESCO as a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity — Orunmila mediates between human ignorance and divine knowledge, interpreting the will of Olodumare for those who seek guidance.

Physically, Orunmila is depicted as a sage-like figure, often elderly, dressed in green and yellow, carrying an iroke (divination tapper) and an opon Ifa (divination tray). He is associated with the palm tree and the palm nut, which serve as his primary divination tools. In Yoruba art, Orunmila appears less frequently than warrior or kingly orishas, reflecting his introspective, intellectual nature. His priesthood — the Babalawos ('Fathers of Secrets') — are exclusively male (in traditional practice) and undergo decades of training to master the 256 Odu Ifa, the sacred verses that comprise the system.

The Ifa corpus contains thousands of verses (ese Ifa) organized into 256 chapters (Odu). Each Odu corresponds to a specific pattern made by casting sixteen palm nuts or a divination chain (opele), and each contains hundreds of stories, proverbs, prescriptions, and moral teachings. When a person consults Ifa, the Babalawo casts the chain, identifies which Odu has appeared, and recites verses relevant to the client's situation. The system is astonishingly comprehensive, addressing everything from health and marriage to business and warfare. Orunmila himself is said to have been present at the beginning of creation, recording the destinies of all souls in the divine ledger.

A key myth tells how Orunmila acquired his knowledge. He traveled widely across the world and to the realm of the dead, learning from every experience and recording his wisdom. When he returned, he systematized this knowledge into Ifa. Another myth describes how Death (Iku) attempted to claim Orunmila, but the god of wisdom tricked Death through his superior knowledge, escaping into the divination system itself — meaning that as long as Ifa exists, Orunmila lives through it.

In Santería, Orula (Orunmila) is syncretized with Saint Francis of Assisi. He is the orisha of destiny, and many followers receive the 'hand of Orula' (mano de Orula) as their first major initiation. His sacred number is sixteen, and offerings include palm nuts, yams, and green and yellow objects.
