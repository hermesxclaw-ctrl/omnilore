Who It Is

Peripillán is the malevolent counterpart to the benevolent Pillán in Mapuche religion. These are the spirits of wicked ancestors and the personified forces of evil who dwell in the lower world (Minche Mapu) and constantly seek to harm the living by causing illness, crop failure, natural disasters, and moral corruption. They are the primary adversaries that the Mapuche shaman (Machi) must combat.

Attested in Oral Tradition / Mapuche Shamanic PracticesEra Pre-Columbian to Present

Field Notes

۩ The Machi's most dramatic ritual — the Machitún — involves a healing ceremony where the shaman battles the Peripillán in the spirit world while in a trance state.

۩ The Wekufü, the most powerful evil spirit, is sometimes depicted as a giant toad whose croaking causes earthquakes.

۩ According to Mapuche tradition, the Peripillán cannot cross running water — a limitation they share with many malevolent spirits in world folklore.

Cultural Significance

The Peripillán play a crucial role in Mapuche explanations of suffering and misfortune. They are a constant presence in Mapuche spiritual life, and many rituals — from offerings to the Machi's healing ceremonies — are directed at countering their influence. The Mapuche concept of witchcraft (kalku) is intimately connected to the Peripillán, and accusations of witchcraft have historically played a significant role in Mapuche community dynamics. The Peripillán tradition has been incorporated into the syncretic folk Catholicism of rural Chile, where these spirits are sometimes identified with the Christian devil.
