Who It Is

In the thunderous storms of the Mapuche world, where lightning splits the sky and the earth trembles, there dwells a spirit of immense power and unpredictability: Peripillán, also known as Wechuwechu. This being is not a distant god but a force woven into the very fabric of daily life, a presence that can bring both destruction and renewal, feared and respected by the people of the land.

Peripillán is considered a powerful and unpredictable spirit, capable of both benevolence and wrath. It is believed to control weather phenomena, especially storms, which can bring life-giving rain or destructive hail and lightning. The spirit is often seen as a guardian of the natural order, punishing those who violate moral or social norms. It is also associated with the power of shamans (machis) who may communicate with it. The personality is not well-defined in the sources; it is more of an impersonal force that can be influenced through rituals and offerings.

Powers
Weather Control

Peripillán has the power to command storms, lightning, thunder, and rain. It can bring beneficial rains for agriculture or destructive storms with hail and strong winds.

Limit: The spirit is not omnipotent; it may be influenced by rituals, offerings, and the actions of shamans. Its behavior is often seen as a response to human conduct.

Punitive Power

Peripillán is believed to punish individuals or communities that transgress moral or social rules, often through lightning strikes, storms, or other natural disasters.

Limit: The spirit's judgment is not always predictable; it may also punish innocent people if the community as a whole has failed in its obligations.

Communication with Shamans

Peripillán can communicate with machis (shamans) during rituals, providing guidance, healing power, or warnings. Shamans may enter trance states to interact with the spirit.

Limit: Only trained shamans with the appropriate knowledge and ritual purity can safely communicate with the spirit. Unauthorized contact may be dangerous.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Peripillán is typically associated with natural phenomena rather than a fixed anthropomorphic form. It is often described as a spirit of the storm, manifesting through lightning, thunder, and strong winds. In some accounts, it may appear as a brilliant flash or a fiery figure in the sky. Symbols — Peripillán is symbolically linked to lightning bolts, thunder, storms, and sometimes volcanoes, as these are the most dramatic manifestations of its power. In Mapuche iconography, lightning is often depicted as a zigzag or a serpent-like form. The spirit may also be associated with the colors of the storm: dark clouds, white flashes, and the red of fire. Domains — Peripillán dwells in the sky, particularly in storm clouds, but also in high mountains and volcanoes, which are considered sacred in Mapuche cosmology. These locations are seen as portals to the spiritual world. The spirit is most active during storms, especially in the Andean region where thunderstorms are frequent. It is also associated with the upper world (Wenu Mapu), the celestial realm of the Mapuche.

Pre-colonial to present

A community neglects to make offerings to Peripillán before the planting season. As a result, a violent storm destroys their crops and homes with lightning and hail. The people learn to respect the spirit and perform the necessary rituals to restore balance. The community suffers losses but gains a renewed sense of spiritual duty. The story reinforces the importance of proper ritual observance.

20th century

A machi seeks the aid of Peripillán to heal a sick patient. Through a trance, the machi ascends to the sky and pleads with the spirit. Peripillán agrees to restore the patient's health in exchange for a promise of continued devotion from the community. The patient recovers, and the machi gains prestige. The community reaffirms its relationship with the spirit.
