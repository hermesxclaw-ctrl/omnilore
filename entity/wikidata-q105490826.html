Who It Is

In the shadow of the Mongol Empire's rise, a name whispered across Asia and Europe conjured visions of unimaginable wealth and splendor: Xanadu. More than a city, it became a symbol of power, a canvas for poetic imagination, and a puzzle that scholars still piece together from fragments of history and legend.

As a summer capital, Xanadu was a place of leisure, hunting, and political gatherings. Kublai Khan held court there, and it served as a retreat from the heat of Khanbaliq. The city was also a site for religious ceremonies, with temples of various faiths.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Xanadu' is a Western corruption of the Mongolian 'Shangdu' (上都), meaning 'Upper Capital'. The term 'Xanadu' was popularized by Samuel Taylor Coleridge's poem 'Kubla Khan'.

Pronunciation — /ˈzænəduː/ (zan-uh-doo)

Aliases — Shangdu, 上都, Khanbaliq's summer capital

Ownership — Xanadu is historically associated with the Mongol Empire and the Yuan dynasty of China, but it has become a global cultural symbol through Western literature and media.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Shangdu was a walled city with an outer city, an inner city, and a palace city. The palace was noted for its grandeur, with gilded roofs and marble floors. The city included parks, lakes, and hunting grounds, as described by Marco Polo. The ruins today show earthen walls and stone foundations.

Symbols — The city symbolized Mongol imperial power and the integration of nomadic and sedentary cultures. It was designed to reflect the cosmic order, with the palace at the center. In Western art, Xanadu became a symbol of exotic luxury and mystery.

Domains — The city was located on the Mongolian plateau, in a grassland region suitable for pasturing horses. It had access to rivers and lakes, and the surrounding area was used for hunting.

Accounts I
Foundation of ShangduYuan Shi; archaeological evidence

1256-1260

Kublai Khan ordered the construction of Shangdu as a summer capital, designed by Chinese architect Liu Bingzhong. The city was built to accommodate both sedentary and nomadic lifestyles. Shangdu became a key administrative and ceremonial center, used by successive Yuan emperors.

Accounts II
Marco Polo's DescriptionMarco Polo's Travels

c. 1275

Marco Polo described a magnificent palace of marble and gold, with a park full of game and a great khan who stayed there in summer. This account fueled European fascination with the East. Polo's descriptions influenced later European perceptions of Asia and inspired the myth of Xanadu.

Coleridge's 'Kubla Khan'Poem by Samuel Taylor Coleridge

1797-1816

Coleridge composed a visionary poem about 'Kubla Khan' and his 'stately pleasure-dome' in Xanadu, blending oriental imagery with dreamlike fantasy. The poem became a classic of Romantic literature. The poem cemented Xanadu as a symbol of exotic splendor and inspired countless artistic adaptations.
