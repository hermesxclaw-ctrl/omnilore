Who It Is

Suea Samut ('sea tiger' in Thai) is a mythical sea creature from Thai maritime folklore, described as a massive, tiger-striped sea beast that dwells in the deep waters of the Andaman Sea and the Gulf of Thailand. It is the king of all sea creatures — sharks, whales, and giant rays are said to be its subjects. Suea Samut is generally neutral but becomes dangerous when its territory is violated: it overturns boats, drags fishermen into the depths, and causes sudden storms. Some accounts describe it as a hybrid creature with the head of a tiger, the body of a serpent, and the fins of a great fish. Thai fishermen traditionally made offerings of rice, flowers, and incense before long voyages to appease Suea Samut. Sightings are still occasionally reported by fishermen, who describe unexplained attacks on boats, mysterious shadows larger than whales, and the distinctive tiger-striped pattern seen briefly before the creature submerges. The Suea Samut legend is related to the broader Southeast Asian belief in giant sea serpents and the Javanese myth of the laut kidul (southern sea spirit). In modern Thai fantasy media, Suea Samut appears as a powerful nature spirit and guardian of the sea, often opposed to human overfishing and pollution.
