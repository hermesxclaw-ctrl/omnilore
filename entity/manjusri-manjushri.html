Who It Is

Mañjuśrī is the bodhisattva of wisdom and insight — the one who cuts through ignorance with the sword of discriminating awareness. He is usually depicted as a youthful prince holding a raised flaming sword in his right hand (which cuts through delusion) and a Prajnaparamita (Perfection of Wisdom) text in his left hand, often seated on a blue lion. Mañjuśrī is one of the 'three great bodhisattvas' of Mahayana Buddhism (along with Avalokiteśvara and Vajrapani). As the embodiment of wisdom, he represents the enlightened mind that sees through the illusion of duality. His mount is a lion, symbolizing the fearlessness of wisdom. He is considered the patron of Buddhist scholars, meditators, and students. His presence is said to be concentrated on Mount Wutai in China, one of the four sacred Buddhist mountains, where he is believed to appear to pilgrims. In the Vimalakīrti Sūtra, Mañjuśrī debates with the layman Vimalakīrti in one of Buddhism's most profound dialogues on non-duality. He is also the subject of the Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa, an important Buddhist text containing both spiritual teachings and astronomical knowledge.

Origins

Mañjuśrī is the bodhisattva who attained perfect wisdom in a previous lifetime. According to tradition, he took a vow to be reborn as a bodhisattva to teach and inspire faith in all sentient beings, rather than entering nirvana. In some traditions, he was a historical prince who became a monk.

Attested in Lotus Sutra / Vimalakīrti Sūtra / MañjuśrīmūlakalpaEra c. 1st century BCE onward
