Who It Is

Before the world, before the stars, before the very idea of 'before,' there was a light that looked at itself and — in that single, silent act of self-recognition — created the entire architecture of the divine. That light is Autogenes, the Self-Begotten: the first being to know itself, and in that knowing, to bring order to the infinite chaos of the Pleroma. He is the divine Mind that bridges the unknowable Father and the world of aeons, the model for every Gnostic seeker who hopes to escape the prison of the material world. He is not a creator of the world, but a creator of the self — and in a universe where the physical realm is a catastrophic error, that self-knowledge is the only salvation.

Autogenes is a passive, generative force. He does not act in a mythological sense (fighting, creating the world, etc.) but rather emanates. His primary action is to 'look upon' the divine light and, through his self-reflection, generate the Four Luminaries (Harmozel, Oroiael, Daveithe, Eleleth) which serve as the heavenly thrones or dwelling places for the divine seed of Seth. He is the archetype of self-knowledge: he knows himself, and in that knowing, he creates.

Powers
Self-Generation (Autogeny)

Autogenes is the only being in the Sethian system that is 'self-born.' He does not emanate from a prior source (like Barbelo emanates from the Father) but rather generates himself through the Father's self-reflection. This makes him the archetype of autonomous divine will.

Limit: He is still subordinate to the Invisible Spirit (the highest God) and Barbelo (the Mother).

Emanation of the Four Luminaries

Autogenes gazes upon the divine light and emanates four great aeons: Harmozel (the first luminary, dwelling of Adamas), Oroiael (second, dwelling of Seth), Daveithe (third, dwelling of the seed of Seth), and Eleleth (fourth, dwelling of the repentant). These Luminaries serve as the heavenly 'mansions' for the Gnostic elect.

Limit: The Luminaries are subordinate to him and act as his stewards.

Bestowal of Divine Mind (Nous)

Autogenes is the 'Mind' of the Pleroma. He is the one who imparts the divine intellect to the aeons, allowing them to contemplate the Invisible Spirit. He is the bridge between the unknowable Father and the knowable divine realm.

Limit: He cannot impart knowledge of the Invisible Spirit directly; that requires a higher grace.

Gatekeeper of the Pleroma

In the ascent texts (Zostrianos, Allogenes), the visionary must pass through the realm of Autogenes to reach the higher aeons. Autogenes serves as a checkpoint, purifying the soul and preparing it for the vision of Barbelo and the Invisible Spirit.

Limit: He cannot grant the final vision of the Invisible Spirit; that is a gift from the Spirit itself.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Autogenes is never described with physical features, as he is a purely noetic (intellectual) entity. He is described as a 'light' that shines in the darkness of the material world. In the Apocryphon of John, he is the 'perfect light' that emanates from the divine Mind. He is often associated with the divine 'Mind' (Nous) and is the first principle that brings order to the Pleroma (the fullness of the divine realm).

Symbols — Light (specifically 'self-begotten light'), Mind (Nous), the 'Son' of the divine triad (Father-Mother-Son), the 'First Thought' or 'Forethought' (Pronoia) made manifest. He is often symbolized by the idea of the 'self-born' Logos, a concept that would later be mirrored in Christian theology of the 'Only-Begotten' Son.

Domains — The Pleroma (the divine fullness), specifically the second level of the divine realm after the Invisible Spirit and Barbelo. He is the overseer of the Four Luminaries, which in turn govern the aeons and the spiritual seed of Seth (the Gnostic elect). He is the gatekeeper of divine knowledge, standing between the highest unknowable God and the lower aeons.

Accounts I
The Self-Begotten LightThe Apocryphon of John

Late Antiquity

The Invisible Spirit, the highest God, looks into the waters of pure light. His thought (Barbelo) emerges as the first emanation. Barbelo requests a 'Forethought' (Pronoia), and from this request, the divine triad is completed. The Spirit and Barbelo then generate a 'perfect light' — this is Autogenes, the self-begotten one. Autogenes, seeing himself, generates the Four Luminaries, which become the heavenly dwelling places for the divine seed. He is the first being to know himself, and in that self-knowledge, he creates order in the Pleroma. The Pleroma is ordered. The Four Luminaries are established as the thrones for the divine seed of Seth. Autogenes becomes the model for the Gnostic seeker: one who knows oneself and thereby knows the divine.

Accounts II
The Ascent of ZostrianosZostrianos (Nag Hammadi Codex VIII)

Late Antiquity

The visionary Zostrianos ascends through the heavenly realms, passing through the aeons governed by the Four Luminaries. He arrives at the realm of Autogenes, who reveals to him the 'power of perfection.' Autogenes instructs Zostrianos on the nature of the divine Mind and the path to the Invisible Spirit. Zostrianos then continues his ascent to Barbelo and the highest God, returning to earth to preach the Gnostic gospel. Zostrianos gains the knowledge of the divine realm and returns as a prophet to teach others. Autogenes is established as a necessary intermediary for divine ascent.

The Vision of AllogenesAllogenes (Nag Hammadi Codex XI)

Late Antiquity

Allogenes (the 'Stranger') receives a revelation from the angel Youel, who describes the ascent through the divine realms. The text emphasizes that the vision of Autogenes is a crucial step before the final silence of the Invisible Spirit. Autogenes is the 'self-begotten' who reveals the Father's light to those who are worthy. Allogenes is prepared for the ultimate vision of the Invisible Spirit, which is beyond all description.

Autogenes is a 'rebellious' figure who defies the highest God. — Autogenes is the perfect, obedient 'Son' of the Invisible Spirit. He is the model of self-knowledge and divine order, not rebellion.
