Who It Is

If ever a supreme god dwelt quietly behind an everyday word, it is O Del — for in the Romani language the faithful do not reach for scripture or a holy book when they need God; they simply say Devla. He is 'the Great God,' O Baro Devel, whose reverent name is the same word that once thundered in Sanskrit as deva and that shares a root with the ancient sky-father *Dyēus of the whole Indo-European world. Del is not a god of carved idols or dramatic myth — there are no famous stories of his battles, no temples built for him. Instead he is the silent order behind everything: the one who sees the heart and the deed, the invisible enforcer of Romanipen, the supreme being whose only real counterpart is his dark opposite, Beng the Devil. In a faith carried across centuries and borders without a fixed home, Del is the one constant — a sky-father so vast and so present that his people rarely dare

As a supreme creator and moral overseer, Del is conceived as seeing and judging: the OmniLore record glosses him as one who 'sees all violations of Romani customary law (Romanipen) and metes out punishment through fate or natural disaster,' and quotes an illustrative saying, 'O Del sees the heart and the deed; nothing is hidden from the Great God's eye.' Name & Tongue

Etymology — The Romani word for God is Del (also Devla, Devel). It descends directly from Sanskrit deva ('god, shining celestial being'), making it a cognate of Latin deus ('god') and divinus ('divine'), Greek Zeus (from the vocative), Lithuanian Dievas, and the reconstructed Proto-Indo-European *Dyēus, the nam

Pronunciation — Del (short e, as in 'dell'); Devla (~'DAY-vla'); Devel (~'DEH-vel'); O Del ('oh DELL'). The initial D is dental. Variant dialectal forms include Dovel and Devles (object/oblique case).

Aliases — Devla, Devel, Devles, Devlam, Devleske, Dovel

Ownership — Romani (the Roma / Romani people), an Indo-Aryan ethnic group of South Asian (Rajasthan-region) origin that migrated west through the Ghaznavid and Byzantine empires and reached Eu

Powers
Supreme creation and cosmic governance

As the supreme being, Del is credited with creating the world and establishing the moral order that governs it (the OmniLore record: he 'created the world and established the moral laws'). In the reconstructed devotional frame he set the order of the world, created the stars, the moon, and the spirits, and created Beng (the Devil) as his cosmological counterpart. This is a creator-king power, distant rather than dramatically exhibited.

Limit: Del operates at cosmic/abstract scale; the attested record gives no personal combat or intervention feats. Punishments are 'through fate or natural disaster' — mediated, not directly dramatized.

Omniscient moral judgment

Del 'sees the heart and the deed' — nothing is hidden from his eye. He is the enforcer of Romanipen ('Gypsiness'/Romani customary law) and Romano Zakono (the Romani Code), meting out consequences for violations.

Limit: Judgment is conceived as operating via fate/social consequence rather than as a personalized legal procedure; no attested mythology of trials or adjudication scenes exists.

Answerer of prayer / protector

Del is invoked in prayer for protection against Beng, illness, and the malice of the gadje (non-Roma outsiders). Prayers to Del form the ritual spine of Romani protection-magic as documented in Romani folklore (which closely ties prayer with protective herbs and amulets), and the reverent euphemisms (O Baro Devel) accompany these invocations.

Limit: Prayer may be channeled through the host religion's intermediaries (Christ, the Virgin Mary via the Shaktist consort-frame), so Del is not necessarily addressed directly even when it is he who is ultimately worshipped.

Source of 'Divla' / divine force (reconstruction)

In the modern polytheistic-reconstruction register, a Bar o Devel / Siv is named as specific 'god of power and music.' This attributes a domain (power; music) to the high-god figure that the abstract supreme-Del register does not carry. It is a reconstruction-presentation, not a classical myth.

Limit: This is a modern/partial reconstruction; it does not reflect a single, uniform, historically continuous formulation. Few believers per the Devlaism note.

Oral tradition; pre-modern through contemporary

Romani oral tradition presents Del as the distant supreme creator who made the world and set the moral laws that govern it. He created the stars, the moon, and the spirits, and established the order in which Beng (the Devil) stands as the opposing force of evil and corruption. Humanity lives under this order and the moral code (Romanipen/Romano Zakono) that Del oversees. There is no widely-attested, single fixed narrative of this creation in the English-language record; the account circulates as a theological premise rather than as a dramatized origin tale. Establishes the moral frame of Romanipen, the counter-position of Beng, and the supreme-authority of Del in Romani spiritual life.

Contemporary and historical oral/ritual practice

The defining 'story' of Del is not a myth cycle but a living ritual idiom: Roma speak of and pray to 'Devla / Del' directly, especially in moments of hardship, illness, danger, or moral reflection. The word functions as both noun ('God') and invocation ('O God!'). Under the Shaktist-influenced practice documented among a minority, prayer to God is channeled through a female consort (the Virgin Mary or Saint Anne). Del is invoked for protection against Beng and the malice of outsiders, and the name is treated with reverent indirection. Keeps Del as an intimate but awe-distant presence in Romani life; sustains the euphemism register (O Baro Devel).

Devlaism: the revivalist movement (modern story)Wikipedia 'Devla' article — Devlaism section

Post-1990s (after the Yugoslav Wars)

After the Yugoslav Wars, some Roma — including the Arlije and Gurbeti groups — deliberately constructed an ethnic religion rooted in older Romani folklore and oriented around Devel. Its holy days are called Djisatedimi, and it retains rites carried over from Christianity and Islam: infant baptism (bona) and religious male circumcision (sunet bijav). 'Devlaism' represents a conscious attempt to rebuild a distinct Romani religious identity anchored in the indigenous Devel-concept rather than in the host religion alone. The record notes it has relatively few believers. Constitutes the clearest contemporary effort to institutionalize Del/Devel as the center of a specifically Romani religion.
