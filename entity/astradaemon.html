Who It Is

A skeletal, eel-like horror that swims through the Astral Plane, feeding on the souls of the dead. Its body is translucent, revealing swirling void inside its ribcage. Astradaemons are drawn to dying gods and powerful mortals, consuming their essence before it can reach an afterlife.

Form

Attested descriptions present astradaemons as vaguely humanoid but deeply alien fiends. They are unnaturally thin and gaunt, with translucent or semi-ethereal bodies that suggest their close connection to the Astral Plane. Their limbs are spindly and end in clawed hands, emphasizing predatory agility. From their upper backs extend a random number of twitching tentacles, adding to their aberrant silhouette and hinting at their ability to maneuver through astral currents. Their heads resemble skeletal, fiendish gulper eels, often described as having two pairs of jaws—an exaggerated, predatory maw suited to consuming souls rather than mere flesh. A long tail, sometimes used as a striking appendage, completes their form. Game art and text emphasize their motion: they are depicted as swimming or flying through astral space like monstrous, translucent sharks, reinforcing the image of a soul-hunting predator. These details are attested in PathfinderWiki and Paizo-derived stat blocks; the emphasis on translucence and eel-like skull is particularly noted in PathfinderWiki’s Appearance section.

Powers

Devour Soul Devour Soul is the astradaemon’s signature supernatural ability. In Pathfinder First Edition, an astradaemon that begins its turn with a grappled living opponent can, as a standard action, attempt to draw out and consume the victim’s soul, killing the creature instantly if it fails a high Fortitude saving throw. The ability works only on living creatures and grants the astradaemon a profane bonus to attacks, saving throws, and checks for 24 hours, scaling with the slain creature’s Hit Dice. Importantly, the ability does not consume the entire soul; enough remains for the victim to be resurrected normally. In Pathfinder Second Edition, Devour Soul is a divine necromancy action used against a grabbed living creature; on a failed Fortitude save, the victim dies instantly, and the astradaemon gains temporary Hit Points and a status bonus to attack and damage rolls, with longer duration if the victim is high level. Survivors gain temporary immunity. In both versions, Devour Soul represents direct spiritual predation rather than mere physical damage. Pathfinder First Edition (Bestiary 2, Archives of Nethys, d20PFSRD); Pathfinder Second Edition (PF2 SRD, Monster Core) The ability only affects living creatures and requires the astradaemon to have the target grappled or grabbed. A successful Fortitude save negates the instant death effect, and in PF2 the target becomes temporarily immune for 1 minute. Devour Soul does not fully annihilate the soul; resurrection remains possible, limiting its permanent narrative impact compared to some other daemon abilities. No direct mechanical contradictions exist between sources, but the flavor emphasis differs slightly: some texts stress that astradaemons drag souls back to their overlords, while Devour Soul’s rules focus on personal empowerment. This tension between personal feeding and service to masters is interpretive rather than explicitly contradictory.

Soul Siphon (Aura) Soul Siphon is an aura that allows astradaemons to draw power from nearby deaths. In Pathfinder First Edition, if a Small or larger living creature dies within 10 feet of an astradaemon, the daemon gains temporary Hit Points and a Strength bonus for a short duration, with bonuses stacking. Incorporeal undead and living spirits traveling outside their bodies (such as astral projection or magic jar users) take damage each round within the aura. In Pathfinder Second Edition, Soul Siphon is a 30-foot aura that grants temporary Hit Points and a status bonus to attack and damage rolls when a living creature dies within it, unless the creature was slain by Devour Soul. Incorporeal undead and disembodied spirits take force damage each round from the spiritual pressure as the astradaemon pulls in fragments of their soul. Pathfinder First Edition (Bestiary 2, Archives of Nethys, d20PFSRD, Roll20); Pathfinder Second Edition (PF2 SRD) The aura only triggers when living creatures die within its radius and provides relatively short-term bonuses. In PF2, creatures slain by Devour Soul do not trigger Soul Siphon, preventing double-stacking of benefits. The aura’s damage to incorporeal undead and spirits is limited and does not automatically destroy them. The radius and specific bonuses differ between editions (10 feet and Strength bonus vs. 30 feet and attack/damage bonus), reflecting system changes rather than lore contradictions. The PF2 rule that Devour Soul kills do not trigger Soul Siphon is a mechanical refinement not present in PF1.

Essence Drain / Energy Drain Astradaemons embody death by negative energy and level drain. In Pathfinder First Edition, their bite, claws, and tail attacks inflict energy drain, imposing negative levels on victims and representing the erosion of life force. They also possess spell-like abilities such as enervation and energy drain, further reinforcing this theme. In Pathfinder Second Edition, this concept is reworked as Essence Drain: when an astradaemon hits with its jaws, claws, or tail, the target takes negative energy damage and the astradaemon regains an equal number of Hit Points. This models a vampiric siphoning of spiritual and vital essence rather than discrete negative levels. Pathfinder First Edition (Bestiary 2, Archives of Nethys, d20PFSRD); Pathfinder Second Edition (PF2 SRD) Essence/energy drain requires successful physical attacks and is subject to normal defenses such as armor, resistances, and saving throws (for some spells). In PF2, Essence Drain’s damage is limited by the creature’s Hit Points and can be mitigated by resistances to negative energy. The shift from negative levels to negative energy damage reflects edition mechanics rather than a lore disagreement. Both versions consistently portray astradaemons as draining spiritual essence.

Displacement and True Seeing Astradaemons constantly benefit from displacement, bending light so that they appear shifted from their true position. This makes them harder to target, as attackers must overcome a miss chance or flat check, depending on edition. They also possess true seeing, allowing them to perceive things as they truly are, ignoring illusions and many forms of concealment. These abilities emphasize their status as extraplanar predators adapted to the deceptive, shifting environment of the Astral Plane. Pathfinder First Edition (Bestiary 2, Archives of Nethys, d20PFSRD, Roll20); Pathfinder Second Edition (PF2 SRD) Displacement can be countered by effects that negate concealment or by feats such as Blind-Fight. True seeing is subject to the usual limitations of the spell or innate ability, including range and inability to pierce certain powerful illusions or divine concealment. No significant contradictions; PF2 provides more detailed rules text for displacement’s interaction with flat checks and specific feats.

Planar Travel and Spell-Like Abilities Astradaemons possess a suite of spell-like or innate spells that support their role as soul hunters and extraplanar predators. In Pathfinder First Edition, they can use greater teleport (self plus limited weight), plane shift, fear, enervation, vampiric touch, locate creature, energy drain, and finger of death, among others. In Pathfinder Second Edition, their innate spells include discern location, finger of death, plane shift, dimension door, detect alignment (good only), and constant true seeing. These abilities allow them to track specific targets, move rapidly across planes, inflict death effects, and manipulate negative energy. Pathfinder First Edition (Bestiary 2, Archives of Nethys, d20PFSRD); Pathfinder Second Edition (PF2 SRD) Spell-like abilities are limited by daily uses and subject to saving throws and spell resistance (PF1) or standard spell defenses (PF2). Their teleportation is often restricted to self plus a small amount of carried objects, limiting mass transport. The exact spell list and levels differ between editions, reflecting system design rather than lore conflict. The core theme—mobility, soul-hunting, and death magic—remains consistent.

Physical Prowess and Resistances Astradaemons are physically formidable: they are Large, fast-moving creatures with high Dexterity and Constitution, capable of running and flying at great speed (90 feet in PF1, 60 feet in PF2). They possess damage reduction against good and silver weapons (PF1) and weaknesses to good damage (PF2), along with immunities to acid, death effects, disease, poison (PF1), and to death effects and negative energy (PF2). They also resist cold, electricity, and fire. These traits make them durable opponents who thrive in hostile planar environments. Pathfinder First Edition (Bestiary 2, Archives of Nethys, d20PFSRD, Roll20); Pathfinder Second Edition (PF2 SRD) Their resistances and immunities can be bypassed by good-aligned weapons, powerful magic, or specific countermeasures. Their large size and reliance on melee attacks can be exploited by highly mobile or ranged opponents. The exact values of damage reduction, resistances, and weaknesses differ between editions, but the overall profile of a resilient, evil outsider remains consistent.

Accounts

According to PathfinderWiki’s synthesis of Paizo lore, astradaemons were created in the depths of time by a powerful daemon lord known as the Bound Prince. Distrustful of the deacons created by his servants—the Apocalypse Riders (also called the Four Horsemen)—the Bound Prince fused hundreds of mortal souls with the essences of lesser daemons to create a new race of deacons: the astradaemons. These creatures were tasked with harvesting souls from the Astral Plane to feed the Bound Prince’s undying hunger. Their creation, however, revealed the Bound Prince’s lack of trust in the Riders. This act planted resentment in the Riders’ minds and contributed to the Bound Prince’s eventual downfall. In the aftermath, the Riders slaughtered most astradaemons and enslaved the survivors, taking the secret of their creation for themselves.

Astradaemons are described as swimming through the River of Souls like aberrant sharks, hunting for vulnerable spirits. After mortals die, their souls travel along the River of Souls through the Astral Plane toward judgment and their final destination. Astradaemons patrol this river, snatching unlucky souls and dragging them back to Abaddon to feed their fiendish masters. They move in loose packs, circling and striking at souls that lack strong protection or guidance. Their touch corrupts and damages the spiritual material of souls, and their bite inflicts horrific wraith-like effects on anything they attack.

In broader daemon lore, astradaemons function as specialized deacons or servitors of Abaddon’s Apocalypse Riders. They are perpetually ravenous, their touch corrupting and damaging the spiritual material of souls. They harvest souls from the Astral Plane and deliver them to Abaddon, where they feed the Riders’ apocalyptic agendas. Their existence reflects the plane’s focus on death by spiritual erosion and negative energy rather than simple physical destruction.

Content Notes

Astradaemons personify death by negative energy and level drain. They hunt mortal souls traveling along the River of Souls, snatching them before judgment and dragging them back to Abaddon. Their Devour Soul ability allows them to kill living creatures instantly by consuming their souls, while Soul Siphon and Essence Drain strip away fragments of spiritual and vital essence. This represents a form of spiritual predation and partial annihilation, in which souls are treated as food or fuel for fiendish hunger.

Within the Pathfinder cosmology, astradaemons exemplify a particular kind of evil focused on spiritual erosion rather than physical torture. Their depiction resonates with broader fantasy and religious motifs of soul-devouring demons and reapers, but remains a game-specific construct. The emphasis on negative energy and level drain reflects tabletop mechanics that model spiritual harm as quantifiable penalties.

Bestiary 2; The Great Beyond; PathfinderWiki; PF2 SRD; d20PFSRD; Roll20 Pathfinder Compendium

PathfinderWiki’s History recounts that after the Bound Prince created astradaemons, the Apocalypse Riders (Four Horsemen) slaughtered the majority of them and enslaved the survivors. This mass killing and enslavement is an internal dark history: astradaemons themselves are victims of fiendish politics, used as tools and then brutally subjugated. Their continued existence as soul-harvesting deacons under the Riders reflects ongoing exploitation.

This narrative illustrates how even monstrous entities can be framed as victims within a hierarchical evil cosmology. It also shows Paizo’s tendency to embed political and power-struggle themes within daemon lore, using mass slaughter and enslavement as markers of fiendish treachery and distrust.

PathfinderWiki – Astradaemon (History section)

By intercepting souls on the River of Souls and dragging them to Abaddon, astradaemons disrupt the intended process of judgment and afterlife assignment. This constitutes a form of cosmic abuse: souls are diverted from their rightful destinations and consumed or exploited by daemons. The resulting imbalance contributes to suffering and injustice in the multiverse.

In fantasy cosmologies, interference with the afterlife often serves as a narrative marker of profound evil. Astradaemons’ role in this interference aligns them with other soul-stealing entities across myth and fiction, though their specific mechanics and cosmological context are unique to Pathfinder.

A way to hold it in your mind

Imagine the River of Souls as a vast, star-lit current where every mortal spirit drifts toward judgment—and then picture something thin and translucent, with an eel’s skull and too many jaws, slipping through that astral tide like a shark. That is an astradaemon: a void-born daemon that does not merely kill bodies, but bites into the architecture of souls themselves. Its hunger is older than the politics of Abaddon, older even than the daemon lords who now claim it, and every flick of its tail in the astral dark is a gamble that a soul will never reach where it was meant to go.
