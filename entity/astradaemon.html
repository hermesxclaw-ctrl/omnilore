Who It Is

Imagine the River of Souls as a vast, star-lit current where every mortal spirit drifts toward judgment—and then picture something thin and translucent, with an eel’s skull and too many jaws, slipping through that astral tide like a shark. That is an astradaemon: a void-born daemon that does not merely kill bodies, but bites into the architecture of souls themselves. Its hunger is older than the politics of Abaddon, older even than the daemon lords who now claim it, and every flick of its tail in the astral dark is a gamble that a soul will never reach where it was meant to go.

Astradaemons are consistently portrayed as ravenous, predatory, and focused almost entirely on the acquisition and consumption of souls. They hunt actively, swimming through the River of Souls and the Astral Plane to snatch unlucky spirits before they can be judged or guided to their proper afterlife. Their behavior is likened to aberrant sharks: they move in loose packs, circling and striking at vulnerable souls or living beings whose spiritual defenses are weakened. Personality-wise, they are not depicted as schemers or tempters like some devils, nor as chaotic destroyers like many demons

Powers
Devour Soul

Devour Soul is the astradaemon’s signature supernatural ability. In Pathfinder First Edition, an astradaemon that begins its turn with a grappled living opponent can, as a standard action, attempt to draw out and consume the victim’s soul, killing the creature instantly if it fails a high Fortitude saving throw. The ability works only on living creatures and grants the astradaemon a profane bonus to attacks, saving throws, and checks for 24 hours, scaling with the slain creature’s Hit Dice. Importantly, the ability does not consume the entire soul; enough remains for the victim to be resurrected normally. In Pathfinder Second Edition, Devour Soul is a divine necromancy action used against a grabbed living creature; on a failed Fortitude save, the victim dies instantly, and the astradaemon gains temporary Hit Points and a status bonus to attack and damage rolls, with longer duration if the victim is high level. Survivors gain temporary immunity. In both versions, Devour Soul represents direct spiritual predation rather than mere physical damage.

Limit: The ability only affects living creatures and requires the astradaemon to have the target grappled or grabbed. A successful Fortitude save negates the instant death effect, and in PF2 the target becomes temporarily immune for 1 minute. Devour Soul does not fully annihilate the soul; resurrection remains possible, limiting its permanent narrative impact compared to some other daemon abilities.

Soul Siphon (Aura)

Soul Siphon is an aura that allows astradaemons to draw power from nearby deaths. In Pathfinder First Edition, if a Small or larger living creature dies within 10 feet of an astradaemon, the daemon gains temporary Hit Points and a Strength bonus for a short duration, with bonuses stacking. Incorporeal undead and living spirits traveling outside their bodies (such as astral projection or magic jar users) take damage each round within the aura. In Pathfinder Second Edition, Soul Siphon is a 30-foot aura that grants temporary Hit Points and a status bonus to attack and damage rolls when a living creature dies within it, unless the creature was slain by Devour Soul. Incorporeal undead and disembodied spirits take force damage each round from the spiritual pressure as the astradaemon pulls in fragments of their soul.

Limit: The aura only triggers when living creatures die within its radius and provides relatively short-term bonuses. In PF2, creatures slain by Devour Soul do not trigger Soul Siphon, preventing double-stacking of benefits. The aura’s damage to incorporeal undead and spirits is limited and does not automatically destroy them.

Essence Drain / Energy Drain

Astradaemons embody death by negative energy and level drain. In Pathfinder First Edition, their bite, claws, and tail attacks inflict energy drain, imposing negative levels on victims and representing the erosion of life force. They also possess spell-like abilities such as enervation and energy drain, further reinforcing this theme. In Pathfinder Second Edition, this concept is reworked as Essence Drain: when an astradaemon hits with its jaws, claws, or tail, the target takes negative energy damage and the astradaemon regains an equal number of Hit Points. This models a vampiric siphoning of spiritual and vital essence rather than discrete negative levels.

Limit: Essence/energy drain requires successful physical attacks and is subject to normal defenses such as armor, resistances, and saving throws (for some spells). In PF2, Essence Drain’s damage is limited by the creature’s Hit Points and can be mitigated by resistances to negative energy.

Displacement and True Seeing

Astradaemons constantly benefit from displacement, bending light so that they appear shifted from their true position. This makes them harder to target, as attackers must overcome a miss chance or flat check, depending on edition. They also possess true seeing, allowing them to perceive things as they truly are, ignoring illusions and many forms of concealment. These abilities emphasize their status as extraplanar predators adapted to the deceptive, shifting environment of the Astral Plane.

Limit: Displacement can be countered by effects that negate concealment or by feats such as Blind-Fight. True seeing is subject to the usual limitations of the spell or innate ability, including range and inability to pierce certain powerful illusions or divine concealment.

Planar Travel and Spell-Like Abilities

Astradaemons possess a suite of spell-like or innate spells that support their role as soul hunters and extraplanar predators. In Pathfinder First Edition, they can use greater teleport (self plus limited weight), plane shift, fear, enervation, vampiric touch, locate creature, energy drain, and finger of death, among others. In Pathfinder Second Edition, their innate spells include discern location, finger of death, plane shift, dimension door, detect alignment (good only), and constant true seeing. These abilities allow them to track specific targets, move rapidly across planes, inflict death effects, and manipulate negative energy.

Limit: Spell-like abilities are limited by daily uses and subject to saving throws and spell resistance (PF1) or standard spell defenses (PF2). Their teleportation is often restricted to self plus a small amount of carried objects, limiting mass transport.

Physical Prowess and Resistances

Astradaemons are physically formidable: they are Large, fast-moving creatures with high Dexterity and Constitution, capable of running and flying at great speed (90 feet in PF1, 60 feet in PF2). They possess damage reduction against good and silver weapons (PF1) and weaknesses to good damage (PF2), along with immunities to acid, death effects, disease, poison (PF1), and to death effects and negative energy (PF2). They also resist cold, electricity, and fire. These traits make them durable opponents who thrive in hostile planar environments.

Limit: Their resistances and immunities can be bypassed by good-aligned weapons, powerful magic, or specific countermeasures. Their large size and reliance on melee attacks can be exploited by highly mobile or ranged opponents.

Accounts I
Creation by the Bound Prince and the Apocalypse RidersPathfinderWiki – Astradaemon (History section, synthesizing Paizo lore)

In-universe "depths of time"; published in modern era (21st-century RPG material)

According to PathfinderWiki’s synthesis of Paizo lore, astradaemons were created in the depths of time by a powerful daemon lord known as the Bound Prince. Distrustful of the deacons created by his servants—the Apocalypse Riders (also called the Four Horsemen)—the Bound Prince fused hundreds of mortal souls with the essences of lesser daemons to create a new race of deacons: the astradaemons. These creatures were tasked with harvesting souls from the Astral Plane to feed the Bound Prince’s undying hunger. Their creation, however, revealed the Bound Prince’s lack of trust in the Riders. This act planted resentment in the Riders’ minds and contributed to the Bound Prince’s eventual downfall. In the aftermath, the Riders slaughtered most astradaemons and enslaved the survivors, taking the secret of their creation for themselves. The story explains astradaemons’ origin, their role as soul-harvesting deacons, and their subsequent subjugation by the Apocalypse Riders. It also provides a mythic rationale for tensions within the daemon hierarchy and the Bound Prince’s downfall. Astradaemons

Accounts II
Hunting the River of SoulsBestiary 2, The Great Beyond, PathfinderWiki (Ecology sections)

Modern RPG publications (late 2000s–2010s)

Astradaemons are described as swimming through the River of Souls like aberrant sharks, hunting for vulnerable spirits. After mortals die, their souls travel along the River of Souls through the Astral Plane toward judgment and their final destination. Astradaemons patrol this river, snatching unlucky souls and dragging them back to Abaddon to feed their fiendish masters. They move in loose packs, circling and striking at souls that lack strong protection or guidance. Their touch corrupts and damages the spiritual material of souls, and their bite inflicts horrific wraith-like effects on anything they attack. This narrative establishes astradaemons as a major threat to the proper functioning of the afterlife in the Pathfinder cosmology. They disrupt the orderly passage of souls, feeding the hunger of daemon lords and contributing to the suffering and imbalance in the multiverse. For player characters and storytellers, astradaemons become emblematic antagonists in campaigns dealing with death, judgment, and planar travel.

Astradaemons as Deacons of AbaddonThe Great Beyond: A Guide to the Multiverse; PathfinderWiki (Ecology and History)

Modern RPG publications

In broader daemon lore, astradaemons function as specialized deacons or servitors of Abaddon’s Apocalypse Riders. They are perpetually ravenous, their touch corrupting and damaging the spiritual material of souls. They harvest souls from the Astral Plane and deliver them to Abaddon, where they feed the Riders’ apocalyptic agendas. Their existence reflects the plane’s focus on death by spiritual erosion and negative energy rather than simple physical destruction. This narrative situates astradaemons within the larger daemon hierarchy and clarifies their function as intermediaries between mortal death and fiendish consumption. It reinforces their thematic role as personifications of negative-energy death and level drain.
