Who It Is

Amitabha (Amitābha, 'Infinite Light'), also known as Amitayus ('Infinite Life'), is the Buddha of the Western Pure Land (Sukhavati) and the central deity of Pure Land Buddhism, the most widely practiced form of Buddhism in East Asia. According to the Longer Sukhavativyuha Sutra, countless eons ago, a king named Dharmakara was so moved by the suffering of sentient beings that he renounced his throne and became a bodhisattva. Before the Buddha Lokeshvararaja, he made 48 great vows, the most famous being the 18th: that any sentient being who, with sincere faith and aspiration, recites his name even ten times at the moment of death would be reborn in his Pure Land—and if this were not so, he would not attain Buddhahood. After five kalpas of accumulation of merit, Dharmakara fulfilled his vows and became the Buddha Amitabha. His Pure Land, Sukhavati, is described as a realm of perfect bliss and beauty, free from suffering, illness, and even the words for evil—an ideal environment for the practice of the dharma and the rapid attainment of enlightenment. Amitabha is typically depicted in deep red or gold, seated in meditation, with his hands in the dhyana mudra (the gesture of meditation). He is flanked by his two attendant bodhisattvas: Avalokiteshvara (compassion) on his left and Mahasthamaprapta (wisdom) on his right. The practice of nianfo (Chinese) or nembutsu (Japanese)—the recitation of 'Namu Amida Butsu' (Homage to Amitabha Buddha)—is the central devotional practice of Pure Land Buddhism, believed to ensure rebirth in Sukhavati through the power of Amitabha's vows. The Pure Land tradition, particularly through the Japanese schools of Jodo-shu (founded by Honen) and Jodo Shinshu (founded by Shinran), has been one of the most socially transformative Buddhist movements, opening the path to salvation to all people regardless of monastic status, education, or social class.
