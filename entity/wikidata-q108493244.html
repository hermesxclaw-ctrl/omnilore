Who It Is

He wakes up in a grey desert with a bullet already in him, pulls it out, and keeps walking. Hank J. Wimbleton is not a hero who survives Madness Combat — he is the reason it never ends: a taciturn, goggle-eyed killer resurrected again and again by unseen Higher Powers to grind through an endless war of grunts, agents, clowns, and demons in Nevada.

Attested behavior only; no internal monologue exists. Hank is taciturn, relentlessly aggressive, and single-mindedly violent. He shows no dialogue, no hesitation, and no mercy toward enemies, dispatching grunts, agents, and monsters with efficient, acrobatic brutality. Displays dark humor through exaggerated physics (using street signs, dumpsters as weapons). Shows loyalty only in later games where he fights alongside Sanford and Deimos (Project Nexus), but in main series operates alone. Powers
Superhuman Durability and Pain Tolerance

Survives gunshots, impalement, falls from cliffs, explosions, and dismemberment that would kill normal Nevada inhabitants. Continues fighting after being shot multiple times, stabbed, and set on fire. This is not invulnerability — he does die (at least three canonical deaths) — but his threshold is far above grunts and agents.

Limit: Can be killed by sufficient trauma (e.g., beaten to death by Tricky in MC9, shredded by Auditor's dark tendrils in MC11: Expurgation cliffhanger). Requires resurrection by Higher Powers to return. Bleeds and shows injury.

Master Gun-Fu and Marksmanship

Expert with all firearms: pistols (Desert Eagle, Glock, M1911), SMGs (MP5, MAC-10, UMP), shotguns, rifles (AK-47, M16), and heavy weapons. Performs acrobatic gun-kata: shooting while flipping, dual-wielding, mid-air reloads, ricochet shots. Near-perfect accuracy even while moving.

Limit: Limited by ammunition; must scavenge weapons from enemies. No supernatural aim-assist; can miss when disoriented by Improbability Drives.

Master Melee Combat

Proficient with blades (dragon sword, machete, katana), blunt weapons (baseball bat, pipe, street sign, hammer), and unarmed combat. Capable of disarming multiple opponents, parrying bullets with sword in later episodes, and using environmental objects as weapons.

Limit: Requires weapon in hand; unarmed against armed groups is disadvantaged. Swords can break.

Acrobatic Agility and Reflexes

Superhuman reflexes: dodges bullets at close range, wall-jumps, backflips over enemies, catches thrown weapons mid-air. Reaction time far exceeds normal grunts.

Limit: Can be overwhelmed by numbers or reality-warping (Tricky's teleportation). Stamina not infinite; shows fatigue after prolonged fights.

Resurrection via Higher Powers

After canonical deaths, Hank is revived by the enigmatic Higher Powers (meta-entities representing creators/audience). Each revival reconstructs his body, sometimes with enhancements (skeletal form, stitched body, halo compatibility). This is not self-triggered; it is an external intervention.

Limit: Cannot self-resurrect; dependent on Higher Powers' will. Revival takes time (between episodes). Body may return damaged/altered.

Improbability Drive Resistance / Halo Interaction

Unlike most characters who go insane or mutate when exposed to Improbability Drives (Tricky, Auditor), Hank shows resistance and later can briefly wield a halo (Auditor's dark halo in MC11). Allows him to operate in reality-warped zones.

Limit: Resistance is not immunity; prolonged exposure still damages him. Halo use corrupts and is temporary; in MC11 he is overwhelmed by Auditor despite halo.

Weapon Improvisation

Ability to use any object as lethal weapon — shovels, signs, dumpsters, bodies of enemies, even his own severed arm in one instance. Demonstrates cartoon-physics strength when throwing heavy objects.

Limit: Comedic physics not consistent; heavy objects still slow him.

Accounts I
Madness Combat 1Madness Combat 1 (Newgrounds, 2002)

2002

Hank is introduced as a lone gunman who enters a nightclub/building to assassinate the Sheriff. He fights through waves of grunts and agents, killing the Sheriff with a shotgun, only to be ambushed and killed himself by more agents. Establishes the loop of violence with no clear motive beyond 'kill target.' No dialogue. Establishes core premise and Hank's death-resurrection cycle; Sheriff's death triggers Jebus seeking revenge; Hank is immediately revived for sequel.

Madness Combat 5: Depredation & 5.5Madness Combat 5: Depredation (2004) and 5.5 (2005)

2004-2005

Hank, now in trench coat, is hunted by Jebus (a Jesus-like figure with supernatural powers) and Tricky the Clown. After surviving an ambush in a canyon and a train chase, Hank confronts Tricky, who is empowered by the Improbability Drive. Hank kills Tricky temporarily by impaling him, but Tricky resurrects in demonic form. Jebus intervenes, showing Hank is caught between two superhuman forces. Introduces Improbability Drives and reality-warping; establishes Tricky as recurring nemesis; Hank's durability escalates to surviving supernatural attacks.

Madness Combat 6: AntipathyMadness Combat 6: Antipathy (2005)

2005

Hank and Jebus form an uneasy truce to stop Tricky, who has become a reality-warping demon after the Improbability Drive overload. They fight through Tricky's zombie-like hordes in a hellish, shifting complex. Hank uses the dragon sword for the first time. Jebus sacrifices himself to detonate Tricky, but Tricky survives. Jebus's apparent death; Hank acquires dragon sword which becomes signature weapon; Tricky's demonic status solidified.

Accounts II
Madness Combat 7: ConsternationMadness Combat 7: Consternation (2007)

2007

Hank, now alone again, infiltrates the Auditor's organization to retrieve the Improbability Drive. He battles elite agents (A.T.P. soldaters), uses the dragon sword to deflect bullets, and confronts the Auditor's forces. The Auditor is revealed as the shadowy employer of the Sheriff and Jebus. Hank is severely wounded but escapes with the Drive. Auditor becomes primary antagonist; Improbability Drive lore expanded; Hank's role shifts from assassin to disruptor of Auditor's reality-control.

Madness Combat 9: AggregationMadness Combat 9: Aggregation (2008)

2008

Hank storms the Auditor's tower with Sanford and Deimos as backup. Tricky, resurrected again, ambushes Hank. In a brutal, extended fight, Tricky overwhelms and beats Hank to death, ripping his body apart. The episode ends with Hank definitively dead, his goggles left in a pool of blood. Sanford and Deimos are left to continue. First definitive on-screen death of Hank that lasts multiple episodes; triggers his most dramatic resurrection as skeletal Mag Hank; shifts focus to Sanford/Deimos for MC9.5-10.

Madness Combat 10: Abrogation & 11: ExpurgationMadness Combat 10: Abrogation (2009) and 11: Expurgation (2019)

2009-2019

Revived by Higher Powers in a charred, skeletal form stitched together, Hank returns as 'Mag Hank' — larger, stronger, with a metal jaw. He carves through Auditor's elite forces, the Magnitudes, and reclaims his halo. In MC11, Hank, Sanford, and Deimos assault Auditor's tower in Hell (the Other Place). Hank briefly wields the Auditor's dark halo, gaining immense power, but Auditor absorbs Tricky's Improbability Drive and overwhelms them. MC11 ends on cliffhanger with Hank being consumed by Auditor's darkness, fate unresolved as of 2026. Hank's resurrection lore confirmed; power escalation to supernatural tier; series left on unresolved cliffhanger pending MC12; established that Higher Powers actively rebuild him stronger each death.

MADNESS: Project Nexus - Story Mode (Canon)MADNESS: Project Nexus (2021) Story Mode, Episodes 1-2

2021-2023

Set between Madness Combat episodes, Hank appears as an ally to protagonists Sanford, Deimos, and new character Christoff. In Nevada's industrial underbelly, Hank helps the crew infiltrate Nexus facilities, fighting Sleepwalker patients and Auditor's science division. Lore terminals reveal Hank is considered a 'Specimen' of interest to the Auditor due to his Higher Powers connection. Hank is playable in Arena Mode with full backstory logs. Expands Hank's relationships (shows he can cooperate); provides in-game codex confirming his resurrection cycle and Improbability Drive resistance; bridges gap between MC7 and MC9 chronologically.

Hank's middle name is 'James' or 'John' and his full name is Hank James Wimbleton. — The 'J.' has never been expanded in any canonical animation, game, or creator statement. Krinkels has explicitly said the initial has no confirmed expansion and was added for style. Any full name beyond 'J.' is fan invention.

Hank is a hero who protects Nevada and fights for justice. — Hank has no attested heroic motive. He is a mass killer who murders hundreds of grunts, many of whom are low-level workers, and shows no concern for civilians. His targets (Sheriff, Auditor) are themselves villains, but his methods are indistinguishable from theirs. Krinkels describes him as 'the protagonist, not the good guy.'

Hank never dies and is immortal/invincible. — Hank dies canonically at least twice on-screen (MC1 and MC9) and is left in ambiguous death in MC11. His returns are not innate immortality but external resurrections by Higher Powers. He can be and has been killed by sufficient force.

Madness Combat is based on a true story or set in the real Nevada desert. — Nevada in Madness Combat is a fictional, purgatorial dimension with clones, Improbability Drives, and demons, not the U.S. state. No real-world basis beyond name. Krinkels chose 'Nevada' for its desert aesthetic and meme potential.
