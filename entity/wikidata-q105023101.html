The Approach

In the shadowed crossroads of Afro-Brazilian faith, where Catholic saints wear the masks of Orishas and the dead speak through living voices, there stands a figure both courtly and fierce: the Caboclo Gentil de Guiné. He is a spirit of the encantaria, a noble Indian chief of the Guinea coast, summoned in the terreiros of Tambor de Mina and other Afro-Brazilian traditions, embodying the resilience and spiritual power of the African diaspora in the Americas.

Content advisory. This entry concerns Afro-Brazilian religious traditions, which have historically been marginalized and misunderstood. Readers should approach with respect for living religious practices. Some accounts may involve syncretic elements that are sacred to practitioners; treat all material with cultural sensitivity.
Identity

Caboclo Gentil de Guiné

Pronunciation ka-BOH-kloo zhen-CHEEL dee gee-NAY

Etymology

The name combines 'Caboclo' (a term for a spirit of indigenous or mixed-race origin in Afro-Brazilian religions, also meaning 'Indian' or 'mestizo') with 'Gentil' (Portuguese for 'gentile' or 'noble') and 'de Guiné' (from Guinea, a region in West Africa). Thus, the name suggests a noble or gentile Caboclo from the Guinea coast.

Cultural Ownership

Afro-Brazilian religions, particularly Tambor de Mina, which is practiced in Maranhão and other parts of Brazil. The figure is part of the broader Caboclo spirit category, which blends indigenous, African, and European elements.

Disputed Identifications

There is no documented dispute about this specific entity, but the term 'Caboclo' is polysemous and can refer to different spirit types in different traditions. Some might conflate this entity with other Caboclos, but no evidence of confusion exists in the available material.
