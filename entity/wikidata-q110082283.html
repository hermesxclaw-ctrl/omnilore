Accounts II
Control Narrative 4: Historical Æthelbald of Mercia — Homonym, Not Source (for disambiguation)Anglo-Saxon Chronicle, Bede's continuations, charters S 92 etc.; modern synthesis in Yorke, Kings and Kingdoms of Early Anglo-Saxon England (1990)

8th century (reigned 716-757)

Æthelbald of Mercia, grandson of Alweo, seized Mercian throne after Ceolred, dominated southern England until murdered by his own bodyguard in 757. Bede's continuator and the Chronicle portray him as powerful but criticized by Boniface for personal conduct. This is a well-documented historical king and is NOT Ethelbald Rex Southmark, but is the most likely source of confusion due to name identity. Included here only to prevent conflation; no evidence links the fictional Southmark ruler to this history. Murder led to brief civil war and rise of Offa. No connection to Southmark.

Control Narrative 5: Historical Æthelbald of Wessex — Homonym, Not Source (for disambiguation)Anglo-Saxon Chronicle s.a. 855-860; Asser, Life of King Alfred

9th century (reigned 855-860)

Æthelbald of Wessex, second son of Æthelwulf, rebelled against his father in 855 and then ruled Wessex 858-860, controversially marrying his stepmother Judith. Died young, succeeded by Æthelberht. Again, this is a historical figure distinct from the fictional Rex Southmark. No source calls him Rex Southmark. Short reign; succession passed to brother. No Southmark polity involved.
