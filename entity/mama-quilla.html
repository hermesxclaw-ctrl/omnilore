Who It Is

Mama Quilla (Mama Killa, 'Mother Moon') is the Inca moon goddess, the sister-wife of Inti (the sun), and the divine mother of the Inca royal line alongside him. She was second only to Inti in the official Inca pantheon. Her temple in the Coricancha complex in Cusco was covered in silver (the moon's metal, as gold was the sun's), and she presided over the Inca calendar, women's rituals, and the menstrual cycles. The phases of the moon were interpreted as Mama Quilla's moods and health: a lunar eclipse was a terrifying event, believed to be a great jaguar or serpent attacking and devouring the moon. Inca priests and people would make tremendous noise — shouting, beating drums, blowing horns — to scare the beast away and restore Mama Quilla to wholeness. She was the patroness of married women, and the quipu (the Inca's knotted-string recording system) was traditionally kept in her shrines, linking her to memory, timekeeping, and the administration of the empire. The Coya (the emperor's principal wife) was considered Mama Quilla's earthly representative, and the intricate ritual life of Inca noblewomen was conducted in her name. After the conquest, Mama Quilla's worship was more thoroughly suppressed than that of Pachamama (whose rural, earth-based cult was harder to eradicate), but she survives in folk traditions and in the modern Quechua and Aymara recognition of the moon as a sacred being. Her silver disc symbolism endures in Andean jewelry and textile designs.
