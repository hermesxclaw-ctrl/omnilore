Who It Is

Taiowa is the supreme, infinite, and unknowable creator in Hopi cosmology — the ultimate source from which all existence emanates. In the Hopi creation narrative preserved by Chief White Bear and recorded in Frank Waters' 'Book of the Hopi' (1963), Taiowa is the first entity, existing before space, time, or substance. Taiowa then created Sotuknang, his nephew, to carry out the work of forming the worlds. Taiowa is so transcendent that Hopi tradition scarcely anthropomorphizes him; he has no physical form, no personality in the human sense, and no direct interaction with the material world. Instead, he is the silent, infinite presence behind all things — comparable to Brahman in Hindu philosophy or the Neoplatonic concept of the One. The Hopi themselves rarely invoke Taiowa directly in ritual, working instead through the kachinas, Sotuknang, and other intermediary spirits. This sophisticated metaphysics — an infinite, impersonal first cause delegating creation to intermediary agents — parallels some of the most abstract theological systems in world history and demonstrates that indigenous American cosmologies were fully capable of profound philosophical abstraction. Taiowa's nature is expressed not in mythic stories but in the Hopi way of life (Hopi means 'peaceful people'), which seeks harmony with the cosmic order he established.
