Who It Is

When the fires of Mount Doom finally went out, three shadows fell not as Nazgûl but as rescue — Gwaihir the Windlord, his brother Landroval, and with them a younger, swifter shape whose name flashes once and is gone: Meneldor. He is Tolkien's briefest Eagle, named only at the moment Frodo and Sam are lifted from annihilation, yet his single appearance ties together the whole theology of Tolkien's Eagles — watchers of Manwë, predators who debate eating hobbits, and the living answer to the question of why the Eagles do not simply fly the Ring to Mordor.

Collectively, Great Eagles display a distinct personality: proud, aloof, not kindly, and not servants. The Hobbit narrator stresses they are 'not kindly birds' and 'not afraid of goblins' but also not inclined to involve themselves in others' affairs unless need is great or debt is owed. They speak with formal, somewhat archaic courtesy, address Gandalf as friend, and negotiate rather than obey. They are predators (they hunt sheep, hares, and would consider eating a hobbit 'like a rabbit' per Marjorie Burns' analysis) yet also capable of pity and nobi

Powers
Immense Flight and Swiftness

As a Great Eagle, Meneldor can sustain high-altitude, long-distance flight at speeds far exceeding natural eagles, traversing from the Field of Cormallen to Mount Doom (approx. 30-40 miles across Mordor) in minutes through ash, heat and turbulence from an erupting volcano. Epithet 'young and swift' suggests exceptional speed even among Eagles.

Limit: Not tireless; requires thermals and rest. Vulnerable to extreme volcanic fumes and heat; Gandalf's rescue succeeds only because the Ring's destruction briefly stills the worst eruption. Cannot carry unlimited weight — one hobbit per eagle in this rescue. Cannot fly stealthily over Mordor while Sauron's power holds.

Keen Sight and Watchfulness

Eagles are the appointed watchers of Manwë, able to see movement of Orcs and armies from mountain eyries leagues away. In Lord of the Rings, Gwaihir's folk gather news for Rivendell and Radagast. Meneldor shares this inherited vigilance.

Limit: Sight is optical, not omniscient; they must be sent or choose to watch. They did not locate Frodo and Sam until Gandalf, via Gwaihir, directed them.

Human Speech and Sapience

Great Eagles speak Westron and/or Sindarin with formal eloquence, converse with Gandalf and Elves, and make independent political decisions (e.g., Lord of Eagles debating whether to help dwarves). Meneldor is sapient and speech-capable by kind, though no lines are attributed to him individually.

Limit: Late Tolkien theology: they have no fëa (soul) and are therefore not Children of Ilúvatar; their speech is taught, not innate incarnate speech. They speak but do not possess immortality of Elves or the Gift of Men. Some scholars debate whether this late note is compatible with published text.

Aerial Combat and Predation

Eagles fight with beak and talons, capable of killing Orcs, Wargs, and in First Age, winged dragons. At the Battle of the Morannon, the Eagles attack Nazgûl on fell beasts. Meneldor participates in this battle as part of Gwaihir's force before flying to Mount Doom.

Limit: Vulnerable to fell beasts and to arrows; not invulnerable. They intervene only at critical junctures, not as routine air force, reflecting their independent will.

Carrying and Rescue

Can bear the weight of a hobbit or man in talons or on back over considerable distance. Meneldor's attested feat is bearing one hobbit (traditionally Samwise or Frodo, depending on reading; text implies each eagle bears one hobbit, with Gandalf on Gwaihir) from the slopes of Mount Doom to safety.

Limit: Requires the passenger to be conscious enough to cling or be gripped; cannot carry the One Ring without corruption risk if prolonged; Gandalf notes Eagles are not beasts of burden to be summoned at will.

Form & Signs

Appearance — No individual physical description of Meneldor is given beyond his membership in the Great Eagles. By kind, the Great Eagles resemble true eagles (Aquila) but at vastly greater scale. Thorondor, greatest of all, is given a wingspan of 30 fathoms (approx. 55 m / 180 ft) in The Silmarillion; Third Age Eagles are smaller but still immense enough to carry a fully grown man, dwarf, or hobbit in their talons or upon their backs without apparent strain.

Symbols — Eagles as a class are symbols of Manwë Súlimo, Lord of the Valar and King of Arda, whose domain is air and winds. They are explicitly called 'Witnesses of Manwë' in Númenor (Akallabêth) and 'Eagles of the Lords of the West' when appearing as storm-clouds. Iconographically they represent watchfulness, kingship, swiftness, and eucatastrophic intervention — the sudden turn from despair to joy. Meneldor inherits this symbolism wit

Domains — Ancestral domain: the peaks of the Crissaegrim encircling Gondolin and later the northern Misty Mountains (north of Goblin Town) for the Third Age colony. Also eyries on Meneltarma and Sorontil in Númenor during Second Age for the species, though Meneldor himself is Third Age. In The Lord of the Rings, Gwaihir's folk patrol widely: Misty Mountains, Wilderland, Isengard, Celebdil (Zirakzigil), and the Morannon/Mount Doom region. Meneldor'

Accounts I
The Rescue from Mount Doom — The Field of CormallenJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King, Book VI, Chapter 4 'The Field of Cormallen'

Third Age 3019, written 1948-1954, published 1955

After Frodo Baggins and Samwise Gamgee destroy the One Ring in the fires of Orodruin, the mountain erupts. The Host of the West, camped at the Field of Cormallen in Ithilien, sees the eruption. Gandalf, who had been with the Host, asks Gwaihir the Windlord to bear him — for a third time — and to bring his brother Landroval and Meneldor young and swift. The three Eagles fly with Gandalf through ash and smoke to the slopes of Mount Doom, where Frodo and Sam lie exhausted on a rock amid the lava. The Eagles lift them and bear them to safety, thence to Ithilien where they are tended. This is Meneldor's sole named appearance; his deed is eucatastrophic rescue at the climax of the War of the Ring. Frodo and Sam survive; the quest is not a pyrrhic martyrdom. Gandalf's prophecy that Frodo's burden would be lifted is fulfilled. Establishes Eagles as agents of mercy under Manwë. Provides narrative closure and demonstrates that pity and cooperation, not power, win the war.

The Battle of the Morannon (The Black Gate Opens)J.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings: The Return of the King, Book V, Chapter 10 'The Black Gate Opens'

Third Age 3019, written 1950-1953, published 1955

As Aragorn's Host of the West marches to the Black Gate to distract Sauron from Frodo, the Nazgûl on fell beasts attack. Gwaihir and his Eagles, including by implication Meneldor as part of his following, arrive from the north and assail the Nazgûl, driving them off and allowing the battle on the ground to proceed until the Ring's destruction causes Sauron's forces to collapse. While Meneldor is not named in this chapter, his presence at the subsequent rescue minutes later implies he was among the Eagles at the Gate. Eagles prevent aerial rout of the Host; their intervention is explicitly called as part of Gandalf's plan. Sets up immediate availability for the Mount Doom rescue.

The Watch upon Morgoth — Thorondor and the Eagles of the First AgeJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Silmarillion, Quenta Silmarillion Ch.3 'Of the Coming of the Elves', Ch.13 'Of the Return of the Noldor', Ch.18 'Of the Ruin of Beleriand'

First Age, written 1917-1937, edited 1977

At Manwë's command, spirits in shape of eagles are sent to Middle-earth to dwell in the mountains north of Beleriand and watch Morgoth. Under Thorondor, Lord of Eagles, they keep Gondolin hidden, bear messages for Turgon, rescue Maedhros from Thangorodrim (Thorondor with Fingon), rescue Beren and Lúthien from Angband, and rescue the survivors of Gondolin's fall. In the War of Wrath, Thorondor leads all great birds against Ancalagon's winged dragons alongside Eärendil. This is the ancestral history of Meneldor's people; Meneldor himself is not alive in this age but is descended from Thorondor's line. Establishes theological role of Eagles as Manwë's agents and explains their later independence and nobility. Provides lineage claim: Gwaihir and Landroval are said to be descendants of Thorondor, and Meneldor belongs to same lineage.

Accounts II
The Witnesses of Manwë on NúmenorJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Silmarillion, Akallabêth; Unfinished Tales, 'A Description of the Island of Númenor'

Second Age, written 1930s-1960s, published 1977/1980

On the island of Númenor, three Eagles guard the summit of Meneltarma, the Holy Mountain, appearing whenever anyone approaches and remaining aloft during the Three Prayers. A pair nests on the King's House in Armenelos; many dwell on Sorontil. The Númenóreans call them Witnesses of Manwë. When the Númenóreans rebel against the Ban of the Valar, Manwë sends eagle-shaped storm clouds — the Eagles of the Lords of the West — as warning. These are not necessarily Meneldor but show the species' sacred function between First and Third Ages. Shows Eagles as religious-political symbols and moral warning system. Explains why later Middle-earth Men revere Eagles. Connects Meneldor's kind to Númenórean memory.

The Eagles of the Misty Mountains — Rescue of Thorin's Company and Battle of Five ArmiesJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Hobbit, Chapters 6-7 and 17

Third Age 2941 (narrative), written 1930-1936, published 1937

A colony of Eagles lives in eyries in the northern Misty Mountains, led by the Lord of the Eagles. They are enemies of goblins but also hunt the Woodmen's sheep, so relations are cool. They rescue Bilbo Baggins, Gandalf and the Dwarves of Thorin's company from burning trees surrounded by Wargs and goblins, carrying them to the Carrock, and later, having observed the mustering of goblins, arrive in great numbers at the Battle of Five Armies to turn the tide. Gandalf's long friendship with the Lord of the Eagles (he once healed him from an arrow wound) explains their aid. This colony is the immediate predecessor community to Gwaihir's folk in Lord of the Rings; Meneldor is of this people, though not yet named. Establishes modern (Third Age) Eagle politics: independent, not summonable, but honor-bound to Gandalf. Explains why Gwaihir later helps Gandalf at Orthanc and Zirakzigil. Provides ecological and social context for Meneldor's generation.

Gwaihir's Services to Gandalf — Orthanc and ZirakzigilJ.R.R. Tolkien, The Lord of the Rings: The Fellowship of the Ring II.2 'The Council of Elrond' and The Two Towers III.5 'The White Rider'

Third Age 3018-3019, written 1939-1949, published 1954

Gwaihir the Windlord, eldest and swiftest of the Eagles, is sent by Radagast to gather news and finds Gandalf imprisoned on Orthanc by Saruman; he bears Gandalf to Edoras. Later, after Gandalf's battle with the Balrog on Celebdil (Zirakzigil), Gwaihir, sent by Galadriel, finds Gandalf dying on the peak and bears him to Lothlórien to be healed and re-clothed as Gandalf the White. These two rescues establish the pattern of Eagle eucatastrophe that culminates in Meneldor's rescue of the hobbits; Meneldor is not present but belongs to Gwaihir's following who perform such services. Demonstrates Eagles as Gandalf's allies by choice and by Manwë's will, not servants. Prefigures the Mount Doom rescue structurally. Explains Gandalf's ability to call Gwaihir, who then calls his kin including Meneldor.

The Eagles could have simply flown Frodo to Mount Doom to destroy the Ring, making the whole quest unnecessary (the 'Eagle plot hole') — Text and Tolkien's letters (Letter 210 to Forrest J. Ackerman, 1958) show Eagles are not taxis: they are proud, independent, not at Gandalf's command; Mordor's airspace was guarded by Nazgûl and Sauron's will; the Ring would corrupt any bearer, including an Eagle; and the Eagles' role is eucatastrophic intervention only when hope is lost, not strategic transport. Meneldor and his kin fly to Mordor only after Sauron falls and the Nazgûl are unhorsed.

Meneldor is the same person as Tar-Meneldur, King of Númenor — Tar-Meneldur (also spelled Tar-Meneldor in early manuscripts) is a Man, fifth King of Númenor, father of Tar-Aldarion, who lived in Second Age c. 543-942. Meneldor the Eagle lives at end of Third Age c. 3019, is a Great Eagle, not a Man. Names are similar because both derive from same Sindarin/Quenya root menel 'heavens', but they are distinct characters separated by ~2000 years and species.

Meneldor speaks lines in The Return of the King — No dialogue is attributed to Meneldor individually. All Eagle speech in that chapter is via Gwaihir or collective action. Meneldor is named only in narration as part of trio; his sapience is inferred from kind, not demonstrated by lines.

All three Eagles at Mount Doom are brothers — Only Landroval is called brother of Gwaihir. Meneldor is described as 'young and swift', indicating junior status but not brotherhood. See Contradictions register.
