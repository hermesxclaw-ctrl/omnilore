The Tempest - Ariel's Servitude and Promise of Freedom (component context: Ariel)William Shakespeare - The Tempest

1611 (Jacobean)

Ariel, an airy spirit, was imprisoned by witch Sycorax in a cloven pine, freed by Prospero, and bound to serve him. Ariel performs magic including shipwrecking, invisibility, and harpy illusion, in exchange for promised liberation. In Act 5, Prospero frees Ariel. Attested, not to be conflated with composite. Established Ariel as archetype of air-spirit, servant-magician, and figure of colonial and postcolonial reinterpretation

Theogony - Birth of the Anemoi (component context: Anemoi)Hesiod - Theogony

c. 700 BCE, Archaic Greece

Eos (Dawn) and Astraeus beget the winds: Zephyrus, Boreas, and Notus. Elsewhere Hesiod describes Typhoeus as source of violent winds. The Anemoi are divine winds with directional domains, later systematized as four (adding Eurus) and eight in Hellenistic tower of winds. Foundation for Greek wind cosmology, iconography, and Roman equivalents (Aquilo, Favonius, Auster, Vulturnus)

Samudra Manthana - Churning of the Ocean (component context: Asura)Mahabharata Adi Parva 18, Bhagavata Purana 8.6-8, Vishnu Purana 1.9

Mahabharata c. 400 BCE - 400 CE; Puranas c. 300-1000 CE

Devas and Asuras cooperate to churn the ocean of milk to obtain amrita (immortality). Vishnu as Kurma supports Mount Mandara. Asuras are tricked by Mohini (Vishnu's female form) and denied amrita, cementing deva-asura enmity. Early Rigvedic Asura (Varuna) is not antagonist; later Puranic Asura is. Defines classical Hindu deva-asura opposition; Buddhist adaptation makes Asuras one of six realms driven by jealousy

The Rape of the Lock - Ariel as Sylph (component context: Ariel homonym)Alexander Pope - The Rape of the Lock

1712-1714

Pope borrows Shakespeare's Ariel to create a sylph, guardian of Belinda, who warns her of impending social disaster (the cutting of a lock of hair). Ariel's failure to prevent the act satirizes heroic epic conventions. Shows how Ariel name migrates across genres. Established Ariel as literary type for airy guardian; influenced later Ariel depictions

Rigveda - Asura as Title for Varuna (earliest Asura usage)Rigveda Mandala 2.27, 5.85, 7.65

c. 1500-1200 BCE

In earliest layers, 'asura' is an honorific for supreme sovereign gods, especially Varuna and Agni, denoting lordship and life-force. Varuna as Asura oversees rta (cosmic order) and punishes oath-breakers. Later semantic shift makes asura antagonistic. Demonstrates that 'Asura' was not originally demonic; scholarly debate on Indo-Iranian *ahura vs daeva inversion
