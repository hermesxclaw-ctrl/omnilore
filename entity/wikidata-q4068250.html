Who It Is

In the high mountain valleys of the North Caucasus, where the forests run deep and the game is swift, the Nart heroes knew that success in the hunt was never a matter of skill alone. It was a gift, granted by a silent, watchful lord of the wilds. His name was Apsat, and to the Circassian and Abkhaz peoples who carried the Nart sagas for centuries, he was the unseen hand that guided the arrow and the protector of the beasts that gave their lives to sustain the people. He was the original 'Hunting Spirit' — a figure of respect, not fear, a reminder that the wilderness was a realm with its own rules, its own master.

Apsat is a patron deity of hunting and the wilderness, particularly of wild animals. He is seen as a protector and provider, governing the fate of game animals and the success of hunters. He is a figure of respect and propitiation, with hunters seeking his favor.
