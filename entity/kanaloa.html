Who It Is

Kanaloa is the Hawaiian god of the ocean, navigation, the deep sea, and the underworld. He is one of the four major akua (gods) of traditional Hawaiian religion alongside Kāne, Kū, and Lono. While Kāne is associated with creation and the life-giving freshwater, Kanaloa complements him as the ruler of the vast saltwater realm that surrounds the islands. He is often depicted as a giant squid or octopus (he'e), and his symbol — the octopus — embodies the reaching, enveloping, and mysterious nature of the ocean. In the great Hawaiian creation chant 'Kumulipo,' Kanaloa is present from the earliest moments of creation, linked to the primordial darkness from which life emerges. After missionaries arrived, Kanaloa was sometimes conflated with Satan (a gross misrepresentation), leading to a distorted perception that persists in some popular accounts. In reality, Kanaloa is a necessary, respected, and powerful deity who governs the realm that gave the Polynesian voyagers their pathway to the islands. He is the patron of ocean voyagers, fishermen, and those who navigate by the stars. His realm touches the underworld (Milu), the place of the dead, reflecting the Polynesian understanding of the ocean as both the source of life and the final destination of souls. Healers (kahuna lāpau) also invoke him for deep, hidden illnesses — those that, like the ocean depths, conceal their true nature.
