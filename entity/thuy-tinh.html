Who It Is

Thuy Tinh (Thủy Tinh, 'Water Spirit') is the Vietnamese god of the waters—the sea, the rivers, the rain, the monsoon, and the flood. He is the eternal rival of Son Tinh, the mountain god, and their cosmic conflict explains the annual cycle of monsoon flooding that characterizes life in the Red River Delta. In the foundational myth, Thuy Tinh was one of two suitors for Princess My Nuong, the daughter of the 18th Hung King. As the god of waters, his powers were immense: he could summon rain with a thought, command the seas to rise, and bring forth typhoons. His domain was the deep, the fluid, the constantly moving and changing world of water. When he lost the contest for My Nuong's hand to Son Tinh, who arrived first with the prescribed wedding gifts, Thuy Tinh's rage was cataclysmic. He raised a great storm, sent floodwaters surging against Tan Vien Mountain where the newlyweds had retreated, and attacked with all the force of the seas. The battle between the two elemental gods continued for weeks, devastating the landscape. Son Tinh prevailed by raising the mountains higher each time the waters rose. Defeated but undefeated—Thuy Tinh has never accepted his loss. Every year, he renews his assault, bringing the monsoon rains, the rising rivers, the typhoons of the summer season—and every year, Son Tinh resists. Thuy Tinh thus embodies the ambivalent nature of water in Vietnamese culture: it is simultaneously life-giving (the water that nourishes the rice paddies) and life-destroying (the floods that sweep away villages). He is not simply a villain but a force of nature whose relationship with humanity is one of negotiation, propitiation, and eternal struggle. He represents the wild, untamable aspect of nature that can never be fully subdued but must be perpetually managed through a combination of respect, ritual, and engineering.
