Who They Are

Kueyen is the Mapuche moon goddess, the wife of Antu (the sun) and the ruler of the night. She governs the tides, the menstrual cycles of women, and the lunar calendar that determines the timing of many Mapuche rituals. Her bright, cool light illuminates the path of travelers and the gatherings of the Mapuche community.

The Approach

In the quiet of a Mapuche night, Kueyen, the moon, casts her silver light over the land, a silent queen among the spirits of the sky. She is no mere celestial body, but a living goddess, wife to the sun and sovereign of the stars, her story woven into the very fabric of the cosmos.

Etymology

The name Kueyen or Kuyen is the Mapuche word for 'moon'. The etymology is rooted in the Mapudungun language.

Cultural Ownership

Mapuche people (indigenous people of south-central Chile and southwestern Argentina)

Disputed Identifications

Kueyen is sometimes mistakenly identified with Anchimallen, a different class of mythological creatures in Mapuche lore. This is a common error in popular sources.

Description

Appearance

Kueyen is the moon itself, a luminous celestial body that illuminates the night sky. As a spirit, she is likely envisioned in anthropomorphic form, though specific descriptions are not provided in the source material.

Symbols & Iconography

The moon is her primary symbol. In Mapuche culture, the moon may be associated with femininity, fertility, and the night.

Behaviour & Personality

As a principal wangulen spirit, Kueyen is a powerful and revered deity. She governs the other star spirits, suggesting a role of authority and order in the celestial realm. Her marriage to Antu, the sun, indicates a complementary relationship between day and night.

Domains & Habitats

Kueyen resides in the heavens, specifically associated with the moon and the night sky. She is a celestial spirit with dominion over the stars.

Cultural Significance

Kueyen is one of the most important female figures in Mapuche religion, reflecting the relatively high status of women in traditional Mapuche society. The Machi (shaman) — often a woman — maintains a special relationship with Kueyen. Mapuche silverwork, one of the most distinctive and admired traditions of indigenous American art, is heavily influenced by moon symbolism. The moon's phases also determine the timing of the Ngillatún ceremony, the most important Mapuche ritual.
