Who It Is

In the neon-lit arenas of the Dead or Alive fighting tournament, Rig is a master of the brutal art of Taekwondo, a man whose past is shrouded in mystery and whose allegiance is as fluid as his combat style. But the name 'Rig' alone conjures a kaleidoscope of meanings—from the towering oil derricks that pierce the sky to the ancient Norse god who fathered mankind. This dossier untangles the many threads of 'Rig', distinguishing the fighter from the fish, the deity from the drilling platform, and the mathematical concept from the land tenure system.

Rig from Dead or Alive is portrayed as calm, focused, and disciplined, with a hidden intensity. He is a skilled fighter who values honor but is also involved in covert operations. The Norse Ríg is depicted as a wise and generous wanderer who establishes social order. Other 'rig' subjects are inanimate or abstract.

Powers
Taekwondo mastery

Rig is a highly skilled practitioner of Taekwondo, specializing in powerful kicks and agile movements. His fighting style emphasizes speed, precision, and devastating leg techniques.

Limit: He is not superhuman; his abilities are within realistic human limits. He can be defeated by other skilled fighters.

Mysterious combat enhancements

In some storylines, Rig is implied to have undergone experimental procedures that enhance his physical abilities, but this is not fully elaborated.

Limit: The extent and nature of these enhancements are unclear and may be speculative.

Norse god's generative power

In Norse mythology, Ríg (Heimdall) is credited with fathering the ancestors of the three social classes: thralls, freemen, and nobles. He possesses the wisdom and authority to establish social order.

Limit: This is a mythological narrative, not a literal power. It is symbolic of social hierarchy.

Mathematical structure: rig

In mathematics, a rig (or semiring) is an algebraic structure with addition and multiplication, similar to a ring but without requiring additive inverses. It is used in various areas of mathematics and computer science.

Limit: It is an abstract concept, not a physical power.

The Lay of Ríg (Rígsþula)Poetic Edda

13th century (estimated)

The god Ríg wanders through the world and visits three homes: a poor hut, a modest farm, and a great hall. At each, he stays for three nights and sleeps between the hosts, leading to the birth of a son. These sons become the ancestors of the three social classes: thralls, freemen, and nobles. The poem explains the divine origin of social hierarchy. The poem establishes the social order as divinely ordained. It also connects Ríg to the god Heimdall.
