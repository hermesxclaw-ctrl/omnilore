Avalokiteshvara

The Lord Who Looks Down with Compassion; Bodhisattva of Infinite Compassion; Gua

also known asLokeśvaraChenrezigGua
TypeBodhisattva / Celestial Savior
CultureBuddhist
Who It Is
Form

Avalokiteshvara is often depicted as a bodhisattva with multiple arms, each holding a different object or symbol, such as a lotus flower, a jewel, or a vajra. He is sometimes shown with a thousand arms, each representing a different aspect of his compassion. In some traditions, he is depicted as a blue or white deity with a peaceful and benevolent expression.

Powers

Miraculous Observing Wisdom Avalokiteshvara's ability to observe and understand the suffering of all sentient beings, and to respond with compassion and wisdom.

Accounts

According to Buddhist tradition, Avalokiteshvara has 108 different forms, each representing a different aspect of his compassion. These forms are said to have been created by Avalokiteshvara in order to help sentient beings in different ways.

According to Buddhist tradition, Avalokiteshvara descended into hell in order to help sentient beings who were suffering there. He used his miraculous observing wisdom to understand the suffering of the beings in hell, and he used his compassion and wisdom to help them escape.
