Who It Is

The original zombie: in Haitian Vodou and folklore, a dead body reanimated through sorcery by a bokor (sorcerer) to serve as a mindless slave. The zombi has no will, no memory, and no soul — the bokor has captured the victim's ti bon ange (the 'little good angel,' one of the soul's components) in a bottle, leaving only the corps cadavre (body) to be controlled. The concept blends West African spiritual traditions (the Kikongo word 'zumbi' means fetish or spirit of the dead) with the trauma of Haitian enslavement — the zombie as the ultimate horror of the enslaved: a body without freedom, laboring in perpetuity. The Haitian Penal Code historically recognized zombification as attempted murder. The ethnobotanist Wade Davis proposed that tetrodotoxin (from pufferfish) combined with datura was used to induce a death-like state, enabling 'resurrection.'
