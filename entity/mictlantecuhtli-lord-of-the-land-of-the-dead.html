Who It Is

Mictlantecuhtli is the grim but essential lord of the Aztec underworld, Mictlan — a vast, multi-level region through which every soul (regardless of moral quality, unless they died in specific ways) must travel for four years, navigating obsidian-blade mountains, wind-knife valleys, and rivers of blood before reaching the final 'oblivion' of Chiconauhmictlan. In the creation myth, Mictlantecuhtli possess the bones of the previous humanity from which Quetzalcoatl must steal the raw material for the new human race. The lord of the underworld is a deceptively shrewd negotiator: he allows Quetzalcoatl to take the bones but sends a quail to startle him, causing him to drop and break them — explaining why humans are of different sizes and why they are mortal. Mictlantecuhtli's figure — often fully skeletal with a starry headband — embodies the Aztec understanding that death is not an absence but a presence, a powerful deity whose realm required constant propitiation.
