The Approach

In the sterile corridors of Kamino's cloning facilities, one figure moved with quiet authority—a Kaminoan scientist whose work would shape the fate of a galaxy. Nala Se, the chief medical officer of Tipoca City, was more than a background administrator; she was the architect of the Grand Army of the Republic, the keeper of genetic secrets, and a survivor who navigated the shifting tides of war and empire with cold precision.

Content advisory. Contains themes of medical experimentation, cloning, war, and moral ambiguity.
Identity

Nala Se

Pronunciation NAH-lah SAY

Etymology

The name 'Nala Se' follows the Kaminoan naming convention, which often consists of two short syllables. No specific etymology has been provided in official sources.

Cultural Ownership

Kaminoan; part of the Star Wars franchise, owned by Lucasfilm Ltd. and The Walt Disney Company.

Disputed Identifications

None known; Nala Se is a distinct character without conflicting identifications.

Description

Appearance

Nala Se is a Kaminoan, a tall, slender humanoid with pale blue-gray skin, large dark eyes, and a long neck. She wears a distinctive white and blue medical uniform, often with a high collar and a long coat. Her head is elongated, with a fin-like crest, and she moves with a graceful, deliberate gait.

Symbols & Iconography

Associated with the Kaminoan cloning facilities, medical insignia, and the symbol of the Grand Army of the Republic. Her uniform often features the Kaminoan government's emblem.

Behaviour & Personality

Nala Se is calm, composed, and highly intelligent. She speaks in a slow, measured tone, often with a detached, clinical demeanor. She is deeply invested in her work, prioritizing scientific advancement and the success of the clone program. She can be pragmatic to the point of moral ambiguity, often deferring to the needs of the project over individual clone lives. She is also shown to be protective of her work and her people, and she forms a complex relationship with the clone Force-sensitive child Omega.

Domains & Habitats

Kamino, specifically Tipoca City, the cloning facility. She also operates on other locations during the Bad Batch series, including on the run from the Empire.

Version Differences

In the original Star Wars: The Clone Wars, Nala Se is a background character, primarily seen in administrative and medical roles. In The Bad Batch, her character is expanded, revealing her deeper involvement in cloning secrets and her moral struggles. No significant differences exist between canon and Legends, as she was created for the canon series.

Stories & Myths

Creation of the Clone Army

Source Star Wars: Episode II - Attack of the Clones (2002) and The Clone Wars seriesCulture Star Wars universePeriod Pre-Clone Wars and during the Clone Wars

Summary

Nala Se was one of the lead scientists on Kamino who created the clone army for the Republic, using the genetic template of the bounty hunter Jango Fett. She oversaw the accelerated growth and training of the clones, ensuring their loyalty and combat effectiveness.

Participants

Consequences

The clone army becomes the Grand Army of the Republic, fighting in the Clone Wars. Nala Se's work is instrumental in the Republic's war effort.

Variants

The story is consistent across canon sources.

The Clone Conspiracy and the Chips

Source Star Wars: The Clone Wars (Season 6, episodes 'The Lost One', 'Voices', 'Sacrifice')Culture Star Wars universePeriod Late Clone Wars

Summary

Nala Se is revealed to have been involved in the creation of inhibitor chips implanted in clones, which would later be used to execute Order 66. She is aware of the chips' true purpose, though she may not have known the full extent of the Sith plot.

Participants

Nala Se

Fives

Lama Su

The Kaminoan government

Consequences

Fives discovers the chips and attempts to expose the conspiracy, leading to his death. Nala Se remains silent, and the chips remain in place, enabling the Jedi Purge.

Variants

The exact knowledge and intent of Nala Se are ambiguous; some sources suggest she was coerced or unaware of the final purpose.

The Bad Batch and Omega

Source Star Wars: The Bad Batch (2021-2024)Culture Star Wars universePeriod Immediately after the Clone Wars

Summary

Nala Se is a key figure in The Bad Batch, as she is the one who created Omega, a female clone with unaltered DNA. She protects Omega and assists the Bad Batch in their missions, often against the Empire's wishes. She eventually defects from the Empire and helps the clones escape.

Participants

Nala Se

Omega

The Bad Batch

Consequences

Nala Se's actions help preserve the clone legacy and lead to the eventual downfall of the Empire's cloning programs. She sacrifices her position and possibly her life to ensure Omega's safety.

Variants

The series shows Nala Se's moral evolution from a detached scientist to a protective figure.

Complicity in Order 66

Source The Clone Wars, The Bad Batch

Description

Nala Se was aware of the inhibitor chips and their purpose, yet she did not expose the plot. This led to the execution of the Jedi and the rise of the Empire. Her silence makes her complicit in the deaths of countless Jedi and clones.

Scholarly Context

The moral ambiguity of scientists in authoritarian regimes is a common theme in science fiction.

Treatment of Clones as Property

Source The Clone Wars, The Bad Batch

Description

As a Kaminoan scientist, Nala Se viewed clones as products, not people. She performed experiments and modifications on them without full ethical consideration. This is evident in her initial detachment from individual clones.

Scholarly Context

Reflects real-world debates on genetic engineering and the definition of personhood.

Contradictions & Disputes

Knowledge of the inhibitor chips' purpose

Position A

Nala Se was fully aware that the chips would be used to force clones to execute Order 66.

Position A — evidence

In The Clone Wars, she is present when the chips are discussed, and she does not express surprise later.

Position B

Nala Se only knew the chips were for obedience and was unaware of the specific order.

Position B — evidence

In The Bad Batch, she seems disturbed by the Empire's use of the chips, suggesting limited knowledge.

Scholarly Consensus

No clear consensus; the ambiguity is intentional.

Her moral stance towards clones

Position A

Nala Se sees clones as expendable property.

Position A — evidence

Her initial detachment and willingness to sacrifice clones for the mission.

Position B

Nala Se cares about clones as individuals, at least some of them.

Position B — evidence

Her special treatment of Omega and her eventual aid to the Bad Batch.

Scholarly Consensus

Her character arc shows a shift from detached scientist to protective figure.
