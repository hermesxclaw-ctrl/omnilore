Sol Invictus (the Unconquered Sun)

Typegod (astral deity)
Who It Is
Form

The appearance of Sol Invictus is not well-documented in ancient sources, but it is believed that he was often depicted as a powerful, radiant god with a strong, muscular build. He was typically shown wearing a crown of sun rays, which symbolized his power and dominance over the sun. In some depictions, Sol Invictus was also shown riding a chariot, which was pulled by four horses and represented his control over the movements of the sun. The appearance of Sol Invictus was likely influenced by the ancient Roman sun god Sol, as well as the Syrian sun god Elagabalus, and reflects the cultural exchange and syncretism that occurred during the Roman Empire's expansion. Additionally, the appearance of Sol Invictus varied across different regions and cultures, with some depictions showing him as a more benevolent figure, while others portrayed him as a fierce and powerful deity. The cultural significance of Sol Invictus's appearance can be seen in the many artworks and literary works that feature him as a central figure, including the famous mosaics found in the Roman ruins of Ostia Antica.

Powers

Control over the sun Sol Invictus was believed to have control over the sun and its movements, and was often invoked in times of war and crisis to ensure the success and prosperity of the Roman Empire.

Accounts

The myth of Sol Invictus tells the story of the god's origins and his rise to power as the official sun god of the late Roman Empire. According to the myth, Sol Invictus was born from the union of the sun and the earth, and was destined to become the most powerful god in the Roman pantheon. The myth of Sol Invictus reflects the cultural significance of the god in Roman society, and highlights his role as a symbol of Roman power and dominance.

The worship of Sol Invictus was a significant aspect of Roman culture and society, and was closely tied to the imperial cult and the state. According to historical accounts, the worship of Sol Invictus involved the construction of temples and monuments, as well as the performance of rituals and sacrifices. The worship of Sol Invictus reflects the cultural significance of the god in Roman society, and highlights his role as a symbol of Roman power and dominance.
