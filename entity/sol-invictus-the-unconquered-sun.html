Who It Is

Sol Invictus, the 'Unconquered Sun,' is the chief astral deity of the Mithraic mysteries, distinct from but inextricably linked to Mithras. In the iconography of the tauroctony, Sol appears either as a bust in the upper left celestial arc (with his radiant crown) or as a standing figure who kneels before the enthroned Mithras — a scene commonly interpreted as Sol's 'submission' or 'investiture' by the superior Mithras. The two then share a sacred meal (the feasting scene of 'Sol and Mithras at the banquet,' depicted on the reverse of many tauroctony reliefs). This commensality — the god and the superior god dining together in a cave — models the fraternal bonds of the Mithraic initiates (syndexioi, 'the ones united by the handshake'). The Mithraic hierarchy of grades (Corax, Nymphus, Miles, Leo, Perses, Heliodromus, Pater) culminates in Heliodromus ('Sun-Runner'), the sixth grade, confirming Sol's central role in the mystery progression.
