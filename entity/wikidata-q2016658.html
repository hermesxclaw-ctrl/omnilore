The underworld, the depths of the earth, the bottom of the sea, the root system of the world-tree, the realm of the dead, and the source of all water and mineral wealth. Alako's domain is the 'Ala' (the below), a place of silence, cold, and primordial power. This is distinct from the Finnish term 'Tuonela', which is the specific realm of the dead, though Alako may dwell within or near Tuonela.

Form

Alako is not explicitly described in primary sources, but based on the general Finnish mythological framework, Alako can be reconstructed as a being of the depths. Likely depicted as a shadowy, formless entity that resides in the dark waters beneath the earth. In some interpretations, Alako may appear as a giant serpent or a fish-like creature, echoing the world-serpent motif found in other Finno-Ugric traditions. The being is often associated with cold, darkness, and the primordial elements of earth and water. No specific iconography has been found, but the spiral and snake motifs on Iron Age amulets may represent Alako's form.

Powers

Control over the depths Alako has absolute dominion over the underworld, the depths of the sea, and the interior of the earth. This includes the ability to cause earthquakes, floods, and the collapse of the ground. Attested in SKVR incantations Alako's power is limited to the below; Alako cannot directly influence the sky or the surface world without a conduit or a ritual. Some sources suggest Alako's power is purely passive, while others depict Alako as actively malevolent.

Guardianship of the dead Alako is the warden of the underworld, preventing the dead from returning to the land of the living and ensuring that the living do not trespass into the realm of the dead. Attested in Kanteletar and oral laments Alako can be bypassed by powerful shamans or heroes who know the correct rituals and have the proper charms. In some regional traditions, the god of the dead is a separate entity (Tuoni), while Alako merely guards the border.

Knowledge of hidden things Alako possesses knowledge of all that is hidden beneath the earth: mineral veins, buried treasure, the roots of plants, and the secrets of the dead. Attested in SKVR treasure-hunting spells This knowledge is only granted to those who make the correct offerings and perform the proper rituals. Some spells suggest that the knowledge is freely given to those who ask, while others imply it must be wrested from Alako by force.

Healing and cursing Alako can cause and cure diseases, particularly those affecting the skin, bones, and internal organs. Alako is often invoked in healing rituals to drive out the 'worms' or 'spirits' that cause illness. Attested in SKVR healing incantations Alako's healing power is specific to ailments of the 'lower' parts of the body; ailments of the head or chest are typically attributed to other deities. The line between Alako's healing and cursing powers is blurred; the same ritual can be used to heal or to harm, depending on the intent of the practitioner.

Behavior & Habitat

Alako is generally a passive and reclusive deity, preferring the quiet of the deep over the affairs of the surface world. When disturbed, Alako can be dangerous, causing floods, earthquakes, or diseases. In some stories, Alako is a guardian of the dead, ensuring that souls do not escape the underworld and that the living do not enter prematurely. Alako is also a keeper of ancient knowledge and treasures, and those who seek such things must make offerings or perform rituals to gain Alako's favor.

Accounts

In the beginning, there was only the primordial water, and above it, the sky. A diving bird (the eagle or the duck) seeks a place to rest and finds the knee of the hero Väinämöinen, who rises from the waters. The bird lays eggs on Väinämöinen's knee, which hatch and form the world. The lower parts of the eggs become the earth, and the upper parts become the sky. Alako is not named, but the 'lower parts' (alinen) are the domain of Alako, suggesting that Alako is the personification of the primordial earth that emerges from the waters.

Väinämöinen, seeking the secret words of origin, journeys to the underworld (Tuonela). He must cross the river of Tuoni, where he is challenged by the daughter of Tuoni. After his journey, Väinämöinen is unable to escape the underworld until he transforms into a serpent and slips through the net. Alako is not explicitly mentioned, but the underworld is Alako's domain, and the journey is a trespass into Alako's realm. The story highlights the dangers of the below and the power of those who dwell there.

The Sampo, a magical artifact that brings prosperity, is forged by the smith Ilmarinen. It is kept in the stronghold of Pohjola (the North). Väinämöinen and his companions steal the Sampo and flee, but they are pursued by Louhi, the mistress of Pohjola. During the battle, the Sampo is broken and falls into the sea. The fragments of the Sampo are scattered, and the sea becomes rich with its bounty. Alako is not named, but the Sampo's fragments sink into the depths, and the sea's wealth is a gift from the below. The story connects Alako's domain to the source of all prosperity.

What We Don't Know

The primary sources for Alako are scattered and often indirect. There is no single, comprehensive narrative that features Alako as a central character. The SKVR database contains the most direct references, but these are often fragmentary and context-dependent. Further research is needed to: 1) Systematically analyze all SKVR entries mentioning 'Ala' or 'Alako'. 2) Compare Alako with similar deities in other Finno-Ugric traditions (e.g., Estonian 'Vanapagan', Hungarian 'Alvilág'). 3) Investigate the archaeological evidence for underworld deities in Iron Age Finland. 4) Explore the reception of Alako in modern Finnish culture and art. 5) Translate and publish more of the Karelian rune singer recordings.

A way to hold it in your mind

Think of Alako as the Finnish equivalent of Hades (from Greek mythology) or Hel (from Norse mythology), but with a more passive and elemental nature. Alako is not a judge of souls or a punisher of the wicked; they are the silent, patient force that holds the world together from below. If the Finnish landscape is a vast, dark forest, then Alako is the deep, cold lake at its center — a place of mystery and power that is both frightening and essential. In a modern context, Alako could be seen as the personification of the deep web, the hidden layers of the internet that are not indexed by search engines. Just as Alako governs the unseen depths, the deep web is a place of secrets, knowledge, and danger that is largely inaccessible to the casual observer.
