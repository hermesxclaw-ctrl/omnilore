Who It Is

An apparition is not merely a ghost, but a category of seeing—a sudden, often inexplicable presence that crosses the boundary between the ordinary and the extraordinary. From the Marian visions that have moved millions to the flickering figures in horror films, the apparition is a phenomenon that challenges the limits of perception and belief.

Apparitions are typically reported as appearing suddenly and disappearing quickly, often delivering a message or performing a specific action. They may be silent or speak, and their demeanor can be benign, neutral, or terrifying.

Powers
Appearance

The ability to manifest visually to one or more persons, sometimes regardless of physical barriers.

Limit: Often limited to a specific location or time; not always controllable.

Communication

Apparitions may communicate verbally or non-verbally, conveying warnings, prophecies, or requests.

Limit: Communication may be cryptic or incomplete.

Bilocation

The ability to appear in two places at once, often attributed to living persons, especially saints or mystics.

Limit: Rare and often associated with altered states of consciousness.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Apparitions are described in a wide variety of forms: full-bodied figures, translucent shapes, mists, lights, or even sensory impressions without visual component. In religious contexts, they may appear as radiant beings or in recognizable forms (e.g., the Virgin Mary).

Symbols — In art, apparitions are often depicted as glowing, semi-transparent figures, sometimes with a halo or in a burst of light. In iconography, a ghostly figure may be shown in white, with a floating posture.

Domains — Apparitions are reported in a variety of locations: homes, battlefields, religious sites, and places of trauma. They are often associated with specific times (night, anniversaries) or events.

Accounts I
The Apparition of the Virgin Mary at LourdesCatholic tradition, documented by the Church

1858

Bernadette Soubirous reported 18 apparitions of the Virgin Mary in a grotto. The apparitions led to the establishment of a major pilgrimage site and numerous claimed miracles. Lourdes became one of the most visited Catholic shrines; Bernadette was canonized.

Accounts II
The Brown Lady of Raynham HallPhotograph taken by Captain Hubert Provand, published in Country Life magazine

1936

A photograph purportedly showing a ghostly figure on the staircase of Raynham Hall became famous. The figure is said to be the spirit of Lady Dorothy Walpole, who died in 1726. The photograph became one of the most famous ghost photos, but its authenticity is disputed.

The Doppelgänger of Abraham LincolnReported by Lincoln himself and others

1860

Lincoln reportedly saw his own double in a mirror, which he interpreted as an omen of his death. Similar accounts exist of other historical figures. The story contributed to Lincoln's mythos as a tragic figure.
