Who It Is

In the misty peaks of the Albanian highlands, a figure in white haunts the mountain paths—a fairy-like guardian whose justice is as swift as it is severe. She is Perria, the protector of bread, who punishes the wasteful with a curse that bends the body as crooked as their disregard for the sacred loaf.

Perria is generally protective, but she can also harm. Her primary role is to punish those who waste bread, a serious transgression in Albanian folk ethics. The punishment is severe: disfiguring the person and making them a 'crooked hunchback'.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Perria is depicted as a female mountain-spirit clad in white. She is described as fairy-like, suggesting a supernatural, ethereal quality. No other physical details are attested.

Symbols — White clothing is a symbol of her otherworldly nature. She is associated with mountains and bread, the latter being a symbol of sustenance and sacredness in Albanian culture.

Domains — Mountain regions of Albania.

Traditional folklore (pre-modern era)

Perria, a mountain spirit, observes a person wasting bread. In response, she disfigures the offender, turning them into a 'crooked hunchback' as a punishment for their disrespect. The offender is physically deformed, serving as a warning to others.
