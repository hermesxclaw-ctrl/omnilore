Who It Is

At the edge of Reeftown, where the reef drops into blue, a small yellow fish with a big question darts forward and asks what no one else thought to ask — what is the ocean, and how does everything in it fit together? That fish is Splash, the yellowback fusilier who turned a PBS Kids reef into a classroom without walls.

Attested personality across all episodes: exuberant, inquisitive, and socially inclusive. Splash functions as the audience surrogate — the newcomer to Reeftown who asks 'why?' and 'how?' and models scientific inquiry (observe, ask, test, share). He is loyal, impulsive in a childlike way, quick to apologize, and eager to include others. He shows mild anxiety when separated from the group (pilot One Big Ocean) but recovers through peer support. Reeftown Ranger Inquiry Method

Not a supernatural power; a modeled scientific practice. Splash consistently initiates the group's inquiry cycle: noticing a phenomenon, asking a question, observing, consulting an expert adult (often his parents or visiting marine animals), and reporting back. This is framed as his core ability within the show's curriculum (The Ocean is a connected system; Ask questions to learn).

Limit: Requires collaboration; Splash rarely solves problems alone and must seek help from Bubbles, Dunk, Ripple, or adult mentors. His impulsiveness sometimes leads to incorrect hypotheses that must be corrected.

Adaptable Swimming and Habitat Exploration

As a fusilier, Splash is shown as a fast, agile swimmer capable of navigating reef crevices, open water, and surface zones. Narrative allows him to visit habitats that in reality would be beyond a single reef fish's range (deep sea, Arctic via visiting characters) — explained as travel with friends or via storytelling, not biological realism.

Limit: Cannot survive out of water; needs to return to reef; no breathing or pressure adaptations beyond cartoon logic. Educational segments explicitly note real fusiliers are reef-associated and would not migrate to polar waters.

Social Bridge-Building and Translation for Audience

Splash translates adult marine science into preschool language, rephrasing expert explanations for his friends and thus for viewers. He also mediates social conflicts among the group (e.g., inviting shy Ripple into play).

Limit: Mediation fails when he interrupts or assumes; episodes model repair. No authority to override adult characters.

No Supernatural Powers

Explicitly, Splash has no magic, transformation, weapon use, or superhuman durability. The series avoids fantasy powers to keep focus on real ocean biology.

Accounts I
One Big Ocean (Pilot Telefilm)Splash and Bubbles, pilot telefilm; PBS Kids; written by John Tartaglia

2016

Splash, a young yellowback fusilier, leaves his home reef to explore the vast ocean and worries he will not find his way back or make friends. He meets Bubbles the mandarin dragonet, Dunk the mudskipper, and Ripple the seahorse. Together they discover that the ocean, though huge, is one interconnected system and that Reeftown can be a home for newcomers. The story establishes the Reeftown Rangers pledge and the series' core question-driven structure. Establishes the central quartet, Reeftown as hub, and the curriculum theme 'One Big Ocean.' Directly leads to series order.

I Only Have Eyespots for You / Double BubblesSplash and Bubbles S01E01 (11-min segments)

2016

In the first segment, Splash learns about eyespots on butterflyfish — markings that confuse predators — and tests whether eyespots make a fish look bigger or scarier. In the second, Bubbles tries to make herself look larger to impress, and Splash helps her understand that her natural bright colors already serve a purpose. Both segments model observation and comparison. Introduces the show's A/B segment structure and recurring 'investigate a trait' plot.

Why Is Bubbles So Blue? / Bubbles' Big Idea (representative color and light story)Splash and Bubbles S01E03-type segment (coloration theme)

2016-2017

Splash notices Bubbles' vivid orange-blue pattern and asks why she is so colorful while he is yellow and white. The group visits adult reef fish who explain camouflage, warning coloration, and mating displays. Splash learns that color has functions, not just beauty, and helps Bubbles appreciate her own pattern without needing to change it. Reinforces biodiversity and self-acceptance themes; used in educator guides for lessons on adaptation.

Accounts II
Dunk's Cleaning Day / Dunk's New FriendSplash and Bubbles S01E05

2017

Dunk the mudskipper, who can live on land and water, is embarrassed about his different abilities. Splash, eager to help clean the reef, learns from Dunk and from cleaner wrasse that different bodies suit different jobs. The story models inclusion of amphibious and intertidal life and explicitly teaches that mudskippers are not 'weird' but adapted. Expands habitat representation beyond coral reef to mudflats; frequently cited in reviews praising disability/inclusion subtext.

The Lure of the Deep / Ripple's Migration TaleSplash and Bubbles mid-Season 1 deep-sea and seahorse episodes (composite deep-sea arc)

2017

The Rangers follow a mysterious light to the deep sea and meet anglerfish and bioluminescent creatures. Splash is initially frightened by darkness and strange bodies, then learns how bioluminescence functions for hunting and communication. A parallel Ripple-focused story explores seahorse male pregnancy and vertical migration, with Splash asking respectful questions about family structures. Introduces deep-ocean literacy and counters fear of 'scary' animals; cited by NOAA partner materials as accurate bioluminescence introduction for preschoolers.

One Big Ocean Revisited — Reeftown Rangers to the Kelp ForestSplash and Bubbles S01E20+ kelp forest arc

2017-2018

The four friends travel to a kelp forest and meet sea otters, garibaldi, and kelp bass. Splash compares kelp holdfasts to coral and learns about forest-like ocean habitats. The narrative explicitly connects reef and kelp forest as parts of One Big Ocean, with Splash charting similarities and differences in a simple field journal. Demonstrates series' curriculum goal of habitat connectivity; final season reinforces that Splash's questioning is a transferable skill, not tied to one reef.

Splash is a dolphin, shark, or generic 'blue fish' — Splash is specifically a yellowback fusilier, a small reef fish. His yellow dorsal coloration and fusilier body shape are distinctive and confirmed by official guides.

Splash and Bubbles is a Disney or Nickelodeon show — It is a PBS Kids series produced by The Jim Henson Company and Herschend Studios, distributed by PBS. No Disney or Nickelodeon involvement.

Splash has superpowers like bubble-blowing or magic — Splash has no supernatural powers; bubble motifs belong to Bubbles and to the show's visual language. His 'power' is asking questions and modeling inquiry.

The 1984 film Splash and the PBS character are related — No relation. The 1984 film is a live-action mermaid romance starring Daryl Hannah; the PBS character debuted 32 years later in an unrelated educational series.

Splash is based on ancient folklore or mythology — Splash is a wholly modern creation (2016) for educational television with no folklore antecedent. No traditional stories, deities, or myths feature this character.
