Who It Is

Falak is a cosmic serpent-dragon of Islamic folk cosmology that exists below the foundations of the universe — the creature beneath everything. In the layered model of the universe found in medieval Arabic cosmographies (particularly in the works of Al-Qazwini and the Kitab al-Bulhan), the earth rests on the bull, the bull on the fish Bahamut, Bahamut in the Encircling Ocean, and below the ocean — below the darkness below the water — is Falak, the great serpent who encircles or supports the entire cosmic structure. The name 'Falak' is related to the Arabic word for 'orbit, sphere, or firmament' (as in 'falak al-buruj' — the celestial sphere), paradoxically connecting this subterranean being to the cosmic order above. Falak is described as so immense that the entire universe of seven heavens and seven earths is a small object in relation to it. If Falak were to open its mouth, it could swallow all of creation. In some traditions, Falak is the creature that will survive the end of the world (after the trumpet blast, after the mountains are ground to dust, after the heavens are rolled up like a scroll) — the last living thing in existence before God uncreates it. In others, Falak is the serpent that guards the treasure of the jinn or the entrance to the lowest hell. Falak represents the ultimate unknowability of the universe's foundation in Islamic folk cosmology — a being so vast and so deep that it is essentially a negative theology in monstrous form: God's creation extends so far beyond human comprehension that the 'bottom' of the universe is a serpent that could contain it all.
