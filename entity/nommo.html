Who It Is

The Nommo are the primordial ancestral spirits of Dogon cosmology — divine beings of water, speech, and order created by Amma to complete and perfect the universe after the flawed creation of the Pale Fox (Yurugu). The name 'Nommo' derives from the Dogon word for 'to make one drink' and refers to their essential nature as water beings. In Dogon thought, water is the primordial substance of life and the medium through which divine speech (the creative word) flows. The Nommo are thus the embodiment of creative communication and life-giving moisture.

According to Dogon mythology, Amma created a pair of Nommo twins — one male, one female — who were perfect beings, complete in their dual nature. These original Nommo multiplied into eight ancestral pairs (the eight primordial ancestors of humanity). The myth of the Nommo's sacrifice is central: to purify the world from the disorder introduced by the Pale Fox, the Nommo voluntarily allowed themselves to be dismembered, and their body parts were scattered across the cosmos. Where each piece fell, it became a source of life and knowledge: their blood became the rain, their breath the wind, their bones the minerals of the earth, and their voice the first human language.

Physically, the Nommo are represented in Dogon art as humanoid figures with fish-like or serpentine lower bodies, emphasizing their aquatic nature. They are often shown with raised arms — a gesture of prayer and also the position from which they pour forth the life-giving water of speech. The famous Dogon 'Nommo' sculptures depict elongated figures with flowing, fluid lines that suggest both water and spoken language. Dogon masks, particularly the 'kanaga' mask with its double-barred cross, represent the Nommo's connection between heaven and earth.

Functionally, the Nommo are the culture-bringers of humanity. They taught the Dogon people every essential art: agriculture (they brought the first seeds), metalworking (they taught the use of the forge), weaving (they taught the patterns that mirror the cosmos), and — most importantly — speech. In Dogon philosophy, speech itself is a watery substance; when a person speaks, they release a vapor that carries meaning, just as the Nommo pour water that carries life. This concept — the 'water of speech' — is one of the most distinctive elements of Dogon thought.

The Nommo also have a profound connection to the Sirius star system, which is central to Dogon cosmology. The cycle of Sirius B (Po Tolo) around Sirius A (Sigi Tolo) — a 50-year orbit — is commemorated in the Sigui ceremony, a once-every-sixty-years ritual of world renewal. The Nommo are said to have come from the Sirius system, arriving on Earth in an 'ark' that descended with thunder and fire, bringing the primordial seeds, animals, and knowledge that would establish human civilization. This account has generated enormous scholarly and popular interest, with some interpreting it as evidence of ancient extraterrestrial contact, though ethnographers emphasize its spiritual and symbolic significance within Dogon religious practice.
