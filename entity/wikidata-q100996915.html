Who It Is

Before the spike pierced the gymnasium's silence, before the national championship trophy gleamed under stadium lights, Kozue Ayuhara was just a girl who loved volleyball more than she loved herself. Her journey from a shy, self-doubting middle schooler to the captain of Japan's national team is a story of grit, friendship, and the quiet power of believing in something bigger than victory.

Kozue is initially shy, self-conscious, and prone to self-doubt, especially when compared to her more confident teammates. She is deeply kind, empathetic, and fiercely loyal, often putting others' needs before her own. Over the course of the series, she develops into a determined, courageous leader who inspires her team through her relentless effort and sportsmanship. She struggles with pressure and fear of failure but learns to channel her emotions into her play.

Dive Receive

Kozue's exceptional ability to dive and receive spikes that seem impossible to reach, often turning defense into offense.

Limit: Requires extreme physical exertion and can lead to injury if overused.

Attack No. 1 Spike

A powerful and precise spike that becomes Kozue's signature offensive weapon, executed with perfect timing and placement.

Limit: Requires a well-executed set and can be blocked by taller or more experienced opponents.

Leadership and Teamwork

Kozue's ability to rally her teammates, understand their strengths, and coordinate team strategy, often serving as the emotional core of the team.

Limit: Her leadership can be strained when personal conflicts arise within the team.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Kozue is depicted as a slender, athletic teenage girl with short, dark hair and expressive eyes. Her typical attire includes her school's volleyball uniform, and later the Japan national team jersey. In the original manga, her design is classic late-1960s shōjo style, with large eyes and a gentle face.

Symbols — Her signature move is the 'dive receive' and the 'Attack No. 1' spike, which became iconic. The number '1' on her jersey symbolizes her pursuit of excellence. The character is often associated with images of volleyballs and the Fuji Academy school emblem.

Domains — The narrative is set primarily in Japanese middle and high schools, volleyball courts, and training camps. Key locations include Fuji Academy High School, the national training center, and international tournament venues.

Accounts I
From Middle School to National ChampionAttack No. 1 manga and anime

1960s-1970s setting (contemporary at publication)

Kozue Ayuhara begins as a timid middle school student who joins her school's volleyball team to follow her friend. Despite initial lack of confidence, she discovers a natural talent for receiving and spiking. She transfers to Fuji Academy, a powerhouse school, where she faces intense competition and forms key rivalries and friendships. Through rigorous training and personal trials, she becomes the team's ace and eventually the captain. The story follows her through national high school championships and culminates in her selection for the Japan national team, leading them to international success. Kozue's journey inspires a generation of Japanese girls to take up volleyball and establishes her as a cultural icon of female athleticism and perseverance.

Accounts II
The Rivalry with Midori HayasakaAttack No. 1 manga and anime

Throughout the series

Midori Hayasaka is a talented and proud player who initially looks down on Kozue. Their rivalry drives both to improve, but it is also marked by personal conflict and mutual respect. They eventually become teammates on the national team, learning to combine their strengths. Their rivalry evolves into a strong partnership, symbolizing the importance of overcoming personal differences for team success.

Overcoming Personal TragedyAttack No. 1 manga and anime

Mid-series

Kozue faces a personal crisis when a close friend or teammate suffers a career-ending injury or personal loss. This event shakes her confidence and makes her question her own path. She eventually channels her grief into her play, honoring her friend's dreams. Kozue emerges stronger and more determined, with a deeper understanding of the fleeting nature of athletic careers.
