Who It Is

Lumawig is the great culture hero of the Igorot peoples of the Cordillera, believed to be the son of the supreme god Kabunian who descended to earth to teach humanity the arts of civilization. In Bontoc mythology, Lumawig taught the people how to build houses, plant rice using the terraced irrigation system for which the Cordillera is famous, weave cloth, forge metal, perform rituals, and organize villages. He established the laws of marriage, property, and governance. Unlike the distant Kabunian, Lumawig walked among humans, ate with them, and knew their joys and sorrows. He is associated with the Banaue Rice Terraces — one tradition says he carved the first terrace wall with his bare hands. Lumawig eventually returned to the sky, but his teachings endure in the elaborate rice terrace agriculture, weaving patterns, and customary laws of the Igorot. Many Cordillera communities claim specific mountains, rivers, and boulders as landmarks of Lumawig's journeys. He is revered not as a god to be worshipped but as the ultimate ancestor and teacher whose example all Igorot strive to follow.
