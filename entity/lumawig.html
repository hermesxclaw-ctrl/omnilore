Who It Is

In the highlands of northern Luzon, where the mountains rise above the clouds, the Bontoc people tell of Lumawig, a great hero and deity who shaped the world, taught their ancestors the arts of life, and still watches over them from the sky. He is not a distant, unknowable god, but a figure woven into the very landscape and daily life of the Igorot peoples.

Lumawig is depicted as a benevolent but demanding deity, who interacts directly with humans. He is a teacher and lawgiver, but also punishes wrongdoing. He is courageous and clever, often overcoming obstacles through wit and strength.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name Lumawig is of Bontoc origin, but its precise meaning is not well-documented. It may be related to concepts of leadership or divinity, but linguistic analysis is needed.

Pronunciation — loo-MAH-wig

Aliases — Lumawig

Ownership — Indigenous peoples of the Cordillera region of northern Luzon, Philippines, particularly the Bontoc. Also revered by other Igorot groups with variations.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Lumawig is typically described as a powerful, heroic figure, often associated with the sky. He is not described in detail in available sources, but he may be imagined as a tall, strong man, possibly with supernatural attributes. He is not depicted in physical form in traditional art.

Symbols — The sky, lightning, and mountains are associated with Lumawig. He is also linked to cultural artifacts like the gong and the head-axe (head-axe used in rituals). No known anthropomorphic depictions.

Domains — Sky, mountains, and the earth. He is believed to reside in the sky but descends to the mountains, particularly in the Bontoc region. He is associated with natural phenomena like storms and earthquakes.

Accounts I
Lumawig and the Creation of the BontocJenks, 'The Bontoc Igorot'

Unknown, recorded 1905

Lumawig came down to earth and, seeing the people living in ignorance, taught them how to plant rice, make tools, and observe proper rituals. He established social order and gave them laws. The Bontoc became a civilized people with agriculture and social structure.

Accounts II
Lumawig and the FloodCole, 'The Tinguian'

Recorded 1922

In a variant, Lumawig sends a great flood to punish the people for their wickedness. Only a few survive by climbing a mountain. The survivors repopulate the earth.

Lumawig's MarriageJenks, 'The Bontoc Igorot'

Recorded 1905

Lumawig married a mortal woman and lived among the Bontoc for a time, teaching them. Eventually, he returned to the sky, promising to watch over them. His descendants are believed to be the ancestors of the Bontoc.
