Name & Tongue

Etymology — The pinyin 'Ji' can represent several distinct Chinese surnames and characters, including 姬 (Jī), 冀 (Jì), 吉 (Jí), and 季 (Jì), each with different meanings. In Korean, 'Ji' (지) is a surname and element in given names, derived from Chinese characters.

Pronunciation — In Mandarin, 'Jī' is pronounced like 'jee' with a high flat tone; 'Jì' like 'jee' with a falling tone; 'Jí' like 'jee' with a rising tone. In Korean, it is pronounced like 'jee'.

Aliases — Jì, Jí, Ji (state), Ji (surname), Ji (Korean name), Hou Ji

Ownership — The name is shared across Chinese, Korean, Indian, and Japanese cultures, each with distinct traditions and meanings.

Form & Signs

Appearance — N/A - 'Ji' is a name, not a physical entity.

Symbols — The Chinese characters associated with Ji (姬, 冀, 吉, etc.) have symbolic meanings: 姬 refers to a noble lady or the royal surname of Zhou; 冀 means 'hope' or 'Hebei'; 吉 means 'auspicious'.

Domains — Geographically, Ji refers to ancient states in Beijing and Shandong, and provinces Hebei and Jilin. Culturally, it spans China, Korea, India, and Japan.

Hou Ji and the Founding of ZhouChinese mythology and historical records

Ancient

Hou Ji (Lord Millet) is a legendary culture hero credited with introducing millet cultivation and is considered an ancestor of the Zhou dynasty. Established the Zhou dynasty's legitimacy and agricultural traditions.

Accounts II
The Ancient State of JiHistorical records

Zhou dynasty

The state of Ji was a small ancient state located in modern Beijing, later absorbed by the state of Yan. The name Ji persisted as a place name in Beijing.
