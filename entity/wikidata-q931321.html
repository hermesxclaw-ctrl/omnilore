Who It Is

In the vast tapestry of Greek mythology, most figures are woven from the threads of great deeds, divine wrath, or tragic love. But Axioche is different: she exists in a single thread, a name on a single fragment of a lost epic, the mother of a pair of conjoined twins who would go on to fight at Troy. She is a genealogical ghost — a woman whose entire mythological existence is reduced to three lines of verse, yet whose bloodline produced one of the most physically extraordinary hero pairs in all of ancient story.

No specific behaviors, personality traits, or actions are recorded for Axioche. She appears solely as a genealogical figure — a daughter, a wife, and a mother — with no independent myths or adventures of her own. This is typical of many minor female figures in the Catalogue of Women, whose primary function was to connect heroic lineages.

Domains — Axioche's domain, if any, would be the region of Thessaly, where her father Phylas and her husband Actor were associated with the city of Phylace. She has no attested sphere of influence, cult, or religious function.

Accounts I
The Lineage of AxiocheHesiodic Catalogue of Women, Fragment 13

Archaic period (c. 6th century BCE)

Axioche is the daughter of Phylas, son of Antiochus, a minor hero of Thessaly. She is married to Actor, son of Myrmidon, the eponymous ancestor of the Myrmidons. Together they have two sons, Eurytus and Cleatus, who are described as 'mighty in battle.' These sons would later become famous as the Moliones, a pair of twin heroes who fought in the Trojan War. Axioche herself plays no active role in the narrative; she is a genealogical link between the house of Phylas and the house of Actor. Axioche's marriage to Actor produced the Moliones, who became significant figures in the Trojan War cycle. The Moliones were known for their unusual physical appearance — they were said to be joined at the waist, sharing a single body with two heads, four arms, and four legs. This made them formidable warriors and also the subject of much mythological curiosity. The genealogy thus connects Axioche to one of the most memorable and physically distinctive hero pairs of Greek myth.
