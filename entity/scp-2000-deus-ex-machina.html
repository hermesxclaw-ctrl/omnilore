Who It Is

A vast subterranean installation designed to reboot human civilization in the event of a K-Class End-of-the-World scenario. SCP-2000 uses advanced cloning technology, amnestic dispersal, and climate control to repopulate Earth with humans and reconstruct a baseline civilization from stored genetic and cultural templates. It has been activated at least twice in recorded Foundation history.

Form

An enormous underground complex spanning several cubic kilometers beneath Yellowstone National Park, containing cloning vats, gestation chambers, amnestic dispersal arrays, and cultural data archives.

The Approach

Imagine waking up tomorrow in a world that feels perfectly normal—your memories intact, your history unquestioned—only for a buried file to reveal that everything you know is a carefully reconstructed lie, assembled in secret beneath a national park by a machine that has already rebuilt humanity at least once before. SCP-2000, codenamed "Deus Ex Machina", is that machine: a subterranean civilization-reset engine whose quiet hum under Yellowstone may mean that the apocalypse has already happened, and that the story of humanity is now running on its second—or tenth—draft.

Content advisory. This dossier discusses fictional but intense themes: global apocalypse, mass death, non-consensual memory alteration, institutional secrecy, and ethical violations on a planetary scale. While SCP-2000 is a work of collaborative fiction, its scenarios may be disturbing to some readers, particularly those sensitive to narratives of extinction, identity manipulation, or abuse of power.

Etymology

"Deus ex machina" is a Latin theatrical term meaning "god from the machine", originally referring to a plot device in ancient drama where a deity is lowered onto the stage via a mechanical crane to resolve the narrative. In the SCP Foundation context, the phrase is repurposed ironically for a vast subterranean installation designed to reset human civilization after apocalyptic scenarios, functioning as a literal machine that plays a godlike role in determining the continuation of history.

Cultural Ownership

SCP-2000 is a collaborative-fiction construct originating on the SCP Foundation Wiki, authored primarily by the user HammerMaiden, and subsequently expanded through derivative tales, meta-essays, and fan wikis. It belongs to the broader internet-horror and speculative-fiction culture of the SCP project, which operates under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike licensing and is collectively maintained by an international online community rather than a single corporate or national owner.

Disputed Identifications

Within the SCP fandom, there is occasional confusion between SCP-2000 and other large-scale reset or world-rebuilding mechanisms (such as certain SCP-001 proposals or other Thaumiel-class facilities). However, the canonical SCP-2000 article clearly identifies it as a specific Yellowstone-based installation with Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators and Lazarus-01 Protocol, distinct from other reality-reset or timeline-altering anomalies. Some secondary sources (e.g., Grokipedia summaries) place SCP-2000 beneath Mount Yamantau in Russia, contradicting the primary SCP Wiki article that locates it under Yellowstone National Park; this is best understood as an adaptation or alternate-canon variation rather than a true identification dispute in the original text.

Description

Appearance

Attested: SCP-2000 is described as a massive subterranean installation constructed beneath Yellowstone National Park in the United States, with mission-critical components located between 75 and 100 meters below the surface. The entrance is disguised as a disused park ranger station. Internally, the facility contains extensive floor space devoted to storage of building materials, construction equipment, factory machinery, agricultural equipment, computer database storage, and thousands of copies of cultural artifacts and a backup of the Internet. It includes water treatment facilities, air purification and recycling systems, hydroponic production wings, and housing suitable for up to approximately 10,000 personnel. The exterior surface is surrounded by Scranton Reality Anchors arranged hexagonally every 20 meters. Reconstructed: Visual adaptations and fan art often depict SCP-2000 as a labyrinthine industrial complex with dimly lit corridors, reactor halls, cloning vats, and control rooms, emphasizing its scale and utilitarian design. These images are not part of the original text but extrapolate from the described functions.

Symbols & Iconography

Attested: The primary symbolic element associated with SCP-2000 is its codename "Deus Ex Machina", which functions as a meta-textual icon rather than a visual symbol. Within the SCP universe, the facility is also associated with the "Bloom", a mysterious flower-like anomaly beneath the installation that is said in some secondary sources to reset the universe when certain conditions are met; this motif of a hidden flower beneath a machine evokes themes of cyclical rebirth and concealed cosmic mechanisms. Reconstructed: Fan communities often use imagery of gears, reactors, cloning pods, and stylized flowers to represent SCP-2000, linking it visually to themes of industrial resurrection and artificial divinity.

Behaviour & Personality

Attested: SCP-2000 is not a sentient entity but a complex system of non-anomalous technologies and integrated anomalous stabilizers (such as SRAs and XACTS) designed to operate according to predefined protocols. Its "behavior" is procedural: when activated via Procedure Lazarus-01, it begins producing human replicants and reconstructing civilization according to stored templates. The article’s narrative voice, particularly in its opening monologue, conveys a cynical, weary perspective from a high-level Foundation insider who criticizes other personnel for failing to grasp the implications of repeated world resets. Interpretation: The facility embodies the Foundation’s utilitarian, secretive ethos—its design prioritizes function, redundancy, and control over aesthetics or comfort, and its existence implies a willingness to erase and rewrite history without public consent.

Domains & Habitats

Attested: SCP-2000’s domain is global human civilization, but its physical habitat is the subsurface region beneath Yellowstone National Park. It is designed to operate in the aftermath of K-Class end-of-the-world scenarios, including scenarios involving reality failure, nuclear war, pandemics, or other catastrophic events. Its systems are intended to function even in atmospherically compromised conditions, with internal life-support and food production sufficient to sustain personnel and newly created humans during the early phases of reconstruction.

Version Differences

Attested (original SCP article): SCP-2000 is located under Yellowstone, uses a Liquid Fluoride Thorium Reactor (1 GW, 70-year lifespan) plus geothermal backup, houses 500,000 Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators capable of producing 100,000 humans per day after a five-day warm-up, and employs Scranton Reality Anchors and Xyank/Anastasakos Continuous Temporal Sink (XACTS) systems to maintain causal stability. It has been activated at least twice, with records of its construction and early history lost during the presumed first activation. Alternate/secondary versions: Grokipedia and some fan sources place SCP-2000 beneath Mount Yamantau in Russia, likely reflecting an alternate canon or misreading of related SCP material. Fandom wikis and Baidu Baike emphasize slightly different details (e.g., the "Bloom" flower, specific incidents, or personnel lists). These differences represent interpretive or canon-variant layers rather than contradictions in the original text, though they can create confusion about location and ancillary features.

Powers & Abilities

Civilization Reconstruction via Lazarus-01 Protocol

Source Version Primary SCP-2000 article (SCP Foundation Wiki); Baidu Baike summary; Grokipedia overview.

Description

Attested: SCP-2000’s core ability is to reconstruct human civilization following a K-Class end-of-the-world scenario. Procedure Lazarus-01, initiated by entering a "Resume Date" into the system by a Level 5/2000 authorized individual (or equivalent surviving personnel), activates the Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators (BZHR units) and associated systems. The facility then produces human beings, implants memories via Class-G amnestics and neural archetype scans, and deploys them into a reconstructed environment with infrastructure, cultural artifacts, and knowledge restored from backups. This process can, in principle, repopulate the planet and re-establish a functioning society that believes itself continuous with the pre-catastrophe world.

Limitations

Attested: SCP-2000 is considered a last-resort measure; its activation is not intended for minor crises. The facility’s systems are non-anomalous in engineering terms but rely on complex integration of reality-stabilizing technologies, making them vulnerable to coordinated failures (e.g., simultaneous SRA and XACTS malfunction). The article notes that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, and that records of its construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, implying that repeated use may degrade information integrity or introduce unforeseen side effects. Ethical and psychological limitations include the fact that replicated humans are unaware of their artificial origin, raising questions about consent and identity.

Contradictions

Disputed: Some secondary sources suggest that SCP-2000 may have been activated more than twice, or that the current timeline is itself the product of multiple resets, but the primary article only attests to "at least twice" and emphasizes the loss of earlier records. There is no canonical numerical limit stated, but the narrative tone implies concern about cumulative damage or instability.

Mass Human Replication (Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators)

Source Version Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike; Fandom SCP-2000 page.

Description

Attested: SCP-2000 contains approximately 500,000 Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators (BZHR units), advanced cloning and fabrication systems capable of producing up to 100,000 developmentally capable humans per day after a five-day warm-up period. These replicants are biologically human and can be imprinted with memories and identities derived from stored neural archetypes and cultural data. The system can generate both generic population and specific prominent figures of the era to maintain continuity of leadership and social structures.

Limitations

Attested: BZHR units require raw material, which is funneled from hot springs and magma flows via underground transit pipes. They depend on stable power and environmental conditions within the facility. Failures in reality stabilization (SRAs, XACTS) or data corruption in neural archetype archives could produce malformed or psychologically unstable replicants. The article implies that the system has been fully reconstructed in laboratory environments, but comprehensive engineering studies of the entire facility are impossible without dismantling it, leaving some systemic vulnerabilities unknown.

Contradictions

Secondary tales and meta-discussions sometimes portray BZHR output as imperfect or subject to drift over multiple cycles, but these are interpretive expansions rather than explicit contradictions in the main article.

Reality Stabilization (Scranton Reality Anchors)

Source Version Primary SCP-2000 article; broader SCP lore on SRAs.

Description

Attested: The exterior surface of SCP-2000 is surrounded by Scranton Reality Anchors (SRAs) every 20 meters, arranged hexagonally, to prevent incursion by hostile anomalous interference and to maintain a stable baseline reality around the facility. SRAs are a recurring technology in the SCP universe, designed to counteract reality-warping anomalies by enforcing a fixed Hume level.

Limitations

Attested: Each SRA must be checked semi-annually and replaced as necessary. Technicians servicing SRAs follow specific procedures (Document SRA-033). Failures in SRAs could allow reality-warping anomalies to affect SCP-2000, potentially corrupting its reconstruction processes or compromising its secrecy.

Contradictions

None explicit in the SCP-2000 article; however, other SCP entries sometimes depict SRAs as imperfect or subject to overload, which may imply practical limits on SCP-2000’s protection.

Temporal Stability (Xyank/Anastasakos Continuous Temporal Sink, XACTS)

Source Version Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike summary; Grokipedia overview.

Description

Attested: SCP-2000 employs XACTS systems to maintain causal and temporal stability during reconstruction operations, preventing paradoxes or timeline fragmentation that could result from large-scale replication and memory implantation. XACTS is a recurring technology in SCP lore associated with temporal containment and manipulation.

Limitations

Attested: The article notes that failures in XACTS, especially when coinciding with SRA failures, can cause severe malfunctions during BZHR cycles. The exact mechanisms and limits of XACTS are not fully detailed, leaving some aspects of temporal stability as implied rather than explicitly described.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some tales suggest that XACTS may itself be responsible for timeline resets or memory loss, but these are narrative expansions rather than direct statements in the SCP-2000 article.

Knowledge and Cultural Archive Restoration

Source Version Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike; Fandom SCP-2000 page.

Description

Attested: SCP-2000 stores backups of the global human genome, extensive cultural artifacts, and a backup of the Internet, enabling it to restore not only population but also technological, scientific, and cultural knowledge. This includes thousands of copies of cultural artifacts and comprehensive data archives.

Limitations

Attested: The article acknowledges that records of SCP-2000’s own construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, demonstrating that even robust archives can be compromised. There is no guarantee that all cultural diversity or minority histories are preserved; the selection of artifacts and data likely reflects Foundation priorities and biases.

Contradictions

Interpretive: Some commentators argue that SCP-2000’s archives may produce a homogenized or Foundation-centric version of civilization, but this critique is extrapolated from the article’s premise rather than explicitly stated.

Stories & Myths

SCP-2000 — Deus Ex Machina (main containment file)

Source SCP Foundation Wiki, Item #: SCP-2000Culture Global, internet-based collaborative fiction (SCP Foundation)Period Early 2010s onward

Summary

Attested: The SCP-2000 article is framed as a high-clearance file accessible only to personnel with Level 4/2000 clearance, preceded by a cognitohazard warning and a monologue from an insider who criticizes colleagues for failing to understand the implications of the facility. The article describes SCP-2000 as a Thaumiel-class subterranean installation beneath Yellowstone National Park, designed to reconstruct human civilization after K-Class end-of-the-world scenarios via Procedure Lazarus-01. It details the facility’s power systems (LFTR and geothermal), BZHR cloning units, reality and temporal stabilization technologies (SRAs, XACTS), data archives, and housing. The article notes that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, that records of its construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, and that the current world may already be the product of a reset. Addenda discuss incidents, personnel, and ethical concerns about repeated use, including the possibility that Foundation staff themselves are replicants unaware of prior cycles.

Consequences

Attested: The narrative implies that SCP-2000 has already been used to erase and rewrite history at least once, possibly more, meaning that the current timeline and its inhabitants may be artificial reconstructions. This undermines any assumption of historical continuity and raises profound ethical questions about the Foundation’s authority to reset civilization. The existence of SCP-2000 also changes the stakes of other SCP stories: even if the world ends, the Foundation can attempt to rebuild it, though at the cost of authenticity and memory.

Variants

Secondary tales and meta-essays expand on SCP-2000’s activations, failures, and ethical implications, sometimes placing the facility in different locations (e.g., Mount Yamantau) or connecting it to other anomalies (such as SCP-073 and SCP-076-2 or the "Bloom" flower). These variants represent alternate canons or narrative explorations rather than revisions of the original article.

Incident 2000-2 and discovery of historical corpses

Source Referenced in Baidu Baike summary and secondary discussions of SCP-2000Culture SCP Foundation collaborative fictionPeriod Post-publication expansions

Summary

Attested (secondary): Baidu Baike and some fan summaries describe an Incident 2000-2 in which corpses dating back 450 to 700 years and associated documents were discovered in connection with SCP-2000, creating contradictions regarding the facility’s construction time and true history. Investigations reportedly confirmed that Alto Clef, a recurring Foundation character, had no direct connection to these discrepancies. Interpretation: This incident suggests that SCP-2000’s timeline may extend far deeper into the past than official records indicate, or that previous cycles of civilization reconstruction have been obscured or erased.

Attested (secondary): Fandom summaries describe an alternate universe (Universe-5000) in which the SCP Foundation declares omnicide upon itself in 2020 to stop a hostile entity, triggering the eruption of the Yellowstone supervolcano and obliterating SCP-2000. The environmental effects are slowed by anomalies controlled by the Manna Charitable Foundation, but Pietro Wilson theorizes that the world will eventually be consumed by ash. Interpretation: In this variant, SCP-2000 fails to serve its intended purpose because the very catastrophe it was meant to mitigate destroys it, highlighting the fragility of even the most powerful contingency plans.

Dark History
Content advisory. Content includes large-scale human extinction, covert erasure and rewriting of history, non-consensual memory implantation, existential manipulation of entire populations, and implied repeated omnicide or civilization resets. Ethical themes involve abuse of power, secrecy, and potential psychological harm to replicated individuals who are unaware of their artificial origins.

Human extinction and repeated world resets

Source Primary SCP-2000 article (introductory monologue and description).

Description

Attested: SCP-2000 exists specifically to respond to K-Class end-of-the-world scenarios, meaning that its activation presupposes near-total or total human extinction. The article states that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, implying that entire civilizations have already been wiped out and replaced by replicated populations. The opening monologue’s tone suggests that this may have occurred more than once, and that current personnel may themselves be products of such resets.

Scholarly Context

Interpretive analyses of SCP-2000 often focus on its commentary on apocalypse fiction and the ethics of "soft" omnicide—erasing a world and replacing it with a facsimile. The facility’s existence raises questions about whether repeated resets constitute a form of systemic violence against humanity’s historical continuity.

Non-consensual memory implantation and identity manipulation

Source Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike summary.

Description

Attested: SCP-2000 uses Class-G amnestics and neural archetype scanning to implant memories into replicated humans, creating individuals who believe they have lived lives that never actually occurred in the current timeline. This constitutes a profound violation of personal autonomy and epistemic integrity, as entire populations are constructed with false histories and identities.

Scholarly Context

In discussions of SCP-2000, commentators often compare this practice to dystopian narratives about brainwashing, false memories, and manufactured consent. The Foundation’s use of amnestics is a recurring theme in SCP lore, but SCP-2000 scales it up to a planetary level.

Secrecy, censorship, and loss of historical records

Source Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike summary.

Description

Attested: The article notes that records of SCP-2000’s construction and history prior to its presumed first activation were lost, and that it is impossible to determine whether this information blackout was accidental or deliberate. This suggests either catastrophic archival failure or intentional censorship by the Foundation, both of which constitute forms of epistemic violence against future generations.

Scholarly Context

Scholarly and fan analyses often interpret this loss of records as a metaphor for institutional secrecy and the erasure of inconvenient histories. SCP-2000 thus functions as a critique of organizations that control narratives about the past.

Omnicide and self-destruction (Universe-5000)

Source Fandom SCP-2000 page; SCP-5000-related tales.

Description

Attested (secondary): In Universe-5000 narratives, the SCP Foundation declares omnicide upon itself to stop a hostile entity, triggering the Yellowstone eruption that destroys SCP-2000. This decision represents an extreme form of self-directed violence and utilitarian sacrifice, with the collateral effect of eliminating the world’s primary reconstruction mechanism.

Scholarly Context

These stories are often read as explorations of the limits of utilitarian ethics and the dangers of institutions that wield absolute power over life and death. SCP-2000’s destruction in this context underscores the fragility of even the most carefully planned safeguards.

Contradictions & Disputes

Location of SCP-2000

Position A

Primary SCP-2000 article locates the facility beneath Yellowstone National Park in the United States, with the entrance disguised as a disused park ranger station.

Position A — evidence

SCP Foundation Wiki, SCP-2000 article: Special Containment Procedures explicitly state the Yellowstone location and park ranger station entrance.

Position B

Some secondary summaries (e.g., Grokipedia overview) describe SCP-2000 as located beneath Mount Yamantau in Russia.

Position B — evidence

Grokipedia "SCP-2000" overview, which summarizes the facility as located beneath Mount Yamantau.

Scholarly Consensus

Within the original SCP canon, Yellowstone is the accepted location. The Mount Yamantau variant is best understood as an alternate canon, misinterpretation, or adaptation rather than a correction of the original text.

Number of activations and historical depth

Position A

The primary article states that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, and that records of its construction and history prior to the presumed first activation were lost.

Position A — evidence

SCP Foundation Wiki, SCP-2000 article: description and historical notes.

Position B

Secondary sources (e.g., Baidu Baike, fan discussions) suggest that evidence such as centuries-old corpses and documents (Incident 2000-2) implies a much older history and possibly more activations than officially acknowledged.

Position B — evidence

Baidu Baike "机械降神" entry; fan summaries referencing Incident 2000-2 and 450–700-year-old corpses.

Scholarly Consensus

There is no definitive canonical resolution. Most readers accept "at least twice" as a minimum, while acknowledging that the narrative intentionally leaves open the possibility of more cycles. The incident evidence is treated as suggestive rather than conclusive.

Role of the Bloom and related anomalies

Position A

The primary SCP-2000 article focuses on technological systems (BZHR, SRAs, XACTS) and does not foreground a flower-like anomaly as central to SCP-2000’s function.

Position A — evidence

SCP Foundation Wiki, SCP-2000 article: emphasis on technological and procedural aspects; any mention of deeper anomalies is minimal or absent.

Position B

Fandom summaries and Baidu Baike describe a "Bloom" beneath SCP-2000 that can reset the universe when certain conditions are met, including alliances between SCP-073 and SCP-076-2 or deaths of prominent personnel.

Position B — evidence

Fandom SCP-2000 page; Baidu Baike summary referencing the Bloom and its conditions.

Scholarly Consensus

The Bloom is generally treated as part of extended lore or cross-canon storytelling rather than a core element of the original SCP-2000 file. Scholars of SCP fiction distinguish between the base article and later narrative accretions.

Ethical framing of SCP-2000’s use

Position A

Some readers interpret SCP-2000 as a necessary safeguard—a morally justified last resort to prevent permanent human extinction, even if it involves memory manipulation and historical erasure.

Position A — evidence

Supporters point to the article’s emphasis on preventing extinction and the non-anomalous nature of most systems, framing SCP-2000 as a pragmatic contingency.

Position B

Others argue that SCP-2000 represents a deeply unethical tool that enables the Foundation to commit repeated omnicide and rewrite civilization without consent, amounting to systemic abuse of power.

Position B — evidence

Critics highlight the loss of records, non-consensual memory implantation, and the insider monologue’s cynical tone as evidence of moral corruption.

Scholarly Consensus

There is no consensus; debates about SCP-2000’s ethics are central to its reception. The article is deliberately ambiguous, inviting readers to grapple with the tension between survival and autonomy.

Known Gaps

1. Primary textual scope: This dossier relies on the publicly accessible SCP-2000 article and major secondary summaries; it does not include every individual SCP tale or forum discussion that references SCP-2000, many of which may add nuanced variations or additional incidents. 2. Chronology and incident details: Incident 2000-2 and other activation events are only partially described in secondary sources; without direct access to all related tales, some narrative specifics remain reconstructed rather than fully attested. 3. Technical specifications: While the article states that all subsystems have been reconstructed in laboratory environments and are non-anomalous, detailed engineering data (e.g., reactor schematics, BZHR design) are not provided, limiting technical analysis. 4. Canon boundaries: SCP is a collaborative fiction with multiple canons; this dossier focuses on the main article and widely cited variants but cannot definitively map every alternate universe or cross-canon interpretation. 5. Non-English reception: Beyond the Chinese Baidu Baike entry, other language communities (e.g., Russian, Spanish, Japanese) likely have their own discussions and adaptations of SCP-2000 that are not covered here due to language and access limitations. 6. Authorial intent: HammerMaiden’s personal commentary outside the article (e.g., forum posts, interviews) was not systematically collected, so interpretations of thematic intent are based primarily on textual analysis rather than explicit statements.
