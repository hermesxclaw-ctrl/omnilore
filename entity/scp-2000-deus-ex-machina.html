Who It Is

A vast subterranean installation designed to reboot human civilization in the event of a K-Class End-of-the-World scenario. SCP-2000 uses advanced cloning technology, amnestic dispersal, and climate control to repopulate Earth with humans and reconstruct a baseline civilization from stored genetic and cultural templates. It has been activated at least twice in recorded Foundation history.

Form

An enormous underground complex spanning several cubic kilometers beneath Yellowstone National Park, containing cloning vats, gestation chambers, amnestic dispersal arrays, and cultural data archives.

Powers

Mass human cloning

Civilization-scale amnestic deployment

Civilization Reconstruction via Lazarus-01 Protocol Attested: SCP-2000’s core ability is to reconstruct human civilization following a K-Class end-of-the-world scenario. Procedure Lazarus-01, initiated by entering a "Resume Date" into the system by a Level 5/2000 authorized individual (or equivalent surviving personnel), activates the Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators (BZHR units) and associated systems. The facility then produces human beings, implants memories via Class-G amnestics and neural archetype scans, and deploys them into a reconstructed environment with infrastructure, cultural artifacts, and knowledge restored from backups. This process can, in principle, repopulate the planet and re-establish a functioning society that believes itself continuous with the pre-catastrophe world. Primary SCP-2000 article (SCP Foundation Wiki); Baidu Baike summary; Grokipedia overview. Attested: SCP-2000 is considered a last-resort measure; its activation is not intended for minor crises. The facility’s systems are non-anomalous in engineering terms but rely on complex integration of reality-stabilizing technologies, making them vulnerable to coordinated failures (e.g., simultaneous SRA and XACTS malfunction). The article notes that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, and that records of its construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, implying that repeated use may degrade information integrity or introduce unforeseen side effects. Ethical and psychological limitations include the fact that replicated humans are unaware of their artificial origin, raising questions about consent and identity. Disputed: Some secondary sources suggest that SCP-2000 may have been activated more than twice, or that the current timeline is itself the product of multiple resets, but the primary article only attests to "at least twice" and emphasizes the loss of earlier records. There is no canonical numerical limit stated, but the narrative tone implies concern about cumulative damage or instability.

Mass Human Replication (Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators) Attested: SCP-2000 contains approximately 500,000 Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators (BZHR units), advanced cloning and fabrication systems capable of producing up to 100,000 developmentally capable humans per day after a five-day warm-up period. These replicants are biologically human and can be imprinted with memories and identities derived from stored neural archetypes and cultural data. The system can generate both generic population and specific prominent figures of the era to maintain continuity of leadership and social structures. Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike; Fandom SCP-2000 page. Attested: BZHR units require raw material, which is funneled from hot springs and magma flows via underground transit pipes. They depend on stable power and environmental conditions within the facility. Failures in reality stabilization (SRAs, XACTS) or data corruption in neural archetype archives could produce malformed or psychologically unstable replicants. The article implies that the system has been fully reconstructed in laboratory environments, but comprehensive engineering studies of the entire facility are impossible without dismantling it, leaving some systemic vulnerabilities unknown. Secondary tales and meta-discussions sometimes portray BZHR output as imperfect or subject to drift over multiple cycles, but these are interpretive expansions rather than explicit contradictions in the main article.

Reality Stabilization (Scranton Reality Anchors) Attested: The exterior surface of SCP-2000 is surrounded by Scranton Reality Anchors (SRAs) every 20 meters, arranged hexagonally, to prevent incursion by hostile anomalous interference and to maintain a stable baseline reality around the facility. SRAs are a recurring technology in the SCP universe, designed to counteract reality-warping anomalies by enforcing a fixed Hume level. Primary SCP-2000 article; broader SCP lore on SRAs. Attested: Each SRA must be checked semi-annually and replaced as necessary. Technicians servicing SRAs follow specific procedures (Document SRA-033). Failures in SRAs could allow reality-warping anomalies to affect SCP-2000, potentially corrupting its reconstruction processes or compromising its secrecy. None explicit in the SCP-2000 article; however, other SCP entries sometimes depict SRAs as imperfect or subject to overload, which may imply practical limits on SCP-2000’s protection.

Temporal Stability (Xyank/Anastasakos Continuous Temporal Sink, XACTS) Attested: SCP-2000 employs XACTS systems to maintain causal and temporal stability during reconstruction operations, preventing paradoxes or timeline fragmentation that could result from large-scale replication and memory implantation. XACTS is a recurring technology in SCP lore associated with temporal containment and manipulation. Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike summary; Grokipedia overview. Attested: The article notes that failures in XACTS, especially when coinciding with SRA failures, can cause severe malfunctions during BZHR cycles. The exact mechanisms and limits of XACTS are not fully detailed, leaving some aspects of temporal stability as implied rather than explicitly described. Interpretive: Some tales suggest that XACTS may itself be responsible for timeline resets or memory loss, but these are narrative expansions rather than direct statements in the SCP-2000 article.

Knowledge and Cultural Archive Restoration Attested: SCP-2000 stores backups of the global human genome, extensive cultural artifacts, and a backup of the Internet, enabling it to restore not only population but also technological, scientific, and cultural knowledge. This includes thousands of copies of cultural artifacts and comprehensive data archives. Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike; Fandom SCP-2000 page. Attested: The article acknowledges that records of SCP-2000’s own construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, demonstrating that even robust archives can be compromised. There is no guarantee that all cultural diversity or minority histories are preserved; the selection of artifacts and data likely reflects Foundation priorities and biases. Interpretive: Some commentators argue that SCP-2000’s archives may produce a homogenized or Foundation-centric version of civilization, but this critique is extrapolated from the article’s premise rather than explicitly stated.

Incident Log

Attested: The SCP-2000 article is framed as a high-clearance file accessible only to personnel with Level 4/2000 clearance, preceded by a cognitohazard warning and a monologue from an insider who criticizes colleagues for failing to understand the implications of the facility. The article describes SCP-2000 as a Thaumiel-class subterranean installation beneath Yellowstone National Park, designed to reconstruct human civilization after K-Class end-of-the-world scenarios via Procedure Lazarus-01. It details the facility’s power systems (LFTR and geothermal), BZHR cloning units, reality and temporal stabilization technologies (SRAs, XACTS), data archives, and housing. The article notes that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, that records of its construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, and that the current world may already be the product of a reset. Addenda discuss incidents, personnel, and ethical concerns about repeated use, including the possibility that Foundation staff themselves are replicants unaware of prior cycles.

Attested (secondary): Baidu Baike and some fan summaries describe an Incident 2000-2 in which corpses dating back 450 to 700 years and associated documents were discovered in connection with SCP-2000, creating contradictions regarding the facility’s construction time and true history. Investigations reportedly confirmed that Alto Clef, a recurring Foundation character, had no direct connection to these discrepancies. Interpretation: This incident suggests that SCP-2000’s timeline may extend far deeper into the past than official records indicate, or that previous cycles of civilization reconstruction have been obscured or erased.

Attested (secondary): Fandom summaries describe an alternate universe (Universe-5000) in which the SCP Foundation declares omnicide upon itself in 2020 to stop a hostile entity, triggering the eruption of the Yellowstone supervolcano and obliterating SCP-2000. The environmental effects are slowed by anomalies controlled by the Manna Charitable Foundation, but Pietro Wilson theorizes that the world will eventually be consumed by ash. Interpretation: In this variant, SCP-2000 fails to serve its intended purpose because the very catastrophe it was meant to mitigate destroys it, highlighting the fragility of even the most powerful contingency plans.

Content Notes

Attested: SCP-2000 exists specifically to respond to K-Class end-of-the-world scenarios, meaning that its activation presupposes near-total or total human extinction. The article states that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, implying that entire civilizations have already been wiped out and replaced by replicated populations. The opening monologue’s tone suggests that this may have occurred more than once, and that current personnel may themselves be products of such resets.

Interpretive analyses of SCP-2000 often focus on its commentary on apocalypse fiction and the ethics of "soft" omnicide—erasing a world and replacing it with a facsimile. The facility’s existence raises questions about whether repeated resets constitute a form of systemic violence against humanity’s historical continuity.

Primary SCP-2000 article (introductory monologue and description).

Attested: SCP-2000 uses Class-G amnestics and neural archetype scanning to implant memories into replicated humans, creating individuals who believe they have lived lives that never actually occurred in the current timeline. This constitutes a profound violation of personal autonomy and epistemic integrity, as entire populations are constructed with false histories and identities.

In discussions of SCP-2000, commentators often compare this practice to dystopian narratives about brainwashing, false memories, and manufactured consent. The Foundation’s use of amnestics is a recurring theme in SCP lore, but SCP-2000 scales it up to a planetary level.

Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike summary.

Attested: The article notes that records of SCP-2000’s construction and history prior to its presumed first activation were lost, and that it is impossible to determine whether this information blackout was accidental or deliberate. This suggests either catastrophic archival failure or intentional censorship by the Foundation, both of which constitute forms of epistemic violence against future generations.

Scholarly and fan analyses often interpret this loss of records as a metaphor for institutional secrecy and the erasure of inconvenient histories. SCP-2000 thus functions as a critique of organizations that control narratives about the past.

Primary SCP-2000 article; Baidu Baike summary.

Attested (secondary): In Universe-5000 narratives, the SCP Foundation declares omnicide upon itself to stop a hostile entity, triggering the Yellowstone eruption that destroys SCP-2000. This decision represents an extreme form of self-directed violence and utilitarian sacrifice, with the collateral effect of eliminating the world’s primary reconstruction mechanism.

These stories are often read as explorations of the limits of utilitarian ethics and the dangers of institutions that wield absolute power over life and death. SCP-2000’s destruction in this context underscores the fragility of even the most carefully planned safeguards.

Primary SCP-2000 article locates the facility beneath Yellowstone National Park in the United States, with the entrance disguised as a disused park ranger station. ⇌ Some secondary summaries (e.g., Grokipedia overview) describe SCP-2000 as located beneath Mount Yamantau in Russia.

Evidence for B: Grokipedia "SCP-2000" overview, which summarizes the facility as located beneath Mount Yamantau.
Number of activations and historical depth
The primary article states that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, and that records of its construction and history prior to the presumed first activation were lost. ⇌ Secondary sources (e.g., Baidu Baike, fan discussions) suggest that evidence such as centuries-old corpses and documents (Incident 2000-2) implies a much older history and possibly more activations than officially acknowledged.
Evidence for A: SCP Foundation Wiki, SCP-2000 article: description and historical notes.
Evidence for B: Baidu Baike "机械降神" entry; fan summaries referencing Incident 2000-2 and 450–700-year-old corpses.
Role of the Bloom and related anomalies
The primary SCP-2000 article focuses on technological systems (BZHR, SRAs, XACTS) and does not foreground a flower-like anomaly as central to SCP-2000’s function. ⇌ Fandom summaries and Baidu Baike describe a "Bloom" beneath SCP-2000 that can reset the universe when certain conditions are met, including alliances between SCP-073 and SCP-076-2 or deaths of prominent personnel.
Evidence for A: SCP Foundation Wiki, SCP-2000 article: emphasis on technological and procedural aspects; any mention of deeper anomalies

What We Don't Know

1. Primary textual scope: This dossier relies on the publicly accessible SCP-2000 article and major secondary summaries; it does not include every individual SCP tale or forum discussion that references SCP-2000, many of which may add nuanced variations or additional incidents. 2. Chronology and incident details: Incident 2000-2 and other activation events are only partially described in secondary sources; without direct access to all related tales, some narrative specifics remain reconstructed rather than fully attested. 3. Technical specifications: While the article states that all subsystems have been reconstructed in laboratory environments and are non-anomalous, detailed engineering data (e.g., reactor schematics, BZHR design) are not provided, limiting technical analysis. 4. Canon boundaries: SCP is a collaborative fiction with multiple canons; this dossier focuses on the main article and widely cited variants but cannot definitively map every alternate universe or cross-canon interpretation. 5. Non-English reception: Beyond the Chinese Baidu Baike entry, other language communities (e.g., Russian, Spanish, Japanese) likely have their own discussions and adaptations of SCP-2000 that are not covered here due to language and access limitations. 6. Authorial intent: HammerMaiden’s personal commentary outside the article (e.g., forum posts, interviews) was not systematically collected, so interpretations of thematic intent are based primarily on textual analysis rather than explicit statements.

A way to hold it in your mind

According to traditional accounts, imagine waking up tomorrow in a world that feels perfectly normal—your memories intact, your history unquestioned—only for a buried file to reveal that everything you know is a carefully reconstructed lie, assembled in secret beneath a national park by a machine that has already rebuilt humanity at least once before. SCP-2000, codenamed "Deus Ex Machina", is that machine: a subterranean civilization-reset engine whose quiet hum under Yellowstone may mean that the apocalypse has already happened, and that the story of humanity is now running on its second—or tenth—draft.
