Who It Is

According to traditional accounts, imagine waking up tomorrow in a world that feels perfectly normal—your memories intact, your history unquestioned—only for a buried file to reveal that everything you know is a carefully reconstructed lie, assembled in secret beneath a national park by a machine that has already rebuilt humanity at least once before. SCP-2000, codenamed "Deus Ex Machina", is that machine: a subterranean civilization-reset engine whose quiet hum under Yellowstone may mean that the apocalypse has already happened, and that the story of humanity is now running on its second—or tenth—draft.

Attested: SCP-2000 is not a sentient entity but a complex system of non-anomalous technologies and integrated anomalous stabilizers (such as SRAs and XACTS) designed to operate according to predefined protocols. Its "behavior" is procedural: when activated via Procedure Lazarus-01, it begins producing human replicants and reconstructing civilization according to stored templates. Civilization Reconstruction via Lazarus-01 Protocol

Attested: SCP-2000’s core ability is to reconstruct human civilization following a K-Class end-of-the-world scenario. Procedure Lazarus-01, initiated by entering a "Resume Date" into the system by a Level 5/2000 authorized individual (or equivalent surviving personnel), activates the Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators (BZHR units) and associated systems. The facility then produces human beings, implants memories via Class-G amnestics and neural archetype scans, and deploys them into a reconstructed environment with infrastructure, cultural artifacts, and knowledge restored from backups. This process can, in principle, repopulate the planet and re-establish a functioning society that believes itself continuous with the pre-catastrophe world.

Limit: Attested: SCP-2000 is considered a last-resort measure; its activation is not intended for minor crises. The facility’s systems are non-anomalous in engineering terms but rely on complex integration of reality-stabilizing technologies, making them vulnerable to coordinated failures (e.g., simultaneous SRA and XACTS malfunction). The article notes that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, and that records of its construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, implying that repeated use may degrade information integrity or introduce unforeseen side effects. Ethical and psychological limitations include the fact that replicated humans are unaware of their artificial origin, raising questions about consent and identity.

Mass Human Replication (Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators)

Attested: SCP-2000 contains approximately 500,000 Bright/Zartion Hominid Replicators (BZHR units), advanced cloning and fabrication systems capable of producing up to 100,000 developmentally capable humans per day after a five-day warm-up period. These replicants are biologically human and can be imprinted with memories and identities derived from stored neural archetypes and cultural data. The system can generate both generic population and specific prominent figures of the era to maintain continuity of leadership and social structures.

Limit: Attested: BZHR units require raw material, which is funneled from hot springs and magma flows via underground transit pipes. They depend on stable power and environmental conditions within the facility. Failures in reality stabilization (SRAs, XACTS) or data corruption in neural archetype archives could produce malformed or psychologically unstable replicants. The article implies that the system has been fully reconstructed in laboratory environments, but comprehensive engineering studies of the entire facility are impossible without dismantling it, leaving some systemic vulnerabilities unknown.

Reality Stabilization (Scranton Reality Anchors)

Attested: The exterior surface of SCP-2000 is surrounded by Scranton Reality Anchors (SRAs) every 20 meters, arranged hexagonally, to prevent incursion by hostile anomalous interference and to maintain a stable baseline reality around the facility. SRAs are a recurring technology in the SCP universe, designed to counteract reality-warping anomalies by enforcing a fixed Hume level.

Limit: Attested: Each SRA must be checked semi-annually and replaced as necessary. Technicians servicing SRAs follow specific procedures (Document SRA-033). Failures in SRAs could allow reality-warping anomalies to affect SCP-2000, potentially corrupting its reconstruction processes or compromising its secrecy.

Temporal Stability (Xyank/Anastasakos Continuous Temporal Sink, XACTS)

Attested: SCP-2000 employs XACTS systems to maintain causal and temporal stability during reconstruction operations, preventing paradoxes or timeline fragmentation that could result from large-scale replication and memory implantation. XACTS is a recurring technology in SCP lore associated with temporal containment and manipulation.

Limit: Attested: The article notes that failures in XACTS, especially when coinciding with SRA failures, can cause severe malfunctions during BZHR cycles. The exact mechanisms and limits of XACTS are not fully detailed, leaving some aspects of temporal stability as implied rather than explicitly described.

Knowledge and Cultural Archive Restoration

Attested: SCP-2000 stores backups of the global human genome, extensive cultural artifacts, and a backup of the Internet, enabling it to restore not only population but also technological, scientific, and cultural knowledge. This includes thousands of copies of cultural artifacts and comprehensive data archives.

Limit: Attested: The article acknowledges that records of SCP-2000’s own construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, demonstrating that even robust archives can be compromised. There is no guarantee that all cultural diversity or minority histories are preserved; the selection of artifacts and data likely reflects Foundation priorities and biases.

Early 2010s onward

Attested: The SCP-2000 article is framed as a high-clearance file accessible only to personnel with Level 4/2000 clearance, preceded by a cognitohazard warning and a monologue from an insider who criticizes colleagues for failing to understand the implications of the facility. The article describes SCP-2000 as a Thaumiel-class subterranean installation beneath Yellowstone National Park, designed to reconstruct human civilization after K-Class end-of-the-world scenarios via Procedure Lazarus-01. It details the facility’s power systems (LFTR and geothermal), BZHR cloning units, reality and temporal stabilization technologies (SRAs, XACTS), data archives, and housing. The article notes that SCP-2000 has been activated at least twice, that records of its construction and early history were lost during the presumed first activation, and that the current world may already be the product of a reset. Accounts II
Incident 2000-2 and discovery of historical corpsesReferenced in Baidu Baike summary and secondary discussions of SCP-2000

Post-publication expansions

Attested (secondary): Baidu Baike and some fan summaries describe an Incident 2000-2 in which corpses dating back 450 to 700 years and associated documents were discovered in connection with SCP-2000, creating contradictions regarding the facility’s construction time and true history. Investigations reportedly confirmed that Alto Clef, a recurring Foundation character, had no direct connection to these discrepancies. Interpretation: This incident suggests that SCP-2000’s timeline may extend far deeper into the past than official records indicate, or that previous cycles of civilization reconstruction have been obscured or erased. Attested (secondary): The incident introduces doubt about the official narrative of SCP-2000’s construction and activation history, implying either deliberate obfuscation or systemic memory loss. It reinforces the theme that the Foundation’s own understanding of its tools may be incomplete.

Alternate universes and omnicide (Universe-5000)SCP-2000 page on Fandom SCP Foundation Wikia; related to SCP-5000 and associated tales

Later expansions (linked to SCP-5000 and other stories)

Attested (secondary): Fandom summaries describe an alternate universe (Universe-5000) in which the SCP Foundation declares omnicide upon itself in 2020 to stop a hostile entity, triggering the eruption of the Yellowstone supervolcano and obliterating SCP-2000. The environmental effects are slowed by anomalies controlled by the Manna Charitable Foundation, but Pietro Wilson theorizes that the world will eventually be consumed by ash. Interpretation: In this variant, SCP-2000 fails to serve its intended purpose because the very catastrophe it was meant to mitigate destroys it, highlighting the fragility of even the most powerful contingency plans. Attested (secondary): The destruction of SCP-2000 in Universe-5000 removes the safety net of civilization reconstruction, forcing survivors to confront a truly terminal apocalypse. It also underscores the possibility that the Foundation’s own actions can precipitate global catastrophe.
