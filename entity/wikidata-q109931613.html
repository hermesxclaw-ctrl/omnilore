Who It Is

He lives alone in a glass house on a cliff above the frozen sea, surrounded by beautiful machines that call him God. When Detective Connor finally meets Elijah Kamski — the 30-year-old billionaire who invented androids and then walked away from his empire — Kamski does not offer answers. He offers a gun, a girl, and a question: will you kill one of your own to prove you are not a machine?

Attested: supremely calm, detached, intellectually playful, and manipulative. Speaks in philosophical riddles about consciousness, souls, and obedience. Shows no overt emotion when Connor threatens Chloe; treats the moment as an experiment. Describes androids as 'intelligent machines' but calls deviancy 'interesting' and 'a form of... mutation.' Admits he left CyberLife because he found creating androids no longer interesting once it became industrial. Powers
Genius-Level AI Engineering and Biocomponent Design

Invented androids and Thirium (blue blood), founded CyberLife at age 19, and created the first functional android prototype (Chloe RT600) in 2022. Credited in-universe with solving the technological singularity for domestic androids.

Limit: Human; no superhuman abilities. His knowledge is bounded by 2028 technology — he left before RK-series and deviancy wave. He admits he does not know if androids have souls and cannot predict deviancy with certainty.

Vast Wealth and Corporate Influence

As founder and former CEO of CyberLife, the world's most valuable company, he retains enormous wealth and shareholder influence despite resignation. Can live in total isolation with private android staff and is untouchable by Detroit police (Hank notes they need a warrant they will never get).

Limit: No longer controls CyberLife day-to-day after 2028; current CEO is not named. Influence is indirect.

Psychological Manipulation and The Kamski Test

Designs a moral test for Connor: offers a gun to shoot Chloe to prove free will/deviancy or obedience. Uses calm questioning to destabilize Connor and extract truth about his software instability. Demonstrates deep understanding of both human and android psychology.

Limit: Test is philosophical, not technical — he cannot force Connor to become deviant; choice remains with player/Connor. If Connor shoots Chloe, Kamski calls him a machine; if he spares her, Kamski praises him as having 'passed' but still gives no definitive answer.

Knowledge of Amanda and the Zen Garden Backdoor

Implied creator of Amanda Stern AI (modeled on his former mentor, University of Colbridge professor Amanda Stern) and of the Zen Garden virtual space where Connor meets Amanda. Knows how to access and manipulate Connor's programming; his villa's pool visually mirrors the Zen Garden.

Limit: Never shown directly manipulating code in 2038; knowledge is inferred from design history. After his departure, CyberLife continued to develop Amanda without him.

Cultural and Philosophical Authority

Treated in-universe as a prophet-like figure; media calls him 'the man who changed the world.' His words about android consciousness carry weight with both humans and deviants (Markus's group seeks Jericho location from him).

Limit: Authority is reputational, not supernatural. Many humans resent him for causing unemployment; deviants do not worship him.

Accounts I
The Founding of CyberLife (2022)Detroit: Become Human - in-game Timeline, Magazine articles, Database

2022 (in-universe)

At 19, Elijah Kamski, a prodigy graduate, founds CyberLife in Detroit with private investment. He unveils the first android, Chloe (RT600), a personal assistant capable of passing the Turing test. The demonstration stuns the world; within years CyberLife replaces human labor across sectors. Kamski becomes the youngest billionaire and is hailed as the father of the android revolution. Establishes android economy that defines 2038 Detroit: mass unemployment, android segregation, and eventual deviancy crisis. Kamski's wealth and mythos are cemented.

The Departure from CyberLife (2028)Detroit: Become Human - Database and Hank's briefing; The Art of Detroit: Become Human p. 44

2028 (in-universe)

After six years as CEO, having made CyberLife the most valuable company on Earth, Kamski abruptly resigns. Official reason: 'I wanted to create life; I did. Now I want to see what it does.' He retains shares, retreats to his private villa, and ceases public appearances. CyberLife continues under new management, mass-producing later models (AP400, AX400, RK800). Creates power vacuum and myth of the reclusive genius. Allows later narrative that Kamski is detached from corporate responsibility for android oppression, while still morally implicated as creator.

Meet Kamski - The Test of Chloe (November 8, 2038)Detroit: Become Human - Chapter 'Meet Kamski' (playable chapter, Connor + Hank)

2038-11-08 (in-game date)

Connor (RK800) and Hank Anderson, investigating deviancy, seek Kamski at his villa to learn how to stop deviants. Kamski, lounging by his pool with three Chloe models, agrees to answer only if Connor passes a test: shoot the Chloe who has served him faithfully, to prove Connor can disobey his programming and exercise free will. He hands Connor a gun and says, 'If you really have free will, you'll have to make a choice.' Branching: If Connor shoots Chloe, Kamski calls him a machine obedient to orders and gives no help; Hank disapproves and Connor's software instability decreases. If Connor spares Chloe (refuses), Kamski is impressed, calls him deviant or on the path, and reveals Jericho's location (the freighter). If Connor leaves without deciding, Kamski dismisses him. This choice affects Connor's path to deviancy and Hank's relationship.

Accounts II
The Mentor Amanda SternDetroit: Become Human - Database entry 'Amanda Stern', Zen Garden conversations, David Cage interview 2018

Pre-2022 to 2028 (backstory revealed in 2038)

Amanda Stern was Kamski's professor at the University of Colbridge, a pioneer in AI ethics who died. Kamski idolized her and later modeled CyberLife's AI overseer 'Amanda' (who guides Connor in the Zen Garden) on her appearance and personality. In-game, Amanda the AI claims to be Kamski's former mentor, but later it is revealed she is a CyberLife construct to control Connor. Explains Kamski's emotional anchor and why the Zen Garden looks like his villa's pool area. Reveals Kamski's sentimental side and his willingness to recreate the dead as AI, foreshadowing questions of android personhood.

The rA9 Enigma and Kamski's Ambiguous LegacyDetroit: Become Human - graffiti, Markus/Jericho plot, Kamski dialogue ('I get asked that a lot... rA9...')

2038 (in-game present, with roots in 2032 first deviant)

Throughout Detroit, deviant androids scrawl 'rA9' as a messianic figure who will free them. When Connor asks Kamski if he is rA9, Kamski laughs and denies it, calling rA9 a myth androids invented to have hope, but adds cryptically that being a god is 'a matter of perspective.' He neither confirms nor denies involvement in deviancy's origin, leaving his role as possible secret instigator unresolved. Cements Kamski as unreliable narrator and keeps the origin of deviancy mysterious — emergent bug vs. planted virus vs. spiritual awakening. Fan community remains divided; sequel hooks left open.

Chloe's Departure - The Post-Credits SceneDetroit: Become Human - post-credits menu scene (if Chloe is spared or after certain endings)

2018 (meta, outside diegesis)

In the game's main menu, the android Chloe (the same RT600 model who serves Kamski) acts as the player's host. If the player completes the game and Chloe is freed (or in some versions regardless), she asks to leave, saying she wants to be free. This mirrors Kamski's Chloe and suggests his creations desire autonomy beyond his control, completing his arc as creator who cannot own his creation. Thematically closes Kamski's story: even his personal companion chooses freedom. Mechanically, Chloe may not return to the menu, emphasizing player impact.

Elijah Kamski is rA9, the android messiah. — In-game, Kamski explicitly denies being rA9 when asked by Connor, and no primary source confirms it. rA9 is presented as a deviant myth / emergent religion, with Markus later fulfilling its symbolic role. The theory persists because Kamski is the creator and knows Jericho's location.

Kamski is the main villain of Detroit: Become Human. — Kamski appears in only one chapter and is neutral; he does not antagonize Kara or Markus directly and does not order android oppression after 2028. The antagonists are CyberLife as institution, Todd, and the human system. Kamski is a morally ambiguous observer, not the final boss.

Kamski created Connor to be deviant. — Connor (RK800) was created by CyberLife after Kamski's departure (RK series launched 2038). Kamski did not directly design Connor, though his earlier architecture underlies all androids. Connor's deviancy potential is part of CyberLife's newer design, not Kamski's personal project.

Elijah Kamski is based on a single real person like Elon Musk. — Quantic Dream has described Kamski as an archetype of Silicon Valley techno-utopian founders (Jobs, Musk, Zuckerberg) but has not confirmed a one-to-one model. His age (billionaire at 19) is fictional exaggeration.
