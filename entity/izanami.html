Who They Are

Izanami is the primordial creator goddess of Shinto who, with her husband Izanagi, stirred the chaotic sea with a jeweled spear and raised the islands of Japan from the brine. Together they birthed the islands themselves, then the gods of wind, mountains, rivers, and trees — until the birth of the fire god Kagutsuchi burned her fatally from within. She descended to Yomi, the polluted land of the dead. When Izanagi came to retrieve her, he broke his promise not to look at her and saw her rotting, maggot-ridden form by torchlight. Humiliated and enraged, she pursued him to the border of the living world, where he sealed the pass with a boulder. Through the rock she vowed to kill a thousand people every day; he answered that he would cause one thousand five hundred to be born. In that exchange Shinto explains both death's constancy and life's stubborn arithmetic — and Izanami, first mother of the world, became its first death goddess.

Origins

One of the seventh generation of primordial deities, she and Izanagi were tasked by the elder gods with solidifying the drifting land. Their courtship ritual around a heavenly pillar is one of the oldest recorded marriage rites in Japanese literature — and its first attempt failed because Izanami spoke first, a detail generations of commentators have argued over ever since.

Attested in Kojiki (712 CE), Nihon Shoki (720 CE)Era Classical Japan

Form

In life, a radiant creator goddess. In Yomi, a decaying corpse-queen attended by the eight thunder kami — a form so terrible that seeing it severed the world's first marriage.

Powers

World-creation, divine birth, and dominion over death itself. Her daily vow of a thousand deaths makes her the engine of human mortality in Shinto cosmology.

Cultural Significance

The Izanami-Izanagi cycle establishes core Shinto concepts: ritual purity, the pollution of death (kegare), and purification (misogi) — the washing that birthed Amaterasu, Tsukuyomi, and Susanoo. Every Shinto purification rite descends from her story.
