Who It Is

In the heart of Istria, under the shadow of Venetian rule, there lived a giant whose heart was as vast as his stature—Veli Jože, the gentle serf who carried the burdens of a town that feared and scorned him, until the sea and a fellow giant awakened in him the fire of freedom.

Friendly, good-hearted, obedient, and initially submissive. He is hardworking and loyal, but capable of anger and eventually develops a desire for freedom and self-respect.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — Croatian 'veli' (big, great) + 'Jože' (a form of Joseph). The name literally means 'Big Joe' or 'Great Joe'.

Pronunciation — VEH-lee YO-zheh

Aliases — Big Joe, Great Joe

Ownership — Croatian literature, specifically Istrian regional culture; part of the broader South Slavic literary tradition.

Powers
Superhuman Strength

Able to throw a donkey into a tree, shake a bell tower, and sweep away an army with oak trees as brooms.

Limit: Strength does not protect him from emotional manipulation or social oppression initially.

Longevity

Jože is three hundred years old, possibly older, indicating an extended lifespan.

Limit: Not clearly defined; he ages but remains strong.

Form & Signs

Appearance — A giant of immense stature, strong enough to throw a donkey into a tree and carry a man with ease. He is described as rugged and imposing, yet his face is benevolent and kind.

Symbols — The Turkey oak tree symbolizes the natural wealth of Istria that the Venetians exploit. The chains and galley represent enslavement. The hills of Istria symbolize freedom and homeland.

Domains — The Istrian peninsula, specifically the town of Motovun and its surrounding forests and fields. Later, he dwells in the hills between Pazin and Motovun.

Accounts I
The Marking of the Turkey OakVeli Jože (1908)

Venetian rule (historically 15th-18th century, but fictionalized)

The Venetian provveditore Barbabianka attempts to mark a Turkey oak for cutting, but Jože throws his donkey into the tree, defying the authority. He is subsequently subdued and humiliated. Jože is forced to kneel and kiss the provveditore's hand, reinforcing his serfdom.

Accounts II
The Voyage to Venice and the Meeting with IlijaVeli Jože (1908)

Venetian rule

Jože is taken to Venice as a novelty. On the ship, he discovers a chained giant named Ilija, who inspires him to fight for freedom. A storm wrecks the ship, and Jože escapes. Jože gains a new understanding of his oppression and vows to fight for freedom.

The Gathering of the Giants and the Battle at Psoglavčevo BrdoVeli Jože (1908)

Venetian rule

Jože gathers twenty giant serfs and they settle on the hills, cultivating the land freely. When the townspeople send an army, the giants sweep them away with oak trees, avoiding bloodshed. The giants establish a free community, and the townspeople are forced to negotiate.
