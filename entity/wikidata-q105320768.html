The Approach

In the heart of Istria, under the shadow of Venetian rule, there lived a giant whose heart was as vast as his stature—Veli Jože, the gentle serf who carried the burdens of a town that feared and scorned him, until the sea and a fellow giant awakened in him the fire of freedom.

Content advisory. Depictions of serfdom, class oppression, and threats of violence; brief mention of poisoning and enslavement; themes of rebellion and freedom.
Identity

Veli Jože

Pronunciation VEH-lee YO-zheh

Known As

Big JoeGreat Joe

Etymology

Croatian 'veli' (big, great) + 'Jože' (a form of Joseph). The name literally means 'Big Joe' or 'Great Joe'.

Cultural Ownership

Croatian literature, specifically Istrian regional culture; part of the broader South Slavic literary tradition.

Disputed Identifications

No known disputes; the character is unique to Nazor's novel, though inspired by local legends of friendly giants.

Description

Appearance

A giant of immense stature, strong enough to throw a donkey into a tree and carry a man with ease. He is described as rugged and imposing, yet his face is benevolent and kind.

Symbols & Iconography

The Turkey oak tree symbolizes the natural wealth of Istria that the Venetians exploit. The chains and galley represent enslavement. The hills of Istria symbolize freedom and homeland.

Behaviour & Personality

Friendly, good-hearted, obedient, and initially submissive. He is hardworking and loyal, but capable of anger and eventually develops a desire for freedom and self-respect.

Domains & Habitats

The Istrian peninsula, specifically the town of Motovun and its surrounding forests and fields. Later, he dwells in the hills between Pazin and Motovun.

Version Differences

No major differences noted; the character appears consistent across the novel.

The Marking of the Turkey Oak

Summary

The Venetian provveditore Barbabianka attempts to mark a Turkey oak for cutting, but Jože throws his donkey into the tree, defying the authority. He is subsequently subdued and humiliated.

Serfdom and exploitation

Source Novel (1908)

Description

Jože is forced to do the hardest labor for the town, is cursed at, and is considered property. The townspeople discuss poisoning him and enslaving him further.

Scholarly Context

Reflects historical realities of feudal serfdom in Istria under Venetian rule.

Enslavement of giants

Source Novel (1908)

Description

Ilija is chained and enslaved on a galley, representing the brutal treatment of captured giants.

Scholarly Context

Symbolizes the oppression of the lower classes and the dehumanization of labor.

Contradictions & Disputes

Jože's obedience vs. his strength

Position A

Jože is initially obedient despite his immense strength, allowing himself to be humiliated.

Position A — evidence

He kneels and kisses the provveditore's hand.

Position B

His strength could easily overpower his masters, but he does not use it until later.

Position B — evidence

He throws the donkey and later fights for freedom.

Scholarly Consensus

This is a character arc from submission to rebellion, not a contradiction.
