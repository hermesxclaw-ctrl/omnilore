Who It Is

SCP-217 is an anomalous virus that infects organic tissue and systematically replaces it with mechanical components — gears, springs, pistons, and clockwork mechanisms. The transformation begins at the site of infection and spreads outward over a period of weeks. Infected tissue converts into brass, steel, and other metals, arranged in intricate mechanical patterns that continue to function in ways analogous to the original biology. A heart becomes a pump. Muscles become pistons and cables. Blood becomes a viscous lubricant that smells faintly of machine oil. The infected individual remains conscious throughout the transformation, their brain gradually converted to mechanical components — though at what point consciousness ceases or changes is not fully understood. In the final stages, infected humans become entirely mechanical, their movements jerky and rhythmic, producing a constant ticking sound. They retain some motor function and appear driven by a compulsion to spread the virus to others. SCP-217 was discovered in a small town in █████, where the entire population had been converted. Foundation recovery teams reported finding the town 'running' — former citizens continuing their daily routines as clockwork automata, moving through the motions of a life they no longer understood. The virus spread was halted through quarantine and incineration of all infected. Samples are maintained in a Class-4 biohazard facility. SCP-217 represents a unique blend of body horror and steampunk aesthetic, and it taps into the fear of losing one's humanity not to disease or death but to technology — becoming a machine while still being, in some terrible way, aware. The entity also serves as a cautionary tale about the Foundation's own limitations: SCP-217 is curable only in early stages, and the converted can never be restored.
