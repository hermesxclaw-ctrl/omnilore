Who It Is

In the frozen heights of the Jura and the Alps, where snow lies deep and avalanches roar, the Barbegazi are said to tread with silent, snowshoe-like feet. These little mountain folk, with their white beards and frosty breath, are as elusive as the winter wind—sometimes guiding lost travelers, sometimes hurling snowballs at the unwary, and always leaving behind only the faintest trace in the drifts.

Barbegazi are generally shy and reclusive, avoiding human contact. They are known to be helpful to lost travelers, sometimes leading them to safety or providing warmth and food. However, they are also mischievous and can be vindictive if provoked or if their mountain homes are disturbed. They are said to communicate in a high-pitched, whistling language. They are most active during winter, especially during snowstorms, and are believed to hibernate or disappear during the summer months.

Powers
Snow-walking

Barbegazi can walk on the surface of deep snow without sinking, thanks to their large, snowshoe-like feet. This allows them to move silently and swiftly across treacherous terrain.

Limit: They are limited to snowy terrain; on bare ground they are slower and more vulnerable.

Avalanche control

Some tales attribute to Barbegazi the ability to trigger or prevent avalanches, either to protect their homes or to punish intruders. They are said to ride avalanches like surfers.

Limit: This ability is not consistently described; it may be a later embellishment.

Cold resistance

Barbegazi are naturally immune to extreme cold and can survive in blizzard conditions that would be fatal to humans.

Limit: No known limitations.

Invisibility or camouflage

Some accounts suggest that Barbegazi can blend into the snow so effectively that they are nearly invisible, especially during snowstorms.

Limit: This may be a natural camouflage rather than a magical power.

Whistling communication

They communicate using high-pitched whistles that can carry over long distances in the mountains, possibly to coordinate or warn each other.

Limit: Not a supernatural power, but a natural adaptation.

Form & Signs

Appearance — Barbegazi are described as small, dwarf-like humanoids, roughly the size of a child, with thick white beards that extend to their knees. Their beards are often depicted as covered in icicles or frost. They wear clothing made of animal skins or coarse wool, usually in shades of white, gray, or brown, which helps them blend into the snowy landscape. Symbols — The primary symbol associated with the Barbegazi is the snowshoe, reflecting their ability to traverse snow. They are also associated with icicles, frost, and the color white. In visual representations, they are often shown with a long beard and a conical or hooded cap.

Domains — Barbegazi inhabit the high alpine meadows, rocky crags, and glacial caves of the Swiss Jura and the Alps. They are particularly associated with areas prone to avalanches, where they are said to ride snow slabs or cause avalanches to protect their territory. They are rarely seen below the snowline and are most often encountered in remote, high-altitude areas.

Accounts I
The Lost ShepherdOral tradition, collected in the 19th century

Undated, likely pre-19th century

A shepherd gets caught in a sudden blizzard while tending his flock in the high pastures. Disoriented and freezing, he stumbles upon a group of Barbegazi who lead him to a sheltered cave, provide him with warm food, and keep him alive until the storm passes. When the weather clears, they guide him back to his village, but warn him never to speak of them. The shepherd keeps his promise, but years later, when he is on his deathbed, he tells the story to his grandson. The shepherd survives and keeps the encounter secret; the tale becomes a family legend.

Accounts II
The Snowball FightOral tradition, collected in the 20th century

Undated

A group of children from a mountain village venture too close to a Barbegazi burrow while playing in the snow. The Barbegazi, angered by the intrusion, pelt them with snowballs that are said to be as hard as stones. The children flee, and the village elders warn them to stay away from that part of the mountain, saying the Barbegazi do not tolerate disrespect. The children learn to respect the creatures' territory; the location is considered taboo.

The Avalanche WarningOral tradition, reported by mountaineers

20th century

A group of mountaineers are warned by a Barbegazi that an avalanche is about to occur. The creature appears briefly, points to a slope, and then disappears into the snow. The mountaineers heed the warning and move to safer ground just before the avalanche sweeps down, destroying the path they had planned to take. The mountaineers survive and later report the encounter, adding to the lore of helpful Barbegazi.
