Who It Is

The Barbegazi are dwarf-like creatures from the folklore of the Alpine regions of Switzerland and France (particularly the Jura and upper Rhône valley). Their name is derived from the French 'barbe-glacée' meaning 'frozen beard,' referring to their most distinctive feature — long, icy beards covered in frost and icicles. Barbegazi are described as small humanoid beings, roughly the size of a child, with white or pale gray skin, snow-white hair, enormous feet that they use like skis to travel across snowfields, and thick fur or wool-like clothing. They inhabit caves and crevasses within glaciers, emerging only during the coldest months of winter. Barbegazi are fundamentally benevolent toward humans: they help lost mountaineers find shelter during blizzards, rescue shepherds buried in avalanches, and guide travelers through treacherous mountain passes. Their enormous flat feet allow them to walk on top of deep snow without sinking, and they can predict avalanches by listening to the snowpack with their large, pointed ears. In return for their help, they ask only for small offerings of cheese, bread, or milk left at cave entrances. However, Barbegazi are intensely shy and disappear instantly if they feel observed or threatened. They cannot tolerate heat, summer, or being indoors, and they are said to melt like snow if forced into a warm building. The legend of the Barbegazi represents the Alpine personification of winter survival — a kindly spirit of the frozen heights who embodies the mountain's protective aspect.
