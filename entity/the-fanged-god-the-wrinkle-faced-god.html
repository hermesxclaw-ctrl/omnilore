Who It Is

In the moonlit valleys of the Moche world, a god bares his fangs from ancient ceramic vessels, his wrinkled face a mask of both wisdom and terror. He is the Fanged God, a being of contradictions—creator and destroyer, healer and warrior—whose image still haunts the arid coasts of Peru.

The god appears to be a powerful, fearsome deity associated with warfare, sacrifice, and possibly the sun. He may be a creator god who demands blood to ensure cosmic order. His wrinkled face suggests age and wisdom, but his fangs indicate ferocity.

Name & Tongue

Etymology — The name 'Fanged God' is a descriptive term used by modern scholars. The original Moche name is unknown. 'Wrinkle-Faced' refers to his aged, wrinkled visage.

Form & Signs

Appearance — The Fanged God is typically depicted as a male figure with a wrinkled, aged face, large fangs (often jaguar-like), and a headdress. He may be shown with a serpent belt, holding a club or a sacrificial knife, and sometimes with a decapitated head. His eyes are often wide and staring, and he may have a fierce expression.

Symbols — Associated with fangs, wrinkles, serpents, jaguars, and sacrificial implements. He is often shown in scenes of ritual warfare, human sacrifice, and the presentation of blood or severed heads.

Domains — Likely celestial, possibly solar, and chthonic associations. He is depicted in ceremonial contexts, often on high platforms or in mountain settings, suggesting a link to the sky and the sacred mountains.

Accounts I
The Sacrifice CeremonyMoche iconography (e.g., mural at Huaca de la Luna)

100–800 CE

The Fanged God is depicted overseeing the sacrifice of prisoners. Warriors present the victims, whose blood is offered to the god. The god may be shown drinking blood or receiving a goblet. This ceremony likely reenacted a mythic event to ensure cosmic order and agricultural fertility. The sacrifice is believed to have been a central ritual for maintaining the world's balance.

Accounts II
The Battle of the GodsMoche pottery and murals

100–800 CE

Scenes depict the Fanged God in combat with other supernatural beings, possibly sea monsters or other deities. These battles may represent cosmic struggles between order and chaos. Victory ensures the continuation of the world.
